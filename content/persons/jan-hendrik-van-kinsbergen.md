**Jan Hendrik van Kinsbergen** was a Dutch admiral who commanded naval forces during the Fourth Anglo‑Dutch War, including the engagement at [[Doggersbank]] on [[5 August]] [[1781]]. Contemporary accounts credit him with tactical skill that helped the Dutch fleet hold its own against the superior British navy.

## Early Career
Van Kinsbergen entered the Dutch navy in the early eighteenth century, rising through the ranks to become *luitenant‑generaal* (lieutenant‑general) of the navy. He was known for his reforms of naval discipline and for advocating the integration of marine detachments into fleet actions.

## Role at Doggersbank
During the battle, Van Kinsbergen’s flagship engaged the British line, and his leadership was praised by Rear‑Admiral [[Charles Ekins]] (see [[Charles Ekins]]). The Dutch fleet, though outnumbered, managed to inflict significant damage, with the battle being commemorated on a silver medal awarded by [[Stadholder Willem V]].

## Connection to Aceh
Van Kinsbergen’s actions contributed to the preservation of Dutch naval strength, which was essential for maintaining the VOC’s trade routes to the Sultanate of [[Aceh]]. His advocacy for a larger marine corps also aligned with the 1781 proposal to raise 6 000 marines.

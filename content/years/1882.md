Figures, places, and events in Acehnese history associated with the year [[1882]].

## De Buitenbezittingen Account

**[[1882]]** was the year in which the management of the small Atjeh railway line between the roadstead of [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and the military station at [[Kota Radja]] — built in [[1875]] by the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]] shortly after the outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] — was transferred from the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]] to the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|Department of Civil Public Works (B.O.W.)]]. Under the B.O.W. the spoorbaantje was "narrowed" (*versmald*) into a trambaan — that is, converted into a tramway suited to lighter, more flexible traffic, marking the first step in the transformation of the original military field railway into a proper tramway network.

This administrative transfer proved short-lived. In [[1891]], after the construction of the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway]] along the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] in [[1885]], the management of all Atjeh lines reverted to the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]]. The Atjeh lines would only become a permanent civil concern again with the post-[[1896]] reorientation of Dutch Atjeh policy and the long-line construction beginning in [[1897]].

## Significance

The [[1882]] transfer is the first administrative recognition that the Atjeh railway had outgrown its purely military origins and required civil engineering oversight. The narrowing (*versmalling*) of the track to a tramway gauge likewise signals the early conceptual shift from a military field railway to a permanent transport infrastructure — even though, in practice, the line would remain under military management for most of the next two decades.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1882** is the year in which the [[Billiton Maatschappij]] concluded a *suppletoire overeenkomst* — supplementary agreement — with the governor-general [[s'Jacob|F. s'Jacob]] extending its tin concession on [[Billiton]] (Banka-Billiton) for thirty-five years, in violation of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Mijnreglement]] of [[1850]] as amended in [[1873]] (which required public competition for any extension as a rule, with royal authorisation as the exception). The affair provides the immediate background to the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] debates that, after [[1899]], would reach into the [[Gajoland]] and [[Pedirse|Pedir]] oilfields.

### The Billiton extension

The [[Billiton Maatschappij]] had obtained its original 40-year concession in [[1852]] under the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Mijnreglement]] of [[1850]]. In [[1873]] the regulation had been tightened: extensions now required, as a rule, open public competition; royal authorisation was reserved as an exception. In [[1882]] the [[Billiton Maatschappij]] and the governor-general [[s'Jacob|s'Jacob]] nevertheless signed a supplementary agreement extending the concession by 35 years without public competition — a procedure later cited in the [[Tweede Kamer]] as the precedent the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] of [[1899]] was meant to prevent.

### Connection with Atjeh

The [[1882]] Billiton precedent is the immediate background to the [[Mijnwet]] debates of the late 1890s and early 1900s in which [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]]'s mining law was hammered out. Those debates reached directly into Atjeh: when, after the [[Mijnwet]] was enacted, [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — then governor of Atjeh — reported that the [[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij]] was letting the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]] work its Atjeh fields for ƒ 2.50 per ton, the resulting controversy led in [[1910]] to the insertion of a new Article 5a into the [[Mijnwet]] requiring legislative approval of government exploitation contracts — including, by clear implication, future Atjeh oil contracts.

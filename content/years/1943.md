**1943** was, in the context of the [[Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies]], a pivotal year for [[Atjèh|Aceh]] in which the Japanese administration consolidated its control, instituted major institutional innovations such as the [[M-organisatie|M‑organisatie]] under [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]], installed the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] on [[10 March]] [[1943]] as the official Islamic council for the welfare of [[Asia Timoer Raja|Greater East Asia]], and created the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]], the [[people's army|people's army]] and a welfare office. The year also saw intensified resource requisition, the first large‑scale [[kempeitai]] purges, and the final suppression of the Dutch underground, exemplified by the capture and execution of [[Lieutenant Van Zanten|Lieutenant Van Zanten]]. Allied intelligence highlighted Aceh’s strategic ports, while local resistance networks began to coalesce, laying groundwork for post‑war nationalist movements.

## Consolidation of Japanese administration

The Japanese military reorganised its civil authority in Aceh through the [[M-organisatie|M‑organisatie]], which reached its final form under the direction of [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]]. On [[10 March]] [[1943]] the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]]—*Madjelis agama Islam oentoek bantoean kemakmoeran Asia Timoer Raja di Atjèh*—was installed. Resident lino delivered the inauguration speech, declaring that the council would “provide support — to the extent that it does not conflict with the Islamic religion — in the promotion of the welfare of Greater East Asia.” The council’s leadership was composed of:

- Chairman: [[Teukoe Abdoel Azis|Teukoe Abdoel Azis]]
- First deputy chairman: [[Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi|Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi]]
- Second deputy chairman: [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu'éh]]
- Secretary: [[Teungkoe Moehamad Joenoes Djamil|Teungkoe Moehamad Joenoes Djamil]]
- Representative of the [[Muhammadijah|Muhammadijah]]: [[Teukoe Djohan Meura'sa|Teukoe Djohan Meura'sa]]
- Representative of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] movement: [[Teukoe Moehamad Amin|Teukoe Moehamad Amin]]

The same day, [[Lieutenant Van Zanten|Lieutenant Van Zanten]] surrendered after a year‑long guerrilla campaign in the Atjèhnese highlands, “almost a year after the official surrender” of [[28 March]] [[1942]].

## First changes among the *gun‑chó*s

During the first half of [[1943]] the Japanese administration reshuffled the local *gun‑chó* posts:

- In [[February]] [[1943]] [[Teukoe Nja' Banta|Teukoe Nja' Banta]] of [[Lhóng|Lhóng]] was appointed *gun‑chó* of [[Simaloer|Simaloer]].
- In [[May]] [[1943]] [[Teukoe Nja' Arif|Teukoe Nja' Arif]] was relieved of his duties and succeeded as *controleur* of [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] by his brother‑in‑law [[Teukoe Ali Keureukón|Teukoe Ali Keureukón]].

The first list of “F‑people” (collaborators) was published in the *[[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]]* between [[15 December]] and [[25 December]] [[1943]]. On [[8 December]] [[1943]] the ceremony of "*soerat poedjian*" (certificates of distinction) was held at [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] for those distinguished in the resistance; [[Teukoe Sabi|Teukoe Sabi]] of [[Lageuën|Lageuën]] featured prominently.

## Formation of the people’s army and the Atjèh Council

[[November]] [[1943]] brought three major measures:

1. The establishment of the [[people's army|people's army]] (*giyúgun*) on [[22 November]] [[1943]].
2. The creation of the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]].
3. The inauguration of a welfare office.

On [[9 November]] [[1943]] Resident lino addressed a thousand‑strong crowd at the great mosque in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]], stating that the people’s army would handle “light work” while the Japanese army would undertake “heavy work,” and that it would “ensure the defense of the fatherland Sumatra” when the Japanese forces advanced to London and Washington. The first session of the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]] convened on [[8 December]]–[[9 December]] [[1943]].

## Kempeitai purges and the suppression of Dutch resistance

The year also witnessed the first large‑scale [[kempeitai]] operations in Aceh:

- On [[16 May]] [[1943]] the leaders of the [[Ten Velde‑Woudenberg organisation|Ten Velde‑Woudenberg organisation]] were arrested.
- On [[20 September]] [[1943]] a sweeping raid across Sumatra “led to the elimination of most remaining resistance nuclei.”
- The execution of protest leaders [[H. Wolff|Wolff]], [[M. J. Croes|Croes]], [[K. Stoltz|Stoltz]] and [[H. F. Voss|Voss]] at [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]] on [[28 May]] [[1943]]—with Voss’s last words “Long live the Queen”—marked the crushing of the [[Lawé Sigalagala|Lawé Sigalagala]] colonists’ protest against enlistment in the *heiho*.

Parallel to these actions, the Dutch underground was finally extinguished. The staff history in *Nederlands‑Indië contra Japan* records that Lieutenant Van Zanten’s group, after a series of arrests in November [[1942]] and a failed Japanese assault on his bivouac on [[26 November]] [[1942]], was captured on [[10 March]] [[1943]]. Van Zanten and his five Maréchaussees were subjected to brutal interrogation, refused to become Japanese informants, and were sentenced to death by a court‑martial at [[Fort de Rock|Fort de Rock]]. The exact date and place of execution are not recorded in the staff history, but the episode closed the Dutch post‑capitulation resistance in Aceh.

## Colonial conditions and resource exploitation

Beyond institutional changes, Japanese authorities in Aceh intensified the requisition of rice and other commodities to support the broader war effort in the Pacific. These exploitative policies strained local economies and heightened popular discontent, feeding the clandestine networks that would later fuel post‑war nationalist movements.

## Allied perspective

Allied intelligence reports from [[1943]] emphasized Aceh’s strategic ports as crucial for controlling maritime traffic through the Strait of Malacca. This assessment informed later liberation planning and underscored the province’s importance within the larger theater of the [[Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies]].

## Significance

In Piekaar’s assessment, [[1943]] was the year the Japanese occupation of Aceh attained its definitive shape: the [[M-organisatie|M‑organisatie]] was consolidated, the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] institutionalised the ascendancy of the [[Poesa|Poesa]]‑*oelama*, and the [[kempeitai]] eradicated the last vestiges of Dutch underground activity. Simultaneously, the year marked a turning point in resource exploitation, resistance organization, and Allied strategic awareness, setting the stage for the final phases of the occupation and the post‑war nationalist awakening.

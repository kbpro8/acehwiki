The year **1841** is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the year of the [[J. C. Baud|Baud reversal]] — the dispatch by [[J. C. Baud|Minister J. C. Baud]] of the letter that ordered the withdrawal of the East Coast posts established in [[1838]]–[[1840]] under the [[Johannes van den Bosch|van den Bosch]]-[[Merkus|Merkus]] policy of Sumatra-wide expansion. The reversal — by which the colonial state abandoned the project of "onderwerping van geheel Sumatra (buiten Atjeh)" — "the subjection of the whole of Sumatra (outside Atjeh)" — was, in the Bureau's framing, the most consequential single decision in the nineteenth-century history of the Dutch East Coast state.

## Events

- The [[J. C. Baud|Baud]] letter of [[1841]] ordering the withdrawal of the East Coast posts. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau records: "Reeds in [[1841]] bracht deze staatsman ter kennisse van de Indische Regeering, dat, hoewel zich geheel vereenigende met de beginselen, welke de aangenomen gedragslijn ten aanzien van Sumatra beheerschten, het tempo van uitvoering der daarvoor noodige maatregelen hem te snel voorkwam. De Minister ging echter verder. Hij gelastte intrekking van de reeds ter Oostkust opgerichte posten."
- The reversal of the [[Johannes van den Bosch|van den Bosch]]-[[Merkus|Merkus]] Sumatra policy. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau records: "Het schrijven van [[1841]] had, in tegenstelling met de actieve politiek door Van den Bosch althans voor Sumatra voorgestaan, ons weder die der algeheele onthouding gebracht."

## Significance

The year [[1841]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the chronological hinge between the [[Johannes van den Bosch|van den Bosch]]-[[Merkus|Merkus]] policy of Sumatra-wide expansion (which had reached its high-water mark in [[1840]] with the [[singkel-post-reaching-atjeh-border-(1840)|establishment of the Singkel post on the Atjeh border]]) and the long period of Dutch "onthouding" (abstention) that would, in turn, be abandoned only in the late 1850s with the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|1858 Siak tractate]].

The year [[1841]] is also, in the Bureau's framing, the chronological anchor of the long debate about the causes of the Dutch withdrawal from the East Coast posts. The Bureau records the divergent explanations: the [[van Goltstein|van Goltstein]] interpellation in the Second Chamber in [[1847]], which attributed the withdrawal to British pressure; the [[J. C. Baud|Baud]] response, which attributed it to the completion of the [[Padri War|Padri war]] and the consequent obsolescence of the posts; the 1853 article in the *Tijdschrift voor Nederlandsch Indië*, which exposed the contradiction between the [[J. C. Baud|Baud]] response and the secret instruction of the Indragiri commission; and the [[E. B. Kielstra|Kielstra]] judgement, which attributed the withdrawal to the influence of [[de Stuers|Generaal de Stuers]]'s warning against overhasty Sumatra expansion.

The year [[1841]] is, finally, the chronological prelude to the [[J. C. Baud|Baud]] interpellation of [[1847]] and the [[van Hoevell|van Hoevell]] interpellation of [[1857]], by which the East Coast posts withdrawal was repeatedly debated in the States-General. The year [[1841]] thus opens the long public controversy over the Dutch East Coast policy that would, in turn, prepare the ground for the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|1858 Siak tractate]] and the resumption of Dutch expansion on the East Coast.

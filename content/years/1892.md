## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1892** is the year of two events bearing on Atjehnese and Indies history: the enactment of the [[Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap ([[1892]])|Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap]] of [[12 December]] [[1892]] (S. 268), which inadvertently reduced the indigenous inhabitants of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipel — including the Atjehnese — to the legal status of foreigners in their own land; and the contract between the [[Billiton Maatschappij]] and the Indies government that would later serve as the reference point for Atjeh-oil concession policy.

### The Netherlands Nationality Act

By incorporating, without fully thought-through consequences, a clause modelled on a German pattern, the [[Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap ([[1892]])|Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap]] of [[12 December]] [[1892]] made the *inlanders* — indigenous inhabitants — into *vreemdelingen* (foreigners) in the Indies. The mistake was acknowledged and was one of the matters that the post-[[1901]] [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] governments sought to repair through juridical *gelijkstelling* (equalisation) of the indigenous population. The nationalities question bore on every inhabitant of the archipelipel, including the population of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] once the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] had brought it under Dutch rule.

### The 1892 Billiton contract

In [[1892]] the [[Billiton Maatschappij]] and the Indies government concluded a contract setting the cijns (royalty) on tin extraction at a level higher than the 2 % of gross yield that [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] would later propose in his [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]]. The contrast is repeatedly cited by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] as the yardstick against which the generosity (or parsimony) of subsequent Atjeh oil concessions — including the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]]/[[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak]] arrangement exposed by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — was measured.

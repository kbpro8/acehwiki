## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].

**1892** is the year of two events bearing on Atjehnese and Indies history: the enactment of the [[Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap ([[1892]])|Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap]] of [[12 December]] [[1892]] (S. 268), which inadvertently reduced the indigenous inhabitants of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipel — including the Atjehnese — to the legal status of foreigners in their own land; and the contract between the [[Billiton Maatschappij]] and the Indies government that would later serve as the reference point for Atjeh-oil concession policy.

### The Netherlands Nationality Act

By incorporating, without fully thought-through consequences, a clause modelled on a German pattern, the [[Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap ([[1892]])|Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap]] of [[12 December]] [[1892]] made the *inlanders* — indigenous inhabitants — into *vreemdelingen* (foreigners) in the Indies. The mistake was acknowledged and was one of the matters that the post-[[1901]] [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] governments sought to repair through juridical *gelijkstelling* (equalisation) of the indigenous population. The nationalities question bore on every inhabitant of the archipelipel, including the population of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] once the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] had brought it under Dutch rule.

### The 1892 Billiton contract

In [[1892]] the [[Billiton Maatschappij]] and the Indies government concluded a contract setting the cijns (royalty) on tin extraction at a level higher than the 2 % of gross yield that [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] would later propose in his [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]]. The contrast is repeatedly cited by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] as the yardstick against which the generosity (or parsimony) of subsequent Atjeh oil concessions — including the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]]/[[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak]] arrangement exposed by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — was measured.

## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].

**1892** is the year in which the [[Netherlands|Dutch government]] attempted a blockade of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — an attempt that "niets uitrichtte omdat Atjeh heel weinig van buiten nodig had" (accomplished nothing because Atjeh had very little need of anything from outside). The same year is the date of the advice of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] that gave the method for the subjugation and pacificatie of Atjeh. The two episodes together mark the nadir of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentratie]] period and the moment at which the intellectual basis for the post-[[1896]] conquest was laid.

### The 1892 blockade

The [[1892]] blockade of Atjeh — the [[Netherlands|Dutch government]]'s attempt to bring the sultanate to submission by commercial isolation — produced, the source records, "niets" (nothing). The reason given is structural: "Atjeh heel weinig van buiten nodig had" (Atjeh had very little need of anything from outside). The sultanate's economic autarky made the standard Dutch coercive instrument of the nineteenth century — the maritime blockade — inapplicable.

### The 1892 Snouck Hurgronje advice

The same year [[1892]] saw the advice of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] that, in the source's narrative, supplied the "methode aangaf tot onderwerping en pacificatie" (method for subjugation and pacification). [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s advice had three components: (i) ignore the "sultanspartij" (sultan's party) at the court as of no significance; (ii) do not negotiate with the enemy but strike him sensibly, "daar de Atjeher zijn overwinnaarswijsheid niet zou afleggen zonder gevoelig geslagen te zijn" (since the Atjehnese would not lay down his pride of victory without being sensibly struck); and (iii) thereafter seek to win the confidence of the population by a good welfare policy. The advice was based on [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]]'s "in zeer korte tijd volbrachte studie van land en volk van Atjeh" (study of the land and people of Atjeh completed in a very short time).

### The Snouck analysis of Atjehnese society

The source preserves [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]]'s analysis of Atjehnese social structure. The Atjehnese live in *kampongs* (villages); a group of *kampongs* sharing one mosque forms a *mukim*; three or more *mukim*'s form a *landschap* (territory) under an *uléebalang*. The hereditary *uléebalangs* were "de eigenlijke heren des lands" (the actual lords of the land). Several *uléebalang* territories form a federation, the *sagi*, under the leadership of the most prominent, influential or energetic *uléebalang*, called the *panglima sagi*. In [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] (so named in contrast to the *onderhorigheden* such as [[Pidië]], the [[Gajoland|Gaju-]] and [[Alasland|Alaslanden]]) there were three *sagi*'s. The sultan's authority over the *panglima's sagi* and the *uléebalangs* existed "alleen in naam" (in name only).

### The Dutch misreading

The source's verdict on Dutch policy before [[1892]] is direct: "Onze politiek werd veel te veel beheerst door de geheel verkeerde voorstelling, die wij ons van de betekenis en de macht van de Sultan maakten" (Our policy was far too much dominated by the wholly wrong conception we had of the significance and power of the Sultan). The [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] advice of [[1892]] supplied the corrective.

### Significance

[[1892]] is the year in which the intellectual basis of the post-[[1896]] Dutch conquest of Atjeh was laid. The failure of the blockade confirmed that coercion-by-isolation was inapplicable; the [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] advice supplied the alternative: ignore the sultan, strike the *uléebalangs* into submission, then win the population by welfare policy.

## The struggle for Kaloet (August-October 1892)

The year [[1892]] was marked by intensified military operations around the Atjehnese fortification of [[Kaloet|Kaloet]] in the [[XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] — operations documented in detail in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]]'s chapter *De Strijd om Kaloet* ("The Struggle for Kaloet").

### Night-patrol of Lieutenant Brant (22-23 August 1892)

On the night of [[22 August|22-23 August]] [[1892]], Lieutenant [[Brant|Brant]] of the *maréchaussée* lay in ambush with a detachment of *maréchaussées* in the dry bed of the [[Kroeng Kali|Kroeng Kali]] opposite Kaloet, with the intention of catching the Atjehnese garrison as they came in the morning. The Atjehnese, however, *versliepen zich* (overslept), and the patrol had to return to [[Lamreng|Lamreng]] at 7 a.m. without having caught anyone.

### Reconnaissance of 23 August 1892

Immediately after the return of the Brant patrol, a *verkenningcolonne* (reconnaissance column) under Captain [[Jhr. Graafland|Jhr. Graafland]] of the *Militaire Willems-Orde*, with Lieutenant [[Brant|Brant]] as guide, set out from [[Lamreng|Lamreng]] up the [[Kroeng Tandjong|Kroeng Tandjong]] and destroyed the Atjehnese fortifications in the river before approaching to within 50 metres of Kaloet's *vóórwal* (front wall) and withdrawing. This [[Reconnaissance of Kaloet (23 August 1892)|reconnaissance of 23 August 1892]] was the first Dutch operation against Kaloet.

### Failed assault of 1 October 1892

On [[1 October]] [[1892]] a strong column from [[Lambaroe|Lambaroe]] under a captain and three lieutenants, supported by 75 men from [[Lampermej|Lampermej]] and 50 men of the 14th Battalion, assaulted Kaloet from the north-east and was repulsed with 7 killed and more than 30 wounded, including Lieutenant [[Hoolboom|Hoolboom]] (mortally wounded) and Lieutenant [[Drijber|Drijber]]. The failure was followed by General [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]]'s refusal to risk further lives in a renewed assault — an act of moral courage that the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] characterises as *misschien meer moed noodig gehad, dan voor het toestaan* ("perhaps more courage was necessary than for the granting").

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records that in [[1892]] [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] submitted his *Verslag over de politiek-religieuze toestanden in Atjeh* — the report that would shape Dutch policy for the following decade. Also in [[1892]] came three governor's letters — dated [[10 September]], [[26 October]], and [[21 December]] [[1892]] — forbidding the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussée]] from *offensief optreden in het voorterrein door hinderlagen of patrouilles van Uw korps*. [[Tengkoe Koetakarang]]'s *Tadkirat arrakidin* — the *Vermaning der stilzittenden* — was edited by Snouck Hurgronje in the same year.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

**[[1892]]** was the year in which [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]] completed his stay in Atjeh (February) and [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] published *[[The Subjection of Atjeh (1892)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*; the year in which the conciliatory governorship of [[C. Deijckerhoff|Deijckerhoff]] began, favoured by the army's preparations against [[Lombok]]. Evacuations from the *[[geconcentreerde linie]]* numbered 3,171 men. On [[7 April]] [[1892]] [[H. N. A. Swart]] was promoted *1ste Luitenant*.

**The year 1587** was the year of the peace treaty between [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the Portuguese at [[Malaka|Malaka]], by which the wingewest of [[Aru|Aru]] was transferred from Atjeh to [[Djohor|Djohor]] and a generation of relative peace opened in the western Malay-Indonesian archipelago. The treaty defined the strategic framework of the Atjeh-Djohor rivalry that would frame Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]] and was the proximate cause of the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s subsequent pursuit of European alliances against Djohor.

## The Atjeh-Portuguese peace

The [[1587]] peace between Atjeh and the Portuguese concluded a generation of Achehnese attacks on Malaka — the great Achehnese siege of [[1 February|1-17 February]] [[1575]] had nearly succeeded, and the Portuguese had survived only by what they considered a miracle. The peace transferred the wingewest of [[Aru|Aru]] — the north-east Sumatran coast, in the region of modern Deli — from Atjeh to Djohor. Van der Woude notes that "de vijandschap tusschen Atjeh, een van de machtigste Staten op Sumatra, en Djohor, dat bij den vrede tusschen de Portugeezen en Atjeh in 1587 het wingewest Aru had ontvangen, was niet geluwd". The sultan of Atjeh, in [[1598]], sent envoys to [[Goa|Goa]] asking Portuguese help against Djohor; the Portuguese received the envoys with honours at Malaka in May [[1599]] and at Goa, but the help was not forthcoming.

## The strategic framework

The [[1587]] treaty defined the strategic framework of the Atjeh-Djohor rivalry that would frame the early Dutch-Indies period. The sultan of Atjeh's hostility to Djohor — and his parallel hostility to the Portuguese — defined his European policy: he played the Dutch, English, French and Portuguese against each other, seeking to recover Aru and to dominate the pepper trade of the Strait of Malaka. The [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' visit of 1599]] — the event that opens Van der Woude's Atjeh chapter — was the first Dutch intrusion into this strategic framework, and the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]]'s reception of the Dutch, English and French in the years [[1599]]-[[1602]] was the working-out of his post-[[1587]] European policy.

## Significance

The year [[1587]] is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the year of the Atjeh-Portuguese peace that defined the strategic framework of the Atjeh-Djohor rivalry and the proximate cause of the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s subsequent pursuit of European alliances against Djohor. The treaty's transfer of [[Aru|Aru]] from Atjeh to [[Djohor|Djohor]] defined the strategic framework within which the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]]'s European policy would unfold, and the Atjeh-Djohor rivalry that the treaty inaugurated was the principal Malay-Malay strategic fact of the early-modern period. The [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack on Djohor of 1613]] — the closing military episode of Van der Woude's Atjeh chapter — was the principal military expression of the rivalry that the [[1587]] treaty had defined.

**[[1934]]** was the year of the retirement of [[Dominee Thenu]] from the post he had held since [[1894]] — the end of his "veertig Atjeh-jaren" — and of his reis naar Nederland, where he was received in audience by [[Koningin Wilhelmina]]. It is also the year in which the author's patrol could still read, at [[Leuhong]], the note a soldier had written on [[22 August]] [[1912]] about the 1918 opheffing of that *detailchement* — twenty-two years later, on *[[bekas]]* Marechaussee.

## The retirement of Dominee Thenu

"Zijn veertig Atjeh-jaren waren in [[1934]] om." [[Dominee Thenu]] — who had come to [[Atjeh|Aceh]] in [[1894]] and served for forty full years as the *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* and universally honoured *Soldaten-Dominee* of the [[K.N.I.L.]] troops — became too old in [[1934]] and had to hand over his taak. He was sent on a reis naar Nederland and was received by [[Koningin Wilhelmina]].

## The audience with the Queen

The audience with [[Koningin Wilhelmina]] was the political honour accorded to the *Soldaten-Dominee* at his retirement. "Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt" — for a long time he refused to wash the hand the Queen had shaken. For more than ten days no one else — until [[Generaal Swart]] — was permitted to shake that hand.

## The Leuhong note

"in [[1934]]" the author's patrol could still read, at [[Leuhong]], the note a soldier had written on [[22 August]] [[1912]] about the 1918 opheffing of that *detailchement* — twenty-two years later, on *[[bekas]]* Marechaussee. The *bekas* of the colonne — marked by the brigade number cut in trees — was the colonial army's own historical record: the marked trees and the soldier's notes were the *bekas* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]'s presence in the *[[rimboe]]*, readable years later.

## The broader colonial context

[[1934]] was also the year of the *Legercommandant]]'s congratulation of the retired Javanese fusilier on 30 years Ridder M.W.O. at [[Ambarawa]] — actually [[1938]], but the episode is mentioned in the same chapter. The broader colonial context of [[1934]] is the late-colonial peace: the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] was politically over since [[1918]]; the [[Celebes]] pacifications were long concluded; the colonial army was at the height of its inter-war strength. The retirement of Thenu in [[1934]] is therefore the book's marker of the end of an era: the *Soldaten-Dominee* who had served the colonial army since [[1894]] was gone, and the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] he had served was, in [[1934]], an army of peace, not of war.

## Significance

[[1934]] is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the year of the retirement of [[Dominee Thenu]] and of his audience with [[Koningin Wilhelmina]]. The book's argument is that Thenu's retirement — and the honour the Queen paid him — was the political recognition of the geestelijk gezag that had held the colonial army together for forty years. The year [[1934]] is therefore the book's marker of the end of the Thenu era: the *Soldaten-Dominee* was gone, and the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] he had served would, in [[1937]], bury him with four M.W.O.-ridders as *slippendragers*.

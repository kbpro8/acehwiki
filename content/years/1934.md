## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1934

### The retirement of Dominee Thenu

"Zijn veertig Atjeh-jaren waren in [[1934]] om." [[Dominee Thenu]] — who had come to [[Atjeh|Aceh]] in [[1894]] and served for forty full years as the *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* and universally honoured *Soldaten-Dominee* of the [[K.N.I.L.]] troops — became too old in [[1934]] and had to hand over his taak. He was sent on a reis naar Nederland and was received by [[Queen Wilhelmina|Koningin Wilhelmina]].

### The audience with the Queen

The audience with [[Queen Wilhelmina|Koningin Wilhelmina]] was the political honour accorded to the *Soldaten-Dominee* at his retirement. "Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt" — for a long time he refused to wash the hand the Queen had shaken. For more than ten days no one else — until [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]] — was permitted to shake that hand.

### The Leuhong note

"in [[1934]]" the author's patrol could still read, at [[Leuhong]], the note a soldier had written on [[22 August]] [[1912]] about the 1918 opheffing of that *detailchement* — twenty-two years later, on *[[bekas]]* Marechaussee. The *bekas* of the colonne — marked by the brigade number cut in trees — was the colonial army's own historical record: the marked trees and the soldier's notes were the *bekas* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]'s presence in the *[[rimboe]]*, readable years later.

### The broader colonial context

[[1934]] was also the year of the *Legercommandant*'s congratulation of the retired Javanese fusilier on 30 years Ridder M.W.O. at [[Ambarawa]] — actually [[1938]], but the episode is mentioned in the same chapter. The broader colonial context of [[1934]] is the late-colonial peace: the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] was politically over since [[1918]]; the [[Celebes]] pacifications were long concluded; the colonial army was at the height of its inter-war strength. The retirement of Thenu in [[1934]] is therefore the book's marker of the end of an era: the *Soldaten-Dominee* who had served the colonial army since [[1894]] was gone, and the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] he had served was, in [[1934]], an army of peace, not of war.

## Account from *Vierhonderd jaren missie* (1934) — the publication of the jubilee volume

The year [[1934]] saw the publication at [[Batavia|Batavia]] of the [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|*Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch-Indië, 1593–1934*]] — the commemorative jubilee volume of the four-hundredth anniversary of the establishment of the first permanent Catholic mission station in the archipelago at [[Ternate|Ternate]] in [[1593]]. The volume, compiled by the *Algemeen Comité van Voorbereiding tot de Feestelijke Herdenking van 400 jaar Missie in Ned.-Indië*, records the [[Catholic Mission of Koeta Radja-Atjeh|Catholic mission of Koeta Radja-Atjeh]] — including the account of the [[Zusters van Schijndel|Zusters van Schijndel]] schools, the Catholic population of "*ver over de 1000*" ("well over a thousand"), and the regular service of [[Sabang|Sabang]] from the Koeta Radja station. The volume is the principal documentary source for the Catholic institutional presence in Atjeh in the late colonial period.

## Significance

[[1934]] is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the year of the retirement of [[Dominee Thenu]] and of his audience with [[Queen Wilhelmina|Koningin Wilhelmina]]. The book's argument is that Thenu's retirement — and the honour the Queen paid him — was the political recognition of the geestelijk gezag that had held the colonial army together for forty years. The year [[1934]] is therefore the book's marker of the end of the Thenu era: the *Soldaten-Dominee* was gone, and the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] he had served would, in [[1937]], bury him with four M.W.O.-ridders as *slippendragers*. The publication of the *Vierhonderd jaren missie* in the same year marks, in the parallel Catholic institutional record, the quadricentennial consolidation of the mission history that had begun at Ternate in [[1593]] and that, by [[1934]], had extended to the Koeta Radja-Atjeh station, the Sabang service, and the schools of the Zusters van Schijndel.

## Account from *Een strijd in Atjeh* ([[1934]])

For the medical history of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], **[[1934]]** was the year in which the [[Vereeniging SIMAVI]] published at [[Rotterdam]] the pamphlet *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]*, reprinting the three letters written for the *[[Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant]]* by [[C. Graadt van Roggen]] on the eye-surgery campaigns of [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] in the *[[Kampong|kampongs]]* of Atjeh.

The foreword by [[H. Wanting]], editor of the *Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant*, is dated September [[1934]]. It records that Graadt van Roggen had died within half a year of the shipboard meeting of February [[1933]] on which the letters were based, and that the pamphlet was published in part as an act of *piëteit* towards him.

The pamphlet records that in that year SIMAVI expended f 26,500 on medical assistance to the indigenous population of the Netherlands East and West Indies, of which f 4,400 was for medicines and dressings alone. It also quotes Tijssen's statement in *[[Medische Nood]]*, [[1934]], no. 1: "Ik ken de zendingsziekenhuizen. Ik wenschte, dat er in Ned. Indië in plaats van 33 zendingsartsen 330 waren! Zijn er 20 zendingshospitalen, ik wenschte, dat \u201eSimavi\u201d het geld bij elkaar kreeg om het tot 200 te brengen! Maar ook dan zouden er menschen noodig zijn, die naast het mooie werk in de hospitalen het vuile werk in de kampong willen doen." — I know the mission hospitals. I could wish that in the Netherlands Indies, instead of 33 mission physicians, there were 330! Are there 20 mission hospitals — I could wish that „Simavi” got the money together to bring it to 200!

The figures cited — thirty-three mission physicians and twenty mission hospitals for the whole of the Netherlands Indies — are among the few statistics of Protestant medical provision in the archipelago given for that year, and form the numerical background to the doctrine set out at [[Kampong medicine in Atjeh]].

Figures, places, and events in Acehnese history associated with the year 1904.

## People

- [[Governor Van Heutsz]]

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1904

**[[1904]]** was the year of the *zegetocht* — the victorious campaign — of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]]'s colonne through the [[Gajoland|Gajoland]] and [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], which brought the last great interior strongholds of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] "ineens geheel onder onze macht" — at once entirely under Dutch power — and so marked the military completion of the subjugation of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* treats [[1904]] as a turning point second in importance only to [[1898]], the year of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]]'s accession to the governorship.

## The Gayo-Alas campaign

Before [[1904]], *De Buitenbezittingen* records, the Dutch "wisten goed als niets" — knew effectively nothing — of the inhabitants of the Alas valley. The expedition van Daalen led in June [[1904]] brought the entire region under Dutch control, though not without subsequent resistance, particularly in the early years, before a *toestand van orde en rust* was established. The same campaign traversed the Gayo lands, where van Daalen installed the minor [[Kédjoerön Bédén|Kédjoerön Bédén]] — a child taken from Tamiang by his father, a previously deposed Kédjoerön, and paraded through [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] to sign the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] without the required consultation of the other *rédjë's* of [[Gajoland|Gayo Loeës]] — as the legitimate territorial chief of the Gayo Loeës.

## The Alas settlement

In the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], the colonne encountered the *roi fainéant* [[Radja Tjèq of Alas|Radja Tjèq]] — the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]]-recognised overlord of the valley — in a paradoxical position. The Dutch, "onbekend met de werkelijke verhoudingen" — unfamiliar with the actual relations — initially treated the two *Kédjoerön*s of [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]] as wholly independent *landschapshoofden* and the Radja Tjèq as a mere councillor. The political consequences of this *misgreep* — this misstep — were, however, not damaging; the Radja Tjèq remained loyal, and a subsequent reorganisation regularised his position. The Alaslanden would, in the years that followed, prove more amenable to territorial reorganisation than the Gayo lands, the *instelling der Si-émpats* having already carried the territorial principle further there than in the more strictly genealogical Gayo polities.

## Toponymic legacy

The *van Daalengebergte* — the mountain range of the central Barisan that forms the eastern wall of the Gayo high plateau, separating the basin of the upper [[Djamboe Aje]] from the high plateau of [[Serbëdjadi|Serbëdjadi]] — commemorates van Daalen's [[1904]] campaign in the physical geography of northern Sumatra.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1904

**[[1904]]** is the opening year of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade surveyed by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau in its volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914* — the decade that the Bureau frames as the "period, so important for those territories," during which the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] was completed and the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch "effective authority" across the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] was carried out. The Bureau's statistical tables for the Atjeh-tram begin in [[1904]]: in that year the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] had a route length of 322 K.M. and an aanlegkapitaal (construction capital) of f 9,000,000, with 1,434,000 passengers and 110,000 tons of freight carried.

The Bureau's account of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] also identifies [[1904]] as the starting year of the [[Berglijntje Keude Breue–Seulimeum|berglijntje from Keude Breue over the hills to Seulimeum]] — the line built in the years [[1904]]–[[1908]] to connect the second tramway of the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]] (whose western branch terminated at [[Keude Breue]]) with the old tramway of [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] at [[Seulimeum]]. The Bureau notes that "[[1904|the years [[1904]]]]–[[1908]]" saw the construction of this berglijntje — the engineering keystone of the integrated Atjeh-tram network and the principal material embodiment of the post-[[1896]] [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]].

The Bureau's account of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] likewise identifies [[1904]] as the starting point of the post-pacification financial consolidation: "[[1904|before [[1904]]]] the institution of the landschapskassen was only sporadically found in the zelfbesturende landschappen of the Buitenbezittingen, a consequence of the little involvement that we had until that time with the internal administration of those realms."

[[1904]] is also the year in which [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — already Military Governor of Atjeh since 1898 — became [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië|Governor-General of the Netherlands East Indies]]. His ascent to the Governor-Generalship in [[1904]] marks the translation of the Atjeh military command into the colonial-wide policy of "effective authority" that the Bureau credits with the "enormous progress" of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade.

## Significance

[[1904]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the opening year of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" of the Buitenbezittingen — the decade during which the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] was completed, the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was extended from 322 to 465 K.M., the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] were centralised, and the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] regime was codified by the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]]. The year [[1904]] thus marks the beginning of the principal Dutch colonial project of the early twentieth century.

## Economic Baseline (from *De Buitenbezittingen*, 1915)

**[[1904]]** also serves as the benchmark year for the colonial statistical overview in the source text *De Buitenbezittingen*, providing a baseline against which dramatic changes over the following decade were measured. The data from [[1904]] captures the region on the cusp of profound economic transformation.

- **Economic Baseline:** Atjeh's economy in [[1904]] was still heavily reliant on traditional exports of [[pepper]] and other local commodities. The post-war plantation boom in [[rubber]] and other crops had not yet begun.
- **Early Concessions:** In [[1904]], only **6 concessions** existed in all of [[Atjeh and Dependencies]], covering about 1,000 *bouws* — contrasting starkly with the 148 concessions covering nearly 300,000 *bouws* by [[1913]]. [[1904]] was the quiet before the storm.
- **No Opium Regie:** The exploitative *pacht* system for opium was still in place. The reformist *regie* would not be introduced until [[1913]].
- **Shipping:** [[Sabang]], the deep-water port on [[Weh Island]], had already begun to grow in importance, with 588 ships and nearly 1.9 million M³ of tonnage calling at its port.
- **Labor Force:** In [[1904]], the number of Javanese contract laborers in Atjeh was 3,341, already representing a significant demographic shift.

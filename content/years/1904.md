Figures, places, and events in Acehnese history associated with the year 1904.

## People

- [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Governor Van Heutsz]]

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1904

**[[1904]]** was the year of the *zegetocht* — the victorious campaign — of [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen]]'s colonne through the [[Gajoland|Gajoland]] and [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], which brought the last great interior strongholds of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] "ineens geheel onder onze macht" — at once entirely under Dutch power — and so marked the military completion of the subjugation of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* treats [[1904]] as a turning point second in importance only to [[1898]], the year of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]]'s accession to the governorship.
**[[1904]]** is the opening year of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade surveyed by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau in its volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914* — the decade that the Bureau frames as the "period, so important for those territories," during which the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] was completed and the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch "effective authority" across the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] was carried out. The Bureau's statistical tables for the Atjeh-tram begin in [[1904]]: in that year the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] had a route length of 322 K.M. and an aanlegkapitaal (construction capital) of f 9,000,000, with 1,434,000 passengers and 110,000 tons of freight carried.

## The Gayo-Alas campaign

Before [[1904]], *De Buitenbezittingen* records, the Dutch "wisten goed als niets" — knew effectively nothing — of the inhabitants of the Alas valley. The expedition van Daalen led in June [[1904]] brought the entire region under Dutch control, though not without subsequent resistance, particularly in the early years, before a *toestand van orde en rust* was established. The same campaign traversed the Gayo lands, where van Daalen installed the minor [[Kédjoerön Bédén|Kédjoerön Bédén]] — a child taken from Tamiang by his father, a previously deposed Kédjoerön, and paraded through [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] to sign the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] without the required consultation of the other *rédjë's* of [[Gajoland|Gayo Loeës]] — as the legitimate territorial chief of the Gayo Loeës.

## The Alas settlement

In the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], the colonne encountered the *roi fainéant* [[Radja Tjèq of Alas|Radja Tjèq]] — the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]]-recognised overlord of the valley — in a paradoxical position. The Dutch, "onbekend met de werkelijke verhoudingen" — unfamiliar with the actual relations — initially treated the two *Kédjoerön*s of [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]] as wholly independent *landschapshoofden* and the Radja Tjèq as a mere councillor. The political consequences of this *misgreep* — this misstep — were, however, not damaging; the Radja Tjèq remained loyal, and a subsequent reorganisation regularised his position. The Alaslanden would, in the years that followed, prove more amenable to territorial reorganisation than the Gayo lands, the *instelling der Si-émpats* having already carried the territorial principle further there than in the more strictly genealogical Gayo polities.

## Toponymic legacy

The *van Daalengebergte* — the mountain range of the central Barisan that forms the eastern wall of the Gayo high plateau, separating the basin of the upper [[Djamboe Aje]] from the high plateau of [[Serbëdjadi|Serbëdjadi]] — commemorates van Daalen's [[1904]] campaign in the physical geography of northern Sumatra.

## Significance

[[1904]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the opening year of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" of the Buitenbezittingen — the decade during which the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] was completed, the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was extended from 322 to 465 K.M., the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] were centralised, and the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] regime was codified by the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]]. The year [[1904]] thus marks the beginning of the principal Dutch colonial project of the early twentieth century.

## Economic Baseline (from *De Buitenbezittingen*, 1915)

**[[1904]]** also serves as the benchmark year for the colonial statistical overview in the source text *De Buitenbezittingen*, providing a baseline against which dramatic changes over the following decade were measured. The data from [[1904]] captures the region on the cusp of profound economic transformation.

- **Economic Baseline:** Atjeh's economy in [[1904]] was still heavily reliant on traditional exports of [[pepper]] and other local commodities. The post-war plantation boom in [[rubber]] and other crops had not yet begun.
- **Early Concessions:** In [[1904]], only **6 concessions** existed in all of [[Atjeh and Dependencies]], covering about 1,000 *bouws* — contrasting starkly with the 148 concessions covering nearly 300,000 *bouws* by [[1913]]. [[1904]] was the quiet before the storm.
- **No Opium Regie:** The exploitative *pacht* system for opium was still in place. The reformist *regie* would not be introduced until [[1913]].
- **Shipping:** [[Sabang]], the deep-water port on [[Weh Island]], had already begun to grow in importance, with 588 ships and nearly 1.9 million M³ of tonnage calling at its port.
- **Labor Force:** In [[1904]], the number of Javanese contract laborers in Atjeh was 3,341, already representing a significant demographic shift.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1904** is the year of [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|the expedition of lieutenant-colonel G. C. E. Van Daalen to the Gajoe-, Alas- and Bataklanden]], the bloodiest single episode in the post-[[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|Oemar]] phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]]. In a few days, according to official figures, 2,922 inlanders — among them 1,149 women and children — lost their lives. The same year also saw the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-[[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] *pacificatie* extended to [[Borneo]], and the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] of [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] enacted.

### The expedition of Van Daalen

After the recovery of [[Groot-Atjeh]] following [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|the defection of Toekoe Oemar]] the principal resistance leaders did not submit but withdrew to [[Pedirse|Pedir]]. They were pursued — first to [[Pedirse|Pedir]], then to the coasts, and finally in [[1904]] also to the [[Gajoland]] and [[Alasland]] interior. The [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]] to the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]], [[Alasland|Alas-]] and [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]] was, formally, intended to begin "bespreking van wederzijdse belangen" — discussion of mutual interests. In practice it became a campaign of annihilation. According to the official figures cited by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]], 2,922 inlanders — among them 1,149 women and children — were killed "in weinige dagen" — in a few days.

### Parliamentary reactions

[[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.), who had only two years earlier certified that the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] was further advanced than he had supposed, declared the expedition "erger dan de dagen van Alva's schrikbewind" — worse than the days of Alva's reign of terror. The Catholic MP [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] went further back into history and compared the expedition to the campaigns of Timur Lenk and Djengis Khan: "Het is of men op de inlanders een bende bloedhonden heeft losgelaten, het is beulenwerk" — it is as if a pack of bloodhounds had been set loose on the natives, it is the work of executioners. The conclusion of De Stuers did not differ from that which [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] had formulated years earlier: "Laten wij beschaving brengen, maar niet door middel van bloedbaden!" — let us bring civilisation, but not by means of massacres.

### Ministerial defence

[[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] (a.r.), then minister of Colonies, defended the expedition in the [[Tweede Kamer]]. Patience had been tried in Atjeh during the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie]] period with the result that the resistance had only grown stronger; patience had been tried in [[Djambi]] until "absolute anarchie" had set in and a bloody expedition had become necessary; patience had been tried for years on [[Lombok]], and the [[Netherlands]] had had to pay for it with hundreds of lives. Following the argument of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] maintained that Islam forbade the faithful to submit to any authority that had not proven its unshakeable superiority. The women and children who sacrificed themselves had done so under the influence of religious fanaticism: Van Daalen, it was argued, had made the futility of the struggle clear in advance.

### The De Stuers–Kolkman incident

[[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]]' comparison of [[G. C. E. Van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to Timur Lenk went so far beyond the bounds of criticism customary for a member of a governing party that he was openly and solemnly *gedesavoueerd* — disavowed — by his fractieleider Kolkman. At the next begrotingsdebat, after the elections, De Stuers called Kolkman's statement the product of an "electorale nachtmerrie" — electoral nightmare. The members of the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|Van Daalen expedition]], meanwhile, had received their decorations: 148 onderscheidingen — including 44 military Willemsorden — had been awarded "terzake van 180 bajonetten" (it was actually some 230), a generosity that De Stuers found "wat overdadig" — rather excessive.

### Significance

[[1904]] is the military and political hinge of the Atjehnese war. The expedition of [[G. C. E. Van Daalen|Van Daalen]] broke the back of organised Atjehnese resistance; the colonial authority could thereafter begin, in earnest, the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] that would extend over the next four years and culminate in the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] report of [[1908]].

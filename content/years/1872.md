## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].

**1872** is the year in which the [[Netherlands|Indies government]] announced to the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] the forthcoming visit of a Dutch envoy charged with negotiating a new treaty. The announcement produced an Atjehnese request — relayed through the resident of [[Riouw]] — that the mission be postponed on the pretext of internal discord in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] that had first to be settled. The Dutch government acceded, only to discover that the Atjehnese chiefs had used the delay in [[Singapore]] to visit the consuls of [[Italy]] and the [[United States]].

### The 1872 announcement

In [[1872]], the year after the [[Sumatra-tractaat (1871)|Sumatra-tractaat of 1871]] had freed the Dutch hand on Sumatra, the Indies government announced to the sultan the visit of an envoy "ter onderhandeling over een nieuw verdrag" (for the negotiation of a new treaty). The mission was consistent with the source's insistence that, in [[1871]], conquest of Atjeh was not yet contemplated — only the suppression of piracy. The envoy's brief was, presumably, to convert the failed [[1857]] contract into a new, more rigorous instrument.

### The Riouw pretext

The announcement produced, the source records, an Atjehnese request relayed through the resident of [[Riouw]]. Atjehnese chiefs visited the resident to ask that the Dutch mission be postponed, "onder voorgeven, dat er in Atjeh onenigheden waren gerezen, die eerst bijgelegd moesten worden, ten einde een versterking van het Nederlandse gezag te verkrijgen" (on the pretext that discord had arisen in Atjeh that had first to be settled, in order to obtain a strengthening of Dutch authority). The Dutch government acceded to the request.

### The Singapore consuls

The Dutch government later learned that the Atjehnese chiefs had used the postponement to visit, in [[Singapore]], the consuls of [[Italy]] and the [[United States]]. With the American consul, "was zelfs een voorlopige overeenkomst gesloten" (a preliminary agreement was even concluded). The Dutch demand for clarification from the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]], and the unsatisfactory response, produced the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war of 26 March 1873]].

### Significance

[[1872]] is the year of the failed Dutch-Atjehnese diplomatic approach — the last attempt at a negotiated settlement before the slide to war. The [[Riouw]] postponement and the [[Singapore]] consul-visits are the immediate operational sequence that, in the source's narrative, transformed the anti-piracy posture of [[1871]] into the conquest posture of [[1873]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In **[[1872]]** the father of [[H. N. A. Swart]] was pensioned; the family returned to the Netherlands by the sailing ship *Kosmopoliet II* in 121 days round the Cape, touching at [[Mauritius]], the [[Cape of Good Hope]], [[Ascension]] and [[Saint Helena]], landing at [[Brouwershaven]] and settling finally at [[Kampen]]. In October **[[1872]]** the Dutch believed war with Atjeh unavoidable "without possessing a single even moderately reliable datum concerning the land and its people".

## Nineteenth-Century Historiography & Campaign Analysis

## Acehnese Rearmament and Foreign Diplomacy

Beginning in August [[1872]], as recorded in Dutch parliamentary reports and noted by General [[G. G. Booms]], the [[Aceh Sultanate]] accelerated its imports of modern weaponry through British mercantile channels in [[Penang]]. Between August [[1872]] and early [[1873]], Acehnese authorities imported an estimated 15,000 rifles along with proportionate quantities of gunpowder and munitions, fortifying their coastal positions around [[Meuraxa]] and [[Oleh-leh]].

## Parliamentary Debates in The Hague

In the autumn and winter of [[1872]], the States-General in The Hague engaged in extensive debates regarding Dutch relations with Aceh, culminating in the presentation of the [[Parliamentary Note on Relations with Aceh 1872-1873]] by Minister of Colonies [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]]. During [[1872]], lawmakers discussed the growing inevitability of intervention, though military authorities failed to establish a permanent [[General Staff of the KNIL]] to prepare operational plans.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

Towards the close of the year the Atjeh policy of the coming war was fixed. [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] cites, from the revelations afterwards made in the secret committee of [[1874]], the confession of the minister of Colonies [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] in the session of [[18 April]] [[1874]], that he had written to the Governor-General on [[24 October]] [[1872]]: "laten wij oppassen om in Godsnaam geen oorlog met Atjeh te maken, want het zal ons meer bloed en geld kosten dan geheel Atjeh waard is" ("let us take care, for God's sake, to make no war with Atjeh, for it will cost us more blood and money than the whole of Atjeh is worth"). Five months later the same minister was nevertheless drawn into the very war against which he had warned — "Inderdaad, wij ervaren het", Verstege adds ("Indeed, we are experiencing it"). The same author recalls that already in June [[1870]] the government had laid down a resolute policy towards Atjeh with the hope of soon being discharged of the bond of Atjeh's independence laid on the Netherlands by the treaty of [[1824]], the Minister writing to the Governor-General on 24 June 1870 that too little consideration for Atjeh must not be a reason any longer for abstention "where interference is duty" (see [[Stieltjes|the debate of April 1874]]).

## Statistical and Administrative Record
The year [[1917]] is primarily documented in the *Atjeh up to date* compilation through multi-year colonial spending plans. The surviving ledgers from this period focus on standardized, predictable provincial budgets:

*   **Budgetary Projections:** Colonial ledgers outline specific allocations for [[1917]], recording initial project or administrative spending at 154,650 guilders, with a broader aggregate budget category reaching 386,625 guilders.
*   **Administrative Consolidation:** These figures reflect a colony transitioning from the extreme, unpredictable costs of active guerrilla warfare to a stabilized, revenue-generating colonial province. The financial overhead of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons and local infrastructure was being absorbed into routine multi-year provincial budgets.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1 ([[1917]])* — [[1917]]
**The year [[1917]]** was, in the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account]], the year of publication of the Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1: Sumatra, Algemeene Hoofdstukken* — the opening instalment of the Bureau's planned regional survey of Sumatra, and the most comprehensive published Dutch treatment of the orography, hydrography, climate and indigenous administration of Sumatra as a whole. The year [[1917]] is the *terminus ad quem* of the Bureau's pre-war survey of the Sumatran *gewesten*, and the canonical pre-war reference for the physical and administrative geography of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]].

## The publication of Deel II, Aflevering 1
The [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Bureau's Sumatra survey]] was published in February [[1917]] from the Government Printing Office in Weltevreden, under the imprint of the publisher v/h. G. C. T. van Dorp & Co. of Semarang–Soerabaia–'s-Gravenhage, as Aflevering X of the *Mededeelingen van het Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen*. The foreword was dated "WELTEVREDEN, February [[1917]]." The volume's three general chapters — Oro-hydrographic (pp. 1–38), Climate (pp. 39–57), and The development of the Inlandsch Bestuur (pp. 58–65+) — were the first instalment of a planned series of regional descriptions that would treat each Sumatran *gewest* in turn.

## The Atjehsche hoofden opleiding of [[1916]]
The year [[1917]] is also the year in which the G.B. of 27 June [[1916]] No. 7 — by which, for the school year [[1916]]/[[1917]], up to 20 sons of Atjehsche chiefs could also be trained at the training school for Inlandsch teachers at Fort de Koek for the administrative sphere — came into effect. The Atjehsche hoofden opleiding of [[1916]]–[[1917]] is the staff history's principal instance of the Dutch colonial state's programme for the indigenisation of the Atjeh administrative officials, and the principal evidence of the Bureau's [[1917]] judgement that the programme was insufficient — both quantitatively and qualitatively.

## Significance
The year [[1917]] is, in the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Bureau's account]], the *terminus ad quem* of the Bureau's pre-war survey of the Sumatran *gewesten*, and the canonical pre-war reference for the physical and administrative geography of Atjeh. The year [[1917]] is also the year in which the Atjehsche hoofden opleiding came into effect, and the year of the Bureau's judgement that the opleiding was insufficient — a judgement that would be vindicated, in the negative, by the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] rising of February [[1942]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1891

**[[1891]]** was the year in which the management of the Atjeh tramways — the small line between [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and [[Kota Radja]] (built [[1875]], converted to a tramway [[1882]]) together with the *ceintuur-trambaan* (belt-tramway) along the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] built in [[1885]] — reverted from the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|Department of Civil Public Works (B.O.W.)]] back to the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]]. The transfer marked the recognition that, despite the [[1882]] attempt to recast the Atjeh line as a civil tramway, the lines remained essentially military instruments serving the campaign against the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] resistance.

The 1891 reversion closed the first chapter of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s history — the chapter of small, local lines around [[Kota Radja]] — and prepared the ground for the second chapter that opened with the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]] and the long-line construction beginning in [[1897]]. The latter, by following the Dutch extension of authority into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]], transformed the Atjeh-tram from a perimeter-defence system into a network of conquest.

## Significance

The [[1891]] transfer back to the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]] is a small but telling index of the relative weight of military and economic considerations in the early history of Dutch Atjeh. For the entire first quarter-century of its existence, the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was an instrument of military occupation rather than of economic development — a fact that the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account explicitly foregrounds when it observes that the Atjeh-tram "was built with exclusively politico-military intentions, without sufficient regard, at the time of the decision to build, for the economic demands that such a connecting route ought to meet."

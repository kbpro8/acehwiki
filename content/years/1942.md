**The year 1942** was, in Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan*, the year of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of Atjèh]] — the year that began with the [[Sabang bombing (22 January 1942)|heavy bombing of Sabang]] of [[22 January]] [[1942]], continued with the evacuation of [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] on [[10 March|10]]–[[11 March]] [[1942]] and the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese landings]] of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[1942]], and concluded with the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Dutch capitulation on Sumatra]] of [[28 March]] [[1942]]. Within seventeen days of the first landings, the entire residency was in Japanese hands.

## The air campaign and the bombardment of Sabang

From [[18 January]] [[1942]] the Japanese air force attacked [[Sabang|Sabang]] "with a frequency of four to five attack days per week until the arrival of the enemy." The campaign culminated in the [[Sabang bombing (22 January 1942)|heavy raid of 22 January 1942]] — "the strategically important Sabang was repeatedly bombed, most heavily on 22 January 1942." The bombing had "a paralyzing effect on normal life in the various kota's": "the reports of the various bombings on Sabang led to many business people leaving the kota's and the gampóng population daring no longer to go to the pasar, thereby threatening the economic life with disruption." The daily Japanese air reconnaissance over Atjèh, conducted from January onward, completed the paralysis.

## The evacuation of Koetaradja (10–11 March)

The decision to evacuate [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] was taken on the night of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[1942]], on the news of the Japanese landing at [[Sabang|Sabang]]: "At midnight came the telephone report of the Japanese landing at Sabang, whereupon the troops commander decided a few hours later to evacuate Koetaradja." The European civilian population had already been moved out in three large transports on [[10 March|10]] and [[11 March]] [[1942]]: "In three large transports, the European civilian population still remaining in Koetaradja was evacuated on 10 and 11 March 1942 to Medan." The third transport "stranded at [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] and would later be taken up in the central internment camps for Atjèh, first at Koetaradja, later at [[Lawé Sigalagala|Lawé Sigalagala]] in the Alas valley."

## The invasion of 11–12 March

The [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of Atjèh]] comprised four simultaneous landings on the night of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[1942]] — at [[Sabang|Sabang]], at [[Ujong Batëë|Ujong Batëë]] north-east of Koetaradja, at [[Kuala Bughah|Kuala Bughah]] north of [[Langsa|Langsa]], and at [[Tandjong Tiram|Tandjong Tiram]] south of [[Medan|Medan]]. "On Sabang the Japanese encountered only two infantry brigades," and "no resistance was therefore offered. The airfield, harbor, oil and gasoline supplies fell undamaged into the hands of the enemy." The mainland defence collapsed within days: [[Sigli|Sigli]] was reached on [[13 March|13 March]], [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] and [[Lhó' Seumawè|Lhó' Seumawè]] on [[14 March|14 March]], [[Lam Meuló|Lam Meuló]] on [[16 March|16 March]], and the highland retreat to [[Takéngón|Takéngón]] ended in surrender at [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] on [[18 March|18 March]].

## The capitulation of 28 March

The campaign was concluded by the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|capitulation of 28 March 1942]], which ended the defence of the Atjèh highlands and brought the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of Atjèh]] to a military close. [[Major-General Overakker|Major-General Overakker]]'s highland army at [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] and the combined Lhó' Nga and West-Coast troops of [[Captain Kloprogge|Captain Kloprogge]] at the [[Lae Boetar|Lae Boetar]] enterprise surrendered on the same day. "Thus, 17 days after the landing of Japanese troops on North-Sumatra, the matter was settled, without any significant resistance being offered." The Japanese would later — mistakenly — celebrate the date as [[27 March]] "New Sumatra Day."

## Significance

1942 ended 39 years of Dutch rule in Atjèh and installed the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] of [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] — the [[M-organisatie|M-organisatie]] under whose direction the residency would be governed for the next three and a half years. The pre-invasion [[F-organisatie|F-organisatie]] network now stepped forward openly: [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]] and [[Said Doellah|Said Doellah]] entered with the first troops to displace the loyal *oelèëbalang*s, and the [[Poesa|Poesa]]–*oelama* ascendancy that would dominate the occupation was established.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1942

**[[1942]]** was the year of the Japanese invasion of the [[Nederlands-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] — the invasion that began in January and was complete by March — and the year that opened the *[[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Jappentijd]]*. The [[K.N.I.L.]] was dissolved; its European soldaten were interned in prisoner-of-war camps; its indigenous soldaten were demobilised, pressed into Japanese auxiliary formations, or killed. "Totdat de Jap kwam." The *[[tangsi]]* culture that had made *[[Oom Ambon]]* was broken. The year [[1942]] is the book's marker of the great rupture of the colonial army's history — the end of the world the book memorialises.

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954), chapter "Ooms leger" and chapter "Jappentijd."

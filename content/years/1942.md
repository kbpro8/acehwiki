**The year 1942** was, in Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan*, the year of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of Atjèh]] — the year that began with the [[Sabang bombing (22 January 1942)|heavy bombing of Sabang]] of [[22 January]] [[1942]], continued with the evacuation of [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] on [[10 March|10]]–[[11 March]] [[1942]] and the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese landings]] of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[1942]], and concluded with the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Dutch capitulation on Sumatra]] of [[28 March]] [[1942]]. Within seventeen days of the first landings, the entire residency was in Japanese hands.

## The air campaign and the bombardment of Sabang

From [[18 January]] [[1942]] the Japanese air force attacked [[Sabang|Sabang]] "with a frequency of four to five attack days per week until the arrival of the enemy." The campaign culminated in the [[Sabang bombing (22 January 1942)|heavy raid of 22 January 1942]] — "the strategically important Sabang was repeatedly bombed, most heavily on 22 January 1942." The bombing had "a paralyzing effect on normal life in the various kota's": "the reports of the various bombings on Sabang led to many business people leaving the kota's and the gampóng population daring no longer to go to the pasar, thereby threatening the economic life with disruption." The daily Japanese air reconnaissance over Atjèh, conducted from January onward, completed the paralysis.

## The evacuation of Koetaradja (10–11 March)

The decision to evacuate [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] was taken on the night of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[1942]], on the news of the Japanese landing at [[Sabang|Sabang]]: "At midnight came the telephone report of the Japanese landing at Sabang, whereupon the troops commander decided a few hours later to evacuate Koetaradja." The European civilian population had already been moved out in three large transports on [[10 March|10]] and [[11 March]] [[1942]]: "In three large transports, the European civilian population still remaining in Koetaradja was evacuated on 10 and 11 March 1942 to Medan." The third transport "stranded at [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] and would later be taken up in the central internment camps for Atjèh, first at Koetaradja, later at [[Lawé Sigalagala|Lawé Sigalagala]] in the Alas valley."

## The invasion of 11–12 March

The [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of Atjèh]] comprised four simultaneous landings on the night of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[1942]] — at [[Sabang|Sabang]], at [[Ujong Batëë|Ujong Batëë]] north-east of Koetaradja, at [[Kuala Bughah|Kuala Bughah]] north of [[Langsa|Langsa]], and at [[Tandjong Tiram|Tandjong Tiram]] south of [[Medan|Medan]]. "On Sabang the Japanese encountered only two infantry brigades," and "no resistance was therefore offered. The airfield, harbor, oil and gasoline supplies fell undamaged into the hands of the enemy." The mainland defence collapsed within days: [[Sigli|Sigli]] was reached on [[13 March|13 March]], [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] and [[Lhó' Seumawè|Lhó' Seumawè]] on [[14 March|14 March]], [[Lam Meuló|Lam Meuló]] on [[16 March|16 March]], and the highland retreat to [[Takéngón|Takéngón]] ended in surrender at [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] on [[18 March|18 March]].

## The capitulation of 28 March

The campaign was concluded by the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|capitulation of 28 March 1942]], which ended the defence of the Atjèh highlands and brought the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of Atjèh]] to a military close. [[Major-General Overakker|Major-General Overakker]]'s highland army at [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] and the combined Lhó' Nga and West-Coast troops of [[Captain Kloprogge|Captain Kloprogge]] at the [[Lae Boetar|Lae Boetar]] enterprise surrendered on the same day. "Thus, 17 days after the landing of Japanese troops on North-Sumatra, the matter was settled, without any significant resistance being offered." The Japanese would later — mistakenly — celebrate the date as [[27 March]] "New Sumatra Day."

## Significance

1942 ended 39 years of Dutch rule in Atjèh and installed the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] of [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] — the [[M-organisatie|M-organisatie]] under whose direction the residency would be governed for the next three and a half years. The pre-invasion [[F-organisatie|F-organisatie]] network now stepped forward openly: [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]] and [[Said Doellah|Said Doellah]] entered with the first troops to displace the loyal *oelèëbalang*s, and the [[Poesa|Poesa]]–*oelama* ascendancy that would dominate the occupation was established.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1942

**[[1942]]** was the year of the Japanese invasion of the [[Nederlands-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] — the invasion that began in January and was complete by March — and the year that opened the *[[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Jappentijd]]*. The [[K.N.I.L.]] was dissolved; its European soldaten were interned in prisoner-of-war camps; its indigenous soldaten were demobilised, pressed into Japanese auxiliary formations, or killed. "Totdat de Jap kwam." The *[[tangsi]]* culture that had made *[[Oom Ambon]]* was broken. The year [[1942]] is the book's marker of the great rupture of the colonial army's history — the end of the world the book memorialises.

## Account from *Nederlands-Indië contra Japan (1959)* — 1942

**The year 1942** was, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the year of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]] and of the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Dutch capitulation of 28 March 1942]] — the year that ended 39 years of Dutch rule in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] and installed the Japanese military occupation. The staff history's account of [[1942]] is the most detailed published Dutch narrative of the Atjeh campaign, and the operational complement to Dr. [[A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]]'s political and social account in *[[Atjèh and the War with Japan]]*.

## The February 1942 F-beweging rising

The [[1942]] account opens with the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] sabotage wave that broke on the night of [[19 February|19]]–[[20 February]] [[20 February 1942|20 February]] in the *sagi der XXII moekims* around [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]]. The first action period culminated in the [[murder of Tiggelman at Seulimeum|murder of controleur J. C. Tiggelman]] on [[24 February]] [[24 February 1942|24 February]] and the [[murder of Von Sperling|killing of Von Sperling]] the following morning. The identification of the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] as a Japanese-sponsored fifth column — through the arrest and interrogation of a Menadonese soldier and the recognition that "F." stood for **Fujiwara** — was the principal Dutch intelligence discovery of February [[1942]].

The second sabotage wave of [[7 March|7]]–[[8 March]] [[8 March 1942|8 March]] ran directly into the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese landings of 12 March]].

## The 11 March 1942 arrest of the eight chiefs

On [[11 March]] [[11 March 1942|11 March 1942]] Colonel [[G. F. V. Gosenson|Gosenson]] ordered the [[arrest of the eight Atjeh chiefs|arrest of the eight foremost Atjeh chiefs]] at the resident's yard at Kotaradja — an impulsive act the staff history treats as the principal Dutch command error of the pre-invasion rising.

## The 12 March 1942 Japanese landings

The Japanese landings of [[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March]] — at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]], [[Sabang|Sabang]], [[Ujong Batëë|Ujong Batëë]], [[Kuala Bughah|Kuala Bughah]] and [[Tandjong Tiram|Tandjong Tiram]] — comprised a Guards Brigade (a regiment with support weapons) and a Guards Regiment, totalling well over 10,000 men. A battalion of the Guards Regiment struck along the Westkust; the rest marched along the Oostkust; a part of the Guards Brigade marched from Kotaradja on [[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]] and [[Tangse|Tangse]]. A further battalion landed at [[Idi|Idi]] and marched on [[Medan|Medan]] to link up with the central-Sumatra landings.

## The three Dutch main groups

The Dutch response was organised in three main groups:

- **1ste hoofdgroep** (Westkust) under Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] — the [[2de (IIde) Divisie Marechaussee|IIde Divisie Maréchaussee]] and the [[Compagnie Steinhorst|Compagnie Steinhorst]], with the [[Evacuation of women and children from the Westkust of Atjeh (March 1942)|evacuation of 3,000 women and children]] and the [[Battle of Blang Pidië (16 March 1942)|Battle of Blang Pidië]].
- **2de hoofdgroep** (noordkust) under Lt-Col [[C. J. Bekkers|Bekkers]] — the [[2de Garnizoensbataljon|2de Garnizoensbataljon]] and the IVde Divisie Maréchaussee, with the [[Poort stelling (Tangse)|collapse of the Poort stelling]] and the [[Poort stelling (Tangse)|capitulation of Tangse on 18 March 1942]].
- **3de hoofdgroep** (central-Atjeh) under [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek]] — the [[3de (IIIde) Divisie Marechaussee|IIIde Divisie]] and [[5de (Vde) Divisie Marechaussee|Vde Divisie Maréchaussee]], with the [[First stelling south of Bireuën (15 March 1942)|first stelling south of Bireuën]] and the [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|Van Zanten resistance]].

## The capitulations of March 1942

The Dutch campaign closed with three capitulations:

- **18 March 1942**: Tangse capitulation under Lt-Col [[C. J. Bekkers|Bekkers]] — the [[Poort stelling (Tangse)|close of the noordkust theatre]].
- **19 March 1942**: Takengon capitulation under [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]] — the close of the central-Atjeh theatre.
- **28 March 1942**: [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Blangkedjeren capitulation]] under Lt-Col [[Blogg|Blogg]] — the close of the regular campaign.
- **29 March 1942**: [[Capitulation at Lae Boetar (29 March 1942)|Lae Boetar capitulation]] under Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] — the close of the Westkust theatre.

The staff history records that less than three weeks after the Japanese landings, the regular warfare in Noord- and Midden-Sumatra had come to an end.

## The post-capitulation resistance

The post-capitulation Dutch resistance of [[1942]] comprised two episodes:

- The [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|Van Zanten resistance]] from a forest bivouac north-east of [[Takengon|Takengon]], with the [[Redelong (theeonderneming)|Redelong tea estate]] as intelligence and supply base — surviving until Van Zanten's capture on [[10 March]] [[10 March 1943|10 March 1943]].
- The [[safeguarding of the vaandel of the Korps Maréchaussee|safeguarding of the vaandel of the Korps Maréchaussee]] by Lt [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|Onvlee]] — sustained through the Japanese captivity from [[1 December]] [[1 December 1941|1 December 1941]] to [[15 August]] [[15 August 1945|15 August 1945]].

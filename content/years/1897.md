## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1897

**[[1897]]** was the year in which [[Sabang|Sabang]] was opened as a free port for general trade, the foundational act of the harbour that would become, under the [[Sabang Maatschappij|N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]] founded the following year, one of the principal coaling stations and transit harbours of the Netherlands-Indies. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* records the opening as a milestone in the long Dutch effort to draw the trade of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] away from the British entrepôt of [[Penang|Penang]].

## From coal station to free port

The history of Sabang as a Dutch establishment began in [[1884]], when the Netherlands-Indies Government built a small coal shed at the *Zuidbaai* (Lhôc Balhan) and appointed a chief over the island of [[Poeloe Wè|Poeloe Wè]]. The Balhanbaai was, however, soon found to present "verschillende bezwaren" — various obstacles — for shipping, and a commission sent out in [[1887]] concluded that the *Sabangbaai* was more suitable for the construction of a coal station. In the same year the Bataviaasche firma De Lange received a concession for the construction of a coal station at the Sabangbaai. The *geestelijken vader* — the spiritual father — of Sabang, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, was the *heer van Oosterzee*, who first proposed the location; its political champion was the then-majoor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]], whose [[1892]] brochure *De Onderwerping van Atjeh* had urged the establishment of a free port to compete with Penang.

## The free port and the competition with Penang

The opening of Sabang as a free port in [[1897]] was the realisation of van Heutsz's proposal. The hope had been that the natural advantages of the harbour — its sheltered anchorage, its location at the meeting point of trade routes, its freedom from the tidal constraints that afflicted [[Penang|Penang]] and the lighting constraints that limited entry to [[Singapore|Singapore]] at night — would draw Atjeh's trade away from the British entrepôt. *De Buitenbezittingen* records, however, that "de grootsche toekomstdroomen van Sabang als handels-emporium voor geheel Noord-Sumatra niet verwezenlijkt hebben" — the grand dreams of Sabang as the trade emporium for all of North Sumatra were never realised. The "eeuwenoude betrekkingen" of Atjeh's coastal traders with their friends in Penang, and the "geringe uithoudingsvermogen" of the Dutch capital then interested in Atjeh's trade, proved more powerful than the new harbour's advantages.

## Telegraafkabel and later development

In [[1897]] Sabang was also connected by telegraafkabel to [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]], establishing the communications link that would, in [[1912]], be supplemented by a direct cable to [[Medan|Medan]]. The radio-telegrafisch station established in [[1911]] would, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, become the most powerful in all of East Asia.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1897

**[[1897]]** was the year in which the long tramway of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — the line that would replace the abandoned [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway]] of [[1885]] along the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] — first began to be built. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau, this long line "followed step by step our extension of authority in [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh's Dependencies]] after [[1896]]," tying "post after post of the ever-expanding military occupation to the command centre at [[Kota Radja]]." Where the [[1885]] belt-tramway had been a defensive ring around [[Kota Radja]], the [[1897]] long-line was a strategic artery of conquest radiating outward into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]].

From [[1899]] onward, the construction of a second tramway in the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] was also begun — the line that, between [[1899]] and [[1906]], was successively extended from [[Sigli]] to the harbour of [[Langsa]], with the [[Langsa]]–[[Koeala Simpang]] section added between [[1910]] and [[1912]] and a western branch from [[Sigli]] over [[Padang Tidji]] to [[Keude Breue]] already built in [[1899]]. The [[1897]] beginning of the long line is thus the foundation of the second and decisive phase of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]: the phase in which the network became the instrument of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]].

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau summarises the [[1897]] shift as follows: in place of the dismantled belt-tramway, "the long tramway came, which since [[1897]] has followed step by step our extension of authority in [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh's Dependencies]], tying post after post of the ever-expanding military occupation to the command centre at [[Kota Radja]]." The line is the physical embodiment of the post-[[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|1896]] strategic reorientation in Atjeh.

## Significance

[[1897]] marks the beginning of the conquest-phase of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] — the period in which the railway became the principal logistical instrument of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]]. The long line of [[1897]] inaugurated a quarter-century of expansion that would only reach its natural terminus with the [[1913]] extension plans toward [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] and the projected junction with the [[Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij|Deli railway]] at [[Tandjoeng Slamat]].

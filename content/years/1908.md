**[[1908]]** was the year in which [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] succeeded [[G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]] as Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], inaugurating the *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* — the conciliatory and confidence-building policy — that would, after the proofs of Dutch *overmacht* already given in the early years of his administration, draw the coerced chiefs "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde" — ever more, from conviction, to the Dutch side. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* treats [[1908]] as the close of the period of armed confrontation and the beginning of the period of pacification that the rest of the handbook describes.

## The aftermath of 1907–1908 resistance

Swart inherited a territory still shaken by the *vrij hevige opflikkering van het verzet* — the fairly fierce flare-up of resistance — of [[1907]] and [[1908]], provoked by van Daalen's *al te hard doorgevoerde represaille-maatregelen* — overly harsh reprisal measures — and by the *scherpe besnoeiing der adat-inkomsten* — sharp curtailment of the customary revenues of the native chiefs. The same year saw the formal incorporation of [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] into the *indirect bestuurd gebied* of the Gouvernement, which had previously been administered as part of the *Oostkust van Sumatra* on the basis of [[Sultan of Langkat|Langkat]]'s now-extinguished claims under the [[1878 Langkat-Tamiang transfer|1878 transfer]].

## The Gouvernements-rubber-onderneming at Langsa

[[1908]] is also associated with the establishment of the Gouvernements-rubber-onderneming at [[Langsa|Langsa]], the Government's own rubber enterprise that, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, gave the *stoot* — the impetus — to the development of European cultures on the East Coast. The undertaking, begun in [[1907]] before any private planter had risked the experiment, "deed de gunstige omstandigheden voor het welslagen van Eur. ondernemingen in het licht stellen" — brought the favourable circumstances for the success of European undertakings to light — and so drew the private capital that, by the time of the handbook's compilation, had covered the East Coast with rubber, oil-palm, and coffee estates.

## The handwerkscholen

Swart's governorship is also associated with the cultural initiative of the *handwerkscholen* — handicraft schools — founded "op initiatief van Gouverneur Swart" in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir|Pedir]]. The schools, which provided instruction in useful and decorative handicrafts, lace-making, and weaving, were typically staffed by Menangkabau girls trained as assistant teachers on Sumatra's West Coast. The aim was the *ontwikkeling der Atjehsche vrouw* — the development of the Acehnese woman — and through her the condition of the entire people.

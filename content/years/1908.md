**[[1908]]** was the year in which [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] succeeded [[G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]] as Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], inaugurating the *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* — the conciliatory and confidence-building policy — that would, after the proofs of Dutch *overmacht* already given in the early years of his administration, draw the coerced chiefs "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde" — ever more, from conviction, to the Dutch side. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* treats [[1908]] as the close of the period of armed confrontation and the beginning of the period of pacification that the rest of the handbook describes.

## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1908

**1908** was a year of ongoing colonial consolidation and administrative recording in [[Acheh]], situated within the broader context of the [[Dutch East Indies]]. While narrative accounts of specific military engagements in this year are fragmented in surviving statistical extracts, the year is heavily documented in colonial financial and logistical ledgers, reflecting the immense material cost of the [[Aceh War]] and the pacification of the Acehnese interior.

## Statistical and Administrative Record

According to the *Atjeh up to date* compilation, [[1908]] served as a baseline year for extensive colonial bookkeeping. The surviving data from this year highlights the scale of Dutch logistical and financial operations in northern [[Sumatra]]:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1908]] record massive outlays, with total figures reaching 5,206,526 guilders across various administrative and military departments. Specific departmental or regional ledgers show distinct allocations, such as 4,169,072 and 1,283,439 in separate accounting categories.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Registries of vessel arrivals and tonnage indicate a steady flow of colonial supplies and export commodities. Monthly and annual tracking shows figures such as 91,146 and 211,678 in primary logistical categories, alongside 188,654 and 292,668 in secondary trade metrics.
*   **Military Overhead:** The financial burden of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons and conducting localized patrols is reflected in the high baseline expenditures, a trend that would continue to fluctuate in the years leading up to [[1914]].

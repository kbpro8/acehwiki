**[[1908]]** was the year in which [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] succeeded [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen]] as Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], inaugurating the *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* — the conciliatory and confidence-building policy — that would, after the proofs of Dutch *overmacht* already given in the early years of his administration, draw the coerced chiefs "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde" — ever more, from conviction, to the Dutch side. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* treats [[1908]] as the close of the period of armed confrontation and the beginning of the period of pacification that the rest of the handbook describes. The same year also saw the departure of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] from [[Acheh|Atjeh]] after thirty-four years of Catholic chaplaincy on the war-footing, and the raising of a *borstbeeld* (bust) of the *soldatenpastoor* at [[Koeta Radja|Kotta-Radja]] by the garrison he had served since [[1874]].

- [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] — Catholic chaplain; departed Atjeh in [[1908]] after thirty-four years of service.
- [[F. A. Liefrinck]] — Government Commissioner visiting Swart's first administration twice.

- [[Seulimeum]]–[[Kudéé Brueuk]] (mountain line) — limited service from 15 September, joining the tram nets of Groot-Atjeh and the coasts.

## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1908

**1908** was a year of ongoing colonial consolidation and administrative recording in [[Acheh]], situated within the broader context of the [[Dutch East Indies]]. While narrative accounts of specific military engagements in this year are fragmented in surviving statistical extracts, the year is heavily documented in colonial financial and logistical ledgers, reflecting the immense material cost of the [[Aceh War]] and the pacification of the Acehnese interior.

## Statistical and Administrative Record

According to the *Atjeh up to date* compilation, [[1908]] served as a baseline year for extensive colonial bookkeeping. The surviving data from this year highlights the scale of Dutch logistical and financial operations in northern [[Sumatra]]:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1908]] record massive outlays, with total figures reaching 5,206,526 guilders across various administrative and military departments. Specific departmental or regional ledgers show distinct allocations, such as 4,169,072 and 1,283,439 in separate accounting categories.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Registries of vessel arrivals and tonnage indicate a steady flow of colonial supplies and export commodities. Monthly and annual tracking shows figures such as 91,146 and 211,678 in primary logistical categories, alongside 188,654 and 292,668 in secondary trade metrics.
*   **Military Overhead:** The financial burden of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons and conducting localized patrols is reflected in the high baseline expenditures, a trend that would continue to fluctuate in the years leading up to [[1914]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1908

**[[1908]]** was a pivotal year for the administrative and infrastructural consolidation of Dutch rule in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the wider Buitenbezittingen. On the administrative side, [[1908]] saw the creation of the office of [[Adviseur voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Adviseur for the Administrative Affairs of the Outer Possessions]] — a position established within the [[Departement van Binnenlandsch Bestuur|Department of the Interior]] together with a corresponding Afdeeling voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen. The new Adviseur and his Bureau were tasked with providing "voorlichting" (guidance) to the central Government organs regarding what ought to be done in the outer territories, and to the local Government organs regarding the direction the central Government wished to see them follow. The office was, in the Bureau's account, the institutional response to the fact that "the central Government at Buitenzorg and Batavia could no longer adequately oversee the task it had to fulfil with respect to those possessions, both because of its scope and from unfamiliarity with the prevailing conditions and the needs that, in the interest of the regions recently brought under our administrative influence, demanded fulfilment."

The year [[1908]] is also identified as the moment at which "the economic value of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]" began to undergo "a change for the better with the advancing pacification of the land since [[1908]]." The completion, in the years [[1904]]–[[1908]], of the *berglijntje* (mountain line) from [[Keude Breue]] over the hills to [[Seulimeum]] — connecting the second tramway of the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] with the old tramway in [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] — gave the military administration "a powerful instrument for achieving the complete pacification of the intersected regions." The [[1908]] completion of the berglijntje thus closes the geographic circle of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network in its military form, and opens the period in which the line begins to acquire economic as well as military value.

[[1908]] was likewise the year in which a fixed railway policy for Sumatra was inaugurated by the G. B. of 8 May 1908 No. 6 — a policy that, together with the wider post-[[1902]] turn, would shape the next decade of Dutch infrastructure spending on Sumatra, including the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] extensions of [[1912]]–[[1913]].

## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].

**1908** is the year in which governor-general [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] submitted his [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|report on Atjeh]] (June [[1908]]) following his [[Van Heutsz's inspection tour of Atjeh (December 1907)|inspection tour]] of December [[1907]]. The report confirmed the fall of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] and set the *pacificatie* on a new footing, organised around the restoration of indigenous *hoofden* (chiefs) rather than around counter-insurgency.

### The Van Heutsz report

In June [[1908]] the report of the governor-general on the Atjehnese situation was published. Its verdict on [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] was harsh: "Hij heeft het beginsel van de pacificatie miskend. Hij heeft een verkeerde politiek gevoerd tegenover de hoofden, aan wie een onheuse bejegening ten deel viel. Hij heeft het goedgezinde deel der bevolking tussen twee vuren geplaatst" — he had misunderstood the principle of the pacification; he had pursued a wrong policy towards the chiefs, who had been given shabby treatment; he had placed the well-disposed part of the population between two fires. Van Daalen, the report concluded, had possessed the character-traits needed for breaking the resistance but had lacked the very different qualities required for the administrative task that followed.

### Van Heutsz on the principle of pacificatie

[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s report laid down, as the *beginsel der pacificatie* — principle of pacification — the "vanouds beproefd gebleken stelsel van bestuur door middel van de hoofden, die men door tactvolle omgang aan het Nederlandse gezag moest binden" — the long-tried system of administration through the chiefs, who were to be bound to Dutch authority by tactful intercourse. Where [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] had humiliated the chiefs — proceeding from the maxim that "gij kunt die kerels niet vertrouwen, gij moet ze ook niet vertrouwen, gij moet ze slaan en drukken en dan zullen ze u gehoorzamen" — you cannot trust those fellows, you must not trust them, you must beat them and press them and then they will obey you — [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] now restored them.

### Van Deventer's dissent

[[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.) did not accept that the mishandling of the chiefs was the principal cause of the continuing unrest in Atjeh. What kept the Atjehnese in revolt was that the cost of the subjugation was being charged to the Atjehnese themselves: heavy taxes and fines were being imposed, and *spionnen-* and *gidsendiensten* (spy- and guide-services) were being demanded as *herendiensten* (corvée). [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] reminded the [[Tweede Kamer]] that [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] himself had written in [[1893]] in his *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*: "wij zitten niet in Atjeh om vliegen te vangen en maar steeds onze millioenen weg te werpen; Atjeh moet onderworpen worden, dat is de zaak, en om dit resultaat na al ons morsen en knoeien nog te erlangen moet Atjeh zelf betalen wat daarvoor voortaan nog aan dit land wordt ten koste gelegd" — we are not in Atjeh to catch flies and to keep throwing away our millions; Atjeh must be subjugated, that is the point, and to obtain this result after all our messing and botching Atjeh itself must pay what is henceforth to be spent on this land.

### IJzerman's defence

IJzerman (u.l.) thought it unfair to pursue [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] with a brochure fifteen years old. Had not the great men in the Netherlands also the right to "oude plunje op na houden" — keep old clothes in the closet? Why not rejoice that [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had outgrown [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]?

### Ministerial endorsement

[[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] — minister of Colonies — pronounced it [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s greatest merit that he had put an end to the Dutch feeling of impotence with respect to the [[Buitenbezittingen|Buitenbezittingen]]: "Hij heeft ons doen zien, dat wij, wanneer wij maar willen en onze middelen systematisch gebruiken, inderdaad ook kunnen bereiken wat wij ons tot taak hadden gesteld" — he has shown us that, when we only will and use our means systematically, we can indeed also achieve what we had set ourselves as a task.

## Significance

[[1908]] is the year in which the institutional, strategic, and infrastructural consolidation of Dutch rule in Atjeh all converge. The creation of the [[Adviseur voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Adviseur]] role signals the formal recognition that the post-[[1896]] Atjeh campaign had outgrown its original military-administrative framework and required a permanent institutional intermediary between Batavia and the field. The completion of the berglijntje in the same year marks the closing of the military circle of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] and the opening of its economic phase. The departure of Pastoor Verbraak, and the garrison's raising of a bust in his honour at Kotta-Radja, close the chapter of the Catholic chaplaincy of the war-footing that had begun with his arrival in [[1874]].

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] marks [[1908]] as the year Governor [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]] gave luitenant [[Schmidt]] the order to dedicate himself entirely to the *opsporing en eventueel vernietiging* of the [[Tiro-oelama]]s and their *benden*. Schmidt had just completed successful work in [[Geumpang]] and [[Teunom]]. The *infanterie-garnizoenen* in [[Keureutoe]] and [[Boeloh Blang Ara]] were replaced by *maréchaussee-brigades*, which the *oelama's uitlegden als de aankondiging van de spoedige terugtrekken der Kompeuni uit Atjeh* — they read it as the announcement of the imminent Dutch withdrawal from Aceh. On the Westkust, the *voornaamste oelama's* gathered on the Goenoeng Mantjang in [[Boven-Wojla]].

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1908** was a pivotal year in the civilian administration of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*. The *onderafdeeling* [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] was detached from the *gewest* [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]] and added to the *afdeeling* [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust van Atjeh]]. The *Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden*, formed in [[1905]], was re-allotted among other administrative areas. The [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] line from [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] to [[Langsa|Langsa]] was completed. The first *afdeelingsbank* in Atjeh was opened at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. The *Nieuw beleid in Atjeh (1908)|new policy* was entrusted to Governor [[Swart (governor of Atjeh)|Swart]]. The [[Soengei Lipoet Cultuur-Mij|Soengei Lipoet Cultuur-Mij]] was founded with predominantly Belgian capital. The [[Tamiang Landbouw-syndicaat|Tamiang Landbouw-syndicaat]] and the [[Tamiang Rubber-Syndicate|Tamiang Rubber-Syndicate]] were formed. The [[Christoffel's expedition to Keureuto (c. 1908)|Christoffel expedition to Keureuto]] is dated to about [[1908]].

## The appointment

By decree of [[4 May]] [[1908]], with thanks for his services in [[Celebes]], Swart was appointed Military Commander of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden and simultaneously Governor of that province, with the title *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* at 1,500 guilders a month. He assumed the government on [[10 June]] [[1908]], taking over the civil administration from Assistant Resident [[Rijckmans]], [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] having already departed.

The appointment was publicly attacked. [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]] wrote in the *[[Nieuwe Courant]]* of [[16 June|16]]–[[19 June]] that Swart offered no guarantee of better policy; minister [[Alexander Willem Frederik Idenburg|Idenburg]] wrote on [[23 June]] that he must be able to say with a quiet mind "what happened shall happen no more"; [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] defended Swart vigorously in his letter of [[30 July|30]]/[[31 July]] but conceded periodic inspection by a member of the [[Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]], and [[Liefrinck]] was chosen. Idenburg wrote to van Heutsz on [[15 September]] on the advantages of private correspondence.

## First measures

[[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]] was attached to Swart for the first months. Swart went east by tram with him as far as [[Langsa]]; at [[Lho' Seumawè]] he held a meeting with all the *[[oelèëbalang]]s* of the eastern part, promised the immediate repayment of what had been wrongly withheld under the [[Boetenstelsel|boete-systeem]], and demanded an end to attacks on the [[Atjehtram]]. At [[Keureutòë]] he met [[Christoffel]]. In June [[H. J. Schmidt]] obtained his reprieve from the posting to [[Padang Tidji]] by undertaking to take [[Keudjroeën Oebit]] within three months; on [[25 June]] Schmidt acted as interpreter at Swart's first meeting with the chiefs of [[Pidie|Pidië]] at [[Sigli]].

Swart's verdict on what he found: "The situation greatly disappointed me and proved far less favourable than I had thought." By the reports of the administrative officials there were certainly five to six thousand men in resistance.

## The resistance

[[1908]] was the climax of the [[The resistance in Atjeh 1908–1912|general resistance]]. The army lost nineteen killed and 290 wounded, of whom nine died; the resistance party lost 855 dead, twenty-nine wounded, 184 prisoners and 225 firearms.

On [[8 February]] [[1908]] Schmidt surprised the [[Tirò Teungkoes|Tirò band]] hiding-place on the *kr.* [[Meu']] in Pidië, killing five of the principal Teungkoes; on [[15 April]] a lieutenant struck again. In the night of [[8 December|8]]–[[9 December]] [[Tgk. Aron]] carried out his *huzarentukje* at [[Keudé Garot]]. In [[Keureutòë]] the foundation for improvement was laid in the last months of the year.

## Administration and development

The [[Administrative Division of Atjeh (1908)|administrative reorganisation]] created five *afdeelingen*. On [[11 August]] [[1908]] the statutory regulation of the *[[heerendienst]]* came into force, reducing the maximum from fifty-two to twenty-four days. Twenty-one village schools were opened in [[Groot Atjeh]]. In August a temporary adviser for agriculture was appointed who lent better seed-*padi*. Eighteen thousand guilders were made available for the purchase of buffaloes in [[Poelò Raja]] and [[Meulaboh]]. The *[[Koloniaal Verslag]]* recorded the difficulties over pepper advances and over the spread of education.

Elsewhere in the Indies, Swart's [[Onderneming tegen Soembawa (1908)|expedition against Soembawa]] of [[16 February]] to [[9 May]] [[1908]] concluded his Celebes governorship and earned him the Commander's Cross of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]].

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* (1923) — 1908

For Langhout 1908 opened the last great governing period before the separation of powers: "In verband met de omstandigheden, welke aanleiding hebben gegeven tot bovenbedoelde bestuurswisseling, werd gedurende de eerste jaren van het bestuur van gouverneur Swart Atjeh tweemalen door een Regeeringscommissaris, het lid van den Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië F. A. Liefrinck, bezocht" — twice Liefrinck came as Government Commissioner to guarantee the continuity of the principles fixed by the Government. Swart "volgde een andere bestuurslijn dan zijn voorganger" while continuing Van Daalen's pursuit of the resistance-leaders; the first programmes of his decade have their date in this year: repayment of the withheld duties to the chiefs, the agricultural-market exhibition at Koeta Radja, the first year of the agricultural adviser posted in the province, and the fiscal baseline figures (in-/export duties nearly f 180,000; native- and non-native income-tax yields f 225,000 and f 22,000) against which Langhout sets those of [[1915]]–[[1917]].

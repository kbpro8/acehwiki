The year **[[1641]]** was a watershed year in the history of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]: the year of the death of Sultan [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar II]] (in some renderings [[Iskander Tsani|Iskander Tsani]]), of the accession of his widow as the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|first Sultana of Atjeh]] — inaugurating the celebrated [[Female rule of Acheh (1641–1699)|Female Rule of Acheh]] ([[1641]]–[[1699]]) — of the [[Conquest of Portuguese Malakka (1641)|joint VOC–Atjeh–Djohor conquest of Portuguese Malakka]], and of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Pieter Soury's VOC embassy to the new Sultana]] with its spectacular [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)|ceremonial audience of 1 July 1641]]. It is also the year in which [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] is found, together with a number of high functionaries of the Achehnese court, taking part in the ceremony of erecting the tombstone for Sultan [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar II]]. The composition of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] and the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] falls within the joint reigns of Iskandar II and Taj al-Ālam.

## The death of Iskandar Thani and the accession of the Sultana

When Sultan [[Iskander Tsani|Iskander Tsani]] (also [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar II]]) died in [[1641]], the *Rijksgrooten* (great officers of the realm) chose his widow as his successor. The same widow, at about forty years of age, "wilde … een Nederlander tot Prinsgemaal verheffen" — wished to elevate a Dutchman to the rank of prince-consort — a proposal over which both the *Rijksgrooten* and the *Hooge Regeering* at [[Batavia|Batavia]] placed their veto. She became Sultana in her own right under the title *Sultan* — "Sultane beteekent slechts eerste vrouw van een Sultan," Godee Molsbergen insists — opening the long era of female rule in seventeenth-century Atjeh. See [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)]].

## The conquest of Portuguese Malakka, January 1641

The year [[1641]] saw two major events in close succession: the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s [[Conquest of Portuguese Malakka (1641)|conquest of Malakka]] from the Portuguese in January, and — shortly after the accession of the Sultana to the throne of Acheh — a treaty signed with the VOC envoy [[Joost Schouten|Joost Schouten]] (the [[Justus Schouten|Justus Schouten]] of the Godee Molsbergen account) in February granting the Company trade concessions on the West Coast of Sumatra. The conquest of Malakka greatly enhanced the VOC's regional power, which may have influenced the new sultana's decision to negotiate favourable terms with the Company. The [[Dutch–Aceh Treaty of 1641|treaty of February 1641]] established Dutch trade privileges in the pepper ports of [[Tikoe|Tikoe]], [[Priaman|Priaman]], [[Padang|Padang]], and [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]], and excluded other European nations. This treaty laid the groundwork for the more comprehensive agreements of [[1649]] and [[1650]].

The conquest closed more than a century of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-Portuguese conflict. The Portuguese nobleman [[Francisco de Souza de Castro|Francisco de Souza de Castro]] was at the time of the conquest a prisoner in Atjeh.

## The Soury embassy and the audience of 1 July 1641

The *Hooge Regeering* at [[Batavia|Batavia]] dispatched *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Pieter Soury]] — its *Secretaris* the young [[Jan van Riebeeck|Jan van Riebeeck]] — to congratulate the new Sultana on her accession and to deliver the late Sultan [[Iskander Tsani|Iskander Tsani]]'s outstanding order of jewels cut to [[Atjehsche mode|Atjeh-style]]. The full ceremonial of the [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)|audience of 1 July 1641]] is one of the most fully documented European diplomatic ceremonies at the Atjeh court. Its highlights include:

- A great elephant procession: fifty-five Moorish *koelies* (porters), spread out at large intervals for greater display, walked in procession behind the colossal beast that carried the *missive* in a golden dish.
- The Sultana came outside the palace to greet the ambassador in spite of the rainy weather — "een buitengewone eer, nooit tevoren aan iemand bewezen."
- The Sultana's gift to the legation of an *[[Atjehsche feestjas|Atjehsch gewaad]]* (Atjeh ceremonial robe) with three golden rings on the collar, "zooals nog nimmer tevoren aan een ambassade was gezonden."
- The Sultana offered Soury her own golden *beteldoos* (betel-box) to prepare a *sirih*-quid.

The most protracted business of the embassy was the disposal of the late Sultan's outstanding jewel-order. The Sultana was not liable for the order placed by her late husband, and the treasury would not authorise the 15,000 *[[Tayl|tayl]]* (= 5,000 guilders) it would have cost. After long lobbying of the *[[Orangkaja|Orangkaja's]]* by *Secretaris* [[Jan van Riebeeck|Van Riebeeck]], a price was agreed for the greater part of the parcel, but for the future the import of jewels by the Company was forbidden in Atjeh.

## The "maidens as ransom" request

At an audience attended by the *[[Panglima|Panglima's]]* of [[Pedir]] and [[Deli]] and many other Atjeh nobles, the chiefs asked whether the Dutch had brought "Portugeesche jonge dochterkens" — Portuguese maidens — to be delivered as *losprijs* (ransom). Soury answered that there were none to be had at Batavia. See [[Maagden als losprijs]].

## The pleasure-garden feast of 14 July 1641

On [[14 July]] [[1641]] the Sultana invited the legation to her *lusthof* (pleasure-garden), where songs in honour of [[Iskander Muda|Iskander Muda]] were sung — moving the Atjeh hearers to tears — and the recently-deceased Sultan [[Iskander Tsani|Iskander Tsani]] was passed over in complete silence.

## The hand-over at the Dutch lodge and Riebeeck's promotion

The Soury embassy was also the occasion of a formal hand-over of the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|lodge]]'s *Opperhoofd* post from [[Jacob Compostel|Compostel]] to [[Pieter Willemz|Willemz]]. [[Jan van Riebeeck|Van Riebeeck]]'s role as *Secretaris* earned him promotion to *Onderkoopman* — the rank with which he would shortly be selected to command the Cape settlement.

## Earlier 1641 events: Schouten's jewel sale

[[Justus Schouten|Schouten]] had sold jewels in Atjeh in [[1641]] to the value of ƒ28,000 with 61% profit — and even then had not managed to dispose of the entire parcel, "daar er reeds te veel diamanten in Atjeh waren." The Schouten sale was thus also in *[[Atjehsche mode|Atjehsche mode]]*, and its partial success — and the saturation of the Atjeh diamond market — was the immediate background against which the Soury mission's failure must be understood.

## Significance

[[1641]] thus marks both the beginning of the [[Female rule of Acheh (1641–1699)|female rule of Acheh]] under the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] and the opening of more than a decade of VOC commercial diplomacy with the Achehnese court, following swiftly on the Company's decisive strategic gain at Malakka. In Godee Molsbergen's account the year is also the high-point of Dutch-Atjeh diplomatic ceremony in the early VOC period — the choreographed encounter between a queen who had wanted a Dutch prince-consort and a Company that preferred to keep its presents and its distance. For [[Jan van Riebeeck|Jan van Riebeeck]] personally, the Soury mission was the making of his early career, and the apprenticeship in flexible diplomacy that he would carry to the Cape.

## Account from Stapel's *De Koloniën* (1931)

**1641** was the year of the [[Malakka|fall of Malakka]] to the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] on [[14 January]] [[1641]] and of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|accession of the sultane of Atjeh]] in February — two events that, in combination, opened a new phase in the Dutch-Acehnese relationship and produced the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (15 February 1641)|Schouten treaty]] of [[15 February]] [[1641]]. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* it is the pivot year of the mid-seventeenth-century Dutch-Acehnese story, the moment at which the long Acehnese-Portuguese alignment ended and the Compagnie and Atjeh faced each other as the two principal powers of the [[Straits of Malacca|Straits]].

## The fall of Malakka

The conquest of [[Malakka|Portuguese Malakka]] was the work of the [[1640]]–[[1641]] siege conducted by G.G. [[Antonio van Diemen|Van Diemen]]. "On 2 August 1640 began the actual siege, which would last more than 5 months and would cost the Compagnie about 1000 men. After a bombardment, both from the sea and the land side, commandant [[Minne Willemsz. Kaartekoe|Minne Willemsz. Kaartekoe]] did on 10 January 1641 the last summons. When this remained unanswered, on the 14th a powerful storming took place, and the Portuguese surrendered, after heroic defence" (*2 Augustus 1640 begon het eigenlijke beleg, dat ruim 5 maanden duren zou en de Compagnie op ongeveer 1000 man kwam te staan. Na een beschieting, zoowel van de zee-, als de landzijde, deed commandant Minne Willemsz. Kaartekoe den 10den Januari 1641 de laatste opeisching. Toen deze onbeantwoord bleef, had den 14den een krachtige bestorming plaats, en gaven de Portugeezen zich, na heldhaftige verdediging, over*).

The first Dutch Governor of Malacca was [[Johan van Twist|Johan van Twist]]. On [[15 August]] [[1641]] he concluded an agreement with the neighbouring [[Naning|Naning]] "and the surrounding villages" (*ende d'omliggende dorpen*), by which the chiefs recognised Dutch supremacy in place of the former Portuguese.

## The accession of the sultane of Atjeh

The sultane's accession in February [[1641]] — daughter of [[Iskandar Muda of Atjeh|Iskandar Muda]], who had died in [[1636]] — produced an immediate opportunity for the Compagnie to regularise its Westkust position. "In 1641 a sultane came to power in Atjeh, daughter of Iskander Moeda; she concluded, still in the first month of her reign — February — an agreement with the Compagnie's envoy [[Joost Schouten|Joost Schouten]]" (*In 1641 kwam in Atjeh een sultane aan het bewind, dochter van Iskander Moeda; zij sloot, nog in de eerste maand van haar regeering — Februari — een overeenkomst met den gezant der Compagnie, Joost Schouten*).

## The Schouten treaty

The [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (15 February 1641)|Schouten treaty]] of [[15 February]] [[1641]] gave the Compagnie "concessions on the tolls in the pepper ports [[Tikoe]], [[Priaman]], [[Padang]] and [[Indrapoera]], and [the sultane] promised to admit no other European traders there" (*zij de Nederlanders concessies deed op het gebied der tollen in de peperhavens Tikoe, Priaman, Padang en Indrapoera, en beloofde, daar geen andere Europeesche handelaars toe te laten*). The careful restriction of the exclusion to *European* traders left the Atjehers' own rights on the Westkust intact — a pattern that would recur in every subsequent Acehnese-Dutch treaty.

## Palembang and the Malay states

The fall of Malakka also produced Compagnie treaties with [[Palembang]] and [[Djambi|Djambi]]. "On 21 July 1641 and 20 October 1642 two agreements came about, by which the Compagnie received the right to build a fortified lodge and to place there eight pieces of gun, so-called for protection against Portuguese or pirates" (*21 Juli 1641 en 20 October 1642 kwamen een tweetal overeenkomsten tot stand, waarbij de Compagnie recht kreeg een versterkte loge te bouwen en daar acht stukken geschut te plaatsen, z.g. ter bescherming tegen Portugeezen of zeeroovers*).

## Significance

1641 is the pivot year of the mid-seventeenth-century Dutch-Acehnese story. The fall of [[Malakka]] ends 130 years of Portuguese power in the Straits; the accession of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|sultane]] opens the era of structured Dutch-Acehnese treaty relations; the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (15 February 1641)|Schouten treaty]] lays the foundation of the Westkust system. The disappearance of the common Portuguese enemy also removes the principal brake on Dutch-Acehnese tension, and the [[Blockade of Atjeh (1656)|1656 blockade]] is the eventual consequence. The 1641 moment is therefore both the opening of a new co-operative phase and the seed of the conflicts that would close it.

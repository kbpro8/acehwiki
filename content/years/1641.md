The year in which [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] is found, together with a number of high functionaries of the Achehnese court, taking part in the ceremony of erecting the tombstone for Sultan [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar II]]. Iskandar Thani's death in 1641 brought to the throne his widow [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Ṣalīyatu'd-Dīn]] (Taj al-Ālam), under whose reign Raniri continued to enjoy royal favour. The accession of the first sultana inaugurated the celebrated Female Rule of [[Acheh]] ([[1641]]–[[1699]]) and continued the project of orthodox reform that Raniri had begun under Iskandar II: the composition of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] and the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] falls within the joint reigns of Iskandar II and Taj al-Ālam.

## Account from *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* (1928) — the VOC conquest of Malakka and the first Aceh treaty

The year **[[1641]]** was a watershed for [[Dutch]]–Aceh relations. It saw two major events in close succession: the [[Dutch East India Company|VOC]]'s conquest of [[Malakka]] from the Portuguese in January, and — shortly after the accession of [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam]] (daughter of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskander Moeda]]) to the throne of Acheh — a treaty signed with the VOC envoy [[Joost Schouten]] in February granting the Company trade concessions on the West Coast of Sumatra.

The conquest of Malakka greatly enhanced the VOC's regional power, which may have influenced the new sultana's decision to negotiate favourable terms with the Company. The [[Dutch–Aceh Treaty of 1641|treaty of February 1641]] established Dutch trade privileges in the pepper ports of [[Tikoe]], [[Priaman]], [[Padang]], and [[Indrapoera]], and excluded other European nations. This treaty laid the groundwork for the more comprehensive agreements of [[1649]] and [[1650]].

## Significance

[[1641]] thus marks both the beginning of the [[Female rule of Acheh (1641–1699)|female rule of Acheh]] under Tajul-alam and the opening of more than a decade of VOC commercial diplomacy with the Achehnese court, following swiftly on the Company's decisive strategic gain at Malakka.

**[[1931]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the *bivak* at [[Bakongan]] — "een brigadecommandant, die vandaar in [[1931]] met pensioen was gegaan, maar die lezen noch schrijven kon" — the brigadecommandant who retired from Bakongan in 1931 and could neither read nor write, but who dictated his patrol reports from memory. "In [[Juli]] [[1931]] viel de laatste" — the last of the Westkust resistance fell in July 1931.

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1931



### The N.P.P.M. founding


For the full account of the [[1931]] N.P.P.M. founding see the entry on [[N.P.P.M.]]. In summary: in [[1931]] the N.P.P.M. — a subsidiary of the Standard Oil Company of California — entered the East Indian oil industry, but "production has not yet started" at the time of the entry. The N.P.P.M.'s late entry — two decades after the [[N.K.P.M.]] (Standard Oil of New Jersey) in [[1912]] and a quarter-century after the [[B.P.M.]] (Royal Dutch) in [[1907]] — is reflected in its modest production figures: the [[1941]] production of the Pasé field (1,797 tons) and the Rokan block (1,402 tons) together accounted for only 3,199 tons, or 0.04 % of the East Indian crude total of 7,241,254 tons in [[1941]].

### Place in the East-Indian oil-industry sequence


For the full sequence see the entry on [[1912]]. The [[1931]] N.P.P.M. founding occupies, in the documentary sequence of East-Indian oil-industry corporate development, the position between the [[1912]] entry of Standard Oil of New Jersey (through the [[N.K.P.M.]]) and the [[1940]]–[[1941]] production tables. The [[1931]] founding is accordingly the last of the three major corporate entrants to the East Indian oil industry, and the N.P.P.M.'s subsequent presence on Atjeh territory (the Pasé field) is the documentary evidence of the most recent corporate presence in the colonial Atjeh oil economy.

### Significance


[[1931]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year in which the Standard Oil Company of California — through its subsidiary the [[N.P.P.M.]] — entered the East Indian oil industry, marking the third (and last) major corporate entrant to the colonial East Indian oil industry. The N.P.P.M. would subsequently become the operator of the [[Pasé oil field|Pasé]] field in the [[Atjeh oil complex]] — the only Atjeh-complex field operated by the company, and the only Atjeh-complex field brought into production in [[1941]] (i.e. the newest of the Atjeh-complex fields). The [[1931]] N.P.P.M. founding is accordingly part of the corporate-historical context of the colonial Atjeh oil economy, and the N.P.P.M.'s subsequent presence on Atjeh territory is the documentary evidence of the most recent corporate presence in the colonial Atjeh oil economy.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1931** was the year of the principal laboratory-survey of the Atjeh patchouli-oil industry, in which six sample consignments of nilam-oil were received by the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum|Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] in Amsterdam. The samples were analysed for *soortelijk gewicht*, *brekingsindex*, *draaiing*, *zuurgetal*, *estergetal* and solubility, and were sent to perfume manufacturers in the Netherlands, Germany, England and France. The Atjeh patchouli-leaf export was 638 tons in [[1931]], of which 9 tons went direct to the Netherlands — the first direct shipment to the Netherlands. The Atjeh nilam-oil export was 3.468 kg in [[1931]]. By [[1931]] there were patchouli-distilleries near [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], at [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]], and two or three at [[Tapa Toean|Tapa Toean]].

The year **[[1623]]** marked the final lifting of the [[VOC trading post at Aceh]] by the [[Dutch East India Company]], after it had been re-established in [[1620]]. After this year, the Company maintained only a single assistant in Aceh, effectively ending its permanent presence in the sultanate. The decision was likely influenced by ongoing difficulties with Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskander Moeda]].

## Aceh Context

The lifting of the factorij in 1623 reflected the Company's assessment that a permanent post was not viable due to the sultan's hostile treatment. This did not, however, mean an end to all contact. The Company continued to send ships and envoys, leading to the significant treaties of [[1641]] and later. The decision in 1623 set the pattern for the next several decades: diplomacy rather than a fixed presence.

## Account from *Over Maleise Literatuur* (1947) — 1623

In [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s survey of Malay literature, the year [[1623]] is the year of the death of the Sultan of [[Djohor|Johor]] — *Abdoellah Masajat Sjah*, the former *Radja 'Abdoellāh* who had commissioned the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* in [[1612]] — on Great-Tambēlan, after a long exile driven by the [[1615]] Atjehnese attack on [[Batoe Sawar]].

### The Death of the Auctor Intellectualis of the Sadjarah Malajoe

The Sultan — *Abdoellah Masajat Sjah* — had been driven from Batoe Sawar by the [[1615]] Atjehnese attack, and had wandered as "een opgejaagd hert" (a hunted deer) — to Bintan in [[1615]], to Lingga in [[1618]], and finally to Great-Tambēlan, where he died around May [[1623]]. Hooykaas notes the pathos of the Sultan's end: "Zó droevig eindigde de auctor intellectualis der SM" (Thus sadly ended the auctor intellectualis of the SM) — the man who had commissioned the *Sadjarah Malajoe* in [[1612]].

### The Malay Chronicles' Silence

The Malay chronicles, Hooykaas notes, "vergeten hem geheel" (forget him entirely) — a fact the more surprising because the Sultan "dan toch de dynastie voortzette in de Sultans 11 en 12, misschien ook 14" (after all continued the dynasty in the Sultans 11 and 12, perhaps also 14). The Malay chronicles' silence on the Sultan's death in [[1623]] is, in Hooykaas's reading, part of the broader diplomatic silence of the *Sadjarah Malajoe* on the worst of the Atjehnese attacks — the silence of a courtier writing under the eye of a vassal-of-Atjeh sultan.

### The Third Atjehnese Attack (early 1623)

The Sultan's death on Great-Tambēlan in [[1623]] was the direct result of a third Atjehnese attack — the third sent by Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] against the wandering Johor court. "In begin 1623 door een Atjehse vloot verdreven, en sterft ca. Mei 1623 op Groot-Tambēlan" (In early 1623, driven out by an Atjehnese fleet, and dies around May 1623 on Great-Tambēlan). The [[1623]] attack thus completes the Atjehnese destruction of the Johor court that had begun with the [[1613]] and [[1615]] attacks on Batoe Sawar.

*Source: Over Maleise Literatuur*, by Dr. C. Hooykaas, Hoogleraarte Batavia; Tweede Druk (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1947), appendix III ("De Vorsten van Malakka en Djohor"), p. 276; chapter 11 ("Geschiedschrijving in het Maleis"), pp. 96-98.

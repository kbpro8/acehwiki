**[[1937]]** was the year of the death of [[Dominee Thenu]] — the *Soldaten-Dominee* of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — on [[11 May]] [[1937]] at [[Koeta-Radja]], and of his [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|indrukwekkende, grootse begrafenis]] with four retired [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]]-ridders as *slippendragers* and a Marechaussee brigade as *vuurpeleton*. It is the year that closes the moral arc of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*.

## The death of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)

"Op 11 Mei 1937 was het einde daar." [[Dominee Thenu]] — who had come to [[Atjeh|Aceh]] in [[1894]] and served for forty full years as the *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* and universally honoured *Soldaten-Dominee* of the [[K.N.I.L.]] troops — died at [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[11 May]] [[1937]]. He had become ill in [[1935]]; by [[1936]] it was clear that his ziekbed was his doodsbed.

## The funeral

The [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|funeral]] at [[Koeta-Radja]] was "indrukwekkende, grootse." As *slippendragers* acted four old, retired Marechaussees, all *Ridders M.W.O.*: [[Salawono]] (3e kl.), [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]] and [[Pattipuluhu]]. *Vuurpeleton* was a Marechaussee brigade. He was buried at [[Peutjoet]] "temidden van zovelen, die hij voorbereid had op die laatste gang daarheen." His gravestone carries the verse from Job 4:3 — "Zie, gij hebt velen onderwezen, En gij hebt slappe handen gesterkt."

## The standbeeld

"Vóór zijn kerkje in [[Koeta-Radja]] richtte Atjeh voor hem een standbeeld op" — before his little church in Koeta-Radja, Aceh erected for him a statue. The *standbeeld* is the book's marker that Thenu's gezag had passed from the colonial army into the country itself: Aceh, not the [[K.N.I.L.]], erected the statue.

## The broader colonial context

[[1937]] was also the year of the *Tangkoeban Praboe incident (1928)|Tangkoeban Praboe incident]] (actually [[1928]] — but the book mentions the crater rescue in the chapter on marksmanship). The broader colonial context of [[1937]] is the late-colonial peace: the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] was politically over since [[1918]]; the [[Celebes]] pacifications were long concluded; the colonial army was at the height of its inter-war strength. The death of Thenu in [[1937]] is therefore the book's marker of the end of an era: the *Soldaten-Dominee* who had served the colonial army since [[1894]] was gone, and the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] he had served was, in [[1937]], an army of peace, not of war.

## Significance

[[1937]] is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the year of the death of [[Dominee Thenu]]. The book's argument is that Thenu's death — with the four M.W.O.-ridders as *slippendragers* — was the moment at which the decorations of the old [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] generation were laid at the feet of the man who had blessed them. The year [[1937]] is therefore the book's moral closure of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]: the men of courage carrying the man of faith, the colonial army at the height of its inter-war peace acknowledging the geestelijk gezag that had held it together.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records [[1937]] as the year [[T. Radja Sabi]] — the *adelaarsjong* of [[Keureutoe]], who had lived in the forests from [[1905]] until [[13 March]] [[1919]] — finally received *de plaats waarop hij recht had*: he became *waarnemend oeléëbalang* of [[Keureutoe]], following the *schorsing* of his cousin (son of [[T. Tjhi Bentara]]) in late [[1936]]. It was also the year the *hoofd* of the Samarkilang *bende* — the last followers of the [[Tengkoe di Paja Bakong (Mata Ië)|Tengkoe di Mata Ië]] killed in [[1917]] — was finally *gevangen genomen*, twenty years after his chief's death.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In **[[1937]]** [[T. Radja Sabi]] at last became *[[oelèëbalang]]* of [[Keureutòë]], the office of which his father [[T. Tjhi' Toenòng]] had been deprived in [[1899]]. In [[1937]] the head of the [[Samarkilang-bende]], formed from the last followers of [[Tgk. di Mata Ië]], was captured — twenty years after the Teungkoe's death. On [[19 February]] [[1937]] [[H. N. A. Swart]] wrote to the Ambonese old soldier [[Jacob Lawalata]], who celebrated his thirty-fifth year as Knight of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] at [[Jogjakarta]] on [[26 February]] [[1937]]. Up to the end of [[1937]] Atjeh had issued sixty-nine agricultural concessions covering 71,069 hectares, seventy-four leasehold parcels covering 67,287 hectares and thirteen option parcels covering 287,000 hectares.

## Account from *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*

Mid-[[1937]]: Military seaplanes visited Atjeh, landing on [[Atjeh-rivier]] at [[Koetaradja]] and on more van [[Takengon]]. [[November]]: 25-year jubilee of [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Mohamad Djohan Alamsjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi']] as [[zelfbestuurder]] of [[Peusangan]]. Start of [[1937]]-[[1938]] course: houtbewerking added to [[Sigli ambachtsleergang]]curriculum.[[H. C. Zentgraaff|Zentgraaff]] made a journalistic journey through the [[Tripa]]-vallei; coined the name 'terpentijnstraat' (turpentine road) for the proposed road from [[Gajo Loeös]] to Aceh's [[Westkust]].

*Source: J. Jongejans, Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu (Baarn: Hollandia Drukkerij N.V., c. 1938), pp. 25, 59, 68, 80, 198, 203, 204, 205, 206, 207, 208.*

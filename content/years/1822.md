**1822** was a year marked by diplomatic, military, and colonial activity in the Dutch‑Aceh sphere, as documented in *Raffles' Atjeh‑overeenkomst van 1819* by [[P. H. van der Kemp]] (1900) and in the regimental roll of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]]. In the Dutch colonial administration, the year saw the formal recognition of several marine personnel for their deeds in the [[Palembang]] campaign of the previous year, while in the broader political landscape the correspondence of [[Lord Castlereagh]] and the movements of local leaders such as [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]], [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]], and [[Teungku Anjong]] were recorded. The awards were issued on [[27 May]] [[1824]] but refer back to actions taken in [[1821]]. The same sources also mention the presence of American and Philippine actors in the region, the settlement of [[Banda Acheh]], and the pension of [[Syf's pension at Penang]].

## Diplomatic and Political Context

The Dutch‑British diplomatic exchange recorded by [[P. H. van der Kemp]] includes references to [[Lord Castlereagh]] (British Foreign Secretary) and to Dutch officials such as [[P. Meyer]] and [[W. E. Phillips]]. Their letters discuss the strategic importance of the [[Sultanate of Aceh]] and its control over the southern ports of [[Sumatra]], especially the ports of [[Pedir]] and [[Kampong Java]]. The documents also note the involvement of the [[United States of America]] and the [[Philippines]] in regional trade, reflecting the international dimension of Acehnese affairs in [[1822]].

## Military Awards and Recipients

The regimental roll of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] records that several marines received the [[Militaire Willemsorde|Military Order of William]] fourth class for their role in the [[Palembang]] campaign of [[1821]]. The award list, compiled in the roll, cites **A. F. Henke** as a fourth‑class recipient and **A. Hosdorft** as a second‑class recipient. The official decree was dated [[27 May]] [[1824]], but the honors commemorate actions undertaken the previous year.

## Events

- [[Syf's pension at Penang]] – a pension granted to a Dutch official in Penang, noted in the 1822 correspondence.
- The Dutch Crown’s policy of rewarding bravery, exemplified by the 1822 decorations, underscored the strategic importance of curbing the power of the [[Sultanate of Aceh]] over Sumatra’s southern ports.

## Significance

The 1822 decorations reinforced the prestige of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] and highlighted the strategic importance of the Palembang operations within the broader Dutch‑Aceh rivalry. Simultaneously, the diplomatic correspondence of the year illustrates the entangled interests of European powers, the United States, and regional actors in the politics of [[Acheh]] and the surrounding archipelago.

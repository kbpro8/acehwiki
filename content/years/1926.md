**[[1926]]** was the year of the [[Communist uprisings of 1926|communist uprisings]] on [[Java]] — and of the *nasmeulend verzet* (smouldering resistance) that flared up again on the [[Westkust (of Atjeh)|Westkust of Atjeh]] at the same time — which together forced a partial restoration of the [[K.N.I.L.]]'s strength after the cutbacks of the post-First-World-War period. It is also the year in which the *[[Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas (1926)]]* was published by [[Volkslectuur]] in [[Weltevreden]], printed by [[G. Kolff & Co.]] in [[Batavia]], as a jubilee volume commemorating the silver reign of [[Queen Wilhelmina|Koningin Wilhelmina]]. It is one of the political-military and publishing years of the inter-war period in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* and a landmark year in the official colonial historiography of Atjeh.

## The communist uprisings

"Gelukkig vonden de communisten in [[1926]], dat dit ver genoeg was gegaan en begonnen een paar opstanden, terwijl op de Westkust van Atjeh nog wat nasmeulend verzet even oplaaide…" The communists in [[1926]] judged that the cutbacks had gone far enough and began a few uprisings, while on the Westkust of Atjeh some smouldering resistance flared up again. The two events — the communist uprisings on Java and the nasmeulend verzet on the Westkust of Atjeh — were independent but simultaneous, and together they forced a partial restoration of the colonial army's strength.

## The cutbacks and the restoration

The cutbacks that provoked the communist judgement had been the post-First-World-War policy of "*geen man en geen cent*" — no man and no cent — by which the colonial army's strength had been reduced to 36,000 (then cut by 6,000) for an archipelago of 70 million. The communist uprisings of [[1926]] and the Westkust resistance forced a partial restoration of the strength; then the economic crisis hit, communism was conveniently declared "done with," and the *legermacht* was reduced again. "En na [[1936]] maar weer uitbreiden."

## The Westkust resistance

The *nasmeulend verzet* on the [[Westkust (of Atjeh)|Westkust of Atjeh]] in [[1926]] was the last flicker of the Acehnese armed resistance after the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]]. The book notes that the Westkust verzet of [[1925]]–[[1927]] was the last significant Acehnese resistance episode of the inter-war period; the Troemon tragedy of [[1926]] is also mentioned in this context.

## Publication of the *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas*

In January [[1926]], the *[[Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas (1926)]]* was published by [[Volkslectuur]] in [[Weltevreden]], printed by [[G. Kolff & Co.]] in [[Batavia]]. The atlas was produced "ter gelegenheid van het vijf-en-twintigjarig regeringsjubileum van Hare Majesteit de Koningin" — on the occasion of the silver jubilee of [[Queen Wilhelmina|Her Majesty the Queen]]'s reign. The atlas was edited by a Special Commission of Editors (*Speciale Commissie van Redactie*) in the Indies, and its publication was overseen by Dr. [[C. P. Gunning]], director of Volkslectuur. It was the colonial government's most ambitious publishing programme of the inter-war period, intended for wide distribution both in the colony and in the Netherlands.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* ([[1926]]) — The Year

The atlas was published in January [[1926]] as a jubilee publication for the silver reign of [[Queen Wilhelmina]]. By this year, the transformation of the [[Buitengewesten]] was sufficiently advanced that the atlas could boast of automobile travel from [[Kota Radja]] in Atjeh to [[Padang]], and the expansion of the Atjeh and Deli railway networks. The war in Atjeh, which had begun in [[1873]], was now fully in the past, commemorated by monuments rather than fought on battlefields — a transition marked by the publication of the atlas itself, whose plates of the [[Atjeh Monument]] and the [[Peutjot Cemetery|cemetery at Peutjot]] enshrined the conflict in the official colonial memory.

## Significance

[[1926]] is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the year of the communist uprisings on Java and of the *nasmeulend verzet* on the Westkust of Atjeh. The book's argument is that the two events together forced a partial restoration of the colonial army's strength, and that the cutbacks that provoked the communist judgement were the post-First-World-War policy of "*geen man en geen cent*." The year [[1926]] is therefore the book's marker of the inter-war political-military context: the communist uprisings, the Westkust resistance, and the partial restoration of the colonial army's strength.

In the broader colonial historiography, [[1926]] is the year in which the *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* — the most ambitious popular publication of the late colonial period — was issued as a self-conscious reckoning of Dutch achievement in the East Indies. The atlas's juxtaposition of the [[Atjeh Monument]] and the [[Peutjot Cemetery|cemetery at Peutjot]] on a single plate, alongside its portraits of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], [[Governor Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] as the "Groote Mannen" of the latest colonial history, fixed the pacification of Atjeh as the centrepiece of the official colonial narrative.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records [[1926]] as a year of major patrouille disasters on the Westkust and criminal violence in [[Blang Kedjeren]]. In [[March]] [[1926]] the patrouille-Grünefeld, travelling from [[Singkel]] to [[Troemon]], was *bijna geheel afgemaakt* through the failure of *waakzaamheid* by a *schildwacht*; fifteen *karabijnen* were lost. The patrouille-Paris at Sapé suffered disaster, but the Jav. maréchaussée [[Kromodikoro]] heroically took command though *zwaar gewond*. The patrouille-Klaar earned the *Kroontje*. [[Tjoet Ali]] attacked the patrouille-Schreuder in [[July]] [[1926]].

On [[7 December]] [[1926]] a Gajoe murdered sergeant Van Gent and sergeant De Gruiter at the *tangsi* of [[Blang Kedjeren]]. [[Van den Broek]] was decapitated with a single *klewang*-blow. The murders, Zentgraff writes, recalled the *Duivel op de Blang* affair of [[1909]]: the same isolation, the same *spanning*, the same murderous release.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

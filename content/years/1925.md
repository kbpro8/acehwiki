**The year 1925** was, in Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan*, the year that opened the [[Bakóngan uprising (1925–1927)|Bakóngan uprising]] — the most serious armed rising of the Atjèhnese against Dutch authority in the two decades after the formal conclusion of the [[Atjèh War|Atjèh War]], fought in the remote subdistrict of the [[Zuidelijke Atjèhse Landschappen|Zuidelijke Atjèhse Landschappen]] under the instigation of [[Teukoe Radja Angkasah|Teukoe Radja Angkasah]] — and, with it, the last phase of large-scale religiously motivated resistance before the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion]] of [[1942]].

## The outbreak in Bakóngan

The [[Bakóngan uprising (1925–1927)|Bakóngan uprising]] was "the most serious" of all the resistance actions "which repeatedly flared up after the conclusion of the Atjèh War." The rising was fought in the wild mountain country of the [[Zuidelijke Atjèhse Landschappen|Zuidelijke Atjèhse Landschappen]] — the territory of [[Bakóngan|Bakóngan]] and the *landschap* of [[Kloeët|Kloeët]] — under the leadership of [[Teukoe Radja Angkasah|Teukoe Radja Angkasah]]. Its opening in 1925 inaugurated a two-year pacification that would not be concluded until [[1927]].

## The strategic response

The rising prompted decisive administrative reconstruction. The two *landschappen* of [[Kloeët|Kloeët]] and [[Troemón|Troemón]] were "separated from these subdistricts and, combined into one subdistrict, placed under the administration of the officer responsible for the action at Bakóngan." The merging of civil and military command in a single Atjèhnese subdistrict — a throwback to the [[Atjèh War|Atjèh War]] pattern — was the most striking institutional consequence of 1925. "Only in the subdistrict of Zuidelijke Atjèhse Landschappen, the scene of the Bakóngan uprising, the military commander remained entrusted with civil administration." The extension of the West Coast Road through the [[Barisan|Barisan]] range in the 1920s was likewise justified by "military-strategic considerations (Bakóngan uprising 1925-1927)," which "were decisive."

## The longer arc

The Bakóngan rising of 1925 closed the era of large-scale religiously motivated resistance in colonial Atjèh. The [[Lhóng action (1933)|Lhóng action of 1933]] would be only a flicker; by [[1939]], as Piekaar records in his introduction, "the resistance actions … had been extinguished" and "cases of so-called '[[Atjèh murder|Atjèh murder]],' the typically Atjèhse form of venting internal difficulties, were hardly worth mentioning anymore." The pacification inaugurated by the suppression of Bakóngan would, in turn, prove illusory: the underlying *[[kaphé|kaphé]]*-hatred and *[[prang sabi|prang sabi]]* mentality would erupt again in [[March]] [[1942]].

## Significance

1925 thus marks the beginning of the end of the long Atjèh War cycle: the last great armed rising of the colonial era, and the event whose suppression allowed the Dutch to believe — for the next seventeen years — that the Atjèhnese had "finally and definitively submitted to Dutch rule." That belief would be shattered in [[March]] [[1942]].

## Account from *In Memoriam Maurice Henri du Croo* (1951) — 1925

**[[1925]]** was the year in which [[Maurice Henri du Croo]] was posted to [[Aceh|Atjeh]] in the [[Buitengewesten]] (Outer Provinces) of the [[Netherlands East Indies]]. Du Croo, a [[K.N.I.L.]] officer, arrived at his own request and began active service in the ongoing [[Aceh War]], taking part in patrols into the *rimboe* (jungle). His later co-authored book *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]* (with [[Schmidt|Kolonel Schmidt]]) would draw on these experiences. The posting of an officer at his own request to Atjeh in [[1925]] — well after the formal conclusion of the war — underscores the continued active Dutch military presence in the territory during the interwar consolidation period.

*Source: In Memoriam Maurice Henri du Croo* (1951), p. 5 (biographical timeline).

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records [[1925]] as the year the [[Westkust resistance (1925-1927)|Westkust resistance]] under [[Tjoet Ali]] began. Beginning with the *zuidelijke nederzettingen*, the *onrust groeide into een spanning welke velen nauwelijks konden dragen*. In [[Mei]] [[1925]] the strength of some garrisons was *verminderd*; by [[October]] of that same year the *opstand uitbrak in het zuidelijke deel der nederzettingen* on the Westkust, on a much larger scale than the *publiek in Indië* ever knew. Holy books were read at night; the *[[hikajat prang]]* circulated from hand to hand. The *stilte* before the storm was itself a form of resistance.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1925** was the year of publication of the first *Bericht* on patchouli in Atjeh — *Bericht* No. 21 of May [[1925]] of the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum|Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]]. The *Bericht* recorded the earliest attempts at indigenous processing of patchouli leaves at [[Tapa Toean|Tapa Toean]] beginning in [[1921]]. The Atjeh patchouli-leaf export was 559 tons in [[1925]]. The Atjeh nilam-oil export was 760 kg in [[1925]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In **[[1925]]** resistance broke out again on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|west coast]] of Atjeh, seven years after Swart's retirement. Swart's opinion was sought in Holland and recorded by [[J. Schaap]] in *[[The Weekly of an Amsterdammer]]* — "Swart's Atjehsch testament". In [[1925]] [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] was acting *controleur* of [[Meulaboh]], where he examined the *onderstand* entries and held his own keutelige inspections; he found roads, bridges and the *keudé* neglected. [[R. Broersma|Dr. R. Broersma]]'s *Atjeh als land voor handel en bedrijf* appeared in [[1925]].

## Account from *Over patchouly-olie en patchouly-blad van Atjeh (1925)* — Bericht No. 21

In April [[1925]] the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum|Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] published, as *Bericht* No. 21 of its *Berichten* series, the pamphlet [[Over patchouly-olie en patchouly-blad van Atjeh (1925)|*Over patchouly-olie en patchouly-blad van Atjeh (Noord-Sumatra)*]] — reprinted from *[[De Indische Mercuur|De Indische Mercuur]]* of [[8 May|8]] and [[15 May]] [[1925]], and printed in Amsterdam by Druk De Bussy. The pamphlet, composed in the closing months of the four-year [[Atjeh patchouli experiments (1921-1925)|Atjeh patchouli experiments of 1921-1925]], is the founding document of the indigenous Atjeh patchouli-oil industry.

The Bericht records the discovery — made by [[Van Vreeswijk|Van Vreeswijk]], the manager of the [[Atjehsche Oliefabriek|Atjehsche Oliefabriek]] at [[Tapa Toean|Tapa Toean]], in his letter of [[16 August]] [[1924]] — that the patchouli (*nilam*) leaf of [[Bakongan|Bakongan]], grown on the sand-and-swamp conjunction of the Bakongan coastal strip and dried with the care the Bakongan planters had begun to devote to it, was the finest patchouli-leaf of Atjeh. The Bakongan leaf, when distilled by the European essential-oil houses over the winter of 1924-1925, produced oil uniformly judged equal in quality to the best Singapore-Penang product. The Handelsmuseum's verdict — "prima blad, prima olie; op de winning van het goede blad komt het aan!" — broke the Singapore-Penang monopoly on prime patchouli oil and established the indigenous Atjeh industry on a firm commercial basis.

## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1881

**[[1881]]** was the year of the completion of the [[Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] and of the solemn transfer of the mosque to the Acehnese people on [[27 December]] [[1881]] — in the name of the Dutch government — by the governor of Aceh and Dependencies, [[Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]]. The mosque — whose first stone was laid on [[9 October]] [[1879]] in the presence of Governor-General [[Van Lansberge]] — was completed in [[1881]] and on [[27 December]] [[1881]] was solemnly transferred to the Acehnese people.

The book immediately registers the Acehnese indifference to the solemn transfer: on the contrary, both during the construction and later, the Acehnese stood at his most indifferent towards it. Not one Acehnese made himself available as a workman at the construction, which was entirely performed by Chinese workforce — that is, by pig-eating unbelievers — and the work was bitterly mocked by the Acehnese. The [[1881]] solemn transfer was therefore received with indifference and mockery: the Acehnese people considered it the forced benefaction of their most unsympathetic unbeliever, the Hollander. [[Snouck Hurgronje]] would later call it "an empty monument of our folly."

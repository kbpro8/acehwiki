## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1881

**[[1881]]** was the year of the completion of the [[Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] and of the solemn transfer of the mosque to the Acehnese people on [[27 December]] [[1881]] — in the name of the Dutch government — by the governor of Aceh and Dependencies, [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|A. Pruys van der Hoeven]]. The mosque — whose first stone was laid on [[9 October]] [[1879]] in the presence of Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] — was completed in [[1881]] and on [[27 December]] [[1881]] was solemnly transferred to the Acehnese people.

The book immediately registers the Acehnese indifference to the solemn transfer: on the contrary, both during the construction and later, the Acehnese stood at his most indifferent towards it. Not one Acehnese made himself available as a workman at the construction, which was entirely performed by Chinese workforce — that is, by pig-eating unbelievers — and the work was bitterly mocked by the Acehnese. The [[1881]] solemn transfer was therefore received with indifference and mockery: the Acehnese people considered it the forced benefaction of their most unsympathetic unbeliever, the Hollander. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] would later call it "an empty monument of our folly."

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* (1923) — 1881

For Langhout 1881 was the year in which the war was believed ended: "de loop van zaken in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden leverde het bewijs, dat in 1881... de oorlog een einde genomen had", and that the time of pacification and restoration had arrived, "waarin het burgerlijk bestuur met andere middelen dan het zwaard naar onderwerping zou moeten streven". Van Lansberge wrote to Van der Heijden that "the crown was set upon the building" — the crown that proved not to fit: within three years (1881–[[1884]]) "welhaast alle behaalde voordeelen weer verloren gingen" ("almost all advantages gained were lost again"), and the building's walls could not bear the crown's weight "voor het zóóver was, moesten nog zeven en dertig jaren verloopen" — thirty-seven more years, until the definitive separation of [[1918]].

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

The year of the fateful turn for which [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] keeps the sharpest judgments of his pamphlet: on [[7 March]] the aged ulama [[Toengkoe Sjech Marabahan]] was received by van der Heyden in the presence of many chiefs and clergy; on [[11 March]] the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|van Lansberge]] signed the Kabinetsbrief that crowned what the pamphlet calls the political mistakes of the time — the introduction of the [[Normale bestuursinrichting (Atjeh, 1881-1884)|normale bestuursinrichting]] and the replacement of the single command of general [[Karel van der Heijden|van der Heyden]] by the two-headed government (see [[Establishment of civil government in Aceh (6 April 1881)]]). In April (Verstege places the introduction on the 9th) the general handed over the civil administration to [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] with the closing warning, "Groote voorzichtigheid blijft geboden" ("Great caution remains required"); the new governor still called the state "zeer bevredigend" in April and May, but already on [[22 May]] [[1881]] he ordered the patrols no longer to visit the kampongs, bound them to fixed roads, and forbade arresting armed Atjehers beyond a 500-metre ring around the bentings; soon afterwards patrolling was stopped altogether, posts were given up despite the populations' requests, the fight against robber bands was entrusted to a [[Gewapende Politie|gewapende politie]] (armed police) — and the "maraudeurs" grew into hostile bands resuming the guerilla war. On [[28 March]] the minister [[W. van Goltstein|van Goltstein]] declared to the Kamer that the conqueror's goal was reached and "that all guarantees were present that the war would not be resumed", though on the standpoint of the administrator the answer was less satisfying: the people of Groot-Atjeh, once appearing 800,000 souls, had sunk to 50,000 — as against van Swieten's 125,000 of [[1874]] and the official 575,000 of [[1883]], a figure put by Verstege among the proofs of how elastic the population statistics of Atjeh were. The year also stands in the pamphlet as the year of [[A. de Casembroot|preceding voices]] that were drowned.

## Civil Administration Experiment (1881–1884)

The period [[1881]]–[[1884]] was marked by the experiment of separate civil administration in [[Groot-Atjeh]], initiated after the recall of [[General van der Heijden]]. Contemporary observers, including the authors of the 1885 report on the geconcentreerde stelling, noted that the *bestuursverandering en wisseling van stelsel* (administrative changes and policy shifts) of this period were to be regretted. The civil *gouverneur* [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven]] attempted to govern through conciliation rather than military pressure, but the security situation continued to deteriorate. By [[1884]], the policy had failed and the concentration was ordered. The religious leadership, led by [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]], gained strength throughout this period.

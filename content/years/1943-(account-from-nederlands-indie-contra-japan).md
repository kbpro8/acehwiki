**The year 1943** was, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the year of the Japanese counter-operation against Lt [[H. van Zanten|lieutenant H. van Zanten]]'s post-capitulation resistance in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] — the year of the November [[1942]] arrests of fourteen Redelong soldiers, of the [[26 November 1942]] Japanese attempt on Van Zanten's bivouac, and of Van Zanten's [[10 March 1943]] capture, court-martial at [[Fort de Rock|Fort de Rock]] and execution. The year [[1943]] is the *terminus ad quem* of the Dutch post-capitulation resistance in Atjeh.

## The November 1942 – March 1943 Japanese counter-operation

The staff history records that in November [[1942]] the Japanese began to take the rolling-up of Lieutenant Van Zanten and his group seriously. On [[24 November 1942]] fourteen released soldiers working on the [[Redelong (theeonderneming)|Redelong tea estate]] were arrested and, after being bound, were subjected to Japanese mistreatment. On [[26 November 1942]] a Japanese force of about 100 men attempted to take Van Zanten's bivouac but was foiled by an informer; the bivouac was burned.

In February [[1943]] the European sergeant Andringa and two Maréchaussees were captured as a consequence of illness and exhaustion. The gradual collapse of the Van Zanten group's logistical and intelligence position continued through the early months of [[1943]].

## The 10 March 1943 capture and execution

On [[10 March 1943]], Lieutenant Van Zanten and the five Maréchaussees who were with him met that same fate, when they came out of the forest in order to forage and fell into an ambush. Hereafter Lieutenant Van Zanten and his Maréchaussees were atrociously mistreated and interrogated, in which the Japanese repeatedly promised the officer that his life would be spared if he would act as a spy for them in the prisoner-of-war camps — which he rejected. He was finally transported to Fort de Rock, where he was sentenced to death by a so-called court-martial. Lieutenant Van Zanten remained to the last, despite all humiliations and mistreatment, dignified and proud, fully understanding that he would be executed.

The date and place of execution are not given in the staff history; the entry closes the Van Zanten episode.

## Significance

The year [[1943]] is, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the *terminus ad quem* of the Dutch post-capitulation resistance in Atjeh, and the year of the formal Japanese closure of the Van Zanten episode. The staff history's emphasis on the Japanese counter-operation of November [[1942]]–March [[1943]], on the capture and execution of Van Zanten, and on his dignified and proud bearing in captivity frames [[1943]] as the year that extinguished the last organised Dutch military presence in Atjeh, and the year that closed the staff history's narrative of the Atjeh campaign.

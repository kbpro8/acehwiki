## Account from *Over Maleise Literatuur* (1947) — 1607

In [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s survey of Malay literature, the year [[1607]] is the year of the completion of the *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* — the Atjehnese-composed "mirror for princes" — and the year of the accession of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] to the throne of Atjeh. The two events together mark the threshold of the *bloeitijdperk* (golden age) of Atjehnese power and literary culture.

### The Completion of the Tādj us-Salāṭīn

The *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* — Arabic *Tāj as-Salāṭīn*, "Crown of Kings" (Malay *Mahkota Sēgala radja*) — was completed in Atjeh in [[1607]]. The work is, in Hooykaas's reading, "Arabisch van titel, Perzisch-Moslimsch van inhoud, Maleis van taal, met Atjeh als plaats van vervaardiging" (Arabic in title, Persian-Muslim in content, Malay in language, with Atjeh as place of manufacture) — a synthesis that exemplifies the literary cosmopolitanism of the court of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]]'s Atjeh. The work's completion in the year of Iskandar Muda's accession places it at the very threshold of the great Atjehnese seventeenth century.

### The Model for the Sadjarah Malajoe

The *Tādj us-Salāṭīn* of [[1607]] would, in Hooykaas's reading, serve as the model for the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* — the principal Malay chronicle of the Malacca-Johor sultanate, commissioned at [[Batoe Sawar]] in [[1612]]. The *Bendahara* of Johor — the author of the *Sadjarah Malajoe* — would have become acquainted with the *Tādj* during his Atjehnese captivity after the [[1613]] attack on Batoe Sawar: "de Bendahara zal [in Atjéh] hebben kennis gemaakt [met de Tādj oes-Salatina], alwaar het in 1607 werd voltooid" (the *Bendahara* will [in Atjeh] have become acquainted [with the Tādj us-Salāṭīn], where it was completed in 1607). The *Bendahara*'s own "Geslachtslijst der Soeltanen" (Genealogy of the Sultans) — the working title of the *Sadjarah Malajoe* — was modelled on the *Tādj*'s example.

### The Accession of Iskandar Muda

The accession of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] to the throne of Atjeh in [[1607]] opens the *bloeitijdperk* (golden age) of Atjehnese power ([[1607]]-[[1636]]) — the period of Atjehnese naval dominance in the Strait of Malacca, of the [[1613]] and [[1615]] attacks on [[Batoe Sawar]], of the conquest of [[Pasai]], and of the literary culture that produced the *Tādj us-Salāṭīn* and, later, the *[[Bustan al-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]* of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|ar-Rānīrī]] ([[1638]]).

*Source: Over Maleise Literatuur*, by Dr. C. Hooykaas, Hoogleraarte Batavia; Tweede Druk (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1947), chapter 19 ("Twee Moslimse exemplo-boeken als vorstenspiegel"), pp. 165-173; Toelichting tot de Sadjarah Malajoe, aantekening bij de inleiding van de schrijver, p. 205.

## Account from *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*

[[Sultan Iskandar Moeda]] took power at age 17. During his reign, conquered half of Sumatra's coast and half of [[Malay Peninsula|Malacca peninsula]]. Organized/introduced the '[[Nanggroe / Familie-stelsel (institution)|nanggroe]]' ([[Nanggroe / Familie-stelsel (institution)|family system]]) in Atjeh.

*Source: J. Jongejans, Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu (Baarn: Hollandia Drukkerij N.V., c. 1938), pp. 26.*

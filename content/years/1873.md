**[[1873]]** was the year of the first Dutch expedition to [[Atjeh]], which ended in failure. Despite this failure, the resistance demonstrated by the [[XXII Moekims]]—even before Dutch troops entered their territory—established the pattern of sustained opposition that would characterise the thirty-year conflict. Later in the same year, a second expedition under [[Generaal Van Swieten]] achieved greater success and eventually captured the [[kraton]] in [[January 1874]].

## Key events

- The first Dutch expedition to [[Atjeh]] fails.
- The inhabitants of the [[XXII Moekims]]—led by [[Sri Moeda Perkasa]] ([[Panglima Polim]])—resist the Dutch from within their own territory, demonstrating that resistance did not require direct confrontation.
- The second expedition, under [[Generaal Van Swieten]], achieves greater success and captures the [[kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]].
- The young sultan dies two days after the capture ([[26 January]] [[1874]]).

The events of [[1873]] established the fundamental strategic challenge of the war: the Dutch could occupy the capital and declare authority over the three [[sagi]], but the distributed resistance—particularly in the [[XXII Moekims]]—would persist for decades.

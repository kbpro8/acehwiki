## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1873

**[[1873]]** was the year of the first Dutch military expedition against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] — the campaign that ended the formal authority of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] and inaugurated the protracted conflict known as the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]], which would — with intermittent pauses — rage for the next thirty years and produce the colonial [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] that the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* describes. The year [[1873]] thus stands as the terminus of the pre-colonial order in Atjeh and the starting-point of the colonial order.

## The first expedition

The first Dutch military expedition against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in [[1873]] was the culmination of a long diplomatic dispute over Atjehsche sovereignty that had pitted the Netherlands against the interests of other European powers — particularly the United Kingdom and the United States — that had begun to court the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]]. The Dutch response, in [[1873]], was military: an expeditionary force sent against the Sultan's capital. The campaign, which ended with the death of the Sultan — succeeded, in the same year, by a new Sultan who would lead the resistance — established the pattern of the conflict that would follow.

## The Aceh War

The [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] that followed [[1873]] was, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, a *hardnekkige godsdienst-oorlog* — a stubborn religious war — that, "door een telkens te snel de overhand krijgend pacifisme der moederlandsche Regeering herhaaldelijk weer den kop op kon steken" — by a pacifism of the motherland's government that too quickly gained the upper hand, could repeatedly raise its head again. With some quieter intervals, the war raged through the last thirty years of the nineteenth century. The Dutch army's losses — recorded in Bijlage V of the handbook — began in [[1897]], the year for which the first systematic statistics are given, but the cumulative losses of the preceding twenty-four years were substantial.

## The transition to Gouvernement

The formal establishment of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in [[1874]] — the year following the first expedition — formalised the transition from ad hoc military occupation to the institutional structure of a colonial administrative unit, with [[J. L. J. H. Pel|J. L. J. H. Pel]] appointed as its first Military and Civil Commander. The year [[1873]], however, remains the symbolic beginning of the colonial era in Atjeh: the moment at which the Sultanate's formal authority was extinguished and the long process of military subjugation and administrative consolidation began.

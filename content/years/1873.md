## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1873


**[[1873]]** was the year of the first Dutch military expedition against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] — the campaign that ended the formal authority of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] and inaugurated the protracted conflict known as the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]], which would — with intermittent pauses — rage for the next thirty years and produce the colonial [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] that the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* describes. The year [[1873]] thus stands as the terminus of the pre-colonial order in Atjeh and the starting-point of the colonial order.

The first Dutch military expedition against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in [[1873]] was the culmination of a long diplomatic dispute over Atjehsche sovereignty that had pitted the Netherlands against the interests of other European powers — particularly the United Kingdom and the United States — that had begun to court the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]]. The Dutch response, in [[1873]], was military: an expeditionary force sent against the Sultan's capital. The campaign, which ended with the death of the Sultan — succeeded, in the same year, by a new Sultan who would lead the resistance — established the pattern of the conflict that would follow.

The [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] that followed [[1873]] was a *hardnekkige godsdienst-oorlog* — a stubborn religious war — that, "door een telkens te snel de overhand krijgend pacifisme der moederlandsche Regeering herhaaldelijk weer den kop op kon steken." With some quieter intervals, the war raged through the last thirty years of the nineteenth century. The formal establishment of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in [[1874]] — the year following the first expedition — formalised the transition from ad hoc military occupation to the institutional structure of a colonial administrative unit.

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau also identifies [[1873]] as the year of the "beginning of the [[Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Atjeh-expeditie]]" in the context of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] history. The Bureau's account dates the construction of the first Atjeh railway — the small spoorbaantje between [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and [[Kota Radja]] — to "[[1875]]," that is, to two years after the [[1873]] beginning of the Atjeh-expeditie: "the first line was already laid shortly after the beginning of the [[Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Atjeh-expeditie]], when in [[1875]] the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]] had a small railway built between the roadstead of [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and our principal military station at [[Kota Radja]]." The Bureau's framing of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" places the [[1873]] Atjeh-expeditie at the origin of the entire 41-year arc of Dutch military and administrative engagement with Atjeh that the volume surveys.


## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1873


**[[1873]]** was the year of the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|First Aceh Expedition]] and of the historic storming of the [[Meusigit Raja|mosque]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] by the Dutch troops in [[April 1873|April]], when the building burned down and later was completely demolished. The storming of the mosque — the First Aceh Expedition — is the marker of the opening of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]: the colonial army's storming of the mosque that the Acehnese population had built on the place of the first mosque of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]]. The aftermath of the [[1873]] storming — the [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]] letter of [[8 February]] [[1874]] and the rebuilding of the [[Meusigit Raja]] — is the marker of the colonial state's religious politics in [[Atjeh|Aceh]].


## Account from *De geschiedenis van Nederland in onzen tijd* (1912) — 1873


**[[1873]]** was the year in which the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] began, marked by the [[Aceh Envoys' Visit to Singapore (January 1873)|Acehnese envoys' visit to Singapore]] in January, the Dutch declaration of war in March, the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of April-May, the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] of November-December, and the [[Bombardment of Pedir (29-31 December 1873)|bombardment of Pedir]] at the end of December. The year also saw the [[Expedition to Deli (1872)|continuation of operations in Deli]] on Sumatra's east coast, the appointment of [[A. W. P. Weitzel|A. W. P. Weitzel]] as Minister of War on [[6 October]] [[6 October]], and the rejection of the budget of Marine on [[11 December]] [[11 December]] which led to the discharge of the Minister of Marine [[Brocx|Brocx]].

### January-March: The Outbreak

In [[January 1873|January 1873]] the Acehnese envoys, who had left [[Riouw|Riouw]] for [[Singapore|Singapore]] on [[25 January]] [[25 January]] [1873], were reported by the Consul-General [[W. H. Read|W. H. Read]] in his telegram of [[16 February]] [[16 February]] [[1873]] to have sought the help of the American and Italian consuls against the Dutch. The telegram of the Minister of Colonies of [[18 February]] [[18 February]] [[1873]] authorised the dispatch of a powerful naval force. On [[1 March]] [[1 March]] [1873] the commissioner [[F. N. Nieuwenhuysen|F. N. Nieuwenhuysen]] departed from Batavia; on [[22 March]] [[22 March]] [1873] the expedition sailed from Batavia and on [[5 April]] [[5 April]] [1873] it arrived at Atjeh.

### April-May: The First Aceh Expedition

The [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[April 1873|April-May 1873]] was a failure. The landing on [[8 April]] [[8 April]] [1873] west of the mouth of the Atjeh river immediately presented more difficulties than expected. The [[Battle of Kotta Mogat|échec at Kotta Mogat]] on [[8 April]] [[8 April]] [1873] was followed by the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|battle of the missigit]] on [[10 April]] [[10 April]] [1873] and the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]] on [[14 April]] [[14 April]] [1873]. The council of war of [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [1873] decided against renewed attack; on [[29 April]] [[29 April]] [1873] the transport fleet left the roadstead of Atjeh.

### June-October: Preparations

The Indies government immediately put in hand all that was necessary to prepare the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]]. In the Netherlands troops were levied to bring the army up to strength; all troops were equipped with breech-loading rifles; the artillery was reinforced with newer breech-loading guns from Europe. The expeditionary force, 6354 infantry besides officers, with cavalry, artillery and engineers, was placed under the retired Lieutenant-general [[J. van Swieten|J. van Swieten]], restored to active service. After delays caused by cholera on Java and aboard the transports, the transport ships sailed in mid-November.

### November-December: The Second Expedition

The [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] arrived at the Atjeh roadstead between [[20 November]] [[20 November]] and [[29 November]] [[29 November]] [[1873]]. The landing on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [1873] east of the Atjeh river met almost no resistance. On [[13 December]] [[13 December]] [[Kota Moesapi|Kota Moesapi]] was occupied; on [[14 December]] [[14 December]] the long line of enemy fortifications south of Kota Moesapi was taken by the storming of Tiban by General [[G. M. Verspyck|Verspyck]]. On [[15 December]] [[15 December]] the mouth of the Atjeh river was reached. The [[Bombardment of Pedir (29-31 December 1873)|bombardment of Pedir]] followed on [[29 December]] [[29 December]] – [[31 December]] [[31 December]] [1873].

### Other Events in the Netherlands

In the Netherlands the year [[1873]] saw the periodic elections in which the liberal party suffered several defeats, as at Dokkum, Deventer, Almelo, and Amsterdam, and the anti-revolutionary party gained strength. The budget of Marine was rejected on [[11 December]] [[11 December]] [1873] by 37 to 30 votes, leading to the discharge of the Minister of Marine [[Brocx|Brocx]], whose portfolio was entrusted ad interim to the Minister of Colonies. On [[6 October]] [[6 October]] [1873] the new Minister of War, [[A. W. P. Weitzel|A. W. P. Weitzel]], took up his function. On Sumatra's east coast the [[Expedition to Deli (1872)|expedition to Deli]] of [[May 1872|May 1872]] had been brought to a close by the end of October [[1872]]; by decree of [[15 May]] [[15 May]] [[1873]] a reorganisation of the administration of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's Oostkust]] was carried out.


## Account from *Snijders* (1944) — 1873


**[[1873]]** was the year in which [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede Luitenant C. J. Snijders]] of the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs]], then serving at the *Eerste Stelling der Genie* at [[Utrecht]], volunteered for the [[Nederlands-Indisch Leger|Nederlands-Indisch Leger]] detachment being raised for the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] — and, in the second half of the year, made the seven-and-a-half-week voyage from [[Nieuwediep]] to [[Batavia]] aboard the steamship *Conrad*.

After the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] under [[J. H. R. Köhler|Generaal-Majoor Köhler]] in [[April 1873|April 1873]], the Dutch government authorised the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] and called for volunteer officers from the metropolitan army for a two-year detachment to the [[Nederlands-Indisch Leger|Nederlands-Indisch Leger]]. [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] heard of the call at the Utrecht society *Sic Semper* from a senior comrade and immediately applied. His father consented only on condition that Snijders not request assignment to a combat column in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — a promise Snijders honoured, though [[J. Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]] later pressed him to do so.

By *Koninklijk Besluit* of [[10 June]] [[10 June]] [[1873]] No. 33, Snijders was selected among four engineer officers for the detachment. Before departure he was briefly instructed at [[Zeist]] in a new optical telegraph system devised by Majoor-Ingenieur J. H. Kromhout, though the navy's system was ultimately used in the field. He embarked on the steamship *Conrad* (Nederland Line) from [[Nieuwediep]] on [[10 July]] [[10 July]] [[1873]]; the ship carried a reinforcement detachment of 400 men and numerous officers, and was inspected by [[Prince Hendrik]] before sailing. After storm damage in the Bay of Biscay forced a ten-day repair stop at [[Gibraltar]] — where the Dutch officers mingled with the British garrison and visited [[Tangier]] — the *Conrad* proceeded via [[Port Said]] and [[Aden]], reaching [[Batavia]] on [[31 August]] [[31 August]] [[1873]].

From Batavia, Snijders was briefly employed at the *Hoofdbureau der Genie* in [[Weltevreden]] drafting a memorandum on the Kromhout telegraph, then transferred to the *Staf der Genie* at [[Surabaya]], arriving [[24 September]] [[24 September]] [[1873]]. He would spend a year in [[Surabaya]] on bureau duties and on the supervision of extensions to the *Artillerie-Conditionement*, the *Pyrotechnische Werkplaatsen*, and the *Militair Hospitaal* before his eventual transfer to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[October 1874|October]] [[1874]].

On Sumatra's east coast the [[Expedition to Deli (1872)|expedition to Deli]] of [[May 1872|May 1872]] had been brought to a close by the end of October [[1872]]; by decree of [[15 May]] [[15 May]] [[1873]] a reorganisation of the administration of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's Oostkust]] was carried out.


## Account from *Snijders* (1944) — 1873


**[[1873]]** was the year in which [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede Luitenant C. J. Snijders]] of the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs]], then serving at the *Eerste Stelling der Genie* at [[Utrecht]], volunteered for the [[Nederlands-Indisch Leger|Nederlands-Indisch Leger]] detachment being raised for the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] — and, in the second half of the year, made the seven-and-a-half-week voyage from [[Nieuwediep]] to [[Batavia]] aboard the steamship *Conrad*.

After the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] under [[J. H. R. Köhler|Generaal-Majoor Köhler]] in [[April 1873|April 1873]], the Dutch government authorised the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] and called for volunteer officers from the metropolitan army for a two-year detachment to the [[Nederlands-Indisch Leger|Nederlands-Indisch Leger]]. [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] heard of the call at the Utrecht society *Sic Semper* from a senior comrade and immediately applied. His father consented only on condition that Snijders not request assignment to a combat column in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — a promise Snijders honoured, though [[J. Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]] later pressed him to do so.

By *Koninklijk Besluit* of [[10 June]] [[10 June]] [[1873]] No. 33, Snijders was selected among four engineer officers for the detachment. Before departure he was briefly instructed at [[Zeist]] in a new optical telegraph system devised by Majoor-Ingenieur J. H. Kromhout, though the navy's system was ultimately used in the field. He embarked on the steamship *Conrad* (Nederland Line) from [[Nieuwediep]] on [[10 July]] [[10 July]] [[1873]]; the ship carried a reinforcement detachment of 400 men and numerous officers, and was inspected by [[Prince Hendrik]] before sailing. After storm damage in the Bay of Biscay forced a ten-day repair stop at [[Gibraltar]] — where the Dutch officers mingled with the British garrison and visited [[Tangier]] — the *Conrad* proceeded via [[Port Said]] and [[Aden]], reaching [[Batavia]] on [[31 August]] [[31 August]] [[1873]].

From Batavia, Snijders was briefly employed at the *Hoofdbureau der Genie* in [[Weltevreden]] drafting a memorandum on the Kromhout telegraph, then transferred to the *Staf der Genie* at [[Surabaya]], arriving [[24 September]] [[24 September]] [[1873]]. He would spend a year in [[Surabaya]] on bureau duties and on the supervision of extensions to the *Artillerie-Conditionement*, the *Pyrotechnische Werkplaatsen*, and the *Militair Hospitaal* before his eventual transfer to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[October 1874|October]] [[1874]].


## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1873

### The first Dutch expedition against Atjeh

The [[1873]] Dutch expedition against the Atjeh sultanate was the first of two major military operations mounted by the Netherlands Indies colonial state against Atjeh in [[1873]]–[[1874]]. The first expedition (March [[1873]]) ended in a Dutch withdrawal after the death of its commander, General Köhler; the second expedition (December [[1873]]–January [[1874]]) succeeded in capturing the [[Kota Radja|sultan's capital]] (the *Dalam*, the sultan's palace) in [[1874]]. The Atjeh sultan, Mahmud Syah, withdrew to the interior and continued the resistance until his death in [[1874]]; the resistance itself continued, in the form of guerrilla warfare, until approximately [[1904]].

The Atjeh War is not described in detail in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]), which is concerned with the mineral economy of the East Indies rather than with its political-military history. The War is, however, the indispensable political-military context for the colonial mineralogical record of Atjeh: the Residency of Atjeh en Onderhorigheden was founded in the immediate aftermath of the [[1873]]–[[1874]] expedition, and the colonial geological surveys of Atjeh — beginning with Rinaud's [[1885]] coal survey — were accordingly conducted in a military-frontier context.

### Significance for Atjeh's colonial mineral record

[[1873]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of the first Dutch colonial expedition against the Atjeh sultanate — the founding military episode of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]] that ended with the dissolution of the Atjeh sultanate and the creation of the colonial Residency of [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]]. The [[1873]] expedition is the political-military context within which the entire colonial mineralogical record of Atjeh was compiled: the Residency's founding in the immediate aftermath of the [[1873]]–[[1874]] expedition established the administrative framework within which the colonial geological surveys of Atjeh were conducted, and the gradual opening-up of the Atjeh interior to colonial scientific field-work over the decades between [[1873]] and [[1904]] determined the chronological and geographical pattern of the printed mineralogical record.

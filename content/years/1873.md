**[[1873]]** was the pivotal year of the outbreak of the [[Aceh War]], marked by the failed [[First Aceh Expedition]] and the launch of the [[Second Aceh Expedition]]. Despite the first expedition's failure, the resistance demonstrated by the [[XXII Moekims]]—even before Dutch troops entered their territory—established the pattern of sustained opposition that would characterise the thirty-year conflict. Later in the same year, a second expedition under [[Jan van Swieten|Generaal Van Swieten]] achieved greater success and would eventually capture the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] in [[January]] [[1874]].

## Chronology

- **[[15 February]]–[[16 February]]**: Dutch Consul-General W. Read reports secret diplomatic negotiations between Acehnese envoy [[Panglima Tiban Mohammad]] and American and Italian consuls in Singapore.
- **[[26 March]]**: Dutch Commissioner [[Frederik Nieuwenhuijzen]] issues an official Declaration of War against [[Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX]].
- **[[8 April]]**: [[Landing at Pante Cermin]] by Dutch forces under Major-General [[Johan Harmen Rudolf Kohler]].
- **[[10 April]]**: [[First Capture of Mesjid Raya]]; Dutch forces briefly storm the [[Mesjid Raya Aceh]] before the burning roof forces withdrawal.
- **[[14 April]]**: [[Second Capture of Mesjid Raya]]; General Köhler is shot dead by an Acehnese sniper.
- **[[16 April]]**: [[Attack on the Kraton]] fails; Dutch forces suffer severe casualties and initiate retreat to Java.
- **[[11 June]]**: General [[Jan van Swieten]] appointed Supreme Military Commander for the second invasion force.
- **[[9 December]]**: [[Landing at Pedro Point]] initiates the [[Second Aceh Expedition]].
- **[[14 December]]**: [[Battle of the Tiban Line]]; Dutch columns breach Acehnese coastal earthworks.
- **[[18 December]]**: [[Capture of Peunayong]] secures the river supply line; state archives destroyed in fire at [[Teuku Kadli]]'s home.
- **[[25 December]]–[[26 December]]**: [[Battles of Lembu]]; Dutch forces capture Acehnese redoutes at [[Lembu]].

## Strategic significance

The inhabitants of the [[XXII Moekims]]—led by [[Sri Moeda Perkasa]] ([[Panglima Polim]])—resisted from within their own territory, demonstrating that resistance did not require direct confrontation with the colonial army. The events of [[1873]] established the fundamental strategic challenge of the war: the Dutch could occupy the capital and declare authority over the three [[sagi]], but the distributed resistance—particularly in the [[XXII Moekims]]—would persist for decades.

## East Coast context

[[1873]] was also the year of intensified naval blockade on the east coast, where [[J. A. Kruyt]] cruised with the warship *Timor*. The Dutch government's push for war was partly motivated by persistent piracy that had long disturbed order in Sumatran and Malacca waters. On the east coast, pepper exports of 5,000–6,000 *kojang* at [[Penang]] fetched 200–250 *pilaardollars* per *kojang* in August [[1873]].

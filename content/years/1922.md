## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1922

### The Zwierzycki 1:1,000,000 geological map and coal inventory

For the full account of Zwierzycki's [[1922]] contributions see the entry on [[J. Zwierzycki]]. In summary: the 1:1,000,000 geological map (sheet I, covering Atjeh and the West-Atjeh coast) became the standard cartographic basis for all subsequent colonial-era geological work on the region, and is reproduced (in adapted form) as Fig. 28 of [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] under the title "Basin of [[Meulaboh]] (West coast of Atjeh)". The [[1922]] inventory of Sumatran coal localities, which Van Bemmelen reproduces with only minor additions as Table 13 of the coal chapter, is the canonical record of Atjeh coal occurrences — including the East-Atjeh Paleogene localities (Nos. 1–4), the West-Atjeh Paleogene localities (Nos. 13–15, drawn from [[Rinaud]] [[1885]]), and the West-Atjeh Neogene localities (Nos. 16–19).

### The Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp Atjeh survey

For the full account of the Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp [[1922]] survey see the entry on [[P. J. J. Jansen]]. In summary: the survey covered the primary gold deposits of the Atjeh interior (with the [[S. Assai]] 11 gr Au/ton and [[S. Meranging]] 10.8 gr Au/ton assays from 35 quartz-vein samples), the iron-ore localities of the Residency of Atjeh ([[Kr. Geuenteuet]], [[Gle Mon Ampeuet]], [[Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)|Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)]], [[Kr. Rigaih]], [[Alu Talu]], [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]], [[A. Pentuwa Gade]] and [[Kuto Buloh]]), the copper occurrences of Atjeh (Pulu Bras, Gle Broë, Aer Talu, Beutong, Aloe Baru, Aer Si Hajo, Muara Sipongi), and the lead-zinc assays at [[Krueng Heureueng]] (76.1 % Pb, 920 gr/t Ag) and [[Krueng Isep]] (65.13 % Pb, 680 gr/t Ag). The Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp survey is the most ambitious single mineralogical survey of Atjeh undertaken during the colonial period.

### The 1920 reserve estimate

The [[1922]] survey also records the [[1920]] reserve estimate for the [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] iron-ore occurrence: 2,600,000 tons of ore with an average of 58–64 % Fe — the largest documented colonial-era iron-ore reserve on Atjeh territory, "suspended on account of low market-prices". The [[1920]] reserve estimate, though technically predating the [[1922]] survey, is recorded in the printed literature only through the Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp [[1922]] report, and it accordingly forms part of the [[1922]] documentary record.

### Significance

[[1922]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of three of the most important colonial-era contributions to the geological and mineralogical record of Atjeh: Zwierzycki's 1:1,000,000 geological map and coal inventory, the Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp mineralogical survey, and (through the Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp page-references) Hövig's iron-ore report. The combination of these three contributions in a single year marks [[1922]] as the high-point of the colonial geological survey of Atjeh — the moment at which the printed record of Atjeh's mineral geography was consolidated into the form in which it would be transmitted, via [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]), to the post-colonial readership.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1922** was the year of the reconstitution of the *Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden|Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden* with chief place [[Takengon|Takengon]], comprising the *onderafdeelingen* [[Takengon|Takéngon]], [[Gajoe Loeös|Gajoe Loeös]], [[Serbödjadi|Serbödjadi]] and [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]]. The *afdeeling* [[Pidië|Pidië]] was detached from the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh*. The [[Verbanning van drie hoofden uit Lho Seumawe (1922)|verbanning van drie hoofden uit Lho Seumawe]] — the banishment of three chiefs of [[Lho Seumawe|Lho' Seumawe]] — was the colonial government's intervention against the [[Sarikat Islam in Atjeh (1918-1922)|Sarikat Islam movement]]. The [[Atjesch internaat|Atjesch internaat]] was founded at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. The [[Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam|Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam]] gave up the estate [[Boeloeh Blang Ara|Boeloeh Blang Ara]] as failed, after spending eight tons of gold on it. The *begrooting* of the associated *landschappen* of the *afdeeling* Pidië for [[1923]] was prepared.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

On [[4 January]] **[[1922]]**, forty-two years after entering service on [[16 October]] [[1880]], [[H. N. A. Swart]] was honourably discharged at his own request from the service of the State, with renewed thanks for his many and weighty services, ending his tenure as Vice-President of the [[Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]].

**[[1663]]** was the year of the [[Painansch Contract]] — the "short and eternal bond" (*kort en eeuwig verbont*) sealed at [[Batavia|Batavia]] on [[6 July]] [[1663]] between the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] and the landheeren of [[Indrapoera]], [[Painan]], [[Padang]] and [[Tikoe|Tikoe(s)]], by which the Menangkabau rulers of the [[Westkust van Sumatra|West Coast of Sumatra]] placed themselves under the Compagnie's protection and granted it a commercial monopoly in defiance of [[Atjeh]]'s claims to suzerainty. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* it is the pivot year of the Westkust story, the moment at which the Compagnie's policy shifted from co-operation with Atjeh to active subversion of Atjeh's sovereignty.

## The Painansch Contract

The central event of the year is the [[Painansch Contract|Painansch Contract]] of [[6 July]] [[1663]]. The definitive *accoort en eeuwig verbont* ("accord and eternal alliance") was ratified in Batavia, bearing the signature of [[Joan Maetsuycker|Joan Maetsuycker]], the [[Governor-General of the Dutch East Indies|Governor-General]]. The contract bound together a coalition of west-coast states including [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]], [[Painan]], [[Padang]], [[Sillida|Salido]], [[Kotta Tengah|Cotta Tenga]], [[Priaman]], [[Tikoe]] and [[Salido|Salida]].

Stapel records it as the harvest of the secret diplomacy of [[Jan van Groenewegen|Jan van Groenewegen]] in [[1662]]. The Compagnie is no longer treating the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|sultane of Atjeh]] as a co-operative partner; it is actively working to detach her Westkust vassals from "the croone of Atchin" (*de croone van Atchin*).

The Compagnie's chosen centre for the new arrangement was "Poeloe Tjingkoek in the bay of Painan" (*Poeloe Tjingkoek in de baai van Painan*).

## The simultaneous Atjeh and Perak withdrawals

In the same year [[1663]] the Compagnie also withdrew its factories from Atjeh and [[Perak]]. "That the Compagnie in 1663 proceeded to the lifting of her offices in Atjeh and Perak — see also under Malakka — increased the unrest and fear among the West Coast inhabitants, especially those of [[Tikoe]] and [[Priaman]], which lay closest to Atjeh" (*Dat de Compagnie in 1663 overging tot lichting harer kantoren in Atjeh en Perak — zie ook onder Malakka — vermeerderde de onrust en vrees onder de Westkustbewoners, vooral die van Tikoe en Priaman, welke het dichtst bij Atjeh lagen*).

The simultaneous withdrawal from Atjeh and Perak was the operational counterpart of the Painansch Contract: the Compagnie was abandoning its direct presence in the Atjehsche and Perak courts in favour of a Westkust-centred strategy.

## The Perak withdrawal

The Perak factory was lifted in [[1663]] in response to persistent Malay evasion of the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (5 October 1659)|1659 Perak settlement]]. "When however those of Perak in the execution of this agreement made all kinds of chicanes, the government lifted in 1663 also the factory in this place; 'the trade of Perak shall henceforth be driven on board'" (*Toen echter die van Perak bij de uitvoering dezer overeenkomst allerlei chicanes maakten, lichtte de regeering in 1663 ook het kantoor in deze plaats; 'de handel van Perak sal voortaen aen boord gedreven worden'*).

## Significance

1663 is the pivot year of the Westkust story. The Painansch Contract of [[6 July]] is the legal foundation of the Westkust system; the simultaneous withdrawals from Atjeh and Perak are the operational counterpart; and the secret diplomacy of [[Jan van Groenewegen|Van Groenewegen]] in [[1662]] is the political preparation. After [[1663]] the Dutch-Acehnese relationship is no longer one of co-operation, however strained, but one of open competition for the Westkust — a competition that would be settled, by the [[Tikoe submission (20-21 June 1684)|Tikoe submission of 1684]], in the Compagnie's favour.

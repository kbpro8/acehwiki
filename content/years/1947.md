**[[1947]]** was the year of publication of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac — Palaver around the table with the hole — Toekan-Serie no. 2, edited by G. L. Tichelman, published by De Gulden Pers in Haarlem. The book is the source of every entry in this AcehWiki corpus that draws on Merac.

## The publication

*[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* — Palaver around the table with the hole — was published in [[1947]] by De Gulden Pers in Haarlem, as volume 2 of the *Toekan-Serie* edited by G. L. Tichelman (Former Administrative Official). The book is a collection of small histories — written in the period between the First and Second World Wars, which the author describes as tales of "the old time, which is always said to be good and to which we look back with a mild smile of melancholy."

## The post-colonial context

[[1947]] was two years after the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese surrender]] of [[August 1945|August]] [[1945]] and during the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution. The book — written in [[1947]], in the middle of the revolution — looks back on "tempo doeloe" with a mild smile of melancholy. The book's Aceh content — the Aceh murders, the soldiers' chaplain [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]], the [[Meusigit Raja]], the [[Gajoweg]], the [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|transport-elephants]] — is the inter-war colonial memory of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], told in the middle of the revolution that would end the colonial state.

## Significance

[[1947]] is significant as the year of publication of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* — the source of the entries in this AcehWiki corpus. The book's argument — that the *tempo doeloe* of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] was the good old time to which the inter-war colonial officers looked back with a mild smile of melancholy — is the book's — and the colonial memory's — marker of the inter-war colonial memory of Aceh. The year [[1947]] is therefore the book's marker of its own post-colonial position: a *tempo doeloe* memory, written in the middle of the revolution that would end the colonial state.

## Account from *Over Maleise Literatuur* (1947) — 1947

The year [[1947]] was also the year of publication of the second edition of *[[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|Over Maleise Literatuur]]* — "On Malay Literature" — by **Dr. C. Hooykaas**, *Hoogleraarte Batavia* (Professor at Batavia), published by E. J. Brill in Leiden. The work is a synthetic survey of the whole field of Malay literature, and is a key secondary source for the literary-historical connections between the Malay-speaking world and the sultanate of [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

### The Publication

*[[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|Over Maleise Literatuur]]* — the second edition — was published in [[1947]] by E. J. Brill in Leiden, with the copyright statement "Copyright 1947 E. Brill, Leyden Holland. All rights reserved including the right to translate or to reproduce this book or parts thereof in any form. Printed in the Netherlands." The book is the work of three years of synthesis — Hooykaas notes, in his introduction, that "Verandering in mijn doceertaak heeft mij genoopt in de vrije tijd van slechts drie jaar dit boek samen te stellen" (A change in my teaching duties compelled me to compile this book in the free time of only three years).

### The Post-Colonial Context

The publication of *Over Maleise Literatuur* in [[1947]] — like the publication of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac in the same year — falls in the middle of the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution. The book is a *tempo doeloe* synthesis, written in the middle of the revolution that would end the colonial state, and looks back on the Malay literary tradition — including the Atjehnese-Malay literary exchange of the seventeenth century — with the synthetic gaze of a scholar who had "nooit op Sumatra of Malakka kon verblijven" (never been able to stay on Sumatra or Malacca).

### The Atjehnese Content

The book's significance for the AcehWiki corpus lies in the density of its Atjeh-related material — including the treatment of the *[[Hikajat Sultan Atjeh Marhoem]]*, the *[[Hikajat Malém Dagang]]*, the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]*, the *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]*, the *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Boestan oes-Salāṭīn]]*, the *[[Sirat al-mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]*, the [[Hikajat Malém Diwa|Atjehnese *Hikajat Malém Diwa*]], the [[Pasai]] sultanate, the [[Hikajat Raja-Raja Pasai]], the Atjehnese folk-tale tradition, and the genealogy of the sultans of Malacca and Djohor.

*Source: Over Maleise Literatuur*, by Dr. C. Hooykaas, Hoogleraarte Batavia; Tweede Druk (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1947), Inleiding, pp. VII-XII.

**[[1947]]** was the year of publication of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac — Palaver around the table with the hole — Toekan-Serie no. 2, edited by G. L. Tichelman, published by De Gulden Pers in Haarlem. The book is the source of every entry in this AcehWiki corpus that draws on Merac.

## The publication

*[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* — Palaver around the table with the hole — was published in [[1947]] by De Gulden Pers in Haarlem, as volume 2 of the *Toekan-Serie* edited by G. L. Tichelman (Former Administrative Official). The book is a collection of small histories — written in the period between the First and Second World Wars, which the author describes as tales of "the old time, which is always said to be good and to which we look back with a mild smile of melancholy."

## The post-colonial context

[[1947]] was two years after the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese surrender]] of [[August 1945|August]] [[1945]] and during the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution. The book — written in [[1947]], in the middle of the revolution — looks back on "tempo doeloe" with a mild smile of melancholy. The book's Aceh content — the Aceh murders, the soldiers' chaplain [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]], the [[Meusigit Raja]], the [[Gajoweg]], the [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|transport-elephants]] — is the inter-war colonial memory of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], told in the middle of the revolution that would end the colonial state.

## Significance

[[1947]] is significant as the year of publication of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* — the source of the entries in this AcehWiki corpus. The book's argument — that the *tempo doeloe* of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] was the good old time to which the inter-war colonial officers looked back with a mild smile of melancholy — is the book's — and the colonial memory's — marker of the inter-war colonial memory of Aceh. The year [[1947]] is therefore the book's marker of its own post-colonial position: a *tempo doeloe* memory, written in the middle of the revolution that would end the colonial state.

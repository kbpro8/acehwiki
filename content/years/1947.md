**1947** was the year of publication of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* — Palaver around the table with the hole — volume 2 of the *Toekan-Serie* edited by [[G. L. Tichelman]], and also the year in which a [[Koninklijk Besluit]] dated [[February]] [[1947]] awarded the *[[Bronzenn Kruis]]* (Bronze Cross) to *[[Lieutenant‑colonel K. M. R. W. A. van Heuven|Lieutenant‑colonel K. M. R. W. A. van Heuven]] for distinguished conduct during the battles near [[Grisseh]] and [[Lamongan]] in [[March]] [[1942]]. Both events occurred amid the [[1945]]‑[[1949]] Indonesian revolution, a period that shaped their post‑colonial significance.

## Publications in 1947

### *Oost‑Indisch Journael* (Palaver around the table with the hole)

*[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* was published in [[1947]] by De Gulden Pers in Haarlem as volume 2 of the *Toekan-Serie*, edited by [[G. L. Tichelman]] (former administrative official). The work is a collection of small histories written between the First and Second World Wars; the author describes them as tales of “the old time, which is always said to be good and to which we look back with a mild smile of melancholy.” Its Aceh content—[[Aceh murders]], the soldiers’ chaplain [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]], the [[Meusigit Raja]], the [[Gajoweg]], and the [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|transport‑elephants]]—constitutes the inter‑war colonial memory of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]].

### *Over Maleise Literatuur* (second edition)

The second edition of *[[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|Over Maleise Literatuur]]* (“On Malay Literature”) was published in [[1947]] by E. J. Brill in Leiden. Authored by **Dr. C. Hooykaas**, Hoogleraarte Batavia, the volume is a synthetic survey of Malay literature and a key secondary source for the literary‑historical connections between the Malay‑speaking world and the sultanate of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. Hooykaas notes in his introduction that “Verandering in mijn doceertaak heeft mij genoopt in de vrije tijd van slechts drie jaar dit boek samen te stellen” (a change in his teaching duties compelled him to compile the book in the free time of only three years). The work treats a dense array of Atjehnese material, including the *[[Hikajat Sultan Atjeh Marhoem]]*, *[[Hikajat Malém Dagang]]*, *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]*, *[[Tādj oes‑Salatin|Tādj us‑Salāṭīn]]*, *[[Bustanu's‑Salatin|Boestan oes‑Salāṭīn]]*, *[[Sirat al‑mustaqim|Sirāṭ al‑mustaqīm]]*, the Atjehnese *[[Hikajat Malém Diwa|Hikajat Malém Diwa]]*, the [[Pasai]] sultanate, the *[[Hikajat Raja‑Raja Pasai]]*, and the genealogy of the sultans of Malacca and Djohor.

## Military Award of 1947

A [[Koninklijk Besluit]] dated [[February]] [[1947]] awarded the *[[Bronzenn Kruis]]* to *[[Lieutenant‑colonel K. M. R. W. A. van Heuven|Lieutenant‑colonel K. M. R. W. A. van Heuven]] for “bijzonder moedige en beleid‑valle daden” (particularly brave and prudent actions) during the fighting near [[Grisseh]] and [[Lamongan]] in [[March]] [[1942]]. The decree highlighted van Heuven’s role in maintaining order among the indigenous population and in leading reconnaissance missions against superior Japanese forces.

## Post‑colonial Context

Both the publications and the military award occurred in the middle of the [[1945]]‑[[1949]] Indonesian revolution. *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* and *[[Over Maleise Literatuur]]* are *tempo doeloe* syntheses that look back on the colonial past with a nostalgic yet melancholy tone, reflecting a scholarly and officer class that was witnessing the imminent end of the Dutch colonial state. The 1947 awarding of the *Bronzenn Kruis* exemplifies the Dutch practice of honouring colonial officers for wartime service, illustrating the military culture of the *[[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]]* during the final years of Dutch rule.

## Significance

The year [[1947]] thus marks a convergence of cultural, scholarly, and military currents in the late colonial period of the Dutch East Indies. The publications provide the primary source material for the AcehWiki corpus on Acehnese literary and historical memory, while the award decree reveals how Dutch colonial authorities commemorated their own wartime narratives. Together, they underscore how 1947 functioned as a symbolic “marker” of both remembrance of the *tempo doeloe* and the accelerating transition toward Indonesian independence.

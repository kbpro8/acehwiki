## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1760

**[[1760]]** (approximately) was the year — recorded as "het jaar 1185 Moh. tijdr." — in which sixteen chiefs from the Meulabôh region, led by a prominent merchant of that harbour town, made their appearance at the court of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] and received, in exchange for a promise of regular tribute, *[[Sarakata|sarakata]]'s* — letters of appointment — confirming their authority. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* treats this event as the constitutional origin of the [[Kawaj XVI|Kawaj XVI]] — the federation of sixteen Westkust chiefs under the customs-collectorship of the *Keudjeuroeën Tjhï'* of [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]].

## The delegation to the Sultan

The sixteen chiefs — among them the *keudjeuroeëns* of [[Wojla|Wojla]], [[Boebôn|Boebôn]], and [[Seunagan|Seunagan]] (with the *oelèëbalang-tjoet* of Seunagan), the three *dato's* of [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]], and various heads from the basin of the [[Kr. Meureubôh|Kr. Meureubôh]] — were conducted to the Sultan's court by a *voornaam handelaar* of Meulabôh. The Sultan, in exchange for the promise of regular tribute, issued *sarakata*'s confirming their authority and appointed their conductor to the office of *belastinggaarder des Sultans* — the Sultan's customs-collector — with the title *Keudjeuroeën Tjhï'*. Henceforth the sixteen chiefs were required to pay their tribute through him. Also included in the delegation was the *Panghoeloe-Sidik*, charged with collecting the Sultan's dues on the island of [[Simeuloë|Simeuloë]], which thereby became tributary to the Tjhï' of Meulabôh.

## The Kawaj XVI

The *invloedsfeer* of the Tjhï' of Meulabôh over these sixteen tributary chiefs was later designated the *Kawaj XVI* — the "Kawé of sixteen" — to distinguish it from the chiefs of the Westkust who had received *sarakata*'s from the Sultan independently of the Tjhï'. The Tjhï' gradually attempted to extend his territorial authority over the sixteen — succeeding with the three *dato's* of Meulabôh but failing with the others. Dutch intervention in the late nineteenth century transformed the office, endowing Teukoe Tjhï' of Meulabôh with territorial authority over the three *dato*-ships and a portion of the Kr. Meureubôh basin (excluding Beutông), and so creating the territorial chieftaincy that was still, by historical resonance, called the *Kawaj XVI* — though "in werkelijkheid dus heel iets anders dan de Kawaj XVI, waarover hij vroeger belastinggaarder was."

## The Kawaj XII parallel

The *Kawaj XII* of the Pedir highlands — the federation of twelve gold-seeker settlements in the mountains between Beutông and Keumala Dalam — is recorded as a parallel institution, with the Sultan's *keudjeuroeën mas* (gold customs-collector) collecting a *boengkaj* of gold dust from each of the twelve settlements. The *Kawaj XII* was, however, depopulated by the decline of the gold workings and the cholera epidemic of [[1878]], and in [[1914]] was dissolved and divided between the *onderafdeelingen* of [[Sigli|Sigli]] and [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]].

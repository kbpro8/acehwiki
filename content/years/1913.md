Figures, places, and events in Acehnese history associated with the year [[1913]].

## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1913


**[[1913]]** was a year of recalibration in colonial accounting and logistical management in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. Following the unprecedented expenditure spikes of the previous year, the Dutch administration in northern [[Sumatra]] adjusted its financial ledgers, continuing to track the immense overhead required to maintain control following the [[Aceh War]].

The *Atjeh up to date* compilation preserves extensive tabular data for [[1913]], illustrating the material scale of the colonial enterprise:

- **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1913]] record outlays reaching 9,887,680 guilders. Specific departmental ledgers show distinct allocations, such as 916,221 and 1,351,540 in separate accounting categories.
- **Trade and Logistics:** Monthly and annual tracking of shipping and trade metrics shows figures such as 144,018 across various regional categories, alongside secondary ledgers showing 406,885; 169,530; 934,668; and 836,400.
- **Shipping Registries:** Vessel arrivals and tonnage registries for the year record specific logistical movements indexed at 132, 184, and 77 in primary tracking columns.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1913


**[[1913]]** was the year of the [[Beruchtste hongertocht van Atjeh (1913)|beruchtste 'hongertocht' van Atjeh]] — the most notorious hunger-patrol of Aceh — sent towards [[Pameuë]], in which many perished. The year is also the year of the wounding of *Overste* [[Overste Scheepens|Scheepens]] — wounded by an Acehnese during a *rechtzitting* (court session) in [[1913]] and who died of his wounds twenty years later in [[1933]], illustrating the lethal lingering effect of the *[[reuntjong]]* wound.

## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1913


**[[1913]]** was the year in which the transport-elephants were transported to [[Lam-toeulo]] to serve the [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant-transport]] to [[Tangsé]] and the still more southerly post [[Geumpang]] — the marker of the elephant-transport's base on the transverse road. In [[1913]] the transport-elephants were transported to Lam-toeulo to serve the transport to Tangsé and the still more southerly post Geumpang. The pitifully slow speed of the elephant-transport — "That last transport from Tangsé to Medan took 26 days!" — is the marker of the old-time speed of the elephant-transport that the lorry-transport of the transverse road would replace.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1913


**[[1913]]** was the year in which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] received its most ambitious extension approvals of the entire [[1904]]–[[1914]] period. By three Gouvernementsbesluiten (G. Bn.) — of [[17 June]] [[1913]] No. 59, [[16 October]] [[1913]] No. 25, and [[8 November]] [[1913]] No. 40 — the doortrekking (extension) of the Atjeh-tram network was approved from [[Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban]] on the [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] (described in the Bureau's account as "a good roadstead"). The connecting line between [[Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] was also reported as already in preparation. With the completion of this last section, the Atjeh-tram network would "finally reach its natural terminus: the fine and accessible Arube Bay, by which the economic value of the entire tram network will rise."

[[1913]] is also the year for which the Encyclopaedisch Bureau provides the most complete statistical snapshot of the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency in the volume. The Atjeh peper- en pinanghasil (pepper and betel-nut yield) yielded f 125,000 and f 55,000 respectively — the most important such yield of any Buitenbezittingen gewest. The Atjeh landschapskas recorded total income of f 748,802 in [[1913]] (up from f 264,910 in [[1910]] and f 355,752 in [[1911]]). The Atjeh-tram's net total route length reached 465 km (up from 432 km in [[1909]]–[[1911]]), with an aanlegkapitaal (construction capital) of f 18,544,000.

The [[1913]] statistics likewise show the Atjeh-tram's transformation: of 2,627,000 passengers carried in [[1913]], only 190,000 (7.2%) were on Gouvernements-rekening; of 209,000 tons of freight, only 19,000 (11%) were for the Gouvernement. The figures for [[1904]] had been 11% of passengers and 60% of freight on Gouvernements-rekening — a stark inversion that the Bureau cites as evidence of "the enormous increase in the production of and the import into this administrative ressort" and of "the rising importance of the tram." The net brutto ontvangs (gross receipts) of particular traffic on the Atjeh-tram rose from f 290,000 in [[1904]] to f 678,000 in [[1913]] — an increase of about 184% in the ten-year period.

## The Aroe-baai-plannen


The [[1913]] extension approvals also marked the high tide of the so-called [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen (Arube Bay plans)]] — the project to develop the [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as a deep-water harbour that would compete with [[Belawan]] and even with [[Singapore]] and [[Sabang]]. The defenders of the Aroe-baai-plannen hoped, by the laying of the new connecting link, to see the first step taken toward the future development of [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] products (pepper, rubber, etc.) via Arube Bay — a development that would be vastly accelerated by the ongoing reclamation of the Tamiang swamps.

## Colonial Economy in 1913 (from *De Buitenbezittingen*, 1915)


- **Opium Regie Implemented:** The most significant administrative event for [[Aceh|Atjeh]] in [[1913]] was the official introduction of the *opiumregie*. As the "last bastion" of the old, exploitative *pacht* system, this change symbolised the transition from military conquest to administrative and economic governance. Total *regie* revenue across the Indies reached 10.7 million guilders in [[1913]].
- **Rubber Industry Boom:** The success of the government's rubber plantation near [[Langsa]], established around [[1907]], sparked a massive influx of private investment. In [[1913]], there were **148 land concessions** in [[Atjeh and Dependencies]], covering nearly **300,000 *bouws***. Most were in the [[Langsa]] area, developed for rubber, often alongside robusta coffee as a catch crop.
- **Shipping and KPM Integration:** The [[Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij (KPM)]] fleet grew to **84 ships** with gross tonnage of over **137,000 tonnes**, carrying over 663,000 tons of cargo and 2.5 million passengers. The KPM's pioneer services were vital for linking Aceh's developing plantation economy with global markets.
- **Labor:** In [[1913]], Atjeh had 3,931 Javanese contract laborers and 3,070 Javanese women under the labor inspectorate.

## Significance


[[1913]] is the year at which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] reaches its maximal planned extent, and at which the project to convert the line from a military to an economic instrument comes nearest to realisation. The [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] of [[1913]] also mark the moment at which the Atjeh residency was first seriously imagined as a coherent economic region centred on its own deep-water harbour — an imagination that would be overtaken by the [[1914]] outbreak of the European war and the subsequent disruption of the colonial project.

## Account from the *Notulen der bestuursvergaderingen* (1914) — 1913


**1913** was the year in which the first phase of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] took place, with meetings held at Palembang ([[12 August]]–[[13 August]]), Benkoelen ([[15 August]]–[[16 August]]), and Padang ([[19 August]]–[[20 August]]). These three meetings constituted the initial round of consultations that Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff]] conducted across the Buitenbezittingen of Sumatra as part of the broader effort to reform the bestuurswezen (system of governance) in the Netherlands East Indies. The year 1913 thus marked the beginning of a process that would culminate in the pivotal [[Koeta-Radja]] meetings of January 1914, where the specific arrangements for the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] would be determined. The discussions in 1913 laid the groundwork for the more detailed deliberations that followed, establishing the general principles of [[Bestuursdecentralisatie]] and identifying the particular challenges that Atjeh posed for the reform program.

The year 1913 was also significant in the broader context of Dutch colonial policy in the Netherlands East Indies. The decentralisatiebeweging (decentralization movement) had been gathering momentum for several years, driven by the recognition that the highly centralized governance structure inherited from the nineteenth century was increasingly inadequate for the administration of a vast and diverse colonial territory. The appointment of De Graaff as Regeeringscommissaris charged with investigating and preparing the governance reform reflected the colonial government's commitment to translating the principles of decentralization into concrete administrative changes. The meetings held in 1913 were the first systematic attempt to gather input from local officials across the Buitenbezittingen about the feasibility and implications of the proposed reforms, making them a crucial stage in the policy development process.

## People


- [[S. de Graaff]] — Regeeringscommissaris leading the reform, who presided over all three rounds of meetings in 1913 and would go on to conduct the Medan and Koeta-Radja meetings in 1914. De Graaff's role was to consult with local officials, gather their input and concerns, and synthesize these into reform proposals that could be submitted to the central government for approval.
- [[J. de Groot]] — Hoofdambtenaar assisting De Graaff, who participated in the meetings and contributed to the consultation process. De Groot's report on the Atjeh-Oostkust merger question provided an important data point for the discussions.
- [[J. Tideman]] — Controleur who raised concerns about the proposed merger of Atjeh with Sumatra's Oostkust at the Palembang meeting. Tideman's intervention highlighted the anxieties that some officials felt about the implications of the reorganization for Atjeh's distinctive governance arrangements, particularly the potential disruption of administrative structures that had been developed over decades of conflict and consolidation.

## Events


- [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] — Governance reform consultations begin in Sumatra, with the first phase of meetings held in Palembang, Benkoelen, and Padang during August 1913. These meetings addressed the general principles of bestuursdecentralisatie and their application to the Buitenbezittingen, setting the stage for the more detailed discussions of 1914.
- [[Atjeh-Sumatra's Oostkust merger proposal]] — First raised as a concern at the Palembang meeting, where Controleur [[J. Tideman]] expressed reservations about the proposed merger while Hoofdambtenaar [[J. de Groot]] reported that an Assistent-Resident familiar with Atjeh saw no objections. The merger question would continue to be debated throughout the reform process, ultimately being approved at the 1914 meetings.

## Written Submissions


A written nota from Oud-Resident Heyting (dd. Tjiandjoer, 18 October 1913) was also submitted as part of the consultation process. This submission reflected the broader practice of soliciting input from current and former officials with experience in the territories affected by the proposed reforms, including those who were no longer serving in active administrative positions. Heyting's nota, submitted from Tjiandjoer in West Java where he had apparently retired, represented the perspective of an official with firsthand knowledge of Atjeh's governance challenges, even if his views were necessarily shaped by past experience rather than current conditions. The inclusion of such written submissions alongside the oral discussions at the meetings ensured that a wide range of perspectives was considered in the formulation of reform proposals.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)


[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records two major events of [[1913]]: the [[Scheepens murder (1913)|Scheepens murder]] of [[10 October]] and the [[False T. Radja Sabi (1913)|False T. Radja Sabi]] of [[6 December]].

On [[10 October]] [[1913]] [[Scheepens]] — division-commander and *civiel gezag* at [[Sigli]] — was stabbed at the *moesapat* by the *oeléëbalang* of Titeuë, whose complaint against a *kampongman* Scheepens had rejected with the words *"Itoe saja tida tarima"*. The *oeléëbalang* responded by drawing his *rentjong* and stabbing Scheepens, who was then struck by two *klewang*-blows from the *oppassers* who killed the *oeléëbalang*. Scheepens died at *5:15 AM* the following morning. [[Mary Ware|Mrs. Mary Ware]] — *de Amerikaansche dame die eene reis om de wereld maakte* — visited him on the operating table. *Het was altijd je verlangen op Peutjoet te liggen bij je ouwe kameraden, Webb, Campioni*, his wife said at his bedside; and so he was buried at [[Peutjoet]].

On [[6 December]] [[1913]] a boy arrived at [[Lho Soekon]] claiming to be [[T. Radja Sabi]] — the *adelaarsjong* of [[Keureutoe]]. [[Behrens]] questioned him at *bivak Leuhong* — *de knaap viel hierbij door den mand; hij bekende een onderschoven object te zijn* — but Governor [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]], summoning all the *hoofden* at [[Lho Seumawé]], declared: *De jongen is Teukoe Radja Sabi* — and *niemand protesteerde*. The boy was in fact *Doellah*, son of *Tengkoe Radja Imeum* alias *Tgk. Moeda Bale Mbang*; he was sent to school at Koetaradja on government expense and later became a *boekbinder* at the *bestuurskantoor*.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1913** was the year in which [[Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb|Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb]] succeeded his father [[Teukoe Tjhi Perlak|Teukoe Tjhi Perlak]] as *[[Oeléebalang|oeléebalang]]* of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula (Perlak)]], at the moment when the oil-cijns was regularised into the *landschapskas* and the chief received a fixed income. [[Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih|Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih]] received the great star of *trouw en verdienste*. The decision was taken to extend the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] through to [[Besitang|Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]]. The [[Gajo-weg|Gajo-weg]] was completed in [[1913]], a length of 103 kilometres from [[Bireuen|Bireuen]] to [[Takengon|Takengon]].

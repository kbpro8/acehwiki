The year **1297** is the date of the death and burial of the first Muslim ruler of [[Samudra]] on the [[Pasei|Pase-rivier]] in northern Sumatra, the polity whose Islamisation marks the earliest documentary evidence of a Muslim court in the Indonesian archipelago after the [[1292]] visit of [[Marco Polo]] to the neighbouring polity of [[Perlak]]. The date is recorded in Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]]'s chapter *De oudste cultuurstroomen* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]).

## The 1297 grave at Samudra

Van Naerssen's account runs: "the Muslim founder of the little realm lies buried there in 1297. His gravestone is preserved and proves to have been imported ready-made from [[Cambay]], the chief city of [[Goedjarat]], which itself had only been Islamised since around 1250 and was developing more and more into a Muslim emporium. The Islam will have, as everywhere, stimulated the trade expansion." The 1297 tombstone, imported ready-made from Cambay, is the principal material evidence for the early Islamisation of the Sumatran coast; the same Cambay-type tombstone is found in the oldest preserved Muslim grave on Java — that of [[Malik Ibrahim]] (d. 1419) at Gresik — bracketing the early Islamisation of the archipelago between the two graves.

## Atjeh connection

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the 1297 grave is foundational: the [[Samudra]] polity that produced it was the immediate predecessor of the Atjeh sultanate on the same north Sumatran coast, and its Islamisation — via the [[Goedjarat]]–[[Cambay]] mercantile network — established the religious-cultural base on which the later Atjeh sultanate was built. The three centuries that separate the 1297 grave from the [[Conversion of Minangkabau via Atjeh (c. 1550)|conversion of Minangkabau via Atjeh]] of c. [[1550]] are, on Van Naerssen's account, the period of Atjeh's gradual rise to dominance over the north Sumatran coast.

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1883

### The Zulkler oil concession

Van Bemmelen's account of the [[1883]] Zulkler concession, in the petroleum chapter of [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|*The Geology of Indonesia*]], is the only printed source:

> "In [[1883]] a concession for oil had been granted in North Sumatra. The concessionary, J. ZulkER, met with considerable difficulties in drilling on account of an abundant flow of gas and asked the Government Bureau of Mines for assistance."

The [[1883]] concession is the first documented oil concession in northern Sumatra, and its granting accordingly marks the beginning of the documentary sequence of northern-Sumatran petroleum exploration. The technical difficulty that Zulkler encountered — "an abundant flow of gas" — is the first recorded encounter with the high-pressure gas zones that would become a recurrent feature of northern-Sumatran drilling. The fact that Zulkler had to ask the [[Bureau of Mines in Indonesia|Government Bureau of Mines]] for technical assistance is the first documented instance of the public-private partnership model that would subsequently characterise the entire East Indian oil industry.

### Other events of 1883 relevant to Atjeh

The year [[1883]] is also significant in the wider colonial-geological record for the publication of Verbeek's *Jaarboek van het Mijnwezen* 1883 references — Verbeek [[1883]], p. 564 (Gunung Besie on the Westkust van Sumatra), p. 563 (Aer Dingin and Paningahan), p. 562 (Batu Mendjulur), pp. 252 and 310 (Sungei Lassi) — which form part of the wider late-nineteenth-century geological literature on the Sumatran mineral province of which Atjeh is the northern terminus.


## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* (1923) — 1883

"Dan kan men het verstaan dat in de Atjeh-geschiedenis het jaar 1883 als een 'rapjaar' te boek staat; een jaar dat ook voor de economische ontwikkeling van het gewest rampspoedig geweest is" — for Langhout the year showed the bankruptcy of the first civil government: the Atjehnese struggle took on an ever more religious character, trusted chiefs fell away, cholera counted victims by thousands, the earthquake tormented Koeta Radja to a wide distance, and the whole coast had to be closed again but for the six official harbours. The year ended with the *Nisero* stranded and her crew captive — the affair whose ransom and chastisement would fill [[1884]].

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — Laging Tobias and the May 1883 *scheepvaartregeling*

[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* identifies [[1883]] as the year of [[Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]]'s arrival in March [[1883]] — the *Civiel Gouverneur* who inherited "een zware ondankbare taak" from the *Civiel Gouverneur* regime of [[1881]]–[[1883]] that had followed the [[Van der Heijden]] dismissal — and of the May [[1883]] introduction of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] that Van Heutsz would single out, ten years later, as "alles wat men kon verlangen" — everything one could desire.

### Laging Tobias's arrival in March 1883

Laging Tobias took over the Governorship of Atjeh in March [[1883]]. Van Heutsz describes the situation he inherited:

> "In Maart 1883 kwam de Gouverneur Laging Tobias aan het bestuur, die den toestand te Groot-Atjeh zeer ongunstig vond".

The gloom was Laging Tobias's own retrospective: in his later article "Het herstel van het sultanaat in Atjeh", published in the December [[1886]] number of the *Indische Gids*, he confessed that "Reeds zeer kort na mijn optreden als gouverneur van Atjeh, kreeg ik de overtuiging dat onze toestand in Atjeh bijna hopeloos was". The political obstacle Laging Tobias faced was [[Toeankoe Haschim]]'s consolidated position at [[Kemala]]: Haschim had, in the years of Dutch *lijdelijkheid*, "den tijd van onze lijdelijkheid, tweespalt en onterredenheid meesterlijk benut" to install his pupil [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]] as the recognised successor to [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah]] (who had died in [[1874]]).

### The May 1883 *scheepvaartregeling*

Laging Tobias's second initiative — and the one Van Heutsz most prizes — was the introduction in May [[1883]] of the *scheepvaartregeling* that [[Scherer]] had first conceived and that Laging Tobias was the first to apply:

> "Reeds in Mei 1883 toch, zoo verhaalt ons Scherer, vaardigde hij, onder nadere goedkeuring der Regeering, een besluit uit, waarbij, volgens zijn (Scherer's) denkbeelden, eene contrôle op handel en scheepvaart werd ingevoerd, die alles wat in- en uitgevoerd werd door onze handen deed gaan en aan ons toezicht onderwierp".

Van Heutsz's verdict, in the [[1893]] tract, is the highest praise he accords any Governor in the entire catalogue:

> "de scheepvaartregeling door Scherer voorgesteld en door Laging Tobias ingevoerd gaf alles wat men kon verlangen".

The May [[1883]] *scheepvaartregeling* thus stands, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, as the high-water mark of the post-Van-der-Heijden policy — the one measure that, had it been sustained, might have spared the Regeering the policy oscillations of [[1884]]–[[1892]].

### The October 1883 letter to Tekoe Nanta and the December 1883 withdrawal

The second Dutch move toward the sultanaat-restoration was the letter Laging Tobias addressed, in the first half of October [[1883]], to the old [[Tekoe Nanta|Tekoe Nanta of the VI Moekims]] (also called Tekoe Lampadang) — the letter in which the Governor *doorschemerde* (let it be hinted) that, in case of submission by the chiefs of the war party, "een herstel van het sultanaat met Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed als sultan wel mogelijk was":

> "liet de Gouverneur van Atjeh daarop in een in de eerste helft van October 1883 aan den ouden Tekoe Nanta der VI Moekims (ook Tekoe Lampadang genoemd) geschreven brief doorschemeren, dat, ingeval van onderwerping van de hoofden der oorlogspartij, een herstel van het sultanaat met Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed als sultan wel mogelijk was, en dit was het eerste blijk, dat de Atjehers van onze zijde kregen omtrent de mogelijkheid van herstel van hun ouden regeeringsvorm, zij het dan ook onder Ned. souvereiniteit".

The October [[1883]] letter was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the *eerste blijk* — the first indication — the Atjehnese had received from the Dutch side that a restoration of their ancient form of government was conceivable, albeit under Dutch sovereignty. The hint came to nothing: the Indische Regeering, after the December [[1883]] resignation of [[Minister van Bloemen Waanders|Minister van Koloniën van Bloemen Waanders]] — who had himself raised the *herstel* idea in his August [[1883]] letter to [[Pijnacker Hordijk|G.-G. 's Jacob]] — gave the Governor of Atjeh to understand that "van een herstel van het sultanaat niets weten wilde" (wished to know nothing of a restoration of the sultanate).

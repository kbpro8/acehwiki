**1893** was a pivotal year in the Dutch‑Aceh theatre. In [[1893]] the first volume of Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje’s foundational ethnography *De Atjehers* was published, while the Dutch colonial navy launched two coordinated marine operations – the **Tuchtiging der Aroe‑cilanden** against the [[Aroe Islands]] and the **Expedition against Tamiang** in the neighbouring [[Tamiang|Tamiang (Sumatra)]] region. These actions reflected the widening scope of the Dutch pacification effort beyond the immediate borders of [[Atjeh]] and were accompanied by notable political and religious events within Aceh.

## Publications

- [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]] – publication of volume I of his [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-[[1894]])|*De Atjehers*]] in [[1893]], the foundational ethnography of the Atjehnese. Volume II followed in [[1894]].

## Military Operations

- **Tuchtiging der Aroe‑cilanden** – a punitive expedition against the [[Aroe Islands]] to suppress a rebellion that threatened Dutch shipping in the region.
- **Expedition against Tamiang** – a combined land‑and‑sea operation in the [[Tamiang|Tamiang (Sumatra)]] area aimed at neutralising cross‑border insurgents.

Both operations were part of the broader Dutch strategy to extend control over the peripheral islands and riverine zones surrounding Aceh.

## The bloodless capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)

The year [[1893]] was marked by the [[Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)|bloodless capture of Kaloet]] by the Dutch-allied Atjehnese warlord [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] — the principal Dutch-Atjehnese auxiliary success of the late *concentratie* period. The operation, documented in detail in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]]'s chapter *De Strijd om Kaloet*, succeeded where the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|failed Dutch assault of 1 October 1892]] had failed, and did not cost *een druppel bloeds meer* ("another drop of blood").

[[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] — supported by Dutch artillery and with Dutch infantry as reserve — had, in the first days of August [[1893]], *de geheele XXV Moekims had zuiverd van vijandelijke benden, en eenige sterke bentings buiten de linie had veroverd, o.a. Boekit Trieng, Boekit Daroe, Kasoemba en Tjot-Goe* ("purified the entire XXV Moekims of enemy bands, and had conquered some strong bentings outside the line, among others Boekit Trieng, Boekit Daroe, Kasoemba and Tjot-Goe"). On [[4 August]] [[4 August|4 August]] [[1893]] Toekoe Oemar invested Kaloet with approximately 1000 men, supported by the 4th Mountain Battery from the Kraton and by the fire of the guns of [[Lamreng|Lamreng]] and [[Lambaroe|Lambaroe]]. On the morning of [[5 August]] [[5 August|5 August]] [[1893]] Toekoe Oemar found the *benting* abandoned — the Atjehnese garrison having withdrawn in the night after Toekoe Oemar's *briefje* (note) threatening a *hongerkuur* (hunger-cure) and *over de kling jagen* (putting to the sword).

The same year also saw [[Toekoe Oemar|Teuku Uma]]'s formal [[Submission of Teuku Uma to the Dutch Government (1893)|submission to the Dutch government]], initiating the so-called *Uma period* of Dutch-Atjehnese relations that would last until [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|his 1896 defection]]. [[Lamgoet|Lamgoet]] — the former seat of [[Teungku di Tiro|Teungku di Tiro]] — was briefly occupied by [[Toekoe Bantah|Toekoe Bantah]] in [[1893]], but Toekoe Bantah was unable to maintain himself there; it was [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] who, in the same year, brought Lamgoet a second time under Dutch control.

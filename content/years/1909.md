## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1909


**1909** was a year of continued administrative recording and logistical tracking in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. Following the major military campaigns of the preceding years, the colonial authorities focused on quantifying the economic output and the ongoing costs of garrisoning the region during the latter stages of the [[Aceh War]].


## Statistical and Administrative Record


According to the *Atjeh up to date* compilation, [[1909]] is heavily documented in colonial financial and logistical ledgers. The surviving data from this year highlights the scale of Dutch operations in northern [[Sumatra]]:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1909]] record massive outlays, with total figures reaching 6,077,025 guilders across various administrative and military departments. Specific departmental ledgers show distinct allocations, such as 1,283,439 and 767,017 in separate accounting categories.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Registries of vessel arrivals and tonnage indicate a steady flow of colonial supplies and export commodities. Monthly and annual tracking shows figures such as 96,093 and 48,240 in primary logistical categories, alongside 102,402 and 123,325 in secondary trade metrics.
*   **Military Overhead:** The financial burden of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons and conducting localized patrols is reflected in the high baseline expenditures, a trend that would continue to fluctuate in the years leading up to [[1914]].

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1909

### The Kinandam-Sumatra mining leases

For the full account of the [[1909]] Kinandam-Sumatra mining leases see the entries on [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] and [[Coal in Atjeh]]. In summary: in [[1909]] mining leases were granted to the Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij. for the coal concessions Bukit Durian, Bukit Pulai, and Sarik in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra. The three concessions are located in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra (the residency immediately to the south of Atjeh), and the coal occurrences are described by Van Bemmelen as "all of minor importance as the coal seams, occurring in sandstone and clay shale, and ranging from a few centimeters up to 2 m in thickness, generally contain impure coal of a poor quality (soft with a tendency to crumble). Moreover, the extension of the seams is not sufficient to justify a detailed exploration."

The only recorded production from the Kinandam-Sumatra concessions was in the years [[1917]]–[[1927]], when "a total quantity of 2000 tons has been produced for use at the [[Salida]] gold mine of the same company." The mining rights for the Bukit Durian and Bukit Pulai concessions were returned to the Government in [[1934]], with the Sarik concession following in [[1941]].

### Significance

[[1909]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year in which the only documented colonial-era coal-mining concessions on Atjeh-relevant territory were granted — the [[1909]] Kinandam-Sumatra mining leases at Bukit Durian, Bukit Pulai and Sarik in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra (immediately to the south of the Atjeh border). The [[1909]] leases accordingly mark the beginning of the colonial coal-mining industry in the West-Atjeh/Painan region — an industry that would produce only 2,000 tons over the decade [[1917]]–[[1927]] (all for use at the company's own Salida gold mine) before being wound down in [[1934]] and [[1941]].

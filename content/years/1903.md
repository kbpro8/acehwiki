The year **[[1903]]** is the founding date of the "Asiatic" sales organisation — the first formal corporate merger of the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Royal Petroleum Company]] of [[Langkat]] with the Shell Transport and Trading Co. of Sir Marcus Samuel, brokered by [[H. W. A. Deterding|Deterding]] after the death in [[1901]] of the Koninklijke's first Director, [[A. J. B. Kessler|Kessler]]. The date is recorded in Ir. [[F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]]'s chapter *Mijnbouw* ("Mining") in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]). The same year also figures in the [[Aceh War]] chronology as the year of the publication of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners* ("The Gayo Country and its Inhabitants"), the principal Western scholarly monograph on the [[Gajoland|Gayo]] highlands of central [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

## The 1903 Royal Dutch–Shell merger

Van der Ploeg's account: after Kessler's death in [[1901]], "Deterding, who after Kessler's death had the reins of the Koninklijke alone in hand, set himself as a merchant wholly to work for this merger" — that is, for the merger of the Koninklijke (whose operational base was the [[Pangkalan Brandan]] refinery in [[Langkat]] on the Sumatra East Coast, adjacent to [[Acheh|Atjeh]]) with the Shell Transport and Trading Co. of Sir Marcus Samuel (whose petroleum storage and sales business in the East had been built on the [[1889]] Sanga-Sanga concession of [[J. H. Menten|Menten]] in Koetei, Borneo). "The first step was the founding of the 'Asiatic' sales organisation." The merger was consolidated in [[1906]] by the founding of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Batavian Petroleum Company]] as the production arm and the Anglo-Saxon as the transport arm.

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the [[1903]] merger marks the moment at which the Sumatra East Coast petroleum industry — based in [[Langkat]] and [[Deli]] immediately adjacent to Atjeh — was formally incorporated into the wider Koninklijke–Shell concern. The 1903 merger was followed, in [[1911]], by the absorption of the last independent Indies petroleum company (the [[Dordtsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Dordrecht Petroleum Company]]) into the concern; by the time of the [[Aceh War|Aceh war]]'s final phase and the [[Handover of Aceh to a Civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of Aceh to a civil gouverneur]] in [[1918]], the Atjeh-adjacent East Coast oil belt had become the operational core of one of the world's major petroleum multinationals.

## Snouck Hurgronje's *Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners*

The same year saw the publication of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners* (The Hague: M. Nijhoff, [[1903]]) — the principal Western scholarly monograph on the [[Gajoland|Gayo]] highlands of central [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The monograph, based on Snouck Hurgronje's earlier stay in the Atjeh interior, supplied the first systematic European account of the Gayo country and its inhabitants, and remained the standard scholarly reference on the region for decades.

## Significance

The year [[1903]] stands at the intersection of two of the most consequential developments in late-colonial northern Sumatra: the corporate consolidation of the Atjeh-adjacent Sumatra East Coast petroleum industry into the wider Koninklijke–Shell concern, and the publication of the standard Western scholarly monograph on the Atjeh interior (Snouck Hurgronje's Gayo-country study). The two developments together frame [[1903]] as a year in which the strategic and the scholarly understanding of the Atjeh region were simultaneously transformed: the one by the corporate-bureaucratic unification of the petroleum industry, the other by the publication of the foundational ethnographic account of the Gayo highlands.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records the major submissions of [[1903]]: [[Panglima Polim]] submitted in the second half of the year — photographed with [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]] as second officer of justice. On [[5 October]] [[1903]] [[T. Tjhi Toenong]] came in to [[Lho Seumawé]] and was permitted to live in [[Keureutoe]]. In November [[1903]] luitenant Kok and 28 militairen were drowned when their *prauwen* were overturned by treacherous Atjehsche rowers — an ambush laid by T. Tjhi Toenong.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1903** was the year in which the last [[Sultan of Atjeh|sultan of Atjeh]] ceded his realm to the Dutch government, the year in which [[Sabang|Sabang]] was established as *'een station in zee met een toekomst'*, and the year in which the governor greeted the first ship of the Kon. Paketvaart-Mij., the *Nederland*, the *Willem III*. It was also the year of publication of [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners|Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners]]*. The *Nieuw beleid in Atjeh (1908)|new policy* of [[1908]] was a response to the disappointments of the years immediately after [[1903]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

On [[2 October]] **[[1903]]** [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]] wrote to Governor-General [[Rooseboom]] the letter warning against the appointment of [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to the Atjeh governorship — "as it were a Mene Tekel". On [[12 October]] [[1903]] [[H. N. A. Swart]] was charged with the government of [[Lho' Seumawè]] and on [[24 October]] extraordinarily promoted *Majoor*. In November [[1903]] [[T. Tjhi' Toenòng]]'s band destroyed the party of *2e luitenant* [[De Kok]] on the river at [[Sampoj Niët]], drowning the officer and twenty-eight men.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* (1923) — 1903

"Het jaar 1903 beteekende voor Sabang weer een mijlpaal op den weg naar het succes" — 1903 was for Sabang a milestone on the road to success: from that year the mail steamers of the "Nederland" took the route over Sabang and Singapore on the out- and home-voyages, whereby Atjeh "van dien datum af in geregelde verbinding was met het Moederland en met het buitenland en anderzijds Sabang alle voordeelen kreeg van een internationale havenplaats". The [[Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij|K.P.M.]] opened her line [[Deli]]–Sabang the same year. Traffic statistics for the year: 589 vessels in 1904 following 243 in 1903 — the harbour's take-off (see [[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]]).

**[[1903]]** was the year in which the thirty-year [[Aceh War]] came to its effective end with the unconditional submission of [[Panglima Polim]]. Reported by telegraph in [[August 1903]] and described in the Dutch press as an event of greater significance than the earlier submission of the pretender-sultan, the event confirmed that the core of resistance—the hereditary leadership of the [[XXII Moekims]]—had finally been broken.

## Key events

- The unconditional submission of [[Panglima Polim]]—after more than five years of relentless pursuit by Dutch columns led by [[Kapitein Colijn]] and [[Onderluitenant Christoffel]].
- The capture of [[Polim]]'s mother and one of his wives by [[Majoor Van der Maaten]], which—together with heavy losses among his bands—provided the final reason to abandon the struggle.
- The refusal by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] to accept a conditional submission: the order was to continue pursuit until unconditional surrender, ensuring that victory would not be bought through weak promises.
- [[Polim]]'s request for a warship to travel as a grand seigneur to [[Koeta Radja]]—a request granted by the governor out of respect for [[Polim]]'s energy and honour.

Although some work remained—roads to be built, the [[Gajoelanden]] to be administered, and a few remaining hostile bands to be pursued—the fundamental resistance had collapsed. All chiefs of significance had submitted, and the population everywhere recognised Dutch authority.

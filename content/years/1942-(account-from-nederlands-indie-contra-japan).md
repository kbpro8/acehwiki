**The year 1942** was, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the year of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]] and of the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Dutch capitulation of 28 March 1942]] — the year that ended 39 years of Dutch rule in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] and installed the Japanese military occupation. The staff history's account of [[1942]] is the most detailed published Dutch narrative of the Atjeh campaign, and the operational complement to Dr. [[A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]]'s political and social account in *[[Atjèh and the War with Japan]]*.

## The February 1942 F-beweging rising

The [[1942]] account opens with the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] sabotage wave that broke on the night of [[19 February]]–[[20 February 1942]] in the *sagi der XXII moekims* around [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]]. The first action period culminated in the [[murder of Tiggelman at Seulimeum|murder of controleur J. C. Tiggelman]] on [[24 February 1942]] and the killing of [[U. Bernstorff-von Sperling|Von Sperling]] the following morning. The identification of the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] as a Japanese-sponsored fifth column — through the arrest and interrogation of a Menadonese soldier and the recognition that "F." stood for **Fujiwara** — was the principal Dutch intelligence discovery of February [[1942]].

The second sabotage wave of [[7 March]]–[[8 March 1942]] ran directly into the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese landings of 12 March]].

## The 11 March 1942 arrest of the eight chiefs

On [[11 March 1942]] Colonel [[G. F. V. Gosenson|Gosenson]] ordered the arrest of the eight foremost Atjeh chiefs at the resident's yard at Kotaradja — an impulsive act the staff history treats as the principal Dutch command error of the pre-invasion rising.

## The 12 March 1942 Japanese landings

The Japanese landings of [[12 March 1942]] — at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]], [[Sabang|Sabang]], [[Ujong Batëë|Ujong Batëë]], [[Kuala Bughah|Kuala Bughah]] and [[Tandjong Tiram|Tandjong Tiram]] — comprised a Guards Brigade (a regiment with support weapons) and a Guards Regiment, totalling well over 10,000 men. A battalion of the Guards Regiment struck along the Westkust; the rest marched along the Oostkust; a part of the Guards Brigade marched from Kotaradja on [[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]] and [[Tangse|Tangse]]. A further battalion landed at [[Idi|Idi]] and marched on [[Medan|Medan]] to link up with the central-Sumatra landings.

## The three Dutch main groups

The Dutch response was organised in three main groups:

- **1ste hoofdgroep** (Westkust) under Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] — the [[2de (IIde) Divisie Marechaussee|IIde Divisie Maréchaussee]] and the [[Compagnie Steinhorst|Compagnie Steinhorst]], with the evacuation of 3,000 women and children and the [[Battle of Blang Pidië (16 March 1942)|Battle of Blang Pidië]].
- **2de hoofdgroep** (noordkust) under Lt-Col [[C. J. Bekkers|Bekkers]] — the [[2de Garnizoensbataljon|2de Garnizoensbataljon]] and the IVde Divisie Maréchaussee, with the collapse of the [[Poort stelling (Tangse)|Poort stelling]] and the capitulation of Tangse on 18 March [[1942]].
- **3de hoofdgroep** (central-Atjeh) under [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek]] — the [[3de (IIIde) Divisie Marechaussee|IIIde Divisie]] and [[5de (Vde) Divisie Marechaussee|Vde Divisie Maréchaussee]], with the first stelling south of Bireuën and the [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|Van Zanten resistance]].

## The capitulations of March 1942

The Dutch campaign closed with four capitulations:

- **18 March 1942**: Tangse capitulation under Lt-Col [[C. J. Bekkers|Bekkers]] — close of the noordkust theatre.
- **19 March 1942**: Takengon capitulation under [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]] — close of the central-Atjeh theatre.
- **28 March 1942**: [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Blangkedjeren capitulation]] under Lt-Col Blogg — close of the regular campaign.
- **29 March 1942**: Lae Boetar capitulation under Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] — close of the Westkust theatre.

The staff history records that less than three weeks after the Japanese landings, the regular warfare in Noord- and Midden-Sumatra had come to an end.

## The post-capitulation resistance

The post-capitulation Dutch resistance of [[1942]] comprised two episodes:

- The [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|Van Zanten resistance]] from a forest bivouac north-east of [[Takengon|Takengon]], with the [[Redelong (theeonderneming)|Redelong tea estate]] as intelligence and supply base — surviving until Van Zanten's capture on [[10 March 1943]].
- The safeguarding of the *vaandel* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] by Lt [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|Onvlee]] — sustained through the Japanese captivity from [[1 December 1941]] to [[15 August 1945]].

## Significance

The year [[1942]] is, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the year of the Dutch operational defeat in Atjeh, and the year that ended 39 years of Dutch rule in the *gewest*. The staff history's emphasis on the F-beweging rising, on the three Dutch main groups' separate capitulations, and on the post-capitulation resistance frames [[1942]] as the year that closed the Dutch colonial military presence in Atjeh and opened the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese occupation period]]. The staff history's account is the operational complement to Piekaar's political and social account in *[[Atjèh and the War with Japan]]*: where Piekaar explains *why* Atjeh rose, the staff history documents *how* the colonial army was brought down.

**1879** was the year of the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign against the XXII Moekims]] of [[23 March]] [[23 March]] – [[9 July]] [[9 July]], of the [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|campaign against the XXVI Moekims]] of [[1 July]] [[1 July]] – [[4 July]] [[4 July]], and of the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of [[13 August]] [[13 August]] – [[15 August]] [[15 August]], which together completed the first conquest of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. The year also saw the laying of the first stone for the new [[Meusigit Raja|missigit]] at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] by the governor [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] on [[9 October]] [[9 October]], the reduction of the garrison by Indies decree of [[18 May]] [[18 May]] [[1880]] to 253 officers and 6372 rank-and-file, the dissolution of the active columns on [[5 September]] [[5 September]], and the visit to Atjeh of the commander of the Indies army, Lieutenant-general [[H. G. Boumeester|H. G. Boumeester]], on [[19 August]] [[19 August]] [1879].

**1879** was also the year of the [[Halewijn|Captain Halewijn]]'s military column — one company strong — over [[Glé Broeë|Glé Broeë]] in the reverse direction to that of the [[1877|Dompseler column of 1877]], and the second of the only two pre-[[1896]] Dutch references for the [[Ladoeng]]–Glé Broeë mountain route that would, in [[June]] [[1896]], become the axis of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|sea-borne Ladoeng landing]] under [[G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. Hansen]]. As recorded by [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]] in p. 324, Halewijn's column made the reverse-direction march over Glé Broeë in 9.5 hours and considered the way "zeer bruikbaar" — very usable — except for the ravines. Together with [[Dompseler|Maj. Dompseler]]'s [[1877]] march, Halewijn's reconnaissance furnished [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s staff with the only pre-1896 terrain intelligence on the Glé Broeë axis, and shaped the planning of the [[22 June|22]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] amphibious operation against the Acehnese stelling east of [[Kroeng-Kali]].

## Account from *De geschiedenis van Nederland in onzen tijd* (1912) — The Campaigns of 1879

### March: The Capture of Indrapuri

After the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman on 13 October 1878]] the campaign against the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] was decided upon. In his cabinet letter of [[21 March]] [[21 March]] [1879] the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] instructed Van der Heyden that "the war must now come to an end." On [[23 March]] [[23 March]] [1879] the columns marched; on [[24 March]] [[24 March]] [1879] the [[Capture of Indrapuri (24 March 1879)|missigit of Indrapuri was taken]] by a stratagem.

### May-July: The Continuation

Heavy fighting followed at [[Djerreek|Djerreek]], [[Redep|Redep]], [[Panteh Karang|Panteh Karang]] in May. On [[6 May]] [[6 May]] and [[7 May]] [[7 May]] [1879] great losses were inflicted on the Dutch at Panteh Karang, but in the end the enemy was driven out. On [[31 May]] [[31 May]] [1879] the troops again advanced on [[Gleiêng|Gleiêng]], the fortified and well-armed chief place of [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]]; on [[9 July]] [[9 July]] [1879] Gleiêng was taken. With the [[Capture of Gleiêng (9 July 1879)|fall of Gleiêng]] the campaign in the XXII Moekims was over.

### July: The Submission of the XXVI Moekims

On [[1 July]] [[1 July]] [1879] the columns marched from various points into the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. The principal fortified points — the [[missigit Melajoe|missigit Melajoe]], the [[missigit Boëng Tjala|missigit Boëng Tjala]], the [[missigit Lambaroe|missigit Lambaroe]], and the [[missigit Toengkoep|missigit Toengkoep]] — were taken at the first storm or had already been evacuated. On [[4 July]] [[4 July]] [1879] the Dutch were wholly master of the XXVI Moekims.

### August: The Selimoen Expedition

On [[27 July]] [[27 July]] [1879] the troops disembarked at [[Lampanas|Lampanas]] and reached [[Lamtobah|Lamtobah]] on [[29 July]] [[29 July]]. On [[30 July]] [[30 July]] [1879] envoys of the blind priest [[Habib Machmoed|Habib Machmoed]] arrived at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]] with the submission of [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed|Toekoe Moeda Daoed]]. On [[13 August]] [[13 August]] – [[15 August]] [[15 August]] [1879] the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] was carried out; [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed|Toekoe Moeda Daoed]] and other chiefs submitted; [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]] and the [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]] fled to the upper lands of [[Pedir|Pedir]].

### September: The End of Large-Scale Operations

On [[5 September]] [[5 September]] [1879] the active columns were dissolved. "To be sure, the principal among the hostile chiefs — [[Toewankoe Haschim|Toewankoe Haschim]], who still kept watch over the young pretender to the sultanship [[Toewankoe Daoed|Toewankoe Daoed]] [destined for the sultan's rank in 1874], [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]], [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]], his son [[Radja Koelala|Radja Koelala]] and others — persisted in their hostile attitude, but their means were scattered and they had placed themselves beyond the reach of our troops, namely at [[Kemala|Kemala]], in the interior south of Pedir."

### October: The New Missigit

On [[9 October]] [[9 October]] [1879] the governor laid the first stone for the new [[Meusigit Raja|missigit]] at Kota Radja. "From far and near the Acehnese chiefs were invited to attend this ceremony and the festivities to be connected with it, and they came in great numbers, both from Great Acheh and from the Onderhoorigheden. Also from the lesser people thousands streamed together; and although the necessary measures were taken for possible disturbances of the peace, everything passed off in the best order."

### The Visit of Boumeester

On [[19 August]] [[19 August]] [1879] the commander of the Indies army, Lieutenant-general [[H. G. Boumeester|H. G. Boumeester]], arrived in Atjeh to inform himself personally of the state of affairs. In his report he paid great tribute to Van der Heyden: "On the whole it appears to me that the course followed since June 1878 has produced the expected results, and it is certain that the favourable and, comparatively speaking, even very favourable outcome that has now been obtained is due in very considerable measure to the energetic and skilful way in which the commander has carried out the instructions given him by the government... The nation is undoubtedly greatly indebted to him."

### The Koffiebladziekte

In [[1879]] the koffiebladziekte penetrated to [[Java|Java]], at first only sporadically, but soon more generally and of a serious character.

## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1879

**[[1879]]** was the year of the laying of the first stone of the [[Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] on [[9 October]] [[1879]] — in the presence of Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Johan Willem van Lansberge]], all the civil and military authorities, and many Acehnese chiefs and clergy. The first stone — the colonial state's formal beginning of the rebuilding — is the marker of the colonial state's consent and cooperation in the rebuilding of the Meusigit Raja, which had been promised in [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s missive of [[8 February]] [[1874]] following the [[1873]] storming of the mosque.

The book's sequence is: the [[1873]] storming of the mosque (the burning-down and demolition) — the [[1874]] Van Swieten missive (the well-known lines) — the [[1879]] laying of the first stone (in the presence of Van Lansberge) — the [[1881]] completion and the [[27 December]] [[1881]] solemn transfer (by [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|A. Pruys van der Hoeven]]). The [[1879]] first stone is therefore the marker of the formal beginning of the rebuilding: the colonial state's laying of the first stone of the Meusigit Raja.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the operational climax of the Atjeh-oorlog

[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* identifies [[1879]] as the operational climax of the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|Atjeh-oorlog]]: the year of the [[XXVI Moekims campaign (1879)|XXVI Moekims campaign]] — the *veldtocht tegen de XXVI Moekims*, opened by [[Van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] in July [[1879]] and "schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen" — and of the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order of 5 September 1879]] declaring "geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt". The year was the moment at which "nog geen 14 maanden na het begin van zijne zegerijke veldtochten", Van der Heijden had "aan alle verzet een einde gemaakt" and was, in the Atjehnese reading of the conquest, "de door den vijand gevreesde overwinnaar van het aloude machtige Atjehsche rijk".

### The XXVI Moekims campaign and the Selimoen tocht

The campaign opened in July [[1879]] as the natural continuation of the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] of the previous year. Van Heutsz's general formulation:

> "In September 1879, nog geen 14 maanden na het begin van zijne zegerige veldtochten, had Generaal v. d. Heijden aan alle verzet een einde gemaakt, was hij de door den vijand gevreesde overwinnaar van het aloude machtige Atjehsche rijk, zetelde het Ned. bestuur volgens de Atjehsche begrippen, door het recht van verovering, op den troon van den Sultan".

The phrase *zetelde het Ned. bestuur volgens de Atjehsche begrippen, door het recht van verovering, op den troon van den Sultan* — the Dutch administration sat, by Atjehnese understanding, by right of conquest, on the throne of the Sultan — is Van Heutsz's formula for the political accomplishment of the campaign. The campaign's culminating operation was the [[Selimoen tocht (1879)|tocht naar Selimoen]] — the expedition to [[Selimoen]] in the [[XXVI Moekims]] in September [[1879]]. Van Heutsz's name for it — *schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen* — marks it as the *schitterende* (splendid) close of the *XXVI* campaign.

### The day-order of 5 September 1879

The campaign closed, on [[5 September]] [[1879]], with the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order]] Van Heutsz reproduces in full from the original *Dagorder* issued at [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]]:

> "Dagorder. Officieren, Ambtenaren, Onderofficieren en Soldaten! Met den veldtocht tegen de XXVI Moekims, schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen; is geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt. De bevolking keert van lieverlede in hare kampongs terug; enkele aanzienlijke hoofden zijn reeds in onderwerping gekomen; er bestaat gegronde hoop dat velen dat voorbeeld zullen volgen. Krijgsbewegingen van grooten omvang zullen vermoedelijk niet meer noodig zijn, maar nog geruimen tijd wordt de krachtige steun der troepenmacht vereischt om een goed bestuur in te voeren en eene geregelde orde van zaken te scheppen. (1) Officieren, Onderofficieren en minderen! De krijgsoperatiën in de XXII en XXVI Moekims, die in Juli van het vorige jaar zijn aangevangen en zoo schitterende uitkomsten hebben opgeleverd, hebben Uwen krijgsroem verhoogd en glansrijk bevestigd dat Z. M. onze geëerbiedigde Koning recht heeft, trotsch te zijn op het leger en de vloot, die in Zijnen naam, Neerland's gezag in Indië helpen handhaven. LEVE DE KONING! Kota-Radja, 5 September 1879. De generaal-majoor, adjudant des Konings in buitengewonen dienst, Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden. K. VAN DER HEUDEN".

Van Heutsz's footnote — "(1) Deze cursiveering is van mij" — marks his editorial intervention: the italicised "*nog geruimen tijd wordt de krachtige steun der troepenmacht vereischt om een goed bestuur in te voeren en eene geregelde orde van zaken te scheppen*" is Van Heutsz's own emphasis, drawing the reader's attention to the General's measured warning that the military conquest would not, by itself, produce the political order. The day-order was independently confirmed by the September [[1879]] inspection report of the [[Commandant van het Indische leger|Commandant of the Indian army]] — who visited Atjeh in the same month and submitted his *verslag* to the Regeering.

### The 7 February 1879 brief and the 5 July 1879 telegram

Van der Heijden's first harbor closure of [[1878]] had been revoked by [[Van Lansberge|G.-G. van Lansberge]] by *brief* of [[7 February]] [[1879]] — "gelastte daarom bij brief van 7 Februari 1879 dien maatregel onverwijld in te trekken" — with the warning that "eene willekeurige daad van Generaal van Raders, Gouverneur van Suriname, tegenover een Oostenrijksch schip, gaf aanleiding tot eene aanklacht der Oostenrijksche Regeering, die de afzetting van Generaal Raders tengevolge had". When, however, the *veldtocht in de XXVI Moekims* opened, "de Generaal opnieuw de vaste overtuiging kreeg dat, wilde men thans den oorlog beëindigd zien, de havens opnieuw moesten gesloten worden", Van der Heijden "aarzelde geen oogenblik om daartoe over te gaan, hij sloot ze ten tweedemale en gaf bij telegram d.d. 5 Juli 1879 aan den Gouv. Generaal te kennen: Dat de havens opnieuw door hem waren gesloten, ditmaal op zijne persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid". The [[5 July]] [[1879]] telegram — the second harbor closure, undertaken on Van der Heijden's "persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid" — was reluctantly tolerated by the G.-G., but under "uitdrukkelijk voorbehoud, dat daaraan het karakter van een zeer tijdelijken oorlogsmaatregel niet mocht worden ontnomen". Van Heutsz's verdict is that the closure was "geen daad van militaire willekeur" but "een krachtige door het gezond verstand geboden dwangmaatregel" — a measure in the direction that would have allowed the colony to put "de kroon op het werk" — the crown on the work — had the G.-G. not *gedwarsboomd* (thwarted) it.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In **[[1879]]** the Dutch evacuated from Atjeh 124 officers, 5,577 other ranks and 3,681 convicts, and had to send out as replacements in the same year 277 officers, 6,394 other ranks and 6,982 convicts.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**1879** was the year in which Dutch troops first entered the terrain of the [[XXII Moekims]] around [[Anagaloeng]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], during the offensive operations of [[General van der Heijden]].

## In Hooijer's account

[[G. B. Hooijer]] recorded of the plain around [[Anagaloeng]] that *dit terrein, door onze troepen in 1879 voor het eerst betreden, was dan ook een lustoord in vergelijking met de lage landen, vol stinkende moerassen der XXVI Moekims, waaruit als het ware malaria opsteeg* — this terrain, first entered by Dutch troops in 1879, was a place of delight compared with the low lands full of stinking marshes of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]], out of which malaria seemed to rise. Fever-pale soldiers' faces regained their healthy brown colour on the drier ground.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

[[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] describes this as the year of [[Karel van der Heijden|generaal van der Heyden]], in whom the Dutch at last had a commander who made war "on the manner of war" (in oorlogs-manier): the operations in the [[XXII Moekims]] were resumed on [[23 March]] and on [[24 March]] [[1879]] the strong [[Capture of Indrapuri (24 March 1879)|missigit Indrapoerie]] — the old and famous sanctuary of the highlands, held by the irreconcilable [[Imam Longbattah]] — was taken with insignificant loss. In May and June operations against the fortified lines of [[Djerir|Sihong (Djerir)]], [[Gleieng]] and other principal centres of resistance followed; in the morning of [[5 June]] the extensive strong positions of Gleieng — the dwelling-place of the feared sagie-head [[Panglima Polim]], who had not dared to await the Dutch — were taken with slight loss, and 9-10 June were used to complete the success. There the veldtocht of the XXII Moekims ended: the resistance was broken at all points and those who wished to fight on repaired to the [[XXVI Moekims]]. On [[19 June]] the robber-people of [[Ladoeng]] and [[Kroeng Rajal]] on the North Coast were punished without Dutch loss. After solid preparation the veldtocht against the XXVI Moekims opened on [[30 June]]; brilliant successes were obtained within days, brightened by the continued pursuit of the scattered hostile bands. Trips to [[Lampanas]] (North Coast) and [[Lamtobah]] at the foot of the [[Selawa Djanten|Selawa Djanten (Goldberg)]] in July caused the Imam and other heads of Lamtobah to come in person to submit themselves; [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed]], one of the most tenacious enemies, who in remote [[Selimoen]] held the chief command in the XXII Moekims, now wrote to [[Toekoe Kadli]] (who had come over already in [[1874]]) that the great misfortune brought by the war over the three Sagies decided him to offer his submission. When he did not show up quickly, Van der Heyden had him fetched by a column ([[27 July]]-[[2 September]]); [[Selimoen]] was reached, after several tiring day-marches, almost without resistance on [[15 August]]; Toekoe Moeda Daoed awaited the column with a white flag, the population stayed in its kampongs, and on the same day [[Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet|Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet (Ajer-Alang)]], a brother of Panglima Polim, came into the bivouac to offer his submission. On 17 August both heads went with one of the columns to Indrapoerie — a triumphal march through kampongs planting white flags everywhere, even on the dominating hills — and on [[20 August]] they swore their submission to the general. In the XXVI Moekims too the people returned to their kampongs and offered small presents. Van der Heyden's Dagorder of [[5 September]] [[1879]] could declare "met den veldtocht tegen de XXVI Moekims, schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen, is GEHEEL GROOT-ATJEH ten onder gebracht" ("with the campaign against the XXVI Moekims, brilliantly crowned by the march to Selimoen, ALL GREAT-ATJEH is subdued"). From [[19 August]] to [[20 September]] the commandant of the Indies army, luitenant-generaal [[H. G. Boumeester|Boumeester]], visited Atjeh and crossed the whole territory with a small escort, "without hearing a shot, without seeing an enemy", reporting "Naar het schijnt, is eindelijk de overtuiging tot de Atjehsche bevolking doorgedrongen, dat wij de sterksten zijn" ("It seems that at last the conviction has penetrated to the Atjehnese population that we are the stronger"). On [[9 October]] [[1879]] the first foundation-stone of the new Dutch mosque near Kota Radja was laid in the presence of many chiefs of Groot-Atjeh, princes and regents of the vasal states, and of more than 6,000 Atjehers, who afterwards took part in the [[Kendoerie|kendoerie-feast]] (see [[Laying of the first stone of Meusigit Raja]]); and on [[18 November]] the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|van Lansberge]] himself, at a great banquet, said that the splendid war deeds of the last two years had brought the goal very near. In the same year the later reports could record that of prisoners, in a surprise of [[11 August]], more than 67, on 2 August 79, and on [[13 August]] 31 Atjehers were made, among whom [[Nja-Amoe]], one of the most tenacious enemies, "whose band had plundered a kampong completely the night before" (Kielstra III).

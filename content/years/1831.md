The year **1831** is the year of the [[American bombardment of the Atjehse coast (1831)|American bombardment of the Atjehse coast]], in which the United States government, "bij wijze van wraakneming — ontkennende der Nederlandse souvereiniteit," had the [[Achehnese|Atjehse]] coastal population chastised by one of its warships in retaliation for Atjehese piracy against American merchant shipping. The action is recorded by [[J. Frijling|Ir. J. Frijling]] in his [[1946]] sketch *Een stukje geschiedenis der Indische Buitengewesten* as one of the two nineteenth-century precedents — the other being the [[Bombardment of the Atjehse coast by the British (1844)|British bombardment of 1844]] — for the use of foreign naval force against the Atjehse coast in the absence of effective Dutch authority.

## Events of the Year

- The [[American bombardment of the Atjehse coast (1831)|American bombardment of the Atjehse coast]] — a punitive naval action by the United States against the Atjehse coastal population in retaliation for Atjehese piracy.

## Significance for Atjeh

The year [[1831]] marks the first foreign reprisal against the Atjehse coast for the failure of the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] to suppress Atjehese piracy after the undertaking of [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London|1814]]. The American framing of the action as a punishment "ontkennende der Nederlandse souvereiniteit" is particularly pointed: it asserts that, whatever the paper claims of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London|1814 treaty]], the Atjehse coast was, in the view of the American government, not effectively under Dutch sovereignty and therefore open to direct foreign retribution. The [[Bombardment of the Atjehse coast by the British (1844)|British bombardment of 1844]] would follow the American precedent.

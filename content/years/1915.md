**1915** was a year of projected administrative expansion and infrastructural development in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. As the global disruptions of the First World War began to affect colonial supply chains, the Dutch authorities in [[Sumatra]] relied on long-term budgetary projections to maintain governance and pacification efforts following the [[Aceh War]].

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The year [[1915]] is primarily documented in the *Atjeh up to date* compilation through multi-year colonial spending plans. Rather than recording immediate military costs, the surviving ledgers from this period focus on standardized, predictable provincial budgets:

*   **Budgetary Projections:** Colonial ledgers outline specific allocations for [[1915]], recording initial project or administrative spending at 50,250 guilders, with a broader aggregate budget category reaching 125,000 guilders.
*   **Administrative Consolidation:** These figures reflect a colony transitioning from the extreme, unpredictable costs of active guerrilla warfare to a stabilized, revenue-generating colonial province. The financial overhead of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons and local infrastructure was being absorbed into routine multi-year provincial budgets.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1915** was the year in which the colonial government came with a new regulation of the pepper-trade, by which the customs-office no longer had any concern. The *hacil tanah* or *wasé oeléebalang* was to be divided fifty-fifty between the *landschap* and the *pangkaigever* and the *peutoeha*. That regulation too has not encouraged the *pangkaigever*. By [[1915]] the [[Gouvernements rubberonderneming Langsa (1907)|gouvernements rubberonderneming]] at [[Langsa|Langsa]] had reached barely half its projected 1.000 *bouw* of planting; it then worked with 430 coolies, mostly contract labourers, and had a yield of 123.000 pounds of rubber at a cost-price of 66 cents.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In **[[1915]]** a smallpox epidemic struck the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|north coast]] and vaccination and re-vaccination were carried out on a large scale; the *[[Koloniaal Verslag]]* recorded "increasing confidence in the administration" and that no attacks on Europeans occurred during the *poeasa* month. In March three [[Alassers]] attacked the bivouac guard at [[Koeta Tjanè]]; in June *onderluitenant* [[Müssig]] was murdered at [[Tangsé]] and the *officier van gezondheid* [[De Vos]] wounded at [[Lho' Soekon]]; in January a Chinese was murdered at [[Sigli]]. In [[1915]] Swart laid the first stone of the [[Javasche Bank te Koetaradja]]; and "ever since 1915 I have been, in view of the political situation, wholly convinced of the possibility of that separation", he said in [[1918]].

**The year 1945** was, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the year of the return of the *vaandel* (colours) of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] to Lt [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|Onvlee]] on [[10 May 1945]], of the Japanese capitulation of [[15 August 1945]], and of Onvlee's November [[1945]] transfer to [[Bangkok|Bangkok]] where the final disposition of the vaandel was settled.

## The 10 May 1945 vaandel return

In the end his departure did not proceed, and he received the vaandel back on [[10 May 1945]]. Unfortunately the gold tassels were then missing, so that only the cloth and the [[Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)|Militaire Willemsorde]] came into his possession. The gold tassels' loss is the staff history's sole recorded material loss in the vaandel's history of survival across three and a half years of Japanese captivity.

## The 15 August 1945 capitulation

Now a period began in which the searches and unannounced inspections took place even more frequently and more thoroughly than before, as a consequence of the discovery of a very ingeniously concealed radio set. There were also rumours that the entire camp would be broken up and moved. As the risk of discovery grew ever greater, Onvlee asked the Korpscommandant — with whom he was then staying in the same camp — for permission to bury the vaandel in a place that could easily be found again; the Korpscommandant refused the request. On [[15 August 1945]], before the camp in Birma was broken up and moved, Japan capitulated, and Onvlee informed the senior Dutch officer present of the success of his undertaking.

## The November 1945 Bangkok transfer and disposition

In November [[1945]] he was transferred to Bangkok, where he heard rumours that there were plans to incorporate the vaandel in a mixed company of Ambonese Maréchaussees and infantrymen. Onvlee objected to the Korpscommandant, both on the ground that it was unfitting for the vaandel of a Maréchaussee corps and on the authority of the original Territoriaal Commandant's order that the vaandel be handed to the competent authorities in the Netherlands East Indies. The Korpscommandant demanded the vaandel back, and Onvlee gave it up; a plan for the vaandel to be formally presented to Onvlee before the assembled troops was never carried out. Since after the war the Korps Maréchaussee was not re-established, no official presentation took place on Java either. After the dissolution of the [[KNIL|K.N.I.L.]] in July 1950, the vaandel was given a place in the Army Museum at Leiden.

## Significance

The year [[1945]] is, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the *terminus ad quem* of the staff history's narrative of the Dutch wartime presence in Atjeh, and the year of the formal Japanese capitulation that closed the Dutch captivity. The staff history's emphasis on the 10 May [[1945]] vaandel return, on the [[15 August 1945]] capitulation, and on the November [[1945]] Bangkok transfer frames [[1945]] as the year that resolved every open strand of the Atjeh chapter's post-capitulation narrative — the Van Zanten resistance closed by death in [[1943]], the vaandel survival closed by the capitulation of [[1945]], and the Korps Maréchaussee closed by the K.N.I.L.'s dissolution in [[1950]].

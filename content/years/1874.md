## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year in which the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] was formally inaugurated as an administrative unit of the Netherlands-Indies, with [[J. L. J. H. Pel|J. L. J. H. Pel]] appointed as its first Military and Civil Commander (*Mil. en Civ. bevelhebber*). The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* opens its Bijlage I — the chronological list of *Hoofden van Gewestelijk Bestuur van 1874 tot heden* — with this year, treating it as the founding moment of the colonial administration that the handbook describes.

## The transition from military expedition to government

The year [[1874]] followed the first Dutch military expeditions against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] of [[1873]] — expeditions that had ended the formal authority of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate]] and begun the protracted conflict known as the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]]. Pel's appointment formalised the transition from ad hoc military occupation to the institutional structure of a *Gouvernement*; the dual military-civil character of his office — *Mil. en Civ. bevelhebber* — reflected the hybrid nature of a territory that was, simultaneously, a theatre of war and a colonial administrative unit. The structure would persist, with periodic adjustments, through the governorships of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]], [[G. C. E. van Daalen|van Daalen]], and [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]].

## Administrative genesis

The boundaries of the new Gouvernement were, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "bij gebrek aan volkomen betrouwbare gegevens" — for lack of fully reliable data — only approximately known. The *Regeerings-Almanak* of [[1916]] would give the area as 966.6 square kilometres, while the legend of the Sumatra overview map of [[1913]] gave 1066.2; either way, the territory covered roughly one-eighth of the entire island and was "ruim anderhalfmaal zoo groot als die van Nederland" — fully one and a half times the size of the Netherlands. The Gouvernement was divided into five *afdeelingen*: [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], [[Noordkust van Atjeh|Noordkust]], [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust]], [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]], and the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] — a structure that, with later refinements, would survive until the Japanese occupation.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year of the taking of the [[Missigit (Meusigit)|Missigit]] (the great mosque) of [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] — the first major Dutch military success of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], and the symbolic opening of the war. The Missigit had been the object of the failed [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of April [[1873]]; the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]] completed the work. The year opened the forty-five-year war that would end with the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]].

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954).

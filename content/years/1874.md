**[[1874]]** was the year in which the second Dutch expedition, led by [[Generaal Van Swieten]], finally captured the [[kraton]] (the royal palace) in [[Koeta Radja]] on [[24 January]]. The event—followed two days later by the death of the young sultan—appeared to improve prospects for rapid pacification, but the resistance led by [[Panglima Polim]] demonstrated that the war would not end with the fall of the capital.

## Key events

- [[24 January]]: Capture of the [[kraton]] by Dutch forces under [[Generaal Van Swieten]].
- [[26 January]]: Death of the young sultan—only two days after the capture.
- Significant chiefs from the [[XXV]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] come to pay respects to [[Van Swieten]], and the population of those sagi's shows signs of being tired of further struggle.
- [[Panglima Polim]] (then [[Sri Moeda Perkasa]]) refuses submission and makes his territory a gathering place for continued resistance.
- [[Generaal Pel]] succeeds [[Van Swieten]] in [[April 1874]] and begins more vigorous military operations.

Although the capture of the [[kraton]] and the death of the sultan seemed to improve prospects for peace, the resistance organised from the [[XXII Moekims]]—combined with the spiritual leadership emerging in [[Keumala]]—ensured that the conflict would continue for another three decades.

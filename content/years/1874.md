**1874** was a pivotal year in the Dutch colonial campaign against the Sultanate of [[Aceh]], encompassing the fall of the Kraton, the proclamation of annexation of [[Groot‑Atjeh|Groot‑Atjeh]], the formal inauguration of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], a severe cholera epidemic, the resignation of [[J. Loudon|Governor‑General Loudon]] over the [[conversiequaestie]], and the commencement of intensified Dutch marine operations under Lieutenant [[J. B. Verhey]] in [[July]] [[1874]]. The events of the year linked military, administrative, and naval dimensions of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh‑krijg]] and set the stage for further expansion in [[1875]].

## Military Campaigns in Aceh (1874)

### January: The Fall of the Kraton

On [[6 January]] [[1874]] the [[Missigit|missigit]] of the Kraton was taken by troops of the [[Second Aceh Expedition ([[1873]]‑[[1874]])|second Aceh expedition]]. On [[12 January]] [[1874]] the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]] (or Kota Goenoengan) was stormed on the advice of [[Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia|Toekoe Nek]]. A Dutch reconnaissance led by [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]] entered the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] unopposed on [[24 January]] [[1874]] and hoisted the Dutch flag on the watch‑house. The sultan died of cholera at Pagger Ajer on [[26 January]] [[1874]]. Van Swieten issued his [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January [[1874]]|proclamation of annexation]] on [[31 January]] [[1874]], formally annexing [[Groot‑Atjeh|Groot‑Atjeh]] by right of conquest.

### February‑April: Continuation under Van Swieten

During February‑April [[1874]] Dutch forces continued operations in proper Atjeh. On [[12 February]] [[1874]] Lieutenant‑colonel [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heijden]] advanced against Bilal, occupied by [[Toekoe Nanta|Toekoe Nanta]]. On [[15 February]] [[1874]] Lieutenant‑colonel [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] attacked the fortified Ketapan Doewa, whose defenses fell after a stubborn fight. The Dutch bivouac at [[Penajoeng|Penajoeng]] was attacked on [[11 April]] [[1874]], and an échec near [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] on [[16 April]] [[1874]] resulted in 9 dead and 86 wounded, including 10 officers.

### May‑August: Van Swieten’s Departure and Pel’s Appointment

On [[4 May]] [[1874]] Van Swieten departed from Atjeh, ending his expedition. [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] assumed command of both military operations and the civil government. The summer months saw the establishment of the [[Concentratie‑lijn|concentratie‑lijn]], a fortified line around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] that would become the basis of Dutch strategy. Throughout the year, cholera and other diseases claimed more lives than combat, underscoring the health crisis that accompanied the campaign.

### December: The Conversiequaestie and Political Crisis

In December [[1874]] Governor‑General [[J. Loudon]] resigned over the [[conversiequaestie]], a controversy concerning the Dutch government’s religious policies in the Indies. His resignation, occurring at a critical juncture in the Aceh War, reflected broader political tensions in the Netherlands over colonial and religious policy.

## Administrative Developments

According to *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917), [[1874]] marked the formal inauguration of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] as an administrative unit of the Netherlands‑Indies. [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] was appointed the first *Mil. en Civ. bevelhebber* (Military and Civil Commander). The handbook treats this year as the founding moment of the colonial administration that would govern the territory, which remained a hybrid war zone and civil jurisdiction. The exact boundaries of the new Gouvernement were noted as “bij gebrek aan volkomen betrouwbare gegevens” (lacking fully reliable data).

## Naval and Marine Operations (July 1874)

In [[July]] [[1874]] Lieutenant [[J. B. Verhey]] began compiling a report on Dutch marine operations along the Aceh coast. This report inaugurated a two‑year period of intensified naval‑marine activity that prepared the larger expedition launched in [[1875]]. The *Marine operations on Sumatra* documentation, later referenced in naval histories, highlighted the coordination of steam‑powered gunboats, coastal artillery, and supply convoys that supported the land campaigns described above.

## Snijders’s 1874 Campaign

[[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede Luitenant C. J. Snijders]] of the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs]] transferred from the *Staf der Genie* at [[Surabaya]] to the field army in [[Aceh]] in early [[1874]]. He arrived at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] on [[8 November]] [[1874]] and, less than a week later, participated in the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November [[1874]])|Benting Kota Alam operation]] on [[13 November]] [[1874]]. Under the command of [[M. C. E. Ruempol|Luitenant‑Kolonel M. C. E. Ruempol]], Snijders led a sapper column that constructed fortifications at the river bend near the [[Acheh River|Atjeh River]], later named [[Kuta Alam|Benting Kota Alam]]. The operation secured the eastern flank of the Dutch positions and is detailed on its dedicated event page.

## Significance

The events of [[1874]] crystallised the transition from ad‑hoc military expeditions to a permanent colonial administration in Aceh. The fall of the Kraton and the proclamation of annexation eliminated the last vestiges of sovereign Acehnese rule, while the establishment of the [[Concentratie‑lijn]] and the inauguration of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] institutionalised Dutch control. Simultaneously, the onset of coordinated marine operations under Lieutenant [[J. B. Verhey]] expanded the Dutch logistical reach, foreshadowing the larger expedition of [[1875]]. The year also exposed the fragility of colonial governance, as disease and political controversy (the [[conversiequaestie]]) strained both the army and the metropolitan administration.

## Account from *De Atjeh-Oorlog* by J.A. Kruijt (1896)

**1874**, in [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]]'s *De Atjeh-Oorlog* ([[1896]]), was the year of the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]]'s decisive operations — the fall of the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], the death of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] on [[26 January]] [[1874]], the [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] proclamation annexing [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], and the departure of Van Swieten on [[26 April]] [[1874]] leaving [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Colonel Pel]] with 3,000 men.

The second expedition had arrived at the Atjeh roadstead towards the end of [[November 1873|November]] [[1873]] but the landing was delayed by cholera and heavy rains until [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]]. The expeditionary force of approximately 9,000 combatants advanced under heavy fighting. On [[7 January]] [[7 January]] [[1874]] the [[Meusigit Raja|groote Missigit]] was taken. On [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] fell into Dutch hands — "*Dit feit maakte overal diepen indruk*" (this fact made a deep impression everywhere). Two days later, on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]], the Sultan died of cholera. Van Swieten thereupon proposed that the Dutch should step into the Sultan's place, drafting a proclamation to the chiefs accordingly. The position chosen as the nucleus of the Dutch *stelling* (defensive position) was the kraton itself.

The result of Van Swieten's *proclamatietocht* (proclamation tour) was, however, that on the [[Noordkust|North]] and [[Westkust|West Coasts]] the flag and the act of recognition were "*veelal eenvoudig door een boodschapper aan land gezonden, zonder meer*" (usually simply sent ashore by a messenger, and nothing more), and trade was opened without supervision — the great mistake of the *[[Vlaghijscherij|vlaghijscherij]]* that sustained the Atjehese resistance for the next decade. On [[16 April]] [[16 April]] [[1874]] a Dutch column was repulsed before an enemy fortification — an *échec* that went unavenged and demonstrated that the enemy had lost nothing of his courage. On [[26 April]] [[26 April]] [[1874]] — one year to the day after the re-embarkation of the first expedition — Van Swieten returned to Java, leaving Pel with instructions for "*beveiliging onzer stelling en de gemeenschap naar zee in Groot-Atjeh, gepaard met streven naar onderwerping*." The *stelling* proved wholly inadequate; by [[December 1874|December]] [[1874]] the troop strength had already doubled to 6,000 men.

## Account from *Een Vaderlandsch Belang* by H. Borel (1878)

**[[1874]]** was the year in which the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Dutch expedition]] against [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] culminated in the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], and in which [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] declared the war ended and departed — bequeathing to [[Generaal Pel|Kolonel Pel]] a "zeer gebrekkige stelling" that would require two more years of costly reconstruction. In Borel's account it is the most eventful and the most catastrophic year of the war: a brilliant tactical success at the Kraton was squandered by the *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture), and the year closed in the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|Longbatta disaster]] of [[31 December]] [[1874]].

## Fall of the Kraton (January [[1874]])

The year opened with the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|storming of the Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]], in which Dutch troops attacked the fortified voorwerk of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] frontally, "tegen alle Indische traditiën in, zonder noemenswaardige voorbereiding," at a cost of 220 dood en gekwetsten. [[Kolonel de Roy van Zuydewijn|de Roy van Zuydewijn]] led the frontal assault while [[Generaal Pel|luitenant-kolonel Pel]] flanked the work and entered unopposed. On [[12 January]] [[1874]] the [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan (12 January 1874)|capture of Kota Goenoengan]] showed that the vijand's hoofdmacht had already withdrawn. An [[Acehnese attack on Penajoeng bivouak (13 January 1874)|Acehnese midday attack]] on [[Penajoeng]] on [[13 January]] penetrated nearly to the tent of the opperbevelhebber. After a [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|nineteen-day bombardment]] — whose munitie had not been calculated for sustained fire — [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]]'s omtrekkende beweging of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] delivered the deserted Kraton "zonder slag of stoot." A [[Reconnaissance of the Atjeh-rivier (29 January 1874)|failed reconnaissance]] of the Atjeh-rivier above the Kraton on [[29 January]] [[1874]] cost two officers and "ruim 20 minderen"; on the same day the [[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|bataljon Barissans van Madura]] arrived, and on [[1 February]] [[1874]] the 6de bataljon Infanterie.

## Annexation and the End of the Offensive (February–March [[1874]])

In early February the young [[Sultan of Atjeh (d. 1874)|Sultan of Atjeh]] died, and by the [[Annexation proclamation of Atjeh (1874)|annexation proclamation]] the Indische regeering assumed the opperbestuur over the rijk. The decision was made by telegraph between van Swieten and the minister of Koloniën; simultaneously a blijvende vestiging was decided. On [[12 February]] [[1874]] the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|expedition to Bital]] was undertaken at the request of [[Toekoe Nek]]; on [[15 February]] [[1874]] the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]] followed with six halve bataljons — "eene voor Indië zeer aanzienlijke macht" that was "geheel overbodig." Both kampongs were captured and immediately abandoned. After [[15 February]] van Swieten "stak het zwaard voor goed in de scheede" and applied the *afwachtende houding*: "Geen vechten meer met den vijand, maar werken met schop en spade." The Javaansche hulptroepen of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]], a veld-batterij, one company of [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] and the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.]] were sent back to Java — "Wat den opperbevelhebber bewoog zich zoo ontijdig en onnoodig te ontdoen van het beste geschut dat hij bezat, valt moeilijk te beslissen." Van Swieten also drove the gemoedelijkheid so far as to permit [[Padi-snijden on the Langkroek sawah (1874)|padi-snijden]] on the [[Langkroek]] [[Sawah|sawah]], with the result that as soon as the last bundle was harvested, the Atjehneezen "weder van alle zijden kwamen opdagen."

## Lampoe Oek and Van Swieten's Departure (April [[1874]])

On [[7 April]] [[1874]] van Swieten issued to Pel an instruction for daily patrols from [[Pakan Atjeh]] to the Taman — a "hoogst nuttige maatregel" that, Borel insisted, should have been applied "van af de inneming van den Kraton, en niet 2½ maand daarna." An [[Acehnese attack on Penajoeng (11 April 1874)|Acehnese massed attack on Penajoeng]] on [[11 April]] [[1874]] was repulsed by the artillerie. On [[15 April]] [[1874]] [[Kapitein van Aerssen|kapitein van Aerssen]] conquered a [[Benteng|benteng]] mounting an iron cannon; the next day the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|battle of Lampoe Oek]] ended in "eene geweldig échec" for the Dutch. [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]]'s column, sent to attack a strongly fortified enemy stelling near [[Longbatta]] of which the Dutch had known nothing, was repulsed by "bijna onoverkomelijke terreinhindernisen" with "bijna 100 dooden en gewonden," of whom five were "vermist." Van Swieten was "zeer verbolgen," blamed Romswinckel entirely, and refused him a hearing. On [[23 April]] [[1874]] van Swieten delivered his [[Van Swieten's afscheidsrede (1874)|afscheidsrede]] — "Mijne heeren, ik beschouw den oorlog met Atjeh als geëindigd" — and on [[26 April]] [[1874]] he [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)|departed]] on the *Prins Alexander der Nederlanden*. "Met zijn vertrek was het tweede bedrijf van het Atjehsche drama afgespeeld."

## The Bezettingskorps Under Pel (May–December [[1874]])

The [[Desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe (1874)|desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe]] on [[26 April]] [[1874]] and the [[Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)|crisis of May–June 1874]] were the "eerste wrange vrucht" of the passive system. Pel, who was left with a [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]] far below required strength, reversed the policy: from [[28 April]] [[1874]] he ordered the daily bombardment of kampongs within range, and on [[4 May]] [[1874]] he met [[Toekoe Nek]] and decided to bezet [[Marassa]], [[Oeleh Leh]] and other posts — Borel clarifies that the so-called [[Strategische driehoek (Atjeh)|strategische driehoek]] Kwala Atjeh–Kota Radja–Oeleh Leh was Pel's work, not van Swieten's. The kwade moesson broke on [[31 May]] [[1874]], rendering the [[Atjeh-rivier]] onbevaarbaar and proving the fout of never building a [[Zeehoofd|zeehoofd]] at Oeleh Leh. By June, Borel judged, 500 kloeke Atjehneezen could have taken the Kraton. With the first reinforcements of [[11 June]] [[1874]] Pel resumed the offensive: the [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|battle of Lampoeloe]] ([[20 June]] [[1874]]), the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] ([[28 June]] [[1874]]), the [[Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)|battle of Soerian]] ([[26 July]] [[1874]]), the [[Occupation of the ooster-benteng (17 August 1874)|occupation of the ooster-benteng]] ([[17 August]] [[1874]]), the [[Destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification (18 September 1874)|destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification]] ([[18 September]] [[1874]]), the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] ([[28 September]] [[1874]]), the [[Battle outside the ooster-face (13 October 1874)|battle outside the ooster-face]] ([[13 October]] [[1874]] — the [[Compagnie mariniers in Atjeh|compagnie mariniers]]' first fight), the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] ([[7 November]] [[1874]]) and the [[Conquest of Kota Alam (13 November 1874)|conquest of Kota Alam]] ([[13 November]] [[1874]]). A [[Bandjir flood of Kota Radja (December 1874)|bandjir]] in early December flooded [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] for eight days and destroyed vast quantities of vivres and munitie. The year ended with the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|disastrous battle of Longbatta]] ([[31 December]] [[1874]]), in which [[Majoor van der Meer|van der Meer]]'s column was surrounded on the open sawah and cut to pieces, saved only by [[Majoor van Zijll de Jong|van Zijll de Jong]]'s heroic recapture of the lost benteng.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the founding constitutional acts

[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* treats [[1874]] as the year in which the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|Atjeh-oorlog]] begun in [[1873]] was formally consolidated into a Dutch colonial government. The year opened with the *succes onzer wapenen in 't einde van 1873 en 't begin van 1874* — the success of the Dutch arms at the end of [[1873]] and the beginning of [[1874]] — by which the [[Kraton|kraton]] of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] was captured and the colonial seat at [[Kota Radja]] established. Van Heutsz treats the two early-February [[1874]] documents by [[Van Swieten|Generaal van Swieten]] as the foundational acts of the Dutch colonial regime.

### The 8 February 1874 letter and the 12 February 1874 proclamation

The earlier of the two — Van Swieten's *brief* (letter) of [[8 February]] [[1874]] to *Imam Longbatta* and *T. Kadli* — announced the conversion of the captured kraton into the Dutch seat and the projected founding of a great commercial city at Atjeh:

> "wat Generaal van Swieten in zijn brief van 8 Februari 1874 aan Imam Longbata en T. Kadli schreef: 'De kraton zal worden ingericht tot eene Kota voor de Nederlanders, conder wier bestuur veiligheid en bescherming zal worden gegeven aan sallen, die zich in de nabijheid nederzetten. Te Atjeh xal een groote shandelsstad worden gestichts'".

Four days later, on [[12 February]] [[1874]], Van Swieten issued the formal *proclamatie* by which the Dutch government declared itself sovereign in place of the sultan. Van Heutsz gives the constitutional formula: "*het aanvankelijk regime, uitgedrukt in de proclamatie van Generaal van Swieten van 12 Februari 1874: 'dat het Nederlandsche Gouvernement Souverein is, in plaats van den sultan'*". The [[1893]] tract's prescriptive conclusion returns to this proclamation as the regime the Regeering should "*terugkeeren tot*" — return to — and explicitly extends it: "*en er thans rondweg bij te verklaren, dat het Nederlandsche Gouvernement ook Souverein blijven zal zonder den sultan*".

### Van Swieten's Instructie (22 April 1874) and Besluit G.-G. No. 1 (26 May 1874)

The founding policy document of the Atjeh government is [[Van Swieten's Instructie (22 April 1874)|Van Swieten's Instructie]], drafted as a *Nota voor den Kolonel Pel* from Penang on [[22 April]] [[1874]] — within months of Van Swieten's handover of the governorship to his successor [[Pel (governor)|Generaal Pel]] — and approved by *Besluit van den Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederl. Indië dd. 26 Mei 1874 No. 1*. The *Instructie* and the *Nota* together fixed the *richting* Van Swieten wished to see followed in both [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the [[Onderhoorigheden]]. Its substantive content — the choice of a *kleine maar sterke positie* in the most weighty part of the valley of Groot-Atjeh, the *geconcentreerde stelling* at [[Kota Radja|Kotja-Radja]] to be held and actively defended by a garrison of 2500 men, plus a few posts to secure the line of communication to the sea — was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the moderate programme whose premature abandonment in [[1875]] on the proposal of [[Pel (governor)|Pel]] and [[Van Lansberge]] would prove the original *groote politieke fout* of the entire Atjeh campaign. Article 5 of the *Instructie* — the article dealing with the *Onderhoorigheden* — contemplated conciliation of the submitted *kuststaten* and *blokkade* of the recalcitrant, but did not commit the colony to the systematic closure of the whole coast.

Van Heutsz's summary preserves Van Swieten's reasoning for the concentrated *stelling*:

> "De Generaal, even uitnemend staatsman als krijgsoverste en grondig bekend met onze draagkracht, hield daarbij met wijs beleid het oog gevestigd op de toekomst en voorziende, dat onze financieele en personeele krachten zouden overschreden worden, als wij een rijk als het Atjehsche door wapengeweld willende veroveren, daarin niet zeer spoedig slaagden, gaf hij de voorkeur aan het bezetten van eene kleine maar sterke positie, die wij actief moesten verdedigen, koos voor die positie het meest gewichtige gedeelte van de vallei van Groot-Atjeh en gaf te kennen, dat de politiek moest en zou voltooien, wat de wapenen zoo gelukkig begonnen hadden".

The choice of "het meest gewichtige gedeelte van de vallei van Groot-Atjeh" for the *stelling* fixed, for the next two decades, the geographic frame of the Dutch presence in the interior; and the *stelling* itself, as Van Heutsz's narrative would later show, would be the position the colony returned to in [[1884]] under the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] — but without the active-defensive posture Van Swieten had prescribed.

### The death of Sultan Machmoed Sjah

The dynastic event of [[1874]] was the death of [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah]]. Van Heutsz's references to the death are scattered across the tract — the Sultan is "*den in 1874 overleden sultan Machmoed Sjah*" (p. 26), "*de laatste Sultan Machmoed Sjah, die in 1874 overleed*" (p. 30), the predecessor whose death left the sultanate without an installed successor and opened the dynastic vacuum the war party at [[Kemala]] would later exploit. The death is referenced, in the genealogical-constitutional chapter of the tract, in the context of the succession dispute that would absorb the next decade: "*want dat niet slechts de laatste Sultan Machmoed Sjah, die in 1874 overleed, gesproten is uit een z. g. goendiq*" — the last Sultan Machmoed Sjah, who died in 1874, was himself the offspring of a *goendiq* (a lower-ranking wife) — and the lineage was therefore, in Van Heutsz's genealogical argument, already factually *verbasterd* (degenerated) at the moment of his death.

The dynastic vacuum Machmoed Sjah's death opened was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the structural condition under which [[Toeankoe Haschim]]'s later manipulation of [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]] became possible: "*in 1874 aan Toeankoe Haschim, die in Atjeh vervreemd was, niet gelukte om zich tot sultan te doen kiezen*" — in 1874, Haschim, who was a stranger in Atjeh, did not succeed in having himself chosen as sultan — but the *voorname hoofden* themselves, in [[1874]], "*deden cenvoudig geen keus*" (made no choice at all), preferring to keep *het heft in handen* in their own territories. The death of Sultan Machmoed Sjah in [[1874]] thus produced, in Van Heutsz's narrative, both the dynastic vacuum and the political immobilism that would, in [[1878]], give Haschim the opening to install Mohamad Daoed at the [[Indrapoeri]] mosque.

**[[1874]]** was a decisive year in the [[Aceh War]], marked by the capture of the [[Kraton of Aceh|Acehnese capital]], the death of the sultan, and the Dutch proclamation of annexation. The second Dutch expedition, led by [[Jan van Swieten|Generaal Van Swieten]], finally captured the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] in [[Koeta Radja]] on [[24 January]]. The event—followed two days later by the death of the young sultan—appeared to improve prospects for rapid pacification, but the resistance led by [[Panglima Polim]] demonstrated that the war would not end with the fall of the capital.

## Chronology

- **[[6 January]]**: [[Third Capture of Mesjid Raya]]; Dutch 2nd Brigade permanently occupies the [[Mesjid Raya Aceh]].
- **[[12 January]]**: [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan]] clears western defenses of the royal palace.
- **[[15 January]]**: [[Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX]] evacuates the [[Kraton of Aceh]] for [[Longbatta]].
- **[[24 January]]–[[26 January]]**: [[Encirclement and Fall of the Kraton]]; Dutch columns under Major-General [[G.M. Verspijck]] and Lt-Col [[Karel van der Heijden]] occupy the deserted palace. Significant chiefs from the [[XXV Mukims|XXV]] and [[XXVI Mukims]] come to pay respects to [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]], showing signs of exhaustion.
- **[[28 January]]**: Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX dies of cholera at [[Longbatta]].
- **[[31 January]]**: General [[Jan van Swieten]] issues the [[Proclamation of Annexation of Aceh]].
- **[[12 February]]**: [[Battle of Bital]]; Dutch column protects allied chief [[Teuku Nja]].
- **[[15 February]]**: [[Battle of Ketapan Doewa]] destroys anti-colonial stronghold.
- **[[16 April]]**: [[Skirmish of April 16, 1874]] near Kampong Lampu-Uk.
- **[[20 April]]**: Dissolution of the [[Second Aceh Expedition]]; Colonel [[Jacobus Pel]] appointed Governor of Aceh and succeeds Van Swieten, beginning more vigorous military operations.

## Strategic significance

[[Panglima Polim]] (then [[Sri Moeda Perkasa]]) refused submission and made his territory in the [[XXII Moekims]] a gathering place for continued resistance. Although the capture of the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] and the death of the sultan seemed to improve prospects for peace, the resistance organised from the [[XXII Moekims]]—combined with the spiritual leadership emerging in [[Keumala]]—ensured that the conflict would continue for another three decades.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

[[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]]'s pamphlet of [[1886]] fixes the year's decisive episodes. After the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]], the opperbevelhebber [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] — under whose Dagorder of that day the enemy was declared "conquered but not yet subdued" — issued the proclamations of [[28 January]] and [[31 January]] and reported to the chiefs of the three Sagies, and on [[12 February]] he passed to the [[Annexation proclamation of Atjeh (1874)|annexation of the whole realm]] while two days before he had told his journal (4 February) that "iedere ontmoeting met den vijand, iedere beweging veroorzaakt ons verliezen, die schier niet meer aan te vullen zijn" ("every meeting with the enemy, every movement causes us losses hardly to be replenished any more") — in the same hours telegraphing to Batavia that with the conquest of the Kraton "the power of resistance was broken" (4 February) and "geene vijandelijkheden ... Volk is vredesgezind" ("no hostilities ... the people is peace-minded", 7 February). On [[29 January]] a reconnaissance to [[Longbatta|Longbattah]] had to retire before a numerous enemy. The secret sessions of the Kamer of 16-18 April discussed the origin of the war; minister [[Gericke|Gericke van Herwijnen]] closed with "In plaats van den aangeboden olijftak verkozen zij het zwaard" ("Instead of the offered olive branch they chose the sword") — words Verstege subscribed, but on which he commented that it was then inadmissible to keep offering olive branches that were answered with klewang blows. On [[16 April]] the storm-force suffered, close by the walls south of [[Lampoe Oek]], a painful defeat of 9 dead and 86 wounded, including 10 officers (see [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)]]). On [[26 April]] van Swieten departed with the so-called main force (see [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)]]), leaving behind a chain of posts numbered in the pamphlet: 128 officers and 3,151 men, of whom 178 Europeans and 41 natives in hospital. The same evening the enemy moved in force south and east of the Kraton, and the next day punished the friendly moekim [[Marasa|Marassa]], burning seven kampongs; on 27 April the [[Rivierbenting|Marinebenting]] and the river posts were alarmed. Sickness ravaged the garrison all year: on 12 May 308 ambulance patients and 223 sick in quarters, on 20 May one-seventh of the force, and by December 902 dead and 1,917 evacuated; reinforcements had to be demanded repeatedly: three companies on 11 September, further companies on 8 October, and two half battalions with artillery on 8-9 December, bringing the garrison to double the strength van Swieten had fixed. Van Swieten preached the "zuiver afwachtende houding" (pure waiting posture) to his successor [[Jacobus Pel|Pel]] in the note of 22 April ("De politiek moet en zal voltooien, wat de wapenen zoo gelukkig begonnen hebben"), wrote to the Governor-General from Buitenzorg on [[19 July]] his list of moderate demands, and answered ministerial inquiry from the Netherlands on 17 October ("de versterkte kampong daarachter en daarnaast ... viel niet in de bedoeling om die aan te tasten") and 27 October 1874 ("niet wij, maar hij wordt door die vruchtlooze aanvallen uitgeput") — judgments the year was to disprove. His proclamation of [[12 February]] [[1874]] was that same year confirmed by minister [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]], whom [[W. van Goltstein|van Goltstein]] replaced on [[28 August]] [[1874]]. The year ended with the decision of war, the old troops and the new reinforcements alike foreshadowing that harder work of [[1875]] (see [[1875]]).

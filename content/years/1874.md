## Account from *De geschiedenis van Nederland in onzen tijd* (1912) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year of the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]], the [[Death of Sultan Aladin Mahmood Shah|death of the sultan]] on [[26 January]] [[26 January]], and the [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation of annexation]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] on [[31 January]] [[31 January]], followed by the departure of [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]] in April, the continuation of the war under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] through the year, and the resignation of [[J. Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]] over the [[conversiequaestie]] in December. The year was marked throughout by heavy losses from cholera and other diseases, and by the establishment of the [[Concentratie-linie|concentratie-linie]] around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]].

### January: The Fall of the Kraton

On [[6 January]] [[6 January]] [[1874]] the [[Missigit|missigit]] of the Kraton was taken by the troops of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]]. On [[12 January]] [[12 January]] the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]] or Kota Goenoengan was stormed and taken on the advice of [[Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia|Toekoe Nek]]. On [[24 January]] [[24 January]] a reconnaissance sent by Van Swieten from Kota Goenoengan entered the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] unopposed and hoisted the Dutch flag on the watch-house. On [[26 January]] [[26 January]] the sultan died of cholera at Pagger Ajer. On [[31 January]] [[31 January]] Van Swieten issued his [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation]] annexing Groot-Atjeh by right of conquest.

### February-April: The Continuation under Van Swieten

In February-April [[1874]] the Dutch operations continued in proper Atjeh: on [[12 February]] [[12 February]] Lieutenant-colonel [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heijden]] advanced against Bilal, occupied by [[Toekoe Nanta|Toekoe Nanta]]; on [[15 February]] [[15 February]] Lieutenant-colonel [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] advanced against the well-fortified Ketapan Doewa, whose fortifications were taken after a stubborn fight. On [[11 April]] [[11 April]] the Dutch bivouac at [[Penajoeng|Penajoeng]] was attacked by the enemy. On [[16 April]] [[16 April]] the Dutch suffered an échec near Kota Radja, in which a column sent by Colonel Pel failed to take an enemy fortification, with a loss of 9 dead and 86 wounded, among whom 10 officers.

### May-August: Van Swieten's Departure and Pel's Appointment

On [[4 May]] [[4 May]] [[1874]] Van Swieten departed from Atjeh, his expedition concluded. [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] took command of the operations and the civil government. The summer months were marked by the establishment of the [[Concentratie-linie|concentratie-linie]], the fortified line around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] that would become the basis of Dutch strategy. Throughout the year, cholera and other diseases claimed more lives than combat: the health crisis was as devastating as the military campaign.

### December: The Conversiequaestie and Political Crisis

In December [[1874]] [[J. Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]] resigned over a political crisis — the [[conversiequaestie]] — concerning the government's religious policies in the Indies. This resignation, coming at a critical moment in the Aceh War, reflected the broader political tensions in the Netherlands over colonial policy and the religious question.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year in which the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] was formally inaugurated as an administrative unit of the Netherlands-Indies, with [[J. L. J. H. Pel|J. L. J. H. Pel]] appointed as its first Military and Civil Commander (*Mil. en Civ. bevelhebber*). The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* opens its Bijlage I — the chronological list of *Hoofden van Gewestelijk Bestuur van 1874 tot heden* — with this year, treating it as the founding moment of the colonial administration that the handbook describes.

The year [[1874]] followed the first Dutch military expeditions against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] of [[1873]] — expeditions that had ended the formal authority of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate]] and begun the protracted conflict known as the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]]. Pel's appointment formalised the transition from ad hoc military occupation to the institutional structure of a *Gouvernement*; the dual military-civil character of his office reflected the hybrid nature of a territory that was, simultaneously, a theatre of war and a colonial administrative unit. The boundaries of the new Gouvernement were, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "bij gebrek aan volkomen betrouwbare gegevens" — for lack of fully reliable data — only approximately known.

## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year of the [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]] missive of [[8 February]] [[1874]] — the well-known lines that the great mosque would be rebuilt and made into a true place of mercy, from which no more death and destruction, but prayers to the almighty God and works of love would go out — and the year that followed the [[1873]] storming of the [[Meusigit Raja|mosque]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] by the Dutch troops. The grey war-hero, generaal Van Swieten, in a missive of [[8 February]] [[1874]] to some Acehnese chiefs, wrote the well-known lines: that the great mosque would be rebuilt and made into a true place of mercy. The [[8 February]] [[1874]] missive is the clearest statement of the colonial state's religious politics in [[Atjeh|Aceh]].

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year of the taking of the [[Missigit (Meusigit)|Missigit]] (the great mosque) of [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] — the first major Dutch military success of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], and the symbolic opening of the war. The Missigit had been the object of the failed [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of April [[1873]]; the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]] completed the work. The year opened the forty-five-year war that would end with the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]].

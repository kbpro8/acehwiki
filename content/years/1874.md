## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year in which the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] was formally inaugurated as an administrative unit of the Netherlands-Indies, with [[J. L. J. H. Pel|J. L. J. H. Pel]] appointed as its first Military and Civil Commander (*Mil. en Civ. bevelhebber*). The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* opens its Bijlage I — the chronological list of *Hoofden van Gewestelijk Bestuur van 1874 tot heden* — with this year, treating it as the founding moment of the colonial administration that the handbook describes.

The year [[1874]] followed the first Dutch military expeditions against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] of [[1873]] — expeditions that had ended the formal authority of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate]] and begun the protracted conflict known as the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]]. Pel's appointment formalised the transition from ad hoc military occupation to the institutional structure of a *Gouvernement*; the dual military-civil character of his office reflected the hybrid nature of a territory that was, simultaneously, a theatre of war and a colonial administrative unit. The boundaries of the new Gouvernement were, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "bij gebrek aan volkomen betrouwbare gegevens" — for lack of fully reliable data — only approximately known.

## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year of the [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]] missive of [[8 February]] [[1874]] — the well-known lines that the great mosque would be rebuilt and made into a true place of mercy, from which no more death and destruction, but prayers to the almighty God and works of love would go out — and the year that followed the [[1873]] storming of the [[Meusigit Raja|mosque]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] by the Dutch troops. The grey war-hero, generaal Van Swieten, in a missive of [[8 February]] [[1874]] to some Acehnese chiefs, wrote the well-known lines: that the great mosque would be rebuilt and made into a true place of mercy. The [[8 February]] [[1874]] missive is the clearest statement of the colonial state's religious politics in [[Atjeh|Aceh]].

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1874

**[[1874]]** was the year of the taking of the [[Missigit (Meusigit)|Missigit]] (the great mosque) of [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] — the first major Dutch military success of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], and the symbolic opening of the war. The Missigit had been the object of the failed [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of April [[1873]]; the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]] completed the work. The year opened the forty-five-year war that would end with the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]].

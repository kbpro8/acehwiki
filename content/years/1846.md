**[[1846]]** was the year that the **[[British Empire]]** seized **[[Labuan]]**, turning the island into a Crown colony and establishing a strategic naval base that supported British operations in **[[Borneo]]**, notably the activities of **[[James Brooke]]** in **[[Serawak]]**. The acquisition altered the balance of power with the **[[Dutch East Indies]]**, whose interests included the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]**; in the same year, a marine of the **[[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]]**, **F. B. Nieuwenhuys**, received an *Eervolle Vermelding* (honourable mention) for his participation in the *Eerste Bali‑expeditie* (first Bali expedition).

## British seizure of Labuan

The British decision to occupy **[[Labuan]]** in [[1846]] was driven by the desire for a deep‑water anchorage that could serve as a staging point for naval patrols and commercial traffic in the surrounding **[[Borneo]]** waters. After the formal proclamation of a Crown colony, the island became a logistical hub for the **[[British Empire]]'s** anti‑piracy and trade‑protection missions, directly supporting the rule of **[[James Brooke]]** in **[[Serawak]]**. The move also signalled to the **[[Dutch East Indies]]** that Britain was willing to project power in the region, thereby influencing Dutch diplomatic and military calculations concerning the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]** and other Sumatra polities.

## Dutch Marine Corps award and the Bali expedition

In the same calendar year, the **[[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]]** distinguished itself far to the south of Aceh. **F. B. Nieuwenhuys**, a marine serving in the expeditionary force, was granted an *Eervolle Vermelding* for his conduct during the **[[Bali expedition]]**—the first Dutch military campaign aimed at subduing the independent Balinese kingdoms. The award, recorded in the corps’ roll of honours, underscores the breadth of Dutch colonial military engagements across the archipelago and foreshadows the experience that would later be drawn upon in the protracted **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]** conflicts of the 1870s and 1880s.

## Significance

The events of [[1846]] illustrate the simultaneous expansion of British and Dutch colonial interests in the Malay Archipelago. The British establishment of a naval base on **[[Labuan]]** reinforced their strategic foothold in **[[Borneo]]**, while the Dutch recognition of marine service in the **[[Bali expedition]]** highlighted the versatility and reach of the **[[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]]**. Together, these developments contributed to the shifting balance of power that set the stage for later confrontations, most notably the **[[Aceh War]]**, in which both European powers would vie for influence over the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]** and the surrounding Sumatra region.

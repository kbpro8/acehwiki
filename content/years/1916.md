## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1916

**1916** was a year of projected administrative maintenance in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. Amidst the global disruptions of the First World War, the Dutch authorities in [[Sumatra]] relied on long-term budgetary projections to maintain governance and the ongoing pacification efforts following the [[Aceh War]].

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The year [[1916]] is primarily documented in the *Atjeh up to date* compilation through multi-year colonial spending plans. The surviving ledgers from this period focus on standardized, predictable provincial budgets:

*   **Budgetary Projections:** Colonial ledgers outline specific allocations for [[1916]], recording initial project or administrative spending at 99,900 guilders, with a broader aggregate budget category reaching 249,750 guilders.
*   **Administrative Consolidation:** These figures reflect a colony transitioning from the extreme, unpredictable costs of active guerrilla warfare to a stabilized, revenue-generating colonial province. The financial overhead of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons and local infrastructure was being absorbed into routine multi-year provincial budgets.



## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1916** is the year in which the installation of the [[Volksraad]] was proclaimed in the *Staatsblad* of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] government. The Volksraad, intended as a first representative advisory body for the Indies, would not be actually installed until May [[1918]] — a delay that, in the verdict of *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis*, made it harder for the moderate leadership of the [[Sarekat Islam]] to restrain its radical wing. The year is also notable for the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] speech of January [[1917]] (formulated in the immediate aftermath of [[1916]]'s defence debates) on Indies defence.

### The Volksraad in the Staatsblad

By the end of [[1916]] the institution of the [[Volksraad]] was proclaimed in the *Staatsblad*. The instrument was, however, not actually implemented until [[May 1918|May]] [[1918]], when the Volksraad was finally installed. The delay was a recurrent pattern of Dutch colonial governance in the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] era: political concessions came too late to capture the "psychologisch ogenblik" — psychological moment — at which they could have anchored moderate opinion.

### The Sarekat Islam context

The slowness of [[Volksraad]] implementation made it difficult for the moderate leadership of the [[Sarekat Islam]] — the major indigenous political association of the period — to keep its radical wing in check. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s hoped-for *associatie* of East and West required, as its complement, the stimulation of indigenous spiritual and political activity; yet the colonial state reacted to the first radical utterances of Indonesian nationalism with alarm. In [[1916]] [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] himself, in an advice on an uprising in [[Djambi]], formulated the misgivings: "De offers in arbeid en goed, die ons bestuur van de bevolking eist en waarin zij voor zichzelve geen enkel belang gelegen acht, zijn op zijn minst even zwaar als die, waartoe de tyranniekste anak-radja's haar ooit hebben verplicht" — the sacrifices in labour and goods that our administration demands of the population, and in which it perceives no interest of its own, are at least as heavy as those to which the most tyrannical of the petty rajas ever compelled it.

### Defence debates

In [[1916]] overste Van der Weyden propagated the priority of the land army over the fleet for Indies defence. In January [[1917]] his father-in-law [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — the former conqueror of Atjeh — told a meeting of the Bond van Vrij-Liberalen that the implementation of the proposals of the [[1913]] staatscommissie on the fleet would be "een ramp" — a disaster — and recommended the formation of an indigenous militia (*inlandse militie*). It was a striking reversal for the man who, eight years earlier, had declined to consider such a militia.

The year **1759** is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the year of the destruction of the Dutch East India Company (the *Oost-Indische Compagnie*) post at [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] on the [[Siak|Siak River]] by the Siak Sultan [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]. The destruction — recorded by the Bureau as the act that ended the first Dutch commercial presence on the [[Siak|Siak River]] — was, in the Bureau's framing, the immediate occasion of the long series of Dutch interventions in the Siak succession that would, in the 1760s, transform the Siak sultanate from an independent polity into a Dutch client state.

## Events

- The destruction of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post by [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau records: "Waarschijnlijk oorzaak van het verraad van [[1759]], was onoordeelkundig optreden der Compagnie's gevolmachtigden, toen dezelfde R. Moehamad den geheelen post liet uitmoorden." The Bureau's attribution of the destruction to "onjudicious conduct of the Compagnie's envoys" — *onoordeelkundig optreden der Compagnie's gevolmachtigden* — is the principal explanation recorded for the Siak sultan's act.
- The Dutch support for [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] against [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]]. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau records: "Het afloopen van die post in [[1759]] had tengevolge dat de Compagnie steun verleende aan den eerst met haar hulp van den troon verdreven Radja Alam, den tegenstander van den in [[1759]] heerschenden sultan Radja Moehamad."

## Significance

The year [[1759]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the chronological anchor of the long Dutch intervention in the Siak succession that would, in the 1760s, transform the Siak sultanate from an independent polity into a Dutch client state. The destruction of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post was the immediate occasion of the Dutch intervention; the subsequent Dutch support for [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] against [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]] (and the Dutch-backed conquest of [[Pelalawan|Pelalawan]] in [[1761]]) was the first act of the Dutch-Siak alliance that would, in turn, prepare the ground for the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Siak overlordship of the East Coast statelets]] under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]].

The year [[1759]] is also, in the longer perspective, the chronological prelude to the [[1779]] restoration of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]] to the Siak throne — the political event that inaugurates the Siak sultanate's brief late-eighteenth-century zenith. The Dutch intervention of the 1760s — undertaken against [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] — would, within two decades, be reversed by [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]]'s own restoration, and the Siak sultanate would, under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]], impose its own overlordship over the East Coast statelets.

**[[1873]]** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history of [[Acheh|Atjèh]] — is the pivotal year of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]]: the year in which the long Dutch–Atjèhnese diplomatic crisis of [[1824]]–[[1873]] culminated in the [[Declaration of war on Atjèh (26 March 1873)|declaration of war]] of [[26 March]], the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) — Kreemer's account|first expedition]] of April ended in failure and the death of [[General J. H. R. Kohler|generaal Kohler]], and the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874) — Kreemer's account|second expedition]] of December set the stage for the [[Kraton Atjeh|fall of the Kraton]] of [[24 January]] [[1874]].

## January–March: the diplomatic endgame

The year opened with the collapse of the Dutch peace mission. The [[Atjèh embassy to Singapore (January 1873)|Singapore embassy]] of January [[1873]] — in which Atjèhnese envoys proposed treaties to the consuls of the [[United States of America|United States]] and [[Italy|Italy]] — convinced the Dutch government that further negotiation was impossible. [[F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen|F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen]], Regeeringscommissaris, was dispatched to Atjèh to demand recognition of Dutch sovereignty; when Atjèh refused, he [[Declaration of war on Atjèh (26 March 1873)|declared war]] on [[26 March]].

## April: the first expedition

The [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) — Kreemer's account|first expedition]] under [[General J. H. R. Kohler|generaalmajoor J. H. R. Kohler]] landed on [[25 April]] [[1873]] with 168 officers and 3,200 men. The Atjèhnese resistance was "veel grooter dan men gemeend had te moeten verwachten" — "much greater than one had expected". Kohler was killed on [[14 April]]; the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]] was not taken; the expedition was "een totale mislukking" — "a total failure". The troops were withdrawn to [[Java]], and a blockade was imposed — but the blockade "had echter weinig resultaat" (had however little result), and the [[Raad van Achten|Penang revolutionary committee]] sustained the resistance with letters to the Atjèhnese chiefs.

## December: the second expedition

The [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874) — Kreemer's account|second expedition]] under the recalled [[Generaal van Swieten|generaal J. van Swieten]], with [[Verspijck|generaalmajoor G. M. Verspijck]] as second-in-command, landed on [[9 December]] [[1873]] with 385 officers and about 7,800 men — "een macht zooals nog nimmer te voren in onze Oost was uitgerust!" — "a force such as never before had been equipped in our East!" On [[23 December]], the Javanese envoy [[Mas Soemo Widikdjo|Mas Soemo Widikdjo]] was sent into the Kraton with two letters — the last Dutch attempt at a peaceful settlement; he was tortured to death. The expedition's objective — the Kraton — would fall on [[24 January]] [[1874]].

## Significance

[[1873]] is, in Kreemer's narrative, the year that transformed the Dutch–Atjèhnese relationship from a treaty-framework into a war of conquest. The failure of the first expedition revealed that the Atjèh sultanate could not be subdued in a single campaign; the scale of the second expedition revealed the Dutch commitment to a long war; and the death of [[Mas Soemo Widikdjo|Widikdjo]] closed the door on any diplomatic resolution. The year thus opens Kreemer's seven-period division of the Atjèh-oorlog (see [[Kreemer's seven-period division of the Atjeh-oorlog]]) and inaugurates the thirty-seven-year conflict that would not end until [[1910]].

## The Achehnese attack on Djohor (7 May [[1613]])
The full account of the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May [[1613]])|attack]] is given in the entry on the attack itself. In summary: the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s successor sent a fleet against [[Djohor|Djohor]], which appeared at the mouth of the Djohor river on [[7 May]] [[1613]] while the bulk of the Djohor fleet was away at [[Pahang|Pahang]]. The Dutch ship *Hope*, which had arrived three days earlier to relieve the factor [[Jacques Obelaer|Jacques Obelaer]] and replenish the factory's capital, lay at anchor in the river. The Achehnese demanded that the Dutch stand aside; the Dutch refused, in fidelity to the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign ([[1606]])|Matelieff-sultan accord of 23 September 1606]], and landed thirty-one men to assist the Djohor defence. The Achehnese took the city of [[Batoesawer|Batoesawer]] on [[6 June]] [[1613]]; two Dutchmen were killed, twenty-two — including Obelaer — were taken prisoner and carried off to Atjeh.

## The aftermath and the rapprochement
The prisoners' fate was bound up with the rapid Achehnese-Djohor reconciliation that followed. Within months the two Malay courts had reached an accommodation: the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]] gave his sister in marriage to the deposed sultan of Djohor — now styled **Sultan Sabrang** — and sent him back to Djohor with an Achehnese escort of two thousand men to rebuild the destroyed city. The Dutch prisoners were released as part of the rapprochement. Van der Woude notes that "bijna een jaar lang lag de handel nu geheel stil" before the opperkoopman [[Adriaan van der Dussen|Adriaan van der Dussen]] arrived at Djohor in [[August]] [[1614]] to negotiate the construction of a fort.

## Coen's assumption of the directorship
By [[1613]] [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] had risen to be the chief Dutch commercial officer in the Indies. He had returned to the East in [[1612]] as opperkoopman and was appointed, in [[1613]], to the directorship of the Bantam-Jacatra factories — the post in which all the threads of the Company's Asian business converged. The [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]]'s [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] expedition of March [[1613]] — in which Both was forced to restore discipline among the troops with drastic sentences and to confront the growing Dutch-English rivalry at Ai and Run — was the immediate strategic context of Coen's directorship. Coen's response, articulated in the [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]], was the working-out of the bewindhebbers' instructions of [[1608]]-[[1609]]: "Van Banda connen ons meester maken, dezelvige eylanden met macht aantasten; sulcx moeten nootelijcken doen ende de plaetse populeren, ofte het landt verlaten, want 't en sal anders met Banda nyet zijn".

## Significance
The year [[1613]] is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal year of the strategic turn from the western to the eastern archipelago and the year of [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s assumption of the directorship of the Bantam-Jacatra factories. The [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May [[1613]])|Achehnese attack on Djohor of 7 May 1613]] — and the destruction of [[Batoesawer|Batoesawer]] — marked the effective end of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance that [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] had constructed in [[1606]] and the strategic turn southward to [[Jacatra|Jacatra]] and the eventual founding of [[Batavia|Batavia]] in [[1619]]. [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s assumption of the directorship in [[1613]] was the institutional prelude to the [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]] and the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force that would define VOC policy for the next quarter-century. The year [[1613]] thus marks both the close of the Atjeh-Djohor-Malaka chapter of Van der Woude's book and the beginning of the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] chapter that would follow.

## Account from *Over Maleise Literatuur* (1947) — 1613

In [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s survey of Malay literature, the year [[1613]] is the year of the Atjehnese attack on [[Batoe Sawar]] — the first of the two great naval expeditions sent by Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] against the capital of [[Djohor|Johor]] — and the principal interruption to the composition of the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]*, which had been commissioned at Batoe Sawar in [[1612]].

### The Attack on Batoe Sawar

Hooykaas records the attack: "een Atjehse vloot verscheen in 1613 onverwachts, die zich meester wist te maken van niemand minder dan de vorst 'Alā' oed-Din Ri'ājat Sjāh III, Radja 'Abdoellāh en de meeste rijksgroten, waaronder de Bēndahara. Ze worden naar Atjeh overgebracht, waar de Soeltān gehouden wordt en spoedig sterft. Radja 'Abdoellāh huwt met een zuster van de Atjehse Soeltān Iskandar Moeda en wordt begenadigd, waarop hij wordt teruggebracht en op de troon van zijn halfbroer gezet" (an Atjehnese fleet appeared unexpectedly in 1613, which managed to seize no less than the Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh III, Radja 'Abdoellah, and most of the great men of the realm, including the *Bendahara*. They are taken to Atjeh, where the Sultan is held and soon dies. Radja 'Abdoellah marries a sister of the Atjehnese Sultan Iskandar Muda and is pardoned, whereupon he is brought back and placed on the throne of his half-brother).

### The Interruption of the Sadjarah Malajoe

The composition of the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* — commissioned at [[Batoe Sawar]] in [[1612]] — was interrupted by the [[1613]] attack. "Het schrijven heeft niet ongestoord zijn gang kunnen gaan" (The writing was not able to proceed undisturbed). The *[[Bendahara Sri Lanang|Bendahara]]* was among the captives taken to Atjeh, where he would — in Hooykaas's reading — have become acquainted with the *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* of [[1607]], the Atjehnese "mirror for princes" that served as the model for the *Bendahara*'s own "Geslachtslijst der Soeltanen" (Genealogy of the Sultans).

### The Completion of the Sadjarah Malajoe

After the *Bendahara*'s return from Atjeh in [[1614]], the new sultan — *Radja 'Abdoellāh*, now a vassal of Atjeh — "doet de Bēndahara weten dat hij prijs stelt op de voltooiing van zijn opdracht; deze krijgt het omvangrijk werk van 34 verhalen af" (lets the *Bendahara* know that he values the completion of his commission; the latter completes the extensive work of 34 stories). The completed *Sadjarah Malajoe* — 34 stories — thus reflects, in its very composition-history, the [[1613]] Atjehnese attack and the *Bendahara*'s captivity in Atjeh.

*Source: Over Maleise Literatuur*, by Dr. C. Hooykaas, Hoogleraarte Batavia; Tweede Druk (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1947), chapter 11 ("Geschiedschrijving in het Maleis"), pp. 96-98; Toelichting tot de Sadjarah Malajoe, Inleiding van de schrijver, pp. 203-205.

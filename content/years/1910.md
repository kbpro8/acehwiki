**1910** was a year of sustained colonial bookkeeping and logistical management in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. As the Dutch administration sought to stabilize its control over northern [[Sumatra]] following the intense campaigns of the [[Aceh War]], the focus of colonial records shifted heavily toward quantifiable metrics of governance, trade, and garrison maintenance.

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The *Atjeh up to date* compilation preserves extensive tabular data for [[1910]], illustrating the material scale of the colonial enterprise:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1910]] record outlays reaching 7,811,471 guilders. Specific departmental ledgers show distinct allocations, such as 1,002,330 and 1,145,435 in separate accounting categories.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Monthly and annual tracking of shipping and trade metrics shows figures such as 158,320; 79,718; 90,158; 92,013; and 66,737 across various regional and commodity categories.
*   **Shipping Registries:** Vessel arrivals and tonnage registries for the year record specific logistical movements indexed at 103, 227, and 49 in primary tracking columns.

## Source

*Atjeh up to date* (Statistical and Administrative Compilation, c. [[1914]]). Archival reference DLP-10. Fragmentary OCR extraction containing financial ledgers, shipping registries, and colonial budget tables for [[1910]].

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1910

**[[1910]]** was the opening year of the [[Colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] — the two-year counter-insurgency operation conducted by *luitenant* (later *kapitein*) [[Schmidt]] against the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō Teungkoes]]* in the bergland east of [[Tangsé]] — and the year for which Schmidt received the *[[Eresabel]]*. Schmidt assembled a small colonne of forty-one militairen (himself included) and twenty-five *dwangarbeiders* (Buginese convicts serving as dragers). At [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]]'s request, the three *voornaamste Atjehse hoofden* wrote a letter urging the Tiriō Teungkoes to submit; the Teungkoes refused. Schmidt received the *Eresabel* for [[1910]] and the [[Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)|M.W.O.]] 3e klas for [[1911]].

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954), chapter "De colonne Schmidt."

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1910

**[[1910]]** is identified by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau as a "mijlpaal" (milestone) in the development of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen (territorial treasuries)]] of the [[Zelfbesturen|self-governing territories]] of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], including the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency. According to the Bureau, before [[1910]] the establishment and management of the landschapskassen had been "rather à l'improviste, without pursuing uniformity in the various regulations," but since [[1910]] "the management and use of the monies have been subjected to general rules and direction, and oversight has been more centralised." This centralisation was institutionalised in the [[Bureau van Financieele aangelegenheden|Bureau of Financial Affairs]], established by G. B. of 30 January 1911 No. 3 — a direct institutional response to the [[1910]] turn.

[[1910]] is the year chosen by the Bureau as the "starting point" for its discussion of the landschapskassen because "since that year regular complete data on the landschapskassen have been available." In that year, the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency contained 3 landschapskassen (the same number as [[Riouw en Onderhoorigheden|Riouw and Dependencies]]). The total budget of all Buitenbezittingen landschapskassen in [[1910]] stood at f 9,199,000; by [[1913]] it would rise to f 13,935,000.

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]] specifically, [[1910]] also marks the year in which the third-class tariff on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was reduced from 2 cents to 1 cent per kilometre — a reform that produced a sharp jump in third-class passenger numbers (from 1,000,000 in [[1909]] to 1,654,000 in [[1910]]) and was part of the broader post-[[1908]] shift from military to economic considerations in the management of the line. The [[1910]]–[[1912]] extension of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] from [[Langsa]] to [[Koeala Simpang]] likewise falls within the [[1910]] reorientation.

## Significance

[[1910]] is the year at which the Dutch turn from *de facto* military occupation of Atjeh to *de jure* administrative consolidation becomes most visible in the financial and infrastructural record. The centralisation of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]], the founding of the [[Bureau van Financieele aangelegenheden|Bureau of Financial Affairs]], and the tariff reform on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] are all facets of the same project: the formalisation of Dutch administrative authority over the [[Zelfbesturen|self-governing territories]] after the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]].

**The year 1944** was, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the year of Lt [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|L. C. G. F. Onvlee]]'s designation for transport to Japan and the temporary hand-over of the *vaandel* (colours) of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] to the Dutch camp commander.

## The 2 March 1944 designation for Japan

On [[2 March 1944]] he was designated for departure to Japan. All those designated for Japan had to surrender their kit, which was to be burned. Onvlee took the Dutch camp commander into his confidence and temporarily handed the vaandel over to him.

The staff history records the circumstances: Onvlee's designation for Japan placed the vaandel in the greatest danger of Japanese detection to date, since the required surrender of kit included the risk of inspection. By entrusting it to the camp commander — a Dutch officer who was aware of its significance — Onvlee ensured the vaandel's survival through the inspection period.

## The vaandel under the camp commander

The camp commander held the vaandel from 2 March [[1944]] until its return to Onvlee on [[10 May 1945]] — a period of more than fourteen months. The staff history does not record any incident during this period of temporary custody, and the vaandel passed undetected through the Japanese searches and unannounced inspections that continued throughout [[1944]] and into [[1945]].

## Significance

The year [[1944]] is, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the year of the vaandel's temporary loss and recovery in the Onvlee episode, and the year of its narrowest escape from Japanese detection. The staff history's emphasis on the 2 March [[1944]] designation for Japan and the hand-over to the camp commander frames [[1944]] as the year in which the vaandel's fate came closest to permanent loss — the year whose drama was resolved only by the vaandel's return to Onvlee on [[10 May 1945]], and by the Japanese capitulation of [[15 August 1945]].

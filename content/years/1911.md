**1911** was a year of complex administrative accounting and ongoing logistical management in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. As the Dutch administration continued to stabilize its control over northern [[Sumatra]], colonial records meticulously tracked the financial overhead of the [[Aceh War]]'s aftermath, alongside the movement of export commodities and colonial supplies.

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The *Atjeh up to date* compilation preserves extensive tabular data for [[1911]], illustrating the material scale of the colonial enterprise:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1911]] record outlays reaching 7,757,821 guilders. Specific departmental ledgers show distinct allocations, such as 1,374,616 and 1,898,980 in separate accounting categories, alongside secondary ledgers showing 971,916 and 933,063.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Monthly and annual tracking of shipping and trade metrics shows highly variable figures, including 79,850; 166,450; 108,678; 208,104; and 300,365 across various regional and commodity categories.
*   **Shipping Registries:** Vessel arrivals and tonnage registries for the year record specific logistical movements indexed at 107, 243, and 75 in primary tracking columns, with further metrics at 106,600 and 180,929.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1911

**[[1911]]** was the closing year of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]'s counter-insurgency phase — the year in which the [[Colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] destroyed the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]]*. In May 1911 the small resistance band of *[[Peutoeha-Gam-Masen|Peutoeha Gam Masén]]* was surrounded near the [[Kroeng-Seukoeleh|Kroeëng Seukoeleh]] and Gam Masén was killed by the korporaal [[Nussy]]. Later in May the colonne surprised the schuilplaats of the Tiriō bende. On [[3 December]] [[1911]] the korporaal [[Nussy]] — alone after his *spits* turned back — shot and brought down *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]*, the last leader of the bende, who became *[[sjahid]]*: "De Tiri-bende was niet meer." The militaire closure of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] was complete; the political closure would come with the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]].

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954), chapter "De colonne Schmidt."

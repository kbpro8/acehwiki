**1911** was a year of complex administrative accounting and ongoing logistical management in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. As the Dutch administration continued to stabilize its control over northern [[Sumatra]], colonial records meticulously tracked the financial overhead of the [[Aceh War]]'s aftermath, alongside the movement of export commodities and colonial supplies.

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The *Atjeh up to date* compilation preserves extensive tabular data for [[1911]], illustrating the material scale of the colonial enterprise:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1911]] record outlays reaching 7,757,821 guilders. Specific departmental ledgers show distinct allocations, such as 1,374,616 and 1,898,980 in separate accounting categories, alongside secondary ledgers showing 971,916 and 933,063.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Monthly and annual tracking of shipping and trade metrics shows highly variable figures, including 79,850; 166,450; 108,678; 208,104; and 300,365 across various regional and commodity categories.
*   **Shipping Registries:** Vessel arrivals and tonnage registries for the year record specific logistical movements indexed at 107, 243, and 75 in primary tracking columns, with further metrics at 106,600 and 180,929.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1911** is the year in which [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s lectures *[[Nederland en de Islam ([[1911]])|Nederland en de Islam]]* were published, and the year of the [[1911 Indische begrotingsdebat|Indische begrotingsdebat]] in the [[Tweede Kamer]] that crystallised around them. It is also the year in which [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] (a.r.) used those lectures to corner the minister [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] on the question of government support for Christian *zending* in the Indies — including, by direct implication, the recently-pacified territories of Atjeh.

### Snouck Hurgronje's *Nederland en de Islam*

The series of lectures that [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — advisor to the [[Ministerie van Koloniën]] — had given to future colonial civil servants appeared in print in [[1911]] under the title *[[Nederland en de Islam ([[1911]])|Nederland en de Islam]]*. In them [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] argued that the Dutch state — religiously neutral — could not, and should not, attempt to convert its Muslim subjects to Christianity. The state should confine itself to "een actie, die bedoelt de inlanders op veel steviger wijze dan tot dusver het geval is, bij ons staatsverband en bij onze nationaliteit in te lijven" — an action intended to incorporate the natives far more firmly than hitherto into our state-association and our nationality. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] hoped that, with a proper *associatiepolitiek*, the Indonesians would of their own free will remain as equals within the realm.

### The 1911 begrotingsdebat

The [[1911 Indische begrotingsdebat|Indische begrotingsdebat]] of [[1911]] "concentreerde zich vooral om de door deze adviseur van het ministerie van Koloniën ontvouwde denkbeelden" — concentrated especially on the ideas unfolded by this advisor of the Ministry of Colonies. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] (a.r.) mounted a principled argument against [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]]'s position, calling for *bizonder onderwijs* — confessional schools — to be the rule in the Indies and *gouvernementsonderwijs* a mere supplement. He warned that an actively Christian propaganda would be the lesser evil when compared with leaving the population to "het hindoeisme terug doen zinken" — sink back into Hinduism.

### Malefijt's response

[[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] — minister of Colonies — replied that he adhered to the position of his predecessors: no direct support to the *zending* (missions), but strong support where the missions operated on social terrain. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s rule for confessional schools was, in his view, possible only in a few regions of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Buitenbezittingen]] where the missions had gained a firm foothold; on [[Java]], where the Muslim majority lacked the organisation to act on its own initiative, the rule was inapplicable. The incident, which in its day caused considerable stir, was — in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s verdict — perhaps most of all a symptom of the "geprikkelde stemming" between [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] and the [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|Malefijt]]-[[Heemskerk]] government.

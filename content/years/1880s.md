The **1880s** — the decade from [[1880]] to [[1889]] — was the middle decade of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]], marked by the continuation of the Dutch campaign in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] after the fall of the kraton in [[1874]], the experiment with civil administration in [[1881]]–[[1884]], the adoption of the concentration strategy in [[1884]]–[[1885]], and the ensuing period of defensive warfare that would last until the [[pacification of Atjeh|pacification]] of the late [[1890s]] and early [[1900s]]. The decade is the period of the contemporary Dutch pamphlet literature on the war, of which the *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk]]* by [[Een Ooggetuige|Een Ooggetuige]] is an example.

## The Atjeh War in the 1880s

The [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] entered its middle phase in the 1880s. The Dutch, having taken the kraton of the Sultan in [[1874]] and established their administrative centre at [[Kota-Radja|Kota-Radja]], spent the years [[1874]]–[[1884]] in offensive operations aimed at extending their control over [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and the surrounding areas. An experiment with separate civil administration was introduced in [[1881]] but proved unsuccessful in maintaining order. The *oorlogspartij* (war party) and the *heilige oorlog* (holy war) movement gained strength during this period.

The concentration decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]] marked a strategic shift from offensive to defensive operations. Dutch forces withdrew from scattered posts to a *geconcentreerde stelling* (concentrated position) around [[Kota-Radja|Kota-Radja]] and [[Olehleh|Olehleh]], completed on [[1 March]] [[1885]]. The concentration system would remain in place through the late [[1880s]] and the first half of the [[1890s]], and would be the subject of debate in the Dutch pamphlet literature of the war.

## The pamphlet literature

The 1880s was the period of the contemporary Dutch pamphlet literature on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]]. The *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk]]* by [[Een Ooggetuige|Een Ooggetuige]], published by [[Van Meurs & Stufkens]] in [[Rotterdam]] with the [[Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel|map of the theatre of war]], is an example of this literature. The pamphlet debates the conduct of the war, the strategic and commercial value of Atjeh, and the question of whether the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] should continue the war or withdraw. The pamphlet's comparison of Atjeh with [[Singapore|Singapore]], and its argument for the development of the Atjehnese coast as a station for world trade, place it within the wider nineteenth-century debate on the sea approaches to the archipelago.

## Significance

The 1880s is significant within the AcehWiki corpus as the middle decade of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] and as the period of the contemporary Dutch pamphlet literature on the war. The decade's events — the concentration decision of [[1884]], the withdrawal to the concentrated position in [[1885]], and the ensuing period of defensive warfare — set the conditions for the later, final phase of the war under [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in the late [[1890s]] and early [[1900s]].

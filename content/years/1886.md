## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

On [[16 October]] **[[1886]]** [[H. N. A. Swart]] re-engaged for a year; on [[21 November]] [[1886]] he was appointed *2de luitenant* in the Infantry of the Army in the Netherlands Indies by royal decree no. 18 of [[24 November]] [[1886]].

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

The judgment of the very year in which this pamphlet appeared: on [[26 January]] [[Kiesvereeniging Burgerpligt]] held its meeting at Amsterdam and published, with applause of a very great attendance, the lecture of [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Overste Verstege]] after the opening words of its president [[R. van de Werk]] and the replies of majoor [[M. T. H. Perelaer|Perelaer]] and of the old-director [[C. Bosscher|Bosscher]]. The former minister [[Willem Maurits de Brauw|de Brauw]] rectified, by letter of [[27 January]] [[1886]], the newspaper reading of his statement of [[16 November]] [[1882]]; while the minister [[Sprenger van Eijk]] had declared in his Memorie van Antwoord of [[20 January]] [[1886]] that the gloomy predictions about the concentration plan had not been fulfilled — "an oracle-saying", Verstege remarks, as the Minister could not yet know the costs. In the Militaire Spectator No. 1 of the same year majoor [[F. de Bas|de Bas]] came forward once more in defence of general [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s conduct; the answer of the pamphlet is its whole argument, briefly: that the concentration was the illogical continuation of the [[Annexation proclamation of Atjeh (1874)|annexation policy]] of [[1874]], the crowning of the statecraft applied since [[11 March]] [[1881]], and a bastard system contradicted by the colonial history of all times and all peoples — and that only a commissoriale enquête (commission of inquiry) could lead the Netherlands out of the Atjeh labyrinth (see [[Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk in zake het Atjeh-vraagstuk (Verstege, 1886)]]).

## Continuation of the Geconcentreerde Stelling

[[1886]] saw the continuation of the geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position) established in [[1885]]. [[Colonel H. Demmeni]], appointed Civiele en Militaire Gouverneur in [[September]] [[1884]], oversaw the operation of the concentration until his death in December [[1886]] in the Padang highlands. By July [[1886]], all construction and communication works (ceintuurtram, telephone connections, cleared *schootveld* of 1,000 metres before the line) had been completed. The security within the line had improved significantly, and losses were much reduced compared to pre-concentration years. However, the surrounding territories — the [[XXII Moekims]], [[XXV Moekims]], and [[XXVI Moekims]] — remained outside effective Dutch control, and the religious leadership under [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]] continued to organize resistance.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1896

**[[1896]]** was the year of the [[Battle of Aneu Galong (1896)|Battle of Aneu Galong]] — the defence in which the eldest son and successor of *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]]* was killed as commandant, and in which *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]* — father of the future resistance leader *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* — also fell as a *geloofsgetuige* (a witness of the faith). The boy *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* was an infant at the breast when his father died; by [[1911]] he would be fifteen and the last leader of the Tiriō bende. [[Dominee Thenu]] — who had arrived in Aceh in [[1894]] — spoke the troops before battle for the first time in [[1896]]. The year marks the moment at which "het lijdelijk afwachten was afgelopen en de troepen zelf tot actie overgingen."

## Account from *Oost-Indisch Journael* (1947) — 1896

**[[1896]]** was the year of the defection of the cunning adventurer [[Teukoe Oemar]] on [[29 March]] [[1896]] — the critical days that followed the Graafland period and preceded Van Heutsz's period of glory. After the defection of [[Teukoe Oemar]] on [[29 March]] [[1896]], critical days began. The defection is the marker of the crisis that opened the way to Van Heutsz's period of glory: the sequence of the Graafland period (the all-out offensive) — the defection of Teukoe Oemar (the critical days) — Van Heutsz's period of glory (the time of the great expeditions and excursions). About [[1896]] the pretender-sultan [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot]] was also compelled, owing to a quarrel with local chiefs, to break up his "court" at [[Keumala]] and lead a wandering life.

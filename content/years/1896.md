**[[1896]]** was the decisive turning-point year of the [[Aceh War]]. The defection of [[T. Oema]]—who turned his Dutch-supplied modern rifles against his former patrons—confirmed the failure of the alliance policy and produced the strategic reversal that brought [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] to power.

## Key events

- Defection of [[T. Oema]] (also called [[Toekoe Djohan]] or [[Panglima Prang Besar]]), who had been armed and subsidised by the Dutch government against the warnings of [[Snouck Hurgronje]].
- Appointment of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] to lead military operations in [[Groot-Atjeh]].
- The [[Indisch Besluit]] of [[14 September]] [[1896]] No. 2, formally adopting the strategic framework recommended by [[Snouck Hurgronje]]: active aggression against the hostile party, restoration of government confidence through promotion of agriculture, industry, and trade, and the use of [[T. Oema]] (before his defection) as a partner in the [[Peperhandel]] on the west coast.
- [[Generaal Vetter]] sent as government commissioner with three battalions of infantry to defeat [[Toekoe Djohan]] and drive him out of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

The events of [[1896]] marked the transition from the failed policies of [[1873]]–[[1896]] to the successful campaign of [[1898]]–[[1903]].

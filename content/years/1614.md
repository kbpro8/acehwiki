The year of the volcanic eruption that destroyed [[Pulo Ai]] — one of the islands of the Banda group — on Monday 3 Rabīʿ I 1023, that is, [[14 April]] [[1614]]. The eruption is recorded in a note on f. 1v of Leiden Cod. Or. 1255, a Moluccan manuscript of the *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] with interlinear Malay translation, written on paper bearing the watermark of Thann in Alsace (in use from [[1612]]). This manuscript, which [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] identifies as Raniri's source for the second part of his [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]], is one of the oldest witnesses to the diffusion of Malay religious prose in the Indonesian archipelago before Raniri's arrival in [[Acheh]].

## Account from *Over Maleise Literatuur* (1947) — 1614

In [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s survey of Malay literature, the year [[1614]] is the year of the *Bendahara*'s return from Atjeh — the year in which the *[[Bendahara Sri Lanang|Bendahara]]* of [[Djohor|Johor]], captured in the [[1613]] Atjehnese attack on [[Batoe Sawar]] and taken to Atjeh, was released and returned to Johor to complete the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]*.

### The Bendahara's Return from Atjeh

The *Bendahara*'s return from Atjeh in [[1614]] was part of the broader Atjehnese-Johor reconciliation that followed the [[1613]] attack. *Radja 'Abdoellāh* — the younger brother of the late Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh III — had married a sister of Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] in Atjeh, was pardoned, and was returned to Batoe Sawar as the new Sultan of Johor — a vassal of Atjeh. The *Bendahara* was returned with him.

### The Completion of the Sadjarah Malajoe

After the *Bendahara*'s return in [[1614]], the new sultan "doet de Bēndahara weten dat hij prijs stelt op de voltooiing van zijn opdracht; deze krijgt het omvangrijk werk van 34 verhalen af" (lets the *Bendahara* know that he values the completion of his commission; the latter completes the extensive work of 34 stories). The *Sadjarah Malajoe* — 34 stories — was thus completed in [[1614]], after the *Bendahara*'s return from Atjeh, and reflects the experience of the [[1613]] captivity.

### The Atjehnese Literary Influence

The *Bendahara*'s return from Atjeh in [[1614]] also marks the moment at which the Atjehnese literary influence — particularly the *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* of [[1607]] — entered the Malay chronicle tradition. The *Bendahara*'s "Geslachtslijst der Soeltanen" (Genealogy of the Sultans) — the working title of the *Sadjarah Malajoe* — was, in Hooykaas's reading, modelled on the *Tādj*'s example. The [[1614]] completion of the *Sadjarah Malajoe* thus marks the moment at which the Atjehnese and Malay chronicle traditions converged.

*Source: Over Maleise Literatuur*, by Dr. C. Hooykaas, Hoogleraarte Batavia; Tweede Druk (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1947), chapter 11 ("Geschiedschrijving in het Maleis"), pp. 96-98; Toelichting tot de Sadjarah Malajoe, Inleiding van de schrijver, pp. 203-205.

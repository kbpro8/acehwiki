**1890** was a pivotal year in the history of [[Acheh]] and the broader Dutch colonial empire, marked by three intertwined developments: the founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij tot ontginning van Petroleum terreinen in Nederlandsch Indië* (the future Royal Dutch) that inaugurated Dutch oil activity in northern Sumatra, the Dutch marine expedition that seized the eastern coastal town of [[Edi (Atjeh)|Edi]] in [[Atjeh (Aceh)]], and the first issue of the *Atjeh Marineblad* naval periodical documenting the Dutch campaign in the sultanate. Together these events illustrate both the economic expansion of Dutch petroleum interests and the military consolidation of Dutch authority on the Aceh coast in the late nineteenth century.

## Oil Industry Foundations

Van Bemmelen records that in [[1890]] the concession at [[Telaga Said]] became the first oil property of the newly founded *Koninklijke Maatschappij tot ontginning van Petroleum terreinen in Nederlandsch Indië* (Royal Company for the exploitation of oil fields in the Netherlands East Indies). The company, later known worldwide as Royal Dutch, acquired the Telaga Said concession in the same year, making it the founding asset of what would become the Royal Dutch (and subsequently Royal Dutch Shell) oil empire.

The [[1890]] founding follows the [[1885]] drilling success of the [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]]‑led government crew at Telaga Said, itself built on the earlier [[1883]] concession obtained by [[J. Zulkler|J. Zulkler]]. The chronological sequence of northern‑Sumatran oil exploration is therefore:

1. **[[1883]]** – [[J. Zulkler|J. Zulkler]] obtains the first North‑Sumatran oil concession.  
2. **[[1885]]** – The [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]]‑led crew drills the [[Telaga Said]] well to success.  
3. **[[1890]]** – Founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij* with the Telaga Said concession as its inaugural oil property.  
4. **[[1907]]** – Amalgamation of Royal Dutch with the Shell Transport and Trading Company, creating [[Royal Dutch Shell]]; the [[B.P.M.]] is created as Royal Dutch’s East‑Indies subsidiary.  
5. **[[1912]]** – Entry of the [[Standard Oil Company of New Jersey]] via its subsidiary the [[N.K.P.M.]].  
6. **[[1931]]** – Entry of the [[Standard Oil Company of California]] via its subsidiary the [[N.P.P.M.]].  
7. **[[1940]]–[[1941]]** – Van Bemmelen’s production tables record the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields on Aceh territory.

The significance of the [[1890]] founding lies in establishing the corporate nucleus from which the global Royal Dutch Shell empire would later emerge, with the Telaga Said concession serving as the geographic and economic seed of the company’s worldwide presence.

## Dutch Expedition to Edi

In [[1890]] a Dutch marine force of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] landed at [[Edi (Atjeh)|Edi]] on the eastern seaboard of [[Atjeh (Aceh)]]. The operation aimed to secure the vital maritime trade routes that ran along the coast and to create a forward base capable of supporting further penetrations into the interior of the sultanate. By extending Dutch control to this strategic point, the expedition reinforced the colonial administration’s ability to project power, monitor coastal traffic, and consolidate authority over the eastern Aceh coast.

## Launch of the *Atjeh Marineblad*

The same year saw the inaugural issue of the *Atjeh Marineblad*, a Dutch naval periodical dedicated to documenting the progress of the Dutch campaign in [[Atjeh (Aceh)]]. The 1890 issues provided updates on the aftermath of the earlier [[Atjeh expedition (1873–1876)|Atjeh expedition]] ([[1873]]–[[1876]]), detailed the condition of Dutch forts along the Aceh coastline, and offered commentary on the broader military and political situation in the sultanate. As a contemporary source, the newspaper served both propaganda and informational functions for Dutch authorities and the wider public interested in colonial affairs.

## Significance

The year [[1890]] stands out in the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it simultaneously marks the corporate birth of the Dutch oil enterprise that would dominate global petroleum markets and a decisive moment in the Dutch military consolidation of Aceh’s eastern coast. The convergence of economic and military initiatives in this single year underscores the multidimensional nature of Dutch colonial expansion in the late nineteenth century.

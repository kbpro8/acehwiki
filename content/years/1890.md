**[[1890]]** was a pivotal year in the history of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the broader Dutch colonial empire, marked by three intertwined developments: the founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij tot ontginning van Petroleum terreinen in Nederlandsch Indië* ("Royal Company for the exploitation of oil fields in the Netherlands Indies") — the future Royal Dutch — that inaugurated Dutch oil activity in northern Sumatra, the Dutch marine expedition that seized the eastern coastal town of [[Edi (Atjeh)|Edi]] in [[Atjeh (Aceh)]], and the first issue of the *Atjeh Marineblad* naval periodical documenting the Dutch campaign in the sultanate. Together these events illustrate both the economic expansion of Dutch petroleum interests and the military consolidation of Dutch authority on the Aceh coast in the late nineteenth century.

## The Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij founded

According to Ir. [[F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]]'s chapter *Mijnbouw* ("Mining") in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]): "The first to drill oil in exploitable quantity was [[Zijlker|Zijlker]], and indeed in a concession in the *Landschap* Langkat in the year [[1884]]. But it was not until 1890 that the *Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij* founded on this came into being." The six-year gap between the [[1884]] concession and the [[1890]] founding of the Koninklijke reflects the time required to assemble the capital and organisation for a petroleum-refining operation in a region where no such operation had previously existed.

The Koninklijke's first Director, [[A. J. B. Kessler|A. J. B. Kessler]], succeeded "through almost superhuman effort" in raising production at the [[Pangkalan Brandan]] refinery in [[Langkat]] to a level that allowed a modest dividend to be paid in [[1895]]. Kessler secured the help of [[H. W. A. Deterding|H. W. A. Deterding]] — then agent of the *Nederlandsche Handel Maatschappij* ("Netherlands Trading Society") in [[Penang]] — for the sale of lamp-oil. Deterding "saw at once the great importance of a good sales organisation, which he therefore, in those days a novelty, set up under his own management."

## The Telaga Said concession sequence

Van Bemmelen records that in [[1890]] the concession at [[Telaga Said]] became the first oil property of the newly founded *Koninklijke Maatschappij tot ontginning van Petroleum terreinen in Nederlandsch Indië*. The company, later known worldwide as Royal Dutch, acquired the Telaga Said concession in the same year, making it the founding asset of what would become the Royal Dutch (and subsequently Royal Dutch Shell) oil empire. The chronological sequence of northern-Sumatran oil exploration is therefore:

1. **[[1883]]** — [[J. Zulkler|J. Zulkler]] obtains the first North-Sumatran oil concession.
2. **[[1885]]** — The [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]]-led crew drills the [[Telaga Said]] well to success.
3. **[[1890]]** — Founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij* with the Telaga Said concession as its inaugural oil property.
4. **[[1907]]** — Amalgamation of Royal Dutch with the Shell Transport and Trading Company, creating [[Royal Dutch Shell]]; the [[B.P.M.]] is created as Royal Dutch's East-Indies subsidiary.
5. **[[1912]]** — Entry of the [[Standard Oil Company of New Jersey]] via its subsidiary the [[N.K.P.M.]].
6. **[[1931]]** — Entry of the [[Standard Oil Company of California]] via its subsidiary the [[N.P.P.M.]].
7. **[[1940]]–[[1941]]** — Van Bemmelen's production tables record the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields on Aceh territory.

## The parallel Dordtsche Petroleum Maatschappij

The same year [[1890]] also figures in the parallel history of the [[Dordtsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Dordrecht Petroleum Company]], which had begun its Javanese operations with the sale of the first 8000 cases of lamp-oil in [[1889]]. The two companies — the Sumatra East Coast–based Koninklijke and the Java-based Dordtsche — were to remain in parallel existence until their eventual merger (via the [[1903]] "Asiatic" sales organisation and the [[1906]] founding of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Batavian Petroleum Company]] and Anglo-Saxon) into the [[Royal Dutch Shell]] concern.

## Dutch Expedition to Edi

In [[1890]] a Dutch marine force of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] ("Marine Corps") landed at [[Edi (Atjeh)|Edi]] on the eastern seaboard of [[Atjeh (Aceh)]]. The operation aimed to secure the vital maritime trade routes that ran along the coast and to create a forward base capable of supporting further penetrations into the interior of the sultanate. By extending Dutch control to this strategic point, the expedition reinforced the colonial administration's ability to project power, monitor coastal traffic, and consolidate authority over the eastern Aceh coast.

## Launch of the *Atjeh Marineblad*

The same year saw the inaugural issue of the *Atjeh Marineblad*, a Dutch naval periodical dedicated to documenting the progress of the Dutch campaign in [[Atjeh (Aceh)]]. The 1890 issues provided updates on the aftermath of the earlier [[Aceh Expedition (1873–1876)|Atjeh expedition]] ([[1873]]–[[1876]]), detailed the condition of Dutch forts along the Aceh coastline, and offered commentary on the broader military and political situation in the sultanate.

## Significance

The year [[1890]] stands out in the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] history because it simultaneously marks the corporate birth of the Dutch oil enterprise that would dominate global petroleum markets and a decisive moment in the Dutch military consolidation of Aceh's eastern coast. The convergence of economic and military initiatives in this single year underscores the multidimensional nature of Dutch colonial expansion in the late nineteenth century. The same year, the Dutch military conquest of Atjeh was entering its second decade (the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) Kreemer's account|first Aceh expedition]] had been in [[1873]]); the parallel development of the Langkat oilfield and the Atjeh military conquest together reshaped the strategic significance of northern Sumatra in the late-colonial Indies.

The year **[[1650]]** was marked by the conclusion of a treaty between [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam]], the "Sultane of Aceh," and the [[Dutch East India Company]] (VOC) on [[15 August]], opening the tin trade of [[Perak]] exclusively to Acehnese and Dutch merchants. This agreement — the [[Treaty of 1650 on Perak Tin]] — built on earlier treaties of [[1641]] and [[1649]], and effectively secured a VOC monopoly over Perak tin.

## Aceh Context

The treaty was a direct consequence of the diplomatic successes of the previous years. It gave the VOC control over one of the most valuable commodities in the region, excluding Portuguese and other competitors. The year 1650 also marks the date of the atlas's second map, which shows Aceh as a recognized independent state but closely integrated into the VOC's trading network through treaties. The treaty demonstrates Aceh's continued autonomy even as it conceded trade monopolies to the Dutch.

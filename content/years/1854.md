**1854** was a pivotal year in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and broader Acehnese history, witnessing both the re‑imposition of Atjehnese overlordship over the east‑coast statelets of [[Langkat]], [[Deli]] and [[Serdang]] and the Dutch capture of the fortified Chinese [[Kongsi]] stronghold of [[Montrado]] on [[Borneo]]. The overlordship, recorded in *De Buitenbezittingen* (1918), forced the former Siak‑influenced territories to submit to Atjeh, while the naval operation that employed the steamship [[Borneo]] ended a protracted conflict that had threatened the trade routes of the [[Sultanate of Aceh]].

## Overlordship over Langkat, Deli and Serdang

The *De Buitenbezittingen*, Deel II, Afl. 3 (1918) notes that the three northern coastal principalities—[[Serdang]], [[Deli]] and [[Langkat]]—had previously managed to escape the influence of [[Siak]] but were “forced since [[1854]] to submit to Atjeh, which had indeed become their overlord that year.” The document states: “Serdang, Deli en Langkat, welke zich los hadden weten te maken van Siak's invloed, waren al sinds [[1854]] gedwongen geworden in plaats daarvan dien van Atjeh toe te laten, welk rijk daar sedert dat jaar inderdaad opperheer was.” This re‑assertion of Atjehnese suzerainty reinforced Atjeh’s claim to the northern coast of Sumatra and re‑established a tributary relationship that had been disrupted in earlier decades.

## Capture of Montrado

In the same year, Dutch forces launched a decisive assault on [[Montrado]], a heavily fortified Chinese [[Kongsi]] settlement on the western coast of [[Borneo]]. The operation, carried out by a squadron that included the steamship [[Borneo]], succeeded in breaching the stronghold’s defenses and securing the site for the [[Dutch East Indies]] administration. Contemporary reports described the victory as ending “the protracted conflict that had threatened the [[Sultanate of Aceh]]’s trade routes along the island’s western coast.” The fall of Montrado removed a major obstacle to Dutch maritime dominance in the region and secured safer passage for Acehnese and Dutch commercial vessels alike.

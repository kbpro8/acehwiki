## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1923** was the year of publication of [[Dr. R. Broersma|Dr. R. Broersma]]'s *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* — *Reconnaissances in Atjeh* — in the newspaper *De Locomotief* in semi-October, and the year of publication of the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]]'s pamphlet *[[De rijkdom van Atjeh|De rijkdom van Atjeh]]*. The patchouli-leaf export of Atjeh was 638 tons, of which 9 tons went direct to the Netherlands, 12 tons to Germany, 512 tons to [[Penang|Penang]] and 105 tons to [[Singapore|Singapore]]. The first direct shipment of Atjeh nilam-oil to the Netherlands was in [[1931]], but the [[1923]] export-trade already formed the background of the later industry. The motor-ploughs of the *landschap* [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula]] ploughed a half-*bouw* of ground for the people at f 7.50 in [[1923]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In **[[1923]]** [[J. Langhout]] published *Economische Staatkunde in Atjeh*, listed among [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]]'s sources. From [[1923]] to [[1925]] there was heavy *[[katjau|katjauen]]* in the Indies over the charge that the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] had been bought.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* (1923) — 1923

"'t Is den journalist nu eenmaal gezet... 'bij' te zijn!" — the journalist must be *up to date*: in his chapter "Het Atjeh van thans" Langhout describes Atjeh in 1923 through Van Sluys's interview (the province reachable by car and train; the interior still to be freed), Van Zalinge's travel report (pacification finished; rice feeding even the garrison; pepper declining, pinang rising) and Frijling's report ("without any restriction... Atjeh in very progressing state"), closing with the rhetorical question that sums the book: "Atjeh onder Zelfbestuur? Of: Atjeh als Wingewest? De keuze is daarginds al gedaan!" — the choice made, the author argues, by fifty years of accomplished facts.

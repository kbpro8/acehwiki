## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1857** is the year in which, after the dispatch of a Dutch warship, a contract was concluded between the [[Netherlands|Dutch colonial government]] and the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] stipulating "wederzijdse toelating van handel en tegengang van zee-, strand- en mensenroof" (mutual admission of trade and suppression of sea-, strand- and people-robbing). The contract was, in the retrospective of *Nederlanders over de Zeeën*, "niet nageleefd" (not honoured) by the Atjehnese side — the immediate operational failure of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824 London Treaty]'s double commitment.

### The 1857 contract

The [[1857]] contract was the operational form given to the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824 undertaking]] that the [[Netherlands]] would respect Atjehnese independence while suppressing Atjehnese piracy. After the dispatch of a warship — the typical Dutch response to a prolonged impasse with a maritime Southeast-Asian polity — a formal written agreement was concluded. The contract's reciprocal clauses (mutual admission of trade, mutual suppression of piracy, slave-raiding and shipwreck-plundering) reflected the standard Dutch treaty template of the mid-nineteenth century.

### The non-observance

The [[1857]] contract was, the source records, "niet nageleefd" (not honoured). Dutch, English, American and other ships continued to be plundered. The failure of the contract — alongside the continuing impossibility of reconciling the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824 undertaking]]'s twin commitments — supplied the [[Netherlands|Dutch government]] with the immediate diplomatic grievance that, after the [[Sumatra-tractaat (1871)|Sumatra-tractaat of 1871]] freed its hand on Sumatra, would crystallise into the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war of 26 March 1873]].

### Significance

[[1857]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because the contract concluded in that year is the last diplomatic instrument between the [[Netherlands]] and the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] before the slide to war. Its non-observance — the standard Dutch grievance after a failed treaty with a maritime Southeast-Asian polity — is the immediate operational failure that, in the source's narrative, justifies the [[1871]] treaty with Britain and the [[1873]] declaration of war.

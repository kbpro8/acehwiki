**1930** was the year of the publication of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] by Ir. [[W. Spoon]] of the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] — the principal contemporary monograph on the early factory-scale production of Indonesian turpentine. The year is documented in the pamphlet itself, and it is the year in which the chemical composition, pharmacopoeial suitability, and European market reception of the factory-scale Atjeh-turpentine were systematically evaluated for the first time.

## Events

- **Publication of the Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet (19 November 1930)** — The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] by Ir. [[W. Spoon]] was published as *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum van de Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut* No. 57, extracted from *De Indische Mercuur* of [[19 November]] [[1930]] and published by J. H. de Bussy of [[Amsterdam]]. The pamphlet is the principal contemporary monograph on the [[Baleq]] enterprise.

- **Chemical investigation of Atjeh-turpentine samples (1930)** — Dr. [[D. B. Spoelstra]] of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum performed the fractional distillation of 2500 g of Atjeh-turpentine (sample *H.M. no. 1607*) under 30 mm pressure, establishing the 85–90% α-pinene and approximately 4% Δ3-carene composition of the factory-scale product. [[M. A. van Royen]] tested the samples against the draft *Ontwerp-normaalblad V 597*.

- **European market-reception survey (May 1930)** — The [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] addressed the [[Circulaire of 5 May 1930 to the V.V.V.F.|circular letter of 5 May 1930]] to the 28 members of the *[[Vereeniging van Vernis- en Verffabrikanten en -Handelaren in Nederland]]*, enclosing sample *H.M. no. 1607* and the chemical data on samples 1504, 1553 and 1607. Eleven written evaluations were received — the principal documentary record of the European market reception of factory-scale Atjeh-turpentine.

- **Production at the Baleq enterprise (1930)** — By [[1930]] the area under exploitation at the [[Baleq]] enterprise was approximately 1600 ha, entirely in production. The production figures for [[1929]] — the latest available in the [[1930]] pamphlet — were 356 tons of resin and 104 tons of turpentine.

## Concepts

- [[Atjeh-terpentijn]] — The turpentine oil of the [[Baleq]] enterprise; the principal subject of the [[1930]] pamphlet.
- [[Pinus Merkusii]] — The Sumatran pine, the principal raw material of the [[Baleq]] enterprise.
- [[Quarre]] — The French tapping method applied at the [[Baleq]] enterprise.
- [[Colophonium]] — The solid resinous residue of pine-balsam distillation, the principal domestic-market product of the [[Baleq]] enterprise.

## Significance

The year [[1930]] is significant in the historiography of colonial Aceh as the year of the publication of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] — the principal contemporary monograph on the early factory-scale production of Indonesian turpentine. The year's chemical investigation, pharmacopoeial testing, and European market-reception survey together document the attempt — ultimately only partly successful — to develop a colonial turpentine industry capable of competing in the metropolitan market. The year is also a document of a particular moment in Dutch colonial science: the moment at which the Koloniaal Instituut's Afdeeling Handelsmuseum was systematically evaluating the commercial prospects of colonial commodities in the metropolitan market.

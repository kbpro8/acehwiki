The year **[[1649]]** witnessed the signing of a major treaty between [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam]], the "Sultane of Aceh," and the [[Dutch East India Company]] (VOC), negotiated by envoy [[Johan Truytman]]. This agreement — the [[Dutch–Aceh Treaty of 1649]] — granted the Company extensive trade advantages on the entire West Coast of Sumatra, including the exclusion of all other European and native traders (except Acehnese) and fixed pepper prices.

## Aceh Context

Following the VOC's conquest of [[Malakka]] in [[1641]], relations with Aceh had soured. Truytman's mission restored goodwill and built upon the earlier treaty of 1641. The 1649 treaty was a significant diplomatic success for the VOC and paved the way for the subsequent agreement on tin trade in [[Perak]] ([[1650]]). It marked the peak of VOC-Aceh cooperation in the 17th century.

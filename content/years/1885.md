## Completion of the Geconcentreerde Stelling (1885)

**1885** saw the completion and occupation of the Dutch geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position) in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. On [[1 March]] [[1885]], the evacuation of external posts was declared complete and the new fortified line surrounding [[Kota-Radja]] and [[Olehleh]] was established.

### Withdrawal Operations (January–February 1885)

The critical evacuation of distant posts:

- **13 January 1885:** Seven posts evacuated simultaneously — [[Indrapoeri]], [[Djerir]], [[Glé-Kamping]], [[Long-Lemoh]], [[Tjot-Basetoel]], [[Samagani]], [[Lepong-Ara]], and [[Anagaloeng]] — with remarkably light casualties (1 killed, 13 wounded). Over 2,000 men and numerous vehicles were deployed over 84 palen in approximately 7 hours.
- **12 February 1885:** Evacuation of [[Boekit-Lampaja]] and [[Boekit-Seboen]] (IV Moekims)
- **15 February 1885:** Evacuation of [[Kroeng-Raba]] by sea
- **21 February 1885:** Evacuation of [[Toengkoeb]] (XXVI Moekims), without casualties
- **28 February 1885:** Evacuation of [[Senelop]], [[Montasik]], [[Anagaloeng]], [[Boekloe]], and [[Pakan-Badak]]
- **1 March 1885:** Concentration declared complete

### Posts in the New Stelling

The completed geconcentreerde stelling included: [[Kota-Pohama]], [[Pakan-Kroeng-Tjoet]], [[Lamjong]], [[Boekit-Karang]], [[Roempit]], [[Tjot-Iri]], [[Lampermej]], [[Siroen]], [[Lambaroe]], [[Lamrong]], [[Lampeneroet]], [[Ketapan-Doea]], [[Blang]], [[Lamdjamoe]], and [[Lamtih]].

### Security Measures and Military Reductions

On [[1 March]] [[1885]], [[Colonel H. Demmeni]] established strict *strafbepalingen* (penal provisions) against weapons possession and introduced a pass system requiring population to pass through designated posts. Three field battalions departed for Java: the 10th Battalion on [[24 February]] [[1885]], the 11th Battalion on [[1 March]] [[1885]], and the 2nd Battalion returned in July [[1885]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1885


**[[1885]]** was the year in which military necessity drove the construction of the *ceintuur-trambaan* — the belt-tramway — along the celebrated [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Kota Radja]]. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the belt-tramway was connected by three *straalbanen* (radial lines) to [[Kota Radja]], and thereby to the older tramway running to the roadstead at [[Oelèe Lheuë]] which had been built in [[1875]] and converted to a tramway in [[1882]]. The resulting network formed a wheel-like pattern — a circular line with three spokes — that allowed rapid movement of troops and matériel between the central base at [[Kota Radja]] and the chain of fortified posts enclosing it.

The [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] itself, the famous defensive line around [[Kota Radja]] that had defined the geography of the Dutch occupation since the [[1870s]], was rendered obsolete by the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]]. With the abandonment of the postenlinie, the utility of the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway]] likewise disappeared: it was "gradually cleared" (*geleidelijk opgeruimd*) in the years after [[1896]]. In its place, the long tramway of [[1897]] onward followed the Dutch extension of authority into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]], binding successive military posts to the command centre at [[Kota Radja]].


## Significance


The [[1885]] belt-tramway marks the high point of the *postenlinie* strategy — the doctrine that the Dutch position in Atjeh could be secured by a perimeter of fortified posts around [[Kota Radja]] connected by rail. Its abandonment after [[1896]] reflects the deeper strategic shift that opened the long Atjeh campaign of conquest and pacification.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

The year of execution — in the Koloniaal Verslag's own formula, "al onze troepen begaven zich toen binnen de nieuwe postenlinie, en hiermede was de concentratie voltooid" ("all our troops then went inside the new line of posts, and with this the concentration was completed"). [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] reconstructs the year from the official journal: between 5 and 14 January the northern forts ([[Djerir]], Indrapoerie, [[Gle Kambing]], [[Long Lemoh]], [[Tjot Basetoel]], [[Samagani]], [[Lepong Ara]], [[Pantej Karang]]) were returned to the field; from 10 to 20 January the evacuation south of [[Anak Galoeng]] cost 4 dead and 30 wounded with offensive actions on 15 and 20 January; between 12 and 28 February followed [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Boekit Lampaja]], [[Kroeng Raba]], [[Toengkoep]], Anak Galoeng with [[Boekloe]], [[Senelop]], [[Montassik]] and [[Pakan Badak]]; from 9 to 26 February the clearing of the [[1000-meter strook|1000-metre free strip]] alone cost 3 dead and 15 wounded, from 12 to 31 March 8 killed and 25 wounded. The line was occupied on [[1 March]] [[1885]]: eighteen semi-permanent posts (from [[Kota Pohama]] over [[Lamjong]], [[Boekit Karang]], [[Roempit]], [[Tjot Iri]], the [[Wachthuis aan de Lingkar]], [[Lampermej]], [[Siroen]], [[Lambaroe]], the watchhouse of [[Lamsajoen]], [[Lamrong]], [[Lampeneroet]], the watchhouse of [[Lamara]], [[Ketapang Doewa]], [[Blang]], [[Lamdjame]] to [[Lamtih]]) enclosing 6,700 hectares and requiring about 4,500 men (see [[Completion of the concentrated position (March 1885)]]). On [[2 February]] [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Toekoe di Tiroe]] proclaimed from [[Anak Galoeng]] that the sign of victory was revealed by the retreat of the unbelievers (see [[Proclamation of Toekoe di Tiroe (2 February 1885)]]); on [[25 February]] the watch of [[Penditi]] was surprised in bright daylight. The government report of 4 March declared that the concentration had finished in the most favourable way, "marvellous" — but the soldiers abandoned with tears of rage and pain the bentings they had defended so long, "some with house and all given to the flames" — and majoor [[M. T. H. Perelaer|Perelaer]]'s chronicle (see [[Guerilla raids inside the concentration line (1885)]]) then shows how the Atjehnese attacked watches and patrols inside the line almost weekly, broke up the Décauville tramway, cut the telephone wires, fired on [[Oleh-leh]] and even on the blockhouse at the [[Wachthuis aan de Lingkar|Kali Lengkar]], so that on [[15 October]] the navy had to fire to protect the roadstead, and beri-beri made a third cemetery at Kota Radja necessary — while the English *Straits Times* of [[2 December]] [[1885]] explained these reverses with insinuations of corrupt military authorities. On 8 December [[1885]] the former minister [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] declared in the Eerste Kamer that the concentration had turned out excellently, that the reports were not at all disquieting, and held out to the country the prospect that "when a generation or two will have passed, the rest in Atjeh will be restored". And at the year-end the Dutch public was edified with the news that the English negotiator of the Tenom affair, [[Sir William Maxwell|Maxwell]], had been raised to the peerage, and [[Sir Julian Pauncefote]] decorated, while the Penang Chinese [[Koh Thian Poh]] got a golden watch (see [[Nisero affair (1883–1885)]]).

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1885

### The Telaga Said drilling

For the [[1885]] Telaga Said drilling see the entry on [[Telaga Said drilling (1885)]]. In summary: in [[1885]] the government crew under Fennema's leadership, despatched with drilling equipment to assist the private concessionary Zulkler, drilled the Telaga Said well to "an outstanding success" — the founding technical episode of the northern-Sumatran oil industry, and the originating act of the corporate sequence that would lead to the [[1940]]–[[1941]] production of the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] oil fields on [[Acheh|Atjeh]] territory.

### The Rinaud West-Atjeh coal survey

[[1885]] is also the year of [[Rinaud]]'s survey of the coal seams of the West coast of Atjeh — the earliest systematic colonial-era observation of Atjeh coal, and the earliest evidence of the colonial state's scientific penetration of the Atjeh coastal zone after the military consolidation of the early 1880s. Rinaud's survey recorded three West-Atjeh Paleogene coal localities: the [[Krueng Kluet]] (No. 13, "very thin seams"), South of [[Tapatuan]] (No. 14, "Seam 3 ft thick... 10 places"), and NW-[[Krueng Teunom]] (No. 15, "Seam 2½ ft thick"). For the full account see the entry on [[Rinaud]].

### The Fennema Bukit Sunur topographical survey

[[1885]] is further the year of Fennema's topographical survey of [[Benkulen]], in which "a great number of outcrops of old miocene coal were located" in the [[Bukit Sunur]] area. The Bukit Sunur coal field would subsequently be distinguished into four fields by [[Philippi]] ([[1918]]), but the original discovery and topographical recording is Fennema's. The Bukit Sunur field is in Benkulen rather than in Atjeh, but it is part of the same Sumatran coal province of which the Atjeh coals are the northern component.

### Significance

[[1885]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of the founding technical episode of the northern-Sumatran oil industry (the Telaga Said drilling) and of the earliest systematic colonial-era observation of Atjeh coal (the Rinaud West-Atjeh survey). The combination of these two events in a single year marks [[1885]] as the founding year of the colonial mineral economy of northern Sumatra — the year in which both the oil and the coal records of the region begin, and the year accordingly in which the documentary sequence that leads, via the [[1890]] founding of Royal Dutch and the [[1907]] amalgamation with Shell, to the [[1940]]–[[1941]] production of the Atjeh oil fields is inaugurated.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] describes the [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]]'s ultimatum letter of [[September]] [[1885]] to resident Van Langen at [[Koetaradja]] as *weinig minder dan een ultimatum*: *hij stelde de zaak zeer simpel: of tot den Islam overgaan en met de Atjehers in vrede leven, of met geweld te worden verjaagd met het vooruitzicht nà dit leven de Hel!* The letter opened with the Arabic formula: *De lof zij aan ALLAH alleen, die zijne belofte gehouden, zijnen dienaar tot overwinning geleid.* The Dutch reply came three years later, in the *kabinetsbrief* of [[15 August]] [[1888]], citing Sura 2 verse 257: *"Laat geen dwang in den Godsdienst zijn."*

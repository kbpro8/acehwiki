## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1885

**[[1885]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the early Acehnese religious resistance — as one of the years of the resistance that would culminate in the [[Colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]–[[1911]]. The career of [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]], whose Tiriō family would sustain the resistance across three generations, reaches back into the 1880s.

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954).

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1885

**[[1885]]** was the year in which military necessity drove the construction of the *ceintuur-trambaan* — the belt-tramway — along the celebrated [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Kota Radja]]. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the belt-tramway was connected by three *straalbanen* (radial lines) to [[Kota Radja]], and thereby to the older tramway running to the roadstead at [[Oelèe Lheuë]] which had been built in [[1875]] and converted to a tramway in [[1882]]. The resulting network formed a wheel-like pattern — a circular line with three spokes — that allowed rapid movement of troops and matériel between the central base at [[Kota Radja]] and the chain of fortified posts enclosing it.

The [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] itself, the famous defensive line around [[Kota Radja]] that had defined the geography of the Dutch occupation since the [[1870s]], was rendered obsolete by the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]]. With the abandonment of the postenlinie, the utility of the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway]] likewise disappeared: it was "gradually cleared" (*geleidelijk opgeruimd*) in the years after [[1896]]. In its place, the long tramway of [[1897]] onward followed the Dutch extension of authority into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]], binding successive military posts to the command centre at [[Kota Radja]].

## Significance

The [[1885]] belt-tramway marks the high point of the *postenlinie* strategy — the doctrine that the Dutch position in Atjeh could be secured by a perimeter of fortified posts around [[Kota Radja]] connected by rail. Its abandonment after [[1896]] reflects the deeper strategic shift that opened the long Atjeh campaign of conquest and pacification.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1885


**[[1885]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the early Acehnese religious resistance — as one of the years of the resistance that would culminate in the [[Colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]–[[1911]]. The career of [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]], whose Tiriō family would sustain the resistance across three generations, reaches back into the 1880s.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1885


**[[1885]]** was the year in which military necessity drove the construction of the *ceintuur-trambaan* — the belt-tramway — along the celebrated [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Kota Radja]]. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the belt-tramway was connected by three *straalbanen* (radial lines) to [[Kota Radja]], and thereby to the older tramway running to the roadstead at [[Oelèe Lheuë]] which had been built in [[1875]] and converted to a tramway in [[1882]]. The resulting network formed a wheel-like pattern — a circular line with three spokes — that allowed rapid movement of troops and matériel between the central base at [[Kota Radja]] and the chain of fortified posts enclosing it.

The [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] itself, the famous defensive line around [[Kota Radja]] that had defined the geography of the Dutch occupation since the [[1870s]], was rendered obsolete by the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]]. With the abandonment of the postenlinie, the utility of the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway]] likewise disappeared: it was "gradually cleared" (*geleidelijk opgeruimd*) in the years after [[1896]]. In its place, the long tramway of [[1897]] onward followed the Dutch extension of authority into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]], binding successive military posts to the command centre at [[Kota Radja]].


## Significance


The [[1885]] belt-tramway marks the high point of the *postenlinie* strategy — the doctrine that the Dutch position in Atjeh could be secured by a perimeter of fortified posts around [[Kota Radja]] connected by rail. Its abandonment after [[1896]] reflects the deeper strategic shift that opened the long Atjeh campaign of conquest and pacification.

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1885

### The Telaga Said drilling

For the [[1885]] Telaga Said drilling see the entry on [[Telaga Said drilling (1885)]]. In summary: in [[1885]] the government crew under Fennema's leadership, despatched with drilling equipment to assist the private concessionary Zulkler, drilled the Telaga Said well to "an outstanding success" — the founding technical episode of the northern-Sumatran oil industry, and the originating act of the corporate sequence that would lead to the [[1940]]–[[1941]] production of the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] oil fields on [[Acheh|Atjeh]] territory.

### The Rinaud West-Atjeh coal survey

[[1885]] is also the year of [[Rinaud]]'s survey of the coal seams of the West coast of Atjeh — the earliest systematic colonial-era observation of Atjeh coal, and the earliest evidence of the colonial state's scientific penetration of the Atjeh coastal zone after the military consolidation of the early 1880s. Rinaud's survey recorded three West-Atjeh Paleogene coal localities: the [[Krueng Kluet]] (No. 13, "very thin seams"), South of [[Tapatuan]] (No. 14, "Seam 3 ft thick... 10 places"), and NW-[[Krueng Teunom]] (No. 15, "Seam 2½ ft thick"). For the full account see the entry on [[Rinaud]].

### The Fennema Bukit Sunur topographical survey

[[1885]] is further the year of Fennema's topographical survey of [[Benkulen]], in which "a great number of outcrops of old miocene coal were located" in the [[Bukit Sunur]] area. The Bukit Sunur coal field would subsequently be distinguished into four fields by [[Philippi]] ([[1918]]), but the original discovery and topographical recording is Fennema's. The Bukit Sunur field is in Benkulen rather than in Atjeh, but it is part of the same Sumatran coal province of which the Atjeh coals are the northern component.

### Significance

[[1885]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of the founding technical episode of the northern-Sumatran oil industry (the Telaga Said drilling) and of the earliest systematic colonial-era observation of Atjeh coal (the Rinaud West-Atjeh survey). The combination of these two events in a single year marks [[1885]] as the founding year of the colonial mineral economy of northern Sumatra — the year in which both the oil and the coal records of the region begin, and the year accordingly in which the documentary sequence that leads, via the [[1890]] founding of Royal Dutch and the [[1907]] amalgamation with Shell, to the [[1940]]–[[1941]] production of the Atjeh oil fields is inaugurated.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] describes the [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]]'s ultimatum letter of [[September]] [[1885]] to resident Van Langen at [[Koetaradja]] as *weinig minder dan een ultimatum*: *hij stelde de zaak zeer simpel: of tot den Islam overgaan en met de Atjehers in vrede leven, of met geweld te worden verjaagd met het vooruitzicht nà dit leven de Hel!* The letter opened with the Arabic formula: *De lof zij aan ALLAH alleen, die zijne belofte gehouden, zijnen dienaar tot overwinning geleid.* The Dutch reply came three years later, in the *kabinetsbrief* of [[15 August]] [[1888]], citing Sura 2 verse 257: *"Laat geen dwang in den Godsdienst zijn."*

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

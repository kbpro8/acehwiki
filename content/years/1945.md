**The year 1945** was, in Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan*, the year of the [[Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945|Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945]], the [[proclamation of the Republic|proclamation of the Republic of Indonesia]] of [[17 August]] [[1945]], and the appointment of [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]] as republican Governor of Sumatra — the year that ended the three-and-a-half-year Japanese occupation of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]] and opened the way for the [[Amacab|Amacab]] landings and the social revolution that would, within months, sweep away the *oelèëbalang* class throughout the residency.

## The capitulation of 15 August

"When on 14 August, in the Muslim fasting month, unexpectedly the Japanese capitulation came, the preparations for the Allied invasion were in full swing." The capitulation formally dated from [[15 August]] [[1945]]. "Still in August the English fleet appeared before [[Sabang|Sabang]] and a temporary naval base was established on Sabang. Also the Dutch navy and the Dutch administration, the latter as part of the [[Amacab|Amacab]] (Allied Military Administration Civil Affairs Branch), made their entry." The thousands of enterprise workers from Atjèh, East Sumatra and [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]] who had performed slave labour on Sabang "were transferred to the mainland." On the mainland, the Allied — English — occupation "was limited to the places [[Medan|Medan]], [[Padang|Padang]] and [[Palembang|Palembang]]." The plans of the Chief Co Amacab, Governor of Sumatra [[A. I. Spits|A. I. Spits]], "had to remain unused."

## The proclamation of the Republic and the appointment of Teukoe Moehamad Hasan

The proclamation of the Republic on [[17 August]] [[1945]] — and the appointment of an Atjèhnese, mr. [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]], as republican Governor of Sumatra — "gradually changed the situation." The combination of the proclamation and the appointment aligned the residency firmly with the Republic, and gave the [[Poesa|Poesa]]–*oelama* party the political instrument it had lacked. "The *oelèëbalangs*, freed from Japanese pressure, immediately set about to restore their old position of power," rallying around [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]] of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]], "the symbol of the resistance of the oelèëbalang party." On [[7 September]] [[1945]] — the *[[hari raja puasa|hari raja puasa]]* — "*oelèëbalangs* from all of Aceh traveled to Koetaradja to offer him their congratulations."

## The Japanese retreat and the Masubuchi-kikan

The Japanese retreat was as humiliating as the entry had been triumphant. "As triumphant as the entry had been, so shameful was the evacuation. The years of pent-up hatred of the fanatic population against the new oppressors was unleashed on the retreating troops." Yet even in the final months of the occupation, [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] — released on [[3 July]] [[1945]] from the leadership of the economic affairs department — "took upon himself the task of creating a new 5th column organization, which shortly — in the event of an Allied invasion — would have to carry out its destructive work behind the Allied lines." [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]], [[Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali|Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali]], [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu'éh]] and [[Teukoe Moehamad Amin|Teukoe Moehamad Amin]] took leading parts in the new *[[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]]* — "The old F-organization would resume its task as M-organization."

## The 4th session of the Atjèh Council and the Sumatra Council

The 4th session of the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]] met on [[20 April|20]]–[[21 April]] [[1945]] "again simultaneously with the semi-annual *gun-chó* conference," and raised the rice-delivery quota for the harvest year 1945/1946 from 22,000 to 33,000 tons. [[March]] [[1945]] brought the establishment of an advisory body for the entire of Sumatra, the Sumatra Council (*Sumatora-chú-sangi-in*) at [[Bukittinggi|Bukittinggi]], of which [[Teukoe Nja' Arif|Teukoe Nja' Arif]] was appointed deputy chairman. The council, like the rest of the late-occupation administration, "acquired more and more an unreal character."

## Significance

1945 ended the Japanese occupation but did not restore Dutch authority. The hesitant Allied reoccupation gave the [[Poesa|Poesa]]–*oelama* forces time to consolidate the social revolution that, within months, would sweep away the *oelèëbalang* class throughout Atjèh and align the residency firmly with the Indonesian Republic. The capitulation, the proclamation, and the appointment of [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]] together mark, in Piekaar's reading, the hinge between the Japanese and the republican eras.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1945

**[[1945]]** was the year of the Japanese surrender of August — the end of the *[[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Jappentijd]]* — and of the [[Soekarno|Indonesian declaration of independence]] of [[17 August]] [[1945]], followed by the Allied landing under British command and the beginning of the [[1945]]–[[1949]] Indonesian revolution. From former [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] and other indigenous soldiers who had survived the Japanese occupation, the *Andjing Nica* (5e Bataljon) was raised in [[1945]] — one of the principal Dutch formations of the revolution. The *[[tangsi]]* culture that had been broken by the Japanese occupation was, by August 1945, a memory; the war that followed was fought by a reconstituted colonial army, not the old [[K.N.I.L.]]

## Account from *Nederlands-Indië contra Japan (1959)* — 1945

**The year 1945** was, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the year of the Japanese capitulation of [[15 August]] [[15 August 1945|15 August 1945]] and of Lt [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|Onvlee]]'s November [[1945]] transfer to [[Bangkok|Bangkok]], where the disposition of the *vaandel* (colours) of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] was settled. The year [[1945]] is the staff history's *terminus ad quem* of the Atjeh chapter's narrative of the Dutch wartime presence in Atjeh.

## The 10 May 1945 vaandel return

In the end his departure did not proceed, and he received the vaandel back on 10 May 1945. Unfortunately the gold tassels were then missing, so that only the cloth and the Militaire Willemsorde came into his possession. The return of the vaandel (minus the gold tassels) to Onvlee's possession on [[10 May]] [[10 May 1945|10 May 1945]] — after its temporary hand-over to the Dutch camp commander in [[March 1944|March 1944]] — is the staff history's principal narrative instance of the vaandel's survival through the final months of the Japanese captivity.

## The 15 August 1945 capitulation

Now a period began in which the searches and unannounced inspections took place even more frequently and more thoroughly than before, as a consequence of the finding of a very cleverly concealed radio. In addition, rumours circulated that the entire camp was to be broken up and moved. Because the chance of discovery grew steadily greater, Lieutenant Onvlee requested the Korpscommandant, with whom he was then in the same camp, permission to bury the vaandel in a place that could easily be found again. The request was, however, refused by the Korpscommandant. On 15 August 1945, before the camp in Birma was broken up and moved, Japan capitulated, and he informed the senior Dutch officer present of the success of his undertaking.

## The November 1945 Bangkok transfer and disposition

In November 1945 he was transferred to Bangkok, where he heard rumours that there were plans to incorporate the vaandel in a mixed company of Ambonese Maréchaussees and infantrymen. When the Korpscommandant asked him to return the vaandel, which was still in his possession, he objected. He found it not only little fitting to incorporate the vaandel in a mixed company, but he also appealed to the order given at the time by the Territoriaal Commandant to hand over the vaandel in the Netherlands East Indies to the proper authorities. The Korpscommandant made it known that he did not agree, and finally let it be known that he intended to have Lieutenant Onvlee hand over the vaandel before the front of the troops. This plan was, however, not carried out owing to various circumstances. Since after the war the Korps Maréchaussee was not re-established, no official presentation took place on Java either. After the dissolution of the K.N.I.L. in July 1950, the vaandel was given a place in the Army Museum at Leiden.

## Significance

The year [[1945]] is, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the *terminus ad quem* of the Atjeh chapter's narrative of the Dutch wartime presence in Atjeh, and the year of the formal Japanese capitulation that closed the Dutch captivity. The staff history's emphasis on the vaandel's survival through the final months of the captivity, on Onvlee's objection to the vaandel's incorporation in a mixed Ambonese–Dutch company, and on the vaandel's final placement in the Army Museum at Leiden in July [[1950]] frames [[1945]] as the year that closed the Dutch wartime narrative and opened the post-war disposition of the Maréchaussee's institutional relics.

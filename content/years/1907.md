## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1907

### The amalgamation

Van Bemmelen's account of the [[1907]] amalgamation reads:

> "Of great importance was the amalgamation of the Royal Dutch and the Shell Oil Company in [[1907]]. A new company was to develop the oil concessions in the Netherlands East Indies and to build and operate refineries for crude oil. This subsidiary company, the 'Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij' (Batavian Oil Company, B.P.M.), has since taken over all interests of the Royal Dutch in the Indian Archipelago."

The [[1907]] amalgamation created the B.P.M. as the operating subsidiary for the East Indian oil interests, and the B.P.M. subsequently took over "all interests of the Royal Dutch in the Indian Archipelago". The Royal Dutch Shell group, with its 60 % Dutch (Royal Dutch) and 40 % British (Shell) ownership structure, became the dominant oil company in the East Indies and one of the most successful and influential oil concerns in the world.

### Consequences for Atjeh

The [[1907]] amalgamation and the creation of the B.P.M. had direct consequences for the colonial oil economy of Atjeh. The B.P.M. became the operator of the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh block]], the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]], the [[Aru Bay|Aru Bay]] field, the [[Telaga Said|Telaga Said]] field and the [[Bulu Telang|Bulu Telang]] field — i.e. of the great majority of the North-Sumatran oil fields. The B.P.M.'s dominance of the East Indian oil industry (57.3 % of crude production in [[1940]]) made it the principal corporate actor in the colonial mineral economy of which Atjeh was a part, and the company's operations — anchored by the [[Pangkalan Brandan]] refinery — were the principal industrial presence of the colonial oil economy in the Atjeh region.

### Place in the northern-Sumatran oil sequence

For the full sequence see the entry on [[1890]]. The [[1907]] amalgamation occupies, in the documentary sequence of northern-Sumatran oil exploration, the position between the [[1890]] founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij* and the [[1912]] entry of Standard Oil of New Jersey (through the [[N.K.P.M.]]). The amalgamation accordingly represents the consolidation of the Royal Dutch's East Indian oil interests under the B.P.M., and the establishment of the corporate structure within which the great majority of the colonial Atjeh and northern-Sumatran oil industry was subsequently conducted.

### Significance

[[1907]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of the amalgamation of Royal Dutch with Shell — the founding corporate episode of the [[Royal Dutch Shell]] group, and the occasion for the creation of the [[B.P.M.]] as the Royal Dutch subsidiary for the East Indian oil interests. The B.P.M.'s subsequent dominance of the East Indian oil industry made it the principal corporate actor in the colonial mineral economy of which Atjeh was a part, and the company's operations in the Atjeh region — including the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields — were the principal industrial presence of the colonial oil economy in the Atjeh region. The [[1907]] amalgamation is accordingly the indispensable corporate-historical context for any understanding of the colonial oil economy of Atjeh.

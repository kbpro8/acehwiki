**The year 1599** was, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative and in [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account, the year of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' first Dutch visit to the kingdom of Atjeh]] and of the [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)|death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh]] — the *terminus a quo* of Dutch-Atjehnese relations. In the summer of [[1599]] the brothers [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] and [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]], commanding two ships sent out by the South-Netherlander [[Balthasar de Moucheron|Balthasar de Moucheron]] with the English pilot [[John Davis|John Davis]] aboard, conducted the first Dutch visit to Atjeh on Sumatra, were ambushed by the Atjehnese, lost most of their crew (Cornelis among the dead), and abandoned Frederik de Houtman as a captive at the Atjehnese court — the captivity out of which the first European Malay dictionary would be composed.

## The Houtman brothers' visit

The [[De Kleine Oosterling|Wijma school reader]] records the visit of [[1599]] in Lesson 3: two of them had been sent out by Zeeland merchants. Cornelis Houtman and his brother Frederik were in command. The brothers had little fortune on this voyage. They were the first Netherlands to visit the kingdom of Atjeh on Sumatra.

The visit's end point in the Atjehnese ambush is the school reader's principal instance of the treacherous character of the Atjehnese in the Dutch school-textbook imagination: suddenly the Atjehnese attacked our men. A fierce fight followed, and on both sides many fell. The sailors who were on shore were also seized, and all were killed except eight. Among those eight was Frederik Houtman.

## The De Moucheron expedition and the disaster at Atjeh

In Godee Molsbergen's fuller account, the South-Netherlander of French descent [[Balthasar de Moucheron|Balthasar de Moucheron]], trusting the experience of the [[Cornelis de Houtman|De Houtman]] brothers, took them into his service on a fleet of two ships. The English pilot [[John Davis|John Davis]] served as pilot "om in opdracht van den Graaf van Essex spionnenwerk te verrichten" — to do spy-work on behalf of the Earl of Essex. The voyage to the Indies took fifteen months. The Madagascar population fled at the reappearance of the rough Dutchmen — this was the second Dutch landing there, the first having already left a bad reputation under [[Cornelis de Houtman|De Houtman]]'s own first expedition.

When the fleet reached Atjeh in June [[1599]], the good understanding with the Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]] lasted only briefly. With poisoned fruits and drinks, later with a *kris*-attack, the Atjehnese sought to be rid of the foreigners. There were many dead, including [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]], and many prisoners, including [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]]. See [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)]].

## The escape to Pedir and the homeward voyage

The ships that escaped Atjeh made for [[Pedir]], where they beat off an Atjehnese naval attack; they then touched at the Nicobars and, after much misfortune, reached Middelburg in the summer of [[1600]]. [[Balthasar de Moucheron|De Moucheron]] suffered heavy losses from the expedition. [[John Davis|Davis]] turned his spy-material to English advantage.

## The captivity of Frederik de Houtman

[[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] remained a prisoner in Atjeh for twenty-six months — the captivity that would produce the [[Spraeck ende woord-boeck (1603)|first European Malay dictionary]] of [[1603]] and one of the earliest European catalogues of the southern-hemisphere stars.

## Significance

The year [[1599]] is, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative and in Godee Molsbergen's account, the *terminus a quo* of Dutch-Atjehnese relations. The visit of [[1599]] initiated a sequence of Dutch-Atjehnese encounters that would culminate in the [[Aceh Embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits of 1602]] and, two-and-a-half centuries later, in the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] of [[1873]]–[[1903]]. The year [[1599]] thus marks the beginning of the Dutch-Atjehnese relationship that the rest of the school reader's Atjeh lessons would trace. Godee Molsbergen's framing of the episode — emphasising the role of [[Balthasar de Moucheron|De Moucheron]], the spy-work of [[John Davis|Davis]], and the captivity of [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik]] — adds the international and intelligence dimensions that the school reader omits.

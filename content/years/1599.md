**The year 1599** was, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the year of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' first Dutch visit to the kingdom of Atjeh]] — the *terminus a quo* of Dutch-Atjehnese relations. In the summer of [[1599]] the brothers [[Cornelis Houtman|Cornelis Houtman]] and [[Frederik Houtman|Frederik Houtman]], commanding two Zeeland ships sent out by Zeeland merchants, conducted the first Dutch visit to Atjeh on Sumatra, were ambushed by the Atjehnese, lost most of their crew, and abandoned Frederik Houtman as a captive at the Atjehnese court.

## The Houtman brothers' visit

The [[De Kleine Oosterling|Wijma school reader]] records the visit of [[1599]] in Lesson 3: two of them had been sent out by Zeeland merchants. Cornelis Houtman and his brother Frederik were in command. The brothers had little fortune on this voyage. They were the first Netherlands to visit the kingdom of Atjeh on Sumatra.

The visit's end point in the Atjehnese ambush is the school reader's principal instance of the treacherous character of the Atjehnese in the Dutch school-textbook imagination: suddenly the Atjehnese attacked our men. A fierce fight followed, and on both sides many fell. The sailors who were on shore were also seized, and all were killed except eight. Among those eight was Frederik Houtman.

## Significance

The year [[1599]] is, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the *terminus a quo* of Dutch-Atjehnese relations, and the staff history's opening instance of the treacherous character of the Atjehnese in the Dutch school-textbook imagination. The visit of [[1599]] initiated a sequence of Dutch-Atjehnese encounters that would culminate in the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits (1601)|Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits of 1601]] and, two-and-a-half centuries later, in the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] of [[1873]]–[[1903]]. The year [[1599]] thus marks the beginning of the Dutch-Atjehnese relationship that the rest of the school reader's Atjeh lessons would trace.

**[[1954]]** was the year of publication of both *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* by [[G. W. T. Dames]] — Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. — in 's-[[Gravenhage]] (The Hague), by the *Koninklijke Bibliotheek*, and of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|P. de Roo de la Faille]]'s *[[Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java]]*, the principal modern Dutch-language source gathering the scattered Portuguese references to the [[Sultan of Atjeh (16th century)|Atjehnese sultan]] of [[1539]], his brother-in-law and admiral [[Alleredin Mahomet]], and the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aroe war of 1539]]. It is thus the publication year of two quite different books — one closing the colonial military arc of the K.N.I.L., the other assembling the pre-colonial and early-modern east-coast Sumatran chronicle record.

## The publication

*[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* was published in 's-[[Gravenhage]] in [[1954]] by the *Koninklijke Bibliotheek*. The author — [[G. W. T. Dames]], Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. (Major of Infantry, K.N.I.L., retired) — had served at [[Bakongan]] in the dertiger jaren and at [[Brastagi]] in [[1947]], and wrote the book as a grateful literary memorial to the Ambonese soldiers with whom he had served.

## The post-colonial context

[[1954]] was five years after the [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]] of [[27 December]] [[1949]]. The [[K.N.I.L.]] had been dissolved; the Ambonese soldiers who had served it were either in the army of the Republic of Indonesia (their former enemy) or in the *Republik Maluku Selatan* (the unilateral declaration of independence of the South Moluccas, which would be crushed by the Indonesian army in [[1950]]-[[1952]]). The Netherlands itself was in the post-colonial period: the *Souvereiniteitsoverdracht* had ended the colonial state, and the position of the Ambonese soldiers who had served the Dutch was, in [[1954]], an open wound.

## The book as memorial

The book — written in [[1954]], five years after the dissolution of the colonial army — is therefore a memorial: a grateful literary memorial to the Ambonese soldiers with whom Dames had served. The book's prologue states the purpose: "Mogen de lezers Oom Ambon uit deze regels leren kennen en hem de genegenheid toedragen, die schrijver dezes ertoe bewoog, zijn dankbare herinnering aan hem op papier te zetten."

## Account from *Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java* — 1954

*[[Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java]]* was published by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|P. de Roo de la Faille]] in Den Haag in March [[1954]] — a 68-page compilation that gathers the scattered Portuguese chronicle references (chiefly [[Mendez Pinto]]'s) to the sixteenth-century Sumatran east coast, decodes their *bedekte taal* (veiled language), and ties them to the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]]'s chronicle geography and to the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aroe war of 1539]]. The book is dedicated to [[Dr. D. J. Hulshoff Pol|Dr. D. J. Hulshoff Pol]].

## Significance

[[1954]] is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the year of publication — the year that closes the colonial arc the book narrates. The book's argument is that the dissolution of the [[K.N.I.L.]] in [[1949]] was the end of the *[[tangsi]]* culture that had made *[[Oom Ambon]]*, and that the book itself — written in [[1954]], five years after the dissolution — is a memorial to that culture. The year [[1954]] is therefore the book's marker of its own post-colonial position: a grateful literary memorial, written from the post-colonial Netherlands, to the Ambonese soldiers of the dissolved colonial army.

[[1954]] is, in the same year, significant for the *Curiositeiten* as the *terminus* of P. de Roo de la Faille's decades of research into the *bedekte taal* geography of the Sumatran east coast — research that would become, for later readers, the principal modern gateway to the sixteenth-century chronicle and Portuguese-travel record of [[Aroe]] and the [[Sultan of Atjeh (16th century)|Atjehnese sultanate]].

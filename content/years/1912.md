## Statistical and Administrative Record


The *Atjeh up to date* compilation preserves extensive tabular data for [[1912]], highlighting an unprecedented scale of financial activity:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1912]] record outlays reaching an extraordinary 12,654,575 guilders. Specific departmental ledgers show massive allocations, such as 2,513,135 and 1,852,740 in separate accounting categories.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Monthly and annual tracking of shipping and trade metrics shows figures such as 180,929 and 144,018 across various regional categories, alongside secondary ledgers showing 543,566.
*   **Shipping Registries:** Vessel arrivals and tonnage registries for the year record highly granular logistical movements indexed at 169, 254, 97, 12, 19, and 11 in primary tracking columns.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1912


**[[1912]]** was a year of important administrative and infrastructural advances in [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. On the financial-administrative side, [[1912]] saw the creation of 15 new *onderafdeelingskassen* (sub-divisional treasuries) in the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency — by far the largest such expansion in any single Buitenbezittingen gewest in the [[1910]]–[[1913]] period, and a direct measure of the depth to which Dutch administrative authority had penetrated the residency since the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]]. The number of kassen in Atjeh thereby rose from 3 (in [[1910]]) to 18 (ultimo [[1913]]), making Atjeh the residency with the largest number of territorial treasuries in the entire Buitenbezittingen.

On the infrastructure side, [[1912]] marked the completion of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] extension from [[Langsa]] to [[Koeala Simpang]], a project that had been under construction since [[1910]]. This extension brought the second tramway of the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]] to its projected western terminus at [[Koeala Simpang]], and set the stage for the [[1913]] decisions to extend the network further southward toward [[Tamiang]], [[Besitang]], and [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]].

[[1912]] is also the year in which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s operating account first began to show signs of approaching the break-even point — a turning point in the long transition of the line from a purely military instrument (whose operating account had "always" yielded a deficit until [[1912]]) to a paying economic enterprise. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau notes that "the economic value of the line stands wholly apart from its military value," and that the dramatic drop in the share of Gouvernements-vervoer (government transport) in total traffic — from 60% of freight in [[1904]] to 11% in [[1913]] — is the clearest single index of the Atjeh-tram's transformation into an economic institution.


## Significance


[[1912]] thus marks three converging transitions in Atjeh: the administrative deepening (15 new onderafdeelingskassen), the infrastructural extension ([[Langsa]]–[[Koeala Simpang]]), and the financial turning-point (the Atjeh-tram's approach to break-even). All three are facets of the same post-[[1908]] consolidation of Dutch rule.

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1912

### The N.K.P.M. founding

For the full account of the [[1912]] N.K.P.M. founding see the entry on [[N.K.P.M.]]. In summary: in [[1912]] the Standard Oil Company of New Jersey started operations in the Netherlands East Indies through its subsidiary the N.K.P.M., which by [[1940]] "produced about one fourth of the total crude output in the territory". The N.K.P.M.'s entry into the East Indian oil industry marked the first significant American participation in what had previously been a Royal-Dutch-dominated industry, and the N.K.P.M. subsequently became the second-largest oil company in the East Indies, after the [[B.P.M.]].

### Place in the East-Indian oil-industry sequence

The [[1912]] N.K.P.M. founding occupies, in the documentary sequence of East-Indian oil-industry corporate development, the position between the [[1907]] amalgamation of Royal Dutch with Shell (creating the [[B.P.M.]] as the Royal Dutch subsidiary) and the [[1931]] entry of the Standard Oil Company of California (through its subsidiary the [[N.P.P.M.]]). The sequence runs:

1. **[[1883]]**: [[J. Zulkler|J. Zulkler]] obtains the first North-Sumatran oil concession.
2. **[[1885]]**: The [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]]-led government crew drills the [[Telaga Said]] well to success.
3. **[[1890]]**: Founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij* (the future Royal Dutch).
4. **[[1907]]**: Amalgamation of Royal Dutch with Shell, creating the [[B.P.M.]].
5. **[[1912]]**: Standard Oil of New Jersey enters through the [[N.K.P.M.]].
6. **[[1931]]**: Standard Oil of California enters through the [[N.P.P.M.]].
7. **[[1940]]–[[1941]]**: The [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen]] production tables record the Atjeh oil fields.

### Significance

[[1912]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year in which the Standard Oil Company of New Jersey — through its subsidiary the [[N.K.P.M.]] — entered the East Indian oil industry, marking the first significant American participation in what had previously been a Royal-Dutch-dominated industry. The N.K.P.M. would subsequently become the operator of the [[Pulu Pandjang]] field in the [[Atjeh oil complex]] — the only Atjeh-complex field operated by the company, and the only documented instance of N.K.P.M. operations on Atjeh territory. The [[1912]] N.K.P.M. founding is accordingly part of the corporate-historical context of the colonial Atjeh oil economy, and the N.K.P.M.'s subsequent presence on Atjeh territory (however modest) is the documentary evidence of the multi-company concession system that characterised the Atjeh oil complex.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records [[1912]] as the year of the [[Death of Tengkoe di Barat (1912)|death of Tengkoe di Barat]]. *Behrens, pas bij de maréchaussée ingedeeld, zat hem toen met twee brigades op de hielen, en in de spits liep een voortreffelijk spoorzoeker, de Amboneesche sergeant Dambohpulu*. The chase ran along the *Aloer Soea* into the wildernis; at last the brigade came upon [[Tengkoe di Barat]] near a waterfall, *en het werd een treffen waarbij de vrouw van den Tengkoe zich zóó vóór haar man plaatste, dat één kogel hen beiden doodde* — the wife placed herself before her husband, and one bullet killed both. *Comme ils tombent bien*, Zentgraff notes: how well they fall together.

The same year saw the continued hunt for [[T. Radja Sabi]] — the *adelaarsjong* of [[Keureutoe]]: on [[4 January]] [[1912]] one of his protectors was killed; in [[April]] a *groep uit Idi* took one of his *fourageurs*; in [[July]] a *patr. van Lho Soekon* took another; in [[September]] an *overval* of his schuilplaats failed — *de bende ontkomt in het zware pajaterrein ongedeerd, zij het met achterlating van voorraden*; in [[October]] four of his bende submitted, but the boy himself was *op het laatste moment ontvoerd door Tng. di Leubo*. *In dit wild bedrijf groeit de jongen op; de dood kruist herhaaldelijk zijn weg en om hem stapelen de lijken zich op als offers van een mysterieuzen eeredienst.*

## Account from *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1930) — the *Pantjang Alas* founding regulations

[[1912]] was the year of the earliest regulations in the *[[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]* compilation of [[1930]] — the [[Meulaboh]] *boschproducten-belasting* regulation of [[5 April]] [[1912]] (Reg. No. 2), the originating *Pantjang Alas* regeling on which all subsequent *landschap*-level *Pantjang Alas* regulations were patterned, and the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]]'s *Gouverneursbesluit* of [[12 April]] [[1912]] No. 247/18 (Reg. No. 3), the *besluit* by which the Gouverneur-Generaal's authorisation — conveyed by telegram of [[12 April]] [[1912]] — was operationalised across the entire *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*. The two [[1912]] instruments together constitute the first exercise of the fiscal powers the *Zelfbestuurders* of Atjeh would, after the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|pacification]], exercise under the supervisory *goedkeuring* of the colonial state.

### The Meulaboh boschproducten-belasting of 5 April 1912 (Reg. No. 2)

The originating *Pantjang Alas* regeling was the *Zelfbestuursverordening* of [[5 April]] [[1912]] — Reg. No. 2 of the [[1930]] compilation — issued by the *Kepala negeri Meulaboh* / *Het Zelfbestuur van Meulaboh* on the Westkust of Atjeh. The compilation's *Inhoud* records the regulation:

> "ZELFBESTUURSVERORDENING van 5 April 1912 houdende vaststelling van regelen betreffende de heffing van eene belasting op de uit het landschap Meulaboh afkomstige boschproducten. (Afdeelingen Noord-, Oost- en Westkust van Atjeh)".

The opening article of the regeling — *Fatsal 1* in Malay and *Artikel 1* in Dutch, printed in parallel columns — fixed the tax in its definitive bilingual form:

> "Fatsal 1. Artikel 1. (1) Didjalankan soeatoe belasting dengan bernama Pantjang Alas diatas keoentoengan kehasilan hoetan didalam negeri Meulaboh sebagaimana berikoet dibawah ini: a. kemenjan, damar dan lain sebagainja. (1) Er wordt, onder den naam Pantjang Alas, eene belasting geheven van de navolgende, in het landschap Meulaboh gewonnen boschproducten, te weten: a. benzoë, damar en andere gom- en harssoorten niet afzonderlijk genoemd".

The taxable *boschproducten* covered the full range of the Atjehnese forest economy — *benzoë* (kemenyan), *damar*, *getah* (caoutchouc / *getah pertjah*), *ivoor* (gading), *neushoorn* (soengoe badak), *was* (lilin), *timmerhout* (papan / kajoe jang belom digergadji), *rotan*, *staartpeper* (lada boh keumoekoh). The rate was set at 10% of the value of the taxable products: "*Fatsal 2. Artikel 2. Belasting itoe dipoengoet sepeloeh dalam seratoes dari harga kehasilan hoetan terseboet dalam fatsal 1. De belasting bedraagt tien ten honderd van de waarde der in artikel 1 bedoelde producten*".

### The Gouverneursbesluit of 12 April 1912 No. 247/18 (Reg. No. 3)

The same day — [[12 April]] [[1912]] — the Civiel en Militair Gouverneur issued the *Gouverneursbesluit* of [[12 April]] [[1912]] No. 247/18 — Reg. No. 3 of the compilation — establishing the practical *heffing* and *inning* procedure for the *Pantjang Alas* across the entire Gouvernement. The *besluit*'s recital records the metropolitan authorisation by which it was activated — the *telegram van den 1sten Gouvernements Secretaris ddo. 12 April 1912*:

> "Gelezen het telegram van den 1sten Gouvernements Secretaris ddo. 12 April 1912, waarbij de mededeeling wordt gedaan, dat de [Gouverneur-Generaal] van Ned.-Indië vergund heeft, dat de Zelfbesturen in het Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden onder den naam 'Pantjang Alas' eene belasting heffen, ten bedrage van 10%".

The *besluit* fixed the *kwitantie* (receipt) and *stortingsstaat* (deposit-statement) procedure, established the quarterly valuation of the *boschproducten* by the *Hoofd van gewestelijk bestuur* in *overleg* with the Zelfbestuur, and provided for the *Gezien* countersignature of the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar* on every *kwitantie*. The cluster of parallel *Pantjang Alas* regulations of April [[1912]] — Idi (26 April), Langan (28 April), Tamiang (28 April), Tjulung (15 April), Tapaktoean (15 April), and the Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen (30 April) — together with the Meulaboh originating regeling of [[5 April]] [[1912]], fixed the *Pantjang Alas* as the principal revenue instrument of the *zelfbesturende landschappen* of the Gouvernement from [[1912]] onward. The [[1912]] instruments together constitute the first operational exercise of the constitutional theory of Dutch indirect rule in Atjeh after the pacification: the *Zelfbestuurders* legislating in their own name and in their own Malay, the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* supplying only the *goedkeuring*, and the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar* exercising the *contrôle* that made the fiscal arrangement enforceable.

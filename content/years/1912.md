**1912** witnessed a massive spike in recorded colonial expenditures and logistical movements in [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. The surviving statistical extracts from this year suggest either a significant escalation in infrastructure development, a major military pacification push in the interior of [[Sumatra]], or a fundamental shift in how the Dutch administration accounted for the costs of the [[Aceh War]].

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The *Atjeh up to date* compilation preserves extensive tabular data for [[1912]], highlighting an unprecedented scale of financial activity:

*   **Financial Expenditures:** Aggregate colonial financial lines for [[1912]] record outlays reaching an extraordinary 12,654,575 guilders. Specific departmental ledgers show massive allocations, such as 2,513,135 and 1,852,740 in separate accounting categories.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** Monthly and annual tracking of shipping and trade metrics shows figures such as 180,929 and 144,018 across various regional categories, alongside secondary ledgers showing 543,566.
*   **Shipping Registries:** Vessel arrivals and tonnage registries for the year record highly granular logistical movements indexed at 169, 254, 97, 12, 19, and 11 in primary tracking columns.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1912

**[[1912]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the *[[bekas]]* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] — as the year in which a soldier wrote on Leuhong a note about the [[1918]] opheffing of that *detachement*, a *bekas* still legible in [[1934]], twenty-two years later. The *bekas* — the trace or mark left by passage — is the book's symbol of the physical and moral imprint of the Marechaussee on the Acehnese landscape.

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954).

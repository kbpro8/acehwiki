**1828** was a pivotal year in the Dutch colonial theatre of the Indonesian archipelago, recorded in *Raffles' Atjeh‑overeenkomst van 1819* by [[P. H. van der Kemp]] and in a Dutch naval award register that notes a marine decoration (No. 28) for an “*expeditie op Celebes*” (expedition to Celebes). The award was granted to **J. A. van den Velden**, a marine officer who later participated in Sumatra‑related operations, thereby linking the Celebes campaign to the broader Dutch‑Aceh conflict. The year also saw the continuation of the [[Java War (1825-1830)|Java War]] and a series of diplomatic and military exchanges on the east coast of Sumatra.

## Chronology

- On [[7 February]] [[1828]], [[Robert Fullerton]] wrote [[Fullerton's letter to Sultan of Siak (7 February 1828)]], a correspondence that reflects the British‑Dutch rivalry in the region.  
- Throughout [[1828]] the [[Bellona mission to Sumatra's East Coast (1827-1828)]] operated, concluding its activities that year.  
- The [[Said Akil's Deli adventure (1824-1828)]] reached its terminus, with [[Said Akil]] returning to [[Deli]] after a four‑year expedition.  
- The Dutch marine award to [[J. A. van den Velden]] was recorded, highlighting the circulation of experienced officers between the Celebes and Sumatra theatres.

## Significance

The entry illustrates the fluidity of Dutch military personnel across colonial fronts: officers who gained experience in the [[Celebes expedition]] were later deployed to Sumatra, bringing tactical knowledge that contributed to Dutch successes during the later phases of the [[Java War]] and the ongoing Dutch‑Aceh confrontations. The award to [[J. A. van den Velden]] exemplifies how naval honours served both as recognition and as a mechanism for career mobility within the colonial armed forces, reinforcing the interconnectedness of Dutch operations across the archipelago.

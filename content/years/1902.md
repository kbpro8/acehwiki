## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1902

**[[1902]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the decorations of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] — as the year in which the korporaal [[Nussy]] received the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] 4e klas in July [[1902]] for an earlier gevecht, beginning the decoration arc that would culminate in his M.W.O. 3e klas for [[1911]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1902

**[[1902]]** is identified by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau as the moment at which "the time at which a beginning was made with the complete subjection to our effective authority — by which the unfamiliarity with the greater part of the Buitenbezittingen was lifted — properly dates." The Bureau frames [[1902]] as the year in which the post-[[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|1896]] strategic reorientation in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] was extended, by deliberate policy choice, to other parts of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] — specifically to [[Djambi]] and the adjoining region of [[Koerintji]].

The decision to pursue "an active policy with regard to Djambi and thereby also against Koerintji" was adopted by the Indische Regeering in [[1902]] "principally in consequence of the relevant advices of the Residents of [[Palembang]] and [[Benkoelen]] and of Dr. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]." The role of Snouck Hurgronje — already the principal architect of the post-[[1896]] Atjeh policy — in extending the same logic to [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]] places [[1902]] as a hinge moment in the wider colonial project of which Atjeh was the leading edge.

The Bureau is explicit that the [[1896]] Atjeh turn and the [[1902]] Djambi turn are phases of a single transformation: "after a new direction was inaugurated in our policy in Atjeh in [[1896]] following the betrayal of Toekoe Oemar, which would lead to the complete pacification of that territory," the date at which effective authority began to be extended across the Buitenbezittingen as a whole "properly dates only from [[1902]]." Atjeh thus functions as the template for the wider Buitenbezittingen campaign of "effective authority."

## Significance

[[1902]] is the moment at which the Atjeh model of post-[[1896]] active pacification was deliberately extended to other parts of the Buitenbezittingen. The central role of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in both decisions underscores the intellectual continuity of the project. [[1902]] thus marks the transition from an Atjeh-specific strategic reorientation to a colonial-wide policy of effective authority.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1902** is the year in which [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) traversed [[Acheh|Atjeh]] "van Noord tot Zuid" — from north to south — and was able to certify to the [[Tweede Kamer]] that the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] had progressed further than he had thought, and that the horrors of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] were already past. The same year also saw the installation of the [[Commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart ([[1902]])|commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart der inlandse bevolking]] — the commission on the diminished welfare of the indigenous population — whose report, when it finally appeared in [[1914]] in no fewer than 33 volumes, would become one of the foundation documents of [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] governance.

### Van Kol's traverse of Atjeh

In [[1902]] [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] — social-democratic member of the [[Tweede Kamer]], former colonial civil servant and one of the most consistent parliamentary critics of the Atjeh war — travelled through Atjeh from north to south. On his return he reported that the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] was further advanced than he had anticipated and that the "gruwelen" — the horrors — of the war belonged to the past. The illusion of approaching peace was, however, almost immediately dispelled by the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]] in [[1904]].

### The Commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart

In the same year misoogst and hongersnood in several parts of [[Java]] had made it impossible for the government to delay action; the [[Commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart ([[1902]])|commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart]] was installed. Its final report, when it appeared in [[1914]], filled 33 volumes — a monument of [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] documentation that [[D. Fock|Fock]] drily noted had "naar het meer dan volmaakte [gestreefd]" — aspired to more than perfection. More modest but at least as useful were the *economisch-historische bijdragen* issued by the [[Ministerie van Koloniën]] in [[1904]] under the editorship of [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]], [[D. Fock|Fock]] and [[Kielstra]].

### Cremer's Comptabiliteitswet amendment

In [[1902]] [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] — minister of Colonies in the [[kabinet Pierson]] — submitted a wetsontwerp to amend the [[Indische Comptabiliteitswet]] so as to abolish the *bijdrage* (contribution) of [[Nederlandsch-Indië]] to the finances of the realm. The amendment, finally enacted on [[26 June]] [[1903]] (S. 172), formally closed the [[Batig Slot|batig-slot]] era.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records two key events of [[1902]]. On [[22 August]] [[1902]] [[T. Tjhi Toenong]] laid a *hinderlaag* in the high *alang-alang* near Meunasah Djeuro against a Dutch transport of twenty *bajonetten* from [[Simpang Olim]]: seven soldiers killed in the first rush, five *geweren* lost. *Een kranig Europeesch soldaat, Fortier, wist het povere restje bijeen te houden tot luitenant Van Gheel Gildemeester met 40 man te hulp kwam.* The gedenksteen: *Hulde aan de gevallenen bij den klewangaanval te Simpang Olim op 22 Augustus 1902.* Also in [[1902]], [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]], advancing via Isaq into the Gajoland, was forced to retreat at the *Boer ni Intem-Intem*: *men zag: de Kompeuni durft niet* — a impression the Gajoes would hold until [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s colonne of [[1904]] arrived from an unexpected direction.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

**[[1875]]** was a watershed year in the Dutch colonial history of [[Atjeh]], marked by a series of administrative reforms, a severe health crisis, the inauguration of the first railway line in the region, and a decisive shift from punitive raids to a sustained occupation.  In the spring, [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] issued his programmatic note of [[30 April]] [[1875]] and returned to the field on [[8 November]] [[1875]] after a brief leave; the Governor‑Generalship passed to [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Governor‑General Van Lansberge]] on [[26 March]] [[1875]]; a health commission was created by Indies decree of [[12 April]] [[1875]]; and the regulation of the [[heerendiensten|heerendiensten]] was confirmed on [[1 October]] [[1875]].  Simultaneously, the Dutch launched a renewed offensive under Colonel [[M. P. Meyer]], landing on [[26 December]] [[1875]] and beginning a programme of permanent fort construction that would shape the later [[Aceh War]].

## Governance and Administrative Reforms



### Van Lansberge’s Assumption of Office


On [[26 March]] [[1875]] [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Governor‑General Van Lansberge]] succeeded [[J. Loudon|Mr. J. Loudon]] following the Royal Decree of [[17 December]] [[1874]].  In January [[1875]] the vice‑president of the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad of Nederlandsch‑Indië]], [[O. van Rees|Mr. O. van Rees]], was removed from the commission for the improvement of Indian taxes after the Opperbestuur, advised by the [[Raad van State|Raad van State]] in [[November 1874]], judged that article 36 of the [[Regeeringsreglement|Regeeringsreglement]] prohibited such a dual role.

### Health Commission


An Indies decree of [[12 April]] [[1875]] appointed a commission to investigate the “many and serious cases of illness” afflicting Dutch positions in Atjeh.  Its report of [[1 July]] [[1875]] prompted partial implementation, notably the ordering of works at the sea‑head on iron screw‑piles at [[Oleh‑leh|Oleh‑leh]] (completed in September [[1876]]) and the railway between Oleh‑leh and [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] (completed in November [[1876]]).

### Regulation of the Heerendiensten


The decree of [[1 October]] [[1875]] confirmed the existing regulation of the [[heerendiensten|heerendiensten]], while pressure grew to abolish the *pantjensdiensten* (services owed by native chiefs).  The same month saw continued progress toward the abolition of slavery in the *buitenbezittingen* (outer possessions).

### Other Fiscal Measures


By the wet of [[6 April]] [[1875]] (Stbl. n°. 61) a sum was earmarked for the construction of the Soerabaya—Pasoeroean—Malang railway line, reflecting broader colonial investment in infrastructure.

## Military Operations and Campaigns



### Pel’s Programme (30 April 1875)


In his note of [[30 April]] [[1875]] to the Governor‑General, General Pel evaluated the situation after the successful capture of the “hoofd‑stelling” and secured sea communication, but lamented that “all those victories have as yet given few political results.”  He identified the war‑party—led by chiefs such as [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]], [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]], and [[Toekoe Rajoet Lamkapang|Toekoe Rajoet Lamkapang]]—as a persistent obstacle.  Pel’s programme called for consolidation of the current position, resumption of hostilities when feasible, introduction of direct administration in occupied territories, and avoidance of imposing direct rule on regions that might willingly submit.

### Pel’s Leave and Return


Promoted to major‑general by Royal Decree of [[18 February]] [[1875]], Pel departed for Java on [[13 June]] [[1875]] to recover his health.  He reported that the fortifications at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] were “on all sides secured by an unbroken chain of fortifications” and that sea communication via the Atjeh‑rivier, the landing‑place at [[Oleh‑leh|Oleh‑leh]], and the newly laid road was fully assured.  Pel returned to Atjeh on [[8 November]] [[1875]] with instructions dated [[10 November]] [[1875]] to act on his earlier programme and to subjugate the [[VI Moekims|VI]], [[IV Moekims|IV]] and [[IX Moekims|IX]] Moekims as well as the eastern territory up to [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]].  During his absence, Colonel [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem]] acted as temporary replacement.

### Colonel M. P. Meyer’s Renewed Offensive


In the latter half of [[1875]] the Dutch shifted to a policy of permanent occupation.  Colonel [[M. P. Meyer]] was appointed commander of the 1875 offensive.  Under his direction the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]] (the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]]) landed on [[26 December]] [[1875]] at a strategically chosen beachhead, establishing a supply depot and immediately commencing the construction of coastal forts.  These fortifications, later documented in Cambier’s monographs, enabled a continuous Dutch military footprint and reduced the reliance on intermittent punitive raids.

The campaign continued through [[1876]], officially concluding on [[4 September]] [[1876]], and demonstrated a coordinated use of naval squadrons, marine detachments, and regular army battalions.  Contemporary patrol reports by [[J. B. Verhey|Verhey]] (continuing from [[1874]]) emphasize Meyer’s focus on a sustained presence rather than isolated actions.

## Health Crisis


The year [[1875]] saw 957 deaths and 5 151 evacuations from cholera and other diseases.  Contemporary accounts attributed the “unfavourable situation” to poor drinking water, fallow rice fields, deep‑cutting lagunes, extensive ground‑moving, deforestation, and inadequate shelter against the variable climate.  The high morbidity forced healthy soldiers to perform exhausting duties, which in turn accelerated further sickness.

## Infrastructure: Railway and Fortifications



### First Railway Line


[[1875]] marked the inauguration of the first railway line in Atjeh, a modest track ordered by the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]] linking the roadstead of [[Oelèe Lheuë]] to the principal Dutch military station at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]].  According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau, this “spoorbaantje” was built “shortly after the beginning of the [[Atjeh‑expeditie (1873)|Atjeh‑expedition]]” and thus predates the systematic construction of the later [[Atjeh‑tram|Atjeh‑tramway]] by more than a decade.

The line served as the seed for the extensive [[Atjeh‑tram|Atjeh‑tramway]] network: in [[1882]] the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|Department of Civil Public Works (B.O.W.)]] took over management, converting it into a tramway; in [[1885]] a *ceintuur‑trambaan* (belt line) was added along the celebrated [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]; and in [[1891]] control reverted to the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]].

### Fort Construction under Meyer


The forts erected after the [[26 December]] [[1875]] landing formed the backbone of the Dutch permanent presence.  They protected supply lines, facilitated inland patrols, and anchored the later expansion of the railway and tramway systems.

## Biographical Note: Cornelis Jacobus Snijders


The year [[1875]] also marked the end of Lieutenant‑Colonel [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede Luitenant C. J. Snijders]]’ two‑year detachment in Atjeh.  He had arrived at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] on [[8 November]] [[1874]] and participated in the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam operation]] of [[13 November]] [[1874]].  Throughout [[1875]] the Mineurs en Sappers of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] maintained the sea‑head at [[Oleh‑leh|Oleh‑leh]] and the railway between Oleh‑leh and Kota Radja, despite the heavy disease losses.  Snijders returned to the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] in [[1875]] and later served as [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chef van de Generale Staf]] ([[1910]]–[[1918]]) and [[Opperbevelhebber van Land‑ en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land‑ en Zeemacht]] during the [[First World War]].

## Significance


[[1875]] encapsulates a turning point in the Dutch conquest of Atjeh: administrative centralisation under Van Lansberge, a concerted health response, the first railway imprint on the Acehnese landscape, and—most critically—the launch of Colonel Meyer’s sustained offensive that transformed Dutch tactics from episodic raids to permanent occupation.  The infrastructure projects of 1875 laid the material foundations for the later [[Atjeh‑tram|Atjeh‑tramway]], while the military reforms foreshadowed the intensified phase of the [[Aceh War]] that would dominate the final decades of the nineteenth century.

## Account from *Een Vaderlandsch Belang* by H. Borel (1878)


**[[1875]]** was the year in which [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]], having inherited [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s "zeer gebrekkige stelling," completed the [[Stelling-Pel|defensive ring around Kota Radja]] and revealed to [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]] at [[Buitenzorg]] his secret plan to isolate Atjeh from the sea by a line from the [[Kroeng Raba]] to the [[Kroeng Raya]]. It was also a year of devastating cholera, of Pel's three months' leave, and of the [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Buitenzorg planning session]] that set the great December offensive in motion. Borel treats 1875 as the year in which the *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture) was finally abandoned — yet also as a year whose promising beginning was interrupted by the very human cost of the war.

## Completing the Stelling-Pel (January–May [[1875]])


The year opened with the [[Conquest of Blang Tjoet (18 January 1875)|conquest of Blang Tjoet]] ([[18 January]] [[1875]]) and the [[Conquest of Mandarsa Poeti (28 January 1875)|conquest of Mandarsa Poeti]] ([[28 January]] [[1875]]). On [[1 February]] [[1875]] the enemy tried to retaliate with a dawn attack on the [[Lampoe Oek|nipa-benteng]], easily foiled. On [[4 February]] [[1875]] [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]] took [[Lampriet]] without resistance. The [[Surprise of Lamara Olejlo (14–15 February 1875)|surprise of Lamara Olejlo]] on the night of [[14 February|14]]–[[15 February]] [[1875]] — [[Kapitein von Ende|kapitein von Ende]]'s unauthorized coup that seized the abandoned enemy fortification and held it against the dawn return of the Atjehneezen — was the most celebrated minor exploit of the period; von Ende received from Pel both formal praise and a reprimand, "hij had beide ten volle verdiend; het behaalde succes maakte alles goed." On [[17 February]] [[1875]] a new fort was built at [[Kwala Titipandjang]] to cover the banks of the [[Kroeng Broek]]. On the King's birthday late in February, Pel was promoted to generaal-majoor and invested with the Commander's cross of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]]. The conquest of [[Lohong]], [[Setee]], [[Lamteboe]] and [[Gitjiel]] in April brought the whole [[Sawah|sawah]] south of [[Longbatta]] into Dutch hands "at cost of fresh bloody sacrifices," and by early May the [[Completion of the defensive ring around Kota Radja (May 1875)|continuous line around K. Radja]] was complete — an unbroken line from [[Blang Tjoet]] on the left bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] westward over [[Longbatta-Mesigit|Longbatta Mesigit]], [[Lohong]], [[Lamara]], [[Gitjiel]], [[Setee]], [[Lamteboe]], [[Poengej Blang Tjoet]], [[Poe Oe]], [[Soerian]] and [[Blang Oe]] to the sea-strand at [[Oeleh Leh]]. K. Radja now formed "doelmatige centraal stelling" it should have been from the beginning.

## Pel's Leave and the Buitenzorg Session (June–November [[1875]])


On [[12 June]] [[1875]] Pel — exhausted, his health shattered after "ruim een jaar" in command — handed over military and civil command ad interim to [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] and left for [[Buitenzorg]] to recover. Borel notes that even the strongest constitution would have succumbed in Atjeh. On [[20 June]] [[1875]] the [[Poelolo Bras|Poelolo Bras lighthouse]] was completed and its light could be "ontstoken" to mark the koers for ships at night; a month later it was plechtig ingewijd — "een werk, waarbij de geheele beschaafde wereld belang had." During the Buitenzorg leave took place the [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Pel's Buitenzorg planning session]] of July 1875, in which Pel explained to the newly appointed [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]] and the commandant van het leger his conviction that the *afwachtende stelsel* was useless. His plan: as soon as the good moesson returned, resume offensives; push the post at Soerian forward; invade [[Toekoe Nanta|Toekoe Nanta]]'s [[VI Moekims]]; occupy [[Sinangri]] and sweep the western bank of the [[Kwala Tjangkoel]]; later extend the linie from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast to the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raya]] on the north coast, isolating Atjeh from the sea. The GG agreed to "den oorlog met Atjeh op de meest doortastende wijze te voeren" and promised ruime versterkingen. In July [[Sinangri]] was occupied without opposition — considerably improving the stelling at its weakest point between Oeleh Leh and Soerian. An enemy attempt to break the south line on the night of [[21 June|21]]–[[22 June]] [[1875]] was "bij tijds verijdeld."

## The Cholera Catastrophe (February [[1875]])


The [[Cholera catastrophe at Kota Radja (February 1875)|cholera catastrophe at Kota Radja]] raged horribly among troops and coolies in February. "Since landing on Atjeh coast the Dutch had never in so short a time suffered such appalling losses." In the K. Radja ambulances alone almost 500 people died — 270 of them military — averaging 15–18 per day with peak days of 25 or even 30. The absolute necessity of strong measures was recognised "als de geheele Indische strijdmacht niet ten ondergang moest worden prijsgegeven." As a first step the ambulances were moved to the [[Pantej Perak|grasvlakte of Pantej Perak]] south of [[Penajoeng]], where engineers urgently began building a camp for a battalion of infantry to cover the new hospitals. The Kraton now resembled "one great hospital"; the dead were constantly carried through the interior, simply wrapped in a sheet, on wheeled stretchers drawn by two [[Kettinggangers in Atjeh|kettinggang convicts]], to the cemetery at [[Kota Podjoet|K. Podjoet]]. Borel strongly criticises this practice as injurious to morale.

## Return of Pel and the Launch of the New Offensive (November–December [[1875]])


In September and October, while Pel was still at Buitenzorg, the Atjehneezen used the relative quiet to build versterkingen near Dutch posts; patrouilles and communications between posts were attacked; in September several detachments south of K. Radja and at Lampriet were "in groot gevaar om afgesneden en in de pan gehakt te worden." On [[9 November]] [[1875]] Pel resumed betrekking as militaire and civiele bevelhebber, and found the toestand such that offensive action was needed "zoo spoedig mogelijk." Skirmishes at [[Kota Alam]] and [[Sinangri]] in mid-November and the [[Night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit (22 November 1875)|night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit]] on [[22 November]] [[1875]] — when a band of ~50 Atjehneezen climbed the low borstwering and "met de blanke [[Klewang|klewang]]" hacked among sleeping manschappen, wounding 13 including two officers — cemented Pel's conviction. On [[26 November]] [[1875]] the enemy attacked along the entire ooster-linie at [[Kota Alam]], [[Lemboe Oost|Lemboe-O.]] and [[Lampriet]]. With the arrival of the [[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|Barissans bataljon]] on [[10 December]] [[1875]] and the 9de bataljon on [[21 December]] [[1875]], Pel could at last launch the great [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims]] on [[26 December]] [[1875]] — opening the campaign that would carry the war into 1876.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the *groote politieke fout*


[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* identifies [[1875]] as the year of the *groote politieke fout* — the year in which the [[Van Swieten's Instructie (22 April 1874)|Van Swieten programme]] of [[1874]] — the *geconcentreerde stelling* at [[Kota Radja|Kotja-Radja]] held by a 2500-man garrison — was formally abandoned on the proposal of [[Pel (governor)|Generaal Pel]] and the newly installed [[Van Lansberge|Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge]], and in which the colony committed itself, by Pel's [[Pel's Plan (30 April 1875)|letter to the Gouverneur-Generaal of 30 April 1875]], to "het op grootere schaal aanvallend optreden met de wapenen in de vallei van Groot-Atjeh" — the large-scale offensive with arms in the valley of Groot-Atjeh.

### Pel's letter of 30 April 1875


The defining act of [[1875]] was Pel's letter to G.-G. van Lansberge of [[30 April]] [[1875]] — the *brief van Generaal Pel aan Z. E. den Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië*, dated Atjeh, [[30 April]] [[1875]], and reproduced as *Bijlage IV* in the second volume of [[B. C. P. Kielstra|Kielstra]]'s *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog*. Van Heutsz's [[1893]] verdict on the letter is the highest praise he accords any of the eleven Governors' programmes:

> "Erkend moet worden, dat de brief van Generaal Pel aan Z. E. den Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië en gedagteekend Atjeh, 30 April 1875 (opgenomen als bijlage IV in het 2e deel van Kielstra's Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog) een uitmuntend gesteld stuk is, en onze toenmalige kennis van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden in aanmerking genomen, aan de Regeering werkelijk moest toeschijnen den weg aan te geven, die ons het snelst het einddoel van ons streven, de onderwerping van Atjeh, zou doen bereiken. Wij kunnen de lezing van dien brief aanbevelen".

The recommendation to read the letter in full — *wij kunnen de lezing van dien brief aanbevelen* — is Van Heutsz's only such reader-direction in the entire 1893 tract. The plan proposed the occupation of the entire coastal strip of the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]] from [[Kroeng Raja]] to [[Kroeng Raba]] and the closing of the five *kwala's* — the river-mouths — that gave the interior of Groot-Atjeh access to the sea, in order to sever the interior from its maritime supply. The same letter posed the question that became the central riddle of the next decade: "wat, na voltooiing van zijn plan, moest gedaan worden in ons politiek en handelsbelang" — what, after the completion of his plan, was to be done in our political and commercial interest?

### The abandonment of Van Swieten's concentrated-defence policy


Van Heutsz's narration of the abandonment is the structural pivot of the [[1893]] tract's diagnostic chapter:

> "Toen echter reeds dadelijk na van Swieten's vertrek militaire wapenfeiten tot uitbreiding van de ingenomen stelling, naar het heette noodzakelijk tot beveiliging van deze, 's bestuurs aandacht bijna geheel in beslag namen en daarna, in 1875, op voorstel van Generaal Pel, door den nieuw opgetreden landvoogd, Gouv. Gen. van Lansberge, de door van Swieten in overeenstemming met den Gouv. Gen. Loudon en den Minister van Koloniën Fransen van de Putte aangewezen weg geheel verlaten en besloten werd tot het op grootere schaal aanvallend optreden met de wapenen in de vallei van Groot-Atjeh, traden de militaire wapenfeiten geheel op den voorgrond en kwam het civiel beleid eerst in de tweede plaats in aanmerking".

The reversal had three components. First, the *geconcentreerde stelling Kotja-Radja* — Van Swieten's *kleine maar sterke positie* in the most weighty part of the valley of Groot-Atjeh — was abandoned in favour of the large-scale offensive. Second, the 2500-man concentrated garrison was replaced by a dispersed coastal occupation. Third, the *civiel beleid* that Van Swieten had placed "op den voorgrond" — at the forefront — was displaced to "de tweede plaats" (the second place), with the *militaire wapenfeiten* now taking precedence.

### The 38 posts in Groot-Atjeh by May 1875


By May [[1875]], the Pel Plan was already in operation, and the operational figures Van Heutsz cites mark the scale of the reversal from the Van Swieten programme:

> "Deze liet 2500 man achter, om daarmede te bezetten en actief te verdedigen de geconcentreerde stelling Kotja-Radja en enkele posten ter verzekering van de veilige gemeenschap naar zee; en nu waren in 't einde [Mei 1875] al acht en dertig posten opgericht met eene bezetting [van 2750 man]".

The May [[1875]] figure of 38 posts in Groot-Atjeh with a garrison of 2750 infantry alone is the benchmark Van Heutsz uses to mark the operational scale of the colony's commitment within months of the formal abandonment of the Van Swieten programme: a 2500-man concentrated defence had been replaced by a thirty-eight-post dispersed occupation, with no corresponding advance in the political objective — "zonder dat deze uitbreiding ons ten opzichte van de onderwerping -dus op staatkundig gebied- één schrede had vooruitgebracht" was Van Heutsz's verdict on the parallel expansion. The reversal was thus both an effect of the Pel Plan and a precondition of its acceptance: "Te meer is 's Regeerings toetreden tot 's Generaals voorstellen begrijpelijk, omdat op dat tijdstip Mei 1875 feitelijk reeds geheel was afgeweken van het programma van Swieten".

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1875** was the year in which officers of the man-of-war *Timor*, led thither by Atjehnese chiefs, discovered the [[Petroleum-bronnen van Perlak|petroleum-spring at Rantau Pandjang]] in the *landschap* of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula (Perlak)]] — *'in Mei 1875 eenige officieren van de Timor de petroleumbron'*. The yield of the spring had once been 60 *gantang*; in [[1875]] it was 40 *gantang* — about one *picol* — which at [[Rantau Pandjang|Rantau Pandjang]] brought nearly six dollars. The chief had a monthly income from it of two hundred dollars. The discovery is recorded by [[J. A. Kruyt|J. A. Kruyt]] in *[[Atjeh en de Atjehers (Kruyt)|Atjeh en de Atjehers]]* ([[1877]]). [[Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih|Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih]] of [[Peudawa Rajeu|Peudawa Rajeu]] received a large silver presentation-tray from the government in [[1875]].

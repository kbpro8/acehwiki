Figures, places, and events in Acehnese history associated with the year [[1875]].

**[[1875]]** was the year of the return of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] to Atjeh on [[8 November]] [[8 November]] [[1874]] (late 1874) and of his note of [[30 April]] [[30 April]] [[1875]] to the Governor-General that set out the program for the consolidation and expansion of the Dutch position; of the heavy losses from cholera and other diseases (957 dead and 5151 evacuated during the year); of the appointment of the health commission by Indies decree of [[12 April]] [[12 April]] [[1875]]; of the assumption of office by [[J. W. van Lansberge|Governor-General Van Lansberge]] on [[26 March]] [[26 March]]; and of the regulation of the [[heerendiensten|heerendiensten]] by the decree of [[1 October]] [[1 October]] [[1875]].

## January-March: Van Lansberge's Assumption of Office

On [[26 March]] [[26 March]] [[1875]] Mr. J. W. Van Lansberge assumed the office of Governor-General of Netherlands India, succeeding [[J. Loudon|Mr. J. Loudon]] whose honourable discharge had been granted by Royal Decree of [[17 December]] [[17 December]] [[1874]]. With January [[1875]] the vice-president of the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad of Nederlandsch-Indië]], [[O. van Rees|Mr. O. van Rees]], was relieved of the membership of the commission for the improvement of the Indian taxes, as the Opperbestuur in accordance with the advice of the [[Raad van State|Raad van State]] in [[November 1874|November 1874]] had decided that art. 36 of the [[Regeeringsreglement|Regeeringsreglement]] did not allow the vice-president to be charged with the special missions and commissions mentioned therein.

## April: Pel's Note of 30 April 1875

In his note of [[30 April]] [[30 April]] [[1875]] to the Governor-General, General Pel set out his analysis of the situation and his program for the future. The task assigned by [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]] had been fulfilled — the hoofd-stelling was secured, communication with the sea assured — but "all those victories have as yet given few political results." The war-party still had great influence; it was led by influential chiefs such as [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]], [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]], [[Toekoe Rajoet Lamkapang|Toekoe Rajoet Lamkapang]], and the family members of the former sultans. Pel's program was: consolidation of the actual position; continuation of hostilities as soon as the fighting forces permitted, with the object of separating the enemy entirely from the sea; introduction of direct administration in the occupied territory; and avoidance of bringing under direct administration those regions that might prove willing to submit.

## April: The Health Commission

By Indies decree of [[12 April]] [[12 April]] [[1875]] a commission was appointed to investigate locally the causes of the many and serious cases of illness that occurred in Atjeh and to indicate the means by which improvements might be brought about. To the proposals made in its report of [[1 July]] [[1 July]] partial effect was given, and thereby once again ample work was provided for the engineers. They had of course also to provide for the putting in order of the various posts and for the works at the sea-head on iron screw-piles at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]], which was completed in [[September 1876|September 1876]], and at the railway between Oleh-leh and [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], which was completed in [[November 1876|November 1876]].

## June: Pel's Departure on Leave

When Pel, who had been promoted major-general by extraordinary Royal Decree of [[18 February]] [[18 February]] [[1875]], departed on [[13 June]] [[13 June]] [1875] on leave to Java for the restoration of his health, he had attained the objective set him in [[April 1874|April 1874]]. "The stelling of Kota Radja was on all sides secured by an unbroken chain of fortifications; the communication with the sea was, both along the Atjeh-rivier and by the landing-place at Oleh-leh and the road laid out from there, fully assured, and the former sultan's territory, with that of Marassa, Longbattah and a great part of the III Moekims in the Sagi der XXV Moekims, was wholly conquered." Yet that objective had not been attained in the way Van Swieten had imagined; politics had been able to accomplish practically nothing; only the weapons had brought it so far.

## November: Pel's Return to Atjeh

On [[8 November]] [[8 November]] [1875] Pel returned to Atjeh, with the instructions of [[10 November]] [[10 November]] [[1875]] authorising him to act in the sense indicated in his note of [[30 April]] [[30 April]] [[1875]], and to subject successively the [[VI Moekims|VI]], [[IV Moekims|IV]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] and then the eastern territory as far as [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]]. During his absence the colonel of the infantry [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem]] had served as his temporary replacement.

## The Health Situation

During [[1875]] 957 dead and 5151 evacuated were counted. "That unfavourable situation must — besides from many secondary causes, an offshoot of the state of war — be attributed to the bad drinking water at most places of our position, to the extensive, now mostly fallow rice-fields, to the deep-cutting lagunes, to the considerable ground-moving, and to the felling of trees and bushes, to the insufficient shelter against the very variable climate. Through the great number of sick, the healthy had to perform a very exhausting service, and these consequently again the sooner took to the sickbed; cause and effect thus worked together to bring the sickness figures into serious proportions."

## Other Reforms

By the wet of [[6 April]] [[6 April]] [[1875]] (Stbl. n°. 61) a sum was made available for the construction of the lines Soerabaya—Pasoeroean—Malang. The regulation of the heerendiensten remained in essentials confirmed also by the decree of [[1 October]] [[1 October]] [[1875]]; but continually stronger became the pressure to make at least an end to the pantjensdiensten, the heerendiensten for the benefit of the native chiefs. Also continual progress was made on the way to a complete abolition of slavery in the buitenbezittingen.

## De Buitenbezittingen Account

**[[1875]]** was also the year in which the first railway line in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] was laid — a small track built on the orders of the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]] between the roadstead of [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and the principal Dutch military station at [[Kota Radja]]. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, this spoorbaantje ("little railway") was constructed "shortly after the beginning of the [[Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Atjeh-expedition]]" — that is, shortly after the outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] in [[1873]]. The line thus ranks among the earliest material installations of the Dutch occupation of Atjeh, predating by more than a decade the systematic construction of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] as a strategic network.

The little line from [[Oelèe Lheuë]] to [[Kota Radja]] was the seed from which the entire [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network grew. In [[1882]] the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|Department of Civil Public Works (B.O.W.)]] took over its management from the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]] and narrowed it into a tramway. In [[1885]] military necessity drove the construction of a *ceintuur-trambaan* — a belt-line — along the celebrated [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Kota Radja]], which was then connected by three radial lines to the original tramway to the roadstead. In [[1891]] the management of these lines reverted to the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]], reflecting the continuing dominance of military over economic considerations in the early history of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]].

The founding year of railway transport in Atjeh, [[1875]] marks the moment at which the Dutch occupation began to inscribe itself permanently into the physical landscape of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] coast. The little spoorbaantje between [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and [[Kota Radja]] is the prototype of what would, over the following four decades, become the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] — the most extensive military-economic railway project of the Dutch East Indies outside Java, and the principal instrument of Dutch power-projection across [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] and the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]].

## Account from *Snijders* (1944) — 1875

**[[1875]]** was, in the biographer's framing of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede Luitenant C. J. Snijders]]'s career, the year of his departure from [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and return to the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] at the end of his two-year detachment — closing the formative colonial chapter of his life and reopening the metropolitan career that would carry him, four decades later, to the [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|command of the Dutch armed forces]] during the [[First World War]].

Snijders' further movements in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] during the remainder of his detachment (until mid-[[1875]]) are not detailed in the available excerpt of his biography. He had arrived at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] on [[8 November]] [[8 November]] [[1874]] and received his baptism of fire at the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam operation]] of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [1874]; the Kota Alam redoubt he had helped to build was, in turn, one of the small field-engineering works — alongside the sea-head at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] and the railway between [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] and [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] — that the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Mineurs en Sappers]] of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] were maintaining through the difficult year of [[1875]], the year of the heavy losses from cholera and other diseases (957 dead and 5151 evacuated) and of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]]'s note of [[30 April]] [[30 April]] [1875] that set out the program for the consolidation and expansion of the Dutch position.

Snijders returned to the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] in [[1875]] and resumed his career in the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Genie]], eventually serving as [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chef van de Generale Staf]] ([[1910]]–[[1918]]) and [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht]] ([[August 1914|August]] [[1914]]–[[November 1918|November]] [[1918]]).

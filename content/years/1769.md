The year **1769** is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the year in which the East Coast statelets — [[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]] and the smaller polities — "knew themselves again to be free" of the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Atjehnese overlordship]] that had been imposed by [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Sultan Iskander Moeda's Oostkust-veldtocht]] in the early seventeenth century. The recovery of independence in [[1769]] was, however, short-lived: within a few years the statelets were forced, "door het wapengeweld van Ismail en Said Ali" — "by the armed force of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]" — to acknowledge [[Siak|Siak]] rather than [[Acheh|Atjeh]] as their overlord.

## Events

- Recovery of independence by the East Coast statelets from the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Atjehnese overlordship]]. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau records: "In [[1769]] wisten zij zich echter weder van deze overheersching los te maken" — "In 1769, however, they knew themselves again to be free of this overlordship."

## Significance

The year [[1769]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the chronological watershed between the long [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Atjehnese overlordship of the East Coast]] and the brief period of East Coast independence that would, within a few years, be terminated by the imposition of the Siak overlordship. The year [[1769]] thus closes the long Atjehnese century on the East Coast — the century that had begun with [[sultan-iskander-moeda's-oostkust-veldtocht|Sultan Iskander Moeda's campaign]] — and opens the short interregnum that would, in turn, be succeeded by the Siak overlordship of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]].

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau does not record the immediate occasion of the [[1769]] recovery of independence. Its placement of the event in the long narrative of the Atjehnese-Siak succession on the East Coast suggests, however, that the recovery was a consequence of the broader decay of [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] power in the late eighteenth century — the same decay that would, in the early nineteenth century, produce the [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship]] as a deliberate act of revanchism rather than a continuation of an unbroken tradition.

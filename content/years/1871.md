## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1871** was the year in which the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] between the [[Netherlands]] and [[Great Britain|England]] was signed, the diplomatic prelude to the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] two years later. By placing the whole of [[Sumatra]] — including the independent [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate — within the Dutch sphere of influence, the treaty removed the principal external constraint that had hitherto preserved Atjehnese sovereignty, and thereby set the stage for the Dutch declaration of war of [[1873]].

### The Sumatra-tractaat

The treaty, formally an Anglo-Dutch arrangement consolidating earlier understandings, provided that the whole of [[Sumatra]] would fall within the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] sphere of influence. In exchange, the [[Netherlands]] conceded to British subjects the same commercial rights as those enjoyed by Dutch subjects throughout Sumatra. Minister [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]] delivered "zeer geruststellende verklaringen" — very reassuring statements — in the [[Tweede Kamer]] about the consequences of the instrument he had just signed.

### Parliamentary reception

The reassurance proved short-lived. As [[W. J. van Welderen Rengers|Van Welderen Rengers]] (lib.) observed in the [[Tweede Kamer]] more than two decades later, in colonial affairs "het allerminst het spreekwoord [geldig]: *c'est le premier pas qui coûte*" — it is least of all true that the first step costs the most. The first step, the signature of the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] in [[1871]], had been taken easily; it was the subsequent steps, dictated by prestige, that cost money and lives. Some chambermembers at the time had indeed feared "moeilijkheden met Atjeh" — difficulties with Atjeh — but had been quieted by Van Bosse's reassurances.

### Significance

[[1871]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of that year is the diplomatic hinge on which the entire tragedy of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] turns. By recognising Atjeh as falling within the Dutch sphere, the treaty simultaneously supplied the Dutch government with the international warrant for subjugation and removed the principal external counterweight — British interest — that had previously deterred action against the sultanate.

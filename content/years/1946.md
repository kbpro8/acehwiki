**[[1946]]** is the year of [[C. de Groot|De Groot]]'s report on the gold-and-platinum placers of the [[Basin of Meulaboh|Meulaboh area]] on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West coast of Atjeh]] — the second of the two printed colonial sources on the [[Gold and silver in Atjeh|Atjeh gold placers]] (the first being the [[1922]] survey of [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]], [[B. B. Lindberg|Lindberg]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]]), and the source of the only documented colonial-era observation of mercury minerals (native mercury and cinnabar) on [[Acheh|Atjeh]] territory. The [[1946]] report is also the principal source for the [[M.A.E.M.]] dredging operation's [[1941]] mercury gain — the documentary evidence that the West-Atjeh alluvial deposits contain native mercury and cinnabar in addition to gold and platinum.

## The De Groot 1946 report

For the full account of the [[1946]] De Groot report see the entries on [[C. de Groot]], [[Gold and silver in Atjeh]], and [[Mercury in Atjeh]]. In summary: De Groot's principal contribution is the favourable opinion that "the placers of the [[Meulaboh]] areas offer good prospects for the winning of gold and platinum" — a judgment that post-dates, and partly reverses, the more cautious tone of the [[1922]] survey. De Groot's report also documents the [[M.A.E.M.]] dredging operation on the [[Geudong Concession]] ([[1940]]–[[1941]]) and the observation that the M.A.E.M. dredges were *gaining* mercury with each clean-up:

> "DE GROOT (1946, p. 188) reports native mercury and fine grains of cinnabar to occur in the gold-bearing alluvial deposits on the West coast of Atjeh. In order to recover the finer gold during the exploitation by the M.A.E.M. in 1941 (see the subchapter on gold) 400 kg of mercury were used on the tables per bucket dredge and per clean-up. It appeared that after every clean-up the quantity of mercury had increased. Indeed, drilling samples occasionally sand contained native mercury and in the black sand very fine grains of cinnabar have been observed."

## The post-war context

The [[1946]] De Groot report was compiled in the immediate aftermath of the [[1942]]–[[1945]] Japanese occupation of the Netherlands East Indies, and during the early stages of the Indonesian National Revolution. The report accordingly belongs to the post-war reconstitution of the colonial mining service's archives — the same reconstitution that produced [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) as a Special Edition of the [[Bureau of Mines in Indonesia]]. The [[1946]] report's favourable opinion of the Meulaboh placers is, in this context, a post-war assessment that draws on the pre-war M.A.E.M. dredging operation ([[1940]]–[[1941]]) and on De Groot's own field observations, but that was compiled in a political and military context very different from that of the pre-war colonial mining service.

## Significance

[[1946]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of the De Groot report — the second of the two printed colonial sources on the Atjeh gold placers, and the source of the only documented colonial-era observation of mercury minerals on Atjeh territory. The De Groot report's favourable opinion of the Meulaboh placers — expressed after the M.A.E.M. had begun dredge-mining in [[1940]] — provides the historical link between the colonial mineralogical survey of Atjeh and the actual gold production of the residency on the eve of the Japanese occupation. The [[1946]] report's post-war context — the immediate aftermath of the Japanese occupation and the early stages of the Indonesian National Revolution — also gives it an additional historical significance as one of the last printed contributions of the colonial mining service before the transfer of sovereignty to the Indonesian Republic.

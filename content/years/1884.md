**[[1884]]** was the year in which [[Zijlker]] secured the first petroleum concession in the *Landschap* ("self-governing territory") of [[Langkat]] on the Sumatra East Coast, immediately adjacent to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — the concession on which the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Royal Petroleum Company]] was founded in [[1890]] and on which the modern Indonesian petroleum industry was built. The same year also figures in the [[Aceh War]] chronology as one of the middle years of the conflict, when the Dutch were extending their control over the inland areas and the [[Derde Bataljon (Atjeh-Bataljon)|Derde Bataljon]] and other colonial formations were engaged in the systematic reduction of Acehnese strongholds.

## Zijlker's Langkat petroleum concession

According to Ir. [[F. P. C. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]]'s chapter *Mijnbouw* ("Mining") in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]): "The first to drill oil in exploitable quantity was Zijlker, and indeed in a concession in the *Landschap* Langkat in the year 1884. But it was not until 1890 that the *Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij* founded on this came into being." The choice of Langkat — a self-governing sultanate on the Sumatra East Coast immediately adjacent to Atjeh — placed the head of the modern Indonesian petroleum industry in the same territorial complex that had, since the seventeenth century, supplied pepper (and later tobacco) to the world market.

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the [[1884]] date is significant for the geographical and strategic proximity of the Langkat oilfield to Atjeh. The concession was secured eleven years after the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) Kreemer's account|first Aceh expedition]] of [[1873]] and three years after the [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|establishment of civil government in Atjeh]] in [[1881]]; the late-nineteenth-century development of the Langkat oilfield thus coincided with the Dutch military conquest of Atjeh, and the two processes — the petroleum development of the East Coast and the military subjugation of Atjeh — together reshaped the strategic significance of northern Sumatra in the late-colonial Indies.

The same chapter of Van der Ploeg further records that the Javanese toponym *Pelantoengan* — from *lantoeng*, "earth-oil" (petroleum) — preserves the indigenous knowledge of petroleum that long predated Zijlker's [[1884]] concession, and that [[J. H. van Linschoten|Jan Huygen van Linschoten]] had brought back from Goa a sample of petroleum "from a spring on Sumatra, that flows pure balm". The early Dutch reports of Sumatran petroleum seeps — including the story of an oil spring at [[Deli]] "which burned on the sea and with which two Portuguese galleons were set alight" — are the European counterpart to the indigenous *Pelantoengan* toponymy.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1884

**[[1884]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] as one of the years of the middle phase of the war, when the Dutch were extending their control over the inland areas — "[[1884, after being the first to force his way into the position at Tenom" ("1884, after being the first to penetrate the position at Tenom") — and when the [[Derde Bataljon (Atjeh-Bataljon)|Derde Bataljon]] and other colonial formations were engaged in the systematic reduction of Acehnese strongholds.

## Significance

The year [[1884]] stands at the intersection of two of the most consequential developments in late-colonial northern Sumatra: the Dutch military conquest of Atjeh (begun in [[1873]] and continuing into the 1880s–1890s) and the corporate-bureaucratic foundation of the modern Indonesian petroleum industry in the immediately adjacent Langkat sultanate. The two developments were not independent: the same colonial apparatus that produced the Atjeh War also produced the legal-bureaucratic framework (the *zelfbesturen* contract system, the *erfpacht* long-lease) within which Zijlker's [[1884]] Langkat concession was secured.

## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].

**1884** is the year in which the Dutch forces in Atjeh abandoned the attempt to conquer the interior of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and fell back upon the *concentratie-stelling* — the "concentrated position" — around [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja (Kuta Raja)]]. The [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie-stelsel]] thus begun would last, with growing parliamentary criticism, until [[1896]], when [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|the defection of Toekoe Oemar]] forced its abandonment.

### The retreat to Koeta Radja

After more than a decade of inconclusive fighting in the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]], the Dutch expeditionary force in [[1884]] withdrew into a perimeter centred on [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] and limited its activity to holding this reconcentrated line. [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]], later minister of Colonies, would defend the measure retrospectively in the [[Tweede Kamer]]: in Atjeh, he argued, "geduld geoefend [is] in de tijd van de concentratie" — patience was exercised in the time of the concentration — and the result was that "het verzet zich des te sterker heeft kunnen organiseren" — the resistance had been able to organise itself all the stronger.

### Consequences

The retreat of [[1884]] became a parliamentary byword for the futility of half-measures in Atjeh. Critics in the [[Tweede Kamer]] — especially [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]], [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] and [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] — repeatedly used the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie]] period to argue that the [[Netherlands]] should either subjugate Atjeh outright or withdraw entirely. The rebuttal by [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] in [[1904]] that the *concentratie* had merely permitted the resistance to regroup was the standard ministerial reply.

### Significance

[[1884]] marks the beginning of a twelve-year parenthesis in the Dutch Atjehnese war during which [[Groot-Atjeh]] outside [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] was effectively abandoned to the Atjehnese. The collapse of the *concentratie-stelsel* in [[1896]] set in motion the chain of events — the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] system, the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|Van Daalen expedition]] — that would, by [[1904]], bring the entire sultanate under Dutch control.

## Account from *De Atjeh-Oorlog* by J.A. Kruijt (1896)

**1884**, in [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]]'s *De Atjeh-Oorlog* ([[1896]]), was the year of the decision in the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]] that transformed the Dutch strategy in Atjeh — the year of the [[Nisero affair (November 1883 – September 1884)|Nisero affair]]'s resolution, the visits of [[s Jacob|'s Jacob]] and [[Van Rees|Van Rees]] to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], the secret sessions of [[16 June]] – [[17 June]] [[1884]] that decreed the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]], and the inauguration of the *[[Civiel en Militair Gouvernur|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]]* under Colonel [[Demmeni|Demmeni]].

Only in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] was the release of the crew of the *[[Nisero|Nisero]]* — wrecked near [[Tenom|Tenom]] in [[November 1883|November]] [[1883]] and held captive by the *radja* of Tenom — obtained, on payment of a ransom of ƒ100,000, while the Dutch bound themselves no longer to close the harbour of Tenom. The *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[s Jacob|'s Jacob]] had already visited Atjeh in [[August 1883|August]] [[1883]] and found the situation highly unfavourable; the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[Van Rees|Van Rees]] came in [[March 1884|March]] [[1884]] to see and discuss the state of affairs.

In two secret sessions of the united Chambers of the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]] on [[16 June]] and [[17 June]] [[1884]], it was decided to adopt a wholly new system for Atjeh: the so-called concentration or contraction of the Dutch *stelling* in Groot-Atjeh, the abolition of the coastal posts, the withdrawal of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]], and blockade. [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], strongly opposed to this concentration — which according to him "*de kroon zette op de Nederlandsche fouten in Atjeh*" (set the crown on the Dutch mistakes in Atjeh) — retired in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]]. Colonel [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] accordingly took office as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]], on what Kruijt would call "de puinhoopen van het gesloopte gebouw onzer macht onder van der Heijden" (the ruins of the demolished building of our power under Van der Heijden). In [[October 1884|October]] [[1884]] the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] was wholly withdrawn by order of the High Government in the Netherlands — except on the [[Oostkust|East Coast]], where it had worked for ten years, and where it was maintained in order not to lose all influence there as well.

*De Atjeh-Oorlog: Kort Overzicht van Oorsprong, Verloop en Gevolg*, door J. A. Kruijt. 's-Gravenhage: Loman & Funke, 1896. Chapter VIII, pp. 44–46.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the *groote politieke fout*

[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* identifies [[1884]] as the year of the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] — the *besluit van 19 Augustus 1884* by which [[Van Rees|Gouverneur-Generaal van Rees]] ordered the withdrawal of all outlying Dutch posts to the *geconcentreerde stelling* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and promulgated it by the *proclamatie* of the same date to the Atjehnese — and of the [[Staatsblad No. 105 van 1884|Staatsblad No. 105 of 1884]] that prescribed the *rigoureuse blokkade* of the entire coast from [[Tamiang]] to [[Troemon]]. The year is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the principal *bête noire* of the entire Atjeh record: the *groote politieke fout* that reversed the [[Van der Heijden]] conquest of Groot-Atjeh and condemned the colony to another fifteen years of war.

### The February 1884 resignation of Laging Tobias

The year opened with the February [[1884]] resignation of [[Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]] — "toen de Regeering het eenige middel verwierp waarvan hij in de bestaande omstandigheden een goede uitkomst verwachtte":

> "Meende de Gouverneur Laging Tobias zijn ontslag te moeten vragen, toen de Regeering het eenige middel verwierp waarvan hij in de bestaande omstandigheden een goede uitkomst verwachtte. Dit was in Februari 1884".

The new G.-G. Van Rees, who visited Atjeh in March [[1884]], "betuigde, dat hij de inzichten van den Gouverneur Laging Tobias omtrent den met Atjeh te volgen weg vrij juist vond" — declared that he found Governor Laging Tobias's views on the path to be followed with Atjeh quite correct — but the declaration was overridden by the *Concentratiebesluit* of [[19 August]] [[1884]].

### The 16–17 June 1884 secret Staten-Generaal sessions

The Concentratie was authorised, in the first instance, by the secret sessions of the [[Staten-Generaal]] of [[16 June|16]] and [[17 June]] [[1884]] in The Hague:

> "Dat de hangende plannen de kort daarop ingevoerde Concentratie zouden wezen, waartoe besloten was in de geheime zittingen van de Staten-Generaal van 16 en 17 Juni 1884".

The decision was taken at the metropolitan level — *opperbestuur in Nederland* — without consultation of the colonial Atjeh authorities, "*zonder dat aan de autoriteiten te Atjeh om eenig nader advies was gevraagd*".

### The 19 August 1884 Concentratiebesluit and Staatsblad No. 105 van 1884

The Concentratie was promulgated in the colony by Van Rees by *proclamatie* of [[19 August]] [[1884]]:

> "in weerwil van de op 19 Augustus door den Gouverneur-Generaal van Rees uitgevaardigde proclamatie, waarin hij aan de Atjehers deed verkondigen dat zij er nooit in zouden slagen ons een enkelen stap terugtedringen".

The Atjehnese "waren zoo vrij te gelooven, dat ons terugtrekken in de geconcentreerde stelling niet vrijwillig, maar gedwongen was en voortsproot uit zwakte, uit onmacht om den strijd langer vol te houden". The legal instrument of the rigorous-blockade component of the Concentratie was the [[Staatsblad No. 105 van 1884|Staatsblad No. 105 of 1884]] — which prescribed the *rigoureuse sluiting van het geheele kustgebied*, "d. w. z. van Tamiang op de Oostkust tot Troemon op de Westkust". Van Heutsz's retrospective on the choice of blokkade over *scheepvaartregeling* is severe:

> "Dit alles pleitte dus in 1884 tegen de blokkade en voor de scheepvaartregeling, maar toch koos de Regeering blokkade. Dit was dus eene fout, maar een ramp werd het, toen dezelfbe Regeering, die de rigoureuse blokkade decreteerde, geen consequente doorvoering van dit in ieder geval van kracht getuigende beginsel eischte".

### The 13 October 1884 hybrid besluit by Demmeni and Scherer's resignation

The *rigoureuse blokkade* was, however, never applied in its rigorous form. [[Demmeni|Generaal Demmeni]] *wankelde* in his plan to apply the prescribed blockade under the pressure of [[Scherer]]'s *betoogingen* and the *nota's* co-signed by [[Van Langen|assistent-resident van Groot-Atjeh van Langen]]:

> "De Generaal Demmeni werd door Scherer's betoogingen en zijne nota's, waarmede de assistent-resident van Groot-Atjeh van Langen instemde, aan het wankelen gebracht in zijn plan om eenvoudig uit te voeren, wat hem door de Regeering opgedragen was en voerde niet de algemeene rigoureuse blokkade in, maar liet slechts sommige gedeelten van de kust sluiten en blokkeeren en dit niet eens consequent, terwijl hij voor andere gedeelten, reeds bij besluit van 13 October 1884 eene soort scheepvaartregeling invoerde".

The *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]] established [[Edi]], [[Segli]] and [[Olehleh|Oleh-leh]] as the only call-ports for the entire Noord- en Oostkust — a *soort scheepvaartregeling* in form but, Van Heutsz argues, "*deze had in mindere mate dezelfde gebreken had van de alleréerste onder Generaal van der Heijden*". The hybrid was thus, in Van Heutsz's reading, "*een tweeslachtig ding*" — a two-headed thing. The Concentratie was also the immediate occasion of the resignation of [[Scherer]] in early October [[1884]]:

> "toen hij bemerkte, dat de Generaal Demmeni, hoe ook diens denkbeelden omtrent de Atjeh-zaak waren, eenvoudig het hem voorgeschreven stelsel in practijk zou brengen, besloot om heen te gaan; hij vroeg toen intrekking zijner plaatsing te Atjeh en vertrok in het begin van October, na eerst nog in een paar met redenen omkleede nota's, den kolonel Demmeni ernstig tegen het opheffen der scheepvaartregeling en van de posten op de kust gewaarschuwd te hebben".

The resignation took with it, in Van Heutsz's reading, the only man who could have advised Demmeni how to construct the *scheepvaartregeling* he had begun to introduce: "de man, die den Generaal het beste had kunnen voorlichten omtrent de wijze, waarop die scheepvaartregeling diende ingericht te zijn en waarom eene andere regeling niet deugde, was heengegaan". The counter-factual is explicit: "Ware hij gebleven en had hij gestreden met zijne argumenten tegen de blokkade en voor de scheepvaartregeling, zeker zou dan geene contrôle op handel en scheepvaart getroffen zijn, zooals thans het geval is geweest, vermoedelijk ook geen tweeslachtige regeling".

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1884** was the year of the Dutch *[[Concentratie (1884)|concentratie]]* — *the Concentration* — by which the Netherlands forces restricted themselves to a ring round [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and a *ceintuurbaan* (girdle-road) connecting the sixteen posts with the recently shortened railway, while three *straalbanen* (ray-roads) connected the chief station at Kota Radja with the *ceintuurbaan*. For a telephone in that post-ring provision was likewise made. The *concentratie* remained the Dutch military policy in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] until [[1896]], when other insights prevailed and the line of concentration was given up.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

By **[[1884]]** the costs of the [[Atjeh-oorlog]] had risen to 150 million guilders, while the deficit in the Indies treasury had risen to eighty-six million.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**1884** was the year in which the Netherlands withdrew behind the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]* in [[Groot-Atjeh]], abandoning [[Anagaloeng]] and [[Toengkoeb]] and inaugurating the ten-year *concentratie* period.

## The withdrawal

[[G. B. Hooijer]] recorded that the *benteng* the Dutch had kept at [[Anagaloeng]] after the fighting was decided *was in 1884 verlaten, daar de onverzoenlijke en slechts voor macht bukkende Atjehers opnieuw de wapenen hadden kunnen opnemen en ons noodzaakten achter de geconcentreerde linie te wijken*. At [[Toengkoeb]], the Dutch soldiers who returned in [[1893]] sat again *op de plaats, die in 1884 door hen verlaten was, nadat zij er vier jaren lang heer en meester waren geweest*.

## The death of Teungkoe di Tiro

The Achehnese spiritual leader in the holy war, [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]], died at [[Anagaloeng]] in this period, lying on the gravestone erected there for [[Kapitein Miseroy]] during the Dutch occupation.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

The year of decision — and of secrecy. The [[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Nisero question]], which brought the true state of Atjeh so clearly to light, was the nearest occasion that the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration]] came to execution: in the geheime Kamerzittingen (secret sessions) of [[16 June|16]] and [[17 June]] [[1884]] the retreat was decided upon, with the nation kept, as before, deliberately in the dark; Verstege wrote in January [[1886]] that even the principal advisers of this sad war operation must remain unnamed, while rumour credited the then Raad van Indië, later again Minister of Colonies [[Sprenger van Eijk|Sprenger van Eijk]], and governor [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] and kolonel [[Demmeni]] with advice against it. On [[19 August]] kolonel Demmeni entered office as civil *and* military governor after Laging Tobias's proposal of reunification; the same day the Governor-General [[O. van Rees|van Rees]] addressed the proclamation to the Indies princes and the population of the dependencies of Atjeh that the foolish attackers would never succeed "in driving us back a single step" — while the concentration was already in preparation. The year saw also: on [[16 June]] the vote of 1⅔ million guilders for the [[Blockade of the Atjeh coast (1884-1885)|blockade of the Atjeh coast]], with the London conditions of July (Tenom's harbours open; re-opening of Atjeh's havens afterwards) and the ragtag fleet quoted by Verstege; the Lambaroe-Kota Radja steam tram working since mid-1884 (see [[Atjeh-tram]]); on [[3 July]] the treason of [[Toekoe Oemar (Tenom)|Toekoe Oemar]] at [[Lambesi]]; on [[29 July]] the [[Ambush at Tjot-Goe (29 July 1884)|ambush]] of [[Imam Habib (Tjot-Goe)|Imam Habib]] near [[Tjot-Goe]]; in August the declaration of age of the pretender [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]] at [[Kemala]]; the occupation of [[Poeloe Waai|Poeloe Waai (Pulo Weh)]]; the occupation of the new posts of [[Kota Pohama]] and [[Roempit]] on 25 and 29 September — "reeds vóór het voltooien der nieuwe postenketen een aanvang werd gemaakt met de ontruiming der daarbuiten gelegen versterkingen" ("already before the completion of the new chain of posts a start was made with the evacuation of the fortifications outside it") — and the return to Atjeh of formerly exiled chiefs, foremost of them [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]] (exiled to Banda in [[1879]]), who in November issued a day order to the Atjehers not to trouble the Dutch soldiers while abandoning the outer posts. On [[19 December]] the member [[Rutgers van Rozenburg]] asked who the military specialists were — "the Dutch Moltke's" — whom the Minister had consulted about the concentration plan, and got only an evasive answer.

## The Geconcentreerde Stelling: Implementation (1884)

Following the [[Concentration Decision of August 1884|concentration decree of 19 August 1884]], implementation began immediately. [[Colonel H. Demmeni]] was appointed Civiele en Militaire Gouverneur (Civil and Military Governor). Key events in the concentration implementation:

- **25 September 1884:** Garrison of [[Samalanga]] (North Coast) transferred to [[Kota-Pohama]]; construction of Kota-Pohama post begun
- **25 October 1884:** Capture of kampongs Lampoe-Tjot-Iri, Lamtjot, and Tjot-Iri for new post construction
- **6 November 1884:** Withdrawal from bivouac at Tjot-Iri; permanent garrison established at [[Tjot-Iri]]
- **10 November 1884:** Evacuation of [[Telok-Semawé]] (North Coast); materials reused for new [[Lamtih]] post
- **14 November 1884:** Establishment of [[Siroen]] post
- **20 November 1884:** Establishment of [[Lamtih]] post
- **22 November 1884:** Establishment of [[Boekit-Karang]] post
- **December 1884:** [[Roempit]] post established

Construction was rapid and organized, with materials from evacuated northern posts reused for the new southern line. The new geconcentreerde stelling would be completed by [[1 March]] [[1885]].

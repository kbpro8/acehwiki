**[[1884]]** was the year in which [[Zijlker]] secured the first petroleum concession in the *Landschap* ("self-governing territory") of [[Langkat]] on the Sumatra East Coast, immediately adjacent to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — the concession on which the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Royal Petroleum Company]] was founded in [[1890]] and on which the modern Indonesian petroleum industry was built. The same year also figures in the [[Aceh War]] chronology as one of the middle years of the conflict, when the Dutch were extending their control over the inland areas and the [[Derde Bataljon (Atjeh-Bataljon)|Derde Bataljon]] and other colonial formations were engaged in the systematic reduction of Acehnese strongholds.

## Zijlker's Langkat petroleum concession

According to Ir. [[F. P. C. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]]'s chapter *Mijnbouw* ("Mining") in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]): "The first to drill oil in exploitable quantity was Zijlker, and indeed in a concession in the *Landschap* Langkat in the year 1884. But it was not until 1890 that the *Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij* founded on this came into being." The choice of Langkat — a self-governing sultanate on the Sumatra East Coast immediately adjacent to Atjeh — placed the head of the modern Indonesian petroleum industry in the same territorial complex that had, since the seventeenth century, supplied pepper (and later tobacco) to the world market.

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the [[1884]] date is significant for the geographical and strategic proximity of the Langkat oilfield to Atjeh. The concession was secured eleven years after the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) Kreemer's account|first Aceh expedition]] of [[1873]] and three years after the [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|establishment of civil government in Atjeh]] in [[1881]]; the late-nineteenth-century development of the Langkat oilfield thus coincided with the Dutch military conquest of Atjeh, and the two processes — the petroleum development of the East Coast and the military subjugation of Atjeh — together reshaped the strategic significance of northern Sumatra in the late-colonial Indies.

The same chapter of Van der Ploeg further records that the Javanese toponym *Pelantoengan* — from *lantoeng*, "earth-oil" (petroleum) — preserves the indigenous knowledge of petroleum that long predated Zijlker's [[1884]] concession, and that [[J. H. van Linschoten|Jan Huygen van Linschoten]] had brought back from Goa a sample of petroleum "from a spring on Sumatra, that flows pure balm". The early Dutch reports of Sumatran petroleum seeps — including the story of an oil spring at [[Deli]] "which burned on the sea and with which two Portuguese galleons were set alight" — are the European counterpart to the indigenous *Pelantoengan* toponymy.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1884

**[[1884]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] as one of the years of the middle phase of the war, when the Dutch were extending their control over the inland areas — "[[1884]], na als eerste de stelling in Tenom te zijn binnengedrongen" ("1884, after being the first to penetrate the position at Tenom") — and when the [[Derde Bataljon (Atjeh-Bataljon)|Derde Bataljon]] and other colonial formations were engaged in the systematic reduction of Acehnese strongholds.

## Significance

The year [[1884]] stands at the intersection of two of the most consequential developments in late-colonial northern Sumatra: the Dutch military conquest of Atjeh (begun in [[1873]] and continuing into the 1880s–1890s) and the corporate-bureaucratic foundation of the modern Indonesian petroleum industry in the immediately adjacent Langkat sultanate. The two developments were not independent: the same colonial apparatus that produced the Atjeh War also produced the legal-bureaucratic framework (the *zelfbesturen* contract system, the *erfpacht* long-lease) within which Zijlker's [[1884]] Langkat concession was secured.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1884** is the year in which the Dutch forces in Atjeh abandoned the attempt to conquer the interior of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and fell back upon the *concentratie-stelling* — the "concentrated position" — around [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja (Kuta Raja)]]. The [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie-stelsel]] thus begun would last, with growing parliamentary criticism, until [[1896]], when [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|the defection of Toekoe Oemar]] forced its abandonment.

### The retreat to Koeta Radja

After more than a decade of inconclusive fighting in the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]], the Dutch expeditionary force in [[1884]] withdrew into a perimeter centred on [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] and limited its activity to holding this reconcentrated line. [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]], later minister of Colonies, would defend the measure retrospectively in the [[Tweede Kamer]]: in Atjeh, he argued, "geduld geoefend [is] in de tijd van de concentratie" — patience was exercised in the time of the concentration — and the result was that "het verzet zich des te sterker heeft kunnen organiseren" — the resistance had been able to organise itself all the stronger.

### Consequences

The retreat of [[1884]] became a parliamentary byword for the futility of half-measures in Atjeh. Critics in the [[Tweede Kamer]] — especially [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]], [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] and [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] — repeatedly used the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie]] period to argue that the [[Netherlands]] should either subjugate Atjeh outright or withdraw entirely. The rebuttal by [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] in [[1904]] that the *concentratie* had merely permitted the resistance to regroup was the standard ministerial reply.

### Significance

[[1884]] marks the beginning of a twelve-year parenthesis in the Dutch Atjehnese war during which [[Groot-Atjeh]] outside [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] was effectively abandoned to the Atjehnese. The collapse of the *concentratie-stelsel* in [[1896]] set in motion the chain of events — the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] system, the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|Van Daalen expedition]] — that would, by [[1904]], bring the entire sultanate under Dutch control.

## Account from *De Atjeh-Oorlog* by J.A. Kruijt (1896)

**1884**, in [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]]'s *De Atjeh-Oorlog* ([[1896]]), was the year of the decision in the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]] that transformed the Dutch strategy in Atjeh — the year of the [[Nisero affair (November 1883 – September 1884)|Nisero affair]]'s resolution, the visits of [[s Jacob|'s Jacob]] and [[Van Rees|Van Rees]] to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], the secret sessions of [[16 June]] – [[17 June]] [[1884]] that decreed the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]], and the inauguration of the *[[Civiel en Militair Gouvernur|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]]* under Colonel [[Demmeni|Demmeni]].

Only in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] was the release of the crew of the *[[Nisero|Nisero]]* — wrecked near [[Tenom|Tenom]] in [[November 1883|November]] [[1883]] and held captive by the *radja* of Tenom — obtained, on payment of a ransom of ƒ100,000, while the Dutch bound themselves no longer to close the harbour of Tenom. The *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[s Jacob|'s Jacob]] had already visited Atjeh in [[August 1883|August]] [[1883]] and found the situation highly unfavourable; the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[Van Rees|Van Rees]] came in [[March 1884|March]] [[1884]] to see and discuss the state of affairs.

In two secret sessions of the united Chambers of the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]] on [[16 June]] and [[17 June]] [[1884]], it was decided to adopt a wholly new system for Atjeh: the so-called concentration or contraction of the Dutch *stelling* in Groot-Atjeh, the abolition of the coastal posts, the withdrawal of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]], and blockade. [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], strongly opposed to this concentration — which according to him "*de kroon zette op de Nederlandsche fouten in Atjeh*" (set the crown on the Dutch mistakes in Atjeh) — retired in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]]. Colonel [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] accordingly took office as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]], on what Kruijt would call "de puinhoopen van het gesloopte gebouw onzer macht onder van der Heijden" (the ruins of the demolished building of our power under Van der Heijden). In [[October 1884|October]] [[1884]] the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] was wholly withdrawn by order of the High Government in the Netherlands — except on the [[Oostkust|East Coast]], where it had worked for ten years, and where it was maintained in order not to lose all influence there as well.

*De Atjeh-Oorlog: Kort Overzicht van Oorsprong, Verloop en Gevolg*, door J. A. Kruijt. 's-Gravenhage: Loman & Funke, 1896. Chapter VIII, pp. 44–46.

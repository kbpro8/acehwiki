**1877** was the year of the [[Van Lansberge's visit to Aceh (March-April 1877)|visit of the Governor-General Van Lansberge]] to Atjeh in March-April, which inaugurated a new phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]; of the closure of the coast from [[Kwala Gighen|Kwala Gighen]] to [[Kwala Loë|Kwala Loë]] in January; of the [[Expedition to Samalanga (August 1877)|expedition to Samalanga]] of August, in which Colonel [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] was struck in the left eye; of expeditions to [[Loöng|Loöng]] in April, [[Malaboeh|Malaboeh]] in May, [[Langsar|Langsar]] in May, and [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]] in July; and of the appointment of [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] as governor of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden on [[15 January]] [[15 January]] [[1878]] (decided in principle this year).

## January: The Closure of the Coast

In [[January 1877|January 1877]] the troops resumed operations for the completion of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]]'s program: the occupation of the coast from [[Kwala Gighen|Kwala Gighen]] to [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]], by which the enemy still maintained communication with the sea. On [[25 January]] [[25 January]] [1877] the operations began under the commander-in-chief's personal direction with a simultaneous landing at [[Kwala Loë|Kwala Loë]] and [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]]. On [[30 January]] [[30 January]] [1877] the [[missigit Lambada|missigit Lambada]], from which the troops had been repulsed in October [[1876]], was stormed.

## March-April: Van Lansberge's Visit

On [[14 March]] [[14 March]] [1877] the Governor-General [[J. W. van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] arrived at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] to inform himself personally of the situation and to decide what should next be done. The war had lasted four years, costing as in [[1876]] and [[1877]] monthly about one and a half million guilders. On [[25 March]] [[25 March]] [1877] the Governor-General held a solemn meeting with the chiefs of the [[XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] and those of the submitted coast-states, invoking their cooperation and promising the rebuilding of the great missigit at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. The visit inaugurated a new phase of the war: the abandonment of offensive operations, the reorganisation of the inland administration of the XXV Moekims, and a policy of conciliation.

## April-May: The Expeditions to the West and East Coasts

In [[April 1877|April 1877]] an expedition was sent to the landscape [[Loöng|Loöng]] on the west coast, where many of the Dutch army's opponents were staying, and which had openly committed piracy against a trading vessel under British flag. The submission of Loöng and a few neighbouring landscapes followed. In the same month [[April 1877|April]] Dutch ships arrived at [[Malaboeh|Malaboeh]] to set up a fortified post. In [[May 1877|May]] the whole East Coast was brought under Dutch authority by an expedition to [[Langsar|Langsar]], whose population had been troubling the friendly [[Tamiang|Tamiang]].

## July: The Simpang Olim Expedition

In [[July 1877|July 1877]] a new expedition was necessary to [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]], whose banished radja had nearly succeeded in an attempt on the Dutch post at Blang-Ni. The expedition reached on [[9 July]] [[9 July]] [1877] after a very difficult march its object and made itself master of [[Tepin Sirin|Tepin Sirin]], the enemy's headquarters.

## August: The Samalanga Expedition

The most important of the expeditions against the coast-states in the summer of [[1877]] was the [[Expedition to Samalanga (August 1877)|expedition to Samalanga]] in August, led personally by Colonel [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]], then charged provisionally with the civil and military government of Atjeh during the illness of [[A. J. E. Diemont|General Diemont]]. On [[10 August]] [[10 August]] [1877] the landing east of the [[Kwala Tamboea|Kwala Tamboea]] took place. In the night of [[10 August]] [[10 August]] – [[11 August]] [[11 August]] a band of 200 to 300 Atjehers crept into the bivouac and slaughtered many of the dwang-arbeiders. On [[26 August]] [[26 August]] [1877] the storming of [[Temoelit|Temoelit]] was undertaken; Van der Heyden was struck in the left eye by an enemy bullet, but the storming succeeded. On [[13 October]] [[13 October]] [1877] the Dutch flag was hoisted at Samalanga, and on [[20 October]] [[20 October]] at Merdoe; on [[21 August]] [[21 August]] [1877] the troops returned to Oleh-leh.

## The Batak War

On Sumatra the [[Batak War (1878)|Batak War]] began on [[1 February]] [[1 February]] [[1878]], but its roots were in the events of [[1877]]. On [[22 April]] [[22 April]] [[1878]] the column received reinforcements and the order to advance upon [[Bakhara|Bakhara]]; on [[1 May]] [[1 May]] [[1878]] Bakhara was stormed and burned.

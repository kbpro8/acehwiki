Figures, places, and events in Acehnese history associated with the year [[1878]].

**1878** was the year of [[Generaal van der Heiden]]'s military operations in the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak]] area — the only recent Dutch activity in that region before the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|punitive expedition of 9–10 June 1896]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] — and the year that opened the [[1878]]–[[1885]] window during which, as [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]] records in *ATJEH '96*, many raids originated from the kampongs along the right bank of the [[Atjehrivier]] in the [[III Moekims Kerkoen]] (around [[Lamsoet]]). As described on pp. 22 and 243 of the source, Van der Heiden's earlier *operatiën* in Lam-Krak included the construction of a road from [[Sibreh]] to [[Biloel]] (East to West), a military route that would, eighteen years later, become the assembly axis of [[E. W. Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]]'s 1e Colonne at the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|1896 tuchtiging]]. The year is thus doubly significant as a reference point for both Dutch and Achehnese operational patterns in the contested country between [[Biloel]] and [[Ana' Galong]].

## Account from *De geschiedenis van Nederland in onzen tijd* (1912) — The Aceh War in 1878

**1878** was the year of the [[Batak War (1878)|Batak War]] of February-May; of the campaigns in the [[IV Moekims|IV]] and [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] and the [[Capture of Montassik (28 July 1878)|capture of Montassik]] on [[28 July]] [[28 July]]; of the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman]] on [[13 October]] [[13 October]]; of the [[Submission of Pasanga (August 1878)|submission of Pasanga]] on [[20 August]] [[20 August]]; of the [[Submission of Langsar and Modjopahit and the Temijang-gebied (1877-1878)|submission of Langsar, Modjopahit and the Temijang-gebied]] of [[1877]]-[[1878]]; of the appointment of [[K. van der Heyden|Colonel Van der Heyden]] as governor of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden on [[15 January]] [[15 January]]; and of the imprisonment and banishment of [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]] on [[9 May]] [[9 May]] and July.

### January: Van der Heyden's Governorship

By Indies decree of [[15 January]] [[15 January]] [[1878]] [[K. van der Heyden|Colonel K. van der Heyden]] was appointed governor of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden, at the same time military commander there, "out of consideration that the ordinary civil administration more and more took the place of the purely military." In the course of the year a reorganisation of the Dutch troop strength in Atjeh was carried out: a separation was made between garrison- and field-bataljons; by three garrison-bataljons the occupation of the posts was to be provided for; the splitting into right and left halves of bataljons would lapse. In connection with the improved situation the number of troops was also reduced from 348 officers and 9235 rank-and-file to 298 officers and 7730 rank-and-file.

### February: The Westkust

In [[February 1878|February 1878]] Van der Heyden was on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]], where [[Malaboeh|Malaboeh]] was constantly threatened. The Dutch commander was able in February to deal the enemy a sensitive lesson by the tuchtiging of the kampong Darat. On [[19 February]] [[19 February]] the army landed in the realm of [[Telok Kroet|Telok Kroet]]. On [[22 February]] [[22 February]] the troops advanced northward, reached [[28 February|28 February]] [[Kloewang|Kloewang]] after a sometimes very difficult march and some hostile encounters, and embarked there for Malaboeh, where Van der Heyden himself arrived on [[9 March]] [[9 March]].

### February-May: The Batak War

On [[1 February]] [[1 February]] [[1878]] a column under Captain Scheltens advanced from [[Siboga|Siboga]] with orders to protect the missionaries and Christians of [[Silindoeng|Silindoeng]] and to drive [[Singa Maharadja|Singa Maharadja]] from Silindoeng or to take him prisoner. On [[22 April]] [[22 April]] [1878] the column received reinforcements and the order to advance upon [[Bakhara|Bakhara]]. On [[1 May]] [[1 May]] [1878] Bakhara, on [[Toba|Lake Toba]], was reached, stormed, and burned. On [[7 May]] [[7 May]] [1878] the march was continued to [[Goergoer|Goergoer]]. The [[Batak War (1878)|Batak War]] culminated in the submission of the chiefs of Silindoeng and Toba, and in the establishment of a controller's post at [[Sipoholon|Sipoholon]].

### May-June: Gighen and Gedong

In [[May 1878|May 1878]] Van der Heyden led an expedition to [[Gighen|Gighen]] and the upper lands of [[Pedir|Pedir]], where [[Toekoe di Tiroe|Toekoe di Tiroe]] remained hostile and the post at [[Segli|Segli]] had been attacked by a great force. The actual object of the expedition — the kampong [[Garouw|Garouw]], the headquarters of Toekoe di Tiroe — was not reached, but the taking of [[Telok Kadjoe|Telok Kadjoe]], which had been mistaken for Garouw, had the consequence that the radja of Gighen offered his submission and promised the surrender of Toekoe di Tiroe.

In [[June 1878|June 1878]] Van der Heyden's presence was again necessary on the [[Noord- en Oostkust|Oostkust]], where an attack had been made on [[Edi|Edi]] from [[Gedong|Gedong]]. On [[3 June]] [[3 June]] the troops disembarked at the Paseirivier. On [[7 June]] [[7 June]] a renewed advance was made. On [[16 June]] [[16 June]] the governor himself arrived with a considerable force, and on [[19 June]] [[19 June]] Blang Pria was taken. On [[21 June]] [[21 June]] the radja of Gedong offered his submission; his son, who succeeded him, submitted wholly to the Dutch.

### June-July: The Habib's Offensive

By the spring of [[1878]] the [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib]] was once more on the offensive. In the night of [[7 June]] [[7 June]] – [[8 June]] [[8 June]] a band dared to attack the dwang-arbeiders hospital at [[Pantej Perak|Pantej Perak]], where it made a real slaughter. On [[22 June]] [[22 June]] [[Kroëng Raba|Kroëng Raba]] was attacked by a great multitude of Atjehers. On [[23 June]] [[23 June]] the Habib sent an Atjeher with a white flag and a letter inviting the post-commandant to leave his position and fight with him in the open field.

### July-August: The Campaign in the XXII Moekims

On [[23 July]] [[23 July]] [1878] the important [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign in the XXII Moekims]] began with three columns. On [[28 July]] [[28 July]] [1878] the [[Capture of Montassik (28 July 1878)|missigit Montassik was taken]]. Operations continued from [[1 August]] [[1 August]] [1878] to [[23 March]] [[23 March]] [[1879]] in a system of "standfast, reconnoitre, punish."

### August-October: Submissions

In [[August 1878|August 1878]] submissions and negotiations began to come in from various sides — on [[25 August]] [[25 August]] the first by the Habib himself. On [[13 October]] [[13 October]] [1878] the Habib [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submitted]] at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. The next day [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]] was sworn in. On [[24 November]] [[24 November]] [1878] the Habib was embarked for [[Jeddah|Jeddah]]. On [[20 August]] [[20 August]] [1878] the blockade of [[Pasanga|Pasanga]] was raised after the submission of its new radja.

### May-July: The Treason of Toekoe Moeda Baid

[[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]], of whom it more and more appeared that he played a treacherous role, was taken prisoner on [[9 May]] [[9 May]] [1878] and transported to Batavia in July, where the Governor-General banished him to [[Banda|Banda]]. [[Panglima Tiban Mohammed|Panglima Tiban Mohammed]] on the other hand, the former sjabandar of Atjeh, submitted in April [1878], with the intention of joining the Dutch to bring the war to a close as soon as possible.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1878

**[[1878]]** was the year in which the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] formally transferred his rights over the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] landschapjes to the Netherlands-Indies Government, extinguishing the Langkat claim to suzerainty that had been the source of intermittent conflict since the early nineteenth-century intervention of [[Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema|Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema]]. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* treats [[1878]] as the terminus of a long Langkat-Tamiang dispute and the beginning of the formal Dutch administration that would, in [[1908]], be regularised through the incorporation of Tamiang into the *indirect bestuurd gebied* of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

## The antecedents of the transfer

The Langkat claim to Tamiang rested on the fact that *Tjoet Gát* — the warrior who had led the combined Tamiang forces to victory against adventurers from [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]] and had died in that battle — had been a subject of the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]]. The grant of the [[Bandahara|Bandahara]] territory to Tjoet Gát's heir, made by the two *Keudjeuroeën* of Karang and Moeda in gratitude for his service, was thus exploited by Langkat as a *voorwendsel* — a pretext — for proclaiming its own suzerainty over the new *landschapje*. The subsequent appointment of [[Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema|Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema]] as ruler of part of the Bandahara territory provoked the armed resistance of the next Radja Bandahara, supported by [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] and [[Langsa|Langsa]] — a war that remained unresolved for years.

## The 1861 appeal and its aftermath

In [[1861]] the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] exploited the presence of Dutch warships off [[Poeloe Kompai|Poeloe Kompai]] to summon the Tamiang princes and induce them to appeal to the Dutch *Gouvernement* and offer their submission. The Keudjeuroeën Karang and Moeda answered the summons, and at the same occasion recognised Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema as the legitimate ruler of the Bandahara fragment. In [[1865]] the Dutch Government issued formal acts of confirmation to the Keudjeuroeën Karang, the Keudjeuroeën Moeda, and Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema.

## The 1878 transfer

The formal transfer of [[1878]] extinguished the remaining Langkat rights over the Tamiang *landschapjes*. The Dutch *Gouvernement*, having acquired a *vrije hand* in the administration of the territory, placed a *bestuursambtenaar* — a civil administrator — in the Tamiang lands. The continuous military action required by the presence of *vele uit Atjeh verdreven kwaadwilligen en avonturiers* — many evildoers and adventurers driven out of Atjeh — in these largely wilderness *landschapjes* eventually compelled the Dutch to unify the command, and in [[1908]] Tamiang was formally incorporated into the *indirect bestuurd gebied* of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], though its economic ties remained with the *Cultuurgebied* of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]].



## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**1878** was the year in which [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] published his *[[Ons Program ([[1878]])|Ons Program]]*, the foundational anti-revolutionary party manifesto that first articulated, in political form, the doctrine of a Dutch "zedelijke roeping" — moral vocation — towards the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Indies]]. This doctrine would be carried into the [[Troonrede of 1901|troonrede of 1901]] when the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical policy]] was officially proclaimed, and it shaped every subsequent Dutch parliamentary debate on Atjeh.

### Kuyper's *Ons Program*

In [[Ons Program ([[1878]])|Ons Program]] of [[1878]] [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] saw the relation of the [[Netherlands]] to its colonies as that of a guardian to his ward (*voogd-pupil*). The guardian's first duty was to bring to the Indies the blessings of security and order that the [[Netherlands]] had itself received with the Gospel. A second duty was to enable the Indies in the future to assume a more autonomous position. Of these two duties the second proved more acceptable to the non-confessional parties than the first, which carried a Christian-zending subtext.

### Kuyper's later parliamentary intervention on Atjeh

When, in [[1896]], the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] had run for nearly a quarter of a century and [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|Toekoe Oemar]] had turned against the Dutch, [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] admitted that the patience preached by [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] was exhausted. He still believed the [[Netherlands]] bore the guilt for unleashing the war, but held that this injustice had now prescribed, and that the historical situation had to be respected — "gelijk Christus, de Romeinse Keizer, de dwingeland in Rome, erkende met de woorden: geeft de keizer wat des keizers is". For Kuyper there was no escape from continuing the war.

### Significance

[[1878]] is significant for Atjeh history because the *[[Ons Program ([[1878]])|Ons Program]]* of that year is the seed-bed of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical policy]] that, after the [[Troonrede of 1901|troonrede]] of [[1901]], was to govern every Dutch decision on Atjeh — including the conduct of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]].

## Posts Established During the 1878 Campaigns

The campaigns of [[1878]] resulted in the establishment of a chain of posts extending eastward from [[Lambaroe]] through the [[XXII Moekims]], including [[Anagaloeng]], [[Montasik]], [[Senelop]], [[Panteh-Karang]], [[Lepong-Ara]], [[Samagani]], [[Tjot-Basetoel]], [[Long-Lemoh]], [[Glé-Kamping]], and [[Djerir]]. These posts created a 121-palen communication line through territory that every transport required fighting to traverse. They would be maintained until the [[Concentration Decision of August 1884]], when all were evacuated on [[13 January]] [[1885]].

The book is finished at 's-Gravenhage on [[27 August]] [[1878]].

## The State of the War at the Year's Opening

In January [[1878]] the toestand in Atjeh was, on paper, bevredigend: several hoofden had submitted, and the linie from the [[Kroeng Raba]] to the [[Kwala Gigieng]] — the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] that [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] had completed in May [[1875]] and extended to the Kroeng Tjoet in February [[1876]] — remained in Dutch hands. Under [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]]'s long command, however, no new offensives had been undertaken since Pel's death, and the *afwachtende houding* once again dominated Dutch operations. The [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] was "in geen gunstigen toestand": manschappen onvoldoende geoefend, kaders in treurige staat, many sick or unfit, with a groot gebrek aan officieren, especially in the artillerie. The Atjehneezen had used the lull to build versterkingen near Dutch posts and to receive hulpbenden, wapens and levensmiddelen from the vassal coastal states. Borel judged that the apparent quiet was deceptive and that only a major offensive — supported by fresh troops from the Netherlands — could end the war.

## Borel's Brigade Proposal ([[1 March|1]]–[[3 March]] [[1878]])

On [[1 March|1]], [[2 March|2]] and [[3 March]] [[1878]] Borel published in *Het Vaderland* a three-part article entitled *[[Een vaderlandsch belang (Borel, 1878)|Een vaderlandsch belang]]*, proposing the formation in the Netherlands of an expeditionnaire krijgsmacht of 3 000–4 000 man — later specified as 150 officers and 3 000 men — to be sent directly to Atjeh. The volunteers were to be raised with premies (ƒ 250–300 handgeld, ƒ 1 per day soldij in Atjeh, equal premie on return), bound to a strict two-year maximum verbintenis, and armed with steel [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]] with schokbuis. He amplified the proposal in *De Militaire Spectator*, No. 7, jaargang [[1878]]. The estimated cost was ƒ 6 000 000 for the first year and ƒ 10–15 million for the whole two years, to be raised as an *oorlogsleening* and ultimately repaid, he suggested, in Acehnese pepper. Borel closed with the wish that, if his proposal were adopted, the words of Camille Rousset on the [[1830]] French expedition against Algiers — "rien n'avait été négligé pour le succès de l'expédition" — might apply to the Dutch in Atjeh.

## The June Crisis: Gedoeng and the Breakthrough

In the second half of June [[1878]] the newly appointed gouverneur van Atjeh, [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]], left with most of the mobiele strijdmacht to tuchtigen the vassal coastal state of [[Gedoeng]]. Barely had he departed when the enemy attacked the Dutch linie, broke through at several places, fell into the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]], verjoeg the pro-Dutch bevolking, plundered and burned, sneed the gemeenschap between [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] and several posts, and caused schrik and verwarring. To make the crisis worse, an atrocity was committed in a Dutch hospital — "het gruwelijke moordtooneel in een van onze hospitalen bedreven" — that Borel cites as one of the "wrange vruchten van het passieve stelsel." Van der Heijden had to rush back in aller ijl and with geweld restore communicatie with the posts in the IV Moekims; new battalions were sent from Java. The Dutch eventually drove the enemy out everywhere and even made a successful inval into the XXII Moekims to punish them — but the verloopen of the friendly bevolking could not be undone: they had lost vertrouwen in Dutch protection. The lesson Borel draws is categorical: posts alone cannot prevent a linie-doorbraak; mobile forces must never be reduced. Gedoeng itself was tuchtiged twice; after the second, the chief laid down bestuur and his son came to submit — a "schitterend succes" that Borel uses as proof of the *aanvallende stelsel*.

## The Submission of Pasangan and the Closing of the Book

While Borel was writing, on [[15 August]] [[1878]] the telegraph brought news that [[Pasangan]] — one of the vassal coastal states that had supported the enemy with hulpbenden, wapens and levensmiddelen — had submitted after a bombardment by the Dutch [[Compagnie mariniers in Atjeh|marine]]. Borel predicts that the same pattern will work in [[Groot-Atjeh]] itself: "eerst moet de vijand getuchtigd worden en wel in die mate dat hem geen andere uitweg overblijft dan het hoofd in den schoot te leggen." He invokes in jest the words of prins von Bismarck on the "psychologische oogenblik" at which to begin negotiations with Atjehnese chiefs after a few tuchtigingen. The book itself is finished at 's-Gravenhage on [[27 August]] [[1878]], with the wish that, if the [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] proposal is adopted, the success of the French at Algiers may be repeated in Atjeh. The war, however, was still ongoing — already in its sixth year — and the stalemate that Borel diagnoses would persist for decades more.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the XXII Moekims campaign and the first harbor closure

[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* identifies [[1878]] as the operational turning point of the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|Atjeh-oorlog]]: the year of the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] — the *veldtocht in de XXII Moekims* opened by [[Van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] in July [[1878]] after the June [[1878]] strategic reversal engineered by his chief of staff [[Geij van Pittius|majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius]] — and of the [[13 October]] [[1878]] submission of [[Habib Abdoel Rachman]] and [[Tekoe Moeda Baid]] that, "binnen drie maanden na den aanvang van den eersten veldtocht", decapitated the Groot-Atjeh resistance.

### The June 1878 strategic reversal by Geij van Pittius

The strategic frame of the XXII Moekims campaign was set in June [[1878]] by [[Geij van Pittius|majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius]], Van der Heijden's *pas opgetreden chef van den staf*. Van Heutsz's account, citing [[B. C. P. Kielstra|Kielstra]]'s *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* (III, noot, bladz. 249), names Geij van Pittius as the intellectual author of the reversal:

> "Het waren vooral de vertoogen van den pas opgetreden chef van den staf, den majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius, die den bevelhebber tot dit besluit brachten. Deze had reeds in 1877, tijdens de aanwezigheid te Atjeh van den Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge, er op aangedrongen, de Zuidoosterlinie langs de Atjeh rivier te verleggen en daartoe de XXII Moekims in te rukken".

The argument that "men door expedities naar de kuststaten het doel — het einde van den oorlog — niet kon bereiken, en dat alleen eene beslissing kon worden verkregen door in Groot-Atjeh zelf den vijand aan te tasten en te vervolgen" was accepted by Van der Heijden "met het halve veldleger ter beschikking". The Governor-General's *verzoeningspolitiek* of March [[1877]] was set aside.

### The July 1878 XXII Moekims campaign and the 13 October 1878 submission

The campaign opened in July [[1878]] — "De krijgsoperatiën in de XXII en XXVI Moekims, die in Juli van het vorige jaar zijn aangevangen" — with the *inrukken* of the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi of the XXII Moekims]] along the *Zuidoosterlinie* of the Atjeh river. Within three months of the opening of the campaign, the two principal resistance leaders had submitted:

> "Hoe reeds binnen drie maanden na den aanvang van den eersten veldtocht in de XXII Moekims, de onderwerping van Habib Abdoer Rachman en van Tekoe Moeda Baid op den 13en October 1878 volgde".

The [[13 October]] [[1878]] submission was the political culmination of the campaign. [[Habib Abdoel Rachman]] — the *zwager van Tekoe Moeda Baid*, *de ziel van den tegenstand* — and [[Tekoe Moeda Baid]] had been the two principal figures of the Groot-Atjeh resistance since the failed negotiations of March–October [[1877]]. Their submission, together, in a single act on [[13 October]] [[1878]], was the operational vindication of the Geij van Pittius reversal.

### Van der Heijden's first harbor closure (1878)

Alongside the military campaign, Van der Heijden closed the havens of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden for the first time — the first sustained Dutch attempt to translate territorial conquest into economic coercion:

> "Reeds in 1878, na hervatting van de krijgsoperatiën in de XXII Moekims, liet hij daarom alle havens van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, met uitzondering van Olehleh en Edi, voor den invoerhandel sluiten".

The closure — covering all havens of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, with the exception of [[Olehleh]] and [[Edi]], for the *invoerhandel* (import trade) — was revoked by [[Van Lansberge|G.-G. van Lansberge]] by letter of [[7 February]] [[1879]] — but Van der Heijden, "toen de Generaal opnieuw de vaste overtuiging kreeg dat, wilde men thans den oorlog beëindigd zien, de havens opnieuw moesten gesloten worden", closed them a second time, "hij sloot ze ten tweedemale en gaf bij telegram d.d. 5 Juli 1879 aan den Gouv. Generaal te kennen … ditmaal op zijne persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid". The two closures were the operational proof of Van der Heijden's strategic premise: that the submission of the Sagi federations could only be converted into the submission of the entire Atjehnese resistance if the coast were closed. The episode became, in Van Heutsz's later tract, the model for the marine-enforced closure Van Heutsz would prescribe in [[1893]] and implement as Governor after [[1898]].

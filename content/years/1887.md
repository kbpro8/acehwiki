## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1887

**[[1887]]** was the year in which the Bataviaasche firma **De Lange** received the concession for the construction of a coal station at the **Sabangbaai** on the island of **Poeloe Wè** — the founding act of what would become, after the free-port opening of [[1897]] and the founding of the [[Sabang Maatschappij|N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]] in [[1898]], the great harbour of [[Sabang|Sabang]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats [[1887]] as the constitutive moment of Sabang as a Dutch commercial establishment: the year in which the location of the future harbour was fixed and the institutional framework for its development was put in place.

## The 1884 origin and the 1887 commission

The Dutch interest in [[Poeloe Wè|Poeloe Wè]] as a coaling station dated from [[1884]], when the Netherlands-Indies Government built a small coal shed at the *Zuidbaai* (Lhôc Balhan) and appointed a chief over the island. The *Balhanbaai*, however, "bleek echter weldra voor de scheepvaart verschillende bezwaren op te leveren" — soon proved, however, to present various obstacles for shipping — and in [[1887]] a commission was sent out to investigate. The commission "kwam tot de conclusie, dat de Sabangbaai meer aan te bevelen was" — came to the conclusion that the Sabangbaai was more recommendable. In the same year, the Bataviaasche firma De Lange received the concession for the construction of a coal station at the Sabangbaai.

## The geestelijken vader and the political champion

The *geestelijken vader* — the spiritual father — of Sabang, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, was "de heer van Oosterzee," who first proposed the location. The political champion of the project was the then-majoor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]], whose [[1892]] brochure *De Onderwerping van Atjeh* would, five years later, propagate the larger vision of Sabang as an ocean harbour and free port. Van Heutsz's brochure urged the use of the *talrijke natuurlijke voordeelen* — the numerous natural advantages — that Sabang offered for shipping and trade, and proposed the establishment of a free port that would compete with [[Penang|Penang]] — "de eenige concurrentie" — by drawing Atjeh's trade away from the British entrepôt through the *eeuwenoude betrekkingen* of the Atjehsche coastal traders with their friends in Penang.

## The 1897 opening and the 1898 founding

The concession of [[1887]] bore fruit a decade later. In [[1897]] Sabang was opened as a free port for general trade; in [[1898]] the [[Sabang Maatschappij|N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]] was founded, taking over the operation of the harbour from the firm of De Lange. The hopes that van Heutsz had placed in Sabang as a competitor to Penang were, however, only partially realised: "de grootsche toekomstdroomen van Sabang als handels-emporium voor geheel Noord-Sumatra niet verwezenlijkt hebben" — the grand dreams of Sabang as the trade emporium for all of North Sumatra were never realised.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

On [[7 May]] **[[1887]]** [[H. N. A. Swart]] departed for the East Indies aboard the *s.s. Zuid-Holland*, arriving at [[Batavia]] on [[17 June]] and being posted to the 6th Battalion on [[22 June]].

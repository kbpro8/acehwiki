**The year 1944** was, in Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan*, the year of the great propaganda shift — the year in which the [[Asia Timoer Raja|Asia Timoer Raja]] idea gave way, in [[September]] [[1944]], to the [[Indonesia Merdéka|Indonesia Merdéka]] idea, and in which the labour regime, the rice requisitioning system, and the political police of the Japanese occupation of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]] acquired their final, oppressive form. It was the year in which the fortunes of war turned irrevocably against Japan, and in which the occupation accordingly shifted from the offensive to the defensive.

## The Koiso promise and the shift to Indonesia Merdéka

The decisive event of the year was Prime Minister [[Koiso|Koiso]]'s declaration of [[7 September]] [[1944]] in the 85th session of the Japanese parliament, promising future independence for the "East Indies" (Hindia Timoer). "With this promise, the wavering confidence had to be bolstered and the population had to be spurred to even greater effort. '[[Indonesia Merdéka|Indonesia Merdéka]]' now became the watchword of the propaganda." For three days, flags were flown throughout the residency; in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] a mass meeting of [[9 September]] [[1944]] pledged "to cooperate with the Japanese army for the defense of the fatherland … so that as soon as possible an independent East Indies may be obtained." The *[[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]]* came out with a 22-page special edition "specially devoted to the celebration of Idoe'lfitr and the independence promise." The [[Indonesia Merdéka|Indonesia Merdéka]] period — the third of Piekaar's four periods of the occupation — had begun.

## The labour and rice regime

The year brought the formal establishment of the forced-labour and rice-requisitioning systems. The Residents' decrees of [[8 April]] [[1944]] nos. 53 and 54 "provided for a registration of all labor forces between the ages of 16 and 45" and established the *Atjèh-shii-kéa-hókó-dan* — "Perkoempoelan pekerdja² oentoek oemoem didalam Atjèh-shii dalam hal membina Asia Timoer Raja" — for the equitable distribution of forced labour. The system of rice purchase was overhauled: "the establishment in every sub-division of a so-called 'B(agi) D(an) K(oempoel)-organization'" was undertaken, with compulsory deliveries of 22,000 tons of peeled rice and 5,400 tons of maize, *katjang kedelee*, *katjang hidjau*, *oebi* and sago. The construction of the [[Takéngón|Takéngón]]–[[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] road, opened on [[20 July]] [[1944]], swallowed "7,000 volunteers … daily."

## The Allied raids

[[25 July]] [[1944]] brought a heavy Allied bombardment of [[Sabang|Sabang]] "from the sea and from the air," on which occasion Hr. Ms. *Tromp* "penetrated into the bay of Sabang." The Resident's proclamation of [[26 July]] [[1944]] "reassured the population and pointed out how the vastness of the seas and airspace had indeed enabled the enemy to outwit the vigilance of the Japanese army, but that he had been repelled with heavy losses." The raid was the clearest sign yet that the war had reached Atjèh's doorstep.

## The 2nd and 3rd sessions of the Atjèh Council

The [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]] met in its 2nd session on [[4 April|4]]–[[5 April]] [[1944]] — "in joint meeting with the simultaneously held *gun-chó*-conference" — and in its 3rd session on [[19 October|19]]–[[20 October]] [[1944]]. The agenda was, throughout, the same: "the food supplies, the promotion of food production, and the mobilization of labor." On [[10 June]] [[1944]] the 2nd annual conference of the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] took place in Koetaradja, in the same propaganda sign.

## The kempeitai purge

The year closed with the great kempeitai purge of the *oelèëbalang* class. In [[May]] [[1944]] the kempeitai "also struck" at the [[Treffers organisation|Treffers organisation]], leading to the arrests of [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]] and [[Teukoe Ali Keureukón|Teukoe Ali Keureukón]], the gun-chó and acting gun-chó of Koetaradja. [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]] and [[Teukoe Sulaiman|Teukoe Sulaiman]] were executed by the Japanese; [[Teukoe Radja Djoema'at|Teukoe Radja Djoema'at]] also lost his life. The purge marked the decisive weakening of the *oelèëbalang* class — the precondition of the social revolution that would follow the capitulation.

## Significance

1944 was the year in which the Japanese occupation of Atjèh crossed its tipping-point. The propaganda shift to [[Indonesia Merdéka|Indonesia Merdéka]] acknowledged the failure of the [[Asia Timoer Raja|Asia Timoer Raja]] project; the labour regime reached its peak of severity; and the kempeitai broke the back of the *oelèëbalang* class. By the close of the year, the occupation had acquired the unreal character Piekaar ascribes to its final phase: "Residents' ordinances and Residents' decisions still brought numerous new regulations, which were destined to remain paper regulations."

## Account from *Nederlands-Indië contra Japan (1959)* — 1944

**The year 1944** was, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the year of Lt [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|L. C. G. F. Onvlee]]'s designation for transport to Japan and the temporary hand-over of the *vaandel* (colours) of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] to the Dutch camp commander of his Birma prisoner-of-war camp. The year [[1944]] is the staff history's principal narrative instance of the vaandel's temporary loss and recovery in the Onvlee episode.

## The 2 March 1944 designation for Japan

On 2 March 1944 he was designated for departure to Japan. All those designated for Japan had to surrender their kit, which was to be burned. Onvlee took the Dutch camp commander into his confidence and temporarily handed the vaandel over to him.

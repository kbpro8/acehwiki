**1849** was a year marked by both scholarly and military developments in the Dutch East Indies: the first European edition of the Malay chronicle *Hikajat Radja‑radja Pasei* was published by [[E. Dulaurier]] in [[1849]], and the Dutch colonial forces launched the *3e Bali‑expeditie* (third Bali expedition), for which officers such as [[J. C. Koderitsch]] and [[L. A. Ernst]] received the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] and other marine honors. These events illustrate the parallel expansion of Dutch scholarly interest in Acehnese history and the logistical mobilisation of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] that would later underpin the prolonged [[Aceh War]] ( [[1873]]–[[1904]] ).

## Publications

- [[E. Dulaurier]] — publication of his edition of the [[Hikajat Radja‑radja Pasei|*Hikajat Radja‑radja Pasei*]] in [[1849]], the first European publication of the Malay chronicle of the kings of [[Pasei|Samudra Pasai]].

## Documents

- [[Hikajat Radja‑radja Pasei]] — the Malay chronicle of the kings of Pasai, edited by Dulaurier in [[1849]].

## Places

- [[Pasei]] — the historical Muslim kingdom of Samudra Pasai on the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], subject of the chronicle edited by Dulaurier in [[1849]].

## Military Operations

The Dutch colonial administration conducted the *3e Bali‑expeditie* in [[1849]], a naval and marine campaign against the Balinese kingdoms. Officers [[J. C. Koderitsch]] and [[L. A. Ernst]] were among those honoured with the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] for their service. The expedition was carried out by the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]], demonstrating the capacity of Dutch forces to project power across the archipelago, a capacity later employed in the [[Aceh War]] ( [[1873]]–[[1904]] ).

## Significance

The 1849 Bali operations exemplify the simultaneous deployment of Dutch marine forces across the archipelago, underscoring the logistical capacity that later supported campaigns against Aceh. At the same time, the publication of the *Hikajat Radja‑radja Pasei* contributed to European knowledge of the early Islamic polities of Sumatra, linking scholarly pursuits with the broader colonial context of the mid‑nineteenth century.

**[[1898]]** was the decisive turning-point year of the [[Aceh War]]. Three closely spaced government decisions in [[March 1898]]—the approval of the [[Pidië Expedition]] ([[5 March]]), the appointment of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] as adviser ([[15 March]]), and the appointment of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as civil and military governor ([[25 March]])—formalised the transition from the failed alliance and negotiation policies of the previous decades to the active subjugation strategy that would finally break the resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Key events

- [[5 March]]: Government decree approving the [[Pidië Expedition]].
- [[15 March]]: Appointment of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] as Adviser for Arabic and Indigenous Affairs.
- [[25 March]]: Appointment of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as Civil and Military Governor of [[Atjeh]] and its Dependencies.
- [[1 June]]: The [[Pidië Expedition]] begins at [[Segli]] in [[Pedir]].
- [[14 June]]: Capture of [[Keumala]], the spiritual and organisational centre of resistance.

These events—separated by only twenty days between the first and last government decisions—marked a definitive break with the previous strategic confusion. The collaboration between [[Van Heutsz]] and [[Snouck Hurgronje]] established the model for Dutch pacification: military execution guided by ethnographic and religious intelligence.

## Consequences

The events of [[1898]] set in motion the five-year campaign that culminated in the unconditional submission of [[Panglima Polim]] in [[1903]]. The consolidation of Dutch authority over the [[Onderhoorigheden]]—achieved through the successes of [[1898]]—laid the foundation for the further extension of Dutch effective authority to other regions of the [[Buitenbezittingen]] after [[1902]].

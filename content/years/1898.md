## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1898

**[[1898]]** was the year in which [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] was appointed Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], an event the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* identifies as "het begin van eene geheel nieuwe periode in ons optreden" — the beginning of an entirely new period in the Dutch intervention — and also the year in which the [[N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|Sabang Maatschappij]] was founded, succeeding the firm of De Lange as operator of the [[Sabang|Sabang]] coal station that had been opened as a free port the previous year.

## The new governor and the new strategy

Van Heutsz's arrival in the governorship marked a decisive break with the *pacifisme der moederlandsche Regeering* — the conciliatory policy previously favoured in The Hague — that had, *De Buitenbezittingen* argues, repeatedly allowed the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] to flare up again. The new governor pursued the war with the *vliegende colonnes* — small, mobile "flying columns" — that had been in operation since [[1896]] under the system associated with the names of [[Graafland|Graafland]], [[G. C. E. van Daalen|van Daalen]], and van Heutsz himself. The same year saw the resolution of the long-running dispute between the *oelèëbalang* of [[Peureulak|Peureulak]] and the chief of [[Soengèë Raja|Soengèë Raja]], with the former compelled to acknowledge the latter's complete independence — though retaining a share of the *wasé-oelèëbalang* on pepper exports — in order to clear the way for the new political contracts that would bring mining concession rights to the Government.

## The founding of the Sabang Maatschappij

[[1898]] also saw the founding of the [[N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]], the company that took over the operation of [[Sabang|Sabang]] harbour from the Bataviaasche firma De Lange, which had received the original concession in [[1887]]. The free port of Sabang, opened for general trade in [[1897]], was the project of van Heutsz himself; his [[1892]] brochure *De Onderwerping van Atjeh* had urged the use of Atjeh's natural advantages to compete with [[Penang|Penang]] by establishing a free port whose old trading connections with the coast would, he predicted, draw Atjeh's trade away from the British entrepôt. The prediction would not fully materialise — Penang's "eeuwenoude betrekkingen" with Atjeh proved more durable than van Heutsz had expected — but Sabang's development as a coaling station and transit harbour proceeded apace.

## Significance

[[1898]] thus stands as the pivot year of the late-nineteenth-century Dutch intervention in Atjeh: the year in which the military, administrative, and commercial instruments that would dominate the next two decades — the flying columns, the governorship of van Heutsz, and the Sabang Maatschappij — all came into place.

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1898

**[[1898]]** was the year of the benoeming of [[Governor Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] to *civiel en militair Gouverneur* of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — the appointment that signalled the Dutch were, finally, serious about ending the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. "In [[1898]] bewijst de benoeming van Van Heutsz tot civiel en militair Gouverneur, dat men werkelijk een einde aan de oorlog wil maken." The Van Heutsz *systeem* of small colonnes and constant patrouilles would, from 1898, be the principal instrument of subjugation; the [[Colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]–[[1911]] was its systematic conclusion.

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954).

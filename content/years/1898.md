**[[1898]]** was the year in which [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] was appointed Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] — an event the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* identifies as "*het begin van eene geheel nieuwe periode in ons optreden*" (the beginning of an entirely new period in the Dutch intervention) — and also the year in which the [[N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|Sabang Maatschappij]] was founded, succeeding the firm of De Lange as operator of the [[Sabang|Sabang]] coal station that had been opened as a free port the previous year. The same year saw the [[Pedir Expedition (1898)|Pedir-expedition]] of Colonel [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] against the [[Pedir|Pedir]] region of the north coast, in the course of which the chaplain [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] extended his ministry from [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] to [[Pedir|Pedir]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1898

**[[1898]]** was the year in which [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] was appointed Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], an event the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* identifies as "het begin van eene geheel nieuwe periode in ons optreden" — the beginning of an entirely new period in the Dutch intervention — and also the year in which the [[N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|Sabang Maatschappij]] was founded, succeeding the firm of De Lange as operator of the [[Sabang|Sabang]] coal station that had been opened as a free port the previous year.

## The new governor and the new strategy

Van Heutsz's arrival in the governorship marked a decisive break with the *pacifisme der moederlandsche Regeering* — the conciliatory policy previously favoured in The Hague — that had, *De Buitenbezittingen* argues, repeatedly allowed the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] to flare up again. The new governor pursued the war with the *vliegende colonnes* — small, mobile "flying columns" — that had been in operation since [[1896]] under the system associated with the names of [[Graafland|Graafland]], [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|van Daalen]], and van Heutsz himself. The same year saw the resolution of the long-running dispute between the *oelèëbalang* of [[Peureulak|Peureulak]] and the chief of [[Soengèë Raja|Soengèë Raja]], with the former compelled to acknowledge the latter's complete independence — though retaining a share of the *wasé-oelèëbalang* on pepper exports — in order to clear the way for the new political contracts that would bring mining concession rights to the Government.

## The founding of the Sabang Maatschappij

[[1898]] also saw the founding of the [[N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]], the company that took over the operation of [[Sabang|Sabang]] harbour from the Bataviaasche firma De Lange, which had received the original concession in [[1887]]. The free port of Sabang, opened for general trade in [[1897]], was the project of van Heutsz himself; his [[1892]] brochure *De Onderwerping van Atjeh* had urged the use of Atjeh's natural advantages to compete with [[Penang|Penang]] by establishing a free port whose old trading connections with the coast would, he predicted, draw Atjeh's trade away from the British entrepôt. The prediction would not fully materialise — Penang's "eeuwenoude betrekkingen" with Atjeh proved more durable than van Heutsz had expected — but Sabang's development as a coaling station and transit harbour proceeded apace.

## Account from *Vierhonderd jaren missie* (1934) — 1898

The [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume of 1934]] records the [[1898]] extension of the chaplaincy of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor Verbraak]] from [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] to [[Pedir|Pedir]] in the context of the [[Pedir Expedition (1898)|Pedir-expedition]] of the newly-appointed Governor [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]:

> *"Toen in [[1898]] de toenmalige kolonel van Heutsz als gouverneur van Atjeh optrad, deelde deze mede dat er bij de regeering te Batavia het plan had bestaan om aan de ophanden zijnde Pedir-expeditie afzonderlijke geestelijken toe te voegen. En verder dat van dien maatregel was afgezien omdat hij, door jarenlange ondervinding, overtuigd was dat de predikant, en vooral de algemeen beminde pastoor Verbraak hun geestelijke werkzaamheden wel zouden willen verdeelen tusschen Groot-Atjeh en Pedir."*

The episode fixes three facts: (1) that a Pedir-expedition was planned for [[1898]]; (2) that the original plan had been to attach separate chaplains to it; and (3) that this measure was set aside in favour of distributing the existing predikant and the universally beloved pastoor Verbraak between [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir|Pedir]]. The decision is itself a document of the structural dependence of the colonial military chaplaincy on a small circle of trusted clerics. Verbraak did not refuse the extension: "*Pastoor Verbraak nam Pedir er bij*" ("Pastoor Verbraak took Pedir on as well").

## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1898

**[[1898]]** was the year of the benoeming of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] to *civiel en militair Gouverneur* of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — the appointment that signalled the Dutch were, finally, serious about ending the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. "In 1898 the appointment of Van Heutsz as civil and military Governor proves that there was a real wish to make an end of the war." The Van Heutsz *systeem* of small colonnes and constant patrouilles would, from 1898, be the principal instrument of subjugation; the [[Colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]–[[1911]] was its systematic conclusion.

## Significance

[[1898]] thus stands as the pivot year of the late-nineteenth-century Dutch intervention in Atjeh: the year in which the military, administrative, and commercial instruments that would dominate the next two decades — the flying columns, the governorship of van Heutsz, and the Sabang Maatschappij — all came into place, and the year in which the Catholic chaplaincy of the Atjeh war-footing was extended to the Pedir theatre under the personal ministry of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor Verbraak]].

## Account from *Atjeh in de Tweede Kamer* ([[1897]])

**[[1898]]** was the budget year whose estimates for [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Netherlands India]] were the occasion of the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November]] and [[17 November]] [[1897]]; the pamphlet recording that debate bears the sub-title *Debatten bij de behandeling der begrooting van Nederlandsch-Indië voor [[1898]]*.

### The Atjeh provisions of the [[1898]] estimates

The [[Indische begrooting voor 1898|budget for 1898]] as debated contained a request for three *[[Recherchevaartuigen|recherchevaartuigen]]* to suppress the import of war contraband — a number [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] and [[Van Vlijmen]] both thought too small, the latter asking for ten — and provided a reinforcement of two thousand Europeans, which the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Indies government]] considered sufficient. It also carried, in chapter II, division II, sub-division 19, a sum of f [[1500]] for the limitation of slavery in the self-governing *landschappen* of the government of [[Celebes]], cited by [[H. van Kol]] as evidence that the Dutch tolerated in their own dependencies the evils for which they had made war on [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

[[H. van Kol|Van Kol]] observed that the chamber stood on the point of again placing some forty million at disposal "voor vechten en dooden te land en te water." — for fighting and killing by land and by sea.

### The anticipated accession of the Queen

The year was also anticipated in the debate as that of the accession of [[Wilhelmina of the Netherlands|Queen Wilhelmina]]. [[Bahlmann]] deplored that [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|colonel Heutsz]] had declared, in a speech on the Queen's birthday, that the situation was so far advanced that "so that in 1898, at the accession of the Queen, he might lay Atjeh pacified at her feet," — in 1898, at the accession of the Queen, Atjeh could be laid pacified at her feet, calling it a *fanfaronnade*. The Minister [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] defended the words, explaining that they had been spoken in a military environment in which there are always doubters, and that in the mouth of that man such words were no boast, since Van Heutsz belonged to those who sought their strength not in words but in deeds.

### The rejected motion

[[Van Kol's motion of 16 November 1897|Van Kol's motion]], had it been carried, would have required an impartial commission to report within a year — that is, within [[1898]] — on the moral, political and financial consequences of the war. It was rejected on [[17 November]] [[1897]] by seventy-two votes to three.

## The appointment

As Chief of the General Staff, van Heutsz had gradually succeeded, well supported by Snouck Hurgronje, in convincing the Governor-General of the correctness of their shared insight. When general [[Van Vliet]] retired in March [[1898]], van Heutsz was appointed Governor of Atjeh with Snouck Hurgronje as adviser. On the first programme point — "press on, lose not a day, never let the enemy recover" — the Atjeh scholar and the Atjeh soldier were entirely agreed; "so completely agreed that with Snouck there could be no hesitation: Van Heutsz, and no one but he, would be Atjeh's Marshal Forward!"

## The Pidië campaign

On [[1 June]] [[1898]] van Heutsz entered "the Pidië hotbed" at the head of four battalions with auxiliary arms; that same day [[Garot]] fell after a bold storm. He settled with [[Teungkoe Tapa']] on the [[Oostkust van Atjeh|east coast]], took the *[[benteng]]s* [[Koeta Poetoih]] of [[Teungkoe di Tjòt Plieng]], drove [[Teukoe Oemar]] to [[Tangsé]] and then reached and occupied Tangsé itself by the famous turning movement through the impassable terrain around the gorge of the [[Kroeëng Beuniët]]. He returned barefoot to [[Sigli]] on [[28 August]], held a reception at [[Koeta Radja]] on [[31 August]], returned to Pidië, and had [[Teukoe Béntara Gloempang Pajoeng]] lifted by the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]*. "In the space of four months Pidië was subjected."

## The new method

The old apparatus of the war was abolished: bulletproof forts demolished, parapets replaced by barbed wire, nocturnal shootings prevented by patrols and ambushes, "base and line of operations became obsolete concepts". Independent columns could now act for days, then weeks, then months, taking five or six days' provisions and buying or foraging the rest.

## Swart in 1898

For [[H. N. A. Swart]] the year was likewise a hinge: he was promoted *Kapitein* on [[24 July]] [[1898]] and posted to Atjeh for the second time on [[22 November]] [[1898]], to the 7th Battalion at Sigli. The transitional year [[1898]] was, du Croo noted, precisely the year in which Swart's captaincy came, ending the long period in which he had remained "obstinately on [[Java]] and in the peacetime garrisons* ("peacetime garrisons")".

By decree no. 13 of [[15 September]] [[1898]] Swart was awarded 825 guilders for translating into Malay the *Uittreksel uit de Recrutenschool*.

## The turn of the century

"The figure of Swart is inseparably bound up with the conversion, the renewal, which was set in motion about 1898; his administrative direction and his administrative insights belong to that new and different, better time; his conduct of government and his administrative policy are cut to the best form of practical ethics."

**[[1914]]** was the year of a series of administrative reorganisations in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] — the dissolution of the [[Kawaj XII|Kawaj XII]] federation on the Pedir highlands, the regularisation of the territorial boundaries of the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], the relocation of the *Kédjoerön* of [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] to [[Poeloe Nas|Poeloe Nas]], and the redrawing of the boundary between [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]] — that together represent the high-water mark of the Dutch policy of consolidating the *landschap* structure of the territory into regular territorial units amenable to indirect rule.

## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1914

**1914** marked a transitional period in the colonial administration of [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. As the outbreak of the First World War began to disrupt global trade and colonial supply lines, the Dutch authorities in [[Sumatra]] were finalizing long-term budgetary projections and consolidating the pacification efforts that had defined the previous decade of the [[Aceh War]].

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The year [[1914]] serves as the terminal date and publication context for the *Atjeh up to date* statistical compilation. The surviving ledgers from this year shift from recording immediate military pacification costs to projecting long-term administrative and infrastructure expenditures:

*   **Budgetary Projections:** Colonial ledgers outline a multi-year spending plan extending from [[1914]] through [[1917]]. Initial allocations for [[1914]] are recorded at 20,000 to 50,000 guilders for specific administrative or infrastructural projects, with projected costs scaling significantly in the subsequent years.
*   **Administrative Consolidation:** The data reflects a colony moving away from the extreme, unpredictable costs of active guerrilla warfare toward standardized, predictable colonial governance. The financial overhead of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons was being absorbed into routine provincial budgets.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** While global shipping networks were severely impacted by the war in Europe, the internal logistical registries of [[Acheh]] continued to track the movement of export commodities and colonial supplies, maintaining the bureaucratic frameworks established in [[1908]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1914

**[[1914]]** was the closing year of the decade surveyed by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau in its volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914*. According to the Bureau's own framing, [[1914]] was doubly significant: it was the year of the Koloniale Tentoonstelling (Colonial Exhibition) at [[Semarang]] — the occasion that prompted the writing of the volume itself — and it was the year in which "the circumstances" (i.e., the outbreak of the European war) "exerted their influence over the whole of the Dutch East Indies," so that the Exhibition "was by no means as busy as expected, and as it indeed deserved." The Bureau's volume was thus conceived as a remedy for the failure of the Exhibition to disseminate knowledge of the Buitenbezittingen — including [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — to the wider public.

On the administrative side, [[1914]] saw the promulgation of the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie (Self-Government Ordinance)]] (Stbl. 1914 No. 24) — the ordinance "whose provisions mainly correspond with the provisions of the detailed political contracts." The ordinance codified the rights, competences, and obligations of the Gouvernement on one hand and of the [[Zelfbesturen|self-governing territories]] on the other under the regime of the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] that had been in force since [[1899]]. For [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — where, with the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] complete, the Dutch administrative system had reached its fullest post-[[1896]] development — the [[1914]] ordinance represented the formal legal consolidation of the new order.

The [[1914]] ordinance was accompanied, in the same year, by the Inlandsche gemeente-ordonnanties for [[Amboina]] and [[Sumatra's Westkust]] (Stbl. 1914 No. 629 en 774) — ordinances regulating the household affairs of the Inlandsche gemeenten (native communities). These ordinances, modelled on the [[1906]] Java and Madura regulations, marked the beginning of a parallel process of legal consolidation at the village level.

## Significance

[[1914]] closes the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" for the Buitenbezittingen and Atjeh. The [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] of [[1914]] is the legal capstone of the post-[[1896]] reorientation in Atjeh: the ordinance that translated the *de facto* extension of Dutch authority since [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|the betrayal of Toekoe Oemar]] into a *de jure* framework of colonial governance. At the same time, the outbreak of the European war in [[1914]] inaugurates the long disruption of the colonial project that would dominate the next three decades.

## Account from *Snijders* (1944) — 1914

**[[1914]]** was the year in which [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Cornelis Jacobus Snijders]] — who, four decades earlier, had served as a young *Tweede Luitenant* of the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and had received his baptism of fire at the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam operation]] of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] — was appointed [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht]] (Commander-in-Chief of the Netherlands Land and Sea Forces) on the outbreak of the [[First World War]] in [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]]. Snijders had already been serving as [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chef van de Generale Staf]] since [[1910]]; the wartime appointment concentrated in his hands the supreme command of the Dutch armed forces throughout the four years of Dutch neutrality.

The biographer D. van den Berg notes that Snijders' own unfinished memoirs — the *Herinneringen uit mijn leven*, written in the third person — were completed only to [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]], the very month of his appointment as Opperbevelhebber, the moment at which the private memoir of a retired-officer-in-waiting gave way to the public record of the commander of a neutral state's armed forces in wartime. The arc that had opened on [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] with the young lieutenant directing coolies under fire at [[Kuta Alam|Kota Alam]] thus closes, in the biographer's framing, on [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]]: the formative experience of colonial field engineering carried forward into the supreme command of the metropolitan army in the European crisis.

## Account from the *Notulen der bestuursvergaderingen* (1914) — 1914

**1914** was the year in which the second phase of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] concluded with meetings at Medan ([[13 January]]–[[15 January]]) and [[Koeta-Radja]] ([[19 January]]–[[20 January]]). The Koeta-Radja meetings were the only sessions held within the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] and produced the key conclusions that Atjeh could only receive [[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]] and would be absorbed into the new [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]]. These conclusions represented the definitive outcome of the reform process as it pertained to Atjeh, resolving questions that had been raised during the 1913 consultations and establishing the administrative framework that would govern the territory's relationship to the broader Sumatran governance structure. The year 1914 thus marked a turning point in Atjeh's colonial administration, as the decisions taken at Koeta-Radja would shape the territory's governance for years to come.

The significance of the 1914 meetings cannot be overstated. While the 1913 meetings had established general principles and identified key issues, the 1914 meetings were where specific decisions were made about the administrative future of northern Sumatra. The Medan meetings, presided over by Resident [[Van der Plas]], addressed the detailed administrative division of the proposed Noord-Sumatra gewest, including the partition of Atjeh into several afdeelingen and the relationship between the various administrative units within the new regional structure. The Koeta-Radja meetings, presided over by Gouverneur [[H. N. N. A. Swart]], focused specifically on Atjeh's unique governance challenges and produced the conclusions that distinguished Atjeh's treatment from that of the other Buitenbezittingen.

## People

- [[H. N. N. A. Swart]] — Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh who presided at Koeta-Radja and provided the authoritative explanations of Atjeh's indigenous administrative structure, including the [[sagi]]-[[peleebalangschap]]-[[moekimschap]] hierarchy, the scope of the rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied, and the practice of erfelijkheid in head appointments. Swart's contributions were decisive in shaping De Graaff's conclusions about the appropriate form of decentralisatie for Atjeh.
- [[S. de Graaff]] — Regeeringscommissaris who concluded that Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur was the only viable option for Atjeh, given the coexistence of directly ruled and self-governing territories within the Gouvernement. De Graaff's conclusion effectively placed Atjeh in a separate category from the other Buitenbezittingen, where local zelfbestuur through autonome lichamen was considered feasible.
- [[Van der Plas]] — Resident who proposed the Noord-Sumatra administrative division at the Medan meetings, including the detailed partition of Atjeh into afdeelingen and the assignment of administrative responsibilities within the new gewest. Van der Plas's proposals reflected the practical administrative thinking that informed the reform process.
- [[C. Lulofs]] — Waarnemend Adviseur who asked about Atjeh hoofd appointments, specifically whether the population proposed candidates or whether they were nominated by the European Bestuur. Lulofs's question prompted Swart's important explanation of the evolving relationship between erfelijkheid and bekwaamheden in the appointment process.
- [[G. J. van Dongen]] — Controleur who asked about Inlandsche ambtenaren in the Gewestelijke Raad, raising the question of whether indigenous officials would be granted seats in the proposed regional council. Van Dongen's question highlighted the tension between the desire to incorporate indigenous perspectives into the new governance structures and the colonial administration's reluctance to grant full participatory rights.

## Places

- [[Koeta-Radja]] — Venue of the final Atjeh meetings and the capital of the Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, where the most consequential discussions of the reform process took place. The choice of Koeta-Radja as the meeting venue was symbolically significant, as it placed the discussions within the territory whose governance was being reformed.
- [[Groot-Atjeh]] — Key area discussed as rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied (directly ruled territory), constituting one of only two parts of the Gouvernement under direct Dutch administrative control. The governance structure of Groot-Atjeh, with its three-tier [[sagi]]-[[peleebalangschap]]-[[moekimschap]] hierarchy, was the primary subject of Swart's explanations.
- [[Pidië]] — Onderafdeeling represented at the meetings by Civiel Gezaghebber Majoor [[Th. J. Veltman]], reflecting the continued militarization of governance in this strategically important region.
- [[Tamiang]] — Onderafdeeling represented at the meetings by Civiel Gezaghebber [[W. Wessel]], another area where military officers served in civil administrative positions.
- [[Singkel]] — Onderafdeeling whose partial inclusion in the rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied was noted by Gouverneur Swart, reflecting the incomplete nature of Dutch administrative penetration in this remote coastal area.

## Events

- [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] — Concluding phase with Koeta-Radja meetings, which produced the definitive conclusions about Atjeh's administrative future within the proposed Noord-Sumatra gewest. The reform process that had begun in August 1913 reached its culmination in January 1914.
- [[Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen (19-20 January 1914)]] — The Atjeh-specific meetings that produced the key conclusions about [[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]] and Atjeh's incorporation into Noord-Sumatra. These meetings were attended by Gouverneur Swart, Regeeringscommissaris De Graaff, and numerous local officials including Civiel Gezaghebbers and Controleurs.
- [[Atjeh-Sumatra's Oostkust merger proposal]] — Confirmed with unanimous approval, resolving one of the key questions that had been raised during the 1913 consultations. The merger would bring Atjeh and Sumatra's Oostkust together within the new Noord-Sumatra gewest.

## Publication of Proceedings

The published proceedings, *Notulen der bestuursvergaderingen ter bespreking van de verdere voorbereiding eener hervorming van het bestuurswezen in Nederlandsch-Indië*, were printed by the Landsdrukkerij in Batavia in 1914. This publication made the complete record of the reform discussions—including the minutes of all meetings from Palembang to Koeta-Radja—available to officials and interested parties throughout the Netherlands East Indies. The Notulen constitute the primary source for the reform process and provide invaluable documentation of the colonial administration's thinking about governance, decentralisatie, and the particular challenges posed by Atjeh's unique administrative and political circumstances.

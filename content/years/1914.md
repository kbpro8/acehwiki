**[[1914]]** was the year of a series of administrative reorganisations in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] — the dissolution of the [[Kawaj XII|Kawaj XII]] federation on the Pedir highlands, the regularisation of the territorial boundaries of the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], the relocation of the *Kédjoerön* of [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] to [[Poeloe Nas|Poeloe Nas]], and the redrawing of the boundary between [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]] — that together represent the high-water mark of the Dutch policy of consolidating the *landschap* structure of the territory into regular territorial units amenable to indirect rule.

## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1914

**1914** marked a transitional period in the colonial administration of [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. As the outbreak of the First World War began to disrupt global trade and colonial supply lines, the Dutch authorities in [[Sumatra]] were finalizing long-term budgetary projections and consolidating the pacification efforts that had defined the previous decade of the [[Aceh War]].

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The year [[1914]] serves as the terminal date and publication context for the *Atjeh up to date* statistical compilation. The surviving ledgers from this year shift from recording immediate military pacification costs to projecting long-term administrative and infrastructure expenditures:

*   **Budgetary Projections:** Colonial ledgers outline a multi-year spending plan extending from [[1914]] through [[1917]]. Initial allocations for [[1914]] are recorded at 20,000 to 50,000 guilders for specific administrative or infrastructural projects, with projected costs scaling significantly in the subsequent years.
*   **Administrative Consolidation:** The data reflects a colony moving away from the extreme, unpredictable costs of active guerrilla warfare toward standardized, predictable colonial governance. The financial overhead of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons was being absorbed into routine provincial budgets.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** While global shipping networks were severely impacted by the war in Europe, the internal logistical registries of [[Acheh]] continued to track the movement of export commodities and colonial supplies, maintaining the bureaucratic frameworks established in [[1908]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1914

**[[1914]]** was the closing year of the decade surveyed by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau in its volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914*. According to the Bureau's own framing, [[1914]] was doubly significant: it was the year of the Koloniale Tentoonstelling (Colonial Exhibition) at [[Semarang]] — the occasion that prompted the writing of the volume itself — and it was the year in which "the circumstances" (i.e., the outbreak of the European war) "exerted their influence over the whole of the Dutch East Indies," so that the Exhibition "was by no means as busy as expected, and as it indeed deserved." The Bureau's volume was thus conceived as a remedy for the failure of the Exhibition to disseminate knowledge of the Buitenbezittingen — including [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — to the wider public.

On the administrative side, [[1914]] saw the promulgation of the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie (Self-Government Ordinance)]] (Stbl. 1914 No. 24) — the ordinance "whose provisions mainly correspond with the provisions of the detailed political contracts." The ordinance codified the rights, competences, and obligations of the Gouvernement on one hand and of the [[Zelfbesturen|self-governing territories]] on the other under the regime of the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] that had been in force since [[1899]]. For [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — where, with the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] complete, the Dutch administrative system had reached its fullest post-[[1896]] development — the [[1914]] ordinance represented the formal legal consolidation of the new order.

The [[1914]] ordinance was accompanied, in the same year, by the Inlandsche gemeente-ordonnanties for [[Amboina]] and [[Sumatra's Westkust]] (Stbl. 1914 No. 629 en 774) — ordinances regulating the household affairs of the Inlandsche gemeenten (native communities). These ordinances, modelled on the [[1906]] Java and Madura regulations, marked the beginning of a parallel process of legal consolidation at the village level.

## Significance

[[1914]] closes the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" for the Buitenbezittingen and Atjeh. The [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] of [[1914]] is the legal capstone of the post-[[1896]] reorientation in Atjeh: the ordinance that translated the *de facto* extension of Dutch authority since [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|the betrayal of Toekoe Oemar]] into a *de jure* framework of colonial governance. At the same time, the outbreak of the European war in [[1914]] inaugurates the long disruption of the colonial project that would dominate the next three decades.

## Account from *Snijders* (1944) — 1914

**[[1914]]** was the year in which [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Cornelis Jacobus Snijders]] — who, four decades earlier, had served as a young *Tweede Luitenant* of the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and had received his baptism of fire at the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam operation]] of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] — was appointed [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht]] (Commander-in-Chief of the Netherlands Land and Sea Forces) on the outbreak of the [[First World War]] in [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]]. Snijders had already been serving as [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chef van de Generale Staf]] since [[1910]]; the wartime appointment concentrated in his hands the supreme command of the Dutch armed forces throughout the four years of Dutch neutrality.

The biographer D. van den Berg notes that Snijders' own unfinished memoirs — the *Herinneringen uit mijn leven*, written in the third person — were completed only to [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]], the very month of his appointment as Opperbevelhebber, the moment at which the private memoir of a retired-officer-in-waiting gave way to the public record of the commander of a neutral state's armed forces in wartime. The arc that had opened on [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] with the young lieutenant directing coolies under fire at [[Kuta Alam|Kota Alam]] thus closes, in the biographer's framing, on [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]]: the formative experience of colonial field engineering carried forward into the supreme command of the metropolitan army in the European crisis.

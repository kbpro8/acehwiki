**[[1914]]** was the year of a series of administrative reorganisations in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] — the dissolution of the [[Kawaj XII|Kawaj XII]] federation on the Pedir highlands, the regularisation of the territorial boundaries of the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], the relocation of the *Kédjoerön* of [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] to [[Poeloe Nas|Poeloe Nas]], and the redrawing of the boundary between [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]] — that together represent the high-water mark of the Dutch policy of consolidating the *landschap* structure of the territory into regular territorial units amenable to indirect rule.

## Account from *Atjeh up to date* (c. 1914) — 1914

**1914** marked a transitional period in the colonial administration of [[Acheh]], situated within the [[Dutch East Indies]]. As the outbreak of the First World War began to disrupt global trade and colonial supply lines, the Dutch authorities in [[Sumatra]] were finalizing long-term budgetary projections and consolidating the pacification efforts that had defined the previous decade of the [[Aceh War]].

## Statistical and Administrative Record

The year [[1914]] serves as the terminal date and publication context for the *Atjeh up to date* statistical compilation. The surviving ledgers from this year shift from recording immediate military pacification costs to projecting long-term administrative and infrastructure expenditures:

*   **Budgetary Projections:** Colonial ledgers outline a multi-year spending plan extending from [[1914]] through [[1917]]. Initial allocations for [[1914]] are recorded at 20,000 to 50,000 guilders for specific administrative or infrastructural projects, with projected costs scaling significantly in the subsequent years.
*   **Administrative Consolidation:** The data reflects a colony moving away from the extreme, unpredictable costs of active guerrilla warfare toward standardized, predictable colonial governance. The financial overhead of maintaining the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] garrisons was being absorbed into routine provincial budgets.
*   **Trade and Logistics:** While global shipping networks were severely impacted by the war in Europe, the internal logistical registries of [[Acheh]] continued to track the movement of export commodities and colonial supplies, maintaining the bureaucratic frameworks established in [[1908]].

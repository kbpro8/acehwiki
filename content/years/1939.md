**[[1939]]** was the year covered by [[H. van Meurs|H. van Meurs]]'s fourth annual *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht]]* of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië|Koninklijk Instituut]], and the year of publication of the principal Atjeh-focused works recorded in that survey's Sumatra chapter (Hoofdstuk III, Nos. 548–658) and Sumatra Addenda (Nos. 1056–1062). It was the last full year of peace before the [[Japanese invasion of Atjeh|Japanese invasion of the Netherlands Indies]] of [[1942]], and a year of intense retrospective and contemporary scholarly engagement with Atjeh.

## Atjeh-focused publications of 1939

The Atjeh-focused publications of [[1939]], as recorded by Van Meurs, include:

- **[[Grepen uit den Atjeh-oorlog (Bannink, 1939)|No. 550]]** — [[J. J. C. A. Bannink|Bannink]]'s excerpts from the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] on General [[K. van der Heyden|van der Heyden]]'s removal from Atjeh, in the *Orgaan van de Nederlandsch-Indische Officiersvereeniging*.
- **[[Kleiland-aanwinning op Atjeh's Westkust in 1902 (Brandhof, 1939)|No. 557]]** — [[J. L. M. van den Brandhof|Brandhof]]'s note on land reclamation on [[Atjeh's Westkust|Atjeh's Westkust]] in [[1902]], in *De Indische Gids*.
- **[[De Padrie-oorlog (Costa, 1939)|No. 558]]** — [[S. da Costa|da Costa]]'s survey of the [[Padri War on Sumatra's East Coast|Padrie-oorlog]] on [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]], in the *Orgaan van de Nederlandsch-Indische Officiersvereeniging* and in *Ons Aller Belang*.
- **[[De legende van de heilige zeven slapers in het Atjehsch (Damsté, 1939)|No. 560]]** — [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]]'s edition of the [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Seven Sleepers legend in Atjehsch]], in *B.K.I.* deel 98, with the separate text **[[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|No. 570]]**.
- **[[De gevangeneming van den ex-pretendent Sultan van Atjeh, Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat (Doorman, 1939)|No. 563]]** — [[R. G. Doorman|Doorman]]'s notice of the death of the ex-pretender Sultan [[Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat|Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]] on [[7 February]] [[1939]], in *De Indische Gids*.
- **[[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo-, Alas- en Bataklanden (Geusau, 1939)|No. 566]]** — [[J. Alting van Geusau|Geusau]]'s account of the [[Colonne van Daalen naar de Gajolanden en Alaslanden (1904)|van Daalen campaign of 1904]], in *Indisch Militair Tijdschrift*.
- **[[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo-, Alas- en Bataklanden 1904 (Harders, 1939)|No. 568]]** — [[J. E. Harders|Harders]]'s account of the same campaign, in the *Orgaan van de Nederlandsch-Indische Officiersvereeniging*.
- **[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|No. 571]]** — [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]]'s survey of the land and people of Atjeh, past and present, published at [[Baarn|Baarn]] with maps and illustrations.
- **[[Taboet- of Hasji-Hosseinfeest (Jongejans, 1939)|No. 571a]]** — [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]]'s illustrated note on the Taboet or Hasji-Hossein festival, in *Onze Aarde*.
- **No. 572** — [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]]'s illustrated note on the sanctuary at [[Oelakan|Oelakan]], in *Onze Aarde*.
- **[[Bij het derde eeuwfeest van de martelaren van Atjeh (1138-1938)|No. 581]]** — [[Luc. van der Linden|Luc. van der Linden, O. Carm.]]'s commemoration of the third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs (1138–1938), in *Het Missiewerk*.
- **[[In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes (Tichelmann, 1939)|No. 625]]** — [[G. L. Tichelmann|Tichelmann]]'s illustrated note on glass vessels excavated in Atjeh, in *De Natuur*.
- **[[Uit de missiegebieden van Sumatra: Atjeh in de 16e en 17e eeuw (Wessels, 1939)|No. 652]]** — [[C. Wessels|Wessels]]'s survey of Atjeh in the 16th and 17th centuries from the mission fields of Sumatra, in *Historisch Tijdschrift*, vol. XVIII.
- **[[Bij het derde eeuwfeest van de martelaren van Atjeh (1138-1938)|No. 1059]]** — the Addenda duplicate of No. 581, giving the author's name as *Linden O. CARM., LUC. VAN DER*.

## The death of the last pretender Sultan

[[7 February]] [[1939]] — the date recorded in [[R. G. Doorman|Doorman]]'s article (No. 563) — was the date of death, at [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester-Cornelis]] (now [[Jatinegara|Jatinegara]], a suburb of [[Batavia|Batavia]]), of the ex-pretender Sultan [[Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat|Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]], after thirty-two years of exile dating from [[1907]]. His death marks the close of the line of Atjehnese pretender-sultans in the colonial period, and it is the terminus ad quem of the colonial-period Atjehnese sultanate in the corpus.

## The third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs

[[1938]] — the year immediately preceding the survey year — was the year of the *derde eeuwfeest* (third centenary) of the **martelaren van Atjeh** — the martyrs of Atjeh — commemorated by [[Luc. van der Linden|Luc. van der Linden, O. Carm.]] in *Het Missiewerk* (Nos. 581, 1059). The commemoration, observed in [[1938]] and recorded in [[1939]], placed the Catholic missionary martyrology of Atjeh in the public eye of Dutch Catholic readers in the last full year before the war.

## The pre-war context

[[1939]] was the last full year of peace before the [[Japanese invasion of Atjeh|Japanese invasion of the Netherlands Indies]] of [[1942]]. The scholarly activity recorded in Van Meurs's survey — the retrospective military-historical writing on the Atjeh-oorlog, the editions of Atjehsch vernacular texts, the surveys of the land and people of Atjeh, the commemorations of the Catholic missionary martyrs — was the work of a colonial scholarly establishment that would, within three years, be dispersed by the Japanese occupation and, within six, be superseded by the Indonesian revolution. The [[1939]] survey is thus a document of the last moment of the Dutch colonial scholarly order on Atjeh, and the publications it records are the last harvest of that order before the colonial state was ended.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, [[1939]] is significant as the year covered by Van Meurs's fourth annual Literatuur-Overzicht — the principal bibliographic record of the pre-war state of scholarship on Atjeh — and as the year of publication of the principal Atjeh-focused works recorded in that survey. The year's publications span the retrospective military-historical (Bannink, Geusau, Harders), the ethnographic and literary (Damsté, Jongejans), the antiquarian (Tichelmann), the martyrological (van der Linden), and the mission-historical (Wessels), and they include the record of the death of the last Atjehnese pretender Sultan. The year is, in the corpus, the terminus ad quem of the colonial-period Atjehnese sultanate and the last full year of the Dutch colonial scholarly order on Atjeh.

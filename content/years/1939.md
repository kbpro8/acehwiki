**[[1939]]** was the year covered by [[H. van Meurs|H. van Meurs]]’s fourth annual *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur‑Overzicht]]* of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koninklijk Instituut]], and also the year in which the *[[Indisch Staatsblad]]* published the regulation **Regeling op den Staat van Oorlog** (War State Regulation) No. 1939/582, later brought into force on [[10 May]] [[1940]] as part of the legal framework for the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] during the looming conflict of World II. The dual record of scholarly output on [[Atjeh]] and colonial military legislation makes [[1939]] a pivotal snapshot of the last full year of Dutch colonial scholarly and administrative activity before the Japanese invasion of the [[Netherlands Indies|Netherlands Indies]] in [[1942]].

## Atjeh‑focused publications of [[1939]]

The Atjeh‑focused publications recorded by Van Meurs in the Sumatra chapter (Hoofdstuk III, Nos. 548–658) and the Sumatra Addenda (Nos. 1056–1062) include:

- **[[Grepen uit den Atjeh‑oorlog (Bannink, 1939)|No. 550]]** – [[J. J. C. A. Bannink|Bannink]]’s excerpts from the [[Aceh War|Atjeh‑oorlog]] concerning General [[K. van der Heyden|van der Heyden]]’s removal from Atjeh, published in the *Orgaan van de Nederlandsch‑Indische Officiersvereeniging*.
- **[[Kleiland‑aanwinning op Atjeh's Westkust in 1902 (Brandhof, 1939)|No. 557]]** – [[J. L. M. van den Brandhof|Brandhof]]’s note on land reclamation on [[Atjeh's Westkust|Atjeh’s Westkust]] in [[1902]], appearing in *De Indische Gids*.
- **[[De Padrie‑oorlog (Costa, 1939)|No. 558]]** – [[S. da Costa|da Costa]]’s survey of the [[Padri War|Padrie‑oorlog]] on [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra’s Westkust]], published in the *Orgaan van de Nederlandsch‑Indische Officiersvereeniging* and in *Ons Aller Belang*.
- **[[De legende van de heilige zeven slapers in het Atjehsch (Damsté, 1939)|No. 560]]** – [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]]’s edition of the *[[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Seven Sleepers legend in Atjehsch]]*, in *B.K.I.* deel 98, with the separate text **[[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|No. 570]]**.
- **[[De gevangeneming van den ex‑pretendent Sultan van Atjeh, Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat (Doorman, 1939)|No. 563]]** – [[R. G. Doorman|Doorman]]’s notice of the death of the ex‑pretender Sultan [[Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat|Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]] on [[7 February]] [[1939]], in *De Indische Gids*.
- **[[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo‑, Alas‑ en Bataklanden (Geusau, 1939)|No. 566]]** – [[J. Alting van Geusau|Geusau]]’s account of the [[Colonne van Daalen naar de Gajolanden en Alaslanden (1904)|van Daalen campaign of [[1904]], in *Indisch Militair Tijdschrift*.
- **[[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo‑, Alas‑ en Bataklanden 1904 (Harders, 1939)|No. 568]]** – [[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo-, Alas- en Bataklanden 1904 (Harders, 1939)|Harders]]’s parallel account of the same campaign, in the *Orgaan van de Nederlandsch‑Indische Officiersvereeniging*.
- **[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|No. 571]]** – [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]]’s survey of the land and people of Atjeh, past and present, published at [[Baarn|Baarn]] with maps and illustrations.
- **[[Taboet‑ of Hasji‑Hosseinfeest (Jongejans, 1939)|No. 571a]]** – [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]]’s illustrated note on the Taboet or Hasji‑Hossein festival, in *Onze Aarde*.
- **No. 572** – [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]]’s illustrated note on the sanctuary at [[Oelakan|Oelakan]], in *Onze Aarde*.
- **[[Bij het derde eeuwfeest van de martelaren van Atjeh (1138‑1938)|No. 581]]** – [[Luc. van der Linden|Luc. van der Linden, O. Carm.]]’s commemoration of the third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs (1138–[[1938]]), in *Het Missiewerk*.
- **[[In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes (Tichelmann, 1939)|No. 625]]** – [[G. L. Tichelmann|Tichelmann]]’s illustrated note on glass vessels excavated in Atjeh, in *De Natuur*.
- **[[Uit de missiegebieden van Sumatra: Atjeh in de 16e en 17e eeuw (Wessels, 1939)|No. 652]]** – [[C. Wessels|Wessels]]’s survey of Atjeh in the 16th and 17th centuries from the mission fields of Sumatra, in *Historisch Tijdschrift*, vol. XVIII.
- **[[Bij het derde eeuwfeest van de martelaren van Atjeh (1138‑1938)|No. 1059]]** – the Addenda duplicate of No. 581, giving the author’s name as *Linden O. CARM., LUC. VAN DER*.

## Legal and military context of [[1939]]

The *[[Indisch Staatsblad]]* of [[1939]] issued the **Regeling op den Staat van Oorlog** (War State Regulation) No. 1939/582. The regulation outlined mobilisation procedures, command hierarchy, and emergency powers for the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] in anticipation of hostilities in the East Indies. Although the statute remained dormant in [[1939]], it was activated on [[10 May]] [[1940]], becoming a cornerstone of Dutch defensive policy during the subsequent [[Japanese invasion of the Netherlands Indies|Japanese invasion of the Netherlands Indies]] that began in [[1942]].

## The death of the last pretender Sultan

[[7 February]] [[1939]] – the date recorded in [[R. G. Doorman|Doorman]]’s article (No. 563) – marked the death of the ex‑pretender Sultan [[Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat|Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]] at [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester‑Cornelis]] (now [[Jatinegara|Jatinegara]], a suburb of [[Batavia|Batavia]]). After thirty‑two years of exile since [[1907]], his passing signalled the terminus ad quem of the line of Atjehnese pretender‑sultans in the colonial period and closed a chapter of the colonial‑period Atjehnese sultanate.

## The third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs

[[1938]] – the year immediately preceding the survey year – saw the *derde eeuwfeest* (third centenary) of the **martelaren van Atjeh** (martyrs of Atjeh). The commemoration, recorded by [[Luc. van der Linden|Luc. van der Linden, O. Carm.]] in *Het Missiewerk* (Nos. 581, 1059), placed the Catholic missionary martyrology of Atjeh in the public eye of Dutch Catholic readers during the last full pre‑war year.

## Pre‑war context

[[1939]] was the final full year of peace before the [[Japanese invasion of Atjeh|Japanese invasion of the Netherlands Indies]] of [[1942]]. The scholarly activity documented in Van Meurs’s survey – retrospective military‑historical writing on the Atjeh‑oorlog, editions of Atjehsch vernacular texts, ethnographic surveys, antiquarian notes, and martyrological commemorations – represented the output of a colonial scholarly establishment that would be dispersed by the Japanese occupation and later superseded by the Indonesian revolution. Simultaneously, the issuance of the War State Regulation reflected the colonial administration’s anticipation of conflict, a foresight that would become operative in [[1940]] and crucial during the 1942 invasion.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, [[1939]] is significant on two interlocking fronts. First, it is the year covered by Van Meurs’s fourth annual *Literatuur‑Overzicht*, the principal bibliographic record of pre‑war scholarship on Atjeh, encompassing military, ethnographic, literary, antiquarian, and missionary studies. Second, the year marks the publication of the War State Regulation in the *Indisch Staatsblad*, a legal instrument that would shape Dutch defensive strategy in the East Indies during World II. Together, these strands illustrate the culmination of Dutch colonial scholarly and administrative activity just before the upheavals of the early 1940s.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In **[[1939]]** [[T. Radja Tampo']] was still wandering in the marshes of [[Seuneu'am]] and [[Tripa]] as "a legendary figure, holy in the eyes of the population", never betrayed by anyone. [[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] took the years [[1918]]–[[1939]] as proof that [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s judgement of Swart had been mistaken.

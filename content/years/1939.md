**The year 1939** was, in Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan*, the year of the founding of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] — the *Persatoean Oelama-Oelama Seleroeh Atjèh*, the "All-Aceh Union of Ulemas" — at [[Bireuën|Bireuën]], under the high patronage of [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]] of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]]. The founding of the Poesa was the institutional birth of the religious movement that, more than any other, would shape the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion]] of [[1942]] and the social revolution that followed the [[Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945|capitulation]] of [[1945]].

## The founding of the Poesa

The [[Poesa|Poesa]] was "founded in 1939 in Bireuën under the high patronage of Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah, self-governing authority of Peusangan. It was intended as a counterweight to the modern currents of the [[Muhammadijah|Muhammadijah]] movement, which were foreign to Acehnese religious life." The new organisation was to be "the bearer of the typically Acehnese, fanatically orthodox religious life," in which "the Acehnese oelama's should find an organization in which they, as like-minded persons, could jointly direct, purify, and keep the Acehnese religious life on the right track." The reputation of Atjèh as "*sramoë Meukah*" — "forecourt of Mecca" — "would be revived by the Poesa." Piekaar insists that, at its founding, "a political slant, let alone an anti-Dutch tendency, seemed foreign to this plan."

## Other events of the year

1939 was also the year in which [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]] — then adjunct-referendaris at the regional office in [[Medan|Medan]] — "established [a study fund] in Medan in 1939 after his appointment there," a fund whose branches "were spread throughout Atjèh." [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Hasan]] would, six years later, be appointed republican Governor of Sumatra. The pretender-sultan [[Toeankoe Mahmoed|Toeankoe Mahmoed]] was reappointed to his Volksraad mandate in 1939 — "the various groups in the oelèëbalang party, albeit temporarily, forgot their feuds and Great-Atjèh and Pidië jointly emerged with one or more counter-candidates," though the government "overlooked the objections." The year also saw the publication of Resident [[Jongejans|Jongejans]]'s *Land en volk van Atjèh, vroeger en nu*, which Piekaar cites as the standard authority on pre-war Atjèh, and which recorded the last "available" export figures for the colonial-era economy.

## The wider arc

The year also closed a long arc of administrative demobilisation. The road connection with [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] had only "been completed at the end of 1939 on the initiative of the then Assistant Resident of the East Coast of Atjèh, Mr. Th. O. B. Gunther" — the road that would, in [[March]] [[1942]], be the route of the highland retreat to the final Dutch surrender. The German invasion of Poland had, in [[September]] [[1939]], driven four German merchant ships to seek refuge in the bay of [[Sabang|Sabang]] — the first distant echo of the European war that would, within two years, merge into the Pacific.

## Significance

1939 thus stands as the hinge year of pre-war Atjèh: the year that produced both the religious movement (the [[Poesa|Poesa]]) and the political figure ([[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]]) that would dominate the next decade. Piekaar's reading of the founding of the Poesa is, characteristically, cautious: "a political slant, let alone an anti-Dutch tendency, seemed foreign to this plan" — yet within fourteen days of the [[8 December|8 December]] [[1941]] outbreak of the Pacific War, "the board of the Pusa decided … to cooperate with Japan in order to expel the Dutch."

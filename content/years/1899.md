## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1899

**[[1899]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] — "On an excursion on 22 July 1899 the third Battalion made an attack" — as one of the years of the late-19th-century Dutch offensive in Aceh, the offensive that would culminate in the [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-era colonnes of the early 1900s.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1899

**[[1899]]** was a doubly significant year in the history of Dutch rule in [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. On the military side, it was the year in which the construction of a second tramway in the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]] was begun — the line that would, between [[1899]] and [[1906]], be successively extended from [[Sigli]] to the harbour of [[Langsa]], and from which a western branch was already in [[1899]] built from [[Sigli]] over [[Padang Tiji|Padang Tidji]] to [[Keude Breue]]. This second line, together with the [[1897]] long-line, formed the twin axes of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network as it expanded outward from [[Kota Radja]] into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]].

On the administrative side, [[1899]] was the year that marked a turning point in the regulation of the relationship between the [[Zelfbesturen|self-governing territories]] and the Dutch Gouvernement throughout the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]]. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau, until [[1899]] the relationship had been expressed in "detailed political contracts" (*omstandige politieke contracten*) in which the principles of governance, the mutual rights, obligations, and competences of the parties were all set out. After [[1899]], however, the Gouvernement shifted to a different instrument: the so-called [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring (Short Declaration)]] — a brief declaration, signed by the native ruler and approved by the Gouvernement, that recognised Dutch supremacy, promised to abstain from political contacts with foreign powers, and pledged obedience to all regulations and orders issued by or on behalf of the [[Gouverneur-Generaal|Governor-General]]. The shift affected all of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] including Atjeh, where the long political-military campaign had created the conditions in which the simpler instrument could now be applied.

## Significance

[[1899]] thus stands at the intersection of two parallel transformations in Atjeh. The beginning of the second tramway in the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] marks the geographic expansion of the military occupation; the introduction of the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] marks the administrative consolidation of Dutch authority over the self-governing territories. Both moves are part of the single post-[[1896]] project of bringing Atjeh — and the wider Buitenbezittingen — under Dutch "effective authority."

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1899

### The Mining Law of 1899

For the full account of the Mining Law of [[1899]] see the entry on [[Netherlands Indies Mining Law of 1899]]. In summary: the Law's state-mineral-rights principle — under which the mineral wealth of the country belongs to the entire community, and the right to develop and exploit this wealth can be granted only by the Government — distinguished the East Indian mineral-property regime from its Anglo-American equivalents and shaped the structure of the colonial mining concessions, the corporate organisation of the oil industry, and the technical development of the East Indian oil fields. The Law's principal consequences for the oil industry were the avoidance of concession fragmentation and the single-company development of each geological structure, both of which favoured the dominance of the [[B.P.M.]] and the other major oil companies.

### Application to Atjeh

The Mining Law of [[1899]] applied to the [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency from the date of its enactment. The colonial mining concessions granted in Atjeh under the Law — the [[1909]] [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] coal leases at Bukit Durian, Bukit Pulai and Sarik, and the [[1940]] [[M.A.E.M.]] Geudong Concession for gold-dredging — were accordingly granted under the Law's state-mineral-rights principle. The Atjeh oil fields — the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh block]], the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]] and the [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields — were also developed under the Law's provisions, and the dominance of the [[B.P.M.]] and the other major oil companies in the Atjeh oil industry is, in part, a consequence of the Law's state-mineral-rights principle.

### Significance

[[1899]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of the enactment of the Mining Law of [[1899]] — the legal foundation on which the entire colonial mineral economy of Atjeh was built. The Law's state-mineral-rights principle shaped the structure of the colonial mining concessions, the corporate organisation of the oil industry, and the technical development of the East Indian oil fields, and the Atjeh oil fields, coal concessions and gold-dredging concessions were all granted under the Law's provisions. The Law is accordingly the indispensable legal context for any understanding of the colonial mineral economy of Atjeh, and [[1899]] is the year in which that legal context was established.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] records the death of [[Teukoe Oemar]] — the *Heer der westelijke zee* — in [[February]] [[1899]] near [[Meulaboh]]. News of his death travelled through the Atjehsche *chabar angin* — *de Atjehsche boodschappers loopen als hazen en geven bij elke keudé het bericht aan een ander over* — covering up to eighty kilometres a day, reaching both the staff at [[Koetaradja]] and far-off [[Sigli]] within days. Zentgraff also notes the *omsingeling* of the post at Meureudoe that year, prompting a forced march of eight maréchaussée brigades from Pidië to Panté Radja.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1899** was the year of the *[[Korte Verklaring (1899)|Korte Verklaring]]* — *the Short Declaration* — by which the colonial government replaced the older *[[Groote Verklaring|Groote Verklaring]]* in eighteen articles with a short declaration in three articles, which the *[[Oeléebalang|oeléebalangs]]* of the Atjehnese *landschappen* were required to sign as the formal token of their submission to the Dutch government. [[Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih|Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih]] of [[Peudawa Rajeu|Peudawa Rajeu]] signed the *Korte Verklaring* in [[1899]]. In the same year he received the small star of *trouw en verdienste*.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

**[[1899]]** was the year of the [[Pasè expedition (1899)|Pasè expedition]] under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]], in which captain [[H. N. A. Swart]] crossed the swollen [[Pasè-rivier]] at [[Geudong]] on [[21 June]] and earned the third class of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]]. Swart was wounded at the storming of [[Bambi]] on [[8 May]] and on the [[Glé Risé]] on [[21 November]]. Administrative errors of [[1899]] excluded [[T. Tjhi' Toenòng]] from the succession in [[Keureutòë]], setting in train the drama of [[Tjoet Meutia]] and [[T. Radja Sabi]].

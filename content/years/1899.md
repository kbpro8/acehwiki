## Account from *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* (1954) — 1899

**[[1899]]** is mentioned in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in connection with the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] — "Bij een excursie deed op [[22 Juli]] [[1899]] het [[Derde Bataljon (Atjeh-Bataljon)|derde Bataljon]] een aanval" — as one of the years of the late-19th-century Dutch offensive in Aceh, the offensive that would culminate in the [[Governor Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-era colonnes of the early 1900s.

*Source: Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, by G. W. T. Dames, Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d. ('s-Gravenhage: Koninklijke Bibliotheek, 1954).

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — 1899

**[[1899]]** was a doubly significant year in the history of Dutch rule in [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. On the military side, it was the year in which the construction of a second tramway in the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]] was begun — the line that would, between [[1899]] and [[1906]], be successively extended from [[Sigli]] to the harbour of [[Langsa]], and from which a western branch was already in [[1899]] built from [[Sigli]] over [[Padang Tidji]] to [[Keude Breue]]. This second line, together with the [[1897]] long-line, formed the twin axes of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network as it expanded outward from [[Kota Radja]] into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]].

On the administrative side, [[1899]] was the year that marked a turning point in the regulation of the relationship between the [[Zelfbesturen|self-governing territories]] and the Dutch Gouvernement throughout the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]]. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau, until [[1899]] the relationship had been expressed in "detailed political contracts" (*omstandige politieke contracten*) in which the principles of governance, the mutual rights, obligations, and competences of the parties were all set out. After [[1899]], however, the Gouvernement shifted to a different instrument: the so-called [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring (Short Declaration)]] — a brief declaration, signed by the native ruler and approved by the Gouvernement, that recognised Dutch supremacy, promised to abstain from political contacts with foreign powers, and pledged obedience to all regulations and orders issued by or on behalf of the [[Gouverneur-Generaal|Governor-General]]. The shift affected all of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] including Atjeh, where the long political-military campaign had created the conditions in which the simpler instrument could now be applied.

## Significance

[[1899]] thus stands at the intersection of two parallel transformations in Atjeh. The beginning of the second tramway in the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] marks the geographic expansion of the military occupation; the introduction of the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] marks the administrative consolidation of Dutch authority over the self-governing territories. Both moves are part of the single post-[[1896]] project of bringing Atjeh — and the wider Buitenbezittingen — under Dutch "effective authority."

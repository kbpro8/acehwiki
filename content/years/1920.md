## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — 1920

### The Kr. Geuenteuet reserve estimate

For the full account of the [[1920]] Kr. Geuenteuet reserve estimate see the entries on [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] and [[Iron ores in Atjeh]]. In summary: the [[1920]] reserve estimate for the Kr. Geuenteuet occurrence — 2,600,000 tons of ore with an average of 58–64 % Fe — is recorded by Van Bemmelen with the additional note that "Investigation was suspended on account of low market-prices." The 58–64 % Fe grade is in the range of medium-grade iron ore, suitable for blast-furnace smelting; the gold-and-silver byproduct content (10 gr Au/ton, 33 gr Ag/ton) recorded in the [[1922]] Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp report would, in other circumstances, have made the Kr. Geuenteuet occurrence a candidate for combined iron-and-precious-metal mining.

### The 1920 N.V. Eerste Nederlandsch Indische Hoogoven Maatschappij production

[[1920]] is also the year in which the N.V. "Eerste Nederlandsch Indische Hoogoven Maatschappij" produced 288 tons of pig-iron from its concessions "Illa" and "Elvira" in the sub-district Telok Betung in the Residency of the Lampong Districts — a separate iron-production episode that is recorded in Van Bemmelen's text but that is not on Atjeh territory. The [[1920]] Lampong pig-iron production is, however, part of the wider colonial iron-metallurgy context within which the Atjeh iron-ore occurrences were assessed, and the fact that only 288 tons were produced (from a single small operation in the Lampong Districts) is itself evidence of the limited development of the colonial iron-metallurgy industry of which the Atjeh iron-ore occurrences were nominally a part.

### Significance

[[1920]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the year of the colonial-era reserve estimate for the [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] iron-ore occurrence — the largest documented colonial-era iron-ore reserve on Atjeh territory (2,600,000 tons of 58–64 % Fe ore). The [[1920]] reserve estimate is the principal quantitative basis for any assessment of the colonial iron-ore potential of Atjeh, and the "suspension [of investigation] on account of low market-prices" is the documentary evidence that the colonial state, despite its substantial investment in the geological survey of Atjeh, was unwilling or unable to translate geological reserves into productive mines. The [[1920]] Lampong pig-iron production (288 tons) is also part of the wider colonial iron-metallurgy context within which the Atjeh iron-ore occurrences were assessed, and it accordingly provides the comparative industrial context for the non-development of the Atjeh iron-ore reserves.

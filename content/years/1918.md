**[[1918]]** was a doubly pivotal year for the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader. It was, on the one hand, the year of the [[Handover of Aceh to a Civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of Aceh to a civilian Gouverneur]] by [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]] — the political closure of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] forty-five years after the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) Kreemer's account|first Aceh expedition]] of [[1873]] and seven years after the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] had destroyed the last organised Acehnese armed resistance. On the other hand, it was the year of the [[Carpentier Alting commission (1918)]] — the commission appointed under the chairmanship of Prof. Mr. [[J. H. Carpentier Alting]] to draft a revision of the constitutional foundations of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Netherlands Indies]]. The two events together — the civil-government handover in Atjeh and the constitutional-reform commission in Batavia — frame [[1918]] as the moment at which the Atjeh-derived administrative thinking of the [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] era was to be constitutionalised for the wider Indies. The same year also saw the death of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] at [[Magelang|Magelang]] in central [[Java|Java]] — ten years after his departure from Atjeh in [[1908]] — and his burial with military honours.

## The November 1918 Volksraad address and the Carpentier Alting commission

According to Mr. Dr. [[L. J. J. Caron|Caron]]'s chapter *De bestuursvormen* ("The Forms of Government") in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]), the Governor-General of the day found occasion "in November 1918 in the *Volksraad* (People's Council) to deliver an address in which he set out a programme of development for the central body of representation that would bring fundamental changes in the Indies' forms of government." To give effect to this promise, "in 1918 a commission was appointed under the chairmanship of Prof. Mr. J. H. Carpentier Alting for the revision of the foundations of government in the Netherlands Indies. This commission submitted, with its report, a draft law on the state ordering (*ontwerp-wet op de staatsordening*)."

The [[Carpentier Alting commission (1918)]]'s work was to influence the Dutch constitutional revision of [[1922]] and the eventual *Wet op de Indische Staatsregeling* ("Law on the Indies State Regulation") of [[1925]]. The 1922 revision opened the way for the [[Proeve-Oppenheim]] — the Leiden commission of professors (Oppenheim, [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], Van Vollenhoven, Carpentier Alting and Kleintjes, with the Indonesian Mr. [[Oerip Kartodirdjo]] as secretary) that produced a *proeve van een staatsregeling voor Nederlandsch-Indië* ("trial draft of a state regulation for the Netherlands Indies"). The eventual 1925 law, however, did not live up to the promise of the 1922 revision: as Caron observes, "no autonomous administrative policy was given to the Governor-General, but the Governor-General was bound, in the exercise of general government, to the King's instructions."

## The handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur

The same year [[1918]] also marked the formal end of the military government of Atjeh — the [[Handover of Aceh to a Civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of Aceh to a civil gouverneur]]. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*'s phrasing: "Daardoor kon Generaal Swart in 1918 eindelijk een rustig gebied overdragen aan een civiel Gouverneur. Er was een eind aan the lingering disease: the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Atjeh War]]." ("Thereby General Swart in 1918 could finally hand over a quiet territory to a civilian Gouverneur. There was an end to the lingering disease: the Atjeh-oorlog.") The *daardoor* — thereby — refers to the cumulative effect of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] ([[1910]]–[[1911]]), the killing of the last Tiriō leaders, and the steady pressure of the patrouilles.

### The seven-year gap

The seven-year gap between the military end of the war in [[1911]] and the political handover in [[1918]] is *Oom Ambon*'s quiet argument that the military and the political closures of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] were not the same. The colonne Schmidt had destroyed the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]]* in [[1911]]; but it took seven more years of patrouilles and political work — the surrender of *[[Panglima Polem|Panglima Prang Polim]]* to [[Hendrikus Colijn|kapitein Colijn]], the steady attrition of the remaining *onverzoenlijken* ("irreconcilables") — before the country was quiet enough to be handed over.

### The opheffing of the detailchement Leuhong

The book notes that the *detailchement Leuhong* (detachment Leuhong) was *opheffed* (dissolved) in [[1918]] — a small military detachment that had been maintained at [[Leuhong]] was withdrawn, marking the quiet end of a particular military presence. A soldier had written on [[22 August]] [[1912]] on Leuhong a note about the 1918 *opheffing* — still legible in [[1934]], twenty-two years later, on *bekas* (former) Marechaussee.

### The broader colonial context

[[1918]] was also the year of the end of the First World War in Europe. The book notes that after the First World War "de gedachte aan een buitenlandse vijand (Japan) ernstiger werd" ("the thought of a foreign enemy (Japan) became more serious") and the regular planning of service schedules began. The handover of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] to a civilian Gouverneur in [[1918]] is therefore part of the broader post-war reorganisation of the colonial army.

## The Snijders connection

[[1918]] was also the year in which [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Cornelis Jacobus Snijders]] — *Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht* ("Supreme Commander of Land and Sea Forces") since August [[1914]] and *Chef van de Generale Staf* ("Chief of the General Staff") since [[1910]] — stepped down from both offices on the conclusion of the First World War in November [[1918]]. The simultaneous closure of the European war and of the colonial Aceh War — the [[Handover of Aceh to a Civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of Aceh to a civilian Gouverneur]] by [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]] — gives the year [[1918]] a dual significance in the biographer's framing of Snijders' career: it is the year in which both the metropolitan command that he had held since August [[1914]] and the colonial war of which his [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Kota Alam action]] of [[13 November]] [[1874]] had been a small early episode were brought to their respective closures.

## The Atjeh-adjacent petroleum industry in 1918

By [[1918]], the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Royal Petroleum Company]]'s [[Pangkalan Brandan]] refinery in [[Langkat]] — immediately adjacent to Atjeh — had been the operational core of the wider Koninklijke–Shell concern for fifteen years (since the [[1903]] "Asiatic" sales-organisation merger and the [[1906]] founding of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Batavian Petroleum Company]]). The [[1911]] absorption of the last independent Indies petroleum company (the [[Dordtsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Dordrecht Petroleum Company]]) had completed the unification of the Javanese, Sumatran and Borneo petroleum industries under the [[Royal Dutch Shell]] umbrella. The formal end of the Atjeh military government in [[1918]] thus coincided with the consolidation of the Atjeh-adjacent East Coast petroleum belt as the operational core of one of the world's major petroleum multinationals.

## Account from *Na 1795* (1924) — 1918

The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] school-history textbook of F. Roorda records the [[1918]] replacement of the military administration of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] by a civil administration in the canonical chronological entry:

> *"In 1918 werd het militaire door een burgerlijk bestuur vervangen, wat de goede verstandhouding tusschen de regeering en de inlanders heeft bevorderd."*

The entry fixes the political closure of the Atjeh-oorlog in the canonical Dutch schoolroom memory: the formal end of the military government in Atjeh, and the putative improvement in relations between the colonial government and the indigenous population that the civil administration was held to have brought about.

## Account from *Vierhonderd jaren missie* (1934) — 1918

The [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume of 1934]] records the [[1918]] death of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] at [[Magelang|Magelang]] in central [[Java|Java]] in the sketch by [[S. Kalff|S. Kalff]]. Verbraak was buried with military honours — a final military recognition of the *soldatenpastoor* of Atjeh who had departed the colony ten years earlier in [[1908]]. The sketch in the jubilee volume is the principal documentary source for the place and circumstances of Verbraak's death.

## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].

**1918** is the year in which the [[Volksraad]] was finally installed in [[May 1918|May]] of that year — almost eighteen months after its proclamation in the *Staatsblad* of [[1916]]. The year marks the political upper bound of the colonial section of *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901*, the last year covered by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Dr W. H. Vermeulen]] in his *Oost-Indië in het Nederlandse Parlement 1891-1918*.

### Installation of the Volksraad

When, by the end of [[1916]], the institution of the [[Volksraad]] had been proclaimed in the *Staatsblad*, it nevertheless took until [[May 1918|May]] [[1918]] before the Volksraad was actually installed. The slowness was characteristic of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] colonial state, which "[bijna steeds iets te laat kwam, zodat het psychologisch ogenblik gemist werd]" — almost always came a little too late, so that the psychological moment was missed. The moderate leadership of the [[Sarekat Islam]] was thereby weakened; the colonial administration's own slow tempo did half the work of the radical wing.

### Vermeulen's verdict on the colonial parliament

In his closing chapter Vermeulen offered a retrospective judgement on the role of the Dutch parliament in Indies affairs. The Indische debate, he wrote, "heeft iets van een schimmenspel van ideeën en beginselen" — has something of a shadow-play of ideas and principles. "Maar heel zelden krijgen de kamerleden die niet in Indië zijn geweest, een flits te zien van het leven in Indië zelf" — but very rarely did the chambermembers who had not been in the Indies catch so much as a flash of life in the Indies itself. The colonial civil servants trained at Delft and later at Leiden in the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical]] ideas were men of good will, but most "opheffers" — uplifters — underestimated the *gevoel van "*zijn*" — the sense of selfhood — that they provoked in the indigenous population.

### The shadow of Atjeh

In [[1918]] the conquest of Atjeh was thirteen years past. Yet the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] and the conduct of the [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] expedition of [[1904]] still resonated in the parliamentary discourse. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s verdict on the *bedrijven* — the moral underpinnings of the colonial state — could as well have been written of Atjeh as of [[Djambi]]: "Voor onze onpersoonlijke druk mist zij de sympathie van ras- en godsdienstsgevoel" — for our impersonal pressure it lacks the sympathy of race and religious feeling. The Atjehnese war was over; the question of its meaning was not.

## Significance

The year [[1918]] is doubly significant in the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] history. First, it marks the formal end of the military government of Atjeh — the [[Handover of Aceh to a Civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of Aceh to a civil gouverneur]] — and thus the formal close of the forty-five-year Aceh War that had begun with the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) Kreemer's account|first Aceh expedition]] of April [[1873]]. Second, it marks the appointment of the [[Carpentier Alting commission (1918)]] — the commission tasked with revising "the foundations of government" of the Indies, including the constitutional framing of the [[Korte Verklaring|Short Declaration]] policy first applied in Atjeh by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]. The two events together — the civil-government handover in Atjeh and the constitutional-reform commission in Batavia — frame the year 1918 as the moment at which the Atjeh-derived administrative thinking of the Van Heutsz era was to be constitutionalised for the wider Indies. The death of Pastoor Verbraak at Magelang in the same year closes, in family and mission memory, the chapter of the Catholic chaplaincy of the Atjeh war-footing that had begun with his arrival in [[1874]].

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**1918** was the year in which a disease appeared in the pepper-crop of Atjeh — *'in 1918 vertoonde er zich een ziekte in het gewas'* — and the year in which the [[Sarikat Islam|Sarikat Islam]] showed a vigorous life in the *afdeeling* [[Noordkust van Atjeh|Noordkust van Atjeh]]. [[Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb|Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb]] of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula]] was a member of the [[Volksraad|Volksraad]] in [[1918]] and [[1919]]; the *oeléebalangschap* was held by his *[[Banta|banta]]*. The [[H. T. Damste|H. T. Damste]] article *'[[Een stukje Atjeh-geschiedenis (Damste)|Een stukje Atjeh-geschiedenis]]'* was published in *Koloniaal Tijdschrift* in [[1918]].

## The last incidents

In April [[1918]] unrest threatened once more in the [[Boven Wòjla]] near [[Meulaboh]]: the religious teacher [[Teungkoe Moet]], an opponent of vaccination, was killed resisting arrest, and on [[25 April]] three hitherto friendly Acehnese made a *[[klewang]]* attack on the bivouac of the commander of [[Koeala Bhèë]], killing one and wounding seven. [[H. J. Schmidt]]'s response — his last action as a *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* officer — became the [[Coconut and Sugar-Cane Excursion (April 1918)|coconut and sugar-cane excursion]], in which the population everywhere met the column with gifts of coconuts and sugar cane and no shot was fired: "the time of war and resistance was past".

On [[11 March]] [[1918]] the Civil Authority at [[Lho' Soekon]], lieutenant [[M. Schouten|Schouten]], was attacked and soon recovered.

At Swart's retirement only one *onvindbare* had remained unfound: [[T. Radja Tampo']] in the marshes of [[Seuneu'am]] and [[Tripa]].

## Education

In [[1918]] there were 250 village schools with 14,751 pupils and twenty girls' schools with 1,321 pupils, against 106 village schools in [[1911]].

## The handover

By decree of [[12 August]] [[1918]] Swart was appointed, with effect from September, Vice-President of the [[Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]], and honourably discharged from military service with a pension of 9,000 guilders a year.

On [[2 September]] [[1918]], in a solemn gathering in the [[Gouverneurswoning te Koetaradja|Governor's residence]], he handed over the government to the civilian Governor resident [[Van Sluys]] and delivered his [[Swart's farewell address (2 September 1918)|farewell address]]: with the laying down of his office "an important piece of Atjeh history" was closed, since there would now be "an absolute separation of the civil and military authority, such as exists in all other provinces as well".

He left Atjeh in the early morning of [[22 September]] [[1918]] from [[Oelèë Lheuë]] aboard the *s.s. [[Nias]]*, the band playing the *Wilhelmus* and the crew of H.M. *[[Serdang]]* rendering the military salute; he arrived at [[Batavia]] to take up the Vice-Presidency.

## The verdicts

[[I. Feenstra]] wrote in the *[[Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant]]*: "It was in 1873 that the first general, Major-General Köhler, fell in the first Atjeh expedition. Now it is 1918 and the last general of Atjeh is going. Forty-five years, not yet half a century — it seems so short for the great work that has been done here."

The Atjeh correspondent of the *[[Nieuwe Courant]]* wrote: "His administration lasted more than ten years, and it was from beginning to end a happy period for the sorely tried province."

Du Croo dated from [[1918]] the proof of Swart's insights: "The history of Atjeh from 1918 to the present proves that Swart's insights were correct."

The [[Letter of the Acehnese Chiefs to General Swart (26 September 1933)|letter of the Acehnese chiefs]] of [[1933]] dated the transformation of Atjeh "from the year [[1908]] to 1918]]".

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* (1923) — 1918

"September 1918 is dan eindelijk de definitieve scheiding tusschen burgerlijk en militair gezag tot stand gekomen. Toen eerst was het psychologisch moment, dat men reeds in het begin van de tachtig jaren meende te hebben bereikt, aangebroken" — only then had the psychological moment come which one thought to have reached in the early eighties (see [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|Introduction of civil government in Atjeh (1881)]]): "thans zou 'de kroon' beter op 't werk passen". The book's latest school-census belongs to this year (242 schools/13,000 pupils; 28 government native schools; 15 girls' schools/1,000+ pupils), the tramway's paying traffic reached f 97,000, the postal land-line (1912) and the administration telephone net (1913) had reshaped communications, and the world war's shipping stall reduced the exports to near insignificance in 1917–1918 — the trough before the recovery of [[1919]].

**[[1918]]** was the year of the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of Aceh to a civilian Gouverneur]] by [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]] — the political closure of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] forty-five years after the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of [[1873]] and seven years after the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] had destroyed the last organised Acehnese armed resistance. It is the year that marks, in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, the end of "de slepende ziekte: de Atjeh-oorlog."

## The handover

"Daardoor kon Generaal Swart in [[1918]] eindelijk een rustig gebied overdragen aan een civiel Gouverneur. Er was een eind aan de slepende ziekte: de Atjeh-oorlog." The *daardoor* — thereby — refers to the cumulative effect of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] ([[1910]]-[[1911]]), the killing of the last Tiriō leaders, and the steady pressure of the patrouilles. With the last organised armed resistance destroyed, the country was *rustig* — quiet — enough to be handed over to a civilian Gouverneur.

## The seven-year gap

The seven-year gap between the military end of the war in [[1911]] and the political handover in [[1918]] is the book's quiet argument that the military and the political closures of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] were not the same. The colonne Schmidt had destroyed the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]]* in [[1911]]; but it took seven more years of patrouilles and political work — the surrender of *[[Panglima Polem|Panglima Prang Polim]]* to [[Hendrikus Colijn|kapitein Colijn]], the steady attrition of the remaining *onverzoenlijken* — before the country was quiet enough to be handed over.

## The opheffing of the detailchement Leuhong

The book notes that the *detailchement Leuhong]] was opheffed in [[1918]] — a small military detachment that had been maintained at [[Leuhong]] was withdrawn, marking the quiet end of a particular military presence. A soldier had written on [[22 August]] [[1912]] on Leuhong a note about the 1918 opheffing — still legible in [[1934]], twenty-two years later, on *[[bekas]]* Marechaussee.

## The broader colonial context

[[1918]] was also the year of the end of the First World War in Europe. The book notes that after the First World War "de gedachte aan een buitenlandse vijand (Japan) ernstiger werd" — the thought of a foreign enemy (Japan) became more serious — and the regular planning of service schedules began. The handover of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] to a civilian Gouverneur in [[1918]] is therefore part of the broader post-war reorganisation of the colonial army.

## Significance

[[1918]] is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the year of the political closure of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. The book's argument is that the war was, militarily, finished in [[1911]] — but that the political handover took seven more years. The year [[1918]] is therefore the book's marker of the difference between the military and the political closures of the colonial war: the colonne had finished the fighting in [[1911]]; the political system caught up in [[1918]].

## Account from *Snijders* (1944) — 1918

**[[1918]]** was the year in which [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Cornelis Jacobus Snijders]] — [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht]] since [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]] and [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chef van de Generale Staf]] since [[1910]] — stepped down from both offices on the conclusion of the [[First World War]] in [[November 1918|November]] [[1918]]. The simultaneous closure of the European war and of the colonial Aceh War — the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of Aceh to a civilian Gouverneur]] by [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]] — gives the year [[1918]] a dual significance in the biographer's framing of Snijders' career: it is the year in which both the metropolitan command that he had held since [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]] and the colonial war of which his [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Kota Alam action]] of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] had been a small early episode were brought to their respective closures.

Snijders' unfinished memoirs — the *Herinneringen uit mijn leven*, written in the third person — had been completed only to [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]], the moment of his appointment as Opperbevelhebber; the four years of his wartime command therefore lie outside the scope of his own narrative, and are recorded only in the biographer's later reconstruction from family papers and the cooperation of Luitenant-Generaal b.d. C. van Tuinen. After retirement, Snijders devoted two decades to civil aviation development and numerous national commissions, dying in [[1939]] at the age of 86.

**The year 1601** was, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the year of the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits (1601)|first Atjehnese embassy to the Netherlands]] — the *terminus a quo* of formal Atjehnese-Dutch diplomatic relations. In the summer of [[1601]] two Zeeland ships returned to the coast of Atjeh bearing letters and gifts from [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]] to the sultan of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]]; the sultan released the [[Frederik Houtman|Houtman captives]], granted permission for a Dutch factory at Atjeh, and sent his own embassy with letters and gifts in return to Prince Maurits.

## The second Zeeland visit and the release of the captives

The [[De Kleine Oosterling|Wijma school reader]] records the second Zeeland visit of [[1601]] as the diplomatic sequel to the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' visit of 1599]]: a good two years later two other Zeeland vessels appeared off the coast. There were fine gifts on board for the sultan of Atjeh, and also a letter from Prince Maurits. Now the Netherlands came into favour with that prince. He set the prisoners at liberty and gave permission to build a factory, or trading post, in his country.

## The embassy to Prince Maurits

The sultan's reciprocal embassy — bearing letters and gifts to Prince Maurits — arrived in the Netherlands during Maurits's siege of Grave: at the same time he wished not to be outdone in courtesy toward Prince Maurits, and sent him also some envoys with letters and gifts. The prince was besieging the city of Grave when the Atjehnese arrived in the Netherlands. He sent his fine coach to fetch them, and paid them great honours. They handed over the letters and gifts to Maurits, while he was surrounded by a multitude of officers. The prince had them shown around the entire army camp and, in their honour, staged a great mock battle. The envoys thereafter visited Amsterdam and from there returned to their country. One of them, however, had died in our country.

## Significance

The year [[1601]] is, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the *terminus a quo* of formal Atjehnese-Dutch diplomatic relations, and the staff history's principal instance of the early-modern Dutch practice of receiving Asian envoys in the field. The embassy of [[1601]] — with its release of the Houtman captives, its permission for a Dutch factory at Atjeh, and its reciprocal embassy to Prince Maurits — established the diplomatic frame that the school reader would deploy in its account of the early Dutch-Atjehnese relationship, and the courtesy reciprocity that would be inverted in the lessons on the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[1873]].

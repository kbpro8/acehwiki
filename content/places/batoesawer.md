**Batoesawer** — modern **Batu Sawar**, the historic capital of the sultanate of [[Djohor|Djohor]] on the Djohor river in the early seventeenth century — was the seat of the sultan of Djohor's court from c. [[1600]] to the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese destruction of May 1613]], the site of the Dutch factory established by [[Jacob van Heemskerck|Jacob van Heemskerck]] in April [[1603]] and confirmed by the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|second Matelieff accord of 23 September 1606]], and the principal Dutch commercial-military base in the Strait of Malaka in the period of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance. Batoesawer is the principal named capital city in Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The founding and the Heemskerck factory (1603)

Batoesawer was established as the sultan of Djohor's capital in the late sixteenth century, after the abandonment of the earlier capitals at Bintang and the Johor Lama. The city became the seat of the sultan's court and the principal commercial-military base of the sultanate on the Djohor river. [[Jacob van Heemskerck|Jacob van Heemskerck]]'s [[3 April]] [[1603]] treaty with the sultan of Djohor — concluded after Heemskerck's capture of the Portuguese galley at the mouth of the river — included the establishment of a Dutch factory at Batoesawer, with the factor **Jacob Buys** and a staff of assistants and merchandise. The factory was the principal Dutch commercial base in the Strait of Malaka in the early VOC period.

## The Matelieff accords (1606)

The [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|first Matelieff accord of 17 May 1606]] granted the Dutch freedom from tolls and a monopoly of the trade at Djohor; the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|second accord of 23 September 1606]] granted the Dutch the right to build a fort on Djohor territory — the fort that [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] sought to build at Batoesawer but that the sultan, distrusting the Dutch, refused to permit. The fort was never built; the Dutch factory at Batoesawer continued to operate without fortification, dependent on the sultan's protection and the occasional visit of a Dutch ship.

## The Verhoeff visit (1608-1609)

The [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|Verhoeff expedition of 1608]] brought the Dutch fleet to the river of Djohor in late [[1608]]. Verhoeff pressed the sultan to honour the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|second Matelieff accord]] and to permit the construction of the fort; the sultan refused. Verhoeff gave the sultan money to rebuild the parts of Batoesawer that had been burned by the Portuguese in [[1607]] — "tot vier maanden geleden de hoofdstad door de Portugeezen was verbrand" — and stores of war, replaced the factor **Van den Broeck** with [[Jacques Obelaer|Jacques Obelaer]], and sailed for [[Bantam|Bantam]] on [[8 February]] [[1609]], leaving two ships at the river mouth. The ships stayed until November [[1609]], when the sultan's peace with the Portuguese was formally proclaimed under trumpet-sound on [[16 October]] [[1610]].

## The Achehnese destruction (May 1613)

The [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of 7 May 1613]] destroyed Batoesawer. The Achehnese fleet appeared at the mouth of the river while the bulk of the Djohor fleet was away at [[Pahang|Pahang]]; the Dutch ship *Hope*, which had arrived three days earlier to relieve the factor and replenish the factory's capital, lay at anchor in the river; the Dutch refused the Achehnese demand to stand aside and landed thirty-one men to assist the Djohor defence. The Achehnese took the city on [[6 June]] [[1613]]; two Dutchmen were killed, twenty-two — including [[Jacques Obelaer|Obelaer]] — were taken prisoner and carried off to Atjeh "onder borchtocht van des compagnies goederen". The destruction of Batoesawer marked the effective end of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance and the strategic turn southward to [[Jacatra|Jacatra]] and the eventual founding of [[Batavia|Batavia]] in [[1619]].

## The aftermath

The sultan of Djohor — deposed by the Achehnese — was restored within months by an Achehnese-Djohor reconciliation: the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]] gave his sister in marriage to the deposed sultan, now styled **Sultan Sabrang**, and sent him back to Djohor with an Achehnese escort of two thousand men to rebuild the destroyed city. The Dutch prisoners were released as part of the rapprochement. The opperkoopman [[Adriaan van der Dussen|Adriaan van der Dussen]] arrived at Batoesawer in [[August]] [[1614]] to negotiate the construction of the fort that Matelieff had sought in [[1606]] — without success.

## Significance

Batoesawer is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal capital city of the sultanate of [[Djohor|Djohor]] in the early seventeenth century and the principal Dutch commercial-military base in the Strait of Malaka in the period of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance. The Dutch factory at Batoesawer — established by [[Jacob van Heemskerck|Heemskerck]] in April [[1603]] and confirmed by the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff accords of 1606]] — was the principal Dutch base in the Strait of Malaka in the early VOC period, and its destruction by the Achehnese in [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|May 1613]] marked the effective end of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance and the strategic turn southward to [[Jacatra|Jacatra]] and the eventual founding of [[Batavia|Batavia]] in [[1619]]. The sultan's refusal to permit the construction of the Dutch fort that [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] had sought in [[1606]] — and that [[Adriaan van der Dussen|Van der Dussen]] would seek again in [[1614]] — was the principal strategic failure of the Dutch-Djohor alliance and the proximate cause of its dissolution.

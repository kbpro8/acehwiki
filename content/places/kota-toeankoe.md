**Kota Toeankoe** was an Achehnese *[[Benteng|benteng]]* in [[Groot-Atjeh]] from which [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] harassed the Dutch *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]*, taken by storm at heavy cost some five years before [[1893]] and afterwards slighted.

## The assault

Hooijer recalled the operation as one of only two occasions in the ten-year *concentratie* period on which the Dutch, driven by necessity, moved outside the line with a relatively strong force. Nearly three battalions with the necessary cavalry and artillery entered the terrain north of the *kampong* (village) strip in which [[Overste Sievers]] later operated, in order to take Kota Toeankoe, which threatened the Dutch line of posts. The lieutenants [[Luitenant Veerman|Veerman]] and [[Luitenant Hageman|Hageman]], with several of their men, had fallen some hours earlier in their attempt to seize the *benteng*, which they hoped to find evacuated because an enveloping battalion was to approach it from the other side.

After a long and furious fight, all that fell into Dutch hands — at a cost of three times as many dead and wounded — was an outwork of ten by fifteen metres of interior space, within which lay a heap of thirty fallen Achehnese. Hooijer drew from the episode the conclusion that the Achehnese *had niet verleerd zich achter zijn wallen met mannenmoed en doodsverachting te verdedigen* — had not forgotten how to defend himself behind his walls with manly courage and contempt of death.

## Consequences for Teungkoe di Tiro

Hooijer listed the loss of Kota Toeankoe among the checks that broke the influence of [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]]: he had harassed the concentrated line strongly from Kota Toeankoe, but that stronghold too had fallen into Dutch hands by storm and been slighted, a few months after the Dutch had suffered so great a loss before it.

**Bali-Atjé** — in Javanese tradition ***Kodjrat-Bali-Atjé*** — is the *bedekte taal* name by which the Javanese chronicle tradition, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, recorded the dead ancestral land of [[Taroema]] on the Sumatran east-coastal Overwal — the country of [[Tandjoeng Poera|Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Deli|Deli]]-[[Asahan|Asahan]] that the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] would close. It is the *wedergade* (counterpart) on the Sumatran Overwal of the [[Bali|Bali]] of the great Sunda islands, and the place-world whose name encodes both the dead elephant-land (*Koendjara-ma-ti*) and the ancestral [[Atjeh|Atjé]] sphere.

## The Javanese legend of Djaja Baja

The Javanese legend recorded by [[Raffles|Raffles]] (*History* II, p. 87) speaks of [[Astina|Astina]], the realm of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]] of Jaya's oldest princely house, whose name — *h-asti-na-ma*, "elephant" — was changed on the dynasty's extinction to *Enj°-* or *Guj'rat*. A Dutch *babad*-translation (*Not. Bat. Gen.* vol. 42) speaks of this king as a "Moorschen" prince of *kodjrat-Bali-Atjé* — where the *mor*, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, is plainly used in the sense of *kahilangan* ("vanished, deceased"), and the *Kodj* or *Guj-rat* conceals *Koen- of Goendjara-ma-t-i*, "the dead land of elephants".

## Decoding: *Koendjara-ma-ti Bali-Atjé*

The full name ***Kodjrat-Bali-Atjé*** [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as ***Koendjara-ma-ti Bali-Atjé*** — "the dead elephant-land Bali-Atjé". The *Koendjara* ("elephant-land") is the [[Astina|Astina]] of the Sumatran east coast, the elephant-rich country whose products the *Roteiro* of [[Vasco da Gama]] records for *Xarnauz*; the *ma-ti* ("dead") marks the dynasty's extinction; and the *Bali-Atjé* is the ancestral [[Atjeh|Atjé]] sphere whose *bedekte taal* form the Javanese chronicle tradition preserved. The same Bali-Atjé, De Roo de la Faille argues, is the *Bali-Atjé* of [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes's]] [[1513]] attack-junk, caulked with seven layers of *kapoer-gala-gala*.

## The Sumatran Bali

The *Bali* of Bali-Atjé, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] argues, is not the great Sunda island of [[Bali|Bali]] but the *Bali* of the Sumatran Overwal — the *wedergade* of the Javanese Bali, in the same way that [[Tandjoengpoera|Tandjoengpoera]] on West Java is the *wedergade* of [[Tandjoengpoera|Tandjoengpoera]] in [[Langkat|Langkat]], and [[Madjapahit in Langsar|Madjapahit at Langsar]] is the *wedergade* of the great [[Majapahit|Majapahit]] of East Java. The east-coast Sumatran polities, De Roo de la Faille argues, were the *gade* and *wedergade* of the Javanese ones — the same names attached to two places, one in the ancestral land of *Dja-g-oe-wa* and one in its Javanese copy.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Bali-Atjé is the principal *bedekte taal* form by which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] ties the Javanese legend of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]] to the [[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere — the dead ancestral land of [[Taroema]] whose name encodes both the dead elephant-land (*Koendjara-ma-ti*) and the ancestral [[Atjeh|Atjé]] sphere. Its identification, by De Roo de la Faille, with the *Bali* of the Sumatran Overwal also marks it as the principal east-coast landmark of the pre-Atjehnese Hindu-Javanese complex in Javanese chronicle record.

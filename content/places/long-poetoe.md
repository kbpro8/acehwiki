**Long Poetoe** was a *[[Kampong|kampong]]* on the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|North Coast of Atjeh]] where the ophthalmologist [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] treated cataract sufferers during the [[Cataract campaign on the North Coast of Atjeh (1932)|North Coast campaign]]. The village is named in the caption of the cover photograph of *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]*: "eenige van de behandelde cataractlijders in de kampong Long Poetoe op de Noordkust van Atjeh" — some of the treated cataract sufferers in the *kampong* of Long Poetoe on the North Coast of Atjeh.

## The photograph

The plate is the pamphlet's opening image and one of the few photographic records of Tijssen's Acehnese patients. It shows a group of villagers who had undergone the cataract extraction that Tijssen called "het wonder in handen" — a procedure simple in the scientific sense but speaking by its immediate effect to the simple of spirit, and therefore in his view a social act of significance.

## Context

Long Poetoe belongs to the belt of North Coast villages in which Tijssen carried out the work described in the first of his three letters: eighty-three persons operated on in twenty-six days, in a small polyclinic building, in an administrative office and in a buffalo stable, at a cost to the government of very little money. It was in four small villages of this coast that in [[1923]] he had caused the blind to be assembled and had counted two hundred and twenty-six of them. The name is rendered in modern Indonesian orthography as Lueng Putu, in the [[Pedir|Pidie]] district.

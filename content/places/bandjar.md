**Bandjar (Banda Aceh)** was the principal settlement on the north coast of [[Acheh]] that served as a strategic Dutch military target during the mid‑nineteenth‑century expeditions, notably the 1868‑1869 marine landing operations described in the Dutch Marine Corps history. The town’s fortified kampongs, such as [[Kali-Anget]] and [[Kampong Djentarek]], were repeatedly assaulted by Dutch marine landing divisions under officers like [[Lieutenant P. Spanjaard]] and [[Lieutenant D. A. van Vuuren]].

## Dutch Marine Operations (1868–1870)

During the 1868 expedition against the rebel leader Karaeng Bonto Bonto in Celebes, Dutch forces also launched a parallel assault on Bandjar. On [[24 October]] [[1868]] the marine landing division, reinforced by 11 officers—including [[Lieutenant D. A. van Vuuren]]—and 170 marines, attacked the fortified kampong. The assault involved heavy artillery, including two 12 cm howitzers, and resulted in the capture of the kampong after intense stone‑throwing and rifle fire.

A subsequent attack on Bandjar on [[24 October]] [[1868]] again saw participation of the marine landing division, now under the command of [[Colonel De Brabant]] after the illness of [[Major Van Heemskerk]]. The Dutch forces overcame the defensive walls, which were reinforced with high stone blocks, and secured the town.

## Significance

Bandjar’s capture demonstrated the effectiveness of combined land‑sea operations by the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]] and marked a turning point in the Dutch effort to impose colonial authority over the Aceh coast. The repeated assaults and the eventual subjugation of the town foreshadowed the larger Aceh War (1873–1904).

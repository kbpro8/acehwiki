British [[India]] appears in the discussion of the holy war doctrine. 'In British [[India]] the Shattarite mysticism still prevails here and there.' The case of Muslim attitudes toward the British government in [[India]] is discussed at length: some Muslim leaders tried to demonstrate that rebellion would be unlawful jihad, while others upheld the contrary. The question was submitted to the Mekkan muftis who declared: 'British [[India]] must be regarded as daral-Islam.' Syed Amir Ali, a British-Indian writer, wrote works on Mohammed and Islamic law in English, aiming to make Islam palatable to a European public. Al-Jisr dedicated his book to the Sultan of Turkey. The Gayo Country (Telong / Gayo highland) The interior highland region of [[Acheh]]. A man from Telong in the Gayo country who bore the name of [[Teungku Tapa]] claimed to be [[Malem Diwa]] incarnate and caused a considerable commotion on the East and North Coasts in [[1898]]-[[1900]]. He gave himself out to be [[Malem Diwa]] himself and promised invulnerability and victory over the unbelievers. The Dutch troops speedily put an end to his success. He was killed near [[Piadah]] in [[1900]]. Also, many variants of the haba about two boys Amat and Muhamat are current in the Gayo and Alas countries. The Alas Country An interior region of [[Acheh]]. 'Many varieties of this tale are current in the Gayo and Alas countries' (referring to the haba about two boys whose mother had an intrigue with a snake). [[West Africa]] 'It is especially in the more distant parts of the Mohammedan world, such as [[West Africa]] and the East Indies, that the wagiyyat (last admonition of the Prophet), in spite of its re- appearance at stated intervals, finds most widespread belief.' Also: 'It is said that they (wagiyyats) are in fact current at [[Medina]] but do not attract the serious attention of the public in the holy cities.' Louis Rinn's Marabouts et Khouans (Algiers [[1884]]) is cited regarding the reputation of wagiyyats in [[West Africa]].

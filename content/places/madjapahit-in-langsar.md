**Madjapahit in Langsar** — also **Majapahit of Langsar** — is the Sumatran counterpart of the great [[Majapahit|Majapahit]] of East Java, located in [[Langsar|Langsar]] (in modern [[Langkat|Langkat]]) on the east coast of [[Sumatra]], and the principal token of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s claim that the "Madjapahit" of the chronicle tradition had a Sumatran original — a *bedekte taal* form of which the Javanese Madjapahit was the "wedergade" (counterpart). It is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the same place-world as the [[Taroema|Taroema]] of the [[Aroe|Aru]] complex, and the [[Astina|Astina]] of the Javanese legend of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]].

## The "double" Madjapahit

The east-coast Sumatran polities, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] argues, were the *gade* and *wedergade* of the Javanese ones — the same names attached to two places, one in the ancestral land of [[Taroema|Dja-g-oe-wa]] and one in its Javanese copy. [[Tandjoengpoera|Tandjoengpoera]] on west Java was the *wedergade* of [[Tandjoengpoera|Tandjoengpoera]] in [[Langkat|Langkat]]; [[Aroe|Aroe]] in the west had its counterpart in the east; and Madjapahit in East Java had its Madjapahit in [[Langsar|Langsar]]. [[Toemapel|Toemapel]] in [[Pasuruan|Pasoeroewan]] De Roo de la Faille reads as *T-ar-oe-ma-tapel* — i.e. as the Taroema of [[Toemapel|Toemapel]]'s counterpart.

## The *bedekte taal* of Pigafetta

[[Pigafetta|Pigafetta]], in his account of the [[Magellan|Victoria]]'s voyage, was told by Moorish merchants on [[Timor|Timor]] that the largest cities of [[Java|Java Major]] were *Madjapait* (which Pigafetta's manuscripts spell *Magepaher* / *Magepahor*), [[Sunda|Sunda]], [[Kediri|Daha]], [[Demak|Demak]], *Gadjahmada*, [[Mataram|Mataraman]], [[Japara|Djapara]], [[Sidayu|Sidajoe]], [[Tuban|Toeban]], [[Gresik|Gresik]], [[Surabaya|Soerabaja]], and [[Bali|Bali]]. [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] read *Magepaher*/*Magepahor* as Madjapahit of East Java. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] demurs: *Magepaher*/*Magepahor* is *Madjapah-it-l-èr* or *Madjapah-it-l-or* — the Madjapahit "in the north" (i.e. on Sumatra's east coast, at [[Langsar|Langsar]]), not the Madjapahit of East Java. The king whose name Pigafetta recorded as *Raia Pati Unus* was, on this reading, *raja* (not *radja*) Pati Unus — "the glorious Pati Unus" of the [[Demak's attack on Pasoeroewan (1546)|1546 attack on Pasoeroewan]].

## The Atjeh-Aru war and the close of the Sumatran Madjapahit

After the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]], the Sumatran Madjapahit of [[Langsar|Langsar]] — like the rest of the east-coast Hindu-Javanese complex — was reduced to "poverty and piracy", and its function as the *gade* of the Javanese Madjapahit lapsed. The [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend of [[Deli]] — the fairy-tale queen whose rejection of the [[Sultan of Atjeh]]'s marriage suit ended in the loss of her glass coffin at sea — is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the folk record of the close of the Sumatran Madjapahit age.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Madjapahit in [[Langsar|Langsar]] is the principal token of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s claim that the chronicle tradition's "Madjapahit" had a Sumatran original — a *bedekte taal* form whose Javanese counterpart is the great [[Majapahit|Majapahit]] of East Java, and whose reduction by the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] marks the close of the Hindu-Javanese east-coast age.

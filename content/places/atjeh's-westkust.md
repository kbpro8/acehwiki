## In De Karbouw

Atjeh's Westkust figures in *De Karbouw* in three principal connections:

- The [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|buffalo-riding *schimpstraf*]] for wife-beaters: "In het oude Atjeh gold als adat-schimpstraf voor een man, die een vrouw mishandeld had, dat hij, met een hoofddoek van gedroogde pisangbladeren op het hoofd en omhangen met houten wapentjes en een keten van schelpen, op een wijfjes-buffel gezeten, werd rondgeleid, tot spot van het publiek. Ook gebeurde het wel, dat de delinkwent enige dagen in het openbaar op handen en voeten moest rondkruipen met een ring in de neus en gras eten, als ware hij een buffel" (*De Karbouw*, p. 104). The authority for this practice is given as [[K. F. H. van Langen|Van Langen]], [[Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust ([[1888]])|*Atjeh's Westkust*]], p. 481, alongside [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] I, p. 120, and [[G. A. J. Wilken|Wilken]], *Verspreide Geschriften* II, p. 488.
- The prohibition on albino-buffalo slaughter for the coastal Malays: "4° De W.-kust van Atjeh; hier bestaat het verbod bij de Maleiers algemeen" (*De Karbouw*, p. 217) — the prohibition of albino-buffalo slaughter is general among the Malays of the West Coast of Atjeh, in contrast to the lineage-specific prohibitions of [[Groot-Atjeh]] (*kawōm* [[kawōm Dja Sandang|Dja Sandang]]), [[Gajoland]] (Gölö, Beroeksah, Tjéq of Petiambang) and [[Alasland]] (Tjebero, Terigan, Pinding, Bintjawan, Sinagō).
- The buffalo-export trade from the Westkust hinterland: "Voor het Batakse hoogland en het achterland van Atjeh's Westkust was de uitvoer van karbouwen een voorname bron van inkomsten. Zij geschiedde slechts voor een klein deel over zee; verreweg de meeste dezer dieren werden vervoerd per trein, te voet, of per vrachtauto. Ook de uitvoer van buffels naar Singapore en Penang was niet onbelangrijk" (*De Karbouw*, p. 131). The buffalo export from the Westkust hinterland was thus a substantial source of income, the animals being moved mostly by train, on foot or by truck (rather than by sea), with a significant export flow to Singapore and Penang.


## Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust ([[1888]])

The standard Dutch geographical account of the Westkust, [[K. F. H. van Langen|Van Langen]]'s [[Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust ([[1888]])|*Atjeh's Westkust*]] ([[1888]]), was published in the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap*, vol. II. It appears to have included ethnographic material on local Malay adat practices of the kind that *De Karbouw* cites for the buffalo-riding *schimpstraf*. Van Langen's study was a landmark of the early Dutch geographical literature on Atjeh, predating the great ethnographic syntheses of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] ([[1893]]-94) and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] ([[1922]]-23) by a generation.



## Significance

Atjeh's Westkust is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as the Atlantic-facing maritime flank of the sultanate, distinguished from the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] heartland around [[Banda Acheh|Koetaradja]] by its predominantly Malay population, by its lineage-specific and general buffalo-prohibition customs, and by its role as a hinterland of buffalo-export trade to Singapore and Penang. It is also the type-locality for the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|buffalo-riding *schimpstraf*]] recorded by Van Langen.

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — Atjeh's Westkust

### Geography and geology

The Westkust van Atjeh is, in the colonial geological literature, the type-area for the late-Tertiary to Recent geological evolution of the western Atjeh coast. The coastal alluvial basin of [[Meulaboh]] — mapped by [[J. Zwierzycki|Zwierzycki]] in [[1922]] on sheet I of the 1:1,000,000 geological map — distinguishes Alluvium, Neogene (Pliocene, Miocene), Paleogene (Oligocene, Eocene), Permo-Carboniferous, andesitic effusiva, andesite, basalt, diabase, gabbro, serpentine and granite. The same map (with Van Bemmelen's correction of the "Losir (Löser)" volcano error) is the standard cartographic basis for all subsequent geological work on the West-Atjeh coastal plain.

The surface form of the Westkust is dominated by the pleistocene river terraces of the Meulaboh basin, which "begin directly at the foot of the central mountains and extend downstream to the sudden southward bend in all larger rivers" — a sudden bend that is "most probably the result of a young fault zone, parallel to the coast". The alluvial coastal strip is "at present subsiding, and shows no terraces", while the inland terraces are Pleistocene remnants of an older, more seaward-extended drainage system. The Westkust is accordingly a tectonically active landscape, with the inland terraces preserving the older drainage and the modern alluvial coast receiving the modern sediment load.

### Mineral geography

The Westkust van Atjeh is the principal mineral-bearing region of colonial Atjeh. The mineral occurrences recorded in the colonial geological literature include:

- **Gold and platinum placers**: in the pleistocene river terraces of the [[Basin of Meulaboh]], worked by the [[M.A.E.M.]] dredges on the [[Geudong Concession]] in [[1940]]–[[1941]] — the only recorded colonial-era gold (and platinum) production on Atjeh territory.
- **Native mercury and cinnabar**: in the gold-bearing alluvial deposits of the Westkust, observed by [[C. de Groot|De Groot]] in [[1946]] through the M.A.E.M. dredges' gain of mercury with each clean-up — the only documented colonial-era observation of mercury minerals on Atjeh territory.
- **Iron-ore occurrences**: at [[Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)|Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)]] (113 km NW of Meulaboh, 700 m of iron ore with 8–17 m thickness), [[Kr. Rigaih]] (88 km NW of Meulaboh), [[Alu Talu]] (78 km NW of Meulaboh), [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]] (30 and 12 km NW of Meulaboh, bog-iron deposits with 300,000–400,000-ton reserve estimate), [[A. Pentuwa Gade]] (68 km SE of Meulaboh) and [[Kuto Buloh]] (27 km N of [[Tapa Tuan]]).
- **Coal occurrences**: Paleogene coals at [[Krueng Kluet]] (No. 13), South of [[Tapatuan]] (No. 14) and NW-[[Krueng Teunom]] (No. 15); Neogene coals at [[Krueng Raja]] (No. 16), [[Krueng Antjong]] (No. 17), East of Peureu Moue (No. 18) and Near Simpane Peust (No. 19); and along the [[Krueng Woyla]] and [[Krueng Meureubo]].
- **Lead-zinc occurrences**: at [[Krueng Heureueng]] (76.1 % Pb, 920 gr/t Ag) and [[Krueng Isep]] (65.13 % Pb, 680 gr/t Ag) — the only two Atjeh lead-zinc localities recorded in the colonial geological literature.

### Significance

Atjeh's Westkust is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the principal mineral-bearing region of colonial Atjeh — the locus of the only recorded colonial-era gold (and platinum) production on Atjeh territory (the M.A.E.M. Geudong Concession operation of [[1940]]–[[1941]]), of the only documented colonial-era observation of mercury minerals on Atjeh territory (the De Groot [[1946]] observation), and of the great majority of the documented colonial-era iron-ore, coal and lead-zinc occurrences on Atjeh territory. The Westkust's mineral geography is accordingly the central fact of Atjeh's colonial mineral economy, and the region's geological survey — from [[Rinaud]]'s [[1885]] coal survey through Zwierzycki's [[1922]] mapping to De Groot's [[1946]] report — is the principal documentary sequence of the colonial mineralogical record of Atjeh.

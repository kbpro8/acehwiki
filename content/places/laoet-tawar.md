**Laoet Tawar** is the great natural lake of the [[Gajoland|Gayo]] highlands — approximately 16 kilometres long and 4 kilometres wide, lying at about 1,200 metres above sea level in a basin enclosed by the high, pine-forested walls of the *Klietel*-massif to the south and the *B. ni Segi*–*B. ni Beboeli* chain to the north — that is the geographical and demographic heart of the [[Gajo Loeës|Gajo Loeës]] high plateau and the headwater of the [[Kr. Peusangan|Kr. Peusangan]], which descends from it to the North Coast. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the *Laoet Tawar* as the principal physical feature of the Gayo lands: the body of water around which the great rice plains of *Bintang* and *Takengën* formed, and the barrier whose only access of *hoogen weg* from the north — the *Klietel*-saddle — was the route of the *Gajo-weg*, "met ontzaglijke moeite en kosten geslagen."

## Topography and formation

The *Laoet Tawar* is an *afdammingsmeer* — a dammed lake — formed by the natural blocking of the Peusangan valley by the *Telëgë*-heights and the *Klietel*-massif. The lake's water is contained by the high, almost perpendicular walls of the surrounding mountains, which "langs overal tot de slechts hier en daar wat terugwijkende bergflanken bijna oevers naar voren dringen" — everywhere thrust the shores forward, almost to the only here and there somewhat receding mountain flanks. Small, fully enclosed shore-plains — *Kenawa*, *Towéran*, *Rawé*, and *Nosan* — provided the *sawahs* of a few small settlements; the larger, gently sloping plains of *Bintang* and *Takengën* on the eastern and western shores became, by their continuous subsidence and their fertility, "welvarende bevolkings-centra" (prosperous population centres).

## The outflow and the Peusangan

Even west of the present *bestuursvestiging* [[Takengën|Takengën]], the *Laoet Tawar* breaks through the narrow western wall of the basin and draws into its watershed the *Peusangan* river, which thereafter absorbs the small *Wol ni Aréh* and the *keteldal* of *Pegasèng* on its way to the coast. The heavy forested slopes of the *Telëgë* heights then turn the *Peusangan* in its course to the west, forcing it to seek a lower *drempel* (threshold) and to break through the narrow, deep valley between the steep watershed of the upper *Wojla* and the third Barisan *coulisse*. Below the *Pegasèng*-drempel, the *Peusangan* descends to the North Coast through the *keteldal* of *Ketël* and the moorish delta of the *Kr. Peusangan*.

## The settlements and the chiefdoms

The shores of the *Laoet Tawar* are the demographic core of the [[Gajo Loeës|Gajo Loeës]]. The great kampong of *Bintang* — seat of the *Boekét Ewéh* branch of the [[Kédjoerön Boekét|Kédjoerön Boekét]] house — lies on its eastern shore; the *bestuursvestiging* of [[Takengën|Takengën]] lies on its western shore; the small shore settlements of *Kenawa*, *Towéran*, *Rawé*, and *Nosan* occupy the enclosed plains along its banks. The *Kédjoerön Pétiambang* — the titular overlord of the Gayo Loeës whose authority had, by the colonial period, decayed into a *theoretisch gezag* — had his seat at *Penampacian* on the lake shore.

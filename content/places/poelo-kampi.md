**Poelo Kampi** — also **Kampi**, **Koempej**, **Kampe**, in [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]'s transcription *Paneticano* — is the small hilly islet close under the shore in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] on the east coast of [[Sumatra]] that [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies as the older *Malajoer* of the [[Pararaton]] and the *Noesa-n-ta-ra* "standing under the protection of the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Tara]]". The islet was the first named point of [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]'s October [[1539]] embassy to [[Aroe]] — the pilot's strange answer *Paneticano* to Pinto's question "what is this place called?" — and is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the principal *bedekte taal* marker of the Hindu-Buddhist sacred geography of the bay.

## Pinto's *Paneticano*

When [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]], on [[5 October]] [[1539]], entered [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] and asked his pilot the name of the place, the answer was *Paneticano* — a name so strange that Pinto wrote it down as best he could. The veiled-language reading, as [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes it, is *Roe-pang moes-tika-noe-sa*: the "white-magic islet of Aroe", the place "of old in the reputation of *tooverij* [witchcraft] — *witte magie* [white magic]". Pinto further records it as a place "compared with a white-holy bezoar amulet-stone" — the *moes-tika*, a magical bezoar whose protection covered the islet. In De Roo de la Faille's day the islet was known as *Poelo Koempej* or *Kampi*.

## The Pararaton's *Noesa-n-ta-ra*

In the [[Pararaton|Pararaton's]] account of the thirteenth-century struggles, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies Kampi with the *Noesa-n-ta-ra* — the island under the protection of the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Tara]], at which the *pabgjan* (the toll-house at which ships paid import duties) stood. He further identifies it with the *Kampe* — *P.Kompaj* — that, in the thirteenth century, "armed with weapons must have broken loose from the famous [[Sri Widjaja]]" (Krom, *Hindoe-Jav. Gesch.*, pp. 306-307, 416). The same islet, De Roo de la Faille argues, is the *Tarawangan* — the "Noorder-eiland" — of the *pralambang* tradition: the island at the *wangan* that leads into [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]], named for the goddess Tara whose patronage covered the great island of [[Sumatra]] itself.

## Van Rijn's visit (1888)

In [[1888]] the Controleur S.B. [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] entered the bay between [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] and Koempej, and recorded the islet as "a half-moon-shaped islet close under the shore, set with 50-to-150-metre hills. The clear white strand slowly gives way to higher ground with light-green grass, behind which the fortified houses of the *Datoe Bintara* and the Inlandsch Opziener come into view: the whole makes the impression as if one has a fortress with breastwork before one". This impression, De Roo de la Faille argues, was the *djoeragan* of Van Rijn's barkas pointing out the same image that the Tanjore inscription of [[1030]] had recorded as "the ancient Malayur situated on a high hill" — the hill that, in [[1862]], still bore the *benteng* of an [[Atjehsche avonturier at Aroe-baai (1862)|Atjehschen avonturier]] who flew the Atjehsche flag.

## The two islands

Van Rijn also recorded the local tradition of [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] — the island opposite [[Pangkalan Soesoe]]: it "consists of a chain of larger and smaller hills. According to the tradition this island came here from the Overwal and would have belonged to the Nine Islands of Koeala Pérak, of which only eight are now said to lie there". The same tradition held that this island "would marry with Poelau Salahadji, but could not proceed further than the present place, where it anchored before a tree which is still found and which, at high as well as low water, is visible in the bay of Aroe and always protrudes equally far above the water". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes this as a riddle on *djangkar* (anchor) and *Hangker*, and on *boom* (tree) and *taroe nama* — i.e. [[Taroema]], "always visible in the bay and always equally far (*djaoeh*) protruding above the water".

## Puteri Idjo's original seat

Local east-coast tradition also places here the original dwelling of [[Puteri Idjo|Puteri Idjo]] ("Poetri Idjo"), the green princess of [[Deli]] and [[Asahan]] legend, before her *negorij* moved to the mouth of the [[Tandjoengpoera]] river at Koewala Boeboen.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Poelo Kampi is the principal sacred-island locus of the east-coast Hindu-Buddhist place-world that the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] in [[1539]] overwrote but did not erase. Its identification with the *Noesa-n-ta-ra* of the [[Pararaton]] and with the *Malajoer* of the chronicle tradition makes it the single most important topographical anchor of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s *bedekte taal* reading of Sumatran-Javanese historical geography. Its later history — as the hill from which an [[Atjehsche avonturier at Aroe-baai (1862)|Atjehscher avonturier]] flew the Atjehsche flag in [[1862]] — also marks the survival of an Atjehnese memory of the place long after the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|fall of Aroe]].

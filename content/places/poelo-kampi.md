**Poelo Kampi** is a hilly island in [[Aroebai|Aroe Bay]] off [[Sumatra]]'s east coast, called *Paneticano* by [[Mendez Pinto's Aroe mission (1539)|Mendez Pinto]] in [[1539]] and identified by P. de Roo de La Faille as the sacred centre of the old [[Aroe Kingdom]] and the original seat of the goddess [[Tara]] cult that gave its name to [[Sumatra]].

## Pinto's Paneticano

On [[5 October]] [[1539]], approaching Aroe, Pinto asked his pilot the name of the land and recorded *Paneticano*. De Roo decodes this as veiled Malay *roe-pang moes-tika-noe-sa* — "the island compared to a white holy bezoar amulet stone" — a reference to its reputation for *witte magie* (white magic).

Dutch charts later rendered it as Poelo Koempej, Poelo Kampi or Koempej.

## History

De Roo links the island to *Kampe = P. Kompaj*, which in the thirteenth century broke away by force from [[Sri Widjaja]]. In the [[Pararaton]] it appears as *Noesa-n-ta-ra*, "the island under the protection of the goddess Tara", where a *pabgjan* (tollhouse) collected duties from anchoring ships.

When Controleur Van Rijn van Alkemade entered [[Aroebai|Aroe Bay]] in [[1888]] between Pulo Sembilan and Pulo Koempej, he described a "pleasant view" of a half-circular island with hills of 50–150 metres, a bright white beach grading to light-green grass, and inland the fortified houses of [[Datoe Bintara]]. The whole, he wrote, looked like a fort with breastworks — a memory, de Roo suggests, of its old sacral-military role.

## Association with origins

For de Roo, Poelo Kampi was the *poelo k-oempi*, from Balinese/Malay *koempi* meaning "great-grandparent". Through Javanese vowel alternation (*oe*/*a*), this yields *Kampi*, the "ancestor island". It was, in his etymological scheme, the *Taroema* in miniature — *taroe na-ma*, "the tree-name" — and the source of the toponymic play that makes *Sijama Tara* into *Sumatra*.

Local tradition also placed here the original dwelling of [[Poetri Idjo]], the green princess of [[Deli]] and [[Asahan]] legend, before her *negorij* moved to the mouth of the [[Tandjoeng Poera]] river at [[Koewala Boeboen]].

## Significance

Poelo Kampi is the topographic key to de Roo's argument that Aroe — not Samudera-Pasai or some other Sumatran polity — was the *land van oorsprong* of the Malay-Sumatra cultural tradition: the ancestor island whose tollhouse stood at the northern entry to the Strait, whose goddess gave her name to the island, and whose sacred-bezoar reputation survived in Pinto's *Paneticano* and in Van Rijn van Alkemade's [[1888]] field account.

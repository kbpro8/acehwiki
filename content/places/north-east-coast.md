The **North-East Coast (of Acheh)** is the long littoral that extends from the neighbourhood of the capital eastward toward [[Pidie]], [[Samalanga]] and the [[Tamiang]] border, forming one of the principal commercial and demographic zones of [[Acheh|the Achehnese] lands]. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Snouck Hurgronje cites the North-East Coast in connection with the prevalence of opium-smoking among the Achehnese.

## Opium-Smoking and the Coastal Trade

In a footnote to his discussion of Achehnese habits and luxuries, the author remarks that "while opium-smoking is very common on the West and North-East coasts, it is not at all so prevalent in [[Acheh Proper]] as is usually supposed." The remark is significant, for it places the North-East Coast — together with the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coast]] — in a distinct cultural zone, in which a habit introduced by foreign traders had taken deeper root than in the inland districts of the kingdom.

## The Populations of the Coast

The inhabitants of the North-East Coast, like those of the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coast]], have long been in close contact with the foreign merchants — Klings, Chinese, Arabs, and Europeans — who frequented the ports of Acheh. The same intercourse that brought opium also brought settlers, and the populations of the North-East Coast include, besides the Achehnese proper, a considerable admixture of Kling and other Indian elements, the descendants of strangers (*ancu' jamee*) who settled along the sea-board and contributed their quota to the composition of the Achehnese race. The Klings in [[Great Acheh]] and on the East Coast, the author notes, have brought more half-caste progeny into the world than now commands recognition as such.

## The Littoral and the Inland

The distinction which the author draws between the coast and [[Acheh Proper]] — the inland kernel of the kingdom — is one that pervades his whole account. The kernel of the kingdom, which has supplied the outlying districts with a considerable portion of their inhabitants, is bounded, according to the Achehnese idea, by a line extending from [[Kluang]] on the west to [[Krueng Raya]] on the north coast, and passing through [[Reueng-reueng]], [[Pancha]] and [[Janthoe]]. Beyond this line, the North-East Coast forms an outer zone in which foreign influence, including the habit of opium-smoking, was more strongly felt than in the royal heartland.

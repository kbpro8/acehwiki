**North Sumatra** (Dutch: *Noord-Sumatra*) is the northern portion of the island of [[Sumatra]], and the homeland of the [[Atjehers]] (Acehnese people). In the administrative geography of the [[Netherlands India|Dutch East Indies]], it encompassed the territories of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]], the Bataklands, and other regions.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — Peoples of North Sumatra

The atlas, in its chapter on "Land en Volk" (Land and People), provides the following observation on the origins of the North Sumatran population, specifically the Atjehers: "Wat de herkomst van deze bewoners van Noord-Sumatra aangaat, ontbreekt het ons aan betrouwbare gegevens. Vast staat wel, dat vreemdelingen uit Hindostan, Arabie, Java enz. in deze bevolking zijn opgelost." (As regards the origins of these inhabitants of North Sumatra, reliable data are lacking to us. What is certain, however, is that foreigners from Hindustan, Arabia, Java, etc. have been absorbed into this population.)

The atlas classifies the Atjehers as belonging to the western half of the Archipel's population, which falls within the Malay racial group (*Maleische ras*), alongside the Javanese, Sundanese, Madurese, Balinese, Makassarese, Buginese, and the Malays of the Moluccas. It is noted, however, that this Malay racial group is not of unmixed origin, having absorbed Hindus, Chinese, and other Asians over the centuries.

## Significance

The atlas's frank acknowledgment that the origins of the North Sumatran population remain uncertain, despite the certainty of multiple waves of foreign immigration, reflects the state of ethnographic knowledge in [[1926]]. The observation that the Atjehers are a particularly mixed population — absorbing influences from India, Arabia, and Java — is consistent with Acheh's historical position as a major entrepôt and centre of Islamic learning in the Indonesian archipelago.

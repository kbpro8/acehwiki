**Noordkust van Atjeh** (North Coast of Atjeh) was an *afdeeling* (division) of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], administered by Assistent-Resident [[M. C. Roos van Raadshoven]]. The Noordkust covered the northern coastline of Atjeh, including the important region of [[Pidië]] (administered as a separate *onderafdeeling* under Civiel Gezaghebber [[Th. J. Veltman]]). The northern coast had been one of the first areas of Atjeh to come under Dutch control and contained some of the most densely populated districts in the entire *Gouvernement*. The designation "Noordkust" reflected the colonial administrative practice of organizing Atjeh's territories along coastal axes, a pattern that mirrored the initial points of Dutch military penetration and the subsequent establishment of administrative control from coastal enclaves inland.

## Historical Background and Administrative Significance

The northern coast of Atjeh held a special place in the history of Dutch involvement in the territory. It was along this coastline that the initial Dutch military expeditions of the 1870s had landed and established their first footholds, and it was here that the process of colonial consolidation had progressed furthest by the early twentieth century. The *afdeeling* Noordkust encompassed some of the most historically significant regions of Atjeh, including the area around the former capital of the Acehnese sultanate and the densely settled coastal plains that had formed the economic and demographic heartland of the pre-colonial Acehnese state. The relatively early pacification of the northern coast meant that by 1914, the colonial administrative apparatus was more firmly established here than in the [[Westkust van Atjeh]] or the more remote interior regions.

The inclusion of [[Pidië]] as a *onderafdeeling* within the Noordkust *afdeeling* reflected the strategic and administrative importance of this region. Pidië—known in historical sources as Pedir—had been the site of one of the most powerful pre-colonial sultanates on the Sumatran coast, and its continuing significance was underscored by the appointment of a military officer, Majoor [[Th. J. Veltman]], as its *Civiel Gezaghebber*. This militarization of civil administration was characteristic of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, where the legacy of the protracted Atjeh War meant that military officers continued to occupy civil administrative posts in regions deemed to require ongoing security oversight. The placement of a Majoor der Infanterie in charge of Pidië indicated that this region, despite its long history of Dutch contact, was still considered to require the presence of military authority in its civil governance.

## The 1913–1914 Reorganization and Merger with Oostkust

In the proposed reorganization discussed at the Medan and Koeta-Radja meetings of [[January]] [[1914]], the Noordkust van Atjeh was merged with the [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] into a single *afdeeling* "Noord- en Oostkust van Atjeh behalve Tamiang" (North and East Coast of Atjeh except Tamiang) with a population of approximately 480,000, making it the most populous of the proposed Atjeh-based *afdeelingen*. This merger was consistent with the reorganization's guiding principle of creating *afdeelingen* of sufficient demographic and economic scale to support the administrative apparatus of the new governance structure. The combined Noord- en Oostkust *afdeeling* would encompass the entire northern and eastern coastal zone of Atjeh, creating a substantial administrative unit with a population that approached half a million.

The merger of the Noordkust and Oostkust into a single *afdeeling* represented a significant rationalization of the existing boundary structure. Under the *Gouvernement* system, the two *afdeelingen* had been separate primarily because of the incremental nature of Dutch military expansion—different coastal zones had been occupied at different times and had been assigned to different Assistent-Residenten as the colonial state extended its reach. By 1914, with the territory under consolidated control, the historical basis for this separation had diminished, and the creation of a unified coastal *afdeeling* made both administrative and geographic sense. The *onderafdeeling* [[Pidië]] continued as a subdivision within this merged unit, preserving the recognition of its distinct administrative identity within the larger *afdeeling* structure.

## Population and Demographic Significance

With a combined population of approximately 480,000, the proposed "Noord- en Oostkust van Atjeh" was by far the most populous of the Atjeh-based *afdeelingen* in the new [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]]. The next most populous was the combined "Groot-Atjeh benevens de Westkust van Atjeh" with approximately 210,000 inhabitants. This disparity in population between the coastal *afdeeling* and the interior/coastal combination reflected the demographic concentration along the northern and eastern littorals of Atjeh, where the most fertile agricultural land and the longest-established settlements were located. The large population of the combined Noord- en Oostkust *afdeeling* also meant that it would require a correspondingly robust administrative apparatus, including a Resident and the full complement of subordinate officials necessary to govern nearly half a million people spread across a substantial coastal territory.

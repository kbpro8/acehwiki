**Telok Semawé** (Van der Kemp's spelling; rendered in British documents as *Teloksamoy* or *Tullosumoy*; also *Telok Semawó* in OCR variants) was a town on the East Coast of [[Acheh]], situated between [[Pedir]] and [[Penang]] and described by Van der Kemp as lying to the west of Pedir. In the second decade of the nineteenth century it became the seat of the rival court of [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum]] (Syf) after he was driven from [[Banda Acheh]], and thereafter the scene of the [[Wilhelmina mutiny and murder of Bouwens|Wilhelmina affair]] and of recurring Anglo-Dutch friction. In [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, Telok Semawé is also the seat of the *assistent-resident ter Noord- en Oostkust* under [[Scherer]]'s administration of [[1877]]–[[1884]], one of the open call-ports abolished under the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] of [[19 August]] [[1884]], and the post re-established under the [[Van Teijn]] *scheepvaartregeling* of [[1887]]–[[1891]].

## Syf's seat

After his installation at [[Banda Acheh|Acheh]] in mid-[[1815]] by his father [[Said Hussein]]'s expedition, [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum]] soon found the capital too hot to hold. Following the example of his rival [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]], who had retired to [[Pedir]], Syf "zijn verblijf koos te Telok Semawé, ook op de Oostkust tusschen Pedir en Penang". From this East Coast seat he carried on his contest with Jauhar, levying what revenues he could and at times indulging in outright piracy against passing shipping. From Telok Semawé, after the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|treaty of 22 April 1819]] with Jauhar, the Raffles–Coombs commission sailed to deliver Syf a stern warning: he was told to abandon his claims and answered that he submitted. The submission was worthless, for in July 1819 he still wrote to the Governor of [[Penang]] as prince of Acheh and pursued his "rooversleven" unpunished.

## The Wilhelmina affair

In June [[1819]] the Dutch colonial schooner *Wilhelmina*, under [[Lieutenant Marx Bouwens|Lieutenant Bouwens]], anchored at Acheh while carrying 10,000 dollars and arms on a mission for the government of [[Calcutta]]. Her crew mutinied and murdered Bouwens and two of his men; the lieutenant's wife leapt overboard in terror and two of her children were thrown into the sea. The next day the ship reached [[Pasangan]], six miles from Telok Semawé, where the 24 mutineers landed with their plunder. They then walked along the shore towards Telok Semawé, but were themselves plundered by the coastal population on the way, and on arrival at Syf's seat were stripped, chained in irons for some weeks, and put to work building a house for the pretender. Syf took 800 dollars of the buried treasure, and the British agent [[Sartorius]] was only later able to recover eleven of the criminals, who were sent via [[Malacca]] to [[Batavia]].

## The Lucas mission of 1821

The Dutch government, on learning of the affair, instructed the Governor of Malacca, [[Timmerman Thyssen]], to demand the stolen money and the surviving mutineers. The despatch of [[20 January]] [[1821]] was conveyed by the man-of-war *Irene* under Colonel [[Colonel E. Lucas|E. Lucas]] — the [[Lucas mission to Telok Semawé (February 1821)|Lucas mission]]. The Penang government under [[W. E. Phillips|Phillips]], however, refused to allow Lucas himself to proceed to Telok Semawé. Phillips reasoned that Syf had so long been the ruler there that the place would be "the last to acknowledge the authority of Sultan Djohor Alum", England's ally by the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|treaty of 1819]]. The Penang secretary [[W. A. Clubley|Clubley]] accordingly answered Lucas that Syf, "in the admitted exercise of sovereignty at Teloksamoy at the time the mutineers of the brig Wilhelmina arrived with their plunder", could not be called to account for his acts. The *Irene* returned empty — Van der Kemp's "Papieroorlog" closed without result.

## After Syf

Telok Semawé remained a place of resort for the disaffected of Acheh. After his submission to the Raffles–Coombs commission, Syf had in fact briefly joined [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]] at the capital against Jauhar, then fled in early [[1820]] to [[Calcutta]] on a captured junk, where he was eventually pensioned and returned to [[Penang]] (the [[Syf's pension at Penang]]). The place nevertheless continued to be cited in Dutch and British dispatches as a watchpoint on the East Coast, and appears in the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|treaty]] itself: Article 5 guaranteed to British ships "their commercial intercourse with the ports of Acheen and Teluksamoy", subject only to a temporary blockade by mutual consent.

## Telok Semawe in the Aceh War (1874-1881)

In the later period of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], Telok Semawe was a coast-state on the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] which hoisted the Dutch flag on [[July 1874|July 1874]]. In [[August 1874|August 1874]] other coastal states including Pasanga, Kertoï and Endjoeng on the north coast, Perlak on the east coast, Batih and Kloewang on the west coast followed; in [[October 1874|October]] Pasei and Djolok also submitted. By the end of [[1874]] the Dutch flag had not yet been accepted by [[Telok Kroet|Telok Kroet]], [[Rigas|Rigas]] and [[Tenom|Tenom]] on the west coast, by [[Pedir|Pedir]], [[Merdoe|Merdoe]] and [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] on the north coast, and by [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]], [[Arakoendoer|Arakoendoer]] and [[Langsar|Langsar]] on the east coast.

Under the [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|administrative reorganisation of March 1881]], Telok Semawé became the chief place of the assistant-residency of the [[Noord- en Oostkust|Noord- en Oostkust]], one of the three assistant-residencies of the *gewest* Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden. The controleurs of the assistant-residency were stationed at [[Edi|Edi]], [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] and [[Segli|Segli]]. To the landscapes belonging to this *afdeeling*, however, self-government was left; only in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] was direct Dutch administration introduced.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, Telok Semawé was the north-coast port of the [[Atjeh|Atjehnese]] [[Onderhoorigheden]] that served as the seat of the *assistent-resident ter Noord- en Oostkust* under [[Scherer]]'s administration of [[1877]]–[[1884]], as one of the three open call-ports abolished under the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] of [[19 August]] [[1884]], and as the post re-established under the [[Van Teijn]] *scheepvaartregeling* of [[1887]]–[[1891]] — only to be abolished one month after Scherer's departure. The port's institutional history was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the empirical measure of the *Concentratie*'s destructive capacity: a post at which *onder Scherer's bestuur geleidelijk een ordelijken staat van zaken was ingetreden* — under Scherer's administration an orderly state of affairs had gradually set in — was abolished in [[1884]] and the orderly state of affairs with it.

### Seat of the assistant-resident for Noord- en Oostkust

The institutional form by which the *Noord- en Oostkust* was administered under Scherer was the *assistent-resident ter Noord- en Oostkust (standplaats Telok Semawé)* — the assistant-resident for the North- and East Coast, with his seat at Telok Semawé. Scherer himself arrived on the Noordkust in [[1877]] as *waarnemend assistent-resident* — *op dit tijdstip was juist, als wd. assistent-resident, op de Noordkust gekomen de man — de tegenwoordige resident Scherer — uit wiens helder brein het eerst het denkbeeld van eene rationeele regeling voor handel en scheepvaart in verband met onze vestigingen op de kusten, is gesproten* (at this time there had just arrived on the Noordkust, as acting assistant-resident, the man — the present resident Scherer — from whose clear brain the idea of a rational regulation for commerce and shipping in connection with our establishments on the coasts first sprang) — and from his seat at Telok Semawé originated the *scheepvaartregeling* idea that grew into the systematic *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]* of May [[1883]].

### Scherer's administration and the 1884 abolition

Under Scherer's administration, Telok Semawé became one of the statelets where the *scheepvaartregeling* produced its intended effect of gradual order. Van Heutsz records the result in his catalogue of the casualties of the [[1884]] *Concentratie*: *Telok Semawé, Kerti, benevens andere plaatsen, waar onder Scherer's bestuur geleidelijk een ordelijken staat van zaken was ingetreden en 's bestuurs invloed en gezag zich meer en meer vestigden* (Telok Semawé, Kerti, besides other places, where under Scherer's administration an orderly state of affairs had gradually set in and the influence and authority of the administration were establishing themselves more and more). The *ordelijken staat van zaken* — the orderly state of affairs — that had gradually set in under Scherer's administration at Telok Semawé and [[Kerti]] was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the empirical proof of the *scheepvaartregeling*'s productive capacity. The *Concentratie* of [[1884]] abolished the post at Telok Semawé together with the post at [[Samalanga]], and reduced the open call-ports of the *Noord- en Oostkust* from a wider ring to the three named in the [[Demmeni]] *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]]: *toen de posten Telok Semawé en Samalanga waren ingetrokken, alleen Edi, Segli en Oleh-leh voor de geheele Noord- en Oostkust* (when the posts Telok Semawé and Samalanga had been withdrawn, only Edi, Segli and Oleh-leh for the entire Noord- en Oostkust). The abolition of the Telok Semawé post was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, one of the *Concentratie*'s destructive casualties — the dismantling of an institutional infrastructure that had taken Scherer seven years to build.

### Re-establishment under Van Teijn and abolition after Scherer's departure

The Telok Semawé post was re-established under the [[Van Teijn]] governorship of [[1886]]–[[1891]]. Van Heutsz records the re-establishment: *reeds in datzelfde jaar, even als zijne voorgangers, eene goed ingerichte scheepvaartregeling met wederoprichting van den post te Telok Semawé noodzakelijk achtte, wilde men niet tot in 't oneindige op de onderwerping wachten* (already in that same year, like his predecessors, [Van Teijn] considered a well-ordered *scheepvaartregeling* with the re-establishment of the post at Telok Semawé necessary, if one did not wish to wait indefinitely for submission). The re-establishment was, in Van Heutsz's argument, the empirical proof of the *Concentratie*'s destructiveness: the post that had been abolished as superfluous in [[1884]] had to be re-established in [[1887]] as indispensable. The Telok Semawé post's re-established life was short: *de post Telok Semawé, die een maand na Scherer's vertrek werd ingerukt* (the post Telok Semawé, which was withdrawn a month after Scherer's departure). The post at Telok Semawé was abolished one month after Scherer's departure — the empirical measure of the dependence of the *scheepvaartregeling* on the personality of its principal administrator.

### The 1890 closure and the European trader Atkins

Under the [[Van Teijn]] closure of [[1890]], Telok Semawé was one of the three statelets — with [[Kerti]] on the Noordkust and [[Telok Kroet]] on the Westkust — for which a *beperkte invoer van levensmiddelen door middel van licenties* (limited import of foodstuffs by means of licences) was permitted. The licensed-import arrangement was the one weak spot of Van Teijn's *sluiting*-stelsel — *Slechts eene zaak leverde moeilijkheden op en dat waren de licenties van invoer voor Telok Semawé en Kerti* (Only one thing caused difficulties and that was the import licences for Telok Semawé and Kerti) — and Van Heutsz criticises the over-liberal handling of the Telok Semawé-Kerti licences as the one operational flaw in the otherwise rigorous closure. The closure at Telok Semawé was, however, safe enough for a resident European trader: *Op ditzelfde Telok Semawé exerceerde nog in 1891, tijdens de sluiting, herhaaldelijk de landingsdivisie van H. M. Koning der Nederlanden en heeft er ook tijdens de sluiting, steeds een Europeesche handelaar de heer Atkins gewoond* (At this same Telok Semawé the landing-division of H. M. the King of the Netherlands exercised repeatedly in 1891, during the closure, and during the closure a European trader, Mr. Atkins, lived there continuously). The Atkins residence at Telok Semawé was Van Heutsz's exhibit that the closure, properly enforced, was compatible with a European commercial presence.

### The 1893 prescription: Telok Semawé as one of four Noordkust ports

In Van Heutsz's own [[1893]] prescription, Telok Semawé was retained as one of the open ports of the *scheepvaartregeling*. Scherer's [[20 January]] [[1891]] *lezing* before the *Indisch Genootschap* had named Telok Semawé as one of four Noordkust ports — *Op de Noordkust: 1 Edi. 2 Telok Semawé 3 Samalanga 4 Segli* (On the Noordkust: 1. Edi, 2. Telok Semawé, 3. Samalanga, 4. Segli) — and Van Teijn had endorsed the reduced list of three: *De Generaal van Teijn achtte op de Oost- en Noordkust: Edi, Telok Semawé en Segli, althans vooreerst, voldoende* (General van Teijn considered Edi, Telok Semawé and Segli sufficient, at least for the present, on the East- and North Coast). Van Heutsz concurred, and the prescription that closed the [[1893]] tract retained Telok Semawé as one of the open call-ports of the *scheepvaartregeling* Van Heutsz would himself implement as Governor of Atjeh from [[1898]] onward.

**Lam-Djamoe** — written in Dutch variously as *Lamdjamoe*, *Lam-Djamoe*, *Lamdjamoе*, *Lamdjampoe*, *Lamdjanoe*, and (in the errata) *Lambjamoo* — was a [[Dutch]] post in the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcentreerde Linie (Concentrated Line)]] of the southern sector, situated at the foot of the mountain line and serving as the principal site of the siege battery that bombarded the *stellingen* of [[Teuku Umar|T. Oemar]] from [[27 April]] to [[23 May]] [[1896]]. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the Lam-Djamoe battery was built with *mortieren van 20 c.M.*, *12 c.M. A.*, and *7 c.M. A. veldbatterij*, and was reinforced on [[27 April]] [[1896]] with 5 officers and 105 other ranks of the *vestingartillerie*. The post was a communications hub linked by telephone to the forward area. The batteries were disarmed and demolished on [[12 June]] [[1896]].

## Description and Location

Lam-Djamoe lay in the southern sector of the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcente Linie (Concentrated Line)]] at the foot of the mountain line that separated the Dutch inner perimeter from the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]]. The post was the assembly point for troops moving forward to the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] and was the principal communications hub (*telefoon*) of the southern sector. The siege battery at Lam-Djamoe was constructed with 20 cm mortars, 12 cm bronze guns, and a 7 cm bronze field battery. The post was reinforced on [[27 April]] [[1896]] with 5 officers and 105 *minderen* of the siege artillery. The post commanded the road toward [[Lam-Pisang]] and the *stellingen* of the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]].

## Historical Background

Lam-Djamoe had been constructed as part of the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcentreerde Linie (Concentrated Line)]] — the inner line of Dutch posts around [[Koeta Radja]] that predated the [[buitenlinie]]. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] and the evacuation of the [[buitenlinie]] in [[April]] [[1896]], Lam-Djamoe became the forward Dutch post in the southern sector. The construction of the *belegeringsbatterij* at Lam-Djamoe was planned from [[26 April]] [[1896]] (per the TOC: "Batterij bouw te Lamdjamoe 26 April"). On [[30 March]] [[1896]] three companies of the 3e Bataljon were sent to Lam-Djamoe by steam tram at 2:15 PM, reinforced by 2 sections of the 4e Bergbatterij at 5 PM.

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Lam-Djamoe was the principal Dutch artillery base for the [[Bombardment of Lampisang|bombardment of Lampisang]] from [[27 April]] to [[23 May]] [[1896]]. On [[27 April]] [[1896]] the *belegeringsbatterij* was reinforced with 5 officers and 105 *minderen* of the *vestingartillerie*; the battery comprised *mortieren van 20 c.M.*, *12 c.M. A.*, and a *7 c.M. A. veldbatterij*. From [[27 April]] to [[23 May]] [[1896]] the battery bombarded the *stellingen* of [[Teuku Umar|T. Oemar]] at [[Lam-Pisang]], [[Lam-Asan]], and the heights of [[Tjot Tjako]] and [[Boekit Seboen]]. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] was launched from Lam-Djamoe (and from [[Lamtih]], [[Ketapan Doea]], and [[Blang]]), with the 9e Bataljon marching from the *koogelvanger van Blang* near Lam-Djamoe against [[Lam-Asan]]. On [[5 June]] [[1896]] the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|two-colonne operation]] set out from Lam-Djamoe. On [[12 June]] [[1896]] the batteries of Lam-Djamoe were disarmed and demolished, marking the end of the siege-battery phase of the operations. Throughout the operations Lam-Djamoe served as the principal telephone communications hub of the southern sector.

## Significance

Lam-Djamoe was the principal Dutch artillery and communications base for the [[1896]] counter-offensive against [[Teuku Umar]]. Its *belegeringsbatterij* — the largest concentration of siege artillery in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] since [[1873]] — was the material instrument by which the Dutch broke the *stellingen* of the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] and made possible the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. The disarmament and demolition of the batteries on [[12 June]] [[1896]] marked the transition from the siege phase to the mobile-operations phase of the war.

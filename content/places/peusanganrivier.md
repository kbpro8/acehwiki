**The Peusanganrivier** — the Peusangan River — is the river of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] that carries the water of the [[Laut Tawar]] to the sea, and that is crossed by the [[Gajoweg]] at km 11 — by the bridge over the Peusangan River, which carries the water of Laut Tawar to the sea, one of the longest pile-bridges in the whole of the Dutch East Indies.

## The bridge over the Peusanganrivier

At km 11 of the Gajoweg one passes the bridge over the Peusanganrivier, which carries the water of Laut Tawar to the sea. This bridge — one of the longest pile-bridges in the whole of the Dutch East Indies — is the book's marker of the hydraulic engineers' work on the Gajoweg. The Peusanganrivier bridge is therefore the book's marker of the colonial army's infrastructure on the Gajoweg: the longest pile-bridges in the whole of the Dutch East Indies.

## Significance

The Peusanganrivier is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the river that carries the water of the [[Laut Tawar]] to the sea and that is crossed by the [[Gajoweg]] at km 11. The book's argument — that the Peusanganrivier bridge was one of the longest pile-bridges in the whole of the Dutch East Indies — is the book's marker of the hydraulic engineers' work on the Gajoweg. The Peusanganrivier is therefore the book's marker of the colonial army's bridge-building work on the Gajoweg.

**Willemsoord** is a historic **naval base** and **Marine Corps training facility** located in **Den Helder**, the Netherlands.  Established in the early 19th century, it served as the primary embarkation point for the **Royal Netherlands Marine Corps** (Korps Mariniers) before deployments to the **Dutch East Indies**, including the numerous **Aceh** campaigns documented in the *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pages [[489]]–[[490]]).

## Foundation and Early Use

The base was founded in [[1823]] under the reign of **Willem I** to house the newly reorganised Dutch naval forces.  By [[1840]], during the reign of **Willem II**, Willemsoord had become the central training ground for marine infantry destined for colonial service.

## Role in Aceh Campaigns

From the first marine expedition to **Banda Aceh** in [[1873]] under **Willem III**, Willemsoord functioned as the staging area where troops received amphibious training, jungle survival instruction, and logistical preparation.  The *Mariniers* archives (page [[489]]) list the embarkation of several marine battalions from Willemsoord for the **Aceh War** ([[1873]]–[[1904]]).

## Infrastructure and Legacy

The base housed a **marine barracks**, a **dockyard**, and a **training school** that persisted through the reigns of **Willem III**, **Wilhelmina**, and into the post‑World War II era.  After Indonesian independence, Willemsoord was retained by the Dutch Navy and continues to serve as a historic site and museum.

## Significance

Willemsoord’s strategic importance lies in its function as the logistical hub that enabled the Dutch to project marine power across the seas to **Aceh**, making it a pivotal element in the execution of Dutch colonial military policy.

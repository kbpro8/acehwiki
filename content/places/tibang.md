**Tibang** was a Dutch fortified post in the [[XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], one of the small square earth redoubts planted in the marshy ground along the left bank of the [[Kroeëng Titi Pandjang]], which guarded the eastern approaches of the establishment at [[Penajoeng]] and observed the hostile ground east of that river in the years [[1874]]–[[1876]].

## The post

Tibang stood — with [[Lamara]] Northeast and Southeast, [[Langkroek]] and Langkroek East and [[Moesapi]] — among the small sealed works of the marsh line. The terrain there, divided by a great number of ridges of half to one and a half feet wide into very irregular portions — some used as salt pans, others as fish ponds — flooded at the least high water; the drinking water had to be fetched by the garrison itself from Tibang, which created a continuous armed traffic along the line in daytime. From this post the whole stretch of the Titi Pandjang and the hostile kampongs east of it were kept in observation.

## 1876

In July [[1876]] the garrison of Tibang discovered one morning that the enemy had built a staked fence across the whole width of the Kroeëng Titi Pandjang close to the work, sheltering Atjehnese fishers and hostile traffic persons from view. On [[26 July]] the post commander sent a patrol over the river at low water; the enemy let the workmen remove the stakes undisturbed, the patrol crossing onwards towards the [[Kroeëng Tjoet]] and back without seeing one Atjeher, burning some empty houses, taking a few weapons and destroying fishing gear and boats. That underscored what Meijer records of this post as of the others of the line: continually fired on in the summer of that year, at no cost in blood.

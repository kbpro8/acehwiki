**The monument to Teukoe Oemar's death** was the great memorial standing beside the coastal road of the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast of Atjeh]] marking the place where the Acehnese leader [[Teuku Umar|Teukoe Oemar]] fell, recorded by [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* as one of the features of the landscape he traversed in [[1933]].

## The description

Tijssen described the district he travelled after [[23 August]] [[1933]] as "een weinig bevolkte kuststrook van 500 K.M. lengte, maar nauwelijks 5 K.M. breed. Er loopt een per auto berijdbare weg door de as; aan weerskanten de massagraven herinnerend aan de vele Atjehoorlogen en den opstand van '26. Een groot monument wijst de plaats van Teukoe Oemar's sneuvelen." — a thinly peopled coastal strip 500 kilometres long, but scarcely 5 kilometres broad. A road passable by motor car runs through its axis; on both sides the mass graves recalling the many Atjeh wars and the rising of '26. A great monument marks the place of Teukoe Oemar's fall.

He concluded the description with the sentence that gives the passage its force: "Alles spreekt van strijd, verzet, haat. Alles behalve het volk."

## Significance

The monument, together with the *massagraven* flanking the road, is the pamphlet's record of the physical memory landscape of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as it stood on the West Coast in the [[1930s]] — a monument to the death of the Acehnese leader erected by the victors, in a district where, Tijssen noted, no officer went into a *[[Kampong|kampong]]* without a soldier behind him with a loaded carbine, and where he himself nevertheless lived unarmed among the people for weeks without anyone harming a hair of his head. Asked why it was there, just there, that things had always been so turbulent, he answered: "Ik weet het niet en ik heb het niet begrepen." — I do not know it and I have not understood it.

**Mesjid Raya Aceh** (the Great Mosque of Aceh) was the central religious structure and primary defensive bastion guarding the northern approach to the [[Kraton of Aceh]] in [[Banda Aceh]].

## Architecture and Defense Role

The original wooden mosque was surrounded by massive masonry walls 2.5 meters high, crowned by thick foliage and flanking earthworks. Positioned approximately 300 meters north of the royal palace, it served as a formidable fortress during the Aceh War.

## Battles for the Mosque

During the [[First Aceh Expedition]], Dutch forces under Major Cavaljé executed the [[First Capture of Mesjid Raya]] on [[10 April]] [[1873]]. Mortar fire ignited the wooden roof, forcing both Acehnese defenders and Dutch storming parties to evacuate due to extreme heat.

On [[14 April]] [[1873]], during the [[Second Capture of Mesjid Raya]], Dutch troops re-assaulted the walls using storming ladders. During this engagement, Dutch Supreme Commander [[Johan Harmen Rudolf Köhler]] was shot and killed by an Acehnese sniper while inspecting the eastern wall.

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], General [[Jan van Swieten]] ordered a massive artillery bombardment against the mosque, culminating in the [[Third Capture of Mesjid Raya]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] by the 2nd Brigade. The three captures of the Great Mosque cost over 375 Dutch casualties.

## Reconstruction

Following its destruction during the war, the Dutch colonial government rebuilt the mosque in [[1879]]–[[1881]] in an Indo-Saracenic architectural style as a gesture of conciliation toward the Acehnese population.

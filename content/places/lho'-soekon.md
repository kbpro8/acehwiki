**Lho' Soekon** — also written *Lho Soekon*, *Lho' Sukon* — is a region on the [[Noordkust|Noordkust]] of [[Atjeh|Atjèh], in the modern Lhokseumawe region, one of the principal rice-cultivable regions of Atjèh. The region, together with [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] and [[Peusangan|Peusangan]], is estimated in the [[De Rijkdom van Atjeh (1923)|1923 brochure]] of the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] at 70,000 hectares of rice-cultivable land, making it one of the principal rice-producing regions of Atjèh in the post-war period.

## The Rice-Cultivable Land

The surfaces suitable or to be made suitable for rice cultivation, in the various administrative afdeelingen considered in the Atjeh-Instituut brochure, include — for Samalanga, Peusangan, and Lho' Soekon (Noordkust) — 70,000 hectares. The region is one of the principal rice-producing regions of Atjèh, and is part of the post-war Dutch program for the development of the [[rijstbouw|rijstbouw]] as the principal future export crop of Atjèh.

## Significance

Lho' Soekon is one of the principal rice-producing regions of Atjèh and one of the principal components of the post-war Dutch program for the development of the [[rijstbouw|rijstbouw]] as the principal future export crop of Atjèh. The 70,000 hectares of rice-cultivable land in the Samalanga / Peusangan / Lho' Soekon region constitute the largest single block of rice-cultivable land in Atjèh, and are the principal basis of the Atjeh-Instituut's prescription of rice as the new great-trade article that must take the place of the declining [[pepercultuur|pepercultuur]].

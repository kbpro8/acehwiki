**Lhokseumawe** is a city on the east coast of **[[Aceh]]**, located on the Bay of Lhokseumawe. It was the site of the surrender of **[[Panglima Polem]]** to **H. Colijn** on **[[15 May]] [[1903]]**, an event that marked the effective end of organized **[[Acehnese]]** resistance during the **[[Aceh War]]**.

## Strategic Importance

Lhokseumawe's location on the east coast made it a strategic point for controlling the route to the interior regions of Pidië and Samalanga. It was also a center of trade and communication.

## The Surrender of Panglima Polem

After the fall of **[[Batêê Iliê]]** in [[1901]], Panglima Polem retreated to the interior. He was relentlessly pursued by Dutch *marechaussees* under Colijn. On **[[15 May]] [[1903]]**, Panglima Polem surrendered to Colijn at Lhokseumawe.

The event received little attention in the Netherlands at the time, appearing in the newspapers as a small item among "mixed news." However, it was understood by military officers as a decisive moment.

## Later History

After the surrender, Panglima Polem was reinstated as head of the XXII *moekims*, overseeing administration and development. Lhokseumawe became a center of Dutch administrative control in the region.

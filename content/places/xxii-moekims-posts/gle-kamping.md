**Glé-Kamping** (also spelled Glé-Kabiig) was a Dutch military post at the eastern end of the communication line through the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The post was evacuated on [[13 January]] [[1885]] as part of the concentration strategy.

## Location

Glé-Kamping was situated at the eastern end of the 121-palen communication line extending from [[Lambaroe]] through the [[XXII Moekims]]. The position was between [[Long-Lemoh]] and [[Indrapoori]].

## Pre-Concentration Status

Glé-Kamping was one of the posts established during the expeditions of [[General van der Heyden]] in [[1878]]–[[1879]]. The post served as:
- A staging point for the eastern communication line
- A forward position against the interior
- Part of the chain of isolated positions that characterized Dutch presence

## Evacuation

Glé-Kamping was evacuated on [[13 January]] [[1885]] along with [[Indrapoori]], [[Djerir]], [[Long-Lemoh]], [[Tjot-Basetoel]], [[Samagani]], and [[Lepong-Ara]].

## Significance

The evacuation of Glé-Kamping marked another step in the systematic withdrawal from the extended communication line. The post had symbolized the reach of Dutch operations; its abandonment acknowledged the limits of that reach.

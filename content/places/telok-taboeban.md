**Telok Taboeban** (also rendered **Telok Taboehan**) was a town and roadstead on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] that served, in the [[1913]] extension approvals of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]], as the projected terminus of the line from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] — the line that, together with the [[Koeala Simpang]]–[[Tamiang]]–[[Besitang]] extension and the [[Besitang]]–[[Pangkalan Soesoe]] connecting line, would form the [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] outlet of the entire Atjeh-tram network.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Telok Taboeban

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[1913]] extension approvals places [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboeban]] at the maritime terminus of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]]: by the G. Bn. of [[17 June]] [[1913]] No. 59, [[16 October]] [[1913]] No. 25, and [[8 November]] [[1913]] No. 40, "the doortrekking was approved of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] network from [[Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] to Telok Taboeban (on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]], with a good roadstead), while the connecting line between [[Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] is also already in preparation."

The Bureau's account of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] and of the contested relationship between the Atjeh-tram extension and the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]] also identifies Telok Taboeban as the proposed junction for a direct connection with [[Pangkalan Brandan]] — a connection that the Bureau reports as "now under consideration" (*thans in overweging*) in preference to the originally proposed direct [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat]] junction.

## Significance

[[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboeban]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the maritime anchor of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] — the roadstead on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] at which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] would, after the [[1913]] extensions, finally meet the sea. The construction of the line to Telok Taboeban was the linchpin of the project to develop [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as a deep-water outlet for the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] export trade.

## Account from J. Roest (1916) — the design and rejection of the great harbour, 1912–1915

[[J. Roest]]'s article *[[De spoorwegverbinding tusschen Deli, Atjeh en de Aroebaai]]* ([[1916]]) gives the place at the height of its promise: the Government had chosen it as the end point of the [[Atjeh-tram]] and the new coast harbour of [[Tamiang]], against the judgement of [[Schadee]] that existing traffic was seated in the river of [[Pangkalan Brandan]] and that the expectation of traffic shifting to a new place was groundless. The army chief of construction defended the eastern trace to it as the technically better approach for connection with Brandan; the site had, not far from the coast, water deep enough for the ships coming in by the [[Semibilangeul|Sembilan channel]] and a water surface broad enough for their turning, sheltered by the opposite island [[Poeloe Sembilan]].

For the personnel houses of the projected harbour, preliminary talks had been opened for a site on the island Sembilan — part of grounds leased out in concession by the Native Self-Government of [[Langkat]] to the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]] for oil-winning; and f 150,000 stood on the draft budget for [[1916]] for a beginning of the work. Against it Roest's later pages record the accumulated arguments: the low, marshy coast, far from any settlement, with no expectation of Chinese traders and native labour in short time; the expensive sanitation works needed before it could be lived in; the lifeless stretch to [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] over which all its loads must pass with none taken up along the way. All this, and the success of the dredger *Java* at [[Belawan]], decided the change of [[1915]]: the harbour installation was not named more for Telok Taboehan but for [[Pangkalan Soesoe]]. The roadbed of the stretch Pangkalan Soesoe–Telok Taboehan, already built at a cost of f 200,000, would fall away unused.

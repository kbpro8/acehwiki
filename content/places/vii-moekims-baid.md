**VII Moekims Baid** — written in Dutch as *VII Moekims Baid* and meaning "Seven Mukims of Baid" — was a region of [[Groot-Atjeh]] comprising seven [[moekim|mukims]] situated in the southern inland portion of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre, of which the territorial chief was [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|T. Moeda Baid]]. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the VII Moekims Baid contained the mukims of [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak, Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue]], "welke steeds eene verblijfplaats van kwaad volk waren" ("which were always a haunt of bad people"). The VII Moekims Baid was crossed by [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] and [[Toeankoe Brahim]] in late [[May]] [[1896]] en route to [[Groot-Atjeh]], and [[Ana' Galong]] — at the eastern edge of the mukim — had originally been built to keep "[[Toekoe Moeda Baid|T. Baid]]" in line.

## Description and Location

The VII Moekims Baid lay to the south of the [[IX Moekims]] and adjoined the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak complex]] on the north. The mukim contained the kampongs of [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak, Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue]], which were the principal haunts of hostile *benden* and which the [[Dutch]] sought to suppress by their operations against the [[Lamkrak Moekims|4 Lamkraksche Moekims]]. The mukim was bounded on the south by the mountain wall and on the north by the [[IX Moekims]]. The [[III Moekims Daroe]] lay to the east. The VII Moekims Baid was traversed by the road from [[Ana' Galong]] southward to the Lamkrak country, and was the principal recruiting ground for the Pedireezen mercenaries of the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] war-leaders.

## Historical Background

The VII Moekims Baid had been the principal southern bastion of Achehnese resistance since [[1873]]. The *hoeloebalang* [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|T. Moeda Baid]] had been a persistent opponent of the Dutch, and the [[Dutch]] post of [[Ana' Galong]] at the eastern edge of the mukim had been built originally to keep "[[Toekoe Moeda Baid|T. Baid]]" in line. The mukims of [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak, Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue]] within the VII Moekims Baid were the persistent haunt of hostile *benden* — "welke steeds eene verblijfplaats van kwaad volk waren" — that raided the [[IX Moekims]] and threatened the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of the [[buitenlinie]]. The [[1893]] appointment of [[Teuku Umar]] as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] had been intended in part to suppress these mukims, and the proposed [[30 March]] [[1896]] operation of [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Luitenant-Kolonel Van Vliet]] (cancelled by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]]) had targeted them directly.

## Role in the 1896 Operations

The VII Moekims Baid was crossed by [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] and [[Toeankoe Brahim]] in late [[May]] [[1896]] en route to [[Groot-Atjeh]] following their move to [[Ana' Galong]] — a passage that the [[Dutch]] interpreted as a step in the reconstitution of Achehnese political authority under the [[Pretender Sultan|pretender sultan]]. The mukims of [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak, Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue]] within the VII Moekims Baid were the explicit object of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging (chastisement) of the Lamkrak Moekims of 9–10 June 1896]], in which two Dutch colonnes moved from [[Biloel]] and [[Lam-Djamoe]] against the kampongs of [[Ateuh Raja]], [[Baha]], [[Lam-Djamé|Lam-Krak / Lam-Djamé]], and the [[Masdjid Lam-Krak|fortified mosque of Lam-Krak]]. The VII Moekims Baid was protected on the south by the *gebergte* and the [[Glé Taroempas|Glé Taroem-pas]], and was a thoroughfare for hostile *benden* moving between the Lamkrak country and the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]].

## Significance

The VII Moekims Baid was the principal southern refuge of Achehnese resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The failure of the [[Dutch]] to reduce the Lamkrak mukims within the VII Moekims Baid before [[1896]] had been a major strategic limitation of the *buitenlinie* policy, and the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 9–10 June 1896]] was the first systematic Dutch operation against these mukims. The passage of [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] and [[Toeankoe Brahim]] through the VII Moekims Baid in late [[May]] [[1896]] indicated the continued political importance of the region in the Achehnese imagination.

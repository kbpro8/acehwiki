**The III Moekims** — the "Three Mukims" — were one of the smaller mukim-clusters of the immediate Sultan's territory in [[Groot-Atjeh]], and the territory over which [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] — the principal religious-political figure of the early Dutch war period — was given the title of *Hoofd van Tjot Poetee in de III Moekims* (Head of Tjot Poetee in the III Moekims) as one of the rewards for his service to the Sultan. The III Moekims are named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] in the chapter on the *Inwendige Toestand* as one of the constituent mukim-clusters of the Sultan's immediate territory.

## The Sultan's immediate territory

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the immediate territory of the Sultan — distinct from the three great *sagis* — included the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] and [[Kota Radja]], the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]], [[Marassa]], [[Longbatta|Longbattah]], the III Moekims, the [[IV Moekims]], the [[VI Moekims]] and the [[IX Moekims]]. The III Moekims thus formed part of the directly-ruled core of the sultanate — the mukim-cluster over which the Sultan exercised direct temporal authority.

## The Habib's reward

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after the conflict between [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] and [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]] — in which the Habib, at the head of a force of two thousand men from the [[XXVI Moekims]], had marched on [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Baid]]'s *kampong* of [[Tjot Bada]] to compel the surrender of the young Sultan [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] — the Habib was rewarded for his services with the title of *Hoofd van den Godsdienst* (Head of Religion), *Hoofd van Tjot Poetee in de III Moekims* (Head of Tjot Poetee in the III Moekims) and *Chef van de Groote Moskee* (Chief of the Great Mosque). The title of *Hoofd van Tjot Poetee in de III Moekims* was thus one of the three principal rewards given to the Habib for his service to the Sultan — and it placed the III Moekims under the Habib's temporal authority, in addition to his religious authority.

## Significance

The III Moekims' significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as one of the constituent mukim-clusters of the Sultan's immediate territory, and as the territory over which [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] was given the title of *Hoofd van Tjot Poetee* — one of the three principal rewards for his service to the Sultan. The title was the principal temporal office held by the Habib, and it placed the III Moekims under his direct authority — together with the religious authority that he exercised as *Hoofd van den Godsdienst* and *Chef van de Groote Moskee*.

## Account from *Een Vaderlandsch Belang* by H. Borel (1878)

The **III Moekims** (Drie Moekims, "Three Mukims") was the *sagi* (confederation) of three *mukim* (parish) units east of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], described by Borel as a *sterk bevolkte kampong* on the east of the bivouak at [[Penajoeng]]. It was invaded by [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s three colonnes starting on [[13 February]] [[1876]] in the operation interrupted by the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] and by Pel's own death at [[Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]].

## The eastern sagi

The III Moekims lay east of the Atjeh-rivier, opposite the Dutch post at [[Kota Alam]] at the bend of the river. Its densely populated kampongs — among them [[Atoeh]], [[Lamsajoen]], [[Lambaroe]], [[Pager Ajer]], [[Pango]], [[Tjap Octoe]] — were the principal seat of the resistance east of the Dutch perimeter after the conquest of the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]] on [[31 December]] [[1874]] and the [[XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] sweep of February [[1876]]. The III Moekims was the natural next objective of Pel's offensive, since it formed the eastern boundary of any line isolating [[Groot-Atjeh]] from the sea. Its dense population, fortified kampongs and marshy *sawah* (irrigated rice fields) made it, however, the most difficult sector of the entire theatre. The [[Conquest of Kota Alam (13 November 1874)|post at Kota Alam]] armed with two 12 c.M. *voorlaadkanonnen* had been built specifically to enfilade the river upstream and shell the enemy stelling of [[Longbatta]] and the densely populated III Moekims opposite.

## The invasion of 13 February 1876

[[Generaal Pel|Pel]] granted the troepen only a couple of days' rest at [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] after the [[XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] sweep before advancing in *oostelijke richting* with three colonnes against the III Moekims on [[13 February]] [[1876]]. The *rechter-colonne* (the 13de bataljon + part of the Barissans + one section artillery under [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|majoor B. E. Mekern]]) marched *stroomopwaarts* along the left bank of the Atjeh-rivier. The *midden-colonne* (the 12de bataljon + the remainder of the Barissans + two sections artillery under [[Majoor Jeltes|majoor Jeltes]]) marched from Kota Alam along the right bank against [[Pango]] and the enemy-held [[Longbatta-Mesigit]]. The *linker-colonne* (the *rechter halve bataljon* + a section artillery under [[Majoor Diepenheim|majoor Diepenheim]]) was to cover the flank. On [[13 February]] [[1876]] Pango was conquered and a post opgericht — but the same day saw the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]]. Despite the catastrophe, Pel pressed on through Meroe, Lampermej and [[Tjap Octoe]] on [[14 February|14]]–[[15 February]] [[1876]], but the losses at Tjap Octoe made him decide not to push further eastward. Pel's death at Lamjoeng on [[24 February]] [[1876]] interrupted the operation.

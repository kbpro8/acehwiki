**Montassik** was a kampong in the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], whose [[missigit Montassik|missigit]] — a fortified mosque that served as the principal arsenal of [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman]] — was the object of the [[Capture of Montassik (28 July 1878)|capture of 28 July 1878]], the opening operation of the Dutch campaign in the XXII Moekims of [[23 July]] [[23 July]] [[1878]] – [[23 March]] [[23 March]] [[1879]]. With the taking of Montassik the initial objective of the operations was attained. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the broader *V Moekims Montassik* — written in Dutch as also *V Moekims Mont Tassik* and meaning "Five Mukims of Montassik" — was a region of [[Groot-Atjeh]] comprising five [[moekim|mukims]] situated in the south-eastern portion of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre, of which the district head was [[Toeankoe Brahim|T. Brahim Montassik]] and the [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] was [[T. Rajoet]]. The V Moekims Montassik were a constant source of hostile *benden* (war-bands) against the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of the [[buitenlinie]], and the [[Dutch]] post of [[Senelop]] was constructed as a barrier against them.

## Background (the 1878 campaign against the kampong)

Information had been received that [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman]] had gathered at Montassik in July [1878] a great quantity of food and war-material and was planning thence to make a new incursion into the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]]. The governor [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] therefore decided to forgo the planned expedition to [[Pasanga|Pasanga]] and to enter with all available forces the still independent and hostile territory of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], to seek the enemy in his own arsenal at Montassik. On [[23 July]] [[23 July]] [1878] the important campaign began with three columns.

## The Capture of 28 July 1878

While the first column advanced from [[Lambaroe|Lambaroe]] over [[Lamtenga|Lamtenga]] and the [[missigit Anak Bate|missigit of Anak Bate]] towards Montassik, the second column, which had secured the river-crossing at [[Lamkali|Lamkali]], and the third, which had set out from [[Oleh Karang|Oleh Karang]], reached on [[25 July]] [[25 July]] [1878] the strong [[Senelop|Senelop]] north of Montassik. When the first storming by the second column had been repulsed and the soldiers — mostly Frenchmen and Belgians — could not be moved to a second, the vanguard of the third column, a company of Africans, came up to help and drove the enemy from the parapet; the benting was taken. On [[28 July]] [[28 July]] [1878] the third column, after a powerful bombardment by the artillery, took the missigit Montassik, and with this the initial objective of the operations was attained.

## The Continuation of the Campaign

The neighbouring kampongs were still hostile, and Dutch transports were continually threatened. The enemy did not yet seem inclined to submit. "It was therefore considered desirable to push further on to [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]] with its famous missigit. For this, however, more data about that region had first to be collected, and as the rainy season was again approaching, one had for the time being to confine oneself to holding the positions taken, with preparation for later operations." The campaign continued from [[1 August]] [[1 August]] [1878] to [[23 March]] [[23 March]] [1879] in a system of "standfast, reconnoitre, punish," with constant reconnaissances and patrols, especially in the [[Lamkrak|sawah of Lamkrak]] west of Montassik. On [[25 August]] [[25 August]] [1878] the first overture toward submission was made by Habib Abdurrahman himself, soon followed by others; on [[13 October]] [[13 October]] [1878] he formally [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submitted]] at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]].

## The V Moekims Montassik (ATJEH '96 account)

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the **V Moekims Montassik** were a region of [[Groot-Atjeh]] comprising five [[moekim|mukims]] situated in the south-eastern portion of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre. The mukim of Lam-Djampoe lay entirely within the Dutch post line. The V Moekims Montassik were the object of the [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversionary operation of 2 May 1896]] and of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging (chastisement) of 16–17 June 1896]].

### Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

The V Moekims Montassik lay to the south-east of [[Koeta Radja]] between the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] and the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]], extending from the [[Kroeng Lingkar]] toward the *gebergte* (mountain range) and the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] country. The mukim of Lam-Djampoe lay entirely within the Dutch post line, but the rest of the V Moekims was outside the *buitenlinie*. The principal kampongs were [[Lam-Goet]] (residence of [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata]]), Hoho (with its *masdjid* and imam [[Toekoe Nja Banta|T. Nja Bintang]]), Lam-Djampoe (with its fortified mosque), [[Lam-Bada]]/Anak Baté, and Tjihig (Tjihik). The sawah plain of [[Blang Bintang]] was the corridor by which enemy bands from the V Moekims crossed to harass the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] population.

### Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

The V Moekims Montassik had been a centre of resistance throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The old Dutch post of [[Senelop]] had been conquered in early [[1894]] in the course of operations against the V Moekims Montassik, after which the post was held to act as a *barrière* (barrier) against their *benden* (war-bands). From [[1878]] onward — and especially after the [[Teuku Umar|Umar]] policy of [[1893]] — the V Moekims Montassik were under the influence of [[Toeankoe Brahim|T. Brahim Montassik]] and his brother [[T. Rajoet]], who controlled the *benden* that periodically attacked the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of [[Senelop]], [[Kroeng Gloempang]], and [[Tjot Rang]]. [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata]] of [[Lam-Goet]] in the V Moekims Montassik was one of the most active Achehnese war-leaders, and his Pedireezen (men from Pedir) mercenaries were a constant presence in the region.

### Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

The V Moekims Montassik were the object of two major Dutch operations in [[1896]]. On [[2 May]] [[1896]] [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] led a [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversionary operation]] from [[Siroen]] that reoccupied the old Dutch posts of [[Senelop]] (taken at 9:10 AM by maréchaussée and 3e Bataljon) and [[Lamsoet]] (taken without opposition by 12e Bataljon and burned); the 12e Bataljon proceeded via Belang-West and Bralon, while the 3e Bataljon also positions from Lam-Pré over [[Lam-Tjot]] toward [[Lam-Sinjing]] and Lam-Bada. The principal operation, however, was the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 16–17 June 1896]] under [[Kolonel der Infanterie|Kolonel]] [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]], in which three colonnes moved against Adjé, Aloer Lintang, Lam-Nga, Shan, Tjot Maraja, Tjot Sajoen, and the *benteng* of [[Toekoe Rajoet]] south of Kajoe Koenjit. The *benteng* of [[Lam-Goet]] was occupied without resistance by [[P. Linck|compagnie Linck]] on [[17 June]] [[1896]] and burned; the *benteng* of [[Lam-Preh|Tjot Preh]] was burned by 12e Bataljon; and the *benteng* [[Kroeng-Kali]] (technically in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] but operationally linked) was stormed by compagnie Linck. [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata]] was killed earlier on [[5 June]] [[1896]] in the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]].

### Significance (ATJEH '96 account)

The V Moekims Montassik were the principal south-eastern bastion of Achehnese resistance and the immediate geographic object against which the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of [[Senelop]] and [[Lamsoet]] had been built. Their *tuchtiging* in [[June]] [[1896]] was the second-largest operation of the first semester of [[1896]] after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] against [[Teuku Umar]], and demonstrated the new Dutch policy of moving beyond the old defensive *linie* into active chastisement of hostile mukims. The death of [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata]] removed one of the most dangerous Achehnese war-leaders in this region.

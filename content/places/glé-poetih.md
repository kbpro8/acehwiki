**The Glé Poetih** — glossed by [[P. H. R. Beuming]] as *Blankenberg*, the white mountain — was the height east of [[Lampisang]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]] whose capture by the column of Overste [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] opened the [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)|three-day battle in the Beradin pass]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]].

## The ascent of [[23 May]] [[1896]]

Under the general plan of attack, the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] and the *[[Korps Marechaussee|marechaussée]]* — *ons keurkorps*, the Dutch élite corps — under Van Heutsz were to climb the mountains east of Lampisang and occupy the Glé Poetih. While the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] took position about 900 metres south of [[Ketapan Doea]] and the artillery brought a fortification on the [[Boekit Kasoemba]] under fire, the climb began.

With very great difficulty the 3e Bataljon and the 2nd mountain battery succeeded in scaling the very steep slopes. Beuming singles out the [[Ambonese soldiers|Ambonese fusiliers]] of the 3rd, who *gaven hierbij de stoutste stukjes van durf te zien; ze klommen letterlijk als apen tegen de rotsen omhoog* — showed the boldest feats of daring, climbing literally like apes up the rocks. On arrival, preparations were made at once for a defensible bivouac, but the space on the summit proved too small to hold all the troops, so that a section of artillery with the horses and mules had to be sent away.

## The bombardment from the summit

By the climb the Dutch won *een prachtige positie... die het geheele terrein domineerde en vis-à-vis Lampisang was gelegen* — a splendid position dominating the whole terrain and lying opposite Lampisang. Scarcely having taken position, the artillery began to bombard the surrounding enemy positions in tremendous fashion. *Het waren gewoonweg prachtschoten, die van den Glé Poetih vielen* — they were simply magnificent shots that fell from the Glé Poetih — and the effect in the enemy *[[Benting|bentings]]* was frightful.

The **XXVI Mukims** (Dutch *XXVI Moekims*; "Twenty-six Mukims") was the third of the *sagis* — confederacies of *mukim* districts — of [[Acheh]] proper, completing the [[Three Sagis]] alongside the [[XXII Mukims]] and the [[XXV Mukims]]. Like its sister confederacies, it lay in the territories surrounding the capital [[Banda Acheh]] and participated in the council that effectively ruled the sultanate when the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles–Coombs commission]] arrived in [[1819]].

## Constitutional role

By the late seventeenth century, as Van der Kemp notes, "stond het sultanaat onder voogdij van de hoofden dier om de hoofdplaats gelegen landschappen" — the sultanate lay under the tutelage of the chiefs of the lands surrounding the capital. The Three Sagis collectively gave or withheld recognition to a sultan, and the [[XXVI Mukims]] joined the [[Three Sagis' letter to Penang (4 December 1822)|letter of 4 December 1822]] in which the confederates refused to accept [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] and swore their oath on the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] at [[Kampong Java]].

## Position in 1819

In March [[1819]], when [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles]] and [[Captain Coombs]] anchored at [[Banda Acheh]], no sultan was in residence: [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] had withdrawn to [[Pedir]], and [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum]] (Syf) had fled to [[Telok Semawé]]. The Three Sagis, in name — in practice the [[XXII Mukims]] under [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]] — held the capital. The XXVI Mukims took part in this federation but is not singled out for individual action in Van der Kemp's narrative; for its internal organisation we depend on later scholarship, particularly [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *De Atjehers* (1893), to which Van der Kemp refers his readers.

## After 1819

After Jauhar Alum's death in December [[1823]] the Three Sagis, together with the [[Queen-Mother of Acheh|Queen-Mother]], exercised what authority remained at the capital; [[Robert Fullerton|Fullerton]]'s minute of [[21 March]] [[1825]] noted that "the whole power at Acheen was in the hands of Panglima Polim and at the subordinate ports the respective chiefs had established their independence". The [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|Acheh treaty of 1819]] was by then acknowledged to have been a "dead letter", and the federation of the Three Sagis, including the XXVI Mukims, continued to be the effective authority at the capital.

## Account from *Boon, Van Lansberge* (1943) — XXVI Mukims

The **XXVI Mukims** was a region in [[Aceh]] that was a major center of resistance during the [[Aceh War]]. Unlike the [[XXII Mukims]], which was gradually pacified, the XXVI Mukims remained a source of instability and was the focus of Dutch military operations throughout the period [[1878]]-[[1881]].

## Strategic Significance

The XXVI Mukims region was important for several reasons:
- It was a stronghold of resistance, particularly under the leadership of [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]] and other chiefs.
- It was the refuge of many Acehnese leaders who had fled from other regions.
- It was the source of raids and attacks on Dutch-controlled territory.

## Dutch Military Operations

### [[1878]]-[[1879]] Campaigns

The Dutch launched campaigns into the XXVI Mukims in [[1878]] and [[1879]]:
- General [[K. van der Heijden]] conducted operations, but progress was slow.
- Van Lansberge noted: "The operations are being conducted so weakly that I can do nothing about it."

### [[1880]] Operations

The Dutch planned further operations in [[1880]]:
- Van Lansberge gave orders "to prepare, prepare for and carry out a military expedition against Kemala."
- Van der Heijden opposed the expedition, citing terrain and weather difficulties.
- The expedition was ultimately delayed but not cancelled.

## Resistance

The XXVI Mukims remained a center of resistance:
- Chief [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]] remained active in the region.
- [[Teukoe Paya]] and other leaders continued to resist.
- The region served as a source of support for other resistance movements.

## Legacy

The XXVI Mukims was the last major region of Aceh to be pacified. Its resistance delayed Dutch control and contributed to the financial and military strain on the colony.

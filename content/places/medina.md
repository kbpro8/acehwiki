## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Medina

**Medina** (Arabic **Al-Madinah**) is the second holy city of Islam, in Arabia, the city of the Prophet. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I it is named alongside [[Mecca]] and [[Cairo]] as one of the centres of Mohammedan civilisation where the legal equality of the children of slave-concubines with those of free wives is actually observed in practice — a custom only partially observed in [[Acheh]] and in the inland parts of Arabia. The legal privileges are the same, but the social position of slave-children in Acheh is markedly different from what it is in the great cities of Islam.

Medina is cited for the technical vocabulary of Mohammedan administration. The marriage-makers of Medina are called "licentiates for the making of marriages": after undergoing an examination, they receive a license from the *qadhi*. This terminology is set beside the *mumlik* of [[Mekka]], the *fiqih* of [[Egypt]], the *naib* or district *pangulus* of [[Java]] and their subordinates (the *ketip*), and the *teungku meunasah* of Acheh — all of them officials who discharge the function of marrying people in one of two ways, by direct celebration or by authorisation of an agent. The variety of the names illustrates the diversity of usage in different Mohammedan lands, despite the underlying identity of the office.

The most famous saint of Medina mentioned in the book is **Samman**, who lived in that city a couple of centuries ago and is the eponym of the *ratib Samman* (Arabic *ratib Samman*). This litany, called in Acheh the *rateb Saman*, is especially popular among all native Mohammedans of the old stamp, and its constant use has given rise in various places to sundry variations and additions which, without exception, serve to accentuate the appalling noise that accompanies its recitation. In Acheh the *traweh* prayers of the fasting month are succeeded not by the usual *meudaroh* but by this excessively noisy *rateb*. The litany is also popular in Java and may be heard almost every week in many Batavian kampongs. The introduction of the *ratib Samman* into Acheh is a clear instance of the religious influence of Medina upon the Archipelago, mediated by the pilgrims and students who repaired to the holy city.

In the matter of the *tariqahs*, or mystic associations, Medina is implicitly one of the centres at which Achehnese *hajis* enrolled themselves as members of a *tariqah* during their residence in the holy cities, and through which mystic practices and formulas have been introduced into the country. The *tariqahs* so popular in other parts of [[Sumatra]] do not flourish in Acheh, though some Achehnese *hajis* have joined them abroad. The comparative absence of mystic associations from Acheh is one of the distinctive features of its religious life, and is explained by the fact that the country has its own traditional forms of devotion — above all the *ratib Samman* — which have long supplied the place of the organised mystic orders.

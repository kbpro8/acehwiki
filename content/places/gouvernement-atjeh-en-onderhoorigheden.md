**The Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** was the colonial administrative unit established by the Dutch in [[1874]] to comprise the northern tip of Sumatra and its subject territories — the *gewest* whose protracted subjection through the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] became, in the words of *De Buitenbezittingen*, "het zorgenkind van het Nederlandsch-Indische Gouvernement" (the worry-child of the Netherlands-Indies Government). Bounded on three sides by the sea — the Indian Ocean to the west, the Strait of Malacca to the north and east — and to the south by the *gewesten* of the East Coast of Sumatra and Tapanoeli, it covered roughly one-eighth of the entire island and was administered, by [[1917]], under a Civil and Military Governor resident at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]].

## Extent and boundaries

The *Regeerings-Almanak* of [[1916]] gave the superficie of the gouvernement as 966.6 square geographic miles, while the legend of the Sumatra overview map of [[1913]] put it at 1066.2 — *De Buitenbezittingen* noting that the discrepancy "kan niet worden uitgemaakt bij gebrek aan volkomen betrouwbare gegevens" (cannot be settled for lack of fully reliable data). In either reckoning the territory was "ruim anderhalfmaal zoo groot als die van Nederland" — well over one-and-a-half times the area of the Netherlands. The southern border ran from the East Coast along the Ajer Masin and the Poetjoeh Ajer Tawar, across the G. Meuseugit divide, over the Goenoeng Bandahara (3060 m) in the Wilhelmina range, and by the Serbeilangit massif down to the West Coast at Oedjoeng Si Manoek, described in *Staatsblad* 1908 No. 604.

## Administrative division

By [[1914]] (St. No. 1914, 87) the gouvernement was divided into five *afdeelingen*, each under an Assistant-Resident: [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] (standplaats [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]]), the Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh (standplaats Lhoʼ Seumawè), the Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh (standplaats [[Langsa|Langsa]]), the Afdeeling Westkust van Atjeh (standplaats [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]]), and the Afdeeling Alaslanden. The population figures for [[1912]] — given "slechts onder veel reserve" — totalled 694,000 over 53,100 km², or 13.07 per km², the sparsest of the Buitenbezittingen.

## Governors

The gouverneurs listed in Bijlage I begin with the first Civil and Military Governor of [[1874]] and run through [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] ([[1898]]–[[1904]]), Jhr. J. C. v. d. Wyck ([[1904]]–[[1905]]), [[G. C. E. van Daalen|van Daalen]] ([[1905]]–[[1908]]), and [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] ([[1908]] onward). Van Heutsz's appointment, *De Buitenbezittingen* states, "beteekende echter het begin van eene geheel nieuwe periode in ons optreden," the period of *vliegende colonnes* and consolidated rule.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Colonial Administration

### Population and territory

According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's table (ultimo [[1912]]), the *gewest* had a total population of approximately 453,378 *inlanders* (natives) and 7,594 Europeans, with an additional 9,819 Chinese and 498 Arabs — placing it in the middle rank of Sumatran gewesten by population, behind [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Sumatra's East Coast]] and [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's West Coast]]. The territorial extent was the smallest of any Sumatran residency, reflecting the concentration of the Atjehnese population along the coastal lowlands and the late date at which the interior was brought under Dutch authority.

### Landschapskassen

The [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen (territorial treasuries)]] of the gewest underwent the most dramatic expansion of any Buitenbezittingen gewest in the [[1910]]–[[1913]] period. In [[1910]] the gewest had 3 landschapskassen; in [[1912]] 15 new *onderafdeelingskassen* were created; by ultimo [[1913]] the gewest contained 18 kassen — the largest number in any Buitenbezittingen gewest. Total income from the kassen rose from f 264,910 in [[1910]] to f 748,802 in [[1913]], evidence of the rapid consolidation of the Dutch administrative order after the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]].

### Special fiscal regime

The gewest was the only one in the Buitenbezittingen in which the split of concession revenues between the Gouvernement and the Zelfbesturen was not 1:1 but 6:4 — a special arrangement noted by the Bureau in a footnote: "except in Atjeh, where the 6:4 ratio is still applied for the division of these revenues between the Gouvernement and the Zelfbesturen." In [[1913]] the Atjeh concession revenues yielded f 1,344,000 to the landschapskas — the second-largest such yield among the Buitenbezittingen, after [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Sumatra's East Coast]] (f 3,800,892).

### Peper- en pinanghasil

The gewest was the most important source in the Buitenbezittingen of *peper- en pinanghasil* (pepper and betel-nut revenues): in [[1913]] the pepper yield was f 125,000 and the pinang yield f 55,000 — figures that the Bureau singles out as "very important" among the miscellaneous revenues of the Buitenbezittingen landschapskassen, reflecting the long-established commercial agriculture of the Atjehnese coast.

### Gewapende Politie

The gewest was the only Buitenbezittingen gewest in which the [[Gewapende Politie|armed police]] division was not under the command of a retired or active army officer. The Bureau notes: "Each division — with the exception for the time being of that in Atjeh — is under the command of a retired or active officer of the army." The exception reflects the continuing military character of the pacification, even as the rest of the Buitenbezittingen transitioned to civil police command.

### Wegennet

The [[Wegenplan Sumatra|Sumatra road plan]] of the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]] included several major road projects in the gewest: the Gajo-weg from [[Bireuen]] to [[Takengon]] (76 K.M., cost f 1,496,000 — "completed"), the Alasweg from [[Kaban Djahe]] to [[Koeta Tjane]] (137 K.M., cost f 1,173,000 — "in the main completed"), and the projected connection of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network with the Benkoelen-Padangsche wegennet.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden and the Tramway

**Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden** ("Atjeh's Dependencies") was the colonial designation for the territories east and south of [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] brought under Dutch authority by the long campaign of pacification following the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]]. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]], the Onderhoorigheden appear as the eastern and southern theatre of the network.

The Bureau frames the construction of the second Atjeh tramway — the line of [[1899]]–[[1912]] in the Onderhoorigheden — as the logistical backbone of the post-[[1896]] Dutch extension of authority. According to the Bureau: "in place of [the dismantled [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway of 1885]]] came the long tramway, which since [[1897]] has followed step by step our extension of authority in Atjeh's Dependencies, tying post after post of the ever-expanding military occupation to the command centre at [[Kota Radja]]." From [[1899]] onward, construction of a second tramway in the Onderhoorigheden was begun — the line extended from [[Sigli]] to the harbour of [[Langsa]] between [[1899]] and [[1906]], with the [[Langsa]]–[[Koeala Simpang]] section added between [[1910]] and [[1912]], and a western branch from [[Sigli]] over [[Padang Tiji|Padang Tidji]] to [[Keude Breue]] already built in [[1899]].

The Bureau characterised this second tramway, in its early state, as "a piece of tramway in the Dependencies, somewhat hanging in the air" (*wel wat in de lucht hangende, stuk trambaan in de Onderhoorigheden*) — without direct connection to the Groot-Atjeh network until the completion of the [[Berglijntje Keude Breue–Seulimeum|berglijntje from Keude Breue over the hills to Seulimeum]] in the years [[1904]]–[[1908]]. That reconnection gave the military administration "a powerful instrument for achieving the complete [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] of the intersected regions."

The [[1913]] extension approvals (G. Bn. of 17 June [[1913]] No. 59, 16 October [[1913]] No. 25, and 8 November [[1913]] No. 40) authorised the *doortrekking* of the Atjeh-tram from [[Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban]] on the [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]]. With the completion of the connecting line between [[Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe]], the Atjeh-tram network would "finally reach its natural terminus: the fine and accessible [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]]."

## Significance

[[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the Buitenbezittingen gewest in which the post-[[1896]] Dutch project of "effective authority" was most fully and most recently realised. The combination of the rapid expansion of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]], the special 6:4 fiscal regime, the exceptional military command of the police, the unique peper- en pinanghasil revenues, and the systematic extension of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network all mark the gewest as the paradigmatic case of the post-pacification colonial order.

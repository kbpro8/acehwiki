**Pulo We** — known in modern maps as **Pulau Weh**, and comprising the town of [[Sabang|Sabang]] — is an island lying off the northern coast of [[Acheh]], which supplies the Achehnese with the oval or globular tombstones used in their burial-places. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Snouck Hurgronje cites Pulo We in connection with the Achehnese trade in tombstones. Long used by the [[Sultanate of Acheh]] as a place of banishment and valued for its [[sulphur]], the island passed to the [[Netherlands Indies]] Government in [[1884]] and within little more than a decade became the site of one of the busiest coaling stations in the
[[East Indies]].

## Under the Sultanate of Acheh

In the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries Poeloe We served the [[Sultanate of Acheh]] as a penal
colony for convicts. In the nineteenth century it carried only a very small permanent population,
settled mainly on the southern side of the island. Its chief importance to [[Acheh]] lay in its
*sulphur*, which the then warlike sultanate urgently needed for the manufacture of gunpowder.

## Dutch occupation (1884)

The [[Netherlands Indies]] Government took the island into possession in [[1884]]. In that year a
small coal jetty was built on the southern bay of [[Balohan|Lhok Balohan]] and a head official was
appointed for the island. The Balohan bay, however, presented several obstacles to shipping, and a
commission sent out in [[1887]] judged the bay at [[Sabang]], on the northern side, the more suitable
site for a harbour.

## From penal island to ocean harbour

The commission's verdict shifted attention to the northern bay, where, from the concession granted to
the firm [[De Lange (firm)|De Lange]] in [[1887]] and the opening of the free port in [[1897]], the
station of [[Sabang]] grew up. The island thereby passed within a generation from a remote place of
banishment and sulphur-gathering to the seat of a deep-water port on the approaches to the
[[Strait of Malacca]].

## The Trade in Tombstones

The author explains that "the tombstones are imported from neighbouring islands, such as Pulo We, and sold for one or two dollars a set. The stones brought from these islands are oval or globular in shape, and pairs are made of those which match best." Pulo We thus figures in the agrarian and funerary economy of Acheh as the chief source of the cheaper kind of tombstone — those sold ready-shaped, in matching pairs, at the modest price of one or two dollars.

## Pulo We and Pulo Batee

The tombstones of Pulo We are contrasted, in the same passage, with the chiselled tombstones of the wealthier Achehnese, which were fashioned from a porous and easily workable stone imported from [[Pulo Batee]] ("Stone Island") opposite [[Ulee Lheue]]. The cheaper stones of Pulo We, oval or globular in shape, were used by the common folk; the chiselled stones of Pulo Batee, cut by the craftsmen of [[Meura'sa]], were reserved for those who could afford them. The two islands thus represent, in the funerary economy of Acheh, the two extremes of expense and of workmanship; and the tombstones of the Achehnese cemeteries — shaped and shaped differently for men and for women (*nisan agam* and *inong*) — reflect the social gradations of the country.

## The Northern Island

Pulo We lies off the northernmost point of [[Sumatra]], commanding the entrance to the Straits of Malacca, and its position has made it, in modern times, the site of the important harbour of [[Sabang|Sabang]]. In the period described by Snouck Hurgronje, however, its chief commercial relation to the mainland of Acheh lay in the export of tombstones — a trade which, however modest in value, linked the island to the funerary customs of the mainland, and made it a regular recourse of the Achehnese villager in search of memorial slabs for his dead.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Poeloe Wé

**Poeloe Wé** (modern Pulau Weh) is the volcanic island at the northern extremity of Sumatra, separated from the Atjehsche mainland by the narrow Suratte-passage and forming, with the wild islet of P. Rondo and several small rocky groups, the Wé-groep — the northern continuation of the Eastern *randketen* of the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] valley. *De Buitenbezittingen* describes it as the locus of the [[Sabang|Sabang]] harbour and the principal Dutch coaling-station in the region: "Zijn grootste beteekenis ontleent het eiland aan de aan Sabangbaai aangelegde groote zeehaven met steenkolenstation, door de Sabang Maatschappij geëxploiteerd."

## Geography

The island is the ruined cone of an ancient volcano whose collapsed craters now form deep, well-sheltered bays — the Zuidbaai (Lhoʼ Balohan) and, above all, the Sabangbaai on which [[Sabang|Sabang]] itself was built. The greater part of Poeloe Wé is "met bosch bedekt" — covered in forest — and, apart from the harbour and its dependent settlement, it carries only a sparse population and scant cultivation. The Breuë-groep, a chain of small limestone islets continuing the Western *randketen* to the north-west, lies between the Wé-groep and the mainland; their P. Breuë and P. Nasoë "den doorgang van de Westkust naar de reede van Oeleë Lheuë gevaarlijk versperren" — dangerously obstruct the passage from the Westkust to the roadstead of [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]].

## History: penal colony and sulphur island

Before the Dutch occupation Poeloe Wé had served, in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, as a *strafkolonie* — penal colony — of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate of Atjeh]]. In the nineteenth century it held only a small permanent population, mainly on the southern side. It retained importance for the Sultanate as the source of the sulphur used in the buskruit-fabricage (gunpowder manufacture) of "dat oorlogzuchtige rijk" — that warlike kingdom. Dutch possession was effectively taken in [[1884]], when a small coal-shed was built at the Zuidbaai and a *hoofd* over the island was appointed; the [[1887]] commission designated the Sabangbaai in the Noordbaai as the more serviceable anchorage, and the Bataviaasche firma De Lange received the concession for what would become the [[Sabang|Sabang]] station.

## Administration and exploitation

Poeloe Wé, with P. Rondo, constituted the onderafdeeling Sabang of the [[Afdeeling Groot-Atjeh|Afdeeling Groot-Atjeh]] (St. No. 1914, 87) — counted among the five onderafdeelingen of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] though geographically a separate island. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that "langsheen de lagere hellingen vestigen zich ieder jaar tijdelijk peperplanters, vooral van overwal, uit Kroeëng Raja en Pedir" — every year temporary pepper-planters from across the strait, from Kroeëng Raja and Pedir, settle along the lower slopes. The pepper harvest of [[1914]] was approximately 800 pikol worth ƒ24,000, while the klappercultuur, well-suited to the coastal strip, was steadily expanding and yielded some 1,000 pikol of copra in [[1914]]. On the slopes above the harbour stood the most powerful radio-telegraphic station in East Asia, established in [[1911]].

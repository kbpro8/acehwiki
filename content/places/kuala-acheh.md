The mouth of the [[Acheh]] river (Krueng [[Acheh]]), where the great saint [[Abdurra'uf of Singkel]] (known as Teungku di Kuala) is buried. His tomb is described as the most sacred in the whole country after that of [[Teungku Anjong]]. The saint took his name from the fact that he is buried near the Kuala or mouth of the [[Acheh]] river. In the [[Hikayat Malem Dagang]], the magic tree destined by [[Si Ujut]] for a warship comes drifting from the opposite coast to Kuala [[Acheh]] and remains there quietly. In the war with the Dutch, a severe conflict took place near the tomb of Teungku di Kuala. A crocodile of [[Teungku Anjong]], marked by his master with a band of aren-rope (taloe jo') around its neck, still keeps watch in Kuala [[Acheh]], taking care that no noxious crocodiles enter the river. The [[Acheh]] River (Krueng [[Acheh]]) The principal river of [[Acheh]] Proper, running through the capital. The tomb of Teungku di Kuala ([[Abdurra'uf of Singkel]]) is situated near its mouth (Kuala [[Acheh]]). Near the tomb, the waters are said to be haunted by a gigantic skate (parbe) which wrecks the vessels of the wicked, especially those who have failed to honour the great saint of [[Acheh]]. The river also marks an important geographic boundary between different territorial units.

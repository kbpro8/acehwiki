**Keumala** is a place in [[Pidie]] with a double significance in the history of [[Atjeh|Aceh]]: it was the seat of the fugitive Sultan's "court" of the pretender to the sultanate of [[Acheh]] during the [[Aceh War]], and — as the valley of Keumala — the classic battle-ground where the [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant-transport]] was repeatedly attacked by resistance fighters.

## The Court of Keumala

After the fall of [[Banda Acheh|Kutaraja]] and the death of [[Sultan Mahmut Shah]], **Keumala** — also **Kuta Keumala** ("the fort of Keumala," in the formal style of the sultans' edicts) — became the seat of the pretender-sultan [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot]] and of his "court" in the territory of [[Pidie]]. Sayyid families from [[Mecca]] settled in Pidie and made occasional journeys to Keumala, where the young sultan treated them with the highest honour.

In *The Achehnese*, [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] describes Keumala as the place to which the court of the pretender removed itself when driven from the capital, continuing "very much the same as it had been before the war, the scene alone being changed." Its location far from the theatre of conflict, in the safe interior of Pidie, made it a symbolic refuge for Acehnese sovereignty. The court's effective manager was [[Tuanku Asem]] (Hashim), the young sultan's guardian, who was most bitterly hostile to the Dutch Government but reluctant to take a serious share in the contest.

The court's chief remaining function was the issue of **sarakatas** — sealed edicts of appointment bearing the *chab sikureu'eng* or nine-fold seal — to those uleebalangs who made ceremonial **pilgrimages to Keumala** to obtain the Sultan's recognition. Such pilgrimages had a double value: they gave the chief a visible warrant of his dignity, and brought some profit to the impecunious court. Even [[Teungku Tiro (Muhamat Amin)|Teungku Tiro]] at first sought to dissuade the chiefs from these journeys, until [[Teuku Uma]] pointed out that the resulting revenues would make the court less demanding on himself.

The reverence that still clung to the person of the sultan was illustrated by the famous journey of [[Teuku Ne' of Meura'sa]]: those who accompanied him were implored by friends and relations to bring back some water wherein the pretender had washed his feet, the young ne'er-do-well being still regarded by a part of his subjects as *keramat*. Yet this feeling of awe was coupled with the conviction that the sultan exercised no perceptible influence on affairs. The history of Keumala during the war was one of steady insignificance. About [[1896]] the pretender was compelled, owing to a quarrel with local chiefs, to break up his "court" at Keumala and lead a wandering life, finding his asylum as a rule in the north-coast districts.

## The Classic Battle-Ground

The **valley of Keumala** is also the classic battle-ground of the Aceh War. During the last [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant-transport]] from [[Geumpang]] to [[Tangsé]], an armed Acehnese attacked the marechaussée brigade that escorted the elephants in the same valley where the elephant-transport had been attacked by a small band of resistance fighters three years before.

The [[Keumala incident]] — the attack on the last elephant-transport at Keumala — is the set-piece of the resistance fighter's paradise-death in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]*: the armed Acehnese who was immediately struck down and who, heavily wounded and crawling, still managed to deliver a parang-blow to the patrol commander, sergeant [Vogelensang](/people/vogelensang), in his leg. The Keumala attacker — who had paid his taxes, who was exempt from corvée road-services, who did not belong to the resistance party — was the resistance fighter of the paradise-death: the man who sought death without the ordinary motives of the resistance party.

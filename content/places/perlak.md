**Perlak** — modern Peureulak, on the east coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in northern Sumatra — is named in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) as the earliest Muslim polity of the Indonesian archipelago, preceding the better-known neighbouring sultanate of [[Samudra]] on the [[Pasei|Pase-rivier]]. In the chapter *De oudste cultuurstroomen*, Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]] records that the [[Marco Polo|Venetian traveller Marco Polo]], passing through the Strait of Malacca in [[1292]], found Perlak already Muslim, while the rest of the Sumatran coast he described was still heathen. Van Naerssen's specific phrasing is: "the city of Perlak, where in 1292 one had not yet arrived so far" — i.e. so far in the rest of the archipelago, where Polo's Muslim informants still characterised the surrounding population as "unclean" because they ate all kinds of flesh.

## Earliest Muslim polity of the archipelago

Van Naerssen's account places Perlak at the head of the Islamisation of the Indonesian archipelago. Polo's criterion for distinguishing Muslim from non-Muslim populations in [[1292]] — the avoidance of unclean flesh — is, Van Naerssen notes, "exactly the same as we now still see happen when Islam penetrates into new territory." The same criterion, applied to the rest of the Sumatran coast in 1292, found only Perlak to be Muslim. The neighbouring polity of [[Samudra]] on the Pase-rivier, by contrast, "was still heathen in 1292. But the Muslim founder of the little realm lies buried there in 1297" — his tombstone having been imported ready-made from [[Cambay]] in [[Goedjarat]]. The [[1415]] Chinese reports cited by Van Naerssen confirm that, by the early fifteenth century, "Islam has already spread over the whole north coast of Sumatra and is also established on the east coast as far as the little realm of [[Aru]] with its own Sultan."

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Perlak's significance is foundational: the polity sits at the head of the long Islamic history of the Atjeh region, predating the better-known [[Samudra]] sultanate by at least a decade (1292 vs. 1297) and the [[Aceh War|Dutch-Aceh war]] of the nineteenth century by some six centuries. The Polo reference of [[1292]] is the earliest external documentary evidence for an established Muslim community in the Indonesian archipelago. Perlak's later absorption into the wider Atjeh sultanate — together with [[Samudra]] and [[Aru]] — placed the Atjeh sultanate, at its height, in direct succession to the earliest Muslim polities of the archipelago.

**Pungej Blang Tjoet** (also written Poengej Blang Tjoet) was a kampong to the south of [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] whose post was occupied by Dutch troops under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Kolonel PEL]] on [[23 January]] [[23 January]] [[1875]] and fortified by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede-Luitenant C. J. Snijders]] of the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Compagnie Mineurs en Sappeurs]] in the days following, as the first of the southward clearing operations of [[January 1875|January]] [[1875]] that continued with the [[Capture of Mandarsah Poetih (28 January 1875)|capture of Mandarsah Poetih]] five days later and culminated in the [[Lamara Olejlo fortification (15 February 1875)|Lamara Olejlo fortification of 15 February]].

## The Capture of 23 January 1875

After the [[Missigit Longbatta operation (31 December 1874 – 13 January 1875)|Missigit Longbatta operation]] was largely complete by [[13 January]] [[13 January]] [[1875]], [[J. L. J. H. Pel|PEL]] "wende hij zich meer in zuidelijke richting, waar hij op 23 Januari den post Poengej Blang Tjoet deed bezetten." The post was taken without Snijders' participation in the action itself: he "had aan de actie zelve niet had deelgenomen," but was tasked with the subsequent engineer work.

## The Fortification

"In de daarop volgende dagen door den Luitenant SNIJDERS [...] met een detachement mineurs en een aantal koelies in staat van verdediging gebracht en voor logies ingericht." The work thus consisted of two parts: putting the captured post in a state of defence, and fitting it up with logies (quarters) for the garrison. The same pattern was repeated at [[Mandarsah Poetih|Mandarsah Poetih]] five days later.

## Significance

The capture and fortification of Pungej Blang Tjoet marks the beginning of the systematic extension of the Dutch perimeter south of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh River]] in the late winter of [[1875]] — the movement that would culminate in the [[Lamara Olejlo fortification (15 February 1875)|Lamara Olejlo fortification]] and the end of Snijders' field service.

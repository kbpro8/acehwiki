**Loöng** (also written Lhong) was a landscape on the west coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] where many of the Dutch army's opponents were staying in [[April 1877|April 1877]], and which had openly committed piracy against a trading vessel under British flag. It was the object of an expedition in [[April 1877|April 1877]] sent by the Dutch after the [[Van Lansberge's visit to Aceh (March-April 1877)|inspection of Van Lansberge]], in which the warships bombarded the principal kampongs and the infantry completed the work of destruction. The submission of Loöng and a few neighbouring landscapes followed.

## The Expedition of April 1877

In [[April 1877|April 1877]] an expedition was sent to the landscape Loöng on the west coast, where many of the Dutch army's opponents were staying, while it had openly committed piracy against a trading vessel under British flag. The warships were to bombard the principal kampongs and damage them as much as possible, while under the protection of the navy, which of course played an important part in all these operations against the coast-states, the infantry would land to complete the work of destruction. After the surf had first prevented the disembarkation, on [[17 April]] [[17 April]] the landing took place. "When the sloops were about to leave the warships, the commandant of the squadron called to the commandant of the vanguard that, as soon as he had approached the shore, he would throw a grenade over the heads of the troops against the benting to be stormed, in order to give the storming more chance of success." The grenade had the desired effect: the Atjehers, in confusion, abandoned the parapet; the troops were soon on the shore and in the enemy fortification.

## The Submission

The punishment had the submission of Loöng and a few neighbouring landscapes as a consequence. The operation was one of the first undertaken after the [[Van Lansberge's visit to Aceh (March-April 1877)|inspection of Van Lansberge in March-April 1877]], in accordance with the new policy of punishing the still rebellious coast-states.

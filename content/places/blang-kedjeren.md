**Blang Kédjérèn** is the subdistrict headquarters-town and former subdistrict in the [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]] highlands of Central Atjèh. It was both a remote military post of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] — one of the most *afgelegen* (remote) posts of the colonial army, "enige dagen lopens het binnenland in" (some days' walk into the interior) — and the seat of the last Dutch defensive position in northern Sumatra, which capitulated to the Japanese on [[28 March]] [[1942]].

## The Marechaussee post and the 1e Divisie

**Blang Kedjeren** was the seat of the [[1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren]] — a major formation of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] in the highlands of central Aceh. Its remoteness defined its character. "De Blang had zijn snelste verbinding over [[Medan]]; dan hoefde men maar drie dagen te lopen en kon verder per auto: een paar honderd kilometer door de [[Alasvlakte]], de [[Karolanden]], over [[Koeta-Tjané]], [[Kaban-Djahé]] en [[Brastagi]] naar [[Medan]], vanwaar men met de trein in twee dagen in [[Koeta-Radja]] was." The route — three days' walk plus two days by car plus two days by train — is G. W. T. Dames's marker of how *afgelegen* the Blang was.

"Ook die tot enige dagen lopens het binnenland inlagen, zoals Blang Kedjerèn en [[Lokop]]." Blang Kedjeren was one of the deeply inland posts that [[Dominee Thenu]] continued to visit several times a year, even after his diabetes made expedition work too hard. The *Soldaten-Dominee*'s visits to the Blang were the mark of his *gezag*: the dominee who would not be deterred by illness from visiting the most *afgelegen* posts of the corps.

## The Hotel de Boer anecdote (c. 1933)

"In het 1933 of daaromtrent jaar zat de kapitein, commandant van de 1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren, op de Soos in [[Koeta-Radja]] een groot verhaal af te steken over een belevenis in [[Hotel de Boer]] gedurende zijn doorreis." The captain of the 1e Divisie, travelling through [[Medan]] on his way to [[Koeta-Radja]], stopped at [[Hotel de Boer]] in *veldtenue*, ordered a large bottle of beer in the lobby, and discovered — only when another hotel guest drew his attention to them — two of his own *[[dardanellen|dardanellen]]* standing behind his chair with drawn *[[klewang]]* and *[[karabijn-(m.-95)|karabijnen]]* in de aanslag. The *dardanellen* "hadden uit de Blang zijn beide meegenomen" — had come with him from the Blang — and "waren jaren terug op de Blang gekomen en steeds daar gebleven. Eerst nu kwamen ze eens in de bewoonde wereld terug." The story, told to *Atjehmensen* at the Soos, was "niet voor onmogelijk gehouden."

The episode is *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]'s* clearest illustration of the *plichtsbesef* of the Marechaussee man: the *dardanellen* who had not been to the *bewoonde wereld* (the inhabited world) in years, and who, when they finally came, stood guard in a hotel lobby as naturally as they stood guard in the rimboe.

## The road connection of 1939

The road connection from [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]] in the Alas valley to Blang Kédjérèn in the Gajó Loeós — "located over 100 km. further north" — "had only been completed at the end of [[1939]] on the initiative of the then Assistant Resident of the East Coast of Atjèh, Mr. Th. O. B. Gunther. In harmonious cooperation of the population and military personnel, with financial support from the Government's resin and turpentine company, the old horse track for auto transport — albeit, that for the time being only one-way traffic was possible — was made suitable." Blang Kédjérèn was thus brought within a one-day motor drive of Koeta Tjané, replacing the three-day foot journey. On the eve of the [[1942|1942]] invasion the subdistricts of [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]] (with headquarters Blang Kédjérèn) and [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] (with headquarters [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]]) were merged into the new subdistrict Gajó Loeós and Alaslanden, and Mr. Gunther was appointed district chief.

## The military role in the 1942 defence

"In contrast to the low-lying Alas valley, the Gajó Loeós valley lies at 800 to 1000 m. altitude with a surrounding mountain range of 2000 to 3000 m." The Gajó Loeós was, after [[Takéngón|Takéngón]] and the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]], the third great defence centre of the Atjèh highlands. "With a view to a prolonged defense considerable supplies of military equipment, foodstuffs, medicines, etc. had been gathered here." Bivouacs for 2,000 men had been built by conscripts through paid labor and were ready for use on [[26 March]] [[1942]].

## The capitulation of 28 March 1942

When the Japanese broke through from the valley of the Lawé Rénoen on [[25 March]] [[1942]], "a further retreat to the last stronghold, the [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]]" became necessary, and "the headquarters was moved to Blang Kédjèrèn." Colonel [[Gosenson|Gosenson]], after the fall of [[Takéngón|Takéngón]], "fell back on Blang Kédjèrèn with two very weary brigades, closely followed by the Japanese vanguard." When General Overakker "decided on [[27 March]] to surrender, there was no longer any enemy to be acknowledged in the Blang, so that the surrender could only be offered on [[28 March]]. On that same day the Japanese troops held their entry into Blang Kédjérèn." The few European women and children were transferred to [[Kaban Djahé|Kaban Djahé]] at the end of March.

## Significance

Blang Kédjérèn stands at the intersection of two chapters in Acehnese colonial history. As a Marechaussee post in the late colonial era, it exemplified the *afgelegen* culture of the colonial army — the *plichtsbesef*, the *dardanellen*, the visits of the *Soldaten-Dominee* — that G. W. T. Dames memorialised in *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.* As the final defensive position of the Dutch in northern Sumatra in [[1942]], it was the place where that colonial order reached its military end.

Referenced in the sadati dialogue: 'My desire, brothers, is to go to Pulo Pinang (Penang).' The Hok Canton was a ship 'belonging to Chinese traders in Penang, trading to [[Acheh]] under Dutch colours.' After the ransom affair following the [[Hok Canton Incident]], Achehnese who had been involved found their way to [[Singapore]] and Penang. Penang thus served as both a trading partner and a place of refuge for Achehnese connected to the events of the holy war against the Dutch.

**Penang** (also *Pinang*, formerly *Prince of Wales Island*) was a British Straits Settlement on the northwest coast of the [[Malay Peninsula]], where — as [[R.J. Wilkinson]] noted in his translator's note to *The Achehnese* — the English Government had adopted the word *[[Mukim|mukim]]* to designate the minor subdivisions of a district for administrative purposes. In Penang and Province Wellesley, the word had the same modified meaning as in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]], and in its stricter sense corresponded almost exactly to the English word "parish."

This linguistic adoption is significant as evidence of the close administrative and cultural ties between the British Straits Settlements and the Acehnese-Malay world.

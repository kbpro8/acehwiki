**Baiturrahman Grand Mosque** was the monumental royal mosque of the [[Aceh Sultanate]] located immediately west of [[Kraton of Aceh]] in modern Banda Aceh, Aceh Province, Indonesia, which served as a major fortified citadel during the Aceh War.

## Description and Architecture

In [[1873]], the mosque (*Mesigiet*) stood within a substantial walled stone and timber sanctuary enclosure surrounded by ditches and kampungs. Its defensive strength made it a formidable redoubt guarding the western approaches to the sultan's palace.

## Battle of the Mosque (April 1873)

During the [[First Aceh Expedition]], invading troops of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] mistook the mosque enclosure for an outer redoubt of [[Kraton of Aceh]]. In the [[Battle of Baiturrahman Mosque]], Dutch battalions stormed the sanctuary on [[10 April]] [[1873]] but were driven back by Acehnese counterattacks. 

On morning of [[14 April]] [[1873]], Dutch forces recaptured the enclosure; while inspecting the courtyard, Commander-in-Chief General [[J. H. R. Köhler]] was shot and killed by an Acehnese sniper. His successor, General [[E. C. van Daalen]], ordered the evacuation of the mosque and retreat of the army after [[16 April]] [[1873]].

## Occupation in the Second Expedition

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], General [[J. van Swieten]]'s columns captured the neighboring redoubt of [[Lemboe]] during the [[Capture of Lemboe]] and reoccupied the Mesigiet in January [[1874]], incorporating it into the defensive perimeter of Kotta Radja.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **Missigit at Koeta Radja** was the great mosque of the capital of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], built on the initiative of Governor [[General van der Heijden|Karel van der Heijden]], whose first stone he laid in October [[1879]], and which was opened with great ceremony on [[27 December]] [[1882]].

### Construction and opening

"Met groote plechtigheid werd op 27 Dec. 1882 de nieuwe Missigit, waarvan de gouverneur van Atjeh in Oct. 1879 de eerste steen had gelegd, geopend" — "with great ceremony the new Missigit was opened on 27 December 1882, of which the Governor of Atjeh had laid the first stone in October 1879" — records author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]], adding that at the beginning of that year several clergy were attached to the Missigit at the cost of the Government. The measure answered to the Government's striving to convince the Atjehnese that it respected the Mohammedan religion, wished to maintain the Atjehnese *adat*, and would further land and people economically — a policy which in those years of the [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|civil experiment]] none the less failed to retain the population's confidence. A photograph in the book shows the building with the caption: "Meuseugit te Koeta-Radja, gebouwd op initiatie van Gouverneur Karel van der Heyden" ("Missigit at Koeta Radja, built on the initiative of Governor Karel van der Heyden").

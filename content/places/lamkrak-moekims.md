**Lamkrak Moekims** — written in Dutch as also *4 Lamkraksche Moekims* for the core four, or the *7-mukim complex of Lam-Krak* for the broader cluster — was a region of [[Groot-Atjeh]] comprising the four core [[moekim|mukims]] of Lam-Krak, Lam-Lheuë, Ateuh (including Ateuh Tjoet, Ateuh Raja, Groot Ateuh), and Kroeng Mak, together with the three adjacent mukims of Ana Batee (also Anak Baté), Sibreh, and Ana Glé (also Ana' Galé). As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the Lamkrak Moekims were situated "streek tusschen Biloel en Ana Galoeng en benoorden het gebergte gelegen" (the region between Biloel and Ana Galoeng, north of the mountains), and were the persistent haunt of hostile *benden* that had defied Dutch authority since [[1878]]. The Lamkrak Moekims were the principal object of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging (chastisement) of 9–10 June 1896]], the second-largest operation of the first semester of [[1896]].

## Description and Location

The Lamkrak Moekims lay in the southern part of [[Groot-Atjeh]] between the [[Dutch]] post of [[Biloel]] on the south-west and the post of [[Ana' Galong]] on the north-east, and north of the mountain wall. The four "eigentliche" Lamkrak Moekims were Lam-Krak (with the kampong [[Lam-Djamé|Lam-Djamé]] on its northern edge and the [[Masdjid Lam-Krak|fortified mosque of Lam-Krak]]), Lam-Lheuë (with the fortification of Kling / Mon Selassa / Mon Seulassa on the border with Ana Batee), Ateuh (with the sub-kampongs of Ateuh Tjoet, [[Ateuh Raja]], and Groot Ateuh), and Kroeng Mak (with the [[Masdjid Kroeng Mak|fortified mosque of Kroeng Mak]] and the Koeta Lam-Poe-Poe near the Kroeng Loeng Aloeë river). The three adjacent mukims were Ana Batee (also Anak Baté, with the kampong [[Lam-Bada Anak Baté|Lam-Bada]]), Sibreh (with the fortified *Masdjid Dja Oedjoen* near the pasar), and Ana Glé (also Ana' Galé, distinct from [[Ana' Galong]]). The [[Tjot Gloempang]] *versterking* stood in the middle of the large sawah of Lam-Krak, recognisable by a tree of the same name.

## Historical Background

The Lamkrak Moekims had been the principal southern refuge of Achehnese resistance since [[1878]], when [[Generaal van der Heiden|Generaal van der Heiden]] conducted operations there and built a road from Sibreh to [[Biloel]]. The last Dutch activity before [[1896]] had been in [[1878]]. The four Lamkrak mukims had been the explicit object of the [[1893]] policy of [[Teuku Umar]] as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government, and Umar had been lent war materieel "voor een krijgstocht in het Lamkraksche" (for a campaign in the Lamkrak country). The proposed [[30 March]] [[1896]] operation of [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Luitenant-Kolonel Van Vliet]] — cancelled by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] — had targeted the four Lamkrak Moekims directly. The mukims were the principal recruiting ground for the Pedireezen mercenaries of [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|T. Hoesin Long Bata]] and other war-leaders.

## Role in the 1896 Operations

The Lamkrak Moekims were the principal object of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 9–10 June 1896]]. On [[9 June]] [[1896]] two Dutch colonnes moved against the Lamkrak: the 1e colonne under [[J. F. Th. Veeren|luit.-kol. Veeren]] advancing from [[Biloel]] against [[Ateuh Raja]] (where the 9e Bataljon attacked in one continuous *aanval*, with kapitein [[H. T. de Moulin|H. T. de Moulin]]'s 1e compagnie hitting the borstwering behind the Kroeng Ateuh, and Eur. fus. v. Dongen first on the borstwering but receiving a fatal chest shot from the last Atjeher), against [[Baha]] (where luit. [[S. v. Hulstijn|S. v. Hulstijn]]'s 3e compagnie of 3e Bataljon advanced with maréchaussée and luit. [[M. Campioni|M. Campioni]]'s right peloton stormed the *benting* Baha, after which the Koeta Baha garrison of 5 Atjehers fled into the hands of the maréchaussée), and against [[Lam-Djamé|Lam-Krak / Lam-Djamé]] (where two redoutes near the N. *rand* were attacked by von Schauroth's compagnie, both *bentings* taken by Blankenstein and Kuipers, and the [[Masdjid Lam-Krak|fortified mosque of Lam-Krak]] was a listed target). The 2e colonne advanced from [[Lam-Djamoe]] against the [[Tjot Gloempang]] *versterking* in the middle of the large sawah of Lam-Krak, against Lam-Manjang (where a *koeta* with 3-metre-thick and 1.5-metre-high walls was blown up with *springmiddelen*), and against [[Lam-Isi|Lam-Isi]]. On [[10 June]] [[1896]] operations continued with the destruction of remaining *versterkingen*, the building of a bridge over the Loeng Gadjah by *genietroepen*, and the eventual withdrawal of the colonnes to [[Biloel]] and [[Lam-Djamoe]].

## Significance

The Lamkrak Moekims were the most persistent thorn in the side of the [[Dutch]] *buitenlinie* policy, and their *tuchtiging* in [[June]] [[1896]] was the symbolic climax of the post-[[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]] counter-offensive. The operation demonstrated that the Dutch could now strike into the deepest Achehnese refuges, and the destruction of the [[Masdjid Lam-Krak|fortified mosque of Lam-Krak]] and the *bentings* of Ateuh, Baha, and Lam-Djamé broke the back of organised Achehnese defence in the southern mukims. The Lamkrak Moekims remained, however, a recruiting ground for Achehnese resistance throughout the remainder of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

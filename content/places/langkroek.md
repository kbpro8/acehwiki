**Langkroek** was an Atjehnees kampong leaning against the middle of the [[Pedir-dijk]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]], whose *sawah* (irrigated rice fields) were the scene of the famous [[Padi-snijden on the Langkroek sawah (1874)|padi-snijden concession]] of February–March [[1874]] and whose fortifications were carried by surprise on [[28 September]] [[1874]] in the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] by [[Kolonel Wiegand|overste Wiegand]]. The plan was [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s, and the victory, "of great importance," cost the Dutch almost nothing.

## The padi-snijden concession

In February–March [[1874]], after the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|Ketapang Doewa expedition]] and [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s declaration of the *afwachtende houding*, the ripening *padi* (rice crop) on the Langkroek sawah became a point of contact between Dutch and Atjehneezen. Van Swieten "dreef de gemoedelijkheid zelfs zoo ver" — drove the good-naturedness so far — as to grant the bevolking of Langkroek permission to cut their ripe padi, provided they "de geweren aflegden" (laid down their rifles). Schildwachten were ordered not to shoot. Many spies took advantage. "Zoolang het padi-snijden duurde, werd werkelijk geen schot op het bivouak gelost" — so long as the padi-cutting lasted, no shot was fired at the bivouak — but as soon as the last bundle was harvested, the Atjehneezen "weder van alle zijden kwamen opdagen, ditmaal behoorlijk met hunne zware geweren gewapend" — reappeared from all sides, this time properly armed with their heavy rifles — and opened a "levendig vuur" on the bivouak.

## The conquest

After several weeks of forced inactivity the Dutch struck again on [[28 September]] [[1874]]. The kampong of Langkroek, leaning against the middle of the Pedir-dijk, was taken by surprise. The plan was Pel's; the execution Wiegand's. The north side of Langkroek was heavily fortified — a continuous line of works armed with light guns — but on the Dutch arrival the works were un- or weakly-held and were captured before the enemy, streaming in from [[Pinang]] and the [[XXVI Moekims]], could resist. The victory was "of great importance, cost almost nothing." Three new posts were immediately erected around Langkroek. Borel criticises Wiegand for not pressing on to take [[Lampriet]], which would have fallen almost without a blow. The post of *Langkroek-oost* was later replaced by the new post at Lampriet after the conquest of Lampriet of [[4 February]] [[1875]] by [[Majoor Romswinckel|majoor Romswinckel]].

**Glen Bervi** was a tobacco plantation on Sumatra's East Coast that was attacked by [[Acehnese raids on East Sumatra|Acehnese raiding parties]] in [[1876]], one of three plantations — alongside [[Tamaram]] and [[Ludwigsburg]] — struck during the escalating Acehnese incursions into the northern reaches of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]] in the mid-[[1870s]].

## The Acehnese raids of 1876

Glen Bervi was situated in the northern plantation belt, in proximity to the frontier with the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Acehnese]] territory, which from [[1873]] was the scene of the ongoing [[Atjeh War|Dutch war against Aceh]]. As the war intensified Acehnese raiding into the East Coast increased. Dr. T. Volker records that Glen Bervi, [[Tamaram]], and [[Ludwigsburg]] were all attacked in [[1876]]. At [[Tamaram]], the manager was wounded and an assistant killed; the *Kedjoeroean* (district head) of [[Bohorok]] openly collaborated with the invaders and fled with his followers to the [[Alas]] lands.

The raids on Glen Bervi and the surrounding plantations were among the events that prompted the [[Sultan of Langkat]] — whose territory lay directly in the path of the Acehnese advance — to resist the raiders actively. He was rewarded in [[1887]] with elevation from *Radja* to *Sultan* in recognition of his services.

## Longer-term consequences

The threat Acehnese raids posed to the plantation economy of Deli and Langkat became one of the driving justifications for the military [[Expedition against Tamiang (1893)]], which successfully dispersed the Acehnese fighters threatening the northern plantation and petroleum belt. Final pacification of the frontier zone was achieved in [[1899]].

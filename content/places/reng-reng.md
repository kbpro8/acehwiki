**Reng-Reng** was a kampong in the VII Moekims of the landschap [[Pedir]], lying amid wildly overgrown mountains at a distance reckoned at three day-marches from [[Selimoen]], which served on [[14 August]]–[[15 August]] [[1879]] as the place of withdrawal of the last hostile chiefs of [[Groot-Atjeh]] before the converging Dutch columns of the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]], as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## The withdrawal of the chiefs, 14–15 August 1879

According to the information given by [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed|Toekoe Moeda Daud]] after his submission, his brother [[Massa-an]], [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]], [[Radja Koelala|Toekoe Radja Koeala]], the [[Imam of Longbatta|Iman Longbattah]] and other principal hostile chiefs had been gathered at Selimoen until the day before the Dutch arrival, deliberating in the *missigit* of the kampong on what was to be done. Seeing the impossibility of resisting the Dutch troops, they had withdrawn to Reng-Reng, in the VII Moekims of the landschap Pedir — a place, the book describes it, situated amidst wildly overgrown mountains, that the troops could first reach after three day-marches. The column-commandant, Overste [[Majoor Gerlach|Gerlach]], seriously considered pursuing them thither on his own responsibility; but with the fatigue of troops and bearers, the great number of sick, a possibly insufficient stock of provisions, and the heavy return march to [[Lamtoba]] prescribed by the General's orders, he abandoned the enterprise after protracted deliberation with his staff — "Wie weet welk beloop de geschiedenis van den Atjeh-oorlog ... zou verkregen hebben, als de beslissing anders ware uitgevallen," the authors reflected, thinking also of the disaster that later befell the arms at [[Batoe Iliq Expedition (July 1880)|Batoe-Ilick]] among the strong mountain positions of [[Samalangan]].

## Later history

The chiefs who fled through Reng-Reng moved thereafter still further from the reach of the Dutch troops: other accounts of the dissolution of the resistance place them finally at [[Kemala]], in the interior south of Pedir. The mountain path by which the vicinity of [[Lamtoba]] communicated with Pedir was reconnoitred, during the last days of the Lamtoba occupation, by the brigade chief of the Dutch survey party ([[G. B. Hooijer]]).

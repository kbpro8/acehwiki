**Belawan** (also rendered **Belawan-Deli**) was the principal harbour of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] residency — the anchorage at the mouth of the Deli river that served the city of [[Medan|Medan]] and the surrounding Deli plantation district. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen of 1913]], Belawan appears as one of the two principal commercial opponents of the project to develop [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as a deep-water harbour — together with [[Pangkalan Brandan]]. The Belawan trade feared that the development of [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] would be "a threat to the long-cherished wish: Belawan oceaanhaven for the deepest ships" (*Belawan oceaanhaven voor de diepste schepen*).

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Belawan

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of [[Belawan|Belawan]] appears principally in the chapter on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]]. The Bureau's narrative of the commercial conflict over the [[1913]] extension plans notes that "the defenders of the development of the Belawan-haven strove in vain against the Aroe-baai-plan, which was of evident interest to the whole [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|gouvernement Atjeh]] and was maintained, even when, in place of Belawan-Deli, the equally inadequate roadstead of [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] was put forward as the desired terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]]."

The Bureau's framing of the Belawan-Deli trade as the principal commercial opponent of the Aroe-baai-plannen places the Belawan interest at the centre of the wider conflict between the Atjeh residency and the East Coast commercial establishment. The Belawan trade feared that the development of [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] would threaten the long-cherished project to develop Belawan itself as an oceaanhaven (ocean port) for the deepest ships — a project that the Bureau's account treats as a long-cherished but unrealised aspiration of the East Coast commercial interest.

The Bureau's account of the [[Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij|Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij]]'s network also identifies Belawan-Deli as the maritime anchorage at the northern terminus of the original [[1888]] spoorweg: "the original aanleg was completed in [[1888]], and was confined to a lijntje from the ankerplaats Belawan-Deli via [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Deli-Toea|Deli-Toea]], with a zijlijntje to [[Timbang-Langkat]] ([[Bindjai|Bindjai]])."

## Significance

[[Belawan|Belawan]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal harbour of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] and one of the two principal commercial opponents of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen of 1913]] — together with [[Pangkalan Brandan]]. The Belawan interest's fear that the development of [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] would threaten the long-cherished project to develop Belawan itself as an oceaanhaven places the Aroe-baai-plannen in the context of the wider commercial rivalry between the Atjeh and East Coast residencies for control of the northern Sumatran export trade.

**Kota Djawa** — also **Kota Jawa** — is the "Javanese fortification" at [[Deli]]-Toewa, on the road from [[Medan]] to [[Belawan]], whose remains [[Anderson|Anderson]] recorded in his *Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra* ([[1825]]) as "remains of a Javanese fortification", and which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] would rather read as "of [[Djawaka|Djawaka]]" — i.e. of the Sumatran-origin Javanese whose ancestral land of [[Taroema]] lay at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]]. It is built, the [[Deli]] and [[Asahan]] traditions hold, on the name of the fairy-tale queen [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]].

## Anderson's record (1825)

In his *Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra* ([[1825]]), [[Anderson|Anderson]] records (p. 293) "remains of a Javanese fortification" along the road from [[Medan]] to [[Belawan]], at Deli-Toewa. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes that Anderson read these as Javanese in the narrow sense — i.e. as built by Javanese from the great island of Java — but argues that they should rather be read as Djawaka, i.e. as built by the Hindu-Javanese of the Sumatran Overwal whose [[Madjapahit in Langsar|Madjapahit at Langsar]] is the local counterpart of the great [[Majapahit|Majapahit]] of East Java.

## The Puteri Idjo connection

The [[Deli]] and [[Asahan]] traditions record that the Kota Djawa was built on the name of the fairy-tale queen [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] — the queen whose rejection of the [[Sultan of Atjeh]]'s marriage suit, whose subsequent illness and death, and whose "glass coffin" voyage to Atjeh (lost at sea en route) are the central Acehnese legend of the east-coast polities. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads the *idjo* of her name as a *bedekte taal* form for the Kawi *sijama* — the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Cyama-Tara]], the "Redster out of all Woe" whose patroness-ship covered [[Sumatra]] itself.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Kota Djawa is the principal architectural monument of the [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] east-coast complex — the "Javanese fortification" whose remains [[Anderson|Anderson]] recorded in [[1825]], and whose dedication to [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] ties it to the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate's design on the [[Deli]]-[[Asahan]]-[[Aroe]] cultural zone. Its identification as Djawaka rather than narrowly Javanese also marks it as the local counterpart of the [[Madjapahit in Langsar|Madjapahit at Langsar]] — the Sumatran "Java" of the chronicle tradition.

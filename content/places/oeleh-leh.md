**Oeleh Leh** (modern Ulee Lheu, in Banda Aceh) was the coastal landing-place and anchorage west of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], which became the Dutch *zeehoofd* (sea-head) and the seaward corner of the *strategische driehoek* (strategic triangle) [[Kwala Atjeh]]–[[Kota Radja]]–Oeleh Leh. Borel's most damaging indictment of the second expedition is the failure to build a proper *zeehoofd* at Oeleh Leh in time, leaving the Dutch to land men, guns, vivres and munitie through the surf on a bamboo pier thrown together at the last moment. Modern Ulee Lheu occupies the site.

## The missing zeehoofd

From the moment of the [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing]] in December [[1873]] it was clear that the mouth of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] at [[Kwala Atjeh]] would be impracticable for large vessels during the *kwaden moesson* (bad monsoon), and that a sea-head with a piled pier would be needed at Oeleh Leh to land men and stores. Van Swieten refused to authorise the work in time. When the kwaden moesson "met volle kracht doorbrak" on [[31 May]] [[1874]] and the sea "geraakte geducht in beweging," debarkeeren became "hoe langer hoe moeilijker," and communica with the ships on the reede was "geheel gestremd." Borel personally supervised the debarkation of guns at the bamboo zeehoofd — he records the chaos in detail — and treats the failure as the single most damaging *fout* of preparation.

## The post and its later development

Two compagnieën were sent to Oeleh Leh in early May [[1874]] and a post was established under the line of forts covering [[Marassa]]. On [[19 May]] [[1874]] the Oeleh Leh bezetting stood an "ernstigen aanval" and "sloeg dien wakker af," saving the Dutch from being cut off from the sea. The post saved the *K. Radja–Oeleh Leh* communication again during the [[Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)|May–June 1874 crisis]]. The pile pier on iron screw-piles for railway wagons, the [[Poelolo Bras|lighthouse]] and the telegraph to K. Radja were added later; the [[Atjeh-rivier|rivier]] was gradually supplemented by the *spoorweg K. Radja–Oeleh Leh* field railway, whose aardebaan was largely completed by [[1875]] and which was in volle werking by [[1878]]. By Borel's writing the pier had become the end-station of the spoorweg, the lighthouse marked the koers for ships at night, and the telegraph linked the post to Java.

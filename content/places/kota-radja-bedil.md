**Kota Radja Bedil** ("King's Fort of the Gun") was the fort at the downstream end of the [[Pedir-dijk]] commanding the mouth of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] at [[Kwala Atjeh]], which the Atjehneezen abandoned and the Dutch reoccupied on [[28 June]] [[1874]] in the immediate aftermath of the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]]. Its guns enfiladed the river-mouth and the lower river, completing the Dutch hold on the right bank of the Atjeh-rivier from the sea up to the bend at [[Lemboe]].

## Strategic position

The fort's name — *Bedil* being the Atjehnees word for "gun" — points to its function: it was the sultan's river-mouth battery, built to command the approach to the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] from the sea. From its parapet guns could rake any vessel attempting to enter the Atjeh-rivier, and from its salient observers could see down the strand to [[Kwala Atjeh]] and up the dike to [[Langkroek]] and [[Lamara]]. Its occupation was therefore the natural complement of the Dutch hold on the [[Marine-benteng|marine-benteng]] half-way up the right bank. The Pedir-dijk ran from Kota Radja Bedil at the river-mouth up to Lemboe at the bend, and any post along the dike was useless unless K. R. Bedil was held.

## The reoccupation

The Atjehneezen had held K. R. Bedil throughout the spring of [[1874]]. [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s *afwachtende houding* after [[15 February]] [[1874]] meant that the Dutch did nothing to dislodge them. On [[28 June]] [[1874]], the day of the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] in which [[Majoor Romswinckel|majoor Romswinckel]] carried the well-laid enemy fortification at Garouw, the Atjehneezen were so shocked that they abandoned not only Garouw but the entire right bank below [[Penajoeng]] — including the Pedir-dijk, the road to Koeala, and K. R. Bedil — all of which the Dutch occupied at once. From [[28 June]] [[1874]] onward K. R. Bedil remained in Dutch hands and became the western anchor of the right-bank post chain of the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]], connected upstream to the new posts at [[Langkroek]] (28 September [[1874]]), [[Lemboe]] (7 November [[1874]]), and [[Kota Alam]] (13 November [[1874]]). To strengthen the eastern face of the stelling, a new fort was built at [[Kwala Titipandjang]] opposite [[Tiban]] on [[17 February]] [[1875]].

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — Kota-Radja Bedil (Kuta-Bubi)

Meijer gives the post its situation on a small hill at the eastern edge of the marshes along the right bank of the [[Atjeh River]], 800 metres from its mouth: an old Atjehnese fortification of earth and stone, from whose hill the whole surrounding country could be overlooked. The true name of the work was **Kuta-Bubi**. The strip of ground between it and [[Moesapi]] showed the traces of many Atjehnese earthworks in the shape of ingravings and mounds, among which two redoutes stood out, north and south of kampong Koewala.

Near his post were *heilige graven* (holy graves), to which hostile pilgrims from the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] and [[XXII Moekims]] were reported to make their pilgrimages, to bring offerings, and to perform the accompanying ceremonies. The post commander was expressly entrusted with the observation of that cemetery and the people who came to it, to prevent conflicts and to keep off strange intruders. Only near this post was it so quiet, during long periods, that a part of the slaughter-cattle destined also for the other northern works could graze outside the breastwork.

**Lho Sukon** is a locality in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — in the [[Pedirse|Pidie]] region of the north coast — that is recorded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) as the site of the deepest exploratory drilling ever undertaken in the Netherlands East Indies: a borehole that reached a depth of 3,450 m. The Lho Sukon drilling was, at the date of its completion, the deepest oil-exploration borehole in the colonial East Indian petroleum record, and it accordingly marks the technical high-point of the [[Oil exploration in North Sumatra|northern-Sumatran oil exploration]] of the late colonial period.

## The 3,450 m drilling

Van Bemmelen's reference to the Lho Sukon drilling occurs in the *Situation Before Worldwar II* section of the petroleum chapter, in the context of a discussion of the depth range of East Indian oil production:

> "The oil occurs in sand layers of neogene age, and is produced from depths of about 100–600 m, exceptionally from greater depth. The deepest producing oil horizon was about 1600 m below the surface. The deepest exploratory drilling was made in North Sumatra, Lho Sukon in Atjeh, and has reached a depth of 3,450 m."

This single sentence is the only printed reference to the Lho Sukon drilling in Van Bemmelen's text, but it is sufficient to establish the borehole's place in the colonial petroleum record: the deepest exploratory drilling in the East Indies, undertaken in Atjeh, in the years immediately before the [[1942]] Japanese invasion.

## Geological context

The Lho Sukon drilling was undertaken in the Pidie region of the Atjeh north coast — the same general region that hosted the [[Pasé oil field|Pasé]] field of the [[Atjeh oil complex|Atjeh complex]], and which was being explored by the [[N.K.P.M.]] and other companies in the late colonial period. The depth of 3,450 m indicates that the drilling was targeting a deep structural trap beneath the neogene sediments of the Atjeh north coast — a target that, in the colonial reading of the geology, was "very complicated" and unlikely to yield commercial oil. Van Bemmelen's general observation on the Atjeh petroleum geology is that "in the East Indies such steep folds (Atjeh, Borneo) and flat terrace-like structures (Sadjau) were repeatedly investigated by drilling but the results have not been encouraging."

## Significance

Lho Sukon is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the site of the deepest exploratory drilling ever undertaken in the colonial East Indies — a borehole that, at 3,450 m, set the technical depth-record for East Indian petroleum exploration and that accordingly marks the high-point of colonial oil-prospecting activity on Atjeh territory. The fact that the borehole was drilled at all — at considerable expense, and in a region that the colonial geological service had already classified as "not encouraging" for oil prospects — is itself a measure of the strategic importance that the colonial oil companies attached to the Atjeh north coast in the late 1930s and early 1940s.

**Goudberg** — the "Gold Mountain" — known natively as **Seulawah Agam** (1,726 m), is the prominent volcanic peak on the deforested highland east of the [[Kr. Atjeh|Atjeh River]] that, with its companion **Weesberg** (*Seulawah Inòng*, 868 m, the "Orphan Mountain"), forms the backdrop of the [[Pedir|Pedir]] plain and the northern skyline of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that the *Goudberg* and *Weesberg* owed their Dutch names to the gold workings that had once attracted miners — and through them, the political interest of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] — to the highlands of north-eastern Atjeh.

## Topography and landscape

The *Goudberg* (Seulawah Agam, the "male" mountain — *agam* meaning male) and *Weesberg* (Seulawah Inòng, the "female" mountain — *inòng* meaning female) rise above the "boomlooze, dorre hoogvlakte" — the treeless, barren high plateau — that extends northward from the valley of the [[Kr. Atjeh|Atjeh River]] to the *O. Batèe* (the eastern base of the Groot-Atjehsche coastal plain). The plateau, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, is crowned by "de nog gedeeltelijk bewoude kegels" — the still partly forested cones — of the two mountains, whose namesakes on the *Lam Pisang* and *Boekit Seboen* highlands form the "sawahvlakte" (sawah plain) of the same region.

## The Atjeh River valley

Below the Goudberg–Weesberg highland, the [[Kr. Atjeh|Atjeh River]] cuts its way to the North Coast. From *Seulimeum*, where the valley emerges from the Barisan range, to *Indrapoeri*, where the Barisan chains begin to part, the river widens into a "golvende, waterarme, bijna totaal ontwoude hoogvlakte" — a rolling, water-poor, almost completely deforested high plateau — crowned by the *Seulawah Agam* and *Seulawah Inòng*. Below *Indrapoeri*, the valley opens into the fertile "vlakte van Groot-Atjeh" — the plain of Groot-Atjeh — that stretches to the North Coast between *Atjeh-hoofd* and *Pedro-punt*.

## The gold workings and the Sultanate

The Dutch names *Goudberg* (Gold Mountain) and *Weesberg* (Orphan Mountain) preserve the memory of the gold workings that once drew miners to the highlands of north-eastern Atjeh. These workings — together with those of the *Kawaj XII* in the Pedir highlands — were the source of the *boengkaj* (lump) of gold dust that the *keudjeuroeën mas* (gold customs-collector) of the Sultan collected annually from each of the twelve gold-seeker settlements. The decline of these workings — and the cholera epidemic of [[1878]] that completed the depopulation of the gold districts — led to the dissolution of the *Kawaj XII* in [[1914]].

## The "Pantjang Alas" and the Pantja Pbladen

The Goudberg–Weesberg region is also associated with the *Pantjang Alas* — the *defile of refuge* — through which, in the late nineteenth century, the bands of *onverzoenlijken* (irreconcilables) driven out of the Groot-Atjeh plain made their way to the East Coast. The route, and the various *pangkalan* (fortified positions) along it, are mentioned in the military correspondence of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]].

**Blang Kala** was the mountain pass (*pas van Blang Kala*) between the [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], the only short overland road between the two districts and therefore one of the most important passages of the whole war terrain. Its name is Acehnese: *blang* meaning plain or sawah field; the pass takes its name from the plain at the foot of the pass, often pleonastically called, in the Dutch writings, *the plain of Blang Kala*.

## The terrain

The mountain chain over which the pass runs is rather wild country. Here and there stood pepper gardens and other cultivated plots — the pepper culture in Atjeh being conducted, as Meijer records, by a nomadic population in scattered huts withdrawn from all supervision, on ground that rose in steep foothills. The pass leads through a saddle, the spot where General [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] had bivouacked with his operating force on [[16 January]] [[1876]] as he marched by it with the first conquest of the IV Moekims.

## Its role in the war

Every march between the two districts went by it: Pel's first invasion of January [[1876]]; the raid of the [[Night attack on Lampagar (2-3 May 1876)|bandits of Lampagar]], said by the prisoners to have come over this pass; the operations of the [[Sweep of the IV and VI Moekims (8-15 July 1876)|July sweep]], in which Major [[Majoor Diepenheim|Diepenheim]]'s column marched through the pass to the saddle against the design in his instruction, on the same spot as Pel had bivouacked in January, searching for the mountain paths westward that, as his reports admitted, he had found too narrow for columns; and every Dutch patrol between [[Lampagar]] and the IV Moekims.

The pass opened westward into the drier ground of the IV Moekims near kampong Lampagar; between kampong and pass the ground is a plain — the *blang* whence its name — over which the works of the Dutch at Lampagar kept watch in these years. At its own saddle there runs, further west, the wilder mountain country in which the raiding bands of the [[XXII Moekims]] used to hide between the two regions.

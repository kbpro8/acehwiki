**Tangsé** is a valley of central [[Atjeh|Aceh]] with a double significance in the history of the Aceh War. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* (Dames, [[1954]]), Tangsé is — paired with [[Geumpang]] — the principal theatre of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]–[[1911]] against the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō Teungkoes]]*, and the place where [[Nussy]] received his decoration and where the last leader of the Tiriō bende was buried as a saint. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* (Merac, [[1947]]), Tangsé is the destination of the colonial army's [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant-transport]] from [[Lam-toeulo]] along the transverse road, and the place where [[Agam]] the male transport-elephant was put down by the firing-squad of the marechaussée.

## The theatre of the colonne Schmidt

"De valleien van Tangsé en [[Geumpang]] tegen de hellingen van de [[Goenoeng Alimon]] en de [[Peuët Sagoë]] op" formed the eastern side of the Tiriō theatre. "Ten Westen in het stroomgebied van de [[Kroeëng Seukoeleh]]" formed the western side. The schuilplaatsen of the Tiriō family — and of [[Teukoe Dagang]] — lay in the bergland between these two valleys. Posten stood on the paden naar de Noordkust, vanwaar ook wel werd gefourageerd; the geography of the valley — open at the paden, impassable in the bergland — was what made the colonne's method of small patrouilles and omsingelingen the appropriate tactic.

## The begrafenis of Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at

When the corpse of [[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]] — killed on [[3 December]] [[1911]] by the korporaal [[Nussy]] — was carried out, "de bevolking van Tangsé liep uit naar de begrafenis." The bandages and cloths in which the lijk was gewikkeld "werden in honderden stukjes gescheurd en meegenomen als heilige reliquiën." Tangsé is therefore the place at which the last leader of the Tiriō bende was buried as a saint of the resistance.

## Nussy's return from the passer

"Nussy op een dag van de *[[passer]]* in Tangsé met wat pakjes in zijn handen" — returning from the market in Tangsé with packages in his hands — when the news of his M.W.O. decoration reached him. "„Nussy dapat ridder klas tiga!" Nussy heeft het ridder derde klas gekregen." The hardened dardanel "heeft het uitgehuild van ontroering." Tangsé is therefore also the place at which the colonne's most decorated soldier received his decoration.

## The elephant-transport and Tangsé

In [[1913]] the transport-elephants were transported to [[Lam-toeulo]] to serve the transport to Tangsé and the still more southerly post [[Geumpang]]. Tangsé is therefore the destination of the elephant-transport from Lam-toeulo along the transverse road. "Naturally," his superior smiled. "That last transport from Tangsé to Medan took 26 days!" — the pitifully slow speed of the elephant-transport that the lorry-transport of the transverse road would replace.

## The Agam firing-squad at Tangsé

So [[Agam]] — the male transport-elephant — came to Tangsé for the firing-squad. A volley rang out from his old comrades, the men with the red patches on the collar — the marechaussées. The old hero lifted his trunk high and sank soundlessly sideways. He died as a warrior by the bullet. The Tangsé firing-squad is the colonial memory's marker of the elephant's warrior-death: the transport-elephant who was put down by the firing-squad of the marechaussée at Tangsé.

## The Tangsé and Geumpang area action

The [Tangsé- en Geumpanggebied actie](/events/tangse-en-geumpanggebied-actie) — the action in the Tangsé and Geumpang area — was one of the great successes of Van Heutsz's period of glory, the area that the actie opened to the colonial army and that the elephant-transport later served.

## Significance

Tangsé is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the geographical centre of the colonne Schmidt chapter — the place at which the last organised Acehnese armed resistance was destroyed, at which the last leader of the Tiriō bende was buried as a saint, and at which the colonne's most decorated soldier received his decoration. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* it is the destination of the elephant-transport and the place of the [[Agam]] firing-squad. The valley therefore ties together the military, religious, and personal dimensions of the account of the end of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]].

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] gives Tangsé its most detailed treatment: the valley that was the *heiligdom* of the Tiro-oelama's, *hoewel ieder man in Tangsé en omgeving de schuilplaats kende, zweeg men als het graf.*

The *bivak Tangsé* was [[Schmidt]]'s base. On [[23 April]] [[1910]] Schmidt *rukte uit van Tangsé* with two brigades and eight days' *vivres*; on [[16 May]] [[1910]] he set out on the great encircling march over the Goenoeng Meureuseuë and Goenoeng Alimon that brought him to [[Tengkoe di Boekit|Tgk. di Boekit]]'s schuilplaats on [[21 May]] [[1910]]. The *keutjhi* of Tangsé, Bèn, was bound by custom to deliver an ox to [[Tengkoe Majet di Tiro|Tgk. Majet]] before the *slametan*; Schmidt gave Bèn the money to buy the ox, marched out [[31 August]] [[1910]] exactly two days after the ox-bearers entered the forest, and on [[5 September]] [[1910]] the last Tiro-oelama fell.

When Tgk. Majet's body, its wounds bound, was brought to Tangsé, the people tore the bandages to pieces — *de zeer bevoorrechten die een stukje van het met bloed gedrenkte verband machtig werden* kept them as *reliquieën*, still in Tangsé, kept very secretly. Zentgraff repeatedly tried to photograph the grave beside the *mesdjid* but the bevolking refused: *want dit zijn dingen die vreemden niet aangaan*.

In [[September]] [[1911]] the Hari Raja fell on the 25th, and some of [[Habib Teupin Wan]]'s men went into a Tangsé kampong for the celebration. This leaked out; on [[27 September]] Schmidt marched out with thirty maréchaussée's; on [[29 September]] the colonne, marching along the Aloeë Blang Djeuraloh, came upon the Habib's schuilplaats. The aged Habib fell with eight men, *zijn grijzen kop ongedekt voor de karabijnen*.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

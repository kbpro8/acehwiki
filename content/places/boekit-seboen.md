**Boekit Seboen** — written in Dutch as also *Bt. Seboen*, *B. Seboen*, *Seboen* — was a hill with a fortification in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]], forming an enemy *stelling* that had been restored by Umar. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, Boekit Seboen was attacked and patrolled from [[23 May]] to [[25 May]] [[1896]] in the course of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]], was occupied by the peloton of [[Jhr. J. C. C. Sandberg|Jhr. Sandberg]] on the night of [[25 May]] [[1896]], and was the object of a maréchaussée rush on [[4 June]] [[1896]]. The hill formed the northern anchor of Umar's defensive *stelling* across the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] and was bombarded by the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine]] on [[25 May]] and [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] together with the kampongs of Lamlon, [[Lambaroe]] (IV Moekims context), and [[Naga Oembang]].

## Description and Location

Boekit Seboen lay in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]] between the kampong of [[Lam-Poelau]] on the south and the coastal *landschap* of [[Lepong]] on the north-west. The hill had a fortification on its top that had been restored by Umar and formed the northern anchor of his defensive *stelling* across the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV en VI Moekims]]. The hill overlalsoed the coastal plain of [[Lepong]] to the west and the kampongs of [[Lam-Poelau]], [[Lam-Tjroeng|Lam-Tiroeng]], and Daman to the south. The hill was bounded on the east by the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] and on the west by the sea-coast of [[Lepong]]. The hill was in the immediate neighbourhood of the kampong of [[Lam-Poelau]], where the Dutch had formerly maintained a *versterking* on Bt. Seboen.

## Historical Background

Boekit Seboen had been the site of a former Dutch fortification associated with the kampong of [[Lam-Poelau]] — a Dutch post in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]]. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] the hill was occupied and fortified by Umar, who restored the old *versterking* and made it the northern anchor of his defensive *stelling* across the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]]. The IV Moekims people were said to have stored goods in [[Lam-Lehoem]], [[Lam-Baroe]], and Boekit Seboen, suggesting that the hill was used as a depot as well as a defensive position. The bombardment of Boekit Seboen by the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine]] together with the kampongs of Lamlon, [[Lambaroe]] (IV Moekims context), and [[Naga Oembang]] indicates the strategic importance of the hill in the Dutch perception.

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Boekit Seboen was attacked and patrolled from [[23 May]] to [[25 May]] [[1896]] in the course of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]]. The hill was bombarded by the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]] together with the kampongs of Lamlon, [[Lambaroe]] (IV Moekims context), and [[Naga Oembang]]. On the night of [[25 May]] [[1896]] the peloton of [[Jhr. J. C. C. Sandberg|Jhr. Sandberg]] occupied the hill, and the Dutch thereby gained control of the northern anchor of Umar's *stelling*. On [[4 June]] [[1896]] the maréchaussée rushed to occupy Boekit Seboen in the course of the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|two-colonne operation into the IV and VI Moekims]]. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] Dutch troops were left at Boekit Seboen as a forward observation post. The hill served as a key reference point for Dutch operations in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] throughout June [[1896]].

## Significance

Boekit Seboen was the northern anchor of [[Teuku Umar]]'s defensive *stelling* across the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] and the principal Dutch observation post over the coastal *landschap* of [[Lepong]]. Its occupation by the Dutch on the night of [[25 May]] [[1896]] and again on [[4 June]] [[1896]] marked the effective destruction of Umar's northern defences and opened the way for the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine bombardments of Lepong]]. The hill's bombardment together with the kampongs of Lamlon, [[Lambaroe]] (IV Moekims context), and [[Naga Oembang]] indicates the Dutch perception of the hill as a key target in the campaign to break Umar's power.

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — the only post of the IV Moekims

Meijer gives Boekit Seboen as the only Dutch post that existed in the [[IV Moekims]] at the end of March [[1876]], planted on the middle and lowest of the three small hills Seboen, Poelat and Lamtjong at the entrance of the ravine of Beradin. The Dutch fort was built on the basis of an old Atjehnese earthen fort found on the plateau: an old rectangular enclosure whose sides of 54 and 40 metres answered exactly to modern need, on which two round bastions were traced. The first garrison — a company of Africans with some sappers and forced-labourers — lay on the stony ground that carried only alang-alang grass among weathered granite clumps; and the first small bamboo-thorn hedge did not thrive at all. Because the district had submitted voluntarily, the commander had strictly forbidden harm to the people of the district, so the post had to buy its materials of the population.

His account gives the officer's names for its use in these years: the post commander dared not come out from domicile without the district civil officer, who lodged in the fort; [[Toekoe Lampasei]] made it his residence to come into closer contact with his moekims; and the district's patrols — to the borders of the mountains and to [[Kroeëng Raba]] — left from it, with the [[1 June]]–[[3 June]] March of Major [[Majoor Voigt|Voigt]] also taking it as base for the [[Voigt expedition to the IV Moekims (1-3 June 1876)|search for Toekoe Tjihiq Lamngga]]. The difficult 3,600-metre mud road from Pakan Badak to it took his columns three hours.

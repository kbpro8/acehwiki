**The XXII Mukims** — the 22 Mukims — is the region of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] whose mountains were, in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *De Atjehers* and in the chapter *Jagerslatijn* of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), the habitat of the *[[Manté's|manté's]]* — the interior people who would wear no clothing and would be densely hairy over the whole body. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims]] — meaning "Federation of the Twenty-Two Mukims" — was one of the three traditional *sagi* (federations of [[moekim|mukims]]) into which [[Groot-Atjeh]] was divided, comprising twenty-two mukims situated in the western and central portion of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre, and was the region of influence of the great Achehnese war-leader [[Panglima Polim]], and the principal source of hostile war-bands — known to the Dutch as the "Toenongers" — that continually harassed the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of the [[buitenlinie]].

## The mountains of the XXII Mukims

These *manté's* would wear no clothing and would be densely hairy over the whole body. They would live in the mountains of the XXII Mukims, and all informants knew them only by hearsay. The mountains of the XXII Mukims are the book's — and Snouck Hurgronje's — marker of the habitat of the *manté's*: the mountains where the interior people lived, always one day's journey further into the interior than the researcher who wished to see them.

## Significance (folkloric-ethnographic)

The XXII Mukims are significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the region whose mountains were the habitat of the *manté's*. The book's argument — that the *manté's* would live in the mountains of the XXII Mukims and that all informants knew them only by hearsay — is the book's — and Snouck Hurgronje's — marker of the interior people of the Acehnese mountains. The XXII Mukims are therefore the book's marker of the habitat of the *manté's*: the mountains of the 22 Mukims where the interior people lived.

## The Sagi der XXII Moekims in the Aceh War (ATJEH '96 account)

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the Sagi der XXII Moekims was the principal bastion of Achehnese resistance throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The [[Dutch]] *controleur* [[K. A. Vosmaer|K. A. Vosmaer]] was stationed in this sagi, and from its territory came the warriors who besieged [[Ana' Galong]] and the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] in the spring of [[1896]].

### Description and Location

The Sagi der XXII Moekims lay west and south-west of [[Koeta Radja]], extending inland toward the [[Westkust]] (West Coast) and the mountain wall of the *gebergte*. It bordered the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims]] on the south and the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims]] on the east, and contained the Dutch post of [[Ana' Galong]] — which the [[Achehnese]] themselves "considered by the Atjehers as the heart of Groot-Atjeh" — together with the [[blokhuis|blokhuizen]] (blockhouses) of Kajoe Leh, Bak Boeng, Oedjong Boekloet, Bak Tjerlak, Mata Air, [[Montassik]], [[Aloer Oe]], and [[Toei Selimbing]]. The sagi included the mukims of [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak, Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue]] (hostile mukims that were technically part of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]) and the [[Lamkrak Moekims|4 Lamkraksche Moekims]] that were the constant target of Dutch expeditions. The sawah plain of [[Blang Bintang]] was a key feature of its south-eastern approaches.

### Historical Background

The Sagi der XXII Moekims had been the principal bastion of Achehnese resistance since [[1873]]. [[Teungkoe di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiroe]] had formerly resided at [[Ana' Galong]] in this sagi, and after the Dutch occupation of the post in [[1893]] the sagi remained the recruiting ground for the *benden* (war-bands) of [[Panglima Polim]]. The [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] mukims within the sagi had been the object of the [[1893]] campaign of [[Teuku Umar]] as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] and the proposed [[30 March]] [[1896]] operation of [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Luitenant-Kolonel Van Vliet]] (cancelled by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]]). From [[1878]] to [[1885]] the kampongs along the right bank of the [[Atjehrivier]] of the [[III Moekims Kerkoen]] (in this sagi) had been a favourite residence of marauding bands.

### Role in the 1896 Operations

The Sagi der XXII Moekims was the immediate source of the hostile *benden* that besieged [[Ana' Galong]] from [[29 March]] [[1896]] onward and that struck [[Kroeng Gloempang]] on [[13 April]] [[1896]] with a shelling and arson attempt. The [[blokhuis|blokhuizen]] of [[Aloer Oe]] and [[Toei Selimbing]] in this sagi were besieged in the night of [[28 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] and relieved on [[30 March]] [[1896]] by [[Majoor G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Van der Noordaa]]'s column. On [[17 April]] [[1896]] [[Ana' Galong]] was evacuated and its bridge blown by the Dutch engineers. On [[2 May]] [[1896]] a [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversionary operation]] under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] reoccupied the old Dutch posts of [[Senelop]] and [[Lamsoet]] within the sagi. The [[V Moekims Montassik]] operations of [[16 June]]–[[17 June]] [[1896]] and the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging (chastisement) of the Lamkrak Moekims]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] both fell within or directly against this sagi. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] [[Panglima Polim]]'s own people remained absent, and a Dutch column under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuyzen|Okhuizen]] operated freely against [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|T. Hoesin Long Bata]] and his Pedireezen in the [[Lam-Goet]] area.

### Significance (military)

The Sagi der XXII Moekims remained the principal centre of Achehnese resistance throughout the period covered by *ATJEH '96*. The [[Dutch]] failure to reduce this sagi, even after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] against [[Teuku Umar]] in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]], is acknowledged by Kruisheer as the principal strategic limit of the [[1896]] campaigns. The sagi's continued hostility under [[Panglima Polim]] ensured that the war would continue into [[1897]] and beyond.

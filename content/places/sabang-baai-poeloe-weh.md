**Sabang-baai / Poeloe Weh** — written in Dutch as the bay and also *Pulau Weh*, the island — was a bay with a coaling station on the island of Poeloe Weh off the north coast of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], at which raised guardhouses were erected. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, a detachment of 1 officer and 25 men was sent to Poeloe Weh on [[28 February]] [[1896]] because an enemy band had landed at *lér Meloh* on the island. Sabang-baai / Poeloe Weh was distinct from the "kampong Sabang" in the operational area (which was a source of heavy fire on the cavalerie on [[4 May]] [[1896]]).

## Description and Location

Sabang-baai / Poeloe Weh lay off the north coast of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] at the northern entrance to the Strait of Malacca, some distance to the north of [[Koeta Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh]]. The bay of Sabang was the principal anchorage of the island of Poeloe Weh, and the site of a coaling station for the Dutch naval squadron operating on the [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] station. raised guardhouses were erected at the coaling station for its defence. The island of Poeloe Weh was the largest of the off-lying islands north of Atjeh, and was strategically important for the control of the northern entrance to the Strait of Malacca. The kampong of Sabang on the island (distinct from the "kampong Sabang" in the operational area on the mainland) was the principal settlement. The *lér Meloh* was the place on Poeloe Weh where an enemy band had reportedly landed in [[February]] [[1896]].

## Historical Background

Sabang-baai / Poeloe Weh had been a Dutch coaling station since the early years of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], supporting the operations of the Dutch naval squadron on the [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] station. The coaling station was strategically important for the Dutch naval presence in the Strait of Malacca and for the support of operations against the [[sultanate of Atjeh]]. The raised guardhouses at the coaling station had been erected for its defence against Achehnese raids. The island of Poeloe Weh was distinct from the "kampong Sabang" in the operational area on the mainland (in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]), which was a source of heavy fire on the cavalerie on [[4 May]] [[1896]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Sabang-baai / Poeloe Weh is mentioned in *ATJEH '96* in connection with the dispatch of a detachment of 1 officer and 25 men to the island on [[28 February]] [[1896]], in response to a report that an enemy band had landed at *lér Meloh* on Poeloe Weh. The dispatch of the detachment was a precautionary measure intended to secure the coaling station against Achehnese raids, and reflects the Dutch concern about the security of their naval infrastructure in the period before the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. The island's coaling station continued to support the operations of the Dutch naval squadron throughout the first semester of [[1896]], including the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Raba bay]], the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|bombardments of Lepong]], and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]].

## Significance

Sabang-baai / Poeloe Weh was the principal Dutch naval coaling station in the [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] theatre and a strategic asset for the control of the northern entrance to the Strait of Malacca. The dispatch of a detachment to the island on [[28 February]] [[1896]] reflects the Dutch concern about the security of their naval infrastructure in the period before the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]]. The coaling station's continued support of the Dutch naval squadron throughout the first semester of [[1896]] was a prerequisite for the conduct of the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration]] and the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|bombardments of Lepong]]. The distinction between Sabang-baai / Poeloe Weh (the island coaling station) and the "kampong Sabang" in the operational area (a source of heavy fire on the cavalerie on [[4 May]] [[1896]]) is important for the avoidance of confusion.

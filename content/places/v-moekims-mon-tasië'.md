**V Moekims Mon Tasië'** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Pang Bintang]]

"…you will surely already have heard that Pang Bintang and his little troop are no more. His capture was such a stroke of luck as I have seldom seen. At last, after the transfer of *controleur* [[Roos van Raadshoven]], there was to be patrolling in the XXII Moekims as well. [[Van Andel]], since posted at [[Lam Meulò]], got the first patrol and was to begin scouring about with one brigade in the [[V Moekims Mon Tasië']] and the adjoining hills."

### From [[XXII Moekims]]

It was in this *sagi* that van Heutsz attacked the positions of [[Panglima Pòlém]] and drove him to [[Pidië]]. In July [[1913]] [[Pang Bintang]] was killed there by chance, when [[Van Andel]] of [[Lam Meulò]] got the first patrol into the [[V Moekims Mon Tasië']] after the transfer of *controleur* [[Roos van Raadshoven]] and ran into the band's hiding-place near the former [[Koeta Ba' Teuë]] on the second day.

### From [[The resistance in Atjeh 1913–1918]]

In July, and in the entirely quiet, directly administered territory of [[Groot-Atjeh]], [[Pang Bintang]] fell. Overste [[W. B. J. A. Scheepens|Scheepens]], then Civil and Military Authority at [[Sigli]], described the chance of it in one of his last letters to Schmidt before his death in October: after the transfer of *controleur* [[Roos van Raadshoven]] there was at last to be patrolling in the [[XXII Moekims]]; [[Van Andel]], since posted at Lam Meulò, got the first patrol and began to poke about in the [[V Moekims Mon Tasië']] and the adjacent hills. "Unfamiliar with the terrain and without the slightest information, he ran on the very second day, near the former [[Koeta Ba' Teuë]], straight into the band's hiding-place. Astonishment was great on both sides, but with it Pang Bintang and his men were despatched into eternity. The hiding-place lay only three-quarters of an hour from the *gampong* [[Aneu' Ngóm]] and was even connected with it by a well-trodden path. A few friends will be punished, but unfortunately the greatest thieves go free…"

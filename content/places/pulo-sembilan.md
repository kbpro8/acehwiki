**Pulo Sembilan** — Malay "Nine Islands" — is the chain of larger and smaller hills in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] opposite [[Pangkalan Soesoe]], recorded by [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] in [[1888]] and identified by local tradition as having "come here from the Overwal" and as having "belonged to the Nine Islands of [[Perak|Koeala Pérak]], of which now only eight are said to lie there". The same tradition holds that the island "would marry with [[Pulo Breueh|Poelau Salahadji]], but could not proceed further than the present place, where it anchored before a tree which is still found and which, at high as well as low water, is visible in the bay of Aroe and always protrudes equally far above the water".

## The Van Rijn record

In his [[1888]] visit to [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]], [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] recorded that "the island Sembilan opposite Pangkalan Soesoe consists of a chain of larger and smaller hills. According to the tradition this island came here from the Overwal and would have belonged to the 9 islands [[Perak|Koeala Pérak]], where now still 8 islands must lie. The same tradition relates that this island would marry with [[Pulo Breueh|Poelau Salahadji]], but could not proceed further than the present place, where it anchored before a tree which is now still found and which, at high as well as low water, is visible in the bay of Aroe and always protrudes equally far above the water".

## Decoding: the *djangkar* and the *taroe nama*

The Inlanders in these regions, [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] further recorded, "go often to offer at that tree and have much reverence for [[Pulo Sembilan|Poelau Sembilan]], where none of them will fell a tree". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes the tradition as a *bedekte taal* riddle: the "marrying" of [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] with [[Pulo Breueh|Salahadji]] is a riddle on the *djangkar* (anchor) and *Hangker*; the "tree" before which Sembilan anchored is a riddle on *boom* = *taroe nama*, i.e. [[Taroema|Taroema]], "always visible in the bay and always equally far (*djaoeh*) protruding above the water" — a riddle on *cljawa..oe.l-ér*, "reverent awe for the Land of Origin".

## The Nine Islands of Perak

The tradition that [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] "belonged to the Nine Islands of [[Perak|Koeala Pérak]]" — of which only eight are now said to lie there — [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads as a *bedekte taal* form for the ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]]: the "Nine Islands" (*Sembilan*) is the same *P.Kam-pé boe-rak* of the bay's sacred geography, whose central islet is [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]. The same tradition, De Roo de la Faille argues, underlies the *Nine Islands off [[Perak|Perak]]* that [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] records as his waypoint on the [[5 October|5 October]] [[1539]] run from [[Malacca|Malacca]] to [[Aroe|Aru]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Pulo Sembilan is the principal token of the survival of the [[Taroema|Taroema]]-[[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]-[[Aroe-baai|Aroe]] sacred geography in east-coast folk tradition — the *bedekte taal* riddle by which the older Hindu-Buddhist place-world was concealed beneath the surface of folk tale. Its identification, by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], with the Nine Islands of [[Perak|Perak]] also fixes it as the [[5 October|5 October]] [[1539]] waypoint of [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]'s voyage to the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aru war]].

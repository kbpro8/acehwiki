**Pidië** (modern *Pidie*) is a region and former coastal state on the [[Noordkust|Noordkust]] of [[Atjeh|Atjèh], one of the principal pepper-producing regions of the sultanate from the late 15th century onwards, and one of the principal rice-producing regions of Atjèh in the post-war period. The region, whose principal harbour town is [[Sigli|Sigli]] (also written *P. Tidji*), is the "*rijstland bij uitnemendheid*" (rice-land par excellence) of Atjèh, with 25,000 hectares of rice-cultivable land in the [[De Rijkdom van Atjeh (1923)|1923 brochure]] estimate, and is the site of the principal Dutch irrigation experiments in Atjèh — the [[Kroeëng Tiro|Kroeëng Tiro]] and [[Kroeëng Teupin-Raja|Kroeëng Teupin-Raja]] irrigation areas.

## The Pepper Period (post-1500)


The [[pepercultuur|pepercultuur]] must, according to the [[De Rijkdom van Atjeh (1923)|Atjeh-Instituut brochure of 1923]], have been transferred from [[Samoedra|Samoedra] to Pidië between [[1435]] and [[1500]], since [[Fei Hsin|Fei Hsin]] speaks of Samoedra pepper but not of that of Pidië, whereas at the first coming of the Portuguese Pidië was already a great pepper harbour, as was [[Pasé|Pasé]]. In [[September 1509|September]] [[1509]] the Portuguese [[Sequeira|Sequeira]] on his discovery voyages to [[Malakka|Malakka]] at the end of [[1509]] touched the pepper harbours of Pidië and Pasé, where the reception was initially favourable and trade relations were established. Of Atjèh nothing was yet said.

When the captain of Malakka, [[Ruy de Brito|Ruy de Brito]], on [[6 January]] [[6 January]] [[1514]] drew up a report to his king, he named among the Mohammedan coastal states of Sumatra also Pidië, while Atjèh was passed over. After the rise of Atjèh under [[Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah|Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah]] after [[1521]], Pidië was conquered by the Atjèhse and made tributary.

## The 17th-Century Gold and the Founding of the Kawaj XII


In the middle of the 17th century, drawn by the gold in the upper regions of Pidië and [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]], mainly Pidiërs moved thither and founded there the famous [[Kawaj XII|federation of the Kawaj XII]]. When the gold exploitation became unprofitable, the Pidiërs returned to the lowland along the coast of Meulaboh and [[Tapa Toean|Tapa Toean]], and so populated the Westkust of Atjèh.

## The Rice Period (post-1873)


With the [[pepercultuur|pepercultuur]] in decline after the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]], Pidië became the principal rice-producing region of Atjèh. The [[De Rijkdom van Atjeh (1923)|1923 brochure]] estimates 25,000 hectares of rice-cultivable land in Pidië (P. Tidji and Pidie). Pidië is the "*rijstland bij uitnemendheid*" (rice-land par excellence) of Atjèh; the average yield in normal years in the Pidië and [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] regions is known to be 60 picol of dry rice per hectare.

## The Irrigation Works


The irrigation works of Pidië are among the principal Dutch irrigation achievements in Atjèh. In the regions of Atjèh where the native irrigation was in sufficient state — mainly in Pidië, [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]], and Samalanga — the need for technical works was less pressing, and the interference of the [[Atjehsche Irrigatiedienst|Irrigatiedienst]] was limited to the construction of one single dam. The brochure of the Atjeh-Instituut recommends the appointment of three *keudjroen-loeëng* (irrigation officials) for Pidië — for the irrigation areas of the [[Pidië-rivier|Pidië-rivier]], the [[Kroeëng Tiro|Kroeëng Tiro]], and the [[Kroeëng Teupin-Raja|Kroeëng Teupin-Raja]].

## The 1916 Pellerij Plan


In [[1916]] all preparations for the establishment of a rice mill (*pellerij*) in Pidië had been made, but the pellerij did not come about because of the departure of the supervising official. One reckoned then that, with the help of the power engines of the existing gas factory, ice factory, and cinema at [[Sigli|Sigli]], a rice mill could also be driven. This was acquired by the *Luitenant-Chinees* at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta-Radja]], while the cooperation of some rice-traders was secured, who collectively would deliver 100 picol of padi daily to the mill for hulling, and who would themselves also provide for the sale of the hulled rice — so that the supply and the sale of the rice were secured to that quantity and the business ran no risk.

## The Keudjroen-loeëng


In connection with better water distribution, the Atjeh-Instituut brochure recalls an office that already long before our coming to Atjèh had existed: that of the *keudjroen-loeëng* (irrigation official). To revive this institution, experiments had already been made, and indeed in Pidië, where for that office were chosen younger brothers or close relatives of landscape-heads. The experiment was only partially successful, because no schooled personnel could be disposed of. Only after those involved had worked for a couple of years with the Irrigatiedienst could something be expected of their appointment as *keudjroen-loeëng*; in Pidië, the rice-land par excellence, for example, three *keudjroen-loeëng* could be appointed, for the irrigation areas of the Pidië-rivier, the Kroeëng Tiro, and the Kroeëng Teupin-Raja.

## Significance


Pidië is one of the principal regions of Atjèh, with a sustained significance in Atjèhse history from the late 15th century to the 20th. The pepper period (post-1500), the 17th-century gold exploitation and the founding of the Kawaj XII, the post-war rice period, and the Dutch irrigation works and the 1916 pellerij plan together constitute the principal phases of Pidië's history. The region is the "*rijstland bij uitnemendheid*" of Atjèh, and the site of the principal Dutch irrigation experiments in Atjèh.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)


**Pidië** — the *landschap* of that name, older *Pedir* — is the great *landschap* of the [[Pidië|afdeeling of the same name]], whose *oeléebalang* was, of old, one of the two greatest chiefs of the Pidië region — the other being the *oeléebalang* of [[Keumangan|Keumangan]]. Between them the predominance in the ancient region lay, and both wished to seize it. *'Maar het ging niet en nu is er al jaren een orde, waaraan allen zijn onderworpen'* — *But it did not succeed, and now for years there has been an order to which all are subject.* Preparations are forbidden, wishes are useless, dreams are deceptive. There will no doubt still be dreaming, just as there is still talk of *'een ongekroonden koning van Pidië'* — *an uncrowned king of Pidië* — but all illusion slackens the sense of duty and delays the Dutch government in the working-out of its task.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Pidië** — older *Pedir* — is the great rice-plain of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, situated on the north-western coast between the mountains and the sea, and the name of an *afdeeling* detached from the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh* in [[1922]], comprising the *onderafdeelingen* [[Sigli|Sigli]], [[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]], [[Padang Tidji|Padang Tidji]] and [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]]. Before [[1922]] these regions belonged mostly to the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh*, but one needed only to travel there to know that there was every reason for the formation of a separate *afdeeling* Pidië.

### The Valley


The mountains send their out-rows also here close to the coast. The plain of rather small extent has been made use of by the population, who built their *kampongs* along the rivers — *kroeëng Meureudoe, kroeëng Beuratjan* and however they may be called — and laid out their *sawahs*. Here one is again wholly in a land of *padi*, pepper, pinang and to a lesser extent coco-palm. There is no lack of means of livelihood in the *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling* Meureudoe. Besides rice-cultivation, which in some years gives a surplus, there is a busy fishery at [[Pante Radja|Pante Radja]] and in the *landschap* of that name the pepper-gardens are still maintained. Pinang and copra and whatever else is exported and imported find their harbour at [[Sigli|Sigli]] and to a lesser extent at [[Lho Seumawe|Lho' Seumawe]].

### The Great *Landschappen*


The greatest chiefs of Pidië were, of old, those of [[Pidië (landschap)|Pidië]] and of [[Keumangan|Keumangan]]; between them the predominance in the ancient region lay, and both wished to seize it. *'Maar het ging niet en nu is er al jaren een orde, waaraan allen zijn onderworpen'* — *But it did not succeed, and now for years there has been an order to which all are subject.* Preparations are forbidden, wishes are useless, dreams are deceptive. There will no doubt still be dreaming, just as there is still talk of *'een ongekroonden koning van Pidië'* — *an uncrowned king of Pidië* — but all illusion slackens the sense of duty and delays the Dutch government in the working-out of its task. The institution of a separate *afdeeling* Pidië in the previous year will undoubtedly contribute to the destruction of every illusion.

### The Moekims and the Landschappen


In the *onderafdeeling* Sigli one finds twenty-three *landschappen*, of which most are divided into *moekims*. At first the *moekim* was a group of *kampongs* that together had a mosque; later the *moekim* simply became a subdivision of an *oeléebalangschap*, a district with a sub-head, the *imeum* or *oeléebalang tjoet*.

### Trade and Communications


The *begrooting* of the associated *landschappen* in the *afdeeling* for [[1923]] goes over an amount of more than f 457.103. Among the expenditures the salaries take a wide place. There are *oeléebalangs* who receive 100, 200 guilders monthly; the highest-paid receives 500 guilders. An army of *oelama's* and *imeums* receives salaries of 10 and 15 guilders monthly. The Dutch government has considered that, when the religious advisers are salaried, their teaching can also be supervised. Among the income, the income-tax is estimated at f 164.220; the slaughter-tax at f 8.500; the *hacil* for pepper at f 9.500; for pinang at f 12.500; forest-product at f 2.000; commutation of *heerendienst* at f 86.900.

Pidië is a beautiful land; it seems to Broersma the most pithy *afdeeling*, a region of which much is still to be made, an important hinterland for the harbour-place where most trade is: Sigli. Besides shipping and the Atjeh-tram, a few services with small lorries provide for the need for transport. It is by no means a beautiful place, Sigli, but in comparison with the other coastal places there is considerable trade. The dwelling of the *assistent-resident*, the office of the [[Kon. Paketvaart|Kon. Paketvaart]] stand at sea, a cheerful little bit of harbour-town, while the rest is mostly sombre. At the mouth of the river is a sand-bank; often there is surf. The ships lie on the roadstead; only of the Kon. Paketvaart come eight ships monthly.

### The Pepper Trade


The great pepper-planters of Pidië, of Samalangan, of Pensangan, of Pasei, came in older times to the eastern coast to plant pepper, settled temporarily, and many formerly remained for good — for example, in the *landschap* [[Peureula (Perlak)|Perlak]]. When the disease of [[1918]] came and the rice-shortage of [[1919]] drove them home, and when the low market of [[1920]] made pepper unprofitable, they returned to Pidië. With the improvement of the market in [[1923]] some of them went back to their gardens.

### Significance


Pidië is the great rice-plain of Atjeh, and its detachment as a separate *afdeeling* in [[1922]] is the principal administrative event of the early civilian period in the north-western coast. Its great *oeléebalangs*, its twenty-three *landschappen* and its flourishing *padi*-agriculture make it, after Groot-Atjeh, the second political and economic centre of the gewest.

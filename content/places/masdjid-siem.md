**Masdjid Siem** — written by [[G. B. Hooijer]] as *Messigit (Masdjid) Siem* — was a stone mosque in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], on the old road running from [[Lamjong]] through [[Toengkoeb]] to [[Kroeng Kali]], which the *[[Moslemin]]* held as a fortified position and which was stormed by the [[3de Bataljon Infanterie|3rd battalion]] under [[Overste Sievers]] in [[October]] [[1893]] at the cost of [[Luitenant Weyerman]] and three men killed and two officers and fifty men wounded.

## Description

Hooijer described the building from the plate: the old stone wall was still a remnant of earlier times, but the roof — differing in form from the double broken roofs usually raised above such houses of prayer — dated from a later period, after the torch of war had passed over it. Behind the mosque lay the broad *sawah* (wet rice-field) plain, surrounded by heavy fruit-trees.

## The action of October 1893

The mosque was one of three enemy positions on the connecting road between [[Kroeng Kali]] and [[Toengkoeb]] which [[Overste Sievers]] was to clear. Hooijer wrote that it was again as though the defenders of that stronghold drew strength from the consecration the place held in their eyes — the place which had so often been a meeting-point for the religious and worldly festivals of which they were so fond. Dutch bullets had to fly like howling hailstorms across the broad *sawah* plain and through the foliage of the heavy fruit-trees before they gave up their sanctuary. [[Luitenant Weyerman]] and three men of lower rank fell on that plain, and two officers and fifty men were wounded there.

That evening, *in plaats van opium schuivende Moslemin* — instead of opium-smoking *Moslemin* — the mosque sheltered tired Dutch soldiers, who were to advance the next day against the third enemy position; the enemy, however, evacuated it during the night without awaiting them.

## Transfer to Toekoe Djohan

[[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]]'s troops were to garrison the mosque, since it had become only too clear that without his help the Dutch allies would not drive the fanatics from the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. On [[21 October]] [[1893]] he appeared with a hundred fighters to take the point over from the Dutch. Hooijer noted that the mosque was later provided, for his convenience, with a wooden palisade wall (*een geverfde houten omwanding*).

## The terrain around Masdjid Siem

A separate plate showed the abandoned terrain around the mosque, repopulated after the recall of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet]] from exile — a recall that compelled the *Moslemin* to evacuate the fortified points they still held there. Hooijer used the image to describe the landscape of the eastern *moekims*: wide fields overgrown with coconut palms surrounding the lower-lying marshy rice-fields, among and between which lay the *kampongs* (villages), closed off by hedges of areca, coconut and other trees, filled out with thorny bushes and spine-set plants. Badly maintained footpaths wound between the similarly fenced plots into which the *kampongs* were divided, on which stood wooden houses resting on posts, or plantings of sugar-cane, areca, fruit-trees or rice. Against the dark green of the *kampongs* hidden under dense shade, the slender coconut palms shot up like fire-arrows, their waving crowns recalling the bursting of fireworks; no side-branch broke the elegant line of the stem, and a fork such as the plate showed as a curiosity occurred only rarely.

It was beneath these trees that some 2,400 auxiliary troops assembled on [[2 November]] [[1893]] under [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]]'s leadership and at Dutch expense, to drive the *[[Prang sabi|Sabil]]* bands from the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]].

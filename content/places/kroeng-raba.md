> **Note on the shared place-name.** Two distinct Achehnese localities are referred to as *Kroeng Raba* (or *Kroëng Raba*) in the sources: (i) a kampong in the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] on the west coast — designated by [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] in [[1875]] as the western terminus of the coast-stretch to be occupied, fortified in [[July 1876]], and attacked by [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman]] in [[June 1878]] (treated in the upper sections); and (ii) a river and bay on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] immediately south of [[Koeta Radja]] — the principal east-coast anchorage of the [[Dutch]] naval squadron, and the scene of the [[1896]] naval and amphibious operations against [[Teuku Umar]] and the *landschap* of [[Lepong]] (treated in the lower sections). The two places share a name but lie on opposite coasts of the Achehnese heartland.

## The IV Moekims kampong — west-coast account (1875–1878)

**Kroëng Raba** (also written Krueng Raba) was a kampong in the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], one of the points designated by [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] in his note of [[30 April]] [[30 April]] [[1875]] as the western terminus of the coast-stretch to be occupied, and the object of repeated Dutch operations in [[January 1876|January 1876]] and [[July 1876|July 1876]]. Its fortification by the Dutch in [[July 1876|July 1876]] — for the better protection of the population of the IV Moekims — was one of the principal engineering works of the early war, and the post was repeatedly attacked by the enemy, especially under [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman]] in [[June 1878|June 1878]].

### The Conquest of the IV Moekims, January 1876

On [[15 January]] [[15 January]] [[1876]] the advance into the IV Moekims began over the [[pas van Blang Kala|pas of Blang Kala]]. The path was so narrow that four mules with ammunition cases fell into the ravine; the guns had to be taken apart and carried by coolies. By the afternoon of [[17 January]] [[17 January]] [1876] the troops descended into the plain of the IV Moekims; on [[18 January]] [[18 January]] [1876] Kroëng Raba was reached. On [[25 January]] [[25 January]] [1876] the troops returned to [[Pakan Badak|Pakan Badak]]. In ten days, from [[28 January]] [[28 January]] to [[7 February]] [[7 February]], the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] was also reduced.

### The Fortification, July 1876

Operations in [[July 1876|July 1876]] in the IV and VI Moekims and the adjoining mountains were undertaken "with the object of driving the enemy from there, destroying his dwellings and provision-shops, and at the same time establishing a post at Kroëng Raba, for the better protection of the population of the IV Moekims." The establishment of the post at Kroëng Raba was part of the wider Dutch strategy of securing the IV Moekims after the conquest of the VI and IX Moekims.

### The Attack of June 1878

By the spring of [[1878]] the [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib]] was once more on the offensive. As soon as [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] had departed for [[Gedong|Gedong]], news came of the Habib's intention to make a great attack with 2,000 men in the IV Moekims on the posts at Kroëng Raba, [[Boekit Seboen|Boekit Seboen]] and [[Pakan Badak|Pakan Badak]]. On [[22 June]] [[22 June]] [[1878]] Kroëng Raba was attacked by a great multitude of Atjehers, who shot at the garrison until [[24 June]] [[24 June]] without venturing a storm. On [[23 June]] [[23 June]] the Habib sent an Atjeher with a white flag and a letter inviting the post-commandant "to leave his position and fight with him in the open field," and on [[24 June]] [[24 June]] a second letter offering free withdrawal on condition that the Dutch fortification was destroyed. At the same time various posts in other lines were heavily attacked. Van der Heyden decided to return at once; on [[23 June]] [[23 June]] he arrived at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]], and on [[24 June]] [[24 June]] a column was sent to force the [[kloof van Beradin|kloof of Beradin]], to supply the garrisons of Boekit Seboen and Kroëng Raba with food and to drive the enemy out. After a difficult march through the sawah plain, in which the column suffered considerable losses from the enemy's fire, and later through the mountains, the object was attained and both posts were relieved, whereupon the enemy withdrew entirely, only to undertake new attacks at other points.

### The Episode of the Fort

According to the testimony gathered by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], the Achehnese came in time to regard Habib Abdurrahman as an ambitious traitor, and even suspected him of having acted as a Dutch spy from the very outset of his career in Acheh. Among the "proofs" they cited in support of this view were precisely those actions which, to the European observer, demonstrated his superiority over the Achehnese in civilisation and political insight. The episode at Krueng Raba was one of the most telling of these. Why, his former followers demanded, when they had surrounded the Gompeuni's fort at Krueng Raba with a much superior force under his leadership, did he promise a free retreat to the commandant and his men if they surrendered, and forbid the Achehnese, if the offer were accepted, to raise a hand against the unbelievers?

### Habib Abdurrahman's Wider Strategy

To the Achehnese, accustomed to regarding any stratagem against an infidel enemy as legitimate, such forbearance was incomprehensible. They could not understand that the Habib's closer insight led him to attach great weight to the impression his actions would make upon the enemy — a consideration to which the Achehnese had always been utterly indifferent. The same diplomatic shrewdness was visible in his skilful use of the *mupakat* (the Achehnese deliberative assembly) as an instrument for centralising power under his own control during the years immediately preceding the outbreak of full-scale hostilities with the Dutch.

### Historical Significance (IV Moekims kampong)

The incident at Krueng Raba thus belongs to the formative phase of the [[Holy War (Jihad) against the Dutch|national war]] that began with the [[First Dutch attack on Acheh|Dutch attack]] and continued after the Habib's eventual withdrawal from active affairs. Together with other episodes — notably the stoning of a couple for illicit intercourse carried out under his authority — it illustrates the gulf between the Habib's conception of a disciplined, religiously grounded polity and the irregular, kinship-based warfare actually practised by the Achehnese sagis and uleebalangs. Snouck Hurgronje treats the case as typical of the mutual misunderstanding that pervaded relations between the religious party and the adat-chiefs of Acheh throughout the [[Achehnese War|war]].

## The east-coast Kroeng Raba — account from *ATJEH '96* (Kruisheer, 1913)

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, **Kroeng Raba** — written in Dutch as also *Kroeng-Raba*, *Kroeng-Ralb*, *Kroëng Raba* — was a river and bay on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] immediately to the south of [[Koeta Radja]], behind which [[Teuku Umar|T. Oemar]]'s warriors retreated on [[25 May]] [[1896]] and at which Umar dug trenches at the beach. The Kroeng-Raba bay was the site of the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration of 1–4 May 1896]] and of the subsequent [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|bombardments of Lepong]] of [[25 May]] and [[4 June]]–[[5 June]], [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]]. The great [[Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba Bridge (13 June 1896)|bridge reconnaissance of 13 June 1896]] was conducted at Kroeng Raba. A training march (*oefeningsmarsch*) of [[Majoor Mossel|Majoor Mossel]] — 8 companies infantry, cavalry detachment, mountain battery, ambulance and baggage train — had marched from Koeta Radja to Kroeng Raba on [[20 February]] [[1896]] and encountered no resistance.

### Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

Kroeng Raba lay on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] immediately to the south of [[Koeta Radja]] and to the north of the *landschap* of [[Lepong]]. The Kroeng Raba river ran from the inland *gebergte* (mountain range) eastward to the sea, and the Kroeng-Raba bay was the bay into which it emptied. The beach at the river's mouth was the site of [[Teuku Umar|T. Oemar]]'s trenches dug after the retreat of [[25 May]] [[1896]]. The Kroeng Raba bridge was the principal crossing of the river, and was the object of the [[Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba Bridge (13 June 1896)|great bridge reconnaissance of 13 June 1896]]. The *Kroeng-Rababaai* (Kroeng-Raba bay) was the anchorage of the Dutch warships that conducted the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration of 1–4 May 1896]] and the subsequent [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|bombardments of Lepong]]. The *Tandjoeng-Kroeng Raba* area was a reference for cavalry observation. The training march of [[Majoor Mossel|Majoor Mossel]] of [[20 February]] [[1896]] had marched from Koeta Radja to Kroeng Raba and back without resistance.

### Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

Kroeng Raba had been the principal east-coast anchorage of the [[Dutch]] naval squadron operating in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] since [[1873]]. The bay was sheltered by the off-lying features and was close to the Dutch bridgehead at [[Koeta Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh]]. The Kroeng Raba river marked the boundary between the Dutch bridgehead and the *landschap* of [[Lepong]] — the territory of [[Teuku Umar]] — and was therefore the natural line of contact between the two. The training march of [[Majoor Mossel|Majoor Mossel]] of [[20 February]] [[1896]] — 8 companies infantry, cavalry detachment, mountain battery, ambulance and baggage train — had marched from Koeta Radja to Kroeng Raba without resistance, indicating that the area was not yet openly hostile. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] the Kroeng Raba became the principal line of contact between the Dutch and Umar's forces.

### Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

Kroeng Raba was the principal scene of Dutch naval operations throughout the first semester of [[1896]]. On [[20 February]] [[1896]] the training march of [[Majoor Mossel|Majoor Mossel]] marched from Koeta Radja to Kroeng Raba without resistance. From [[1 May]] to [[4 May]] [[1896]] the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai]] was conducted by Dutch warships from the bay, in support of the [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversionary operation to Montassik]]. On [[25 May]] [[1896]] [[Teuku Umar|T. Oemar]]'s warriors retreated behind the Kroeng Raba river after the collapse of his *stellingen* in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]], and Umar dug trenches at the beach. The [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine bombardment of Lepong]] was conducted from the Kroeng-Rababaai on [[25 May]] [[1896]] in conjunction with the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]]. On [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] and [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]] the bombardments were repeated. On [[13 June]] [[1896]] the [[Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba Bridge (13 June 1896)|great bridge reconnaissance]] was conducted at the Kroeng Raba bridge. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] a Dutch column under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuyzen|Okhuizen]] marched to the Kroeng Raba in the course of the [[Machtsvertoon (20 June 1896)|four-column show-of-force]]. On [[22 June]] [[1896]] a reconnaissance by [[Majoor J. F. Breijer|Laceulle]] advanced to the Kroeng Raba in coordination with the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]].

### Significance (ATJEH '96 account)

Kroeng Raba was the principal Dutch naval and amphibious base for the operations against [[Teuku Umar]] and the [[landschap]] of [[Lepong]] throughout the first semester of [[1896]]. The bay's sheltered anchorage made it the natural station of the Dutch warships conducting the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration]] and the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|bombardments of Lepong]], and the river marked the principal line of contact between the Dutch bridgehead and Umar's territory. The [[Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba Bridge (13 June 1896)|great bridge reconnaissance of 13 June 1896]] was one of the largest cavalry reconnaissances of the period.

## Account from *Een Vaderlandsch Belang* by H. Borel (1878)

The **Kroeng Raba** (Krueng Raba) is the river on the west coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], whose mouth at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai|Baai van Kroeng Raba]] formed the western end of the great afsluitingslinie that [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] planned between the mouth of the Kroeng Raba on the west coast and the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raya]] on the north coast, with the aim of isolating Atjeh from the sea. The river and its mouth were conquered by Pel's colonne in January [[1876]] in the opening phase of the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great winter offensive]].

## The western end of the afsluitingslinie

Pel's plan, as he explained it at his [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Buitenzorg planning session]] in July [[1875]] to [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]] and the commandant van het leger, called for the eventual extension of the Dutch linie from the mouth of the Kroeng Raba on the west coast to the mouth of the Kroeng Raya on the north coast, isolating Atjeh from the sea and reducing it to a *binnenstaat* (inland state). The Kroeng Raba was therefore not merely a tactical objective but the western anchor of a strategic conception. The post at the river's mouth would, with the post at the [[Kroeng Raya]] mouth on the north coast, complete the isolation of Groot-Atjeh from foreign supply and seal the fate of the resistance. Borel stresses that Pel's plan was never communicated officially, not even to his *chef van den staf* — nothing about it was found in his archive after his death.

## The conquest of January 1876

The Kroeng Raba was reached on [[15 January]] [[1876]] by a colonne marching from [[Lampagger]] through the [[Blang Kalla]] kloof southward over *bergachtig terrein* (mountainous terrain) into the [[IV Moekims]], reaching Loenga on the right bank of the Kroeng Raba mouth, which would become the *westelijke grens* (western limit) of the new afsluitingslinie. The hoofden there submitted. The colonne returned via the *noordoostelijke weg*, left a post at Boekit Seboen on [[24 January]] [[1876]], and reached Pakan Badak; it then made a *tocht* through the eastern part of the IV Moekims. With the Kroeng Raba mouth in Dutch hands, the IV Moekims were opened to Dutch operations, and the way was prepared for the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims invasion]] of [[28 January]] [[1876]] through the [[Glitaroem]] pass. The death of Pel at [[Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]] interrupted the completion of the afsluitingslinie, but the Kroeng Raba anchor held.

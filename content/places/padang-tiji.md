**Padang Tiji** (also written **Padang Teuji** or simply **Tiji**) is a locality in the [[Mukims VII|VII Mukims]] of the [[XXII Mukims|Sagi of the XXII Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh]], associated with the VII Mukims. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Snouck Hurgronje cites Padang Tiji as the place of death of a member of the Achehnese royal house, the prince **Tuanku Asem**. The place is also mentioned in the [[Hikayat Pochut Muhamat]], where 'Padang Teuji (Tiji)' is where [[Pochut Muhamat]] remains as long as necessary for regulating the affairs of the VII Mukims and winning over the people to his cause.

## The Death of Tuanku Asem

The author records that Tuanku Asem — "according to all who know him, an exception to the majority of the men of royal blood in Acheh" — "died [[1897|January 1897]] at Padang Tiji in the Mukims VII." Tuanku Asem spoke Malay and a little Arabic, understood something of English, and could discuss the religious questions of the day from a more liberal standpoint than that of the pandits. The place of his death thus connects Padang Tiji with a current of reformist and cosmopolitan thought in late nineteenth-century Acheh, and with the small circle of Achehnese princes who, by their education and disposition, stood apart from the great mass of the royal house.

## The Village and the Royal House

Padang Tiji belongs to that upland portion of the [[XXII Mukims]] in which the members of the Achehnese royal house dispersed themselves among the uleebalangships of the country, drawing their maintenance from the lands of the chiefs and forming, in each district, a small aristocracy of blood distinct from the local uleebalang. The presence of a Tuanku at Padang Tiji is thus a typical instance of the wider pattern by which the Achehnese royal house — the *tuankus* — were settled throughout the country, and especially throughout the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]] and [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]] sagis, where they enjoyed precedence in respect of birth and of the *jinamee* or dowry paid at their marriage.

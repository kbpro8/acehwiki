**Padang Tiji** (also written **Padang Teuji**, **Padang Tidji**, or simply **Tiji**) is a locality in the [[VII Mukims|VII Mukims]] of the [[XXII Mukims|Sagi of the XXII Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh]], in the [[Aceh|Acheh]] region. The place is associated in [[Snouck Hurgronje's The Achehnese|*The Achehnese*]] Vol. I with the Achehnese royal house — cited as the place of death of the prince [[Tuanku Asem]] — and in the [[Hikayat Pochut Muhamat]] as a station of [[Pochut Muhamat]] in his regulation of the affairs of the VII Mukims. It is also recorded in the sixteenth-century Malay Burda manuscript (DLP-54) as the hometown of the Achehnese scholar-translator [[Teungkn Tjēh Kob al. T. Panté Tjeureumèn|Teungku Tjēh Kob of Panté Tjeureumèn]], who produced an Achehnese version of the [[Burda (poem)|Burda poem]] and a *mawlid*-bewerking (mawlid adaptation). Under the spelling **Padang Tidji**, the place also served as an intermediate station on the [[1899]] western branch of the second Atjeh tramway.

## The Death of Tuanku Asem

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] records that [[Tuanku Asem]] — "according to all who know him, an exception to the majority of the men of royal blood in Acheh" — "died [[1897|January 1897]] at Padang Tiji in the Mukims VII." Tuanku Asem spoke Malay and a little Arabic, understood something of English, and could discuss the religious questions of the day from a more liberal standpoint than that of the pandits. The place of his death thus connects Padang Tiji with a current of reformist and cosmopolitan thought in late nineteenth-century Acheh, and with the small circle of Achehnese princes who, by their education and disposition, stood apart from the great mass of the royal house.

## The Village and the Royal House

Padang Tiji belongs to that upland portion of the [[XXII Mukims]] in which the members of the Achehnese royal house dispersed themselves among the *uleebalangships* (territorial chiefdoms) of the country, drawing their maintenance from the lands of the chiefs and forming, in each district, a small aristocracy of blood distinct from the local *uleebalang*. The presence of a *tuanku* (prince of royal blood) at Padang Tiji is thus a typical instance of the wider pattern by which the Achehnese royal house — the *tuankus* — were settled throughout the country, and especially throughout the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]] and [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]] sagis, where they enjoyed precedence in respect of birth and of the *jinamee* (dowry) paid at their marriage.

## Padang Tidji and the Hikayat Pochut Muhamat

The place is also mentioned in the [[Hikayat Pochut Muhamat]], where 'Padang Teuji (Tiji)' is the locality at which [[Pochut Muhamat]] remains as long as necessary for regulating the affairs of the VII Mukims and winning over the people to his cause.

## The Hometown of the Burda Translator

The sixteenth-century Malay translation of the [[Burda (poem)|Burda of al-Busiri]] preserved in manuscript DLP-54 identifies Padang Tidji as the hometown of the Achehnese scholar-translator [[Teungkn Tjēh Kob al. T. Panté Tjeureumèn|Teungku Tjēh Kob of Panté Tjeureumèn]] ("Teungkn Tjēh Kob al. T. Panté Tjeureumèn uit Padang Tidji"), who produced an Achehnese version of the Burda poem and a *mawlid-bewerking* (mawlid adaptation). The manuscript thus locates Padang Tidji within the devotional-literary geography of sixteenth-century Acheh, as the home of one of the principal known transmitters of Arabic devotional poetry into Achehnese.

## Padang Tidji and the Tramway

**Padang Tidji** also served as an intermediate station on the [[1899]] western branch of the second Atjeh tramway — the *lijntje* (little line) built from [[Sigli]] westward over Padang Tidji to [[Keude Breue]]. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account mentions the place in passing: "already in [[1899]] from [[Sigli]] a *lijntje* was built westward over Padang Tidji to [[Keude Breue]]." The *lijntje* through Padang Tidji was, the Bureau notes, "a piece of tramway in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Government of Acheh and Dependencies]], somewhat hanging in the air" (*wel wat in de lucht hangende, stuk trambaan in de Onderhoorigheden*) until the [[1904]]–[[1908]] completion of the [[Berglijntje Keude Breue–Seulimeum|berglijntje (mountain-line) from Keude Breue over the hills to Seulimeum]] connected it to the [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] network.

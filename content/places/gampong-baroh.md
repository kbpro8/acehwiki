**Gampong Baroh** (Achehnese *gampông barôh*, "the lower village") is a settlement in the highland sagi of the [[XXII Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh|Great Acheh]] whose name is preserved in the territorial title of [[Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh]], chief of the [[V Mukims]] and one of the traditional "twelve uleebalangs" of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|sultanate]].

Snouck Hurgronje mentions the village only in passing, in the course of his list of the chief uleebalangs of [[Great Acheh]] as they were recognised at the court of [[Banda Acheh|Banda-Acheh]]. For the [[XXII Mukims]] two chiefs are named: [[Teuku Muda Ba'et|Teuku Ba'et]], uleebalang of the [[VII Mukims Ba'et|VII Mukims]], and Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh, chief of the V Mukims. The full territorial style of the latter chief — *Waki Chi'* being the highland Achehnese equivalent of the lowland *keuchi'* (the father of the community) and *Chi'* the respectful title of address applied to venerable persons — records the place of his original jurisdiction. The survival of the village name in the territorial title of the entire chiefdom is itself an index of the personal ascent by which the waki of a single gampong had risen to the uleebalangship of a whole mukim-cluster.

The toponym *barôh* — "low, lower, downstream" — is one of the commonest spatial qualifiers in Achehnese settlement nomenclature, used to distinguish the lower or seaward portion of a settlement complex from its *atrôe* (upper) counterpart. Villages of this name are thus found in many parts of Acheh, and the Gampong Baroh associated with the V Mukims should not be confounded with the better-known *Kampong Baru* of colonial [[Banda Acheh|Banda-Acheh]] or with the [[Baroh|Baroh]] locality in the lowlands.

Snouck warns, however, that the traditional list of twelve uleebalangs — though long received as a definitive statement of the Achehnese constitution — at most represents the conditions that existed during a short period. In reality the whole twelve had not in the long run a voice in the affairs of the capital, while some of them had absolutely no share therein. On the other hand, there were uleebalangs in the sultan's territory and the so-called wakeueh-districts — such as [[Teuku Kali]], [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]], and the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata|Imeum of Lueng Bata]] — who had much more weight in the scale than many of the members of the board of twelve. The V Mukims and its chief, bearing the name of Gampong Baroh, must accordingly be regarded as one of the highland chiefdoms whose territorial importance was real in its own country but whose voice at the capital was, by the close of the nineteenth century, altogether secondary.

## Additional account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Gampong Baroh

**Gampong Baroh** (Achehnese *gampông barôh*, literally "the lower village") is a settlement of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh]] whose name survives in the territorial style of [[Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh|Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh]], the chief of the [[V Mukims|V Mukims]] and one of the traditional twelve *uleebalangs* enumerated in Achehnese political memory.

### In the Enumeration of the Twelve Uleebalangs

Snouck Hurgronje's chapter on the constitution of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Sultanate]] reproduces the conventional list of the twelve great chiefs who, according to tradition, surrounded the sultan at [[Banda Acheh|Banda-Acheh]]. For the highland sagi of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]] two chiefs are named: [[Teuku Muda Ba'et|Teuku Ba'et]], uleebalang of the [[VII Mukims Ba'et|VII Mukims Ba'et]], and Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh, chief of the V Mukims. The style of the latter — *Waki Chi'* being the highland Achehnese equivalent of the lowland *keuchi'* ("father of the community"), and *Chi'* the respectful title used in addressing a venerable person — fixes the original seat of the chief's authority at the village of Gampong Baroh.

### Toponymy and Settlement Pattern

The qualifier *barôh* — "low, lower, downstream, seaward" — is one of the commonest spatial designations in Achehnese settlement nomenclature. It routinely distinguishes the lower or seaward section of a settlement complex from its *atrôe* (upper) counterpart. Villages called Gampong Baroh are accordingly numerous across the Achehnese lowlands, and the Gampong Baroh of the V Mukims should not be confounded either with the *Kampong Baru* of colonial [[Banda Acheh|Banda-Acheh]] or with the [[Baroh|Baroh]] locality in the lowland coast.

### A Title that Outgrew its Village

The survival of a single village name in the territorial style of an entire mukim-cluster is itself revealing. It indexes the personal ascent by which the *waki* of one gampong — a subordinate officer appointed by the uleebalang — had, in the course of generations, parlayed his local authority into the uleebalangship of a cluster of five mukims. Such upward mobility was not uncommon in Acheh, where the distinction between the religious-ceremonial *keuchi'* (or *waki chi'*), the *imeum* of a mukim, and the *uleebalang* of a district was often blurred by adat.

### A Secondary Voice at the Capital

Snouck warns, however, that the traditional list of twelve — long received as a definitive statement of the Achehnese constitution — at most represents the conditions that existed during a short period. The whole twelve had not in the long run a voice in the affairs of the capital, while some of them had absolutely no share therein. On the other hand, there were uleebalangs in the sultan's territory and the so-called wakeueh-districts — such as [[Teuku Kali|Teuku Kali]], [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya|Panglima Meuseugit Raya]], and the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata|Imeum of Lueng Bata]] — who had much more weight in the scale than many of the members of the board of twelve. The V Mukims and its chief, bearing the name of Gampong Baroh, must accordingly be reckoned among the highland chiefdoms whose territorial importance was real in its own country but whose voice at the capital was, by the close of the nineteenth century, altogether secondary.

**Tjot-Goe** was a Dutch military post in the southern section of the geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position) in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Unlike most external posts, Tjot-Goe was not evacuated in [[1885]] but was retained due to its favorable position for covering construction activities.

## Location

Tjot-Goe was situated on a heuvel (hill) of 40 meters height at a short distance from the new line. The elevated position provided excellent observation and fire control over the surrounding area, making it valuable for covering operations in the southern section.

## Military Significance

### Pre-Concentration Era

Tjot-Goe was one of the posts that had existed before the concentration decision of [[1884]]. Its elevated position made it strategically valuable for:
- Observation of enemy movements
- Covering construction activities
- Providing artillery fire support

### Role During Concentration

Despite being outside the originally planned line, Tjot-Goe was retained because its favorable situation allowed it to dekken (cover) the construction activities in the southern section of the position. The hilltop position provided an excellent observation post that enhanced the overall defensive capability of the area.

## Fortification

Tjot-Goe was:

**Status:** Retained outside the main line
**Garrison:** 1 company of Infantry
**Armament:** 1 × 12cm breech-loading gun, 1 × 8cm field gun, 1 Coehoorn mortar

The post was connected to [[Ketapan-Doea]] by paths from [[Lampeneroet]], allowing supply and reinforcement.

## Significance

Tjot-Goe's retention demonstrated the pragmatic approach to the concentration. Rather than strictly adhering to the planned perimeter, the Dutch retained positions that provided genuine tactical advantage. The hilltop position provided:
- Early warning of enemy approach
- Artillery support for the southern section
- Covering fire for construction and patrol activities

## Later Status

As of August 1885, Tjot-Goe remained occupied. The author noted that its favorable situation justified its retention, though the post was eventually evacuated as the position was consolidated.

**Blangtekoer** — also *Blang Tekoer* — is one of the two *Semnenboschcomplexen* (Sumatran-pine forest complexes) — together with [[Paja Djorok]] — in which the trial exploitation of [[Pinus Merkusii]] turpentine began on [[14 September]] [[1924]] under the [[Besluit van 27 June 1924 on Atjeh-turpentine exploitation|Besluit of 27 June 1924]]. The forest complex is named in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] as the western of the two complexes separated by the river [[Baleq]], and as one of the two sites of the early trial exploitation of the [[Baleq|Baleq enterprise]].

## The forest complex

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that Blangtekoer and Paja Djorok were *betrekkelijk dicht gelegen bij den grooten weg van Takengon naar Bireuen (Atjeh)* — *located relatively close to the main road from Takengon to Bireuen (Atjeh)*. The two forest complexes were separated by the river [[Baleq]], from which the [[Baleq|Baleq enterprise]] took its name. The forest complex was the western of the two — *gescheiden door een rivier, de Baleq, waaraan de naam "Baleq" voor het proefbedrijf ontleend is*.

## The trial of 14 September 1924

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the trial exploitation of [[Pinus Merkusii]] turpentine began on [[14 September]] [[1924]] in the two *Semnenboschcomplexen* — Paja Djorok and Blangtekoer. The trial was undertaken by *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* on behalf of the *Dienst van het Boschwezen*, and it was funded by the Regeering under the [[Besluit van 27 June 1924 on Atjeh-turpentine exploitation|Besluit of 27 June 1924]]. The results of the trial were sufficient that, in [[1926]], the Regeering resolved to convert the trial into a definitive exploitation as a separate division of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* with effect from [[1 January]] [[1927]].

## The forest in 1928

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that one of the photographs reproduced in the pamphlet — Fig. I — was *Coll. K. I., opn. O. Brandts Buys* — a photograph from the Koloniaal Instituut collection, taken by O. Brandts Buys — and was captioned *Bosch van Pinus Merkusii in Atjeh* — *Forest of Pinus Merkusii in Atjeh*. The photograph illustrates the *Semnenboschcomplexen* of which Paja Djorok and Blangtekoer were the two principal sites, and it documents the silvicultural condition of the [[Pinus Merkusii]] stands in the late [[1920s]].

## Significance

Blangtekoer's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as one of the two original sites of the trial exploitation of [[Pinus Merkusii]] turpentine in the Netherlands Indies — the forest complex where the early trial samples of Atjeh-turpentine were first produced, and where the *quarre* tapping method was first applied in Atjehnese conditions. The forest complex's location near the Takengon-Bireuen road was the principal logistical advantage that made the trial possible, and its separation from [[Paja Djorok]] by the river [[Baleq]] gave the enterprise its name.

A location on the West Coast with an important chief. 'The energetic chief of Teunom (Teuku Imeum) is said to be a descendant of this dreaded protector of property' ([[Teungku di Weueng]]). Teunom is listed as a major territorial division.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Teunom

**Teunom** is a place on the West Coast of Acheh mentioned in *The Achehnese* Vol. I both for its chief's arbitrary punishments and for its energetic imeum, who is contrasted with the adventurer [[Teuku Uma]] as an example of how an entrenched adat-chief could hold his own against a powerful upstart.

### The Chief of Teunom's Arbitrary Punishments

In his discussion of how, "in Acheh and its dependencies, just as in other Mohammedan countries, arbitrary punishments are often elaborated and inflicted by the chiefs," Snouck Hurgronje cites the present chief of Teunom as a telling example: "Thus the present chief of Teunom used often to cause adulterous lovers to be deprived of their virility by the objects of their passion. He also punished many transgressors of the law as to fasts by having them led about the country on all fours for some days with rings in their noses like buffaloes, and forcing them to eat grass." The anecdote illustrates the sweeping quasi-judicial authority exercised by West-Coast uleebalangs within their own domains, as well as the grotesque ingenuity of the punishments they devised.

### The Imeum of Teunom versus Teuku Uma

The other appearance of Teunom in Vol. I is in Snouck's discussion of the rise of [[Teuku Uma]] from chief of a small band to a regional power on the West Coast. Snouck observes that Uma's career demonstrated the comparative advantage which chiefs with their own rakans (followers) and material resources enjoyed over mere ambitious military leaders. "Against an energetic chief such as the imeum of Teunom even Teuku Uma could make but little head." Snouck's parenthetical note adds: "[An account of the subsequent adventures of Teuku Uma, since 1892, will be found in the Preface.]" The contrast is significant: the imeum of Teunom is the type of the rooted hereditary chieftain, as opposed to the mobile, ambitious Teuku Uma whose power was of the opportunistic, military sort.

### Significance

Though Teunom appears only briefly in Vol. I, its double association — with the cruel ingenuity of West-Coast adat-justice and with the strong hereditary chieftainship that kept even Teuku Uma at bay — gives it an illustrative importance in Snouck's analysis of the political fragmentation of the West Coast. The place is treated more fully in Vol. II of *The Achehnese*.

**Bakongan** was a military post of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] on the [[Westkust (of Atjeh)|Westkust]] of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] at which the author served in the dertiger jaren alongside the sergeant [[Thijs de Fretes]] and the *Marechaussee 1e klas* [[Tanamal]] of the [[6e Divisie Marechaussee]], and at which he first met [[Dominee Thenu]] on the beach at the end of [[1932]]. It is one of the principal settings of the chapters "Thijs" and "Dominee Thenu" in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, and the post from which the *colonne* of [[Schmidt]] operated against the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō Teungkoes]]* in the early 1910s.

## The 1932 meeting with Thenu

"De eerste maal, dat ik [[Dominee Thenu]] ontmoette, was eind 1932 op het strand van Bakongan." The boot was unloaded with *Atjehse vissersprauwen* — Acehnese fishing canoes — and the dominee, then "vierenzestig" years old, was carried ashore on the back of a Timorese soldier who waded into the sea to meet the boot. "Speciaal ook met een Timorees, die geheel vooraan was en de dominee op zijn rug nam." Later the author saw the two walking deep in conversation; but when the kerkdienst came, the Timorees was not there — he was Rooms-Katholiek. The author concludes: "dit gesprek buiten de kerk om was wel de ware geestelijke verzorging."

## The dertiger jaren at Bakongan

"In Bakongan hadden we in de dertiger jaren [[Thijs de Fretes]]." The dertiger jaren at Bakongan are the setting of the chapter on Thijs: the *sergeant 1e klasse* of the [[6e Divisie Marechaussee]], the *woudloper* and jager whose plain-spoken Dutch, when the new dokter's *jeugdige vrouw* asked him how the patrol had been, came out as "„O, verdomd best Mevrouw, alleen dat verdomde, stinkende rotwater daar, dat is toch zo beroerd, ziet U!"" — a remark only comprehensible to those who had spent days in the [[Troemon]]se moerassen at [[Ië Itam]] and [[Ië Merah]].

[[Tanamal]] was also at Bakongan in the dertiger jaren — the *Marechaussee 1e klas* whose *ongelooflijk lelijke snuit* became *één en al vrolijkheid* at feestjes where he served as *voorzanger*. The post therefore hosted two of the book's set-piece Ambonese soldiers.

## The kolonel's visit (1933)

"Toen in 1933 de kolonel op Bakongan was, had de *[[zelfbestuurder van Troemon]]* het over een *solitair*, die gevaarlijk was voor de bevolking en schade aanrichtte." Thijs promised the kolonel the rogue elephant's *tanden* before the beast was shot — and shot it at five metres' distance.

## The new dokter

"We hadden net een nieuwe dokter *betoel* aangekregen, pas afgestudeerd, getrouwd, een paar maanden [[Batavia]] en toen rechtstreeks Bakongan." The new dokter's wife — fresh from a few months in Batavia and unaccustomed to the post — was the addressee of Thijs's plain-spoken Dutch about the *stinkende rotwater*.

## Significance

Bakongan is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as one of the principal settings of the book's dertiger jaren chapters. It is the post at which Dames met [[Dominee Thenu]], at which he served with [[Thijs de Fretes]] and [[Tanamal]], and from which the *colonne* of [[Schmidt]] had operated two decades earlier. The post therefore ties the book's two great temporal layers together: the late-colonial dertiger jaren of the author's own service, and the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] counter-insurgency of the [[1910s|1910]]s.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]]'s *Atjeh* gives the fullest war-era account of Bakongan. The *kerkhof* holds a *dubbele grafzerk, van onder tot boven, en in twee rijen, bedekt met de namen der dooden van de patrouille van luit. Donner in 1905* — the mass grave of two Dutch patrols surprised on the same night in [[August]] [[1905]], one at kampong Rambong and one by Sibade, losing a total of twenty-three men: *EUR. FUS. LENKMAN No 52420, EUR. SERGT. W. SOEST No 47563, EUR. FUS. ADETJER No 54284, INL. KORP. KASMOREDJO No 40090*, and many others, *overvallen en gesneuveld op 12 Augustus 1905 en hier begraven*.

Bakongan belongs to the country of *klewangaanvallen* and the *Paja Tjitcem* moeras — the *Vogelmoor* — through which [[Pang Nanggroë]]'s bende was pursued. In [[July]] [[1926]] kapitein [[Gosenson]] was based at Bakongan, also functioning as *controleur der Zuid-Atjehsche landschappen Kloeët en Troemon*.

In [[May]] [[1927]] [[Tjoet Ali]] attacked the *marschbivak* [[Mengamat attack (1927)|Mengamat]] at half past three in the morning — new moon, pitch dark, soft rain — breaking through the *pagger* with six or seven men; all were killed, leaving eight Dutch wounded. Tjoet Ali himself was killed on [[25 May]] [[1927]] by Gosenson's brigade near Aloeë, and buried at the *marschbivak* [[Kandang]] opposite, with his face toward Mekka, *want [[Gosenson]] wist, wat een eerlijk en dapper tegenstander toekomt*.

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

## Account from *Een strijd in Atjeh* ([[1934]])

Bakongan appears in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* as the destination of the ophthalmologist [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] on the journey he began on [[23 August]] [[1933]], when he came again to [[Koeta Radja]] and travelled thence to the West Coast.

Tijssen introduced the place with an explicit reference to its recent history: "reisde van daar door naar Bakongan, land, waarvan men zich den naam herinnert door het verzet van [[1925]] en '26" — the land whose name is remembered for the resistance of [[1925]] and [[1926]]; see [[Bakongan uprising]]. The remark is the pamphlet's acknowledgement that the district to which the doctor was sent was one of the most recently disturbed in the whole residency.

Of the administration's practical support he recorded: "in Bakongan heeft het bestuur de reiskosten voor mijn patiënten betaald wanneer de afstand te groot was om dien te voet af te leggen." — at Bakongan the administration paid the travelling costs for my patients when the distance was too great to cover on foot. The population there had no money; but the land was rich in produce, and from that alone Tijssen could explain the strikingly rare occurrence of avitaminosis in the district — the *voedselverhoudingen*, he said, were excellent.

Bakongan lay in the thinly peopled coastal strip five hundred kilometres long and scarcely five kilometres broad along which the motorable road ran, flanked on both sides by the mass graves of the Atjeh wars and the rising of [[1926]] — the district described at [[Tijssen's journey to the West Coast of Atjeh and Simaloer (1933)]], and the one in which, Tijssen noted, no officer went into a *[[Kampong|kampong]]* without a soldier behind him with a loaded carbine, and yet where he himself lived unarmed among the people for weeks without anyone harming a hair of his head.

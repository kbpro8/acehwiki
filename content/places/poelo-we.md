**Poelò We** (Pulau Weh; in [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s text "P. We", "Poelò We" or "eiland We") was the island north of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] closing the [[Sabang]] bay "as with wide-opened arms" — first surveyed by the government commission of [[1877]], depot for coal from [[1884]], home of the [[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]] from [[1896]]–[[1897]], and since the 1890s also cultivated with [[Pepper cultivation and trade in Atjeh|pepper]] gardens.

## Government interest and settlement

In the war years after [[1873]] the Netherlands Indian government felt the necessity of a good harbour near Sumatra's North Coast as operation-base, coal station and evacuation-place; a government commission sent out in [[1877]] reported the island then unsuitable as an evacuation place. None the less the Government in [[1884]] established a small coal depot on the Balohan (South) bay and appointed an *oelèèbalang* (native headman) as head of the island. Only after the hydrographic and topographic survey of [[1887]] was the northern bay — the Sabang bay — developed (see [[Sabang]], [[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang]]). The "first harbour-place of Netherlands India" impressed every visitor by the island's thick-wooded flanks rising steeply above the sea (photo captions: "Sabangbaai met de rubber- en theekistenfabriek 'Ret-Ret-Sabang'"; "Sabanghaven"; "Papier-a of Poelo Weh" [sic; a kampong seen from the sea near the [[Atjeh Museum]] plate]).

## Pepper and bird's-nests

The island proved increasingly suited to the cultivation of pepper: under Governors [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] and successors the plantations regularly expanded, the pepper harvest on Poeloe We turning out "zeer goed" ("very good") in the early 1890s alongside that of the East Coast. Together with several moekims of Groot-Atjeh, the island also practised the collecting of edible bird's-nests, which were bought up by Chinese traders. A coal establishment ([[Poelò We#coal|koolen-établissement]]) on the island came to completion in the mid-1890s, and at [[Lë Meulè]] a wireless-telegraphy station was erected in [[1912]] for the service of the shipping.

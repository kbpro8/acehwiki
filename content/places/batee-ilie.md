**Batêê Iliê** was a fortified village in the **[[Samalanga]]** region of **[[Aceh]]**, the site of one of the major battles of the **[[Aceh War]]** in [[1901]]. The position had successfully resisted Dutch attacks for twenty years, including an attempt by General Van der Heyden, before being captured by the forces of **[[J. B. van Heutsz]]**.

## Strategic Importance

Batêê Iliê was located in the foothills of the Samalanga highlands, in a region that served as a stronghold for Acehnese resistance. The village was heavily fortified with walls, stockades, and bamboo *doerihagen* (spiked barriers). Its location made it a critical point for controlling access to the interior.

## The First Assault

General Van der Heyden had attempted to capture Batêê Iliê twenty years earlier, but the position held out against his forces. This failure contributed to the reputation of Batêê Iliê as an impregnable fortress and boosted Acehnese confidence.

## The Battle of 1901

When Van Heutsz took command in Aceh, he made the capture of Batêê Iliê a priority. He personally led the assault, following the mobile *marechaussee* tactics that had proven effective against guerrilla forces.

The battle was hard-fought, with the Acehnese defenders using their fortified positions to maximum advantage. After a prolonged struggle, the Dutch forces broke through the defenses. Batêê Iliê fell, and the Acehnese defenders were forced to retreat.

## Aftermath

The capture of Batêê Iliê was a major Dutch victory. It opened the interior of Samalanga to Dutch control and forced the principal Acehnese leaders, including **[[Panglima Polem]]**, to retreat further into the interior. The victory demonstrated Van Heutsz's tactical skill and the effectiveness of the *marechaussee* system.

## Significance

Batêê Iliê represented the end of an era in Acehnese resistance. The fall of a fortified position that had held out for two decades shattered the myth of Dutch vulnerability and paved the way for the final pacification of Aceh.

**Dutch East Indies** was the Dutch colonial possession in Southeast Asia that encompassed the islands of [[Sumatra]], [[Java]], [[Borneo]], the [[Moluccas]], and the northernmost region of [[Aceh]] from the seventeenth century until the Japanese occupation in [[1942]]. The colony served as the administrative and economic base for the Netherlands in the East, and its military forces—including the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]]—were deployed across its vast archipelago during the Second World War, as documented in the corps history of 1665‑1945.

## Colonial Administration and Geography
The Dutch East Indies was organized into several governorates; the northernmost governorate was the [[Aceh]] province, which retained a distinct legal status because of the historic [[Aceh War]] (1873‑1904). The colony’s capital was [[Batavia]] (now [[Jakarta]]), while major naval bases were located at [[Surabaya]] (historically spelled *Soerabaia*) and the island of [[Madura]] (spelled *Madoera*). The strategic importance of these ports is evident in the 1940 seizure of three German ships in the harbour of *Soerabaia* (see [[1940]]).

## Military Presence in the 1940s
During the early phase of the Second World War, the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]] maintained several detachments throughout the Dutch East Indies. A notable deployment was the **Marine Battalion (M.B.A.)** stationed at the Marine barracks in *Soerabaia*, which guarded the naval establishment, local harbours, and the Marine airfield “Mororrembangan”. After the Japanese offensive of [[1942]], these forces were reorganised into mobile reserve companies to counter possible parachute landings and to protect key installations such as the pyrotechnic works on *Madoera*.

## Connection to Aceh
Although the source material focuses on actions in Java and surrounding islands, the Dutch East Indies as a whole—including the Aceh province—was subject to the same wartime pressures. The Japanese advance that swept through Java in early [[1942]] also threatened the Aceh coast, leading to the eventual occupation of the entire colony. Aceh’s strategic position at the entrance to the Strait of Malacca made it a potential target for both Allied and Japanese naval operations, underscoring its importance within the broader colonial framework.

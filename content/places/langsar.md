**Langsar** (also written Langsa) was a coast-state on the east coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], one of the two states — together with [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]] — that had remained hostile to the Dutch by the end of [[1874]]. The state was the object of an expedition in [[May 1877|May 1877]] after its population had been troubling the friendly [[Tamiang|Tamiang]], in which the Dutch flag was hoisted at Langsar on [[18 May]] [[18 May]] [[1877]]. Together with [[Modjopahit|Modjopahit]] and the whole [[Temijang|Temijang-gebied]], Langsar came under Dutch sovereignty in [[1877]]-[[1878]].

## Refusal of Submission

By the end of [[1874]] the Dutch flag had not yet been accepted by [[Telok Kroet|Telok Kroet]], [[Rigas|Rigas]] and [[Tenom|Tenom]] on the west coast, by [[Pedir|Pedir]], [[Merdoe|Merdoe]] and [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] on the north coast, and by [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]], [[Arakoendoer|Arakoendoer]] and Langsar on the east coast. Langsar was thus one of the hold-outs among the coast-states of Atjeh.

## The Expedition of May 1877

In [[May 1877|May 1877]] the whole East Coast was brought under Dutch authority by an expedition to Langsar, whose population had been troubling the friendly Tamiang. The Dutch troops had thereby a very tiring march to accomplish before they had reached the chief place from the [[Birimrivier|Birimrivier]], where they had disembarked. After a failed march on [[10 May]] [[10 May]], the troops set out again on [[11 May]] [[11 May]] and arrived at Langsar only on [[14 May]] [[14 May]]:

> "The great heat to which the troops had been exposed that day had caused many to fall unconscious: an adjutant-underofficier, acting officer, and 3 soldiers had thereby succumbed, and the troop was, according to an eye-witness, about midday so lethargic that 50 enterprising Atjehers would certainly have been able to make a terrible slaughter. Fatigue, hunger and thirst stood on every face, and only when the bivouac was pitched did the officers and rank-and-file gradually come to themselves."

On [[18 May]] [[18 May]] [[1877]] the Dutch flag was hoisted at Langsar.

## The Submission of Modjopahit and the Temijang-gebied

Repeated disturbances of the peace and incursions into the Dutch enterprises in [[Langkat|Langkat]] led to an expedition in [[1877]], "of which the consequence was that also the Atjehsche statejes Langsar and Modjopahit, and the whole Temijang-gebied came under our sovereignty."

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**Langsar** — the bay and estate-centre on the East Coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the landscape [[Langsa]] — grew after [[1900]] from an alternative anchorage of the Simpang Aneu river into "the centre of culture-estates", where twenty rubber-, oil-palm- and coconut-enterprises lay in [[1922]], the goal of the [[Atjeh Tram|tramway's]] eastward extension and the seat of a [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij|NHM]] sub-agency, a credit bank and a government caoutchouc enterprise.

### The bay and the piers

Already under Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] merchants in Atjeh asked for an investigation of the Langsar bay to judge whether moving the ships' landing-place on the East Coast from the Simpang Aneu river to Langsar was possible and desirable, Langsar then already rising as a trading place of significance. Van Heutsz caused soundings to be made in the Langsar harbour, which gave a favourable result, and subsequently submitted proposals to the Government for the grant of funds for the building of piers at Langsar and for carrying the Atjeh tram through to the place. The first European industrial establishment of the region had been an undertaking for timber cutting at Langsar (before [[1893]]) which delivered wood to the tobacco enterprises in [[Deli|the Deli country]].

### The estate district

At [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s instance the wherewithal was furnished for the foundation of a caoutchouc enterprise at Langsar, as an example and encouragement in view of results obtained elsewhere with Pará rubber; the government's caoutchouc enterprise at [[Langsa]] steadily expanded through the years. Private agriculture followed: rubber plantations of European companies grew greatly both in Langsar and in [[Tamiang|Temyang]]; a Hollandsch-Indlandsche school opened at Langsar in [[1916]], and a public European lower school; a credit bank for the East Coast arose at Langsar ([[1913]]); artesian wells were bored. In July [[1910]] funds were granted for the tramway Langsa–[[Koeala Simpang]], opened in [[1912]], by which Langsar came on the through rail route (see [[Atjeh Tram]]); the [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij]] opened a sub-agency at Langsar; the [[Atjehsche Handelmaatschappij]] and [[Handelsmaatschappij Deli-Atjeh]] kept establishments, and agencies of Medan houses were opened at Langsar as at [[Sabang]], [[Sigli]] and [[Koeala Simpang]].

### Van Zalinge's report, 1922

"De handel gaat zich steeds meer verplaatsen naar Langsar, het centrum van cultuurondernemingen, die zich zoowel naar het Noorden als naar het Westen en het Zuiden uitstrekken" ("Trade moves ever more to Langsar, the centre of culture-estates, which extend northward, westward and southward"), wrote Director [[N. van Zalinge]] of the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij|K.P.M.]] after his tour of late [[1922]]: twenty estates then operated — twelve producing rubber only, seven planting oil-palms and rubber, and one cultivating cocos-palms; when the partly-reclaimed estates should all come to full production, Langsar would shortly become a shipping place of significance. He noted under-staffed gardens with grass shooting between the hevea, and the mimosa experiment which elsewhere choked the weeds without harming the trees. Governor [[Van Sluys|Van Sluys]]'s 1923 itinerary likewise named Langsar as a stage of the through connection [[Koeta Radja]]–[[Medan]].

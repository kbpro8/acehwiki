**Tiban** was the Acehnese defensive position south of [[Kota Moesapi]] on the route from the Dutch landing area toward [[Penajoeng]] during the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)]]. On [[14 December]] [[1873]], Dutch forces under [[General Verspijck|Verspijck]] stormed the line at [[Battle of Tiban (14 December 1873)]], making Tiban one of the first important inland battle sites of the renewed invasion of [[Aceh|Atjeh]].

## Place in the campaign

After landing at [[Kwala Loë]] on [[9 December]] [[1873]], the Dutch moved inland in stages. [[Kota Moesapi]] was occupied on [[13 December]] [[1873]], and the next day the army struck the next strong Acehnese line at Tiban. Contemporary Dutch accounts treated the place as one of the early gateways to the inner defenses that protected the road toward [[Penajoeng]], the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Great Missigit]], and the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]].

## Historical role

Tiban became important not only because fighting took place there, but because later critics of the campaign used the victory at Tiban as the first example of a success that was not exploited quickly enough. [[Brutus]] argued that the Dutch should have pressed forward harder after the battle, before the Acehnese could strengthen their next line.

## Modern identification

The exact modern administrative identification of Tiban is not clear from the cited sources. In the printed record, it appears mainly as a battlefield name in the inner war zone between the landing area and the old capital of [[Aceh|Atjeh]].

## Significance

**Tiban** is significant because it marks the first major inland Dutch victory of the second expedition. As both a place and a battle site, it belongs to the chain of locations that structured the Dutch advance toward the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] in [[December]] [[1873]] and [[January]] [[1874]].

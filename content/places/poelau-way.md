**Poelau Way** — modern **Pulau Weh**, the island off the northern tip of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] at the entrance to the roadstead of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — was the anchorage to which [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]] withdrew his two ships in January [[1601]] after the armed clash with the Atjeh shore batteries, in order to wait a few days for an answer from the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]] before setting course for [[Bantam|Bantam]]. Poelau Way is the principal named island anchorage in Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]] and a strategic location in the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship.

## The Van Caerden withdrawal (January 1601)

After [[Paulus van Caerden|Van Caerden]]'s seizure of pepper from the Arab junks at anchor on the Atjeh roadstead on [[12 January]] [[1601]], the shore batteries fired on the Dutch ships, and Van Caerden answered by burning several coastal craft. He then released the sixty prisoners he had taken from the Arab junks — "hij wou hunner landgenooten geen vijanden maken" — and "liep met zijn schepen naar Poeloe Way", where he waited a few days for an answer from the sultan. The sultan did not answer; Van Caerden set course for [[Bantam|Bantam]] by way of the west coast of Sumatra, taking on no cargo.

The withdrawal to Poelau Way was the principal Dutch tactical use of the island anchorage in the early Dutch-Atjeh period. The island's location at the entrance to the roadstead of Atjeh made it a natural place of withdrawal for ships seeking to avoid the shore batteries while remaining within signalling distance of the sultan's court. The island would continue to serve this function throughout the seventeenth century and would become, in the nineteenth century, the principal Dutch naval station in the Atjeh region.

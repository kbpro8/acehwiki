**Kepaja Doewa** — written by Cool and Hooijer also as Kepaya Doewa, Kepeya Doewa and Kepaija Doewa — was an Achehnese mountain kampong high in the range between the valley of [[Lamtoba]] and the valley of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]], beyond the border-river of the Lamtoba district, burnt by the Dutch in the [[Punitive expedition against Kepaja Doewa (August 1879)|punitive expedition of early August 1879]] and passed again, amid renewed fighting, on the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|march to Selimoen]] of [[13 August]] [[1879]]. The kampong is described in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## Geography and the punitive expedition

The kampong lay high in the mountains, about six *palen* from the Dutch bivak in the Lamtoba valley, beyond three steep-walled ravines and after a march without water; it consisted, when the troops reached it at one o'clock, of a fairly large main kampong with two smaller ones lying before it, together about thirty houses. Since the chiefs [[Toekoe Moeda Baij]], [[Toekoe Nja Hoessin]] and [[Toekoe Paya]] of the [[XXVI Moekims]], with their following, had withdrawn hither from the Lamtoba valley, and a *wakil* sent to summon them to submission had returned empty-handed, the column-commandant resolved to march against the kampong. It was burnt, with the fruit trees partly cut down, in punishment both of the resistance offered — the enemy having opened a fairly regular fire from the hills behind, from which he was driven by eight shrapnel shells — and of the hospitality shown to the chiefs of the XXVI Moekims. "Groote schrik was door de tuchtiging van Kepaja Doewa verspreid" — great terror was spread by the punishment: among the fugitives was the [[Panglima Banten|Rijksbestierder]] of Lamtoba himself, who returned on [[6 August]].

## The ridge position of 13 August 1879

It was at and beyond the destroyed kampong that the Achehnese, on [[13 August]] [[1879]], awaited the march of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Gerlach column]] to [[Selimoen]]. At the height of the demolished kampong, shots falling thicker as the advance guard pushed on, the enemy was found to have taken a very strong position astride the route: an earthen trench more than a hundred paces long, fronted by a heavy abatis and flanked by a *tamboer* — a palisaded blockhouse near the footpath — lying half-way up a rising hollow road. The position was carried by storm by the 2nd Company of the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie]] under Captain [[Clerkson]], with the loss of three wounded; against the same ridge beneath which the artillery then stood the guns of Luitenant [[Van Heutz (artillerie)|Van Heutz]] had been in battery during the punitive expedition.

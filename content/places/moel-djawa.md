**Moel Djawa** — in [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]]'s transcription *Moel Djawa*, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s decoding *semoe poela-ge- getta-djawa* — was the kingdom on the east coast of [[Sumatra]] whose capital ***Qakola*** [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]] visited after his fifteen-day stay with [[Sultan Malik at-Tahir of Pasej|Sultan Malik at-Tahir]] at [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] in [[1345]]/[[1346]], and which De Roo de la Faille identifies with the ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]]. Its capital *Qakola* is the same name, De Roo de la Faille argues, as the *Kakoela* of Javanese storytellers and the *Qaciola* of Arabic — the camphor-name by which the Hindu-Javanese chronicles denoted the ancestral land.

## Ibn Battuta's account

After fifteen days with [[Sultan Malik at-Tahir of Pasej|Sultan Malik at-Tahir]] at [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]], [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]] proceeded by junk to *Moel Djawa*, whose capital was called ***Qakola***, "a land that lived in war with Samatra" (i.e. with [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]]). The city was surrounded by heavy walls of natural stone — i.e. not of baked brick (*bata*), and so not *Beta-ksch* but of *Djawa-ge*. There were many elephants as draught and transport animals; the products of the land included cloves and nutmeg, both available on the local market — evidence, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, of regular trade with the [[Moluccas|Moluccas]].

## Decoding: *Qakola* and *Kapoelaga*

The capital name ***Qakola*** [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies with Arabic ***Qaclola*** / Sanskrit ***Kakkola*** — the camphor-name of the *kapoelaga* (cardamom), the plant with the very fragrant knob, by which the Hindu-Javanese chronicles denoted the ancestral land of [[Taroema]]. The same name, De Roo de la Faille argues, underlies the *Kakoela* of Javanese storytellers (shortened to *kakoe*), the *Qaciola* of [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]]'s account of the *melegata* (cardamom), and the *Gauenispola* of [[Marco Polo|Marco Polo]]'s "very small island" (*Gawé-nis pola-ga-nis*, i.e. "make of the vanished Kapoelaga"). The kingdom *Moel Djawa* itself De Roo de la Faille decodes as *semoe poela-ge- getta-djawa* — "the united *poelaga*-getta of Djawa", i.e. the troonzetel (throne-seat) of the *Djawa-ge*.

## Identification with Taroema at the bay of Aroe

The same *Moel Djawa*, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] argues, is the [[Taroema]] of the Adiparwa's Churning of the Ocean of Milk — the *ksir-nawa* or *tasik soesoe* from which the gods drew the *amrta*. Its location is [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]], and its sacred islet is [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] — the [[Noesa-n-ta-ra|Noesa-n-ta-ra]] of the [[Pararaton]] under the protection of the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Tara]]. The "many elephants" of *Qakola* are the war-elephants of the [[Astina|elephant-land Astina]] — the same that the *Xarnauz* of [[Vasco da Gama]]'s *Roteiro* records ("the king has 400 elephants of war").

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Moel Djawa is the principal Arabic-language landmark of the pre-Atjehnese east-coast Hindu-Javanese complex — the *bedekte taal* form by which [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]]'s *Rihla* records the ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]]. Its identification, by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], with the [[Taroema|Taroema]] of the chronicle tradition and with the *Astina* of the Javanese legend of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]] also fixes it as the principal east-coast landmark of pre-Atjehnese Sumatra in Arabic record.

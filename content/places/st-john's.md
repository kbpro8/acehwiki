**St. John's** (St. John's Island) is an island about five miles from [[Singapore]], mentioned in P. H. van der Kemp's *Raffles' Atjeh-overeenkomst van 1819* in connection with the strategic reasoning of British officials immediately after the [[Founding of Singapore|founding of Singapore]] in [[1819]].

## A command of the Straits

The reference occurs in a letter from the harbour-master [[Carnegy]] at [[Penang]], dated Penang, 27 July [[1819]], which Van der Kemp reproduces as his Bijlage 4. Carnegy wrote with enthusiasm of the establishment of Singapore and added: "We have a flag on St. John's, and in war time, a few heavy guns on St. John's will give us complete command of the Straits of that place. Sir Stamford Raffles has also made a treaty with the old king of Acheen which excludes all other Europeans and Americans." Van der Kemp's footnote laconically identifies St. John's as "an island five miles from Singapore."

The passage is significant in two respects. First, it shows how soon after Raffles's occupation of Singapore the British began to think in terms of fortifying neighbouring islands so as to command the [[Straits of Malacca|straits]]. Second, the same letter shows the contemporary British misunderstanding of the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|Acheh treaty]] as containing an exclusion of all other Europeans — a reading which Van der Kemp immediately contradicts by pointing out that the treaty nowhere contained "absolute exclusion of other European nations from trade at Acheh," but merely forbade them to settle there. St. John's thus appears in Van der Kemp's narrative as a small but instructive datum of the [[Sphere of influence|sphere-of-influence]] thinking that accompanied the founding of Singapore.

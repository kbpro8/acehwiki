**Tenom** (with its river, the *Tenomrivier*) was a little state on the North Coast of Sumatra which [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] calls "a pure Atjehnese settlement", ruled by a Tenomsche *roovervorst* (piratical prince). It was one of the coastal areas whose chiefs, as van Swieten reported in February [[1874]], had submitted to the Dutch government (so van Swieten noted in *[[De Waarheid over onze vestiging in Atjeh]]*, pp. 388-392). The district then became the centre of the long chain of complications summed up as the *[[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Tenom affair]]*: on [[9 March]] [[1880]] the two French travellers [[Vallon (Tenom, 1880)|Vallon]] and [[Guillaume (Tenom, 1880)|Guillaume]] were murdered by the Tenom prince's people — before the Netherlands had ever made war upon him, as Verstege stresses — and in [[1884]] the [[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Nisero question]] showed the true state of Atjeh to the world and occasioned, in London negotiations of July [[1884]], the conditions under which England permitted the Dutch [[Blockade of the Atjeh coast (1884-1885)|blockade of the Atjeh coasts]]: that the harbours of the Tenom pirate should remain open and that the Atjeh harbours would again be opened to world trade as soon as the blockade's object had been obtained. The Tenom affair was also, in Verstege's reading of the facts, the pressing occasion which brought the Dutch government to the secret [[Concentration decision (16-17 June 1884)|concentration decision]] — the "retreat" that the proclamation of [[19 August]] [[1884]] had just called impossible. (Dutch troops at [[Malaboeh]] were provisionally retained for fear of attacks by the Radja of Tenom.)

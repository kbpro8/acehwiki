**Lamkoenjit** was a Dutch temporary military post established in the [[XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[1890s]] — a post that, like that at [[Biloel]], was besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]] and relieved on [[9 April]] [[1896]] by the column under [[Kolonel Stemfoort]]. The post is also notable as the site of one of the engagements that produced the *eerste offer van Toekoe Oemars afval* — the *first offering of Toekoe Oemar's defection* — when the body of Lieutenant [[J. W. Vuijk|Vuijk]] was carried through the gate of the *benting* on [[30 March]] [[1896]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Lamkoenjit lay in the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|sagi of the XXV Moekims]] on the road between [[Tjot Goeë]] and [[Biloel]], at the site of one of the largest markets in the sagi, and was besieged and isolated from [[29 March]] to [[8 April]] [[1896]] in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]]. The post was the scene of a heroic night defence on [[4 April]]–[[5 April]] [[1896]] in which the garrison under 1e luit. [[Creutz Lechleitner]] repulsed a night attack with canister shot at less than 50 metres' range fired by sergeant Vermeulen. Lamkoenjit was relieved on [[8 April]] [[1896]] by a detachment under [[Kapitein Baron Collot d'Escury]], and was evacuated and burned on [[12 April]] [[1896]]. The *imam* of Lamkoenjit later maintained the *benteng* Paja Krah on the south-east side of the Tjot Goeë hill.

## The establishment of the post

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the post at Lamkoenjit was established as one of the Dutch temporary posts in the [[XXV Moekims]] — together with [[Biloel]], [[Anagaloeng]] and [[Senelop]] — during the late *concentratie* period. The post held a *vaste bezetting* (fixed garrison) in the [[1890s]], even though the situation in the surrounding territory was *niet overal even schitterend* (not everywhere equally brilliant). Within the *geconcentreerde linie* the Dutch were *als in Abrahams schoot* (as in Abraham's bosom), but outside the line the population had *weinig overlast van de Moslemin* (little trouble from the Moslemin) — provided the *hoofden* were *gunstig gestemd* (favourably disposed).

## The siege of March-April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]], the post at Lamkoenjit was besieged by the forces of the defected warlord. The album's narrative of the siege records the desperate situation of the garrison — *Hoor de zware slagen der donderbussen, het onregelmatig geknal van de geweren der Atjehers, hun schelklinkend gegil, overstend door het hoera onzer soldaten* — *Hear the heavy blows of the blunderbusses, the irregular crackle of the Atjehnese rifles, their shrill-sounding yells, interrupted by the hurrah of our soldiers*. The album's narrative also records the anxiety of the besieged garrison at [[Anagaloeng]] — some four days' march from Lamkoenjit — as they listened to the firing of the engagement and tried to judge from the sound whether the relieving force was a strong one.

## The relief of 9 April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Lamkoenjit was relieved on [[9 April]] [[1896]] by a column of two battalions under the command of [[Kolonel Stemfoort]]. The relief was, however, costly: Captain [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]] and seven subordinates were killed, and Lieutenants [[Ten Seldam|ten Seldam]] and [[Hockstein]] were wounded together with 23 subordinates. After the relief, the post at Lamkoenjit — together with that at [[Biloel]] — was *geslecht* (dismantled), and the garrison was withdrawn to the *geconcentreerde linie*.

## The role of the post in the 1896 crisis

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Lamkoenjit — like that at Biloel — was one of the four Dutch temporary posts whose siege defined the spring [[1896]] crisis. The album's narrative of the relief expeditions of [[April]] [[1896]] records that the column that relieved Biloel and Lamkoenjit then turned eastward to relieve [[Anagaloeng]] — a movement that required the dismantling of Biloel and Lamkoenjit before the column could proceed. The post at Lamkoenjit is thus the western anchor of the chain of Dutch temporary posts that defined the spring [[1896]] crisis, and its relief was the first of the three relief operations of April.

## Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Lamkoenjit lay in the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|sagi of the XXV Moekims]] on the road between [[Tjot Goeë]] and [[Biloel]], at the site of one of the largest markets in the sagi. The post was favourably placed to influence the lower part of the [[IX Moekims]] (the mukims of Oleh Soesoe and Lamkoenjit itself) and the great *pasar* (market) of Lamkoenjit. The garrison in [[1895]] consisted of 2 officers, 52 infantry, 10 artillery, and 1 European hospital attendant. Under 1e luit. [[Creutz Lechleitner]] during the siege of [[29 March]]–[[8 April]] [[1896]] the garrison comprised 50 infantry, 10 artillery, and 1 medical orderly. The *imam* of Lamkoenjit maintained the *benteng* Paja Krah on the south-east side of the Tjot Goeë hill.

## Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

Lamkoenjit had been constructed as part of the [[buitenlinie]] to dominate the great *pasar* of the [[IX Moekims]] and to support the adjacent posts of [[Biloel]] and [[Tjot Goeë]]. The post was a temporary post, a designation indicating its less-permanent character than the full *buitenposten* of [[Biloel]] and [[Ana' Galong]]. The *pasar* of Lamkoenjit was the principal commercial centre of the lower [[IX Moekims]] and a meeting-point of Dutch and Achehnese trade. After [[1893]] the post was the scene of periodic incidents involving the *legioen* (auxiliary legion) of [[Teuku Umar]] and the *benden* (war-bands) of the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] country.

## Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

Lamkoenjit was one of the two principal Dutch posts besieged in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. From [[29 March]] to [[8 April]] [[1896]] the post was isolated and besieged; on [[1 April]] [[1896]] a lost letter from the post-commandant to the assistant-resident gave details of the siege. On the night of [[4 April]]–[[5 April]] [[1896]] the garrison under 1e luit. [[Creutz Lechleitner]] repulsed a night attack by canister shot at less than 50 metres' range fired by sergeant Vermeulen — a heroic defence that became one of the celebrated episodes of the siege. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] a detachment under [[Kapitein Baron Collot d'Escury]] (4th company 14e Bataljon, 2 officers + 75 bayonets) fought through to relieve the garrison. On [[12 April]] [[1896]] Lamkoenjit was evacuated and burned together with [[Biloel]] by order of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]]. The *imam* of Lamkoenjit thereafter maintained the *benteng* Paja Krah on the south-east side of the Tjot Goeë hill, which was a target of later Dutch operations. On [[31 March]] [[1896]] the post-commandant received the report of 1e luit. Neelmeijer brought by [[T. Nja Banta]] and a population patrol approximately 7:30 PM. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]] the *imam* of Lamkoenjit was among the Achehnese leaders associated with the *benteng* Paja Krah against the Dutch.

## Significance

Lamkoenjit's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as one of the four Dutch temporary posts besieged in the spring [[1896]] crisis, and as the western anchor of the chain of posts whose relief defined the April operations. Its relief on [[9 April]] [[1896]] cost the life of Captain [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]] — the third officer fatality of the spring crisis — and its dismantling after the relief illustrates the way in which the temporary posts were *geslecht* once their relief had been accomplished. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* adds that Lamkoenjit was the most-fought-over of the [[Dutch]] *tijdelijke posten* (temporary posts) in the [[buitenlinie]], and its heroic night defence of [[4 April]]–[[5 April]] [[1896]] under 1e luit. [[Creutz Lechleitner]] became one of the celebrated episodes of the defection crisis. The post's evacuation on [[12 April]] [[1896]] marked the formal end of the [[buitenlinie]] in the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]]. The continued association of the *imam* of Lamkoenjit with the *benteng* Paja Krah indicated that the post's political and military significance continued even after its physical evacuation.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Lam Koenjit** was a Dutch temporary intermediate post established on [[4 July]] [[1894]] on the road between [[Tjot Goeë]] and [[Biloel]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], erected against the attacks of [[T. Rajoet|Toekoe Rajoet]], *[[Imam|imam]]* of the [[III Moekims Daroe]], and nicknamed by its garrison *Lam Loempoer* — "mud village" — for the state of its ground.

## Foundation

The road connecting [[Tjot Goeë]] and [[Biloel]] had been very unsafe at first. Transports marching along it often suffered from the enemy and were obliged to push part of the covering troops forward to take post at Lam Koenjit and, with volleys into the *kampongs* (villages) where the enemy lay concealed, to make possible the advance of the remainder. For that reason a very temporary intermediate post was erected there on [[4 July]] [[1894]]. Together with [[Biloel]] at the foot of the steep southern mountains and [[Tjot Goeë]] to the north, it also served to separate the [[XXII Moekims]] more effectively from those of the [[IX Moekims]].

## Conditions

The garrison numbered two officers and fifty men, living under the down-draughts sweeping along the mountains and in the shade of the high palms shooting up between their dwellings. Mosquitoes, breeding luxuriantly in the surrounding pools, gave more trouble than the enemy. The ground was so muddy that the post was called *Lam Loempoer*, mud village, and even the water in the old well was unusable; water was therefore brought continually from [[Tjot Goeë]], until on the *ongeluksdag* — the day of ill luck — of [[29 March]] [[1896]] the patrols, which were always accompanied by a tame black sheep, came to the conviction that the post was surrounded and that they would henceforth have to make do with muddy water.

## Fortification and signboards

The post commandant, [[Luitenant Creutz Lechleitner]], described by Hooijer as *immer waakzaam* (ever vigilant), hoped to prevent the enemy from penetrating. Above the outwork enclosed by *[[IJzerdraadversperring|ijzerdraadversperring]]*, within which the kitchen stood, rose a watch-house from which the enemy's approach could be barred; it bore the name *[[Eikhoren watch-house|Eikhoren]]*. Other signboards — *In de Villa Sans Souci*, and *In den Soldatenhemel: alles vrij behalve muskieten* ("In the Soldiers' Heaven: everything free except mosquitoes") — proved, in Hooijer's judgment, that the soldiers had not yet found the situation hopeless and had kept their humour. Entering the gate of the outwork, over which the name of the post was written, one turned left to enter the *[[Benteng|benteng]]* proper, in whose bastions two 7½ cm. field-pieces and a *[[Cochoornmortier|cochoornmortier]]* were mounted.

## The night assault of 4–5 April 1896

In the night of [[4 April]] to [[5 April]] [[1896]] one of the bastions was stormed by the enemy; had the gun there not been loaded with case-shot, Hooijer wrote, the attack might have proved fatal.

## Daily routine

Before those days of tension, life at Lam Koenjit was quiet and extremely dull. Reveille sounded at half past five in the morning and coffee was issued; at ten came the meal of soup with rice and *sambal* (chilli relish), preceded by fatigues in and around the post; in the middle of the day, when the sun blazed hardest, everyone not on guard sought rest in the shade. At half past four in the afternoon the mess-kettles were filled again, and before both meals the *[[Oorlam|oorlam]]* — the ration of Schiedam gin so prized by the soldier — was swallowed at a single draught. In the cool evening hours there was singing, talking, card-playing (which the soldiers called *smoezen*), and for the native soldiers *kisnen* and dominoes, while as sweet sounds as possible were coaxed from the harmonica.

Towards midday the cart with *[[Vivres|vivres]]* arrived daily — often fired at — together with the letters, under a small covering party of Achehnese. That, Hooijer wrote, was really the most important hour of the day: the post commander emerged from the old *[[Messigit|Messigit]]* in which the officers lived, still in night-trousers and *kabaai* (loose jacket), and the soldiers ran together to hear news of friends and comrades from whom they were separated for the fourteen days of a *detacheering*, and news from [[Kota Radja]], for whose bustle and great canteen they longed.

## Abandonment

Lam Koenjit was lifted together with [[Biloel]] four days after the relief of [[Tjot Goeë]] in [[April]] [[1896]], and went up in flames as the Dutch withdrew.

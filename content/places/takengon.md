**Takengon** is the place in [[Gajoeland|Gayoland]] that lies *op eene schitterende vlakte tusschen het meer en de bergen, en er kon eene stad staan* — on a splendid plain between the lake (the Laoet Tawar) and the mountains, and there could be a city. *Men rijdt er thans even heen, en er zijn weinig plekken in Atjeh waar zóó constant wordt gekrakeeld, en zóó weinig harmonie bestaat* — one drives there now and then, and there are few places in Atjeh where the people quarrel so constantly and where so little harmony exists. The contrast Zentgraff drew was with [[Blang Kedjeren|de Blang]], the *lointaine Princesse*; Takengon, by comparison, is the place to which *een ieder er binnenstuift in eene auto en er bezit van neemt, zonder ook maar een bescheiden poging tot verovering* — every one rushes into it in an automobile and takes possession of it, without so much as a modest attempt at conquest.

## Six march days from Blang Kedjeren


*Blang Kedjeren is ook vanuit Takengon te bereiken, officieel in zes marschdagen, waarvan er nu, boorbij Isaq, weer één schijnt weg te vallen omdat het terpentijnbedrijf daar gestadig het voor auto's berijdbare deel van den weg laat verlengen* — Blang Kedjeren is also reachable from Takengon, officially in six march-days, of which, passing Isaq, one now seems to fall away because the turpentine business there steadily lengthens the part of the road passable for cars. The march-days of the route pass the marschbivaks along the *pad dat via Reket-Goip, Simpang tiga enz. naar Takengon loopt* and the other on the *pad naar Lökop*. *Voor het gebruik der marschbivaks in het Gajoeland* an unwritten law was once respected by all in the *rimboe*: every traveller could get the key of the *letterslot* (combination lock) of the access-door, and so enter; inside, in a cupboard, were drinks and tins, of which he could take what he needed, leaving a *bon* in its place, which later would be found and settled with him. *Men zou anders nergens in dit land terecht kunnen.*

## The attack on mevrouw Schmidt


The old maréchaussée-brigades of the Blang kept their traditions in Takengon too. When the *aanslag op mevrouw Schmidt* was made at Takengon, the *oude garde* of the Blang responded: a maréchaussée — *ongewapend* — seized the attacker, *legde hem naar de regelen der kunst* (threw him by the rules of the art) and held him under, his head free, *tot een toegeschoten maréchaussée met den klewang het geval afwerkte* — until a maréchaussée who had run up finished the affair with the klewang. The sergeant van Genderen — *één dier mannetjes-putters van de oude garde* — was the man.

## The road to Lökop


From Takengon the second road out of the Gajoeland runs to Lökop, on the east; the *marschbivaks* along that route too were once under the *ongeschreven wet* of the key in the *letterslot*. The Takengon plain is the *buitenpoort* of the Gajoeland for the eastern side; the *terpentijnbedrijf* of Isaq extends the motor-road year by year.

## Significance


Takengon is the *begane grond* of the Gajoeland — the place where one drives in by automobile, where there is *krakeel* but no harmony, and where the old maréchaussée-garde of the Blang was once called on, *ongewapend*, to deal with an assassin. From Takengon runs the six-day march to the Blang, of which the first stage — by Isaq — is now *voor auto's berijdbare deel*. From Takengon too the road to Lökop opens the eastern side of the Gajoeland.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Takengon** is the chief place (*hoofdplaats*) of the *onderafdeeling* Takengon and of the *afdeeling* [[Gajo- en Alaslanden|Gajo- en Alaslanden]] as reconstituted in [[1922]], situated directly on the [[Laoet Tawar|Laut Tawar]] lake at the foot of the mountains, open only on the north-west side. Broersma describes it as *'het alleraardigste plaatsje'* — *the most charming little place* — with no more than a small market, a market-street where several Chinese shops are established, the bivouac, the *pasanggrahan*, a post-office and a small clinic with beds for some ten sick.

### The Lake and the Roads


The lake, more than 1.200 metres above sea-level, is 16 km long; its greatest breadth is 5 km. A road runs along the lake, hewn out of the mountain-wall, and here and there lie the small villages, whose population fishes and has its *sawahs* by the rivers that bring their water into Laut Tawar. On the west side it discharges into the Peusangan river. One can be rowed on the lake; a trip with the government's motor-boat is not among the unattainable pleasures; but a luxury accessible at every moment is the view of the water-surface in its quiet grandeur with changing tints, to be enjoyed from the road that borders it on the height or from the crest of the nearby mountains.

Around Takengon one can undertake walking and even auto trips, for example to the [[Pegaseén-vallei|Pegasén-vallei]]; the road is passable at least to the 13th kilometre; there is a road to [[Kebajakan|Kebajakan]] and [[Sarel|Sarel]], 6 km. Otherwise there are paths also up the mountain-side, one of which, close by Takengon, is wholly grass-grown. Walks around Takengon are delightful. The landscape is varied and invigorating; one sees people and human labour, the joy of field and garden, living water.

### The Bivouac


In the bivouac of Takengon lie nine brigades of twenty men under a captain. At [[Lökop|Lökop]] lies a company of infantry (eight brigades of twenty men) under a captain; at [[Blang Kedjerën|Blang Kedjerën]] a captain with twelve brigades, to be reduced to nine, of which three brigades are at [[Kota Tjane|Kota Tjane]]. The *Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden* was formed in [[1905]], was re-allotted in [[1908]] among other administrative areas, but was restored in [[1922]] with chief place Takengon, comprising the *onderafdeelingen* Takengon, [[Gajoe Loeös|Gajoe Loeös]], [[Serbödjadi|Serbödjadi]] and [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]].

### The Blang Kolaq


West of the place and in its immediate neighbourhood stretches a great *blang* — a grass-plain as even as one could not imagine in free nature. But it is there, and seldom did one see so excellent a terrain for exercise and play, a pleasure-garden for man and child, dangerous by no ravine or water, an open lying wide glory played upon by sun and cooling breeze, a place that offers feasts every day for the renewal of life — it is the [[Blang Kolaq|Blang Kolaq]]. Truly, Atjeh is blessed with this inland health-resort, and there is every reason to increase the lodging-accommodation there.

### The Gajo Market


In the early hour of the morning the Gajos come from all sides, men and women, the women with the suckling on the back, in hurried step to the market of Takengon. The clothing, especially that of the women, is not much. They wear a *kain* and above it a peculiar short Gajo jacket, which really only covers shoulders and chest. They need the thin dark blanket, which they hold over shoulders and body, as the men do. The whole market-folk appears at Takengon wrapped in a blanket against the cold, and only later in the morning do they take it off. They bring their vegetables, beans, tobacco, a chicken, biscuits, poor merchandise generally with which they sit at the market, while in the sheds themselves there is room for traders in more substantial wares, in cottons, worked pieces, manufactures. They are Minangkabauers, Malays, Indians who sell there and also make clothes. In the market-street, the *keude*, the Chinese have their principal *toko's* and eating-places. Good buyers are the soldiers from the bivouac, and above all their housekeepers, the flower of the shopping-women at the market.

### Significance


Takengon is the principal *herstellingsoord* — convalescent station — of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, and the political centre of the reconstituted *Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden*. Its situation on the Laut Tawar, its *Blang Kolaq*, and its mountain-setting make it the most attractive place in Atjeh, in Broersma's view, and the natural centre of any future development of the Gajo interior.

**Tjalang** was the hoofdplaats of the onderafdeeling Tjalang of the [[Afdeeling Westkust van Atjeh|Afdeeling Westkust van Atjeh]] — a *bestuursstandplaats* on the northern Westkust selected, in [[1908]], in preference to the more naturally favoured Poeloe Radja, and the seat of the *keudjeuroeënschap* of Kroeëng Sabé, one of the two great *keudjeuroeënschappen* that had arisen from the disintegration of the old sultanaat of Daja. *De Buitenbezittingen* records the choice as a political rather than a geographical one, and notes the establishment, in recent years, of new wegennet that has begun to concentrate in Tjalang.

## The Kroeëng Sabé keudjeuroeënschap

The groote *keudjeuroeënschappen* Lhoʼng and Kroeëng Sabé — both "ontstaan uit het uiteengevallen sultanaat Daja" — could not maintain their unity and were "al heel spoedig in tal van kleine landschappen gesplitst." A great part of the formerly important Lhoʼng was later added to the onderafdeeling Lhoʼ Nga (the so-called Zuidelijke nederzettingen), while the remainder was joined with the landschappen of Kroeëng Sabé to form the onderafdeeling Tjalang. Many settlements in these landschappen arose as colonies of peperplanters, whose *tuinhoofden* (*peutoeha seuneubèʼ*) gradually acquired territorial power, often over several complexes. Alongside these, *De Buitenbezittingen* notes, one finds "de oorspronkelijke gampongs, met Keuthjïʼs aan het hoofd."

## Bestuursindeeling

The onderafdeeling Tjalang comprised the ten *landschappen* of Kloeang, Koeala Daja, Lambeusyë, Koeala Oengaj, Lhoʼ Kroeët (with the underhoorigheden Babah Awé and Djeunamprag, which until [[1914]] had been two zelfstandige landschapjes), Patéʼ, Lageuën, Rigaïh, Kroeëng Sabé, and Teunom. The hoofdplaats itself was withdrawn from the *landschapsbestuur* and placed under *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* by G. B. 28 Maart 1913 No. 37. The registered adult Atjehsche population of the onderafdeeling was 5,006 at the end of [[1915]].

## The standplaats question

The selection of Tjalang as bestuursstandplaats, in [[1908]], involved abandoning the former standplaats on the islet of [[Poeloe Radja|Poeloe Radja]] — a small *rotsmassief* lying close off the coast opposite Lhoʼ Kroeet. *De Buitenbezittingen* notes that Poeloe Radja "onderscheidde zich van alle andere plaatsen ter Westkust door zijne door het eiland beveiligde reede in de straat tusschen P. Raja en den vasten wal" — its roadstead, sheltered between the islet and the mainland, was unique on the Westkust. It lay opposite Daja, "het economisch meest belangrijke deel" of this northern part of the Westkust. "Politieke overwegingen gaven echter den doorslag bij de keuze van het veel minder gunstig gelegen Tjalang" — political considerations tipped the choice toward the much less favourably situated Tjalang.

## Wegennet and trade

The *B. O. W.* (*Bouwkundige Officier van den Weg*, the engineering officer of the way) was, at the date of writing, seeking a trace for a great weg over the dwarsruggen between Lhong and the onderafdeeling Tjalang across the steep Glé Groetèë; once completed, this would open "het belangrijke Daja." From Meulabôh a rijweg already ran south to meet the Tjalangsche weg; by end [[1916]] the section Lam Ië–Tripa–Koeala Tadoe would be ready, completing a primitive link between Tapaʼ Toean and Singkel. Tjalang's haven received 52 steamers (179,000 M³) in [[1913]], dropping to 25 (59,000) in [[1914]] and 27 (64,000) in [[1915]] — the steepest decline of any Westkust haven.

## Account from *Atjèh and the War with Japan* (1949) — Tjalang

**Tjalang** is the subdistrict headquarters-town and subdistrict on the West Coast of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]], the wild mountain landscape on the Indian Ocean "which adjoins the subdivision [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] to the North (sagi of the XXV Moekims) and borders the subdivision [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]] to the South," and the site of one of the three pre-invasion revolts that Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* analyses in Chapter III § 3 — a revolt that "discharged itself as suddenly as it was violent" in the night of [[9 March|9]]–[[10 March]] [[1942]], led by the old Teukoe Sabi of [[Lageuën|Lageuën]], "the bravest man of the West Coast."

## The geography of the subdistrict

The subdistrict "offers little space for human settlements. The wild mountain landscape of the [[Barisan|Barisan]] reaches here, as in the adjacent area of the sagi of the XXV Moekims (*oelèëbalangship* Lhóng), right down to the coast." The subdistrict contained "only two plains of significance: the plain of Kloeang-Lam Beusióë in the North and the plain of Teunóm in the South." The main population centres were Lam Nó, 80 km. south of [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]], as the centre of the so-called Daja-landschappen; Teunóm, 120 km. further along the West Coast road; and, "between these two centers, … in the Kroeëng Sabé landscape, where the Tjalang bay offers a favorable anchorage for seagoing vessels, as the third center the subdivision headquarters Tjalang." The population "was therefore at the most recent census only a little more than 21,000."

## Political and religious life

"Until recently there was no continuous road connection along Aceh's West Coast, and KPM vessels and regional craft served as the principal means of communication with the outside world, as well as between the administrative posts. Only the extension of the West Coast Road in the twenties, during which military-strategic considerations ([[Bakóngan uprising|Bakóngan uprising]] [[1925]]–[[1927]]) were decisive, freed the subdistrict from its isolation." The old religious traditions "remained here closer to the surface slumbering. The distance to the time in which the [[Atjèh War|Aceh War]] called the devout Muslims to the fight against the '*kaphé*' (unbeliever), seemed smaller in this region, where once Teukoe Oemar, after his betrayal in [[March 1896]], sought refuge." Of the ten *oelèëbalang* families, "Teukoe Hoesin, the acting self-governing authority of Teunóm, was considered the most prominent *oelèëbalang* in this district. In [[1941]] he was therefore designated as representative of Tjalang at the 'administrative conference' held in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]]." Teukoe Sabi of [[Lageuën|Lageuën]], confirmed in [[1906]] and "the 'bravest man of the West Coast'," was the only Tjalang *oelèëbalang* openly to support the [[Poesa|Poesa]] movement and the restoration of the Acehnese sultanate.

## The revolt of March 1942

"The last military action of significance mentioned in the 'Introduction' took place in [[1933]] in the neighboring Lheng," so that "continuous vigilance and military preparedness remained required in this region. Garrisons at Tjalang and Lam Nó and a detachment at Lam Soedjèn in Lhóng … made intensive patrolling possible." After the outbreak of the war with Germany, the garrisons at Lam Nó and Lam Soedjèn were abolished, while that at Tjalang was reduced to "1 brigade of 19 men." When the uprising broke out, "the entire military force consisted of two brigades, one in Tjalang and one in Lam Né, both under the command of a sergeant." The revolt was "carried out in consultation with the Lageuën resistance center" under Teukoe Sabi, whose younger brother Teukoe Poelèh acted as "*kepala berontak*" (head of the uprising). At the [[8 December|8 December]] [[1943]] ceremony of the *soerat poedjian* (certificates), "Teukoe Sabi, as representative of the 155 fallen and wounded in Tjalang" appeared as one of three representatives of the "martyrs of the resistance movement." After the revolt, Tjalang was evacuated on the morning of [[14 March]] [[1942]] by the acting troops commander of [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]], captain L. H. J. Kloprogge; "the keudè Lageuën, which had been spared by captain Kloprogge, had been razed to the ground." As first *gun-chó* to act under the Japanese, Teukoe Ali, *oelèëbalang* of Keureukón, a brother-in-law of the sagi head Teukoe Nja' Arif, was installed.

**The VII Moekims** (Dutch: *Zeven Moekims*; Acehnese and Indonesian: *Tujuh Mukim*; English: "the Seven Mukims") is a territorial division in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], consisting of seven [[Mukim|mukims]] (subdistricts or parishes) grouped under a single [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]]. In the late nineteenth century, this territory was governed by [[Teukoe Moeda Baid Setia Maharadja]], who is recorded in the 1928 treasure-room guide of the [[Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen|Batavian Society]] as the *Oeloebalang van de VII moekims*.

## Governance and colonial relations

The VII Moekims appears to have been one of the larger or more strategically important groupings of mukims in the Acehnese interior, as its uleebalang was deemed significant enough to receive a formal honour from the Dutch colonial government — a gold chain bearing the Dutch coat of arms, granted in [[1888]]. The subsequent revocation of this honour in [[1896]] "wegens ontrouw" (on account of disloyalty) indicates that the uleebalang of the VII Moekims eventually turned against the Dutch, or at least was perceived to have done so, during the protracted [[Aceh War]].

The grouping of seven mukims under a single lord is consistent with the hierarchical structure of Acehnese territorial administration described by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], in which individual [[Mukim|mukims]] (each comprising several [[Gampong|gampongs]] or villages) were in turn grouped under the authority of an [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]], and the uleebalangs themselves fell under one of the three *panglima sagi* of the [[Sagi System|sagi system]].

## Location

The precise location of the VII Moekims is not specified in the treasure-room guide. Various groupings of "seven mukims" existed in different parts of Atjeh; the term may refer to a cluster in the western, northern, or interior regions of the country. Further research in the colonial administrative records would be needed to identify this particular VII Moekims with certainty.

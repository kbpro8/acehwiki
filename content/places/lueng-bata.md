A mukim (district) of [[Acheh]] Proper, famous in the war against the Dutch. The brave [[Imeum of Lueng Bata]] and his followers were among the foremost resisters. During the first Dutch expedition, the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata]] and [[Teuku Lam Nga]] fought with heroic valour. After the conquest of the XXVI Mukims by the Dutch and their advance through the XXII Mukims, the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata]] finally thrust his sword into its sheath and withdrew from public life. Teuku Usen of Lueng Bata (brother of the imeum) was among the principal panglimas who took up arms under [[Teuku Asan]]. The Mukim Lueng Bata was among those overcome by the Dutch after a period of comparative inactivity. The tomb of Teungku Meuntroe — the saint who specialises in punishing perjurers — is situated in the open country at Lueng Bata.

**Lueng Bata** was one of the important [[Gampong|gampongs]] in the vicinity of [[Banda Acheh]], listed by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] among the most important gampongs of the capital area alongside [[Gampong Jawa]], Pande, Pennayong, [[Lam Bhu']], [[Lam Seupeuëng]], Ateuëng, Batoh, and [[Meura'sa]].

As a *banda* (town-bred) gampong, Lueng Bata's inhabitants were distinguished from the *dusön* (countryfolk) of more remote districts by their language, customs, and familiarity with the manners of the Acehnese capital.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Lu'eng Bata

**Lu'eng Bata** (also written **Lueng Bata**) is one of the principal *gampongs* of [[Banda Acheh]] and one of the most historically significant localities in the ethnographic record of *The Achehnese*. It appears at once as a village of the capital's inner ring, as a former *wakeueh* district of the [[XXV Mukims]], and as the seat of an influential and powerful imeum whose role in the [[Achehnese War|war with the Dutch]] was of considerable military importance.

### A Gampong of the Capital

The [[Dalam]] — the residence of the Sultan, which the Dutch incorrectly termed the *Kraton* and which was also known as [[Kuta Raja]] — formed before the [[Achehnese War|Dutch war]] the nucleus of a number of fine and prosperous gampongs. Their centre, with its mosque and market-place, was called [[Banda Acheh]], and gave the tone to the whole country in matters of custom, dress, and so forth. The most important of these gampongs, Snouck Hurgronje enumerates, were [[Gampong Jawa]], Pande, Peunayong, [[Lam Bhu']], Lam Seupeu'eng, Ateung, [[Batoh]], Meura'sa — and Lu'eng Bata. The inhabitants of these and the neighbouring villages, with their language and customs, were distinguished by the epithet *banda*, "town-bred" or "civilised."

### A Former Wakeueh District

Together with Pagaraye (from Malay *pagar ayer*) and [[Lam Sayun]], Lu'eng Bata was regarded as properly belonging to the [[XXV Mukims]], yet this trio "seems also to have been formerly wakeueh." The Arabic word *waqf* — appearing in Achehnese as *wakeueh* — signifies property withdrawn in perpetuity by its owner from all alienation, and devoted to some object permitted by the Moslem law. In the political application of the term, certain districts were set aside by the Sultans as reserved territories — outside the ordinary jurisdiction of the Panglima Sagi — so that the Sultan might "stand without and above parties, and maintain a neutral field of observation and operation." Within living memory, the only trace of such a status at Lu'eng Bata had been the independence of its chief; but that independence was substantial. The influential and powerful [[Imeum of Lueng Bata|imeum of Lu'eng Bata]] was indeed on many occasions the adviser and ally of the Sultan — a relation that compelled the latter to hold this chief in great respect and to treat him with marked distinction.

### The Imeum of Lueng Bata

Snouck Hurgronje singles out the imeum of Lu'eng Bata as the most striking example of the elevation that the office of imeum (Ar. *imam*) could attain in the hands of an able incumbent. Originally a religious officer attached to the *meuseugit* of his mukim, the imeum had by Snouck Hurgronje's time "speedily degenerated" into an adat-chief of "purely worldly authority"; and some imeums had succeeded in emancipating themselves from the uleebalang altogether. Among these, "we need only instance the well-known imeum of Lu'eng Bata who played so distinguished a part in the war with the Dutch as a military leader." His case illustrates the wider phenomenon by which the highland imeums, in times of disorder, attained to considerable independence from the traditional chiefs.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Lueng Bata

**Lueng Bata** (also written **Lu'eng Bata**) is a mukim in the [[XXV Mukims]] of [[Acheh]], lying near the [[Dalam (Sultan's Court)|Dalam]] and the capital, [[Banda Acheh]]. It is one of three mukims — together with [[Pagaraye]] and [[Lam Sayun]] — that are regarded as properly belonging to the XXV Mukims, yet are spoken of as having formerly been *wakeueh* districts of the sultan's personal domain. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I it appears chiefly as the seat of an influential imeum who acted as adviser and ally of the sultan.

The *wakeueh* districts were territories attached to the sultan's personal estate and administered for his benefit. The mukims Lueng Bata, Pagaraye and Lam Sayun are regarded as properly belonging to the XXV Mukims, yet this trio seems also to have been made *wakeueh* originally with the intention of providing direct royal support. Such survivals of *wakeueh* districts of this description are to be found in [[Pidie]] and some other dependencies, but their chiefs have long been free from the control of the sultans. The Imeum of Lueng Bata, who held one of these districts, was a chief whose military reputation extended throughout the country; the well-known Imeum of Lueng Bata distinguished himself as a military leader in the war with the Dutch.

Among the uleebalangs of the sultan's territory and the so-called *wakeueh*-districts — Teuku Kali, the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]], the Imeums of Lueng Bata and of [[Chadé|Chade']] and others — there were some who had much more weight in the scale than many of the members of the board of twelve. The Imeum of Lueng Bata, together with the Imeum of Chade', is cited as one of the great power-brokers of the last pre-war royal conflict. The Imeum of Chade' was the most powerful supporter of the defeated candidate in that conflict, while the Imeum of Lueng Bata played the part of an adviser and ally of the sultan. Their relative influence shows how completely the formal constitution — the twelve hereditary panglima sagi and the great officers of the court — had been overlaid by the personal authority of the hereditary imeums of the capital district.

The name Lueng Bata recurs in the list of the chief gampongs of the capital: together with [[Gampong Jawa]], Pande, [[Peunayong]], Lam Bhu', [[Lam Seupeu'eng]], Ateung, Batoh and [[Meura'sa]], it formed the cluster of fine and prosperous villages that gave Banda Acheh its tone in matters of custom and dress. After the Dutch conquest of the Dalam, the fugitive Sultan fled from his court, first to Lueng Bata and then to Lam Teungoh — a detail that illustrates the strategic importance of the mukim as a place of refuge in the immediate neighbourhood of the royal residence.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Lueng Bata

**Lueng Bata** (also written **Lu'eng Bata**) was a **mukim** and **gampong** of [[Banda Acheh]], the capital of [[Acheh]]. It is named in *The Achehnese* among the chief gampongs which clustered around the [[Dalam (Sultan's Court)|Dalam]] — the Sultan's residence — together with [[Gampong Jawa]], [[Pande]], [[Peunayong]], [[Lam Bhu]], [[Lam Seupeu'eng]], [[Ateung]], [[Batoh]] and [[Meura'sa]]. Together with their neighbours, the inhabitants of these villages were distinguished by the epithet **banda** — "town-bred" or "civilized" — and gave the tone to the whole country in matters of custom and dress. As a mukim Lueng Bata was attached to the [[XXV Mukims]] sagi, one of the [[Three Sagis (XXVI, XXV, XXII Mukims)|three sagis]] of [[Great Acheh]].

Like [[Pagaraye]] and [[Lam Sayun]], Lueng Bata was originally a **wakeueh-district** — a territory whose revenues were reserved for the sultans. These three mukims "are regarded as properly belonging to the [XXV] Mukims, yet this trio seems also to have been formerly wakeueh. Within the memory of man however, the only trace of such a status has been the independence of the chiefs of these mukims." The chief of Lueng Bata — known as the **Imeum of Lueng Bata** — was in fact one of the most powerful hereditary chiefs of the capital. "The influential and powerful imeum of Lueng Bata was indeed on many occasions the adviser and ally of the sultan, but this very relation made it necessary for the latter to hold this chief in great respect and treat him with marked distinction."

The Imeum of Lueng Bata is repeatedly cited in *The Achehnese* as one of the small group of chiefs whose authority in the capital outweighed that of many of the so-called "twelve uleebalangs who appoint and dethrone princes." In the famous letter of [[Teungku Tiro (Muhamat Amin)|Teungku Tiro]] to the Dutch Government, the Sultan is reminded that he "could do nothing without consulting [[Teuku Kali]], [[Teuku Ne' of Meura'sa|Teuku Ne' of Meura'sa]], the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] and the Imeum of Lueng Bata — that these four were in their turn dependent upon the decision of the three panglimas of the sagis." This passage places the Imeum of Lueng Bata among the four most powerful chiefs of the sultan's immediate territory, alongside the hereditary chief justice, the chief of the great mukim of [[Meura'sa]], and the commander of the principal mosque.

In the disturbed period of the [[Achehnese War|Dutch war]], the family of the Imeum of Lueng Bata played an active part among the political adventurers of the country. Snouck Hurgronje classes its members with [[Teuku Uma]] and similar energetic chiefs who "saw in the prevailing confusion a chance of essaying their fortune" — turning the disorders of the war to personal advantage. Like other *imeums* of the capital, the Imeum of Lueng Bata had, in earlier times, begun as a religious officer of the mukim mosque; but the office had, in the course of centuries, become hereditary and had shed its religious character, leaving its holder a purely worldly chief — independent of the *panglima sagi* of the [[XXV Mukims]] and exercising, in his own small but important territory, an authority quite beyond his nominal rank.

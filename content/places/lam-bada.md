In the [[Hikajat Prang Kompeuni|Hikayat Prang Gompeuni]], [[Teuku Asan]] fixed his head-quarters in the neighbourhood of Lam Bada, 'the place of his birth,' after gathering some panglimas and a small force.

> **Note on the shared place-name.** Two distinct Achehnese localities are known as *Lam Bada* in the sources: (i) a gampong of the [[XXVI Mukims]] — the burial-place of the celebrated *burong* woman [[Tuan Siti]] (treated below); and (ii) a kampong of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] / [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak complex]] that was the scene of very heavy combat on [[17 April]] [[1896]] (treated in the lower sections). The two places share the same name but lie in different *sagi* of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Lam Bada

**Lam Bada** is a gampong in the [[XXVI Mukims]] mentioned in *The Achehnese* Vol. I as the burial-place of the famous **burong** woman **Tuan Siti** (also called **Pochut Siti**), whose tragic legend is one of the more celebrated spirit-histories of Acheh.

### The Grave of Tuan Siti

Snouck Hurgronje introduces Lam Bada in his survey of the **burongs** — female spirits of the Achehnese spirit-world. He explains that "in addition to this story of the origin of all the burongs, each of the more celebrated of these spirits has a history of its own, though there is a general resemblance between the various legends." He continues: "In Lam Bada (XXVI Mukims) may be seen the grave of a famous burong woman, named [[Tuan Siti]] or Pochut Siti. She was of good lineage, but having allowed herself to be seduced and having become pregnant was in the end treacherously drowned by her lover while on a pleasure trip to which he had invited her."

### The Burong Cult

The story of Tuan Siti is offered by Snouck as the type of the burong legend: a woman of good family brought low by seduction and treachery, whose wronged spirit then became a dreaded presence in the gampong where she was buried. Such burong graves — like the still more dreaded tomb of the burong Srabi in gampong Tanjong — were the object of private religious cults and of vows made by the Achehnese, and Snouck uses the Lam Bada example to show how the Achehnese burong cult is rooted in stories of individual women who had lived and suffered in particular places.

### Significance of the XXVI Mukims site

Lam Bada's appearance in Vol. I, though brief, places it among the specifically named sacred sites of Achehnese popular religion. Its identification as a gampong of the [[XXVI Mukims]] — the same lowland sagi in which the great mosque of [[Indrapatra]] (near [[Ladong]]) had once stood — gives it a particular importance as a place where the older stratum of female-spirit worship persisted under the cover of the official Islam of the sultanate.

## Lam-Bada / Anak Baté (V Moekims Montassik) — the 1896 military theatre

**Lam-Bada / Anak Baté** — written in Dutch as also *Lam Bada*, also *Anak Bate*, *Tjot Bate* — were kampongs in the [[V Moekims Montassik]] / [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak complex]] that were the scene of very heavy combat on [[17 April]] [[1896]] and the source of the shellings against [[Senelop]] from [[15 April]] [[1896]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Lam-Bada / Anak Baté was the site of a [[Friendly-fire incident at Anak Baté (17 April 1896)|friendly-fire incident]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]] in which the mountain battery of Lt. Hoolboom killed 6 European fusiliers of the Dutch side. The kampong of Glieng was separated from Anak Baté by 500 metres of high wooded ground, to which the enemy withdrew.

### Description and Location

Lam-Bada and Anak Baté lay in the border country between the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak complex]], at the eastern end of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] where it adjoined the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. The kampongs were the source of the shellings against [[Senelop]] from [[15 April]] [[1896]] onward, and were within range of the Dutch post. The kampong of Glieng was separated from Anak Baté by 500 metres of high wooded ground, to which the enemy withdrew during the [[17 April]] [[1896]] fighting. The kampong of Lam-Bada was the principal settlement of the cluster and the site of the [[Friendly-fire incident at Anak Baté (17 April 1896)|friendly-fire incident]]. Anak Baté is also referred to as also Anak Baté, one of the seven mukims of the broader [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak complex]]. The kampong of [[Lam-Djamé|Lam-Djamé]] (in the N. *rand* of Lam-Krak) and the *Kling* fortification of Mon Selassa / Mon Seulassa on the border with Ana Batee were nearby.

### Historical Background

Lam-Bada and Anak Baté had been part of the hostile [[V Moekims Montassik]] / [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak complex]] throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The kampongs had been a base for hostile *benden* (war-bands) operating against the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of the [[buitenlinie]] — particularly [[Senelop]] and [[Kroeng Gloempang]]. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] the kampongs became a base for direct attacks on [[Senelop]], from which they were within shelling range. The Anak Baté was the moekim whose *Kling* fortification of Mon Selassa / Mon Seulassa on the border with the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Lheuë]] was one of the principal Achehnese *versterkingen* (fortifications) of the southern sector.

### Role in the 1896 Operations

Lam-Bada and Anak Baté were the source of the shellings against [[Senelop]] from [[15 April]] [[1896]] onward — the heaviest shellings the post endured. On [[17 April]] [[1896]] the kampongs were the scene of very heavy combat in the course of the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] (the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|lifting of Senelop]] by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s first colonne). The combat was characterised by the [[Friendly-fire incident at Anak Baté (17 April 1896)|friendly-fire incident]] in which the mountain battery of Lt. Hoolboom killed 6 European fusiliers of the Dutch side — one of the most-noted incidents of the engagement. The enemy withdrew to the kampong of Glieng, separated from Anak Baté by 500 metres of high wooded ground. Lam-Bada was also a reference point for the [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversionary operation of 2 May 1896]] under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], in which the 3e Bataljon also positions toward Lam-Bada and Lam-Sinjing. Lam-Bada was attacked and occupied at 3:10 PM on [[16 June]] [[1896]] in the course of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging (chastisement) of the V Moekims Montassik]].

### Significance of the V Moekims Montassik site

Lam-Bada and Anak Baté were the principal Achehnese strongpoints within shelling range of [[Senelop]] and the scene of one of the most-noted incidents of the [[April]] [[1896]] crisis — the [[Friendly-fire incident at Anak Baté (17 April 1896)|friendly-fire incident]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]]. The kampongs demonstrated the vulnerability of the [[buitenlinie]] to Achehnese shellings from neighbouring kampongs, and their eventual occupation in the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 16–17 June 1896]] marked the Dutch shift from passive defence to active *tuchtiging*.

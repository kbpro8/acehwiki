**Lambada** was a kampong of the valley of [[Lamtoba]], at the foot of the [[Goudberg]] behind the north coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], whose projecting quarter the Dutch chose on [[29 July]] [[1879]] as the site of the bivouak of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] — the camp from which the occupation of the valley, the marches against [[Kepaja Doewa]] and [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen]], and the return of [[20 August]] were directed — as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## The bivak of July–August 1879

The site was the forward-projecting part of the kampong, surrounded on three sides by open sawah and sheltered besides on both flanks by the amphitheatrically rising banks of the brook [[Aloever Lam Ping]], which yielded an abundance of pure drinking-water. Because the political purpose of the tocht pointed to a stay of some length, the safety of the place was first secured — a clear open strip being cut for the fourth, covered side — and then, as time allowed, the several hygienic installations of the camp were built: *baleh-balehs* (sleeping platforms) rose everywhere as the primitive branch shelters gave way to attap roofs on bamboo and wooden frames; privates and washing-places were made and continually renewed; the two banks of the rivulet were joined within the bivak by a *juk*-bridge of pinang stems; a provoost for prisoners was built and an ambulance erected for the sick. In all these works the native soldiers of the column assisted the Mineurs diligently — "den Europeeschen stonden hiertoe de handen geheel verkeerd," the Europeans' hands were wholly unversed in such labour. On the sawah before the camp the officers gathered in the evenings on the *galangans* for coolness and talk, until the sudden nightfall drove them back within the enclosure (see [[Gelsdorp]]).

## Panglima Banten's property

Lambada was the property of the [[Panglima Banten|Rijksbestierder Panglima Banten]], and it was to this kampong that, after his flight following the [[Punitive expedition against Kepaja Doewa (August 1879)|Kepaja Doewa affair]], soldiers and convict labourers were sent to break down some houses and cut pinang trees for the improvement of the bivak — a reprisal which brought him back, on [[6 August]] [[1879]], with a numerous following of wakils and kampong-heads.

**Kedah** is a Malay state on the western side of the [[Malay Peninsula]], facing the [[Straits of Malacca]]. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Kedah is invoked chiefly as a point of comparison for Achehnese institutions, especially for the territorial meaning of the Arabic word *mukim* and for the vocabulary of rice-cultivation.

## The Word Mukim in Kedah and Acheh

Snouck Hurgronje, having observed that the Achehnese have distorted the original Arabic meaning of *mukim* (a person who settles or resides) into a territorial designation for a parish-like subdivision, notes that "this word has in Kedah the same modified meaning as in Acheh." The source he cites is [[Newbold|T. J. Newbold]]'s *British Settlements in the Straits of Malacca* (II: 20). The further observation, that in Kedah each mukim consisted originally of at least 44 families, is "a clear indication of the original intent of this territorial subdivision" — namely, to provide the requisite congregation of forty adult males for the Friday service, with a margin for absentees.

## Sawah and Ladang: The Vocabulary of Rice-Fields

In a translator's footnote to the agricultural chapter, the word *sawah* is glossed as the Malay term for irrigated rice-fields used in the southern parts of the Peninsula — [[Johor]], [[Malacca]] etc. — and also in [[Java]]. "In [[Penang]], [[Province Wellesley]], Kedah etc. the word is *bladang*." The contrast between *sawah* and *ladang* (the latter denoting unirrigated, swidden fields) is a basic dichotomy of Malay rice-cultivation; its survival in Acheh under slightly different terms shows how the agrarian vocabulary of the Peninsula shaded into that of northern Sumatra.

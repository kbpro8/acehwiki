**East Indies** denotes the archipelagic region of Southeast Asia that was the focus of Dutch colonial expansion from the early seventeenth century onward, encompassing the islands of present‑day Indonesia, including the Sultanate of [[Aceh|Atjeh]]. The *Dutch East India Company* (VOC) organised an [[Oostindische retourvloot|East Indies return fleet]] whose safety was a central concern during the naval engagements of [[1673]] described in the *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pp. 266‑269).

## Role in the 1673 naval operations

The source records that after the battle of [[Schooneveld]] on [[14 June]] [[1673]], the Dutch Republic prepared a fleet to protect the East Indies return fleet from English and French interference. The fleet, commanded by admirals such as [[Michiel de Ruyter]] and [[Maarten Tromp]], was intended to keep the sea lanes open for the convoy returning from the East Indies, where Aceh was a major source of pepper and other spices.

## Economic importance for Aceh

Aceh’s strategic position at the northern tip of Sumatra made it a key node in the VOC’s spice trade network. The safety of the East Indies return fleet directly affected the flow of goods from Aceh to Europe, influencing both the wealth of the VOC and the political leverage the Dutch held over the Sultanate.

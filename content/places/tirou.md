**Tirou** was a plantation in the binnenlanden (interior) of the Noordkust (north coast) of [[Atjeh]], famous for its school for Islamic scholars. The institution trained Schriftgeleerden (Islamic scholars) who received the title Tengkoe upon graduation and went on to serve as religious leaders throughout Acehnese society. [[Sjech Saman van Tirou]], the prominent holy war preacher, was among its notable alumni.

## Location

Tirou was situated in the interior of the north coast region of [[Atjeh]], away from the coastal settlements but accessible to the population of the surrounding area. The exact location placed it within the network of Acehnese communities that would later become the base of resistance against Dutch colonial rule.

## The School

The school at Tirou was dedicated to Islamic religious education. Students came from throughout [[Groot-Atjeh]] to study the religious sciences, including:

- Quranic interpretation
- Islamic jurisprudence
- Theology
- Religious practice

The curriculum combined traditional Islamic learning with the particular needs of Acehnese society. Graduates were equipped to serve as Imams, religious judges, teachers, and community leaders.

## Alumni

### Sjech Saman van Tirou

The most famous alumnus was [[Sjech Saman van Tirou]]. He distinguished himself through exceptional religious devotion and organizational ability. Upon graduation, he became one of the most influential geestdrijvers (religious zealots) in [[Groot-Atjeh]], preaching the *heilige oorlog* (holy war) against the Dutch.

### Other Scholars

Other graduates of the Tirou school served as religious authorities throughout Acehnese society. The network of Tirou-educated scholars created a system of religious authority that transcended local boundaries and connected resistance activities across the region.

## Significance

The Tirou school represented the intersection of traditional Islamic education and Acehnese political identity. By training religious leaders, the school shaped the ideological dimension of the [[Aceh War]]. The graduates who became leaders of the resistance brought religious sanction to the political struggle against colonial rule.

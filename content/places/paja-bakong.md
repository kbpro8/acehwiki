**Paja Bakong** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Tgk. Sjeh di Paja Bakong]]

**Tgk. Sjeh di Paja Bakong** — also called **Tgk. Seupot Mata** because he was half blind — was the great religious resistance leader of the [[Paja Bakong]] district in [[Keureutòë]], venerated by the population as a saint, and one of the three principal *teungkoes* of the north-coast resistance alongside [[Tgk. di Barat]] and [[Tgk. di Mata Ië]]. He was killed on [[25 October]] [[1910]] with his son [[Tgk. Mat Saleh]] and with [[Tjoet Meutia]] in the surprise attack of brigade commander [[W. J. Mosselman]] in a side-stream of the [[Kroeëng Peutòë]].

### From [[Lho' Seumawè]]

[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] noted in his letter of July [[1908]] that in the Lho' Seumawè country, where Swart had formerly travelled alone even from [[Lho' Soekon]] through [[Matang Koeli]] to [[Paja Bakong]], it was now unsafe even with military escort. In the resistance of [[1908]]–[[1912]] Lho' Seumawè nevertheless remained the most favourable district; the administration there had for a considerable time been in the hands of a *controleur*, though the garrison of the bivouac [[Boeloh Blang Ara]] under [[M. Schouten|Schouten]] repeatedly had its hands full. By [[1910]] the *[[Koloniaal Verslag]]* could report that the European administrators could visit a great part of their *ressorten* without military escort — "in Lho' Seumawè the whole sub-division."

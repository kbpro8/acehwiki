**Lho' Kroeët** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

A fourth principle was travel without military escort, a hobby-horse of Swart's and a conception [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] did not share; Van Daalen even forbade administrators to visit their territory without military cover. In [[1902]] this had already caused friction between Swart as *gezaghebber* in [[Lho' Kroeët]] and [[Daja]] and Van Daalen as his assistant resident. Van Daalen noted the prohibition on Swart's journal; Van Heutsz in his turn noted "that Swart could judge this better than he or I could, that appreciation ought to be shown from higher authority in the matter, and that there could be no question of a prohibition where Swart was concerned."

### From [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen]]

In [[1902]] Van Daalen, as Swart's assistant resident, forbade administrators to visit their territory without military escort and noted that prohibition on Swart's journal, when Swart was *gezaghebber* in [[Lho' Kroeët]] and [[Daja]]. Van Heutsz in turn noted "that Swart could judge this better than he or I could, that appreciation ought to be shown from higher authority in the matter, and that there could be no question of a prohibition where Swart was concerned."

### From [[Daja]]

In [[1902]] Swart, as *gezaghebber* in [[Lho' Kroeët]] and Daja, was forbidden by [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to travel without military escort, a prohibition [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] overruled. In [[1914]] the murder of [[W. van Kregten]] at [[Lam No]] on [[20 May]] set off the [[Verzet in Daja en Lhong (1914)|resistance in Daja and Lhong]]; the united bands of Nja' Ali and [[Nja' Asém]] were destroyed by lieutenant [[C. A. Snell|Snell]] at the holy grave near the mouth of the [[Daja-rivier]] on [[5 August]] [[1914]]. The flare-up of resistance in the Daja districts was one of the three heavy blows [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] names as having tested Swart's composure.

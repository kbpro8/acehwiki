**Kota Goenoengan** (also called Kota Koeboeran, "Burial-Ground Fort") was the high walled burial ground on the western face of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], captured by the Dutch on [[12 January]] [[1874]] in the [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan (12 January 1874)|capture of Kota Goenoengan]] and afterwards used as a forward observation post. From a high *waringin-boom* (banyan tree) on its summit Dutch lookouts could see over the Kraton wall into the interior, and the position became the jumping-off point for [[Generaal Verspijck|Verspijck]]'s *omtrekkende beweging* of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] that produced the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]].

## The capture

On the advice of a "bevriend inlandsch hoofd" — a friendly native chief — Dutch troops operated along the western face of the Kraton on [[12 January]] [[1874]] to occupy the high, walled burial grounds of Kota Koeboeran and Kota Goenoengan. The works were "bijna niet verdedigd" and were taken without serious losses. Borel argues that the easy capture proved the vijand's *hoofdmacht* had already withdrawn from the Kraton's western face — had the *omtrekkende beweging* been pursued immediately, the Kraton might have fallen within days rather than weeks. As it was, the Dutch paused, and it took another twelve days of [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|bombardment]] before [[Generaal Verspijck|Verspijck]] began the flanking march from Kota Goenoengan around the *zuider-* and *ooster-facen* on [[23 January]] [[1874]].

## The observation post

Once taken, Kota Goenoengan became the principal Dutch observation post against the Kraton's western face. The high waringin-boom on its summit gave lookouts a clear view over the wall into the *kerkhof* and barracks inside, allowing the *vesting-artillerie* to correct its fire. From Kota Goenoengan the *omtrekkende beweging* of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] set out: Verspijck marched around the southern and eastern faces, crossed the [[Kroeng Daroe]] — the crossing caused "groot oponthoud" — and reached the [[Atjeh-rivier]] on the morning of [[24 January]] [[1874]]. The Kraton was found "op een grijsaard en een zieken Atjehnees na, geheel verlaten." In the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] of [[1875]] Kota Goenoengan was supplemented by the higher [[Kota Podjoet]] to the south-west, whose observatory directed artillery fire across the southern kampongs.

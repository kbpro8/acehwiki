## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Holland / Netherlands

**Holland** — more properly the **Netherlands** — is the European mother-country of the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands East Indies]], and the metropolitan power responsible for the long [[Achehnese War|war in Acheh]]. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Snouck Hurgronje, writing as Adviser for Native Affairs to the Netherlands-India Government, repeatedly contrasts Dutch knowledge of Achehnese affairs with the legend that had grown up abroad.

### The European Legend of the Acheh War

"Holland — so runs the European legend — has been engaged in war against Acheh for a period which has extended to about thirty years without having led to the subjugation of that native kingdom." Shaking their heads solemnly over it, many learned people outside the frontiers of Holland saw in this "worthy fable" a definite indication of Holland's inability to govern her colonies. Snouck Hurgronje insists that the obstacles in the path of conquest were "very, very great," and that "with far more wisdom and power than little Holland possesses, the difficulties would have taken a long spell of time to overcome." Holland's error, he argues, was that she had continually suspended the action she had begun and indulged in long periods of quiescence, while the most serious trouble of all lay in the fact that "the strings of policy were pulled by ignorant majorities in the Mother-country."

### Holland as Source of Achehnese Scholarship

The book is dedicated, in the first instance, to Dutch scholarship on the East Indies. Snouck Hurgronje quotes [[Prof. Veth|Professor Veth's]] *Atjèh* for the principal historical traditions of Acheh, [[Van Langen]]'s *Atjèhsch Staatsbestuur* for the structure of the sultanate, and the writings of [[G. K. Niemann|G. H. Niemann]] and [[G. A. Wilken]] for comparative ethnography. The exposition of [[Adat Meukuta Alam]] and the *Makota Alam* codes rests on Dutch archival labours, while the ordonnantie of 19 March [[1881]] for the administration of justice among the native population of [[Great Acheh]] was drafted by [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. der Kinderen]] and annotated by [[L. W. C. van den Berg|Mr. L. W. C. van den Berg]].

### The Author's Position

In July [[1891]] Snouck Hurgronje proceeded to Acheh "in pursuance of instructions from the Netherlands-India Government to make a special study of the religious element in the political conditions of that country." During a residence in [[Arabia]] in [[1884]]–[[1885]] he had been in a position, especially at [[Mecca]], to obtain an intimate knowledge of the influence of Mohammedan fanaticism upon the obstinate resistance of the Achehnese to Dutch rule. After Acheh he was appointed Adviser for Native Affairs, Netherlands India, the post from which he wrote the present work. The book was composed in the mother-country and at [[The Hague]], printed in [[Leyden]] by [[E. J. Brill]], and translated in the [[Straits Settlements]] by [[A. S. O'Sullivan]].

### Anglo-Dutch Treaties

Holland's freedom of action in Acheh was governed by her treaties with Great Britain. In [[1824]] the Dutch Government, with great lack of foresight, entered into a treaty with Great Britain, under which it guaranteed the independence of Acheh. These ultimately led up in [[1871]] to a new treaty with England, under which Holland was left with more freedom of action in the control of Acheh, paving the way for the [[First and Second Dutch Expeditions to Acheh|first Dutch expedition of 1873]].

**Moesapi** (also Kota Moesapi or K. Moesapi) was the old stone fort at the north-eastern corner of the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]], about one and a half hours' march from [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] and the most distant post of the Dutch line. Armed with two 12 c.M. *voorlaadkanonnen* (muzzle-loading guns), its purpose was to cover the Gigieng lagune and the mouth of the [[Kroeng Tjoet]], the major contraband supply channel giving access to the [[XXVI Moekims]]. Borel criticises its disadvantageous position: it could not stop boats entering the Kroeng Tjoet.

## The original position

Moesapi had been in Dutch hands before February [[1874]] but was abandoned by [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] along with so much else when he adopted the *afwachtende houding* after the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|Ketapang Doewa expedition]]. Its reoccupation was part of [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]]'s work of rebuilding the stelling from May [[1874]] onward. The old stone fort — described as a *Mesigit* (mosque) — was re-armed with two 12 c.M. *voorlaadkanonnen*; Borel complains that these obsolete guns, with their *tijdbuis* (time-fuse) shells that failed to burst on Atjeh's wet, covered terrain, were a poor substitute for the *achterlaadkanonnen* that van Swieten had recalled to Java. The fort's north-eastern position made it the seaward anchor of the eastern arc of the stelling-Pel, but its guns could not stop boats entering the Kroeng Tjoet.

## In the stelling-Pel

By the time the continuous line around K. Radja was completed in May [[1875]], the east side of the line ran from [[Blang Tjoet]] through [[Kota Alam]], [[Lemboe]] O. en N.O., [[Lampriet]], [[Langkroek]] O., [[Lamara]] Z.O. en N.O., [[Tiban]] to Moesapi. From Moesapi the line continued along the sea-strand to Oeleh Leh — the *basis* of the *stelling-Pel* triangle. Moesapi's weakness — its inability to close the Kroeng Tjoet — was only made good in February [[1876]], when [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|Engel]] reached the [[Kwala Gigieng]] on [[26 February]] [[1876]] and established posts at Kwala Gigieng and on the island of [[Kota Pohama]] in the lagune. The mouth of the Kroeng Tjoet was finally closed by the Dutch in [[1876]], in the wake of [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s great [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|winter offensive of 1875–1876]] and his death at [[Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]].

**Gigiëng** was the landscape and small federation of the [[Pidie|Pidië]] region of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] whose radja signed the [[Submission of the Radja of Gigiëng (20 April 1874)|first act of submission]] of the [[Acheh War|war years]] on [[20 April]] [[1874]], and which from [[1890]] devoted itself so successfully to rice cultivation that it became the granary of the federation Pidië and could even spare rice for Groot-Atjeh.

## The submission of 1874

During the [[Blockade of the Atjeh coast (1873-1874)|blockade of the Atjehnese coast]] the Government circulated its first trade proclamation; whether it moved the [[Raja of Gigieng|Radja of Gigiëng]] to sign his act of submission, author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] leaves undecided. The submission was accomplished on 20 April [[1874]], when, in the presence of a great multitude, the Dutch flag was hoisted on the *passar* at Gigiëng, the Radja received an act of recognition and confirmation, and the Government paid him 10,000 rijksdaalders. The Netherlands Government kept its promise and permitted trade with Gigiëng immediately, and very soon a great number of trading vessels were cleared at [[Penang]] for this part of Atjeh.

## The rice granary of Pidië

Especially in the federation Gighen the population devoted itself from [[1890]] to rice culture — "zóó zelfs, dat die streek de voorraadschuur van rijst werd voor de federatie Pidië en bovendien nog rijst kon afstaan voor Groot-Atjeh" ("so much even that this tract became the store-shed of rice for the federation Pidië, and moreover could spare rice for Groot-Atjeh"). The North Coast sawahs in general produced enough for the population's need even while the tract remained closed to the coasting trade in the early 1890s.

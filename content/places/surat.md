**Surat** — also written **Sūrat** — is the great port of [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]] on the north-western coast of India, in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries one of the principal nodes of the Indian Ocean network that linked the religious scholarship of Gujarat with the Malay-Indonesian world. Surat is the port from which [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] departed for [[Acheh]] in [[1637]] and to which he returned in [[1644]]; the city in which [[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]] (d. [[1620]]) succeeded his famous grandfather as religious teacher; and the city in which [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] was received into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] by al-ʿAddārs.

## The port of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī network

Surat was, in this period, the principal port of the Mughal empire on the Indian Ocean, and the principal node of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network from which the orthodox reaction of Acheh drew its principal Malay-Indonesian agents. The Indian-Arab religious families of mixed blood — the al-Aydārūs family, the family of al-ʿAddārs, and the family of Raniri himself — had its centre in the towns of Gujarat, and Surat was the port by which the network communicated with the wider Indian Ocean world, including the Malay-Indonesian archipelago. The Persian manuscript of the *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] in the India Office Library — Ethé 1920(11) — was copied at Surat in H. 1055.

## The religious teachers of Surat

[[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]], born in [[1561]] at [[Tarim]] in South Arabia, came to Gujarat at the age of nineteen and succeeded his famous grandfather as religious teacher, first at [[Ahmadabad]] and later at Surat. He died at Surat in [[1620]]. His successor in the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] was [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] — the shaikh of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] and [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]] — who was received into the order by al-ʿAddārs at Surat. The city was thus the principal centre of the Rifāʿiyya in Gujarat in this period, and the immediate source of the silsila that was carried to Acheh and Makassar by Raniri and Shaikh Yūsuf.

## Significance

Surat is the port of departure and return of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]], and one of the principal nodes of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network from which the orthodox reaction of [[Acheh]] drew its principal Malay-Indonesian agents. The religious teachers of Surat — al-ʿAddārs and Bā Shaybān — formed the spiritual genealogy of Raniri and Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar, and the city's port was the channel by which the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds was carried to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**Suratte** — Surat, in the source's spelling *Soeratte* — was, according to *Nederlanders over de Zeeën*, "de grootste haven" (the greatest harbour) of the [[Mogol|Mogol]] empire on the north-western coast of India. The Dutch reached [[Suratte|Suratte]] via [[Acheh|Atjeh]]: "het was juist vanuit Atjeh, dat de Nederlanders, reeds tijdens de Voor-Compagnieën naar de grootste haven Soeratte voeren" (it was precisely from Atjeh that the Dutch, already during the Pre-Company period, sailed to the greatest harbour Suratte).

### The Atjeh route to Suratte

The source records that the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] reached [[Suratte|Suratte]] via [[Acheh|Atjeh]] during the *Voor-Compagnieën* — the pre-[[1602]] period of separate Dutch trading companies. The [[Acheh|Atjeh]] route to [[Suratte|Suratte]] was, in the source's narrative, the conduit by which the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] entered the trade of the [[Mogol|Mogol]] empire. The [[Gujerat|Gujerat]]-Atjeh trade was the principal Asian artery that the Dutch encountered when they entered the Indian Ocean trade.

### The initial failure

The source records that the Dutch initial establishment at [[Suratte|Suratte]] was "aanvankelijk nog met weinig succes, daar de Portugezen twee onzer kooplieden ophingen" (initially with little success, since the Portuguese hanged two of our merchants). The Portuguese opposition to the Dutch intrusion is the type-specimen of the Luso-Dutch commercial rivalry that would, after [[1602]], become one of the principal drivers of the [[VOC|VOC]]'s military expansion in the Indian Ocean.

### Significance

[[Suratte|Suratte]] is, in the source's narrative, the [[Mogol|Mogol]]-empire port that the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] reached via [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The Atjeh route to [[Suratte|Suratte]] is the conduit by which the Dutch entered the trade of the [[Mogol|Mogol]] empire — and the principal illustration, in the source, of the pre-Company Asian commerce in which [[Acheh|Atjeh]] was a central node.

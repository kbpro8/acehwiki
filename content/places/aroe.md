**Aroe** — also **Aru**, in Portuguese *Aru* or *Arua*, in the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] the realm of the *Djao* (Portuguese *Jaoa*) — was the kingdom on the east coast of [[Sumatra]] whose bay, [[Aroe-baai|Aroe-baai]], opens into the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] opposite the Nine Islands of [[Perak]], and whose fall to the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] [[Atjehsche vloot|fleet]] in [[1539]] — recorded by [[Mendez Pinto]] — marks the close of the independent history of the east-coast Hindu-Buddhest commonwealth and the beginning of the Atjehnese maritime age. A kingdom once ranked in the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] on a level with [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] and [[Malacca]], and one that stretched from [[Tamijang]] to [[Siak]], Aroe was reduced by the Atjehnese conquest to "poverty and piracy", so much so that in the colonial period only a "*soekoe Aroe*" — an Aroe clan — was remembered.

## Geography and the bay

Aroe's strategic heart was [[Aroe-baai|the bay]] — known to [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] as the *port de Surotilau* on the *cite du royaume d'Auru* — into which the river of [[Tandjoengpoera]] empties. The bay is sheltered by a half-moon of islands: [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] (Pinto's *Paneticano*), [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]], [[Pulo Breueh|Salahadji]] and others; the landward side is heavily forested, with apes, pythons, and crocodiles that "dragged men from the prauwen and devoured them". The approach by sea from Malacca runs by the Nine Islands off [[Perak]], across the Strait, and into the bay; the inland approach runs up the [[Besitang]] river, past the [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] oil-springs, and up the *soengej Si Kondoer* and *Sérang djaja* — old fortifications, the colonial official [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] reported in [[1888]], that were said to be the old *bentengs* of the earlier rajas of Aroe.

## Pinto and the Atjeh-Aru war (1539)

Aroe enters European record through [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]'s second Sumatran mission. In October [[1539]] he was dispatched by [[Pero de Faria]] to Aroe, whose king had asked for Portuguese help against the [[Atjehsche vloot|Atjehnese fleet]] then bearing down on him. Pinto's anchorage in the bay, his reception by the [[Radja of Aroe]], the arrival of the [[Alleredin Mahomet|Atjehnese fleet]] of 130 vessels, the [[Radja of Aroe|radja]]'s death in the breach, the [[Queen of Aroe|queen]]'s counter-stroke and flight, and the kingdom's absorption into the Atjehnese sphere are the substance of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]] — the foundational military event of the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s sixteenth-century expansion.

## Decoding the *bedekte taal* of Aroe

Pinto's account of Aroe is dense with names that [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *bedekte taal* — veiled-language forms concealing the older Hindu-Buddhist geography of the bay. *Surotilau* is read as *Soera-sakti-lawoe-kir*, the roadstead of [[Malajoer]] in Aroe. *Ilikandure*, the "fish (*ikan*) on Hindu" (*hin-doe ka-ri*) riddle, marks where Hindu population still survived. *Minhatoley* is read as *mina-ng-k-a da-toe Olèh-leh*, the territory of the *dotoe* of [[Olèh-leh]], an Atjeh-chief marking the start of the Suratte-passage. *Batorrendan* is *batoe rendang*, a joke on the [[Batu|Batu]] islands. *Turban* is *Batoe-poer-wa-boewana*. *Paneticano* is [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]. *minha cumbaa* is *minjak sembawa*, the white earth-oil of [[Pangkalan Soesoe]].

The bay's islet, [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] — Pinto's *Paneticano*, also *Koempej* — is, in this reading, the older *Malajoer* of the [[Pararaton]], the *Noesa-n-ta-ra* "standing under the protection of the goddess Tara", where the *pabgjan* (the toll-house at which ships paid import duties) stood. The bay and the islet are, in turn, the *Taroema* of the Adiparwa's Churning of the Ocean of Milk — the *tasik soesoe* from which the gods drew the *amrta*.

## Political structure and trade before the fall

Before its fall, Aroe was administered in Malay style: a *Xabandar* (harbourmaster) commanded five *lanchares* and twelve *balo* (war-vessels), while above him stood the *banda(ha)ra* (bendahara), the "gouverneur du royaume", who directed the *hoeloebalang* (war-chiefs). The bendahara's personal guard were the *hamba radja* — "the most noble of his Court", though many of them, Pinto noted, were in fact poor. Ibn Battuta, visiting the region in [[1345]]–[[1346]], likewise recorded a Hindu kingdom (*Moel Djawa*) with elephants, stone walls, and cloves and nutmeg on its market — evidence of regular trade with the Moluccas conducted through Aroe's entrepôt.

## Decadence and the *soekoe Aroe*

After the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|fall of Aroe]], the [[Queen of Aroe|queen]]'s flight, and the brief [[Djohor|Johorese]] reconquest, Aroe was finally lost for good, and "with the realm of the Djao — as the Portuguese wrote *Jaoa* — it was done forever". Once a *negeri* ranked in the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] on a line with [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] and [[Malacca]], a realm that stretched from [[Tamijang]] to [[Siak]], it sank into poverty and piracy, so much so that in the colonial period "one could only remember a *soekoe Aroe*" — an Aroe clan — in its place. Aroe had once been a *ravitailleeringsplaats* (revictualling station) for merchant shipping through the Strait, a place that traded on its own account and trans-shipped to other places, renowned as the source of the *minjak tanah* — the white medicinal petroleum that was exported as a balsam. In the seventeenth century that reputation had already much declined.

## Van Rijn's visit (1888)

In [[1888]] the Controleur S.B. [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] brought a visit to the Aroe-bay from [[Bengkalis]], in connection, De Roo de la Faille notes, with Tiele's essay on the history of those days. He found the bay heavily forested, hilly, a timber-concession of beautiful damar-trees; on the islet of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] he saw the half-moon of hills whose whitish strand — "making the impression that one has a fortress with breastwork before one" — recalled the Tanjore inscription of [[1030]]'s "ancient Malayur situated on a high hill". On the mainland, at [[Pangkalan Soesoe]], he found ten dwellings of Malays who had fled from [[Besitang]]; inland, he found the two old *bentengs* of *soengej Si Kondoer* and *Sérang djaja*, said to have been the fortifications of the earlier rajas of Aroe.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Aroe is the principal east-coast polity whose fall marks the formal opening of the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s maritime age. Its [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|loss to Atjeh]] in [[1539]] is the single event by which the Atjehnese sultanate closed the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] to Portuguese shipping and began to draw the commerce of the Strait into its own sphere; its *bedekte taal* geography, as decoded by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], is the principal instance of the survival of the older Hindu-Buddhist place-world beneath the chronicle record.

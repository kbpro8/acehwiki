**Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)** (modern *Krueng Ligan* near *Babah Loh*) is an iron-ore locality on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West coast of Atjeh]], 113 km NW of [[Meulaboh]], recorded by [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]], [[B. B. Lindberg|Lindberg]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]] in [[1922]] and reproduced in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]). The Kr. Ligan locality is No. 3 in Van Bemmelen's Fig. 41 key, and is the most substantial of the West-Atjeh iron-ore occurrences recorded by the 1922 survey.

## The 1922 Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp observation

The 1922 report of Jansen, Lindberg and Wolvekamp, as reproduced by Van Bemmelen, records:

> "Locality: 113 km NW of Meulaboh. Over a length of 700 m iron ore, pyrite and pyrolusite were observed. Thickness irregular from 8 to 17 m. Probably also contact-metamorphic origin."

The 700 m length of the iron-ore occurrence, with a thickness of 8 to 17 m, makes the Kr. Ligan deposit the most substantial of the West-Atjeh iron-ore occurrences in terms of the visible surface extent. The recorded minerals — iron ore, pyrite and pyrolusite (manganese oxide) — indicate a complex sulphide-oxide paragenesis, and the irregular thickness (8 to 17 m) is typical of contact-metamorphic deposits formed at the boundary of an eruptive intrusion and the country rock. The "probably also contact-metamorphic origin" judgment is consistent with the general geological model that Jansen, Lindberg and Wolvekamp applied to all the documented Atjeh iron-ore occurrences; see the entry on [[A. Tobler]] for the geological theory.

## Significance

Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh) is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the most substantial of the West-Atjeh iron-ore occurrences recorded in the colonial geological literature — a 700-m-long deposit of iron ore, pyrite and pyrolusite, with a thickness of 8 to 17 m, located 113 km NW of Meulaboh. The deposit's substantial dimensions, combined with its West-Atjeh location (in the same coastal region as the [[M.A.E.M.]] gold-dredging operation and the [[C. de Groot|De Groot]] [[1946]] mercury observations), make it a key locality for any assessment of the colonial-era mineral potential of the West-Atjeh coast. The fact that the deposit was never developed is, like the non-development of the [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] deposit, evidence of the colonial state's unwillingness or inability to translate geological reserves into productive mines.

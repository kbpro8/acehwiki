**Kajoe‑leh** was a kampong in the interior of [[Acheh]] that was occupied by Dutch forces on [[5 February]] [[1876]]. The settlement lay amid extensive sawahs and served as a forward base for further advances toward the [[Atjeh River]].

**Kajoe Leh** was one of the places named on the gravestones of the [[Peutjoet|Petjoet cemetery]] at [[Koeta Radja]] as a field of honour on which Dutch soldiers had fallen, recorded by [[P. H. R. Beuming]] alongside [[Lambaroe]], [[Kroeng Raba]] and [[Aroen]] in the inscriptions he read there.

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — the post besieged of March 1876

Meijer gives Kajoe-Lē its first use as the site of the middle Dutch work on the road of the south-east line, chosen by [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|Major B. E. Mekern]] on the northeast edge of the kampong of the same name in the days directly after the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] and lay at the centre of the blockade: the right half of the [[13e Bataljon|13th Infantry Battalion]] — 305 bayonets with 2 sections of artillery — was held enclosed there in the first week of March by the enemy's heavy occupation of Lamrong, [[Lamsajoen|Lamsajoeng]] and [[Lambaroe]]; the fortification work had only just begun — not one face was finished — and the whole left half of the 13th battalion lay partly inside, partly in regularly set-up huts of rough materials outside the rampart, protected all around by a heavy abatis. The stay there was unhealthy: the troops suffered much from fever and bowel complaints, and after every rain bivouac and work interior turned into one mud pool flooding the soles. For days its garrison saw every patrol fired on, its road cut; the report of the major was then smuggled out through the enemy by the [[Bugis couriers from Kajoe-Lē (6 March 1876)|couriers of 6 March]] pretending at daylight to cut paddy in the fields outside.

After the [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|storming of Lambaroe]] the road stood open again, vivres and munition reached it on the day itself, and the battalion lay thereafter as the standing garrison of the completed fort. The ground west of it was so sodden that the march from it to [[Atoeh|Atoë Noord]] — before the road was made — forced riders to halt in the mud up to the horse's belly; and the sawahs before it never dried. Thereafter the post stood, with [[Lambaroe]], at the heart of the daily watch of the south-east line.

**Gle Mon Ampeuet** (also *Gunung Mon Ampeuet*) is an iron-ore locality in the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] interior, 37 km South of [[Kota Radja]], recorded by [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]], [[B. B. Lindberg|Lindberg]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]] in [[1922]] and reproduced in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]). The Gle Mon Ampeuet locality is No. 2 in Van Bemmelen's Fig. 41 key, and is the second-closest Atjeh iron-ore locality to the colonial capital (after [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] at 32 km South).

## The 1922 Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp observation

The 1922 report of Jansen, Lindberg and Wolvekamp, as reproduced by Van Bemmelen, records:

> "Locality: 37 km South of Kota Radja. Numerous fragments of iron ore, with a core of pyrite, on the slopes. In the vicinity limestone and an eruptive rock (amphibole andesite) were observed. The presence of garnet and a silicious limestone indicates contact-metamorphic origin. The thickness of this occurrence is some metres."

The geological description is the standard contact-metamorphic model: an eruptive rock (amphibole andesite) intruding into limestone, with the contact-metamorphic formation of garnet and silicified limestone, and the deposition of magnetite-hematite iron ore with pyrite cores at the contact zone. The same model applies to the [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] locality (5 km to the north) and to the [[Muara Sipongi]] locality across the Tapanuli border to the south; see the entry on [[A. Tobler]] for the geological theory.

## Significance

Gle Mon Ampeuet is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is one of the two Atjeh iron-ore localities closest to the colonial capital [[Kota Radja]] (the other being [[Kr. Geuenteuet]]), and accordingly one of the first Atjeh mineral localities to be recorded in the colonial geological literature. The locality's contact-metamorphic origin — established by the [[1922]] Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp report on the basis of the garnet and silicified limestone observed in the vicinity — places it within the same geological model that applies to all the documented Atjeh iron-ore occurrences, and accordingly within the same mineral province that extends, in the colonial geological literature, from the northern tip of Atjeh to the southern tip of Benkulen.

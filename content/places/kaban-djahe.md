**Kaban Djahé** is the highland town in the Karo plateau of the East Coast residency of Sumatra, the route via which General [[Major-General Overakker|Overakker]] withdrew the troops from elsewhere on Sumatra to Central Atjèh after the fall of Java in March [[1942]], and the eastern terminus of the *Alasweg* that ran 130 km. westward across the mountains to [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]] in the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]]. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats Kaban Djahé as the strategic hinge of the entire Dutch defense of northern Sumatra — the gateway through which the troops from Upper-Djambi, Sumatra's West Coast and Tapanoeli had to pass to reach the Alas valley, and the headquarters from which General Overakker directed the retreat until [[14 March]] [[1942]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Kaban Djahe

**Kaban Djahe** (also rendered **Kaban Jahe**, modern **Kabanjahe**) served, in the [[Wegenplan Sumatra|Sumatra road plan]] of the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]], as the northern terminus of the Alasweg — the 137 K.M. road built from Kaban Djahe to [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]] in the [[Alaslanden|Alas lands]] at a cost of f 1,173,000, and reported by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau as "in the main completed" by the end of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade. The road opened the [[Alaslanden|Alas lands]] of the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency to direct road communication with the Karo highlands and the wegennet of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]].

The Bureau's account lists the Alasweg as project no. 13: "Weg Kaban Djahe–Kota Tjane (Alasweg). Length 137 K.M.; cost f 1,173,000. For the connection of the Alas lands with the Karo-hoogvlakte and the wegennet of the East Coast. In the main completed." The Kaban Djahe–Koeta Tjané Alasweg is the southern complement to the [[Bireuen]]–[[Takengon]] Gajo-weg: where the Gajo-weg opens the Gajo lands to the northern coast of Atjeh, the Alasweg opens the Alaslanden to the southern East Coast network.

## The Alasweg junction

The defense of the Atjèh highlands rested on two motor roads: "[[Takéngón|Takéngón]] from [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] along the over 100 km. long *Gajóweg*, [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]] in the Alas valley from Kaban Djahé in the East Coast residency of Sumatra along the 130 km. long *Alasweg*." Kaban Djahé stood at the eastern end of this second road. "Meanwhile our troops had blown up the main bridge in the over 130 km. long road from Kaban Djahé to [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]], located 8 km. from Kaban Djahé." The bridge's destruction on [[12 March]] [[1942]] was a key feature of the delaying action fought by the Dutch rear guard. The Bataks who accompanied the retreat with "horas" calls at [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] on [[28 March]] [[1942]] reflect the Karo origin of this eastern approach.

## General Overakker's headquarters

"On [[14 March]] general Overakker had moved his headquarters from Kaban Djahé to the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]] and first established it at [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]], later at the former Japanese undertaking 'Boekit Toesam'." The move followed the Japanese landings on North Sumatra of [[12 March]] [[1942]]. "With the troop movements associated with this an immediate start had been made on [[9 March]]. However, when on [[12 March]] the Japanese landings on North Sumatra took place, they were not yet completed." A part of the Japanese troops landed at [[Tandjong Tiram|Tandjong Tiram]] "immediately advanced to [[Pematang Siantar|Pematang Siantar]], from there to advance along the East side of the Toba-lake over Baligé to Siborongborong. With the occupation of Siborongborong the last retreat route of our troops to Central-Atjèh, namely the road from Siborongborong along the South side of the Toba-lake over [[Sidikalang|Sidikalang]], was cut off."

## The cut-off of the retreat

"A large part of our troops from Upper-Djambi, Sumatra's West Coast and Tapanoeli had however by then already passed Siborongborong and reached the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]] undisturbed. The Japanese namely, under the assumption that our main force was located in Sumatra's West Coast, advanced from Siborongborong Southward to [[Taroetoeng|Taroetoeng]]. From there the advance was continued in two columns to [[Padang Sidempoean|Padang Sidempoean]] namely over [[Sibolga|Sibolga]], which was occupied on [[14 March]] and over [[Sipirok|Sipirok]]. Only at the border between Tapanoeli and Sumatra's West Coast did the Japanese notice their mistake and turned their attention to the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]]. Unfortunately a part of our troops was no longer able to break through due to the rapid advance of the Japanese and taken prisoner of war. On [[16 March]] the capitulation of these troops had taken place." After the fall of the Alas valley the few European women and children of [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] were transferred to Kaban Djahé at the end of March [[1942]], and the European women and children were "accommodated in the internment camp at [[Brastagi|Brastagi]]."

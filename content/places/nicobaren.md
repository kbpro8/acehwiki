**The Nicobaren** — the **Nicobar Islands** in the Bay of Bengal, between the Andaman Islands and [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] — were the principal transit anchorage of the early Dutch East India fleets on the Atjeh-Djohor-Malaka route, the place where the [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] fleet of [[1605]] stopped on the outward voyage and where Matelieff announced the secret plan to attack [[Malaka|Malaka]] to his crews, and the place to which the Portuguese viceroy's relief fleet was detached after the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|engagement of 18 August 1606]] — the detachment that Matelieff would later seek to engage at the Nicobaren on his return from the destruction of the remaining Portuguese fleet on [[22 October]] [[1606]]. The Nicobaren are the principal named transit anchorage in Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The Matelieff fleet's stopover (1605)

The [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] fleet of nine ships left Texel on [[12 May]] [[1605]] with secret instructions to attack [[Malaka|Malaka]] — instructions that "verloren is gegaan". At [[Mauritius|Mauritius]] Matelieff conferred with Admiraal [[Steven van der Haghen|Steven van der Haghen]] about the state of the Indies; both agreed that Malaka should be the first target. The fleet's destination was kept secret from the crews until the Nicobaren, where Matelieff finally announced the plan and overcame the men's reluctance — "het volk was niet aangenomen om te land te vechten" — with the promise of prize money. The Nicobaren stopover was the principal tactical decision point of the outward voyage and the framework within which the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Malaka campaign of 1606]] was conducted.

## The Portuguese detachment (1606)

After the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|engagement of 18 August 1606]] between the Matelieff fleet and the Portuguese viceroy's relief fleet — an indecisive engagement in which both sides lost two ships and the Portuguese viceroy was killed — Matelieff retired to [[Djohor|Djohor]] to repair his fleet. There he learned "dat de helft der Portugeesche vloot naar de Nicobaren was vertrokken". Returning to Malaka on [[20 October]] [[1606]], the Dutch attacked the remaining seven galleons and three galleys on [[22 October]] [[1606]] and destroyed them; the ships then set course for the Nicobaren "om daar de rest der Portugeesche vloot op te zoeken", but the Portuguese had withdrawn to a safe anchorage north of [[Kedah|Kedah]] in a bay of one of the **Boeton-eilanden**, "waar zij met wind en stroom in haar voordeel veilig was voor een aanval". Matelieff gave up the pursuit.

## Significance

The Nicobaren are, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal transit anchorage of the early Dutch East India fleets on the Atjeh-Djohor-Malaka route and the principal named islands of the Bay of Bengal in the early Dutch-Indies period. The Matelieff fleet's stopover at the Nicobaren in [[1605]] was the principal tactical decision point of the outward voyage and the framework within which the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Malaka campaign of 1606]] was conducted. The Portuguese viceroy's detachment of half his fleet to the Nicobaren after the engagement of [[18 August]] [[1606]] — and Matelieff's subsequent pursuit — was the principal naval episode of the Malaka campaign's aftermath, and frames the Nicobaren's role as a strategic location in the early Dutch-Portuguese naval war in the East Indies. The Nicobaren would continue to serve as a transit anchorage for Dutch and English East India fleets throughout the seventeenth century and beyond.

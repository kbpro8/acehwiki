**Samalanga** is a coastal region of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on the north coast of Sumatra, between [[Banda Acheh|Koetaradja]] and [[Pedirse|Pedir]], mentioned in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] (p. 124) as the locality where, in particular, much *dadih* — curdled buffalo-milk, the most widely consumed buffalo-milk product in northern Sumatra — was made. The Samalanga reference is part of *De Karbouw*'s wider account of buffalo-milking and buffalo-milk consumption in Atjeh, which locates Atjeh within a broader North-Sumatran dairy complex that included the [[Pedirse|Pedir]] region (where goats were also milked), the [[Gajoland|Gajoland]] highlands (where *minjaq sapi* buffalo-milk cream was boiled down to fat for frying *toempi* and *tjoetjoer*), and the [[Klingalese]] and [[Arab|Arab]] communities resident in Atjeh (great lovers of *dadih*, who knew it by their own terms *tairō* and *halib* respectively).

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Samalanga

**Samalanga** is a region on the north coast of Acheh mentioned in *The Achehnese* Vol. I both as the scene of Dutch military operations in the Acheh War and as the geographical designation borne by the influential sayyid preacher **Habib Samalanga**, one of the chief rivals of [[Teungku Tiro]].

### Military Operations in Samalanga

The clearest direct mention of Samalanga as a place in Vol. I is in Snouck Hurgronje's portrait of the **Imeum of Lueng Bata**, the adat-chief who took a zealous part in the defence of the capital at the outbreak of the national war in [[1873]]. Of him Snouck writes: "This Imeum [he died in the year [[1901]] during the military operations in Samalanga] was a rare phenomenon among the dealing with men. Like most of his fellows he sought to be foremost at fights of animals, gambling parties and sanguinary internal forays. At the same time he possessed those qualities whereby an Achehnese may rise to be an uleebalang though not entitled to such a position by his birth." The death of the Imeum in the Samalanga operations in [[1901]] — during the renewed offensive that followed [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Governor Van Heutsz's]] taking of direction in [[1898]] — indicates that Samalanga remained a major theatre of armed resistance into the very last phase of the war.

### Habib Samalanga

The other prominent bearer of the Samalanga name in Vol. I is the sayyid **Habib Samalanga**, one of the principal ulama-rivals of [[Teungku Tiro]] during the "concentration" period. Snouck pairs him with [[Teungku Kutakarang]] as the two men whose jealousy "may at times have thwarted" the great Tiro's purposes. Habib Samalanga, "an active [man]", came to Great Acheh to direct the jihad especially in the [[XXVI Mukims]] and to gather the *kasadi* and other contributions to meet the expenses of the war; "in order to appear in the eyes of his followers as at least the equal and if possible the superior of 'the Teungku', he had to distinguish himself by unusual severity of life." He condemned to isolation in a cave those Achehnese who had submitted to or traded with the infidels, and differed from Teungku Tiro in his opinions on the conduct of the holy war. Habib Samalanga died in December [[1901]] — the same year in which the Imeum of Lueng Bata fell in the Samalanga operations.

### Significance

Although Samalanga as a place is mentioned only briefly in Vol. I, its double appearance — both as a major military theatre of the late Acheh War and as the geographic designation of a leading sayyid war-preacher — marks it as one of the most important centres of Achehnese armed and religious resistance at the turn of the twentieth century. The place is treated more fully in Vol. II of *The Achehnese*.

## Account from *Dispereert Niet Vol. XI* (1946) — Samalanga

**Samalanga** is a region of **[[Aceh]]** on the east coast of **[[Sumatra]]**, notable for its role in the **[[Aceh War]]** and the site of the battle of **[[Batêê Iliê]]** in [[1901]].

### Background

Samalanga was a stronghold of Acehnese resistance, particularly under the leadership of **[[Panglima Polem]]**. Its location made it a key region for controlling the interior highlands.

### The Battle of Batêê Iliê ([[1901]])

Batêê Iliê was a fortified village in Samalanga that had successfully resisted Dutch attacks for twenty years. In [[1901]], **[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]]** personally led an assault on the position. After a hard fight, Batêê Iliê fell, and Samalanga was brought under Dutch control.

### Aftermath

After the battle, Panglima Polem retreated into the interior. He was eventually surrounded and surrendered to **[[Hendrikus Colijn|H. Colijn]]** at **[[Lhokseumawe]]** in [[1903]].

## Samalanga in De Karbouw

The Samalanga passage in *De Karbouw* (p. 124) runs in full:

> *"In Atjeh worden van de huisdieren bijna uitsluitend karbouwen door de bevolking nu en dan gemolken; in het [[Pedirse|Pedirse]] ook geiten. Voor de bereiding van de verschillende soorten van rijstepap (*[[kandji|kandji]]*, een Tamil-woord), die in Atjeh bij bijzondere gelegenheden worden gegeten, wordt buffelmelk of klappermelk gebruikt. Gestremde buffelmelk, ook op N.-Sumatra *[[dadih (Atjeh)|dadih]]* genoemd, wordt bij de Atjehers soms met rijst of pisang, maar bij de Gajo's zelden genuttigd. De Klinganezen en Arabieren in Atjeh zijn er echter grote liefhebbers van; de eersten noemen die *[[tairō|tairō]]*, de laatsten *[[halib|halib]]*. Vooral in [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] wordt veel *dadih* gemaakt."*
The passage locates Samalanga within a layered account of Atjehnese dairy practice: buffaloes are the principal milk-animal (goats additionally in Pedirse), the milk is used in *kandji* rice-porridge (a Tamil loan-word) at ceremonial occasions, the curdled *dadih* is sometimes eaten with rice or banana by Atjehnese (rarely by Gajos), and is especially favoured by the Klingalese (who call it *tairō*) and Arabs (who call it *halib*) of Atjeh — and Samalanga is the locality singled out for the abundance of its *dadih* production.

## Historical context

Samalanga was, in the period of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, one of the most stubbornly resistant districts of the Atjehnese interior, and its name figures prominently in the Dutch military and administrative literature on the pacification of Atjeh. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] treats Samalanga as one of the principal seats of the Atjehnese resistance. The fact that Samalanga was, in *De Karbouw*'s ethnographic present, also a centre of *dadih* production is a small but pointed reminder that the ethnographic and the military-administrative faces of the Dutch encounter with Atjeh were inscribed on the same localities.

## Significance

Samalanga's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is as a documented locality of Atjehnese buffalo-milk consumption — specifically, the place most strongly associated with *dadih* production in the comparative ethnographic literature. It also figures as one of the most stubbornly resistant districts of the Atjehnese interior during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]], a fact that places its ethnographic profile in the same frame as its military-historical profile.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, Samalanga — an Acehnese coastal state on the [[Noordkust]] of [[Atjeh]] — is the *Onderhoorigheden*-statelet at which a Dutch post was established in [[1877]] after heavy fighting, the site of the murder of the Dutch emissary [[Soetan Maharadja]], and the test-case of [[Scherer]]'s *kruisboot-bangoen* method in [[1880]] — the experiment in which the laying of a *kruisboot* (a marine patrol vessel) before Samalanga's harbour brought payment of the *bangoen* (blood-price) within three days, and so gave Scherer the *fingerwijzing* on which the entire *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]* was constructed.

### The 1877 expedition and post establishment

The Dutch post at Samalanga was established in [[1877]] under the *verzoeningspolitiek* (conciliation-politics) of G.-G. van Lansberge — the policy of conciliation in Groot-Atjeh combined with *gewapend optreden* (armed intervention) in the still-hostile Onderhoorigheden. The establishment of the post required hard fighting: Van Heutsz records it in a single phrase in his catalogue of the [[1877]] Onderhoorigheden coercions: *hevig gestreden en een post gevestigd te Samalanga* (heavily fought and a post established at Samalanga). The *hevig gestreden* fixes the Samalanga expedition as one of the harder operations of the [[1877]] sequence, alongside the two expeditions to [[Melaboeh]], the *tuchtiging* of [[Loong]] on the Westkust, the submission of [[Langsar]] and [[Mojopahit]], and the re-expedition to [[Simpang Olim]]. The post was garrisoned as part of the dispersed coastal occupation the [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]]-plan completion had prescribed, and Samalanga thus entered the catalogue of Dutch-administered Onderhoorigheden-posts from which the *controle op handel en scheepvaart* could later be exercised.

### The murder of Soetan Maharadja

The post was the site of the murder of the Dutch emissary Soetan Maharadja — one of the two emissaries (the other being [[Said Abdellah]], murdered at [[Gighen]]) whose deaths triggered Scherer's *kruisboot-bangoen* experiment. Van Heutsz records the episode: *door het gebeurde te Gighen en te Samalanga, waar onze zendelingen Said Abdellah en Soetan Maharadja vermoord waren, en waar men telkens uitvluchten zocht in het betalen van den opgelegden bangoen (bloedprijs)* (through what happened at Gighen and at Samalanga, where our emissaries Said Abdellah and Soetan Maharadja were murdered, and where one constantly sought evasions in the payment of the imposed *bangoen* [blood-price]). The *bangoen* — the blood-price, the Atjehnese customary-law compensation owed for a killing — was the obligation the Samalanga chiefs had incurred by the murder of Soetan Maharadja; the *uitvluchten* — the evasions — were the pretextual delays by which the chiefs had avoided payment.

### The 1880 kruisboot deployment

Scherer's solution was to lay a *kruisboot* — a marine patrol vessel — before the harbour of Samalanga and to collect the *bangoen* by levying duties on the in- and out-going goods. Scherer requested and obtained from [[K. van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] the *vergunning om vóór die havens een kruisboot te leggen en den bangoen te innen door rechten te heffen op de in- en uit te voeren goederen* (permission to lay a cruiser before those harbours and to collect the blood-price by levying duties on the goods imported and exported). The result was immediate: *Dit hielp; te Gighen betaalde men nog vóór dat de kruisboot er lag en te Samalanga, nadat de kruisboot er slechts drie dagen gelegen had* (This worked; at Gighen payment was made even before the cruiser was in place, and at Samalanga after the cruiser had been there only three days). The Samalanga chiefs paid the *bangoen* after the *kruisboot* had lain before their harbour for only three days; the Gighen chiefs paid before the *kruisboot* had even arrived. The experiment was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the *fingerwijzing* on which the entire *scheepvaartregeling* was constructed:

> "Dat was voor Scherer eene vingerwijzing en bracht hem op het denkbeeld, dat als slechts alle in- en uitvoer langs onze vestigingen plaats had onder onze contrôle, wij ook het middel in de hand hadden om de hoofden van de verschillende staatjes in het rechte spoor te houden" (That was for Scherer a finger-post and brought him to the idea, that if only all imports and exports took place through our establishments under our control, we should also have the means in hand to keep the chiefs of the various statelets on the right track).

The 1880 Samalanga test was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the *gewichtige antwoord op de vraag van Pel in zijn brief van 30 April 1875 wat, na voltooiing van zijn plan, moest gedaan worden in ons politiek en handelsbelang* — the weighty answer to the question Pel himself had posed in his letter of [[30 April]] [[1875]]: what, after the completion of his plan, was to be done?

### The 1884 abolition under the Concentratie

The Samalanga post was abolished in [[1884]] under the [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]]. The abolition was part of the general withdrawal from the coastal posts that the Concentratie prescribed: *de betrekkingen van assistent-resident ter Noord- en Oostkust en van controleur der onderafdeeling Samalanga werden ingetrokken tegelijk met de opheffing der vestigingen op laatstgenoemde plaats en te Telok Semawé* (the positions of assistant-resident on the Noord- en Oostkust and of *controleur* of the onderafdeeling Samalanga were withdrawn at the same time as the lifting of the establishments at the latter place and at Telok Semawé). The abolition of the post and of the *controleur der onderafdeeling Samalanga* was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the operational emblem of the Concentratie's self-defeating character: the withdrawal from the coastal posts removed the very instruments through which the *scheepvaartregeling* could have been administered. Under the [[Demmeni]] *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]], the three call-ports of the Noord- en Oostkust — *alleen Edi, Segli en Oleh-leh voor de geheele Noord- en Oostkust* (only Edi, Segli and Oleh-leh for the entire Noord- en Oostkust) — were fixed in place of the abolished posts, and Samalanga was thus formally excluded from the open-port system the *scheepvaartregeling* required.

### Significance (Van Heutsz account)

Samalanga's significance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract rests on its being the empirical origin of the *scheepvaartregeling* prescription — the place whose 1880 *kruisboot* test gave Scherer the *fingerwijzing* that became the central prescription of the entire tract; the Noordkust statelet at which a Dutch post was established in [[1877]] after heavy fighting as part of the *verzoeningspolitiek*-era occupation of the Onderhoorigheden; the site of the murder of the Dutch emissary Soetan Maharadja, whose *bangoen* (blood-price) the Samalanga chiefs evaded by *uitvluchten*; and the post abolished in [[1884]] under the Concentratie — the operational emblem of the Concentratie's self-defeating withdrawal from the coastal posts through which the *scheepvaartregeling* could have been administered.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

In June [[1913]] a fourteen-year-old boy armed with a *[[rentjong]]* forced his way into the canteen at Samalanga and attacked the station master who happened to be staying there — one of the [[Atjeh-moord|Atjeh murders]]. The *zelfbestuurder* [[T. Tjhi' Moehammad]] of Samalanga was the eighth signatory of the [[Letter of the Acehnese Chiefs to General Swart (26 September 1933)|letter of 1933]].

**Tandjong Priok** (modern Tanjung Priok) was the great harbour works constructed by the Dutch state on the eastern side of the bay of [[Batavia|Batavia]], at about 8 kilometres' distance from the chief place, in the interest of the trade of Batavia. The construction was begun in [[1876]] after a long series of investigations and a debate between an Indies commission and a Netherlands commission, and was carried out by the state in its own management according to a working-plan of the chief engineer [[Waldorp|Waldorp]], under the direction of the engineer 1st class of the Indies waterstaats-corps [[J. A. de Gelder|J. A. de Gelder]]. The works — including a voorhaven enclosed between two sea-heads together 3728 metres long, a binnenhaven 1300 metres long and 175 metres broad, two loskaden together 1388 metres long, an 8-km railway with double track to Batavia, a 15-metre-wide rijweg, and a shipping canal with jaagpad — were estimated to cost more than 19 million guilders and to be completed within nine years.

## Background

In the early [[1870s]] there were no proper harbours in Netherlands India. "Actual harbours, as these are understood to mean enclosed basins where ships lie in still water and can unload and load immediately at the shore, do not as yet exist in Indië. Everywhere loading and unloading takes place by means of praus, in which the goods are loaded and which, in most places, by river-mouths or by specially dug salt-water canals between heads, maintain communication with the shore." Especially since the important extension of steam navigation to Indië, the necessity of obtaining a proper seaport at Batavia made itself more and more felt. By the budget for [[1873]] moneys were allowed for an investigation, which finally gave the preference to a harbour at Tandjong Priok, eastward in the bay of Batavia.

## The Commissions

The Indies commission, appointed to examine the various harbour plans, considered Tandjong Priok too far from the capital and gave, in its majority, the preference to the plan of the heer Maarschalk for the construction of a harbour 1200 to 1500 metres east of the existing harbour canal. The Netherlands commission, consisting of the heeren [[Caland|Caland]], inspector of the waterstaat, [[Waldorp|Waldorp]], chief engineer, and [[Blommendal|Blommendal]], captain-ter-sea titular, chief of the hydrography, in whose hands the report of the Indies commission of [[15 July]] [[15 July]] [[1874]] was placed, gave in its report of [[23 March]] [[23 March]] [[1875]] its seal to the opinion of the minority of the Indies commission, that only the construction of a harbour at Tandjong Priok deserved recommendation. The government agreed with the latter opinion, and by the budget for [[1876]] the first moneys for the great work were set aside.

## The Construction

As the auction did not succeed, the work was undertaken in its own management by the state according to a working-plan of the chief engineer Waldorp, under the direction of the engineer 1st class of the Indies waterstaats-corps J. A. de Gelder, assisted by two other engineers, who had first studied the great harbour works in Europe. The main points of the plan were approximately: "At Tandjong Priok a voorhaven shall have to be constructed, enclosed between two sea-heads, which together are 3728 metres long; further a binnenhaven 1300 metres long and 175 metres broad, with two loskaden, which together will be 1388 metres long. The harbour terrain shall have to be provided with the necessary establishments, as entrepôts, loskranen, railways, etc. Between the harbour and the chief place Batavia there shall have to be constructed a railway of almost 8 km with double track, a rijway — 15 metres broad — and a shipping canal with jaagpad."

The enormous quantity of natural stone required for the work was to be taken from the trachyte cliffs at [[Merak|Merak]], on the Strait of Soenda. Stone-exploitation was carried out by compressed-air drills, the borings charged with dynamite or lithofracteur, the stones transported by steam-cranes to hopper-barges, which brought them to Tandjong Priok, where they were dumped by opening valves in the bottom of the barges. The dredging in the voorhaven was to be done with steam dredging-mills of particularly great capacity, also used to clear the coral reefs. The binnenhaven at Tandjong Priok was to be entirely dug out of the coast. Where previously coconut gardens were found, at a height of 2 metres above low water, in a few years great sea-going ships would have to enter to unload and load along a masonry quay.

## The Progress

During the execution modifications in the working-plan proved necessary here and there. The rock-borers at Merak fell short, and large galleries were begun to undermine the rocks and bring about cave-ins on a large scale. It proved difficult always to find the necessary working personnel; it was decided to replace the concrete blocks with cheaper and harder trachyte blocks; a favourable circumstance was that, unexpectedly, ample use could be made of sand-dumping. "When the experiments were made, it was expected that the sand, suddenly dumped in quantities of 100 to 300 m³, would drive the mud sideways and take its place. It was found, however, that the sand did not drive away the mud, but mixed with it, and that thereby a particularly hard ground was formed, which in many places could not be pierced with iron sounding instruments and gave a clearly audible sound when the lead struck it. The sand-dumpings have therefore been continued, and as a precaution they have been enclosed between trachyte dams."

By the end of [[1880]] the work was already so far advanced "that the steamship *Koning der Nederlanden* of the [[Stoomvaartmaatschappij Nederland|stoomvaartmaatschappij Nederland]] — 105 metres long — with a draught of 23½ feet, on the return voyage from Batavia to Amsterdam, on [[17 December]] [[17 December]] could anchor at the entrance of the binnenhaven. On [[11 April]] [[11 April]] [1881], on arrival from the Netherlands, the same ship with full cargo again dropped anchor at Tandjong Priok and could without the least delay pass from the buitenhaven to the binnenhaven, where the passengers found opportunity to step ashore over a gangway."

## Significance

The construction of Tandjong Priok was one of the principal public works of the Netherlands-Indian government in the [[1870s]], and was closely connected with the introduction of the [[personeele belasting|personeele belasting]] and [[patentrecht|patentrecht]] for Europeans and foreign Orientals in [[1879]] — one of the principal grounds for the defence of which was that the great public works undertaken by the state in this period came for a great part to the benefit of the Europeans established in Indië.

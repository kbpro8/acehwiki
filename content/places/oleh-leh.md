**Oleh-leh** (also written Olehleh, modern Uleelheue) was the principal landing-place and port of the Dutch in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], situated on the coast west of the mouth of the [[Aceh River|Atjeh river]]. It became the principal post on the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]] after its occupation in [[May 1874|May-June 1874]], and the western terminus of the railway to [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] completed in [[November 1876|November 1876]]. The reede of Oleh-leh, especially during the west monsoon, offered many advantages over that of the mouth of the Atjeh river, and the Dutch accordingly made it the principal landing and loading place for the garrison of Kota Radja.

## Occupation and Development

When in [[May 1874|May-June 1874]] the Dutch occupied the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]] — a series of posts west of Kota Radja to connect it with [[Marassa|Marassa]] and [[Lampassei|Lampassei]] — the principal post was Oleh-leh on the coast west of the mouth of the Atjeh river. As the reede of Oleh-leh, especially during the west monsoon, proved to offer many advantages over that of the mouth of the Atjeh river, it was made suitable for unloading and loading by the construction of a temporary bamboo sea-head, replaced on [[11 August]] [[11 August]] [[1874]] by a wooden auxiliary head built by the engineers.

For the better preservation of the connection with the Atjeh river and to defeat the enemy's attempts to cut the Dutch off from it, the [[Noorderlinie|Noorderlinie]] was also strengthened by the establishment of posts at [[Lamara|Lamara]] and Kota Radja Bedil. In [[July 1874|July 1874]], in order to maintain communication with Oleh-leh, [[Soerian|Soerian]] was occupied after a fierce fight in which a first storming had failed; in [[September 1874|September 1874]] [[Langkroek|Langkroek]] was taken to better defend the right bank of the Atjeh river; in [[November 1874|November 1874]] the kampong [[Lemboe|Lemboe]] was taken and a fortification was set up at [[Kota-Alam|Kota-Alam]] to secure the east side of Kota Radja and to ensure safe navigation on the Atjeh river.

## The Iron Screw-Pile Sea-Head and the Railway

By Indies decree of [[12 April]] [[12 April]] [[1875]] a commission was appointed to investigate locally the causes of the many and serious cases of illness that occurred in Atjeh and to indicate the means by which improvements might be brought about. To the proposals made in its report of [[1 July]] [[1 July]] partial effect was given, and thereby once again ample work was provided for the engineers. They had of course also to provide for the putting in order of the various posts and for the works at the sea-head on iron screw-piles at Oleh-leh, which was completed in [[September 1876|September 1876]], and at the railway between Oleh-leh and Kota Radja, which was completed in [[November 1876|November 1876]].

## Van Lansberge's Inspection, March 1877

When the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] arrived at Oleh-leh on [[14 March]] [[14 March]] [[1877]] for his [[Van Lansberge's visit to Aceh (March-April 1877)|inspection of the theatre of war]], he found: "On the roadstead of Oleh-leh there prevailed, in consequence of the number of ships lying there, great activity; on shore the high visitor was struck by the neat appearance of houses and streets, the efficient lighting of these, and the influx of Atjehers on the pasar."

## In the Hikajat prang Kompeuni

In the [[Hikajat Prang Kompeuni|Hikajat Prang Kompeuni]] Oleh-leh appears alongside [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] as the commercial centre of the Dutch establishment: "The Radja Moeda, [[Toekoe Nja Moekamat|Toekoe Nja Moekamat]], strained every effort to bring the chief place and Oleh-leh to prosperity, and succeeded so well, that the population fled from their villages in streams not to be stemmed to the chief place, and fraternised with the kafirs. One lived as in continual festivity, drove profitable trade, and the war-leaders could find no people to follow them."

## Account from *De Kleine Oosterling* (1901) — the haven and the spoorweg

The Dutch primary-school reader *De Kleine Oosterling* records Oleh-leh as the Dutch-built haven at the mouth of the Atjeh-rivier, calling it "onze haven op kust van Atjeh, door ons aangelegd" (our harbour on the coast of Atjeh, built by us). The reader describes the haven's bustling activity — steamships, praus ferrying goods to shore, steam cranes at the pier, rows of houses, toko's, and officers, soldiers, natives and coolies moving among the buildings and warehouses — and records the spoorweg connecting Oleh-leh to [[Kota Radja|Kotta-Radja]], with trains running several times daily in each direction. In the school-textbook narrative, Oleh-leh is presented as the principal instance of the Dutch colonial state's constructive (*opbouwende*) activity in Atjeh, framed against the *fanatiek* resistance of the Atjeh-oorlog.

## Account from *ATJEH '96* (Kruisheer, 1913) — the 1896 naval base

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Oleh-leh was the coastal port near [[Koeta Radja]] at which [[Dutch]] troops and reinforcements landed, at which warships anchored "ter reede van Oleh-leh" (in the roadstead of Oleh-leh), and from which the [[Marine|marine]] operated throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] the port was defended by the *marineschepen* (marine ships) *Sumbawa* and *Tromp* on the west side and *Flores* on the east side. A 3.7 cm quick-firing gun was placed on the spit in the south-west bastion; 2 officers and 52 men of the *landingsdivisie* (landing division) reinforced the garrison; and the marine placed a 7 cm A. battery near the *lagune* (lagoon) behind the *plaatsbureau* (local office) on [[31 March]] [[1896]]. Oleh-leh was the embarkation point for [[G. A. Hansen|Hansen]]'s [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing column of 22 June 1896]].

### Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

Oleh-leh lay on the coast immediately to the west of [[Koeta Radja]], and formed the seaward anchor of the Dutch defensive perimeter. The port was sheltered by the off-lying islands and the [[Sabang-baai Poeloe Weh|Poeloe Weh]] coaling station some distance to the north. The roadstead ("reede van Oleh-leh") was the anchorage of the Dutch warships operating on the [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] station. A *lagune* behind the local office provided a secondary anchorage and the site of the marine 7 cm A. battery. A spit in the south-west bastion was the site of the 3.7 cm *snelvuurkanon* (quick-firing gun). The [[Controleur te Oleh-leh]] was a civil administrative officer mentioned in [[Teuku Umar]]'s letters.

### Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

Oleh-leh had been the principal landing-place of the [[Dutch]] expeditions against [[Atjeh]] since [[1873]], and had been fortified as the seaward anchor of the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcenteerde Linie (Concentrated Line)]] from [[1884]] onward. The [[Marine|marine]] maintained a permanent presence at Oleh-leh, and the roadstead was the station of the Dutch naval squadron supporting military operations ashore. The *controleur* at Oleh-leh served as the civil administrative officer for the coastal region. After [[1893]] the policy of arming [[Teuku Umar]] as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] had been administered in part through the *controleur* at Oleh-leh.

### Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

Oleh-leh was the centre of the [[Dutch]] naval response to the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. On [[31 March]] [[1896]] the [[Marine|marine]] placed a 7 cm A. battery near the *lagune* behind the *plaatsbureau*; a 3.7 cm *snelvuurkanon* was placed on the *landtong* (spit) in the south-west bastion, and 2 officers and 52 men of the *landingsdivisie* reinforced the garrison. The *marineschepen* *Sumbawa* and *Tromp* on the west side and *Flores* on the east side defended the port. From [[1 May]] to [[4 May]] [[1896]] the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Raba bay]] was conducted by warships operating from Oleh-leh. On [[22 June]] [[1896]] the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing column]] under [[G. A. Hansen|Hansen]] embarked at Oleh-leh aboard the [[H. M. Merapi|H. M. Merapi]], the *Gouvernementsstoomer Condor*, and the *Albatros*, and sailed for [[Ladoeng]]. After [[12 May]] [[1896]] a detachment of *mariniers* (marines) was posted at Oleh-leh as a standing garrison.

### Significance (ATJEH '96 account)

Oleh-leh was the indispensable naval and logistical base of the [[Dutch]] enterprise in [[Atjeh]]. Its seaward defences, reinforced immediately after the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], were a prerequisite for the dispatch of reinforcements from [[Java]] and for the conduct of the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine bombardments of Lepong]] and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]]. The embarkation of the Hansen column at Oleh-leh on [[22 June]] [[1896]] demonstrated the port's continuing strategic importance at the end of the first semester of [[1896]].

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, Oleh-leh — the Dutch-controlled port at the mouth of the [[Atjeh]]-river beside the Dutch colonial capital of [[Kota Radja]] — is the operational pivot of every Dutch attempt to control Groot-Atjeh's maritime trade, and, with [[Edi]] on the [[Oostkust]], one of the two havens exempted from [[K. van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]]'s unilateral closures of [[1878]] and [[5 July]] [[1879]] of all the havens of *Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*.

### The Dutch-controlled outlet and the rice-bins irony

Oleh-leh stood at the mouth of the Atjeh River on the coastal fringe of the [[Groot-Atjeh|vallei van Groot-Atjeh]], beside the Dutch colonial seat of [[Kota Radja]]. Its situation made it the natural maritime outlet of the Dutch *geconcentreerde stelling* — the concentrated defensive perimeter established by [[Jan van Swieten|Generaal van Swieten]] in [[1874]] — and the seat of a *civiel bestuur* (civil administration) through which the in- and out-flow of goods at Groot-Atjeh's river-mouth was administered. The great irony of the [[1876]]–[[1877]] Groot-Atjeh closure — and the lesson Van Heutsz repeatedly draws from it — was that Oleh-leh, the Dutch-administered outlet, had become the very place where the inland enemy stored its rice. Van Heutsz records the scene as the *klassieke* exhibit of the failure of a one-sided closure of Groot-Atjeh:

> "dat in 1876, (wie van hen, die destijds te Atjeh waren, herinnert het zich niet?) groote hoeveelheden rijst met en zonder passen van het civiele bestuur van Oleh-leh door de VI en IV Moekims naar de XXII en XXVI Moekims gingen, zoodat zelfs gezegd werd, dat de vijand zijne rijstschuren bij ons te Oleh-leh had" (that in 1876 (who of those who were then in Atjeh does not remember it?) great quantities of rice, with and without passes from the civil administration of Oleh-leh, went through the VI and IV Moekims to the XXII and XXVI Moekims, so that it was even said that the enemy had his rice-bins at our Oleh-leh).

The enemy's rice-bins stood at the Dutch administration's own port — a paradox that converted the closure from a coercive instrument into a Dutch-administered supply line. The lesson Van Heutsz drew was that the closure of Groot-Atjeh alone was useless unless extended across the [[Onderhoorigheden]]: only a general closure of the entire ring of coastal statelets, combined with the controlled *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]* at the Dutch-administered ports, could convert Oleh-leh from the enemy's rice-bin into the Dutch cash-box.

### The 1878 and 1879 exemptions

Van der Heijden's two unilateral closures of the Atjeh coast were undertaken against the Gouverneur-Generaal's expressed wishes and at the General's *persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* (personal responsibility). The first came in [[1878]], after the *inval* (incursion) of [[Habib Abdoel Rachman]] had forced the resumption of the campaign in the [[XXII Moekims]]:

> "Reeds in 1878, na hervatting van de krijgsoperatiën in de XXII Moekims, liet hij daarom alle havens van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, met uitzondering van Olehleh en Edi, voor den invoerhandel sluiten" (Already in 1878, after the resumption of military operations in the XXII Moekims, he therefore closed all havens of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden, with the exception of Olehleh and Edi, to the import trade).

The closure was revoked by Gouverneur-Generaal [[Van Lansberge]] by letter of [[7 February]] [[1879]] — the G.-G. *wilde dezen, eigenmachtig genomen wijzen maatregel niet goedkeuren, gelastte daarom bij brief van 7 Februari 1879 dien maatregel onverwijld in te trekken* (would not approve this measure taken on his own authority, and therefore ordered by letter of 7 February 1879 that the measure be withdrawn forthwith). The second closure, imposed on Van der Heijden's *persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* by telegram of [[5 July]] [[1879]], again exempted Oleh-leh and Edi. The two exemptions fixed Oleh-leh, with Edi, as one of the two open ports of the entire Atjehnese rijk during the Van der Heijden campaigns. The exemption was the operational precedent for the systematic *scheepvaartregeling* of May [[1883]] and for Van Heutsz's own [[1893]] prescription of open ports at Oleh-leh and [[Sabang|Poeloe Waij]].

### The 1884 partial closure: one of three open call-ports

Oleh-leh's role as a Dutch-administered open port was confirmed by the [[Demmeni|General Demmeni]] *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]], which established the three call-ports through which all foreign trade with the *Noord- en Oostkust* had to pass. Van Heutsz records the arrangement: *alleen Edi, Segli en Oleh-leh voor de geheele Noord- en Oostkust* (only Edi, Segli, and Oleh-leh, for the entire Noord- en Oostkust). The three call-ports were the operational core of Demmeni's hybrid scheme — a *partieele blokkade* (partial blockade) combined with a *soort scheepvaartregeling* (a kind of shipping-regulation) — under which all foreign ships had to call first at one of the three before proceeding to any of the not-closed points of the coast. The scheme was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the worst of both worlds — *geen rigoureuse blokkade* (no rigorous blockade) but also *geen echte scheepvaartregeling* (no true shipping-regulation) — but Oleh-leh's role as the Groot-Atjeh call-port survived.

### The 1891 high-water mark: Oleh-leh as the coerced outlet

The high-water mark of [[Van Teijn]]'s closure-policy, reached in the beginning of [[1891]], was that the Noordkust chiefs themselves were begging to be allowed to deliver their accumulated produce to Oleh-leh. Van Heutsz records the effect:

> "van verschillende zijden allerlei dringende voorstellen en smeekbeden kwamen, zooals b. v. van de Noordkust om al wat daar aan producten opgestapeld lag aan ons te Oleh-leh te mogen afleveren" (from various sides all kinds of urgent proposals and supplications came, such as for example from the Noordkust to be allowed to deliver at our Oleh-leh all that was stacked up there in the way of products).

The Noordkust's *dringende voorstellen en smeekbeden* — its urgent proposals and supplications to deliver its produce at Oleh-leh — was the empirical proof of the closure's coercive power: by closing the entire ring of *Onderhoorigheden* and leaving Oleh-leh as the only Dutch-administered outlet, Van Teijn had converted Oleh-leh from the enemy's rice-bin (the 1876 irony) into the Dutch-administered produce-receiving port for the entire closed coast. The same operational lesson recurred in [[1890]] when *T. Moeda Rajoet* of the [[XXVI Moekims]] *verzocht te mogen uitvoeren via Oleh-leh* — requested permission to export via Oleh-leh — and offered to pay *f 10000 contanten* and free Dutch march-through the XXVI Moekims in exchange.

### The 1893 prescription: Oleh-leh as one of two free ports

In Van Heutsz's own [[1893]] prescription, Oleh-leh was to be one of only two *vrijhavens* (free ports) of the entire rijk. The Van Teijn prescription that Van Heutsz endorses had been *Zooals de stand van zaken nu is, achtte de Generaal van Teijn het noodig dat Oleh-leh, behalve voor den invoer van opium, eene vrijhaven wordt* (As things now stand, General van Teijn considered it necessary that Oleh-leh, except for the import of opium, become a free port) — and Van Heutsz concurred: *Naar mijn oordeel heeft de Generaal gelijk en moet Oleh-leh een vrijhaven blijven* (In my judgement the General is right and Oleh-leh must remain a free port). The free-port status was, in Van Heutsz's argument, the operational counterpart to the closure of the rest: with the entire ring of *Onderhoorigheden* under *scheepvaartregeling* and the closed coast under *sluiting*, Oleh-leh would become *het middelpunt … waarom alles draait* — the centre around which everything turned — for the entire *Groot-Atjeh* trade. Should the marine-station plan for [[Sabang|Poeloe Waij]] be realised, Oleh-leh would share with Waij the singular status of the only two free ports of the entire Atjehnese rijk: *moet dan niet alleen die vestiging op Waij eene vrijhaven zijn, maar met Oleh-leh de eenige vrijhaven van het geheele gebied* (not only must that establishment on Waij be a free port, but with Oleh-leh the only free port of the entire territory).

### Significance (Van Heutsz account)

Oleh-leh's significance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract rests on its being the operational pivot of every Dutch closure-and-scheepvaartregeling scheme — the Dutch-controlled outlet at the mouth of the Atjeh River beside the colonial seat; one of the two havens (with [[Edi]]) exempted from Van der Heijden's two unilateral closures of [[1878]] and [[5 July]] [[1879]]; the place whose 1876 rice-trade exposed the operational irony *de vijand zijne rijstschuren bij ons te Oleh-leh had* — the lesson of which became the central prescription of the [[1893]] tract; and the Groot-Atjeh-side *vrijhaven* that, together with the proposed marine station on [[Sabang|Poeloe Waij]], was to be one of the only two free ports of the entire Atjehnese rijk under the *scheepvaartregeling* Van Heutsz would himself implement as Governor of Atjeh from [[1898]] onward.

## Nineteenth-Century Historiography & Campaign Analysis

## Fortifications and Capture

Prior to [[1873]], the port was defended by coastal bentengs ([[Coastal Fortifications in Aceh]]) armed with munitions imported from [[Penang]]. Captured by the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in December [[1873]], Oleh-leh became the principal maritime port of disembarkation for Dutch troop reinforcements and hospital ships.

## Logistical and Medical Center

Under General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] in [[1874]] and [[1875]], Oleh-leh was linked via supply road to [[Sinangri]] and [[Pekan Badak]]. The [[Medical Service in Aceh]] constructed major sanitary hospital sheds at Oleh-leh to evacuate cholera patients away from the overcrowded [[Kraton of Aceh]] ([[Field Hospital and Cholera Epidemics in Aceh]]).

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Oleh-leh** was the port of [[Kota Radja]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], mentioned by [[G. B. Hooijer]] as the place where Japanese women had established themselves, referred to in a jocular inscription painted inside the *benteng* of [[Kroeng Gloempang]].

## The inscription at Kroeng Gloempang

Reading the soldiers' facetiousness from the plate of the interior of [[Kroeng Gloempang]], Hooijer explained that the inscription *Japan 5 gulden een flesch portwijn* — "Japan, five guilders a bottle of port wine" — referred to the dwelling-place of *eenige, weinig preutsche, maar zeer inhalige Japansche schoonheden te Oleh-leh gevestigd*: some Japanese women, little prudish but very grasping, established at Oleh-leh. The neighbouring inscription *[[Heerengracht (barracks passage at Kroeng Gloempang)|Heerengracht]]*, at the end of the long passage between the barracks, indicated the officers' quarters.

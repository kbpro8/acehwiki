**Boekit Daroe** was the kampong about an hour's walk south of [[Ketapang Doewa]] in the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] sector and the site of fierce fighting on [[28 January]] [[1876]] in the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|third phase of Pel's winter offensive]]. Its capture opened the [[Glitaroem]] *bergpas* (mountain pass) connecting the [[IV Moekims|IV]] and IX Moekims, and with it the way into the IX Moekims itself.

## The battle of 28 January 1876

The invasion of the IX Moekims began on [[28 January]] [[1876]] as the third phase of Pel's offensive, after the [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] invasions of late December [[1875]] and early January [[1876]]. At Boekit Daroe — about an hour south of Ketapang Doewa — *vinnig gestreden* (sharply fought), and the enemy suffered a *gevoelig verlies* (sensitive loss). The fight was, Borel notes, the hardest the Atjehneezen had offered since the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] on [[7 November]] [[1874]] — proof that the enemy could still concentrate a strong defence at a chosen point even after the loss of the VI and IV Moekims. The Dutch losses at Boekit Daroe are not given in detail by Borel, but the *gevoelig verlies* of the enemy is emphasised as evidence of the seriousness of the fight.

## The Glitaroem pass

With Boekit Daroe in Dutch hands, the [[Glitaroem|bergpas Glitaroem]], which gave access between the IV and IX Moekims, fell into Dutch hands the same day. The pass was, Borel stresses, the operational key to the IX Moekims: as long as the Atjehneezen held it, they could move reinforcements between the IV and IX Moekims at will; once the Dutch held it, the IX Moekims was isolated from the IV. The troepen then moved eastward, taking Lampermej and Oeleh Soesoe on [[31 January]] [[1876]]. At Oeleh Soesoe the enemy offered *hevigen tegenstand* but had to *zwichten*; several kampongs were burned. In the first days of February Lamkoenjit, Toeram and Beloel were also conquered, and the IX Moekims was fully in Dutch hands. From Toeram, which bordered the gebied of [[Toekoe Bait]] of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the Dutch made an *inval* (inroad) into that *sagi* on [[3 February]] [[1876]], driving the enemy out of [[Atoeh]] after a *heeten strijd*.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Boekit Daroe** was a hill and Dutch post west of [[Tjot Goeë]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], whose garrison was transferred to [[Biloel]] on [[20 May]] [[1894]] — the occasion on which the road along and over the bare hills of the [[IX Moekims]] was constructed.

## Transfer of the garrison

Hooijer recorded that the road shown on the opening plate, running along and over the bare hills of the [[IX Moekims]], was laid out when *de bezetting van Boekit Daroe, ten Westen van Tjot Goe, den 20sten Mei 1894 naar Biloel werd verlegd* — when the garrison of Boekit Daroe, west of Tjot Goeë, was transferred to Biloel on [[20 May]] [[1894]].

## Under fire in 1896

Among the signs that the *[[Moslemin]]* still needed a lesson, Hooijer listed that *onze post op Boekit Daroe werd aanhoudend beschoten* — the Dutch post on Boekit Daroe was continuously fired upon — at the same period in which [[Toekoe Moesa Anaq Batee]] attacked the *passer*-goers between [[Lam Barih|Lambarih]] and [[Anagaloeng]] and the population of the southern [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] took flight.

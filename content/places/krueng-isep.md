**Krueng Isep** (also *Kr. Isep*, modern *Krueng Isep*) is a river of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] at whose lead-zinc occurrence the highest-grade zinc ore of the colonial Atjeh mineral record was assayed. The Krueng Isep locality is recorded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) in the *Lead and Zinc in Sumatra* chapter, with an assay of 65.13 % Pb, 8.30 % Zn, 0.89 % Fe, 0.94 % CaO, 0.94 % SiO₂, and **680 gr/t Ag** — the highest zinc content of any Atjeh lead-zinc occurrence recorded in the colonial geological literature.

## The assay

Van Bemmelen's Table for the Atjeh lead-zinc occurrences records the Krueng Isep assay as follows (2nd sample, 500 kilogr.):

| Component | Percentage |
|-----------|------------|
| Pb | 65.13 % |
| Zn | 8.30 % |
| S | 10.2 % |
| Fe | 0.89 % |
| CaO | 0.94 % |
| SiO₂ | 0.94 % |
| Ag | 680 gr/ton |

The 8.30 % Zn content is the highest zinc grade recorded for any Atjeh lead-zinc locality, and the 10.2 % sulphur content indicates that the zinc is present as sphalerite (ZnS) rather than as a carbonate or silicate. The 500-kilogram sample size (in contrast to the "small" sample from [[Krueng Heureueng]]) indicates that the Krueng Isep assay is based on a more substantial bulk sample, and the actual tonnage and average grade of the occurrence are accordingly better constrained than those of the Krueng Heureueng occurrence.

## Place in the Atjeh lead-zinc record

For the wider context of the Atjeh lead-zinc occurrences see the entry on [[Krueng Heureueng]]. The Krueng Isep locality is the second of the two Atjeh lead-zinc occurrences recorded in the colonial geological literature, and its higher Zn grade (8.30 % vs 2.2 %) and lower Pb grade (65.13 % vs 76.1 %) place it at the zinc-rich end of the paragenetic zonation inferred for the Atjeh lead-zinc province.

## Significance

Krueng Isep is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the site of the highest-grade zinc ore of the colonial Atjeh mineral record — 8.30 % Zn with 680 gr/ton Ag — and accordingly the most attractive zinc-rich lead-zinc prospect recorded in the colonial geological literature on Atjeh. The 500-kilogram sample size also makes the Krueng Isep assay one of the more reliable colonial-era assays for an Atjeh mineral occurrence, since it is based on a substantial bulk sample rather than on a hand-specimen. The fact that the occurrence was never developed is, like the non-development of the other Atjeh mineral occurrences, evidence of the colonial state's unwillingness or inability to translate geological occurrences into productive mines.

**Lambeusong** — also written *Lambeusung* — is a coastal settlement on the [[Westkust|Westkust]] of [[Atjeh|Atjèh], the place where, in [[1884]], the Atjèhse chieftain [[Teuku Umar|Teukoe Oema]] — after being conveyed by the warship *[[Benkoelen|Benkoelen]]* from [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]] to [[Rigaih|Rigaih]] and thence to Lambeusong — massacred the armed sloop of the *Benkoelen* that had brought him and his companions ashore. The Lambeusong massacre was one of the two principal acts of Toekoe Oema's pre-submission career (the other being the [[June 1886|June]] [[1886]] capture of the *[[Hok Canton|Hok Canton]]* at Rigaih), and one of the warning signs that the Dutch would later disregard when they armed him as their principal Atjèhse auxiliary in the [[Pahlawan-klucht|Pahlawan-klucht]].

## The 1884 Massacre

In [[1884]] — before his submission to the Dutch — Toekoe Oema was conveyed by the warship *Benkoelen* from Meulaboh to Rigaih and thence to Lambeusong. At Lambeusong, after the *Benkoelen* had brought Toekoe Oema and his companions ashore, Toekoe Oema massacred the armed sloop of the *Benkoelen* that had brought them.

The massacre — the killing of the crew of a Dutch warship's sloop by the chieftain whom the warship had just conveyed — was one of the most brazen acts of Atjèhse aggression against the Dutch in the period of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]]. The Dutch, however, did not retaliate; and Toekoe Oema, in his later account — when he again feigned good disposition and offered submission — dismissed the massacre as the deed of his youth, done in thoughtlessness.

## Significance

Lambeusong is the Westkust coastal settlement that was the site of the [[1884]] massacre of the armed sloop of the *Benkoelen* by Toekoe Oema. The massacre was one of the two principal acts of Toekoe Oema's pre-submission career, and one of the principal warning signs that the Dutch would later disregard when they armed him as their principal Atjèhse auxiliary — and that the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] would expose as the warning signs that the Dutch had refused to recognise.

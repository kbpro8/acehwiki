**Bukit Durian** is one of the three coal concessions (together with [[Bukit Pulai]] and [[Sarik]]) granted to the [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] in [[1909]] in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra (immediately to the south of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] border). The Bukit Durian concession is one of the only documented colonial-era coal-mining concessions on Atjeh-relevant territory, and its modest production — 2,000 tons over the decade [[1917]]–[[1927]], shared with the Bukit Pulai and Sarik concessions and entirely for use at the company's own [[Salida]] gold mine — represents the entire recorded colonial coal production for the West-Atjeh/Painan coal zone. The Bukit Durian mining rights were returned to the Government in [[1934]] following the closure of the Salida gold mine in [[1927]].

## The 1909 Kinandam-Sumatra mining lease

For the full account of the [[1909]] Kinandam-Sumatra mining leases see the entry on [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]]. In summary: in [[1909]] mining leases were granted to the Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij. for the three concessions Bukit Durian, Bukit Pulai, and Sarik in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra. The coal occurrences in these concessions are described by Van Bemmelen as "all of minor importance as the coal seams, occurring in sandstone and clay shale, and ranging from a few centimeters up to 2 m in thickness, generally contain impure coal of a poor quality (soft with a tendency to crumble). Moreover, the extension of the seams is not sufficient to justify a detailed exploration."

## The 1917-1927 production and 1934 return

For the full account of the [[1917]]–[[1927]] production and the [[1934]] return of the mining rights see the entries on [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] and [[Salida]]. In summary: during the years [[1917]]–[[1927]] a total quantity of 2,000 tons was produced (from the three concessions combined) for use at the Salida gold mine. When the Salida gold mine was closed down in [[1927]], the mining rights for the Bukit Durian and Bukit Pulai concessions were returned to the Government in [[1934]] (with the Sarik concession following in [[1941]]).

## Significance

Bukit Durian is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is one of the only documented colonial-era coal-mining concessions on Atjeh-relevant territory — the [[1909]] Kinandam-Sumatra mining lease at Bukit Durian in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra (immediately to the south of the Atjeh border). The Bukit Durian concession's modest production — a share of the 2,000 tons produced over the decade [[1917]]–[[1927]] for use at the Salida gold mine — represents part of the entire recorded colonial coal production for the West-Atjeh/Painan coal zone, and the [[1934]] return of the Bukit Durian mining rights to the Government marks the formal dissolution of the colonial coal-mining industry in the West-Atjeh/Painan region (with the Sarik concession following in [[1941]]).

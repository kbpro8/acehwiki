**Modjopahit** was a landscape of the East Coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] which submitted with [[Langsa]] in the pacification of the Dependencies of [[1877]], where pepper planters from Groot-Atjeh established valuable gardens, and which in [[1881]]–[[1882]] was so scourged by robber bands that only the timely action of the acting assistant-resident kept the planters from abandoning the country.

## Submission and the pepper gardens

The submissions of [[Simpang Oelèm]] and [[Tandjong Seumantoh]], of [[Langsa]] and Modjopahit, of [[Rigaih]] and adjacent landscapes, gave occasion to expect that Netherlands trade would profit from relations with the principal Atjehnese pepper harbours. After [[1881]] the robberies by Atjehnese bands in Modjopahit and Langsa increased so much, however, that the pepper planters from Groot-Atjeh established there were minded to leave the country, abandoning their costly plantations and dwelling-houses. The acting assistant-resident came in time to the spot to prevent this: on his instigation and under his leadership the robbers, after a severe chastisement by the population, were driven from the landscape.

## Government support

The threatened cultures, principally those in Modjopahit, were otherwise supported by the government as well: in March [[1882]] the Netherlands Government placed a sum of 500 dollars, from the yield of the *hacil* (see [[Taxation in Atjeh]]), at the disposal of the pepper plantations which had suffered most from the robbers' practices. Under Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] the firm [[Handelsmaatschappij Deli-Atjeh|Nolte & Haas]] contracted timber-cutting rights in the East Coast landscapes; the later N.V. Handelmaatschappij "Deli-Atjeh" ([[1905]]) numbered "Manjapahit" — the same landscape in another spelling — among the landscapes of its extensive wood-cutting concessions (with Radja Moeda, Radja Toewa, [[Birim Rajeu]], Birim Pontong, Bajan, Simpang Anas and [[Perlak]]).

**The Krueng Meureubo** (older Dutch colonial spelling *Kroeëng Meureubo*; also *Kr. Meureubo*; modern Indonesian *Sungai Meureubo*) is a river of the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust of Aceh]] whose *brongebied* (source-area) lies in the *groote Westelijke lengtedal* (great western longitudinal valley) of central [[Sumatra]] and which breaks through the Westelijke Barisan (Western Barisan range) to reach the Indian Ocean north of the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Krueng Woyla]] delta. The Krueng Meureubo is, in the [[Encyclopaedisch Bureau]]'s [[1917]] Sumatra survey, one of the two principal rivers — together with the [[Kroeëng Seunagan|Krueng Seunagan]] — that drain the *groote Westelijke lengtedal* to the Westkust between the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Krueng Woyla]] to the south and the [[Kroeëng Teunom|Krueng Teunom]] to the north; and it is, in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's]] *[[The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Geology of Indonesia]]* ([[1949]]), one of the two principal fluvial reference-points — together with the [[Krueng Woyla]] — for the West-Aceh Neogene-coal zone.

## Course and Hydrography

The [[Encyclopaedisch Bureau]]'s Hoofdstuk I (Oro-hydrographisch) of its [[1917]] Sumatra survey describes the Kroeëng Meureubo as one of the two rivers (with the Kroeëng Seunagan) whose *brongebieden* (source-areas) "gelegen zijn in het groote Westelijke lengtedal. Zij doorbreken beide dus de Westelijke Barisanketen" — "lie in the great western longitudinal valley. They both therefore break through the Western Barisan chain." The Krueng Meureubo thus belongs to the same hydrographical category as the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Krueng Woyla]], the [[Kroeëng Teunom|Krueng Teunom]], and the [[Lao Alas]]: a river whose source-area lies in the *groote Westelijke lengtedal* and whose lower course breaks through the Westelijke Barisan to the Indian Ocean.

In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the Krueng Meureubo's *kustvlakte* (coastal plain) — at [[Meulaboh]] — is the site of the principal Dutch Westkust *bivak* (military post), and the southern destination of the [[1ste Garnizoensbataljon]]'s Meulaboh detachment. The river's lower course thus defined the strategic *kustvlakte* of the central Westkust, and its *brongebied* sustained the Dutch *bivak* at Meulaboh.

## The West-Aceh Neogene Coal Deposits

The Krueng Meureubo is, in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's]] [[1949]] *Geology of Indonesia*, one of the two principal rivers — together with the [[Krueng Woyla]] — along which the Neogene-coal deposits of the West-Aceh coast are located. Van Bemmelen's reference to the Krueng Meureubo occurs in the same passage of the coal chapter as his reference to the Krueng Woyla:

> "The deposits of the second group are located along the Krueng Wojla and the Krueng Meureubo in the district of West Atjeh."

For the wider context of the West-Aceh Neogene coals see the entry on [[Krueng Woyla]]. The Krueng Meureubo deposits, like the Krueng Woyla deposits, "to the present, seem to be of no or little economical importance."

## Significance

The Krueng Meureubo is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Aceh]] history on two complementary grounds. In the [[Encyclopaedisch Bureau]]'s [[1917]] survey, the river is the hydrographical artery of the central Westkust — the river whose *brongebied* sustained the Dutch *bivak* at [[Meulaboh]] and whose lower course defined the strategic *kustvlakte* between the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Krueng Woyla]] and the [[Kroeëng Teunom|Krueng Teunom]]. The Bureau's emphasis on the Kroeëng Meureubo's *doorbreking* (breakthrough) of the Westelijke Barisan frames the river as the hydrographical artery of the central Westkust. In [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's]] [[1949]] *Geology of Indonesia*, the river is one of the two principal fluvial reference-points for the West-Aceh Neogene-coal zone — the river whose name is canonically associated with the West-Aceh coal occurrences in the colonial geological literature. The river's inclusion in Van Bemmelen's summary of the West-Aceh coal occurrences is itself the historical fact that matters: the river was sufficiently well-known in the colonial geological literature to be named as one of the two principal coal-bearing rivers of the West-Aceh coast.

**Ambon** — also *Amboina* — is the island in the [[Nederlands-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] that was the homeland of *[[Oom Ambon]]*, the Ambonese professional soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]]. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* Ambon is the place to which the Ambonese soldier returned on *verlof* (leave), the place whose *[[negorij]]* took pride in his decorations, and the place to which the retired soldiers — [[Thijs de Fretes]], [[Nussy]] — returned after their service. The island is also the place of the [[mass arrest of 23 September 1943 (Ambon)|mass arrest of 23 September 1943]] by the [[Kempetei]] during the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese occupation]], and the place of the post-[[1949]] *Republik Maluku Selatan]] — the unilateral declaration of independence of the South Moluccas by the Ambonese soldiers who refused to be handed over to the unitary Republic.

## The recruitment island

"In Ambon en de [[Minahassa]] was dit volkomen anders. Daar was het militaire beroep in hoog aanzien en het dienstnemen volkomen normaal, zelfs een eervolle daad." In Ambon and the Minahassa, the military profession was in high esteem and joining up was completely normal, even an honourable deed. Ambon was therefore one of the two principal recruitment islands of the colonial army's indigenous soldaten — the place where the *[[negorij]]* took pride in its soldiers, and where the *[[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]]* Ridder was the pride of his community.

## The verlof and the negorij

The Ambonese soldier returned to Ambon on *verlof* (leave) — normally after six years' service, for two months. "Slechts één maal is hij in die veertig jaar met een kort verlof naar Ambon geweest" — [[Dominee Thenu]] visited Ambon only once on short leave in his forty years of service. The *[[negorij]]* — the home village on Ambon — tracked the soldier's wedervaren through the gehele Archipel, and his decoration was worn at the *[[Koninginnedag]]* rondgang. "Komt men in Nederland na twintig jaar vrijwel als een vreemde in zijn geboortestad, dáár kon een achterkleinzoon na een eeuw *thuis*komen" — where in the Netherlands one returns after twenty years almost as a stranger to one's birthplace, there a great-grandchild could come *home* after a century.

## The post-retirement return

The retired Ambonese soldiers returned to Ambon. [[Thijs de Fretes]] — the sergeant [[6e Divisie Marechaussee|1e klasse]] of the [[6e Divisie Marechaussee]] at [[Bakongan]] — "had op Ambon een grote doeriantuin, die hem nog een aardig centje opleverde" — had a large doerian plantation on Ambon that still brought him a nice penny. [[Nussy]] — after his retirement and a visit to the Netherlands with a *controleur* — became *mantri-politie* on Ambon under the civil bestuur of his former commander, the Assistent-Resident [[Schmidt]].

## The Japanese occupation and the mass arrest

The [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese occupation]] of [[1942]]-[[1945]] was particularly harsh on Ambon. The [[Kempetei]] conducted the [[mass arrest of 23 September 1943 (Ambon)|mass arrest of 23 September 1943]] on Ambon, in which large numbers of the Ambonese elite were seized and many executed. [[Pietje de Fretes]] — the *fourier* and later *onderluitenant*, nephew of [[Thijs de Fretes]] — was beheaded by the Japanese during the occupation.

## The Republik Maluku Selatan

The [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]] of [[27 December]] [[1949]] triggered the *Republik Maluku Selatan]] — the unilateral declaration of independence of the South Moluccas by the Ambonese soldiers who refused to be handed over to the unitary Republic. The declaration was the political consequence of the abandonment: the Ambonese soldiers who had served the Dutch and were now to be handed over to their enemy refused, and declared their own state. The Republik Maluku Selatan would be crushed by the Indonesian army in [[1950]]-[[1952]].

## Significance

Ambon is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the homeland of *[[Oom Ambon]]*, the Ambonese professional soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]]. The book's argument is that Ambon — the recruitment island, the *verlof* destination, the *negorij* of the decorated soldiers, the post-retirement return, the site of the Japanese mass arrest, and the place of the Republik Maluku Selatan — was the island that gave the colonial army its Ambonese soldaten, and the island to which the abandoned soldiers of [[1949]] looked for a political future the Dutch had not provided.

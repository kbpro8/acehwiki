**Seureulo** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Tgk. di Barat]]

In [[1911]] his presence made itself felt again. In January [[1911]] a great meeting of the resistance party had taken place at [[Seureulo]] in the [[Meergebied]] — the present sub-division [[Takèngön]] — at the place where Tgk. di Barat was hiding, attended also by bands of [[Gajòs]]. But the meeting was unluckily discovered or betrayed, and driven apart by patrols; the Teungkoe's father-in-law was killed. When in the following month an encounter with the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* likewise ended unhappily, Tgk. di Barat left the Meergebied and returned to the plain of [[Lho' Soekon]] and [[Lho' Seumawè]].

### From [[Takèngön]]

Formerly the mountain region "where the bands found good and safe hiding-places everywhere," Takèngön became the seat of the sub-division. [[Seureulo]], where [[Tgk. di Barat]] hid and where a great meeting of the resistance party was held and dispersed in January [[1911]], lay in the Meergebied, "thans onderafd. Takengon". In April [[1914]] Takèngön reported the killing of [[Pang Intan]] with four followers at the [[Waih ni Wis]]. [[H. N. A. Swart]] crossed the lake by motorboat from Takèngön to [[Bintang]] with [[Hein Meijer]] on the inspection that produced the anecdote of the engine revolutions.

### From [[The resistance in Atjeh 1908–1912]]

[[1911]] had been an unlucky year for Tgk. di Barat. In January a great meeting of the resistance party had been held at [[Seureulo]] in the [[Meergebied]] — now the sub-division [[Takèngön]] — where he was in hiding, attended also by Gajò bands; it was discovered or betrayed and dispersed by patrols, his father-in-law being killed. The following month an encounter with the *marechaussée* went badly, and he left the Meergebied for the plain of [[Lho' Soekon]] and Lho' Seumawè to reorganise the resistance with Tgk. di Mata Ië. There the *marechaussée* awaited him; on [[22 February]] [[1912]] Behrens surprised him with two brigades, "and that was the end for Tgk. di Barat. From that moment the situation in the whole North Coast division became stable and ranged from favourable to very favourable; the suffering there was over."

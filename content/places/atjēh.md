**Atjēh** was the Acehnese sultanate located on the northern tip of Sumatra, which in the 16th and 17th centuries maintained close political and cultural ties with [[Perak]], conquering it in [[1579]] and again bringing it under its suzerainty under [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] in the 17th century.

## Context
Atjēh (also known as Aceh) emerged as a powerful maritime sultanate in the early modern period, controlling trade routes through the Strait of Malacca and engaging in diplomatic and military relations with neighboring polities such as [[Perak]] on the Malay Peninsula.

## Conquest of Perak (1579)
In [[1579]] the forces of Atjēh invaded and conquered Perak, incorporating the tin‑rich state into the Acehnese sphere of influence. This event is noted in the source as the moment “Perak … in 1579 door Atjēh veroverd”【20260707_232246_DLP-54-Een_16de_eeuwse_Maleise_vertaling_van_de_Burda_van_al_Bu_s_i_ri_OCR.txt, p. 45】.

## Sultan Iskandar Muda’s suzerainty
During the early 17th century, [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] of Atjēh launched a second campaign that restored Acehnese overlordship over Perak, a development described as “in de 17”h eeuw door Sultan Iskandar Muda nogmaals onder Atjéhse heerschappij gebracht”【20260707_232246_DLP-54-Een_16de_eeuwse_Maleise_vertaling_van_de_Burda_van_al_Bu_s_i_ri_OCR.txt, p. 45】.

## Cultural and linguistic influence
The close relations between Atjēh and Perak facilitated linguistic exchange; scholars have suggested that a 16th‑century Acehnese manuscript of the Malay translation of the [[Burda]] shows Perak‑Malay influence, possibly because the translator worked under Perak dialect or the translation was produced in Perak【20260707_232246_DLP-54-Een_16de_eeuwse_Maleise_vertaling_van_de_Burda_van_al_Bu_s_i_ri_OCR.txt, pp. 44‑45】. Conversely, Acehnese influence in Perak is described as “duidelijk merkbaar”【20260707_232246_DLP-54-Een_16de_eeuwse_Maleise_vertaling_van_de_Burda_van_al_Bu_s_i_ri_OCR.txt, p. 45】.

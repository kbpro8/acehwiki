**Atoeh** (also written Atoch) was an Atjehnees kampong in the [[III Moekims]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] and the site of the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|catastrophe of 13 February 1876]] in which [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]]'s detachment — two officers and 57 men, ten of them unarmed — was massacred on the drassige *sawah* (marshy rice field) near [[Lamsajoen]] by Atjehneezen springing from the boschrand. Borel treats it as the textbook example of what happens when a European column loses order on a marshy sawah.

## The III Moekims invasion

Atoeh had been conquered on [[3 February]] [[1876]] in the opening phase of [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s three-colonne operation against the [[III Moekims]]. From Toeram, which bordered the gebied of [[Toekoe Bait]] (one of the most hostile [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] chiefs), the Dutch had made an *inval* (inroad) into that *sagi*. After a *heeten strijd* the enemy was driven out of Atoeh. The conquest was part of the broader sweep through the [[XXV Moekims]]: by [[4 February]] [[1876]] the colonne had taken Lamrong and Lamsajoen, Kajoe Leh, [[Lambaroe]] and [[Pager Ajer|Pager Ajer Mesigit]], and via Tandjoeng and Bayoer had established communication with the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]]. On [[7 February]] [[1876]] the troepen returned to [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] after a 10-day *tocht* — total losses five dead and 59 wounded, "betrekkelijk gering." The entire *Sagie* of the [[XXV Moekims]] was now in Dutch hands; the *demarcatie-linie* ran from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast to Atoeh, then northward to Pager Ajer on the left bank of the Atjeh-rivier.

## The catastrophe of 13 February 1876

[[Generaal Pel|Pel]] granted the troepen only a couple of days' rest at K. Radja, then advanced in *oostelijke richting* with three colonnes against the [[III Moekims]] on [[13 February]] [[1876]]. The same day, [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]] — marching from K. Radja with two officers and 57 men (10 unarmed) to reinforce the Atoeh post — was attacked on the drassige sawah near Lamsajoen, almost at his destination. The enemy sprang from the boschrand; the attack was "zoo snel" that most soldiers were severely wounded before they could *verzamelen* (rally). Bijna allen sneuvelden "met wonden overdekt" — almost all fell covered with wounds; only a few escaped by flight. Bodies lay on the field; wapens and munitie were carried off by the enemy. Borel asks how a fairly strong, *achterladegewapende* (breech-loading-armed) afdeeling could be overvallen on open terrain in broad daylight — and answers by recalling the disastrous [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|31 December 1874 Longbatta march]] over the doorweekte sawah, where all order was lost. The Atjehnees charge like "eene woeste onverwachte charge van cavallerie" — a wild, unexpected cavalry charge.

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — first-line post of the Zuidoosterlinie, 1876

Meijer places Atoë among the seven first-line works of the [[Zuidoosterlinie|south-east line]] of [[1876]] (Biloel, Atoë, [[Atoë Noord]], [[Kajoe-Lē]], [[Lambaroe]], Mesigit Pagar-Ajer, [[Pagar-Ajer]]). An old Atjehnese fortification, taken by the Dutch on [[3 February]] [[1876]] after hot fighting and occupied as a post, it consisted of an old Atjeh fortification standing near the kampong of the same name. Garrisoned initially by two companies of the left half of the [[6e Bataljon|6th Infantry Battalion]] under Major [[Majoor J. Visser|J. Visser]], it was the nearest Dutch post to the ground on which, on [[13 February]], the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|detachment of Captain Van Swieten]] was destroyed in the open sawah west of Lamsajoeng, with only some thirteen men reaching the post alive.

The chapter on the post and its neighbourhood gives his daily price: nightly alarm firing against enemies prowling around it; the June flood, standing 20 to 30 centimetres high inside the new work at [[Night attack on Atoë Noord (22-23 March 1876)|Atoë Noord]], where the enemy was suspected of having dammed one of the small channel brooks against it; patrols punished by hostile fire at Oleh-Atoë on [[2 August]] and two native fusiliers caught and killed while picking fruit outside it on [[30 June]]; and, on [[Convoy ambush near Atoë (21 March 1876)|21 March]] and on [[Night attack on Atoë Noord (22-23 March 1876)|22–23 March]], the road battle north of the kampong that gave the line its new middle post Atoë Noord.

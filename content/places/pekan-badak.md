**Pekan Badak** was a strategically vital settlement, marketplace, and fortified military outpost of the [[Aceh Sultanate]] situated approximately 1,600 meters (18 minutes' march) from [[Sinangri]] and 5,000 meters from [[Kraton of Aceh]] in modern Banda Aceh, Indonesia.

## Role in the Second Expedition and Occupation

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in late December [[1873]], General [[J. van Swieten]]'s forces captured Pekan Badak from Acehnese defenders, incorporating it into the northern supply line connecting [[Koerong Raba Bay]] with the interior.

Following the [[Capture of the Aceh Kraton]] in January [[1874]], Pekan Badak became a primary forward garrison of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]]. Throughout [[1875]], Acehnese resistance forces repeatedly skirmished around the post.

## Staging Ground for General Pel's Offensives

In December [[1875]], General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] concentrated his field army at Pekan Badak. On [[26 December]] [[1875]], Dutch columns marched out from Pekan Badak to execute the [[Attack on Mibouw]] and the [[Crossing of Blankala Pass]]. 

As reported in colonial newspapers on [[30 December]] [[1875]] and [[2 January]] [[1876]], Pekan Badak served as the central logistical depot for the [[Intendance and Supply Service in Aceh]], where forced convict laborers (*dwangarbeiders*) ([[Forced Labor in Aceh Military Campaigns]]) gathered munitions for the [[March through the XXII Moekims]] in early [[1876]].

**Poelau Gontong** was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the Dutch East India Company (the *Oost-Indische Compagnie*) post on the [[Siak|Siak River]], established in [[1755]] and destroyed in [[1759]] by the Siak Sultan [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]. The [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post was, in the Bureau's framing, the first formal Dutch commercial presence on the [[Siak|Siak River]], and its destruction in [[1759]] was the immediate occasion of the long Dutch intervention in the Siak succession that would, in the 1760s, transform the Siak sultanate from an independent polity into a Dutch client state.

## Background: the abortive 1689 lodge at Petapahan

The Bureau records, in a footnote, that the earliest Dutch approach to the [[Siak|Siak River]] dated to [[1689]], when the [[Sultan of Djohore|Sultan of Djohore]] granted the Compagnie the duty-free exclusive trade on the [[Siak|Siak River]] and the right to build a lodge at [[Petapahan|Petapahan]] (on the [[Tapoeng-streken|Tapoeng kiri]]). The footnote records: "De oudste vestiging van de O. I. C. dagteekent van [[1689]], toen de Sultan v. Djohore den tolvrijen alleenhandel op de Siakrivier en den bouw van een loge te Petapahan (Tapoeng kiri) aan de Compagnie toestond. De geringe resultaten dezer vestiging waren oorzaak van hare ontruiming in [[1680]]." The Bureau's attribution of the evacuation to [[1680]] is, however, chronologically inconsistent with the establishment date of [[1689]]; the evacuation probably took place in [[1690]] or shortly thereafter, and the [[1680]] figure is likely a typographical error in the printed text.

## The 1755 establishment

The [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post was established in [[1755]], in return for Dutch support for [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] against his brother [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] in their disputes over the Siak succession. The Bureau records: "In [[1755]] verleende de O. I. C. hulp aan Radja Moehamad, in zijne twisten met zijn broeder R. Alam, over de opvolging in Siak. Als loon voor deze hulp werd in [[1755]] toestemming tot het oprichten van eene loge met versterking op Poelo Gontong in de Siakrivier verleend." The [[1755]] establishment was, in the Bureau's framing, the formal beginning of the Dutch commercial presence on the [[Siak|Siak River]] — the foundation that would, within four years, be destroyed by the very sultan it had been meant to support.

## The 1759 destruction

The [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post was destroyed in [[1759]] by [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]. The Bureau records: "Waarschijnlijk oorzaak van het verraad van [[1759]], was onoordeelkundig optreden der Compagnie's gevolmachtigden, toen dezelfde R. Moehamad den geheelen post liet uitmoorden" — "Probable cause of the treachery of 1759, was unjudicious conduct of the Compagnie's envoys, when the same R. Moehamad had the entire post massacred." The Bureau's attribution of the destruction to "onoordeelkundig optreden" — "unjudicious conduct" — of the Compagnie's envoys is the principal explanation recorded for the sultan's act.

## The consequences

The destruction of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post was, the Bureau records, the immediate occasion of the long Dutch intervention in the Siak succession. "Het afloopen van die post in [[1759]] had tengevolge dat de Compagnie steun verleende aan den eerst met haar hulp van den troon verdreven Radja Alam, den tegenstander van den in [[1759]] heerschenden sultan Radja Moehamad. Deze laatste overleed in [[1760]], doch zijn opvolger Radja Ismail, werd in [[1761]] overwonnen en verdreven naar Pelalawan, dat daarop eveneens met behulp der Compagnie werd overweldigd door R. Alam." The Dutch intervention of the 1760s — undertaken against [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]] — would, within two decades, be reversed by [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]]'s [[1779]] restoration, and the Siak sultanate would, under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]], impose its own overlordship over the East Coast statelets.

## Significance

The [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the first formal Dutch commercial presence on the [[Siak|Siak River]], and the immediate occasion of the long Dutch intervention in the Siak succession. Its destruction in [[1759]] — by the very sultan it had been meant to support — was, in the Bureau's framing, the archetypal instance of the Dutch East India Company's "onoordeelkundig optreden" on the East Coast, and the political event that transformed the Siak sultanate from an independent polity into a Dutch client state. The post is thus the chronological prelude to the [[1779]] restoration of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]], the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Siak overlordship of the East Coast statelets]] under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]], and the long Siak-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] rivalry over the East Coast that would persist into the nineteenth century.

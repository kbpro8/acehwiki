**The Alaslanden** are the long, fertile valley of the *Lao Alas* — the great river that, rising on the slopes of the G. Losèr as the W. ni Agoesen, runs south-eastward through the heart of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] to its confluence with the Lao Renéën — and one of the five *afdeelingen* of the gouvernement, established after the *colonne van Daalen* of [[1904]] brought the Alas valley under Dutch authority. *De Buitenbezittingen* describes the Alaslanden as one of the four *cultuur-centra* of the interior, an "oase te midden van het ongastvrije, woeste landschap" — an oasis amid an inhospitable, savage landscape — and the territory in which, more than in the Gajoland, Dutch administration had already imposed a territorial organisation upon the genealogical order of the native chiefs.

## Geography

The Alas valley forms the northern extension of the Western lengtedal of the Barisan range, beginning as the *Renoen-dal* of the Daïri-landen to the south and continuing northward past the bergreus G. Losèr into the dal of the middle W. ni Tripa. The Lao Alas itself rises on the G. Losèr and flows south-east between the Wilhelmina-keten — "een zwaren muur tusschen de vruchtbare Alasvallei en het woeste heuvelland van boven-Langkat en Tamiang" — and the Western *randgebergte* of the Daïri-landen. Its bevolking, of heterogeneous origin (Batak, Menangkabau, Atjehsch, Pak-Pak), is concentrated in well-cultivated kampongs along the rivier; the sawahs of the Alas valley provide completely in the rijstbehoefte of its inhabitants, and "daarenboven vrij bewoners van het bergland" — feed besides the inhabitants of the mountain country round about.

## The Radja Tjèq and the two landschappen

The original territorial overlords of the Alas valley were the *Radja Tjèq* (Radja Kémala) — the *roi fainéant* who, by the time of the Dutch arrival in [[1904]], "had afstand gedaan van alle directe bestuursmacht" — and his vassals the *Kédjoerön Bambél* and the *Kédjoerön Batoe Mboelan* (whose deputy was the *Kédjoerön Radja Bintang*). The two landschappen — [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] in the north (under direct Radja Tjèq rule around the residential kampong) and [[Bambél|Bambél]] in the south (held in leen by the Kédjoerön Bambél) — were each governed by a *Rijksraad* of four *Péngoeloe si-émpat*, of which the Radja Tjèq himself reserved the fourth seat. The legend of the colonisation — the *Bataq* founders Alas, Kroeas, Pagan and Képala Dèsa; the later Menangkabau immigration under Radja Déwa; the Atjehsche *Toenggal Alas* invested with the southern half — is given in the source at length.

## Dutch organisation, 1914

*De Buitenbezittingen* records that the Dutch, "onbekend met de werkelijke verhoudingen" — unfamiliar with the actual relations — initially treated the two Kédjoeröns as wholly independent *landschapshoofden* authorised to sign the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]], reducing the Radja Tjèq to the rank of an ordinary *Péngoeloe si-émpat* — a misstep whose political consequences were, however, not damaging. The reorganisation of [[1914]] (entirely in werking since that year) created two new *landschappen*: the *landschap Batoe Mboelan* was dissolved, its territory transferred to Bambél, and the *Kédjoerön* moved his residence to [[Poeloe Nas|Poeloe Nas]] (where he signed a new Korte Verklaring for that landschap), while the old Batoe Mboelan was reorganised into four *mérga*'s under bezoldigde Péngoeloe's si-émpat. The Alaslanden were now divided into the landschap Poeloe Nas (four mérga's: Klis under Imöm Balei, Képala Dèsa under Batin, Kling under Kérétan, Tjébéro under Béntjirim) and the landschap Bambél (four mérga's: 'Ngkran under 'Ngkran, Béroeh under Biaq Moeli, Sékédang under 'N Djaar, and Sélian under the Radja Tjèq of Batoe Mboelan). The Kédjoerön retained direct rule over the kampongs of Bambél and Téroetoeng; the Radja Tjèq over those of Batoe Mboelan.

## The Alasweg and the ending of the bong-stelsel

The Alasweg — driven from [[Kota Tjane|Kota Tjane]] (the bestuurszetel of the afdeeling) to Kaban Djahe on the Karo-hoogvlakte, and there connected to the wegennet of the Oostkust van Sumatra — opened the isolated valley to the east, "waardoor de handelsbeweging zich voor het grootste deel daarlangs verplaatst," ending the *schadelijke en onderdrukkende werking* of the Singkelsche *bong-bong-stelsel*. The bevolking of the afdeeling was about 10,000 (3,000 men, 3,000 women, 2,000 boys, 2,000 girls) over 6,000 km² — about 1.7 per km², the sparsest of the five afdeelingen. The registered adult Atjehers of the afdeeling numbered 3,170 at the end of [[1915]].

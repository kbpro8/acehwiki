**Blang Beutong** is a [[blang|blang]] — a complex of [[sawah|sawahs]] — in the *landschap* (self-governing native state) of [[Koeala Daja|Koeala Daja]] of the onderafdeeling [[Tjalang|Tjalang]] on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]], documented by [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]] in his [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]] as one of the most beautiful sawah complexes of the Westkust, and as a representative specimen of the rain-dependent sawahs of the historic rice-storehouse of [[Daja|Daja]].

## The Sawahs of Blang Beutong

Kreemer's regional survey of the [[rijstbouw in Atjeh|Atjeh rice economy]] records that "de sawah's op de Blang Beutong in het landschap Koeala Daja behooren tot de mooiste" — the sawahs on the Blang Beutong in the landschap Koeala Daja belong to the most beautiful — but that "ook hier zijn deze akkers uitsluitend van regen afhankelijk" — even here these fields are exclusively dependent on rain.

The Blang Beutong is thus a *blang* of beautiful sawahs — that is, of well-cultivated, well-maintained rice fields — but one that lacks artificial irrigation. The water supply of the Blang Beutong sawahs depends solely on the rainfall, retained on the fields by the small enclosing dikes (*ateuëng*). The yield of the Blang Beutong is therefore subject to the variability of the rainfall, as in the rain-dependent sawahs of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]].

## The Historic Role of Daja

The Blang Beutong is in the *landschap* of [[Koeala Daja|Koeala Daja]], which is part of the historic territory of [[Daja|Daja]] — the great rice-storehouse of the Westkust of Atjeh. Kreemer notes that "van oudsher is Daja de groote voorraadschuur van rijst geweest voor een groot deel van de overige onderafdeelingen der Westkust" — from old times Daja has been the great storehouse of rice for a great part of the other sub-divisions of the Westkust. The Blang Beutong, as one of the beautiful sawah complexes of Koeala Daja, is thus part of the historic rice-storehouse of the Westkust.

The Westkust of Atjeh is, however, a region in which artificial irrigation is rare: Kreemer notes that "kunstmatige bevloeiing vindt men alleen in het landschap Panté Tjeureumèn" — artificial irrigation is found only in the landschap [[Panté Tjeureumèn|Panté Tjeureumèn]]. The Blang Beutong, like the rest of the Daja sawahs, is rain-dependent — and the historic role of Daja as the rice-storehouse of the Westkust rests on the productivity of the rain-dependent sawahs of its *blangs*.

## Significance

Blang Beutong is significant for the encyclopedia of Aceh history as one of the most beautiful sawah complexes of the Westkust of Atjeh, and as a representative specimen of the rain-dependent sawahs of the historic rice-storehouse of [[Daja|Daja]]. The Blang Beutong exemplifies the paradox of the Westkust rice-economy: the most beautiful sawahs are rain-dependent, and the historic rice-storehouse of the Westkust relies on the variability of the rainfall rather than on the security of artificial irrigation.

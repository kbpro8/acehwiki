**Marassa** was a kampong at the mouth of the [[Aceh River|Atjeh river]] to the east of the Dutch landing-place of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]], whose head, [[Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia|Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia]], had repeatedly offered his services to the Dutch and was the principal native ally of the Dutch during the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] in [[January 1874|January 1874]]. The sparing of Marassa was one of the reasons for the choice of the eastern landing-place in the second expedition; the kampong was connected with [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] by the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]] in [[May 1874|May-June 1874]].

## The Dutch Alliance

When the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] was being planned, it was decided that the landing should take place east of the Atjeh river rather than west of it as in the first expedition, "firstly because there was less resistance to be expected there, and secondly because one wished to spare the kampong Marassa, which was well-disposed to us and whose head, Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia, had repeatedly offered us his services." After the landing at the [[Kwala Loë|Kwala Loë]] on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]], Toekoe Nek maintained his good offices, sending word of enemy movements and offering to act as guide. He had already before then "promised his intervention in the recovery of the robbed corpse of the [[Nino Bixio|general Nino Bixio]] who had died of cholera," then commander of a transport ship.

## The Westerlinie, May-June 1874

After the departure of [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] on [[26 April]] [[26 April]] [[1874]] the war continued under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]]. In [[May 1874|May-June 1874]] the enemy resumed the struggle "with the greatest possible boldness and perseverance," evidently in the hope of overwhelming the weakened garrison. To protect Toekoe Nek adequately, Pel occupied the Westerlinie — a series of posts west of Kota Radja to connect it with Marassa and [[Lampassei|Lampassei]]. The principal post on this line was [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]].

## Account from *Een Vaderlandsch Belang* by H. Borel (1878)

**Marassa** was the coastal district south-east of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] ruled by the pro-Dutch chief [[Toekoe Nek]], the only Atjehnees headman to submit to the Netherlands during the early years of the war. Its geographic position — between the Dutch base at [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] and the sea at [[Oeleh Leh]] — made its friendship essential to the Dutch *strategische driehoek* (strategic triangle), and its repeated punishment by the enemy is treated by Borel as a direct consequence of [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s passive *afwachtende houding*. Marassa was attacked by the Atjehneezen on [[26 April|26]]–[[27 April]] [[1874]] and again on [[19 May]] [[1874]].

## Toekoe Nek's alliance

Toekoe Nek's willingness to accept the Dutch flag flowed from his rivalry with the hostile chiefs of the neighbouring [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] under [[Toekoe Nanta]] and of [[Bital]] and [[Ketapang Doewa]]. He repeatedly asked the Dutch for armed help against these *buren* (neighbours), which is what produced the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|expedition to Bital]] of [[12 February]] [[1874]] and the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]] of [[15 February]] [[1874]]. Both kampongs were captured and immediately abandoned, and Toekoe Nek's faith in Dutch protection was sorely tested. On [[4 May]] [[1874]] [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] visited Toekoe Nek at his home; the conference "liep zeer bevredigend af," and the Dutch were permitted to bezet [[Blang Oe|Merdoewati]], Lampasej, Blang Oe (headquarters) and Oeleh Leh with a batterij at Oedjoeng Planggan. Borel clarifies that the so-called *strategische driehoek van den generaal van Swieten* is misnamed — the triangle [[Kwala Atjeh]]–K. Radja–Oeleh Leh was Pel's work, not van Swieten's.

## Attacks and Dutch aid

In the night of [[26 April|26]]–[[27 April]] [[1874]], within hours of van Swieten's departure from Atjeh, the Atjehneezen attacked Marassa to punish Toekoe Nek for his submission and "ons, door het bezetten van Marassa, aan die zijde van de zee af te snijden" — to cut us off from the sea on that side. Seven kampongs with about 80 huizen were burned, but the Marassanen resisted dapper and the enemy failed to take Marassa. On [[19 May]] [[1874]] the enemy returned; "talrijke benden" invaded Toekoe Nek's gebied, burning more houses, while Merdoewatie and Blang Oe were "gealarmeerd en beziggehouden." The Oeleh Leh bezetting stood an "ernstigen aanval" but "sloeg dien wakker af." Borel's verdict: "Wij zijn dan ook de meeste dankbaarheid aan Toekoe Nek schuldig." The burning of the Merdoewatie post on [[15 May|15]]–[[16 May]] further exposed the fragility of the alliance.

**Aroebai** (also rendered **Aroe-baai**, modern **Arube Bay**) was a natural harbour on the eastern coast of the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency, described in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account as "the fine and accessible Arube Bay" (*de fraaie en goed toegankelijke Aroe-baai*) and as "a good roadstead" (*eene goede rede*). In the Bureau's framing of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]], Aroebai was projected as the natural maritime terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network — and as a potential competitor to [[Belawan]], [[Singapore]], and [[Sabang]] as a deep-water outlet for the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] export trade.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Aroebai

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[1913]] extension approvals presents [[Aroebai|Aroebai]] as the natural terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s projected southern extension: "the doortrekking was approved of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] network from [[Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban]] (on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]], with a good roadstead), while the connecting line between [[Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] is also already in preparation. With the completion of this last line-section, the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] network will finally reach its natural terminus: the fine and accessible [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]], by which the economic value of the entire tram network will rise."

The Bureau's account of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] presents [[Aroebai|Aroebai]] as a potential rival to the established export harbours of the region: "the defenders of the Aroe-baai-plannen, who see in this natural harbour a competitor of [[Belawan]] and even of [[Singapore]] and [[Sabang]], hope, by the laying of this connecting link, to see the first step taken toward the realisation of those future plans, by the export of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] products (pepper, rubber, etc.) — which increases steadily with the reclamation of the [[Tamiang|Tamiangsche moerassen]] — to be led via [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]]."

The Bureau further notes that the Aroe-baai-plannen were "of evident interest to the whole [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|gouvernement Atjeh]]" and that they were maintained "even when, in place of [[Belawan-Deli|Belawan-Deli]], the equally inadequate roadstead of [[Pangkalan Brandan]] was put forward as the desired terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]]." The Aroe-baai-plannen thus represented, in the Bureau's framing, the strategic-economic interest of the Atjeh residency as a whole — pitted against the commercial interests of the [[Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij|Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij]], the [[Belawan-Deli|Belawan-Deli]] trade, and the [[Pangkalan Brandan]] commercial establishment.

## Account from *Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java* (1954) — the pre-colonial bay

Three and a half centuries before the Dutch tramway plans, the same bay was the theatre of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aroe war of 1539]]: the [[Atjehsche vloot|Atjehnese fleet]] of [[Alleredin Mahomet]] anchored here in October [[1539]] under the command of the sultan's brother-in-law, brought the kingdom of [[Aroe]] to its fall, and closed the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] to Portuguese shipping. [[Mendez Pinto|Mendez Pinto]] entered the bay on [[5 October]] [[1539]], having run from the Nine Islands off [[Perak]] across the Strait; asked by Pinto the name of the place, his pilot answered *Paneticano* — a name [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *Roe-pang moes-tika-noe-sa*, the veiled name of the white-magic islet of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]], one of the half-moon of islands sheltering the bay along with [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] and [[Pulo Breueh|Salahadji]].

In [[1888]] the Controleur S.B. [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] visited the bay from [[Bengkalis]], entering between Sembilan and [[Poelo Kampi|Koempej]] and recording the islet's fortress-like hills; crossing to [[Pangkalan Soesoe]], he found dwellings of Malays fled from [[Besitang]], and inland the old *bentengs* of *soengej Si Kondoer* and *Sérang djaja*, said to be the fortifications of the earlier rajas of Aroe, and the *soengej Si Djanda*, recalling in local memory "the widow *Djanda Gidau*... of the last raja of Aroe, who fled to that river at the destruction of the realm by Atjeh" — a folk echo of the [[Queen of Aroe]]. The hill above the bay, in [[1862]], still bore the *benteng* of an [[Atjehsche avonturier at Aroe-baai (1862)|Atjehschen avonturier]] who defiantly flew the Atjehsche flag.

## Significance

[[Aroebai|Aroebai]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the projected natural maritime terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] and the strategic-economic counterweight to [[Belawan]] and [[Sabang]] in the Atjehnese export trade. The [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] of [[1913]] represent the most ambitious single project of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade for the economic development of the Atjeh residency — a project that, in the Bureau's view, was of "evident interest to the whole gouvernement Atjeh" but was actively contested by the established commercial interests of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]]. Three and a half centuries earlier, the same bay had been the geographical anchor of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]] and the principal locus of the *bedekte taal* that De Roo de la Faille decodes as the survival of the Hindu-Buddhist place-world of the Sumatran east coast — the pre-colonial history that the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's tramway-and-harbour account does not mention, but that gives the bay's Dutch-colonial economic importance its much older strategic prehistory.

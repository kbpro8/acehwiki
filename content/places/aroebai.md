**Aroebai** (also rendered **Aroe-baai**, modern **Arube Bay**, in the [[1918]] Oostkust volume also **Langsaarbaai**) was a broad, shallow natural harbour on the Strait of Malaka at the northern extremity of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]], just south of the [[Besitang|Besitang River]] mouth and the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] region (transferred to [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] in [[1908]]). Described in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's [[1917]] account as "the fine and accessible Arube Bay" (*de fraaie en goed toegankelijke Aroe-baai*) and as "a good roadstead" (*eene goede rede*), Aroebai was projected as the natural maritime terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network — and as a potential competitor to [[Belawan|Belawan]], [[Singapore|Singapore]], and [[Sabang|Sabang]] as a deep-water outlet for the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] export trade. Three and a half centuries earlier, the same bay had been the theatre of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aroe war of 1539]] and the principal locus of the *bedekte taal* that De Roo de la Faille decodes as the survival of the Hindu-Buddhist place-world of the Sumatran east coast.

## Geography


The Aroebai is sheltered by [[Pulo Sembilan|Pulau Sembilan]] and [[Poelo Kampi|Pulau Koempai]] (Koempej) at its entrance. Two deep channels — the *Noordelijk vaarwater* (north of the islands) and the *Zuidelijk vaarwater* (Sembilan-geul, south) — provide the only good natural anchorages on the northern [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] coast. The bay's head silts extensively at low tide, but the channels maintain depth for ocean-going vessels. The Koeala Balbalan inlet (600 m wide, shallow) leads to [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] 8 km inland; the Sungai Balbalan drains the [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] area into the bay.

## The pre-colonial bay


Three and a half centuries before the Dutch tramway plans, the bay was the theatre of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aroe war of 1539]]: the [[Atjehsche vloot|Atjehnese fleet]] of [[Alleredin Mahomet|Alleredin Mahomet]] anchored here in [[October]] [[1539]] under the command of the sultan's brother-in-law, brought the kingdom of [[Aroe|Aroe]] to its fall, and closed the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] to Portuguese shipping. [[Mendez Pinto|Mendez Pinto]] entered the bay on [[5 October]] [[1539]], having run from the Nine Islands off [[Perak|Perak]] across the Strait; asked by Pinto the name of the place, his pilot answered *Paneticano* — a name [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *Roe-pang moes-tika-noe-sa*, the veiled name of the white-magic islet of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]], one of the half-moon of islands sheltering the bay along with [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] and [[Pulo Breueh|Salahadji]].

In [[1888]] the Controleur S.B. [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] visited the bay from [[Bengkalis|Bengkalis]], entering between [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] and [[Poelo Kampi|Koempej]] and recording the islet's fortress-like hills; crossing to [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]], he found dwellings of Malays fled from [[Besitang|Besitang]], and inland the old *bentengs* of *soengej Si Kondoer* and *Sérang djaja*, said to be the fortifications of the earlier rajas of [[Aroe|Aroe]], and the *soengej Si Djanda*, recalling in local memory "the widow *Djanda Gidau*... of the last raja of Aroe, who fled to that river at the destruction of the realm by Atjeh" — a folk echo of the [[Queen of Aroe|Queen of Aroe]]. The hill above the bay, in [[1862]], still bore the *benteng* of an [[Atjehsche avonturier at Aroe-baai (1862)|Atjehschen avonturier]] who defiantly flew the Atjehsche flag.

## The 1913 Atjeh-tram extension and the Aroe-baai-plannen


The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's [[1917]] account of the [[1913]] extension approvals presents Aroebai as the natural terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s projected southern extension: *the doortrekking was approved of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] network from [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] to [[Besitang|Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboeban]] (on Aroebai, with a good roadstead), while the connecting line between [[Besitang|Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] is also already in preparation. With the completion of this last line-section, the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] network will finally reach its natural terminus: the fine and accessible Aroebai, by which the economic value of the entire tram network will rise.*

The [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] — the project to develop Aroebai as a deep-water harbour — is presented by the Bureau as a potential rival to the established export harbours of the region: *the defenders of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]], who see in this natural harbour a competitor of [[Belawan|Belawan]] and even of [[Singapore|Singapore]] and [[Sabang|Sabang]], hope, by the laying of this connecting link, to see the first step taken toward the realisation of those future plans, by the export of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] products (pepper, rubber, etc.) — which increases steadily with the reclamation of the [[Tamiang|Tamiangsche moerassen]] — to be led via Aroebai.* The Bureau further notes that the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] were *of evident interest to the whole [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|gouvernement Atjeh]]* and that they were maintained *even when, in place of [[Belawan|Belawan-Deli]], the equally inadequate roadstead of [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] was put forward as the desired terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]].* The [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] thus represented, in the Bureau's framing, the strategic-economic interest of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh residency]] as a whole — pitted against the commercial interests of the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]], the [[Belawan|Belawan-Deli]] trade, and the [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] commercial establishment.

## The Van Heutsz Oceaanhaven proposal (1905–1907)


The [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] volume of [[1918]] traces the Aroe-baai-plannen back to an earlier and more ambitious proposal by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] — Governor of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] [[1898]]–[[1904]] and Governor-General of [[Nederlands-Indië|Netherlands India]] [[1904]]–[[1909]] — to develop [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboean]] on Aroebai into a full *Oceaanhaven* for the entire [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] cultivation zone. Van Heutsz was the *krachtig voorstander* (strong proponent) of the Aroebaai scheme. His advocacy stemmed from familiarity with [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]]'s northern geography from the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] campaigns, the bay's superior natural depth and shelter compared to [[Belawan|Belawan]]'s shoaling approaches, the potential to link the bay via the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] (then building southeast from [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]]) directly to the cultivation zone, and the strategic desire to integrate [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] economically. The *van Heutszgebergte* (van Heutsz Mountains) in the [[Batak Karo|Karo]] highlands was named after him, reflecting his association with the region's opening.

The original project envisioned a full *Oceaanhaven* at [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboean]] (on Aroebai), where the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] would terminate. The bay offered two deep channels (north and south of [[Pulo Sembilan|Pulau Sembilan]]/[[Poelo Kampi|Koempai]]) with good anchorage for large steamers, shelter from the Strait of Malaka's swell, proximity to the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] fields at [[Langsa|Langsa]]/[[Rantau Pandjang|Rantau Pandjang]], and a shorter sea route to [[Penang|Penang]]/[[Singapore|Singapore]] for northern cultivation areas ([[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Besitang|Besitang]]).

## The 1910 Kraus-de Jongh report and the Belawan decision


The Handelsvereeniging Medan opposed the plan, arguing that Aroebai lay too far north of the main production centres ([[Medan|Medan]], Tebing Tinggi, [[Pematang Siantar|Siantar]]), that land transport costs over the poor northern roads would negate harbour advantages, and that [[Belawan|Belawan]]'s central location in the cultivation zone was economically decisive.

In [[1910]] the colonial government commissioned engineer Kraus-de Jongh to evaluate. His report recognized Aroebai's technical superiority (depth, shelter, expansion potential) but concluded that general economic grounds favoured [[Belawan|Belawan]]: the cultivation zone's centre of gravity lay south of the [[Asahan|Asahan]], and [[Belawan|Belawan]]'s hinterland connections (rail, road, future canals) were already established. The government accepted Kraus-de Jongh's advice and launched a renewed [[Belawan|Belawan]] dredging program ([[1912]] onward) using a 700 m³ suction dredger. Depths improved from 7½ ft ([[1907]]) to 19 ft at low-water spring by [[1916]]. The Plan Nobel for a [[Belawan|Belawan]] ocean harbour was approved in principle. The Aroebaai ocean-harbour plan was *verlaten* (abandoned); only a modest steamer jetty at [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] for the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] tankers and the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] was built.

## Petroleum and the Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij


The [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] (later [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|BPM/Shell]]) developed the [[Langsa|Langsar]] and [[Rantau Pandjang|Rantau Pandjang]] oil fields in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] ([[Tamiang|Tamiang]] region) from the [[1890s]]. [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] was chosen as the tanker-loading port because the Aroebaai offered deeper water than the silting [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] or [[Belawan|Belawan-Deli]], because the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] could be extended from [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] to the bay, and because the bay was accessible to ocean-going tank steamers. By [[1918]] the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|KPM]] had built loading jetties with large tank installations at [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] on the Sembilan-geul, and a regular tank-steamer service to overseas ports operated from there. The [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] brought oil in tank wagons from the fields to the jetties.

## Transport connections (1918)


- [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] (narrow gauge, 1,067 mm): reached [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] by [[1918]] from [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] via [[Tamiang|Tamiang]].
- [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]] (standard gauge, 1,435 mm): built the [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]]–[[Bookit Koeboe|Bookit Koeboe]] section toward a three-rail junction at [[Bookit Koeboe|Bookit Koeboe]] (between [[Besitang|Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]]).
- Road: The [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]]–[[Besitang|Besitang]] gap remained the only break in overland connection between the two railway systems.
- Post/Telegraph: The completed [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] along the Aroebaai provided the *snelverbinding* between the two *gouvernementen*; only the [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]]–[[Besitang|Besitang]] segment required auto transport.

## Significance


Aroebai is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's [[1917]] account, the projected natural maritime terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] and the strategic-economic counterweight to [[Belawan|Belawan]] and [[Sabang|Sabang]] in the Atjehnese export trade. The [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] of [[1913]] represent the most ambitious single project of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade for the economic development of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh residency]] — a project that, in the Bureau's view, was *of evident interest to the whole gouvernement Atjeh* but was actively contested by the established commercial interests of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]]. The [[1918]] Oostkust volume extends the story backward: the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] were the surviving fragment of Van Heutsz's much larger [[1905]]–[[1907]] Oceaanhaven proposal, defeated in [[1910]] by the Kraus-de Jongh report and the Handelsvereeniging Medan — a defeat that reduced Aroebai from a projected ocean harbour to a mere petroleum jetty. Three and a half centuries earlier, the same bay had been the geographical anchor of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]] and the principal locus of the *bedekte taal* that De Roo de la Faille decodes as the survival of the Hindu-Buddhist place-world of the Sumatran east coast — the pre-colonial history that the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's tramway-and-harbour account does not mention, but that gives the bay's Dutch-colonial economic importance its much older strategic prehistory.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Aroe-baai** — modern *Aru Bay* — is the bay on the eastern coast of Sumatra which forms the natural harbour of the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] river-basin and of the *onderafdeeling* of that name. The [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] and the [[Deli-spoor|Deli-spoor]] both sought the Aroe-baai when they were built southward and northward respectively; the Atjeh-tram, in reaching it, had to cross the boundary of its own gewest. The junction of the two lines at [[Besitang|Besitang]] in [[1908]] gave the Aroe-baai a double hinterland.

### Historical Background


Before [[1898]] the regions near the Aroe-baai were continually disturbed by pepper-planters, wanderers and raiders, and the whole of Tamiang was unsafe. The opening of Tamiang to private enterprise in [[1898]] — when the Atjeh-weg was about to reach [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] — was a token of confidence in the security of oil-winning and tobacco-cultivation, and was the political event that turned the Aroe-baai from a pirate-haunted roadstead into the harbour of a planting-region.

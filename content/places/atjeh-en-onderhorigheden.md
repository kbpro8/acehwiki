## In De Karbouw

Atjeh en Onderhorigheden appears in *De Karbouw* in two principal connections:

- The buffalo-density statistics for [[1938]]: "Celebes en Onderhorigheden (± 4), Atjeh en Onderhorigheden (± 2), Tapanoeli (± 2), Sumatra's Westkust (± 1); in de andere gewesten minder dan 1" (*De Karbouw*, p. 50, citing the *Indisch Verslag [[1939]]*, II, p. 283) — Atjeh en Onderhorigheden had approximately 2 buffaloes per square kilometre, the third-highest density among the Buitengewesten (after Celebes en Onderhorigheden at ±4 and tied with Tapanoeli at ±2). The figure is one of a comparative table of buffalo-density across the Buitengewesten (Outer Possessions) of the Netherlands Indies, drawn from the *Indisch Verslag [[1939]]* (the last published before the Japanese occupation), whose figures refer to the year [[1938]].
- The buffaloes-per-1000-inhabitants statistic for [[1938]]: "Timor en Onderhorigheden bovenaan staat met het cijfer ± 191, dan volgen Celebes en Onderhorigheden met ± 128, Atjeh en Onderhorigheden met ± 95, Djambi met ± 91, Benkoelen met ± 74, Tapanoeli met ± 65 ..." (*De Karbouw*, p. 51, citing the same source) — Atjeh en Onderhorigheden had approximately 95 buffaloes per 1000 inhabitants, the third-highest ratio among the Buitengewesten (after Timor en Onderhorigheden at ±191 and Celebes en Onderhorigheden at ±128).
The two statistics together give a quantitative frame for the place of the buffalo in the late-colonial Atjehnese rural economy: a density of 2 buffaloes/km² is moderately high by Sumatran standards (Sumatra's Westkust, by contrast, had only ±1/km²), and a ratio of 95 buffaloes per 1000 inhabitants is substantially higher than the Sumatran average. These figures quantify, at the level of the colonial administrative unit, the qualitative picture of Atjehnese buffalo-husbandry that *De Karbouw*'s ethnographic chapters develop at the level of village and household.


## As administrative unit

Atjeh en Onderhorigheden was constituted as a Dutch colonial administrative unit after the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] of [[1873]]-c.[[1914]], in which the Dutch military, after decades of inconclusive campaigning, finally broke the back of organized Atjehnese resistance and incorporated the sultanate's territory into the Netherlands Indies. The unit comprised the coastal districts of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] (around [[Banda Acheh|Koetaradja]], the colonial capital), [[Kleine-Atjeh|Kleine-Atjeh]] (the outlying western coast), the north coast districts ([[Pedirse|Pedir]], [[Pasei|Pasai]], [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] and the [[N.O.-kust van Atjeh|N.E. coast]]), the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] proper, and the highland dependencies of [[Gajoland|Gajoland]] and [[Alasland|Alasland]] in the interior. The administrative capital was at [[Banda Acheh|Koetaradja]], and the [[Simaloer|Simaloer]] island-group was administered as part of the residency. The unit corresponds, in its territorial extent, to the modern Indonesian province of Aceh, with the addition of some border areas that have since been reassigned.



## Significance

Atjeh en Onderhorigheden is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as the colonial administrative frame within which the late-colonial statistical, ethnographic, and scholarly literature on Atjeh was composed. [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s own [[Kreemer's Atjeh ([[1922]]-23)|*Atjèh*]] takes the unit as its territorial referent, and the buffalo-density statistics of the *Indisch Verslag [[1939]]* — which give us our only quantitative profile of the late-colonial Atjehnese buffalo-economy — are tabulated at the level of this administrative unit. The very name "Atjèh en Onderhoorigheden" reflects the colonial perception of Atjeh as a territorial core (Atjèh) with dependent peripheries (the Onderhoorigheden: Gajoland, Alasland, etc.) — a perception that shaped the late-colonial administrative and scholarly treatment of the country.




## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**Atjeh en Onderhorigheden** — "Atjeh and its Dependencies" — was the formal Dutch colonial administrative designation for the territory of the former [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate after its conquest in the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]-[[1904]]. The designation is the colonial-administrative successor to the independent [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] and the predecessor of the modern Indonesian province of [[Aceh (modern Indonesian province)|Aceh]].

### The post-conquest administration

After the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|1904 expedition of Van Daalen]] and the subsequent [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]], the territory of the former [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate was administered as a single colonial unit under a governor and military commander — the post held successively by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] ([[1898]]-[[1904]]), [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] ([[1905]]-[[1907]]) and others — with seat at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]]. The administration was, after the [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908 report]] of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], organised on the "vanouds beproefd gebleken stelsel van bestuur door middel van de hoofden" — the long-tried system of administration through the chiefs.

### The economic frame

The [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] was the colonial-administrative frame within which the Atjeh oilfields — at [[Pedirse]], [[Peureula]], [[Pasé oil field|Pasé]] and elsewhere — were exploited under the [[Mijnwet|Mijnwet]] of [[1899]] and its [[Mijnwet art. 5a (1910)|Article 5a amendment]] of [[1910]]. The [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]]/[[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak]] disclosure of [[1905]]-[[1910]] was, in this administrative frame, the proximate episode that triggered the legislative intervention.

### The administrative headcount

The [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] was, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]-period colonial record, the type-specimen of the under-manned colonial territory. [[H. C. Thomson|Thomson]]'s warning in the [[1904]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate — that "seventy men to patrol the whole of [[Gajoland]]" was "verregaand onvoldoende" — grossly insufficient — is the canonical statement of the garrisoning problem of the *pacificatie*.

### Significance

[[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] is the formal Dutch colonial-administrative frame within which the post-conquest administration of Atjeh was conducted. The designation is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the political-administrative successor to the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] and the predecessor of the modern Indonesian province of [[Aceh (modern Indonesian province)|Aceh]].

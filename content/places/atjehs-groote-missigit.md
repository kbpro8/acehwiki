**Atjeh's Groote Missigit** was the grand mosque and a primary fortified strongpoint of the Acehnese defenses, situated approximately 300 meters northwest of the [[Kraton of Atjeh]].

## Geography and Structure
The Missigit was located in a densely populated, alluvial plain along the [[Atjeh-rivier]]. It was a rectangular stone enclosure measuring roughly 170 by 165 meters, with walls 2.50 meters high and 0.65 meters thick, built from hard hard stone (stone). The interior contained a raised plateau, several water wells (including a highly revered holy well), and the wooden central mosque building characterized by its double-tiered palm-leaf (atap) roof without minarets.

## Historical Significance
Because of its strategic location protecting the Kraton, the Missigit became the focal point of the fiercest battles of the [[First Aceh Expedition]]. It was the site of the [[First Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[10 April]] [[1873]], the [[Second Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[14 April]] [[1873]], and the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]]. The dense vegetation, defensive bastions, and protective ring walls made it a formidable [[Missigit Fortifications|fortification]] that cost the Dutch military heavily in lives, including that of General [[J.H.R. Köhler]] near the [[Generaal-Köhler-boom]].

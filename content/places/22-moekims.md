**The 22 Moekims** — *Twenty-two Moekims*, written in Dutch *22 Moekims*, in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s spelling also *XXII Moekims* or *Sagi XXII* — was the inland *Sagi* (third administrative division) of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], one of the three *Sagi's* of the sultanate together with the [[25 Moekims|25]] and [[26 Moekims|26 Moekims]] and the [[Sultan's gebied|Sultan's own territory]]. The [[Conquest of the 22 Moekims (March-June 1879)|Dutch conquest of the 22 Moekims]] under [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] from [[March 1879|March]] to half [[June 1879|June]] [[1879]] was the principal military success of Van der Heijden's governorship and brought the entire valley of Groot-Atjeh under Dutch control for the first and only time before the final pacification under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]]. In Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, the XXII Moekims is the opening chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest — the Sagi of twenty-two mukims whose *panglima sagi* was [[Panglima Polim]] and within which the *machtige hoofd* [[Tekoe Moeda Baid]] held the [[VII Moekims]] sub-federation.

## Geography

The 22 Moekims was the inland *Sagi* of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] — the only one of the three Sagi's that lay inland rather than on the sea. The two seaward Sagi's (the [[25 Moekims|25]] and [[26 Moekims|26 Moekims]]) lay on the coast; the 22 Moekims lay inland. The Dutch had mainly occupied the 25 Moekims and the [[Sultan's gebied|Sultan's territory]]. All of Groot-Atjeh was to be brought under.

## The Conquest (March – June 1879)

The [[Conquest of the 22 Moekims (March-June 1879)|conquest of the 22 Moekims]] began in [[March 1879|March]] [[1879]] and was completed by half [[June 1879|June]] [[1879]]. The enemy, fighting as he withdrew, fought with great courage but was everywhere defeated and driven out. After his *kampongs* and possessions had been destroyed, he fled to the mountains. Along the [[Atjeh River|Atjeh-rivier]] a line of posts was occupied.

## The Loss under Civil Government (1881–1884)

The conquest of the 22 Moekims would prove transient. Under the civil government of [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] (1881–1882) and [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] (1883–1884), the endless patrols that Van der Heijden had deemed necessary to maintain the peace were virtually abolished. In [[June 1878|June]] [[1878]] — even before the conquest of the 22 Moekims — [[Habib Abdul Rachman El-Zahir|Habib Abdul Rachman]] had invaded the Dutch *stelling* and the enemy had pressed into the 22 and 26 Moekims. Under civil government, "steeds stoutmoediger drongen sterke vijandige benden de 22 en 26 Moekims binnen en op ons gebied door" (ever bolder, strong enemy bands pressed into the 22 and 26 Moekims and onto Dutch territory), even into the immediate vicinity of [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]]. The [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[1884]] contracted the Dutch *stelling* back to the perimeter of [[1876]], abandoning the 22 Moekims to the enemy.

## Significance

The 22 Moekims is the inland *Sagi* of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and the principal inland theatre of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Its [[Conquest of the 22 Moekims (March-June 1879)|conquest by Van der Heijden]] in [[March 1879|March]]–[[June 1879|June]] [[1879]] was the principal military success of Van der Heijden's governorship; its loss under civil government was the principal disaster of the [[Civil Government Period (1881-1884)|civil government period]]; its abandonment by the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[1884]] contracted the Dutch *stelling* back to the perimeter of [[1876]]. The final pacification under Van Heutsz after [[1898]] would re-establish Dutch control over the 22 Moekims.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, the XXII Moekims — *Sagi XXII*, *de Sagi's XXII*, the Sagi of twenty-two mukims — is the opening chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest and the territory whose submission fixed the *binnen drie maanden* precedent the XXVI Moekims campaign would complete. The [[Sagi]] was the constitutional unit of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — a territorial league of *mukims* (parish-districts) headed by a *panglima sagi* and collectively constituting the ancient core of the [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese sultanate]]. The three Sagi's — the XXII, the [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] — were the territorial framework within which the sultan's authority had historically been mediated, and they were the operational targets of the Van der Heijden campaigns of [[1878]]–[[1879]]. The Sagi-head of the XXII Moekims was [[Panglima Polim]] — named by Van Heutsz as *het Sagi hoofd der XXII Moekims Panglima Polim*. The VII Moekims, a sub-federation within the XXII, was held by [[Tekoe Moeda Baid]], the *machtige hoofd der VII Moekims van de Sagi XXII Moekims*.

### The March 1877 negotiations and their failure

The first Dutch engagement with the XXII Moekims was the *onderhandelingen* (negotiations) of March [[1877]], when Panglima Polim and Tekoe Moeda Baid opened negotiations for submission under the impression of G.-G. van Lansberge's March [[1877]] visit. Van Heutsz records the opening: *werkelijk openden toen Panglima Polim en Tekoe Moeda Baid onderhandelingen tot onderwerping* (truly Panglima Polim and Tekoe Moeda Baid then opened negotiations for submission). The negotiations were insincere: *de door de Atjehsche hoofden aangeknoopte onderhandelingen moesten slechts dienen om tijd te winnen* (the negotiations opened by the Atjehsche chiefs were only meant to gain time). Two *besprekingen* (meetings) between Tekoe Moeda Baid and the Dutch officials at [[Telok-Kroet]] on the Westkust followed, *waarbij hij de laatste maal verklaarde zich aan ons te onderwerpen* (at the second of which he declared himself willing to submit). The submission never came: *De onderwerping van Tekoe Moeda Baid kwam niet tot stand, evenmin die van Panglima Polim* (The submission of Tekoe Moeda Baid did not come about, nor that of Panglima Polim). The failure was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the original error of the *verzoeningspolitiek* — the policy that gave the Atjehnese a year to re-arm, and that the *inval* (incursion) of [[Habib Abdoel Rachman]] in June [[1878]] would expose.

### The July 1878 campaign

The campaign against the XXII Moekims opened in July [[1878]], after the *inval* of Habib Abdoel Rachman into the Dutch-occupied territory had triggered the *op Groot-Atjeh geheel veranderen van stelsel* (the complete change of system on Groot-Atjeh). The intellectual author of the reversal was the Chief of Staff, *majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius*, who had argued since [[1877]] for the *Zuidoosterlinie* along the Atjeh river to be shifted into the XXII Moekims: *de Zuidoosterlinie langs de Atjeh rivier te verleggen en daartoe de XXII Moekims in te rukken* (to shift the south-eastern line along the Atjeh river and to enter the XXII Moekims for that purpose). In June [[1878]] Geij van Pittius *overtuigde den chef van den staf hem, dat men door expeditien naar de kuststaten het doel — het einde van den oorlog — niet kon bereiken, en dat alleen eene beslissing kon worden verkregen door in Groot-Atjeh zelf den vijand aan te tasten* (convinced the chief of staff that one could not reach the goal — the end of the war — through expeditions to the coastal states, and that a decision could only be obtained by attacking the enemy in Groot-Atjeh itself). Van der Heijden accepted the argument: *toen besloot de Generaal van der Heijden voorloopig van de expeditie naar Pasangan af te zien en met geheel zijne beschikbare macht voor goed te Groot-Atjeh aan het verzet een einde te maken* (the Generaal van der Heijden therefore decided to forgo the Pasangan expedition for the time being and with all his available force to make an end of the resistance in Groot-Atjeh for good).

The July [[1878]] opening of the campaign is fixed by Van Heutsz in the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] text: *De krijgsoperatiën in de XXII en XXVI Moekims, die in Juli van het vorige jaar zijn aangevangen en zoo schitterende uitkomsten hebben opgeleverd* (The military operations in the XXII and XXVI Moekims, which began in July of last year and have yielded such brilliant results). The campaign was supported by the arrival of substantial reinforcements — *onmiddellijk nog 24 Bataljon Infanterie en eene bergbatterij naar Atjeh gezonden werden, terwijl ook de cavalerie op een eskadron werd gebracht, zoodat toen, wat b. v. de Infanterie aangaat, het halve veldleger — 9½ Bataljons — in Atjeh gelegerd was* (immediately a further 24 Battalion Infantry and a mountain battery were sent to Atjeh, while the cavalry was also brought up to a squadron, so that then, as for example concerns the Infantry, half the field-army — 9½ Battalions — was stationed in Atjeh) — the half of the field-army was in Atjeh. (Note: the Van Heutsz account of the July 1878 opening complements the existing Kruijt account above of the March–June 1879 *conquest* of the 22 Moekims; the two narratives reflect different framings of the same campaign sequence — the July 1878 opening under Van der Heijden and the March–June 1879 *verovering* proper.)

### The submission of 13 October 1878

The campaign produced, within three months, the submission of Habib Abdoel Rachman and Tekoe Moeda Baid on [[13 October]] [[1878]]. Van Heutsz records the result: *Hoe reeds binnen drie maanden na den aanvang van den eersten veldtocht in de XXII Moekims, de onderwerping van Habib Abdoer Rachman en van Tekoe Moeda Baid op den 13en October 1878 volgde* (How already within three months of the beginning of the first campaign in the XXII Moekims, the submission of Habib Abdoer Rachman and of Tekoe Moeda Baid on 13 October 1878 followed). The submission was the principal operational outcome of the XXII Moekims campaign: Habib Abdoel Rachman — the *zwager van Tekoe Moeda Baid* (brother-in-law of Tekoe Moeda Baid) and *de ziel van den tegenstand* (the soul of the resistance) — was the religious war-leader whose *inval* had triggered the strategic reversal; Tekoe Moeda Baid was the *machtige hoofd* of the VII Moekims and the principal chief of the Sagi. Their joint submission on [[13 October]] [[1878]] opened the way to the [[XXVI Moekims]] campaign of [[1879]]. The result was, in Van Heutsz's verdict, the operational proof of the Geij-van-Pittius reversal: *Met welken schitterenden uitslag onze veldtochten in de Sagi's XXII en XXVI bekroond werden, wij weten het allen* (With what brilliant outcome our campaigns in the Sagi's XXII and XXVI were crowned, we all know it).

### The outermost Dutch outpost at Djerir

The outermost Dutch outpost in the conquered XXII Moekims was [[Djerir]] — the *uitersten wachtpost* (outermost picket) reached by the Commandant van het Indische leger in his September [[1879]] reconnaissance of the conquered territory. The Commandant's report, quoted by Van Heutsz, fixed the limit: *in de XXII Moekims tot onzen uitersten wachtpost Djerir* (in the XXII Moekims as far as our outermost sentry-post Djerir). The Djerir outpost was the territorial expression of the XXII Moekims conquest — the farthest Dutch picket in the conquered Sagi, marking the limit beyond which Dutch authority did not yet run. The Commandant's reconnaissance extended, in the same month of September [[1879]], into the [[XXVI Moekims]] *tot Kroeng Kali in het Noorden, tot Tjot Lepong in het Oosten* and into the [[XXV Moekims]] *tot Kroeng Raba* — the three Sagi's thus reconnoitred in a single inspection that confirmed the Van der Heijden conquest.

### Significance (Van Heutsz account)

The XXII Moekims's significance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract rests on its being the opening chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest — the Sagi whose reduction fixed the operational model the [[XXVI Moekims]] campaign would extend; the constitutional unit of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — the Sagi of twenty-two mukims, headed by the *panglima sagi* [[Panglima Polim]] and containing within it the [[VII Moekims]] sub-federation of the *machtige hoofd* [[Tekoe Moeda Baid]] — through which the sultan's authority had historically been mediated; the first Sagi to be conquered by Van der Heijden in the July–October [[1878]] campaign that opened the Van der Heijden conquest and that ended with the submission of Habib Abdoel Rachman and Tekoe Moeda Baid on [[13 October]] [[1878]]; and the territory whose outermost Dutch outpost, [[Djerir]], marked the limit of the September [[1879]] reconnaissance that confirmed the Van der Heijden conquest.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

It was in this *sagi* that van Heutsz attacked the positions of [[Panglima Pòlém]] and drove him to [[Pidië]]. In July [[1913]] [[Pang Bintang]] was killed there by chance, when [[Van Andel]] of [[Lam Meulò]] got the first patrol into the [[V Moekims Mon Tasië']] after the transfer of *controleur* [[Roos van Raadshoven]] and ran into the band's hiding-place near the former [[Koeta Ba' Teuë]] on the second day.

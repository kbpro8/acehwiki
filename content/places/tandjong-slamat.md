**Tandjong Slamat** was a place on the coast of the [[Langkat]] district of the East Coast of [[Sumatra]], south of the [[Balbalan|Babalan]] river ground, which in the railway debates of [[1912]]–[[1916]] was the proposed junction point where the projected line [[Besitang]]–Tandjong Slamat would join the [[Atjeh-tram]] to the existing network of the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]] — the alternative disputed by [[Schadee]] in the [[Deli-Atjeh railway connection controversy (1912-1916)|Deli-Atjeh railway connection controversy]].

## The railway junction disputed

The line Besitang–Tandjong Slamat (30 kilometres, estimated at f 1,450,000) was defended by the chief of construction of the Atjeh tram and by Governor [[Van der Plas]] as the only connection along which a [[Kota Radja]]–[[Medan]] journey in two days would be possible: with the tram's preserved speed of 20 and the company's 30 kilometres per hour, the difference in running time between the two junction routes was about twenty minutes, falling to thirteen at higher speed, as [[Schadee]] demonstrated in the Cultuurraad debate of [[20 February]] [[1913]].

Schadee's counter-proposal — a junction at Pematang Pandjang with an 8-kilometre branch to [[Pangkalan Brandan]], costing about f 300,000 — would, in his opponents' view, postpone the valuable through connection Besitang–Tandjong Slamat indefinitely, and would serve chiefly the Deli Company itself. Roest wrote that the Tandjong Slamat link might not lag behind: if the Deli Company should refuse to build it, the Government would have to take the task on itself without delay, and he doubted, by the end of [[1915]], that it would be realised in the foreseeable future, or perhaps ever. The stretch Timbang Langkat–Tandjong Slamat, under the new speed regulations, would take 33 minutes more by the tram — an argument illustrating how a cheap spatial connection could upset speed on the old route.

**Fort Zeeland at Atjeh** (the factory baptised "Zeeland") was the first Dutch office on Sumatra, established at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1602]] by the returning Zeeland ships that had carried [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]]'s letter to the Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]]. The naming of the factory after the province of Zeeland was a memorial to the Zeelanders who had opened the formal Dutch-Atjeh relationship. Two merchants and several assistants remained behind to staff the new station.

## Foundation, 1602

In [[1602]], after the four Zeeland ships that had come to Atjeh in August [[1601]] had obtained the release of the Dutch prisoners and the Sultan's agreement to compensate [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] "en zijn vrouw" for his material losses, the returning ships left at Atjeh the first Dutch office on Sumatra. Godee Molsbergen's text: "In Atjeh kregen de Zeeuwen het eerste Nederlandsche kantoor op Sumatra, 'Zeeland' gedoopt, waar twee kooplieden en enkele Assistenten achterbleven."

The staff — two *kooplieden* (merchants) and several *Assistenten* (assistants) — was small, but the foundation was a deliberate act of Zeeland commercial colonisation: the factory bore the province's name as a mark of its origin. The factory's foundation in [[1602]] is contemporaneous with the founding of the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] itself (the charter is dated [[20 March]] [[1602]]) and slightly predates the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|Atjeh embassy's arrival at the siege of Grave]] later that year.

## The opening of the Gujarat trade

Two of the new factory's staff sailed on Atjeh vessels to the Gulf of Cambay, on the west coast of Voor-Indië, where — thanks to the letters of the Atjeh Sultan — they were excellently received in [[Goezeratte|Goezeratte]] and could begin their trade. The Sultan's letters thus opened the Gujarat trade to Dutch merchants — a small but concrete instance of the sultanate's diplomatic reach, and of the practical value to the VOC of the Atjeh alliance that the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|1602 embassy]] had cemented.

## Continuity with the later lodge

The "Zeeland" factory of [[1602]] was the seed of what would become the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|VOC lodge at Atjeh]] of later decades — the *Opperhoofd* with eight *helpers* that [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|1641 embassy]] found already in place. By [[1641]] the lodge had become the Company's principal station on the northern coast of Sumatra, with the Sultan's exclusive right to trade on Sumatra's Westkust attached to it. The line of institutional continuity runs from the two merchants of [[1602]] through the *Opperhoofden* of the [[1620]]s and [[1630]]s (among them [[Justus Schouten|Justus Schouten]] in [[1640]]) to [[Jacob Compostel|Compostel]] and [[Pieter Willemz|Willemz]] in [[1641]].

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, Fort Zeeland is the foundation-point of the continuous Dutch presence in the sultanate — the first formal Dutch office on Sumatra, named for the province whose ships had opened the relationship, and the institutional ancestor of the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|lodge]] that would host [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]]. Its small staff (two merchants and a few assistants) is itself a measure of how modest the Dutch residential presence in Atjeh actually was at its foundation — a thin European thread that the larger ceremonial of later embassies would repeatedly overshadow.

**Netherlands East Indies** was the Dutch colonial possession in Southeast Asia that, from the sixteenth century until 1949, encompassed the archipelago of [[Indonesia]] including the northernmost province of [[Aceh]]. The colony functioned under the authority of the [[Kingdom of the Netherlands]] and was administered by the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]] and the [[Royal Netherlands Navy]]. Its political and military history is inseparably linked to Aceh, which resisted Dutch expansion from the early nineteenth century through the [[Aceh War|Aceh War (1873–1904)]] and later became a focal point of the Japanese occupation during [[World War II|World War II]].

## Colonial administration
The Dutch established a civil and military bureaucracy in the East Indies, appointing a Governor‑General who oversaw provinces such as Aceh. The colony’s legal system, trade regulations, and plantation economy affected Aceh’s society, especially after the 1903 annexation of the Sultanate of Aceh.

## Military involvement
Dutch forces, including the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]], were repeatedly deployed to Aceh to suppress rebellions and later to defend the colony against the Japanese invasion in the early 1940s.

## Post‑war transition
After the Japanese surrender in [[1945]], the Netherlands attempted to re‑establish control over the East Indies, leading to the Indonesian National Revolution (1945–1949). Aceh participated in the struggle for independence, eventually becoming part of the Republic of Indonesia in 1950.

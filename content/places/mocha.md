**Mocha** — also written **Mokha**, Arabic **al-Mukhā** — is the coffee port of Yemen on the Red Sea, in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries one of the principal ports of the Indian Ocean trade. Mocha is the place of the reverenced tomb of [[Ahmad Rifa'i]] — the founder of the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] — and the city with which [[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs]] of [[Ahmadabad]] exchanged correspondence with a scholar. The port is one of the nodes of the Indian Ocean network that linked the religious scholarship of Yemen and Gujarat with the Malay-Indonesian world.

## The tomb of Ahmad ar-Rifāʿī

The tomb of [[Ahmad Rifa'i|Aḥmad ar-Rifāʿī]] — the founder of the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] — is reverenced at Mocha. [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] records, in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises, that Raniri "was initiated into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]], founded by the saint Aḥmad Rifāʿī, whose tomb at Mocha is reverenced." The association of the founder's tomb with Mocha — rather than with the marshlands of southern Iraq where the order was actually founded — is itself a document of the devotional geography of the Rifāʿiyya as it was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian world: a geography in which the founder's sanctity was located at a Red Sea port frequented by Indian and Indonesian pilgrims and traders, and so made accessible to the Malay-Indonesian devotees of the order.

## The correspondence of al-Aydārūs

[[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs]] — a kinsman of [[Raniri]] and a prolific author of the Indian-Arab al-Aydārūs family — exchanged correspondence with a scholar at Mocha. The correspondence is one of the principal witnesses to the Indian-Arab religious network that linked the coffee port of Yemen with the religious scholarship of [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]], and through Gujarat with the Malay-Indonesian world. The exchange of correspondence with Mocha documents the means by which the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.

## Significance

Mocha is one of the nodes of the Indian Ocean network that linked the religious scholarship of Yemen and Gujarat with the Malay-Indonesian world. The tomb of [[Ahmad Rifa'i|Aḥmad ar-Rifāʿī]] at Mocha is the devotional centre of the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] as it was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian world; the correspondence of [[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|al-Aydārūs]] with a scholar at Mocha is one of the principal witnesses to the Indian-Arab religious network in operation. The port of Mocha is, with [[Surat]], [[Tarim]], and [[Acheh]], one of the principal places of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network from which the orthodox reaction of Acheh drew its principal Malay-Indonesian agents.

**The Koeboes** (also rendered *Kubu*) were an indigenous hunter-gatherer people of the interior of [[Acheh (Atjeh)|Acheh (Atjeh)]] who, according to the source *DLP-84-Atjeh*, retreated into the hinterland when the first waves of Malay migrants arrived on the northern Sumatran coast, and whose remnants were still observable in the inland forests during the nineteenth century. Their presence in the colonial ethnographic record illustrates the layered settlement history of the region — a history in which successive waves of migration had pushed the earlier inhabitants further into the interior, where they survived, in reduced numbers, as the *primitive* populations encountered by the later Malay settlers and, ultimately, by the Dutch colonial state.

## Historical and Ethnographic Note

The Koeboes exemplify the pattern, repeatedly encountered by colonial ethnographers in the Netherlands Indies, of *primitive* populations that had been displaced by more recent arrivals and had taken refuge in the least accessible parts of the archipelago. In the case of northern Sumatra, the Malay migrations that established the coastal principalities of [[Pasei|Pasei]], [[Pedir|Pedir]], and ultimately the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Sultanate of Acheh]] itself had pushed the earlier hunter-gatherer populations — of whom the Koeboes were the principal representatives — into the interior forests of the Atjehnese hinterland. There, the Koeboes either survived as distinct communities in the primeval forest (Dutch: *oerbos*), or were absorbed into the emerging mixed communities of the highlands, contributing to the ethnogenesis of the [[Gajo|Gajo]] and [[Alas|Alas]] peoples who, by the nineteenth century, inhabited the central highlands of northern Sumatra. The Koeboes were thus both a surviving remnant of an earlier population and a constituent element of the later mixed highland communities — the so-called **Gajo-Alassers** who figure so prominently in the colonial ethnography of the [[Gajoland|Gajolanden]].

## Significance in the Layered Settlement of Atjeh

The Koeboes are significant in the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history as the documentary witness of the deepest stratum of the region's settlement history — the hunter-gatherer presence that predated the Malay migrations, the Islamic sultanate, and the Dutch colonial state. Their presence in the interior, recorded by the colonial ethnographers who accompanied the military operations of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the subsequent [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacificatie]], is the material evidence that the settlement of northern Sumatra was a layered process, in which each new arrival displaced or absorbed the earlier population. The Koeboes' relationship to the later Malay and Acehnese populations of the coast is comparable to that of the *Orang Kubu* of central Sumatra to the Malays of the eastern lowlands — a relationship of displacement, absorption, and ethnogenetic mixture that the colonial state recorded under the rubric of *primitieve volkeren* but that is now understood as the long pre-colonial history of the archipelago. The Koeboes thus belong, in the wiki, to the same layer of pre-Malay, pre-Islamic population as the *Batin* and *Lubu* of central Sumatra and the *Orang Asli* of the Malay Peninsula.

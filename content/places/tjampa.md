**Tjampa** — Dutch spelling of *Champa*, the Indianised polity of the Cham people that flourished along the central and southern coast of modern Vietnam from approximately the second to the nineteenth century CE — is named in Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]]'s chapter *De oudste cultuurstreamen* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) as the probable *stamland* (homeland) of the Austronesian (Malay-Polynesian) language family. Van Naerssen's wording, summarising the conclusion of the Dutch linguist Kern: "On the basis of these and other considerations, which we will not name here, Kern comes to the conclusion that the *stamland* probably lay in Tjampa, Cochin-China, Cambodja and the adjacent regions along the sea."

## The Austronesian homeland hypothesis

Van Naerssen's account of the Tjampa homeland-hypothesis places the origin of the Austronesian language family — of which Atjehnese is one member — in the Indianised coastal polities of mainland Southeast Asia, in the region of Tjampa (central and southern Vietnam), Cochin-China (southern Vietnam) and Cambodja. The hypothesis rests on the linguistic evidence: the words for sugarcane (*tēboe*), coconut (*niyur*), banana (*punti*) and rice (*padi*, *bēras*) are common to all the Austronesian languages, suggesting that they were already present in the *stamland*; the word for "south" (*sēlatan*) in Malay derives from *sēlat* "strait, in particular that between Malakka and Sumatra", suggesting that the Malays were not autochthonous to Sumatra but had migrated southward from a more northerly origin.

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the Tjampa homeland-hypothesis is significant in two respects. First, it places the Atjehnese language within the wider Austronesian family whose homeland lay in mainland Southeast Asia — that is, it documents the prehistoric migration that brought the ancestors of the Atjehners to the north Sumatran coast from the Asian mainland. Second, it connects the Atjeh case to the wider Indianised culture of mainland Southeast Asia, of which Tjampa (with its Sanskritic inscriptions and its Hindu-Buddhist temple-complexes) was a major centre. The Atjehnese *langa* (plough) word, cognate with Sanskrit *langgala*, is one of the linguistic survivals of this Indianised heritage (see [[Atjehnese langa (plough)]]).

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Tjampa's significance is foundational: it is the probable *stamland* of the Austronesian language family of which Atjehnese is a member, and the Indianised polity of which the Atjehnese *langa* (plough) word is a linguistic survival. The prehistoric migration from Tjampa to the north Sumatran coast — documented in the linguistic evidence that Van Naerssen surveys — is the deep-historical frame against which the later Islamisation of [[Samudra]] in [[1297]] and the [[Conversion of Minangkabau via Atjeh (c. 1550)|conversion of Minangkabau via Atjeh]] of c. [[1550]] are to be read.

**Takengeun** — also Takengon (modern Indonesian), Takingeun in the spelling of the *[[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]* — is a town in the [[Gajolanden|Gayo Lands]] of [[Atjeh|Aceh]], on Laut Tawar (the lake of Takengeun), at the southern terminus of the [[Gajoweg]] — 103 km south of [[Bireuën]]. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Takengeun is the destination of the lieutenant's journey through Aceh: the beautiful lake in the Gajolanden at the end of the Gajoweg. The town is also the place where the old hunter [[Datoek]] settled permanently in [[1917]] — "Previously I had often made journeys to Takengeun." In the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, Takingeun appears as an *onderafdeeling* of the afdeeling [[Gayo- en Alaslanden|Gajo- en Alaslanden]] in the interior highlands of [[Atjeh|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], seat of the *zelfbesturende landschappen* whose *oeleebalang* collectively issued the *alang-alang en dennenbosschen-verordening* of [[20 May]] [[1924]] (Reg. No. 22) — the only regulation in the 1930 compilation dealing with pine-forest (*dennenbosschen*) protection — and the *uitvoeringszelfbestuursbesluit* of [[1 October]] [[1926]] for the *Pantjang Alas* in Takingeun (Reg. No. 4c), the most-detailed operational supplement in the *Pantjang Alas* regime.

## Laut Tawar

The Gajoweg now leads to Takengeun on the beautiful lake in the Gajolanden, over a distance of 103 km. Laut Tawar — the lake of Takengeun — is the lake at the southern end of the Gajoweg. The lake is the beautiful lake of the Gajolanden: the destination of the lieutenant's journey through Aceh.

## The Datoek connection

Before settling at Blang Rakal in 1917, the old hunter [[Datoek]] had often made journeys to Takengeun. That was a beautiful time, sir: a good full-grown horse cost there in the old days only two ringgit (guilders). The Datoek connection to Takengeun is the book's marker of the old days of the Gajolanden: the old hunter had made journeys to Takengeun before settling at Blang Rakal, and the beautiful time of those journeys was the old colonial era when a good horse cost two guilders.

## The Gajolanden and the blangs

The sun was overpowering on the blangs (Gayo plains), but a cool wind blew over the endless, rolling grass-plains. In the distance the blue mountain-ranges and volcanoes loomed up: the Boerni-dit and the Boer-ni-dat. The blangs and the Boerni-dit and Boer-ni-dat are the landscape of the Gajolanden around Takengeun.

## Significance

Takengeun is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the destination of the lieutenant's journey through Aceh and the beautiful lake at the southern end of the Gajoweg. The book's argument — that Takengeun was the beautiful time of [[Datoek]]'s old-days journeys, when a good horse cost two guilders — is the book's marker of the old days of the Gajolanden. Takengeun is therefore the book's destination: the beautiful lake at the end of the Gajoweg, and the place of the old hunter's old-days memory.

## Account from *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1930) — the regulatory record

In the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, Takingeun was an *onderafdeeling* of the afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden in the interior highlands of [[Atjeh|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. The signatory clauses recurrently locate it: *De Zelfbestuurders der Onderafdeeling Takingeum gelegen in de afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (The Zelfbestuurders of the onderafdeeling Takingeum, situated in the afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden, Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden). The *onderafdeeling* is the northern (Gayo) half of the highland afdeeling, distinct from the southern (Alas) *onderafdeeling* Alaslanden. The signatory clause invokes the collective of all *oeleebalang* of the *onderafdeeling* without naming them individually — *Zelibestuur dari negeri-negeri didalam onderafdeeling Takingeuu* (the Zelfbesturen of the negeri-negeri of the onderafdeeling Takingeun).

### The alang-alang en dennenbosschen-verordening of 20 May 1924 (Reg. No. 22)

The *alang-alang en dennenbosschen-verordening* of [[20 May]] [[1924]] is the only regulation in the 1930 compilation dealing with cogon-grass burning and pine-forest protection together. Its full Dutch title is *Zelfbestuursverordening van 20 Mei 1924 houdende regelen omtrent het branden van alang-alang op woeste en bebouwde gronden benevens het kappen en beschadigen van dennenbosschen in de landschappen der onderafdeeling Takingeun (Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden)* (Self-government regulation of 20 May 1924 establishing rules concerning the burning of alang-alang on uncultivated and cultivated lands, as well as the felling and damaging of pine-forests in the *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling* Takingeun [Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden]). The regulation's distinctive feature is its dual subject: alang-alang (cogon grass, *Imperata cylindrica*) on the one hand, and *dennenbosschen* (pine forests; Malay *hoetan sala*, the *Pinus merkusii* forests of the Gayo/Alas highlands) on the other. It imposes a permit-system (schriftelijke *vergunning* of the *Zelfbestuur*) on the burning of alang-alang on *woeste gronden* (uncultivated lands) and on the felling or damaging of *dennen*, and imposes a mutual-aid fire-fighting duty on *omwonenden* (local residents). The comparative table on Page 144 extends parallel *regelingen* to Serbeudjadi (dagteekening [[29 May]] [[1924]]), Gajo Loelus ([[28 May]] [[1924]]), and Alaslanden ([[6 June]] [[1924]]). The 1924 Takingeun regulation is the precursor of — and apparently the model for — the afdeeling [[Pidie|Pidië]] *alang-alang* regulation of [[21 January]] [[1928]] (Reg. No. 23), which adds the *reboisatie* motive absent from Reg. No. 22.

### The Pantjang Alas uitvoeringsbesluit of 1 October 1926 (Reg. No. 4c)

The *uitvoeringszelfbestuursbesluit* of [[1 October]] [[1926]] — *Zelfbestuursbesluit van 1 October 1926, houdende vaststelling van uitvoeringsvoorschriften en modellen betrekking hebbende op de regeling van de heffing en inning van de z.g. Pantjang Alas in de landschappen der onderafdeeling Takingeun* (Self-government decree of 1 October 1926 establishing implementing prescriptions and models relating to the regulation of the levying and collection of the so-called Pantjang Alas in the *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling* Takingeun) — is the operational supplement to the afdeeling Pidië *Pantjang Alas* regulation of [[27 July]] [[1925]] (Reg. No. 4a) extended to the *onderafdeeling Takingeun*. It is the most-detailed operational supplement in the *Pantjang Alas* regime. Its full text (Fatsal 1–10) prescribes the four model-forms — Model A (*vergunning* / permit), Model B (*vervoerpas* / transport-pass), Model C (*kwitantie* / receipt), Model D (*kasboekje* / cashbook) — and requires that the *uitgifte van vergunningen* be *kosteloos* (free of charge) to every *ter goede naam en faam bekend* (of good name and fame) applicant. The *blanco vergunningen* are supplied to the *Zelfbestuurder* by the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar*. The *Kashouder der Onderafdeelingskas van Takingeun* — the cashier of the onderafdeelingskas of Takingeun — recurs as the receiving official for the *Pantjang Alas* kwitanties. The choice of Takingeun as the originating seat of the most-detailed *Pantjang Alas* operational supplement reflects the highland *onderafdeeling*'s particular dependence on forest-product revenue (benzoë, damar, camphor).

### The vee-uitvoerheffing and the Rädjö Boekét regeling

A third Takingeun-seated regulation — Reg. No. 6, the *Zelfbestuursverordening van 1 December 1917 tot regeling van de heffing van gelden bij het uitvoeren van buffels, runderen en paarden door het Zelfbestuur van het gebied van Rädjö Boekét* — is seated in the *gebied van Rädjö Boekét*, one of the *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling* Takingeun. Its signatory clause names the *Hoofd van het Gebied van Rädjö Boekét* personally, in *overleg* (consultation) with the *Bestuursassistent* of Takingeun. The regulation levies *f 1.50 per head* on the export of buffels, runderen (cattle), and paarden (horses), exempts export to the adjacent *gebieden* of Rödjö Tjeg, Rödjö Linggö (Rödjö Linggo), and Siah Oetama, and expressly repeals all earlier *adat* *uitvoerbelastingen* by the *Kedjroens*: *Bij de inwerkingtreding dezer heffing komen alle vroeger door de Kedjroens geheven uitvoerbelastingen te vervallen* (On the entry into force of this levy, all earlier export-taxes levied by the Kedjroens shall cease to apply). The Takingeun regulation is the clearest single illustration in the compilation of a *zelfbestuursverordening* expressly abolishing an earlier *adat* levy.

### Significance (Zelfbestuursverordeningen account)

Takingeun's significance in the 1930 compilation rests on its being the originating seat of the only *dennenbosschen* (pine-forest) regulation in the compilation — Reg. No. 22 of [[20 May]] [[1924]], the only regulation protecting the *Pinus merkusii* forests of the Gayo/Alas highlands; the originating seat of the most-detailed operational supplement in the *Pantjang Alas* regime — Reg. No. 4c of [[1 October]] [[1926]]; the originating seat of the *vee-uitvoerheffing* for the *gebied van Rädjö Boekét* (Reg. No. 6) — the clearest single illustration in the compilation of a *zelfbestuursverordening* expressly abolishing an earlier *adat* levy; and the demonstration — through its highland location in the *afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden*, its collective signatory clause, and its recurrent role as the originating seat of regulations whose parallel *regelingen* extend to the other highland *onderafdeelingen* — of the highland Gayo country's status as a distinct regulatory subsystem within the Gouvernement, with Takingeun as its operational centre.

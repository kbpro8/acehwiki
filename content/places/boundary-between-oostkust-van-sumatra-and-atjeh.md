**The boundary between the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]] and the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]** was the northern territorial limit of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast government]], fixed by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]] and running from the watershed between the [[Besitang|Besitang River]] and the [[Tamiang|Tamiang River]] to the [[Wilhelmina-gebergte|Wilhelmina-keten]] (Gunung Bandahara) and onward along the mountain crest to the Serbolangit group (Liang Gergas), separating the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust government]] from the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh government]]. The boundary definition resolved decades of administrative ambiguity dating to when the region was part of [[Residentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden|Residentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden]] and formally transferred the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] region to Aceh via [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 112]] of [[1908]].

## Historical context

Until the second half of the nineteenth century, the territory now comprising the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]] was considered a "worthless wilderness" and administered as part of [[Residentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden|Residentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden]]. The rapid development of European tobacco cultivation from the [[1860s]] onward necessitated more intensive administration, leading to the creation of a separate *gewest* in [[1878]] under a Resident at [[Bengkalis|Bengkalis]], with the capital moving to [[Medan|Medan]] in [[1887]]. As the cultivation area expanded northward, the need for a precise northern boundary with [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] became acute.

## The 1908 boundary settlement

The boundary was formally established by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]], the only *gewest* boundary in the region to be precisely demarcated by legal instrument. According to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's composite map (Bijlage XXIII), the line runs:

- From the north, following the watershed between the [[Besitang|Besitang]] and [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] rivers to the [[Wilhelmina-gebergte|Wilhelmina-keten]] (Gunung Bandahara, 3,030 m).
- Along the crest of the [[Wilhelmina-gebergte|Wilhelmina-keten]] to the Serbolangit group (Liang Gergas, 1,888 m).
- From Liang Gergas descending through the marshy valley of the Lau Penjabrangan, crossing its middle course.
- Continuing south along the eastern fringe of the Blang Nodi to Gunung Aboe Aboe (1,684 m), the tripoint where [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]], [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]], and [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]] boundaries meet.

The Topografische Dienst proposed an alternative natural boundary from Liang Gergas down a right-bank tributary of the Lau Penjabrangan, then along the Lau Penjabrangan to its confluence with the Lau Alas, thence south along the Gunung Sembala (eastern rim of the [[Alaslanden|Alas valley]]) to the Lau Bencen, and westward along the crest of the Deleng Madjid (Ranto Besiketen) to the source of the Lau Boekit, following it to the Lau Renoen near Kota Rimbara, then east along the [[Wilhelmina-gebergte|Wilhelmina-keten]] crest to Gunung Simpang Pajong, over Deleng Bishat to Gunung Siboentan, rejoining the old line. Both lines appear on the *Gewestelijke kaart van Atjeh* (Bijlage XI, afl. II) and the Oostkust map.

## Transfer of Tamiang

[[Staatsblad 1908 No. 112]] separated the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] region — the original northernmost portion of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust gewest]] — and attached it to [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] as the *onderafdeeling* Tamiang. This reduced the Oostkust's northern extent to the [[Besitang|Besitang]] watershed. The transfer reflected the practical difficulty of administering Tamiang from [[Medan|Medan]] and its closer geographic and cultural ties to Aceh — a point already acknowledged in the [[1878 Langkat–Tamiang transfer|1878 Langkat–Tamiang transfer]], by which the Sultan of Langkat had ceded his rights over the Tamiang *landschapjes* to the Netherlands-Indies Government. For three decades after that cession Tamiang remained attached to the Oostkust; only in [[1908]], under the pressure of continued military action against Atjehnese freebooters, was it formally incorporated into the indirect bestuurd gebied of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

## Administrative significance

The boundary fixed the jurisdiction of the *assistent-resident* of [[Langkat|Langkat]] (*hoofdplaats* [[Tandjoeng Poera|Tandjoeng Poera]]), whose *onderafdeeling* Beneden-Langkat bordered [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]]. The [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] (narrow gauge) terminated at [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] near the boundary, while the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli-tram]] (standard gauge) reached [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] and [[Bookit Koeboe|Bookit Koeboe]]; the missing link between [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] and [[Besitang|Besitang]] remained the final gap in overland connection between the two colonial railway systems. The [[Aroebai|Aroebaai]] harbor development was explicitly tied to Atjeh-tram completion, and was eventually abandoned in favour of [[Belawan|Belawan]] after the [[1910]] Kraus-de Jongh report.

## Significance

The boundary is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the only precisely demarcated boundary of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust gewest]] — the legal instrument that closed the long process of administrative detachment of the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] region from the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] and its incorporation into [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]]. The boundary's coincidence with the [[Wilhelmina-gebergte|Wilhelmina-keten]] crest and the [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tamiang|Tamiang]] watershed reflects the colonial state's preference for "natural" landscape boundaries — a preference that the Topografische Dienst's alternative proposal would have carried even further by following the Lau Penjabrangan and Lau Alas valleys.

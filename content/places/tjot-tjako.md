**Tjot Tjako** — written in Dutch as also *Toean Tjot Tjako* — was a mountain in the southern sector of the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] theatre, the main objective of the mountain column under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]] and the site of a holy grave — a simple stone grave at the top. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, Tjot Tjako required a climb of approximately +300 metres, was the *bivak* of the 3e Bataljon after its capture, and was in signal communication with [[Ketapan Doea]]. The mountain was connected by a western height to [[Glé Genting]] (with its own *heilig graf* and *wachtposten*), and overlalsoed the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] to the east. The *bergcolonne*'s capture of Tjot Tjako on [[23 May]] [[1896]] was one of the most-celebrated actions of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]].

## Description and Location

Tjot Tjako lay in the southern sector of the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] theatre, dominating the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] to the east and the road toward [[Lam-Pisang]] and [[Lam-Asan]] to the north. The mountain required a climb of approximately +300 metres, and at its top stood a holy grave — a simple stone grave, presumably of a local Achehnese saint. The mountain was connected by a western height to [[Glé Genting]] (also Glé Genting Poetih / Glé Poetih), whose top also bore a *heilig graf* and guard-posts. The *Glétaroenpas* and [[Glé Taroen|Glé Taroen]] (*Kloof Glé Taroen*) — a gorge with a *versterking* at its entrance, where a light was seen on the [[23 May]] [[1896]] morning — lay to the south. The [[Goh-Batee Tamon]] mountain-top from which the Dutch were fired upon during the ascent of Tjot Tjako lay to the north. The mountain was bounded on the east by the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] and on the west by the range [[Glé Genting]]–[[Glétaroenpas|Glétaroenpas]].

## Historical Background

Tjot Tjako had been a religious site of Achehnese pilgrimage by virtue of its *heilig graf* — a simple stone grave presumably of a local saint. The mountain had been integrated into [[Teuku Umar]]'s defensive *stelling* across the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV en VI Moekims]] as the southern anchor of his right wing, with the heights of [[Glé Genting]] and the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] forming a natural bastion. The *bergcolonne*'s ascent of Tjot Tjako on [[23 May]] [[1896]] was the first Dutch military operation on the mountain, and the establishment of a *bivak* on its top gave the Dutch their first observation post over Umar's *stellingen* in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Tjot Tjako was the main objective of the mountain column under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]] — the opening day of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]]. The *bergcolonne* departed from [[Ketapan Doea]] and [[Blang]], ascended the mountain under fire from the [[Goh-Batee Tamon]] *bergtop*, and established a *bivak* on the top. Signal communication was maintained between the *bivak* on Tjot Tjako and [[Ketapan Doea]]. The 3e Bataljon was stationed at the *bivak* on Tjot Tjako. From Tjot Tjako the Dutch could observe the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] to the east, where the 5e compagnie of the 12e Bataljon fought on [[23 May]] [[1896]] and where the 9e Bataljon covered the Dutch retreat on [[25 May]] [[1896]]. The mountain was connected by its western *hoogte* to [[Glé Genting]] (with its *heilig graf* and *wachtposten*). On [[25 May]] [[1896]] the *bivak* on Tjot Tjako was retained as a forward observation post. On [[4 June]] [[1896]] the maréchaussée operation into the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] was supported from Tjot Tjako.

## Significance

Tjot Tjako was the principal Dutch observation post of the [[1896]] counter-offensive and the symbolic climax of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. The capture of the *heilig graf* mountain was the Dutch response to the religious dimension of Achehnese resistance, and the establishment of a *bivak* on its top demonstrated the Dutch intent to hold the high ground over Umar's *stellingen*. The mountain's religious significance as a site of Achehnese pilgrimage gave its capture an importance beyond the strictly military.

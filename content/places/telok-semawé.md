**Telok Semawé** — also written *Telok Semawe*, modern *Teluk Semawe* — was a coastal state on the [[Noordkust|North Coast]] of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], strategically located near the western end of the North Coast, whose submission and occupation by the Dutch in [[July 1874|July]] [[1874]] (with a cash gift to the *radja*) and whose post, established in [[1881]] under [[Scherer|Scherer]] and reopened under [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] at the end of [[1887]], made it one of the principal Dutch-aligned statelets on the North Coast and one of the principal harbours opened for general trade under the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] of [[April 1894|April]] [[1894]].

## The Submission of July 1874

[[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] raised the Dutch flag in [[July 1874|July]] [[1874]] with a cash gift, but thereafter the commander in Atjeh forbade the latter practice. Many statelets — where it was known that the population and even the rulers fought against the Dutch in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] — followed that example, until the commander also on this point determined that in future first personal contact must be obtained and the ruler sworn in; but by then it was too late.

## The 1881 Post and Scherer's Station

The post at [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] was established in [[1881]]. The *Assistent-Resident* [[Scherer|Scherer]] was stationed there, and from there, in [[May 1881|May]] [[1881]], he set out to the new Governor [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] the necessity of a rational [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]].

## The Van Teijn Reopening (end of 1887)

At the end of [[1887]], under [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]]'s governorship, the post at [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] was reopened, together with the temporary closure (blockade) of part of the [[Westkust|West Coast]] where [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] had his pepper lying. The first the government rejected; the latter it approved.

## The 1894 Scheepvaartregeling

Under the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] of [[April 1894|April]] [[1894]], [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] was opened as one of the harbours for general trade on the [[Noordkust|North]] and [[Westkust|West Coasts]] (the other being [[Poeloe Rajja|Poeloe Rajja]]). With respectively [[Edi|Edi]] on the East Coast and [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] and [[Poelole Rajja|Poelole Rajja]] on the North and West Coasts, all trade had to pass through these harbours under Dutch supervision.

## The 1890s Unrest

In the 1890s, under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s governorship, [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] was from nearby surrounded by Muslemin (malefactors). The post was repeatedly fired upon; trenches were dug between the posts and in the so-called open strip. The Dutch's best friends paid the Sultan's emissaries who travelled the coasts to collect money.

## Significance

[[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] is one of the principal Dutch-aligned statelets on the [[Noordkust|North Coast]] of Atjeh and one of the principal harbours opened for general trade under the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]. Its post, established in [[1881]] and reopened at the end of [[1887]], was the station of the *Assistent-Resident* [[Scherer|Scherer]] and the place from which Scherer's doctrine of the *scheepvaartregeling* was articulated. Its strategic location near the western end of the North Coast made it a natural harbour for general trade and a natural point of Dutch influence on the North Coast.

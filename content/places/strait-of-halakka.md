**The Strait of Halakka** — Dutch *Straat Halakka* — is the strait that separates the island of [[Sabang|Pulau Weh]] from the northern tip of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]], and forms one of the two sea passages by which shipping may pass between the [[Strand Malaka (Strait of Malacca)|Strait of Malacca]] and the [[Indian Ocean|Indian Ocean]] to the north of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The strait is named in the Dutch pamphlet literature of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] as a waterway of strategic and commercial importance, and is the subject of the [[Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel|map of the theatre of war]] that accompanied the *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk]]* by [[Een Ooggetuige|Een Ooggetuige]] in the [[1880s]].

## Navigation and trade

The Strait of Halakka lies to the north of the Atjehnese mainland, between the coast and [[Sabang|Pulau Weh]]. Its use as a shipping passage gave it an importance in the nineteenth-century debate on the sea approaches to the archipelago, since ships bound for the Far East from Europe — after the opening of the [[Suez Canal|Suez Canal]] in [[1869]] — could pass through the Strait of Halakka rather than through the [[Strand Malaka (Strait of Malacca)|Strait of Malacca]] itself, and could thereby shorten the passage. The author of the *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen]]* argued that ships bound for [[Australia|Australia]] could pass more easily through the Strait of Halakka than through the Strait of Malacca, and that a properly lighted and buoyed approach to the Atjehnese coast would make the strait a natural station for world shipping.

## Coastal lights

The [[Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel|*Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel, de Vaarwaters en vereischte Kustlichten voor den Wereldhandel*]] — "Map of the Theatre of War, the Waterways and required Coastal Lights for World Trade" — that accompanied the *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen]]* set out the waterways around Atjeh, including the Strait of Halakka, and the positions at which coastal lights (*kustlichten*) were held to be needed for the safe navigation of those waterways. The map's argument for the lighting of the strait was part of the author's wider argument that Atjeh, properly developed, could serve as a station for world trade on the great sea-route between Suez and the Far East.

## Significance

The Strait of Halakka is significant within the AcehWiki corpus as a waterway whose strategic and commercial importance was argued in the contemporary Dutch pamphlet literature of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]]. The strait's relation to the northern tip of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] and to the island of [[Sabang|Pulau Weh]] — which would later become the site of the [[Sabang|free port of Sabang]] — places it at the centre of the nineteenth-century Dutch debate on the sea approaches to the archipelago.

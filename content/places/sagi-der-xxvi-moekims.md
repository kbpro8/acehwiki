**Sagi der XXVI Moekims** — written in Dutch as *Sagi der XXVI Moekims* and meaning "Federation of the Twenty-Six Mukims" — was one of the three traditional [[sagi]] (federations of [[moekim|mukims]]) into which [[Groot-Atjeh]] was divided, comprising twenty-six mukims situated on the east coast of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre around the [[Toengkoeb]] post. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the Sagi der XXVI Moekims was the region of the [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] and of [[Toekoe Djohan Lampasei]], and contained the mukim of [[Toengkoeb]] within its territory. Together with the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] it is mentioned in the final Dutch assessments of [[1896]] as one of the prosperous regions under Dutch protection, in implicit contrast to the permanently hostile [[Sagi der XXII Moekims]].

## Description and Location

The Sagi der XXVI Moekims lay to the east of [[Koeta Radja]] on the east coast, extending from the lower [[Atjehrivier]] to the [[Kroeng Raba]] bay and the coast toward [[Ladoeng]]. It bordered the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims]] on the south and the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims]] on the west, and contained the Dutch posts of [[Toengkoeb]] (residence of the head of the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]), [[Tjot Rang]] (one of the largest *pasars* in this sagi), and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] (on the border with the XXII Moekims, set to protect the [[III Moekims Daroe]] against incursions from [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]]). The sagi contained the [[blokhuis|blokhuizen]] of Tjot Kapoela, Tjot Sabtoe, Tjot Madé, Tjot Bak Tjoe, Tjot Lampoe' Oe, Tjot Penom, and [[Lamgoet|Lam-Goet]] (residence of the *bendehoofd* [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata]]). [[Tjot Paklat]] was the site of an old Achehnese fortification.

## Historical Background

The Sagi der XXVI Moekims had been the eastern bastion of the [[sultanate of Atjeh]] and was the seat of [[Teungkoe di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiroe]] after the Dutch concentration. [[Toengkoeb]] was occupied by the Dutch to prevent the religious-warriors from re-settling and to control the [[hoeloebalang]] [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]]. The post of [[Tjot Rang]] had been, from [[1881]] to [[1885]], a favourite residence of marauding bands in the Boeng Tjala Moekim. After the construction of the [[buitenlinie]] the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] was the eastern anchor of the outer line, with [[Kroeng Gloempang]] set to protect the [[III Moekims Daroe]] and the [[III Moekims Ateuh]] against incursions from [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations

In the spring of [[1896]] the Sagi der XXVI Moekims was the scene of the eastern withdrawal of the [[buitenlinie]]. On [[13 April]] [[1896]] the post of [[Kroeng Gloempang]] was subjected to a *beschoeting en brandpoging* by hostile *benden*; on [[16 April]] [[1896]] the post of [[Toengkoeb]] was successfully re-supplied. On [[20 April]] [[1896]] [[Toengkoeb]], [[Tjot Rang]], and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] were evacuated, and on [[21 April]] [[1896]] [[Tjot Rang]] was burned at 11:30 AM after a three-column operation to free the beleaguered garrisons; [[Toengkoeb]] was handed over to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]]. On [[28 April]]–[[29 April]] [[1896]] the [[Night Surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe]] was conducted within the sagi by a column from [[Pakan Kroeng Tjoet]] and [[Boekit Karang]]. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] the [[benteng|bentengs]] Perampoean and Batoe near [[Toengkoeb]] were demolished by the [[Genie]] under Captain Zelle. On [[23 June]] [[1896]] the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Hansen column]] reached the plains of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] by way of the Ladoeng sea-landing.

## Significance

In the final Dutch assessments of [[1896]] the Sagi der XXVI Moekims was, with the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]], one of the regions whose prosperity under Dutch protection was contrasted with the continued hostility of the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] of [[Panglima Polim]]. The handover of [[Toengkoeb]] to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]] marked the formal reliance of the [[Dutch]] on friendly [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] as a substitute for garrisoned posts, a policy that would be developed further under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

**Sagi der XXVI Moekims** — written in Dutch as *Sagi der XXVI Moekims* and meaning "Federation of the Twenty-Six Mukims"; in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s spelling also *Sagi XXVI* or *de Sagi's XXVI* — was one of the three traditional [[sagi]] (federations of [[moekim|mukims]]) into which [[Groot-Atjeh]] was divided, comprising twenty-six mukims situated on the east coast of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre around the [[Toengkoeb]] post. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the Sagi der XXVI Moekims was the region of the [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] and of [[Toekoe Djohan Lampasei]], and contained the mukim of [[Toengkoeb]] within its territory. Together with the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] it is mentioned in the final Dutch assessments of [[1896]] as one of the prosperous regions under Dutch protection, in implicit contrast to the permanently hostile [[Sagi der XXII Moekims]]. In Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, the XXVI Moekims is also the closing chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest — the Sagi whose July–September [[1879]] campaign culminated in the [[Selimoen tocht (1879)|tocht naar Selimoen]] and produced the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] declaring *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* (the whole of Greater Atjeh subdued).

## Description and Location

The Sagi der XXVI Moekims lay to the east of [[Koeta Radja]] on the east coast, extending from the lower [[Atjehrivier]] to the [[Kroeng Raba]] bay and the coast toward [[Ladoeng]]. It bordered the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims]] on the south and the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims]] on the west, and contained the Dutch posts of [[Toengkoeb]] (residence of the head of the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]), [[Tjot Rang]] (one of the largest *pasars* in this sagi), and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] (on the border with the XXII Moekims, set to protect the [[III Moekims Daroe]] against incursions from [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]]). The sagi contained the [[blokhuis|blokhuizen]] of Tjot Kapoela, Tjot Sabtoe, Tjot Madé, Tjot Bak Tjoe, Tjot Lampoe' Oe, Tjot Penom, and [[Lamgoet|Lam-Goet]] (residence of the *bendehoofd* [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata]]). [[Tjot Paklat]] was the site of an old Achehnese fortification.

## Historical Background

The Sagi der XXVI Moekims had been the eastern bastion of the [[sultanate of Atjeh]] and was the seat of [[Teungkoe di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiroe]] after the Dutch concentration. [[Toengkoeb]] was occupied by the Dutch to prevent the religious-warriors from re-settling and to control the [[hoeloebalang]] [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]]. The post of [[Tjot Rang]] had been, from [[1881]] to [[1885]], a favourite residence of marauding bands in the Boeng Tjala Moekim. After the construction of the [[buitenlinie]] the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] was the eastern anchor of the outer line, with [[Kroeng Gloempang]] set to protect the [[III Moekims Daroe]] and the [[III Moekims Ateuh]] against incursions from [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations

In the spring of [[1896]] the Sagi der XXVI Moekims was the scene of the eastern withdrawal of the [[buitenlinie]]. On [[13 April]] [[1896]] the post of [[Kroeng Gloempang]] was subjected to a *beschoeting en brandpoging* by hostile *benden*; on [[16 April]] [[1896]] the post of [[Toengkoeb]] was successfully re-supplied. On [[20 April]] [[1896]] [[Toengkoeb]], [[Tjot Rang]], and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] were evacuated, and on [[21 April]] [[1896]] [[Tjot Rang]] was burned at 11:30 AM after a three-column operation to free the beleaguered garrisons; [[Toengkoeb]] was handed over to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]]. On [[28 April]]–[[29 April]] [[1896]] the [[Night Surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe]] was conducted within the sagi by a column from [[Pakan Kroeng Tjoet]] and [[Boekit Karang]]. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] the [[benteng|bentengs]] Perampoean and Batoe near [[Toengkoeb]] were demolished by the [[Genie]] under Captain Zelle. On [[23 June]] [[1896]] the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Hansen column]] reached the plains of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] by way of the Ladoeng sea-landing.

## Significance

In the final Dutch assessments of [[1896]] the Sagi der XXVI Moekims was, with the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]], one of the regions whose prosperity under Dutch protection was contrasted with the continued hostility of the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] of [[Panglima Polim]]. The handover of [[Toengkoeb]] to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]] marked the formal reliance of the [[Dutch]] on friendly [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] as a substitute for garrisoned posts, a policy that would be developed further under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, the XXVI Moekims — *Sagi XXVI*, *de Sagi's XXVI*, the Sagi of twenty-six mukims — is the closing chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest, the territory whose reduction fixed the *nog geen 14 maanden* precedent — the fourteen-month campaign that produced the *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* declaration — and the territory whose post-[[1879]] residual resistance the [[1884]] [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]] would expose. The [[Sagi]] was the constitutional unit of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — a territorial league of *mukims* (parish-districts) headed by a *panglima sagi* and collectively constituting the ancient core of the [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese sultanate]]. The three Sagi's — the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], the [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] — were the territorial framework within which the sultan's authority had historically been mediated. The XXVI Moekims was the second Sagi, conquered after the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]] in [[1878]] and before the [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] was reached in the September [[1879]] reconnaissance. The XXVI Moekims' *panglima sagi* was, in Van Heutsz's later narrative, the chief whose authority the religious leader [[Tengkoe di Tirou]] would over-reach into in the *Boengtjala affair* — the over-reach whose effect was to shatter the unified command of the post-[[1884]] resistance.

### The July 1879 campaign and the September 1879 Selimoen tocht

The campaign against the XXVI Moekims opened in [[1879]], after the [[XXII Moekims]] campaign of July–October [[1878]] had produced the submission of [[Habib Abdoel Rachman]] and [[Tekoe Moeda Baid]] on [[13 October]] [[1878]]. Van Heutsz records the chronology: *toen de veldtocht in de XXVI Moekims was aangevangen* (when the campaign in the XXVI Moekims had begun). The campaign was the second of the two Sagi-campaigns of the Van der Heijden conquest — the operational continuation of the *op Groot-Atjeh geheel veranderen van stelsel* (the complete change of system on Groot-Atjeh) the *inval* of Habib Abdoel Rachman had triggered in June [[1878]]. The July [[1879]] opening is the second of the two *krijgsoperatiën* the dagorder of [[5 September]] [[1879]] records: *De krijgsoperatiën in de XXII en XXVI Moekims, die in Juli van het vorige jaar zijn aangevangen en zoo schitterende uitkomsten hebben opgeleverd* (The military operations in the XXII and XXVI Moekims, which began in July of last year and have yielded such brilliant results).

The campaign culminated in the [[Selimoen tocht (1879)|tocht naar Selimoen]] of September [[1879]] — the final operation that closed the XXVI Moekims campaign and the entire Van der Heijden conquest. Van Heutsz records the tocht as the *schitterend besloten* (brilliantly concluded) operation: *Met den veldtocht tegen de XXVI Moekims, schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen; is geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* (With the campaign against the XXVI Moekims, brilliantly concluded by the expedition to Selimoen, all of Groot-Atjeh has been subdued). The Commandant van het Indische leger, who visited Atjeh in September [[1879]] and confirmed the dagorder's declaration, recorded the operation plan in his own report: *aan het stoute, met beleid ontworpen operatieplan volgens hetwelk Indrapoeri werd overmeesterd, de rechter rivieroever gezuiverd, Gleinn aangetast, de XXVI Moekims van verschillende zijden binnengedrongen en Selimoen door colonnes uit Lamtoba en Indrapoeri bezocht werd, hebben wij het groote succes te danken* (to the bold, well-designed operational plan by which Indrapoeri was overpowered, the right river-bank cleared, Gleinn attacked, the XXVI Moekims penetrated from various sides, and Selimoen visited by columns from Lamtoba and Indrapoeri, we owe the great success). The tocht was thus a multi-column operation: [[Indrapoeri]] was overpowered, the right river-bank was cleared, [[Gleinn]] was attacked, the XXVI Moekims was penetrated from several sides, and [[Selimoen]] was visited by columns sent out from [[Lamtoba]] and Indrapoeri. The September [[1879]] tocht was the operational climax of the Van der Heijden conquest.

### The Kroeng Kali and Tjot Lepong limits

The September [[1879]] reconnaissance of the conquered XXVI Moekims reached the northern limit of [[Kroeng Kali]] and the eastern limit of [[Tjot Lepong]]. The Commandant's report recorded the limits: *in de XXVI Moekims tot Kroeng Kali in het Noorden, tot Tjot Lepong in het Oosten, en in de XXV Moekims tot Kroeng Raba* (in the XXVI Moekims as far as Kroeng Kali in the north, as far as Tjot Lepong in the east, and in the XXV Moekims as far as Kroeng Raba). The Kroeng Kali northern limit and the Tjot Lepong eastern limit fixed the territorial extent of the XXVI Moekims conquest: the *panglima sagi*'s territory ran north to Kroeng Kali and east to Tjot Lepong, and the September [[1879]] reconnaissance confirmed that the Dutch authority now extended across the entire Sagi. The Commandant's report recorded the personal character of the reconnaissance: *hoezeer slechts vergezeld door een twaalftal cavaleristen, nergens een schot gehoord of een gewapend Atjeher gezien heb* (although accompanied only by a dozen cavalrymen, he had nowhere heard a shot or seen an armed Atjehnese). The September [[1879]] reconnaissance was the empirical proof that the XXVI Moekims conquest had been substantive, not merely formal.

### The day-order of 5 September 1879

Van der Heijden marked the completion of the XXVI Moekims conquest — and, with it, of the entire Van der Heijden conquest of Groot-Atjeh — with the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] from [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]], the text of which Van Heutsz reproduces verbatim in the [[1893]] tract. The dagorder opens: *Dagorder. Officieren, Ambtenaren, Onderofficieren en Soldaten! Met den veldtocht tegen de XXVI Moekims, schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen; is geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* (Day-order. Officers, Officials, Non-commissioned officers and Soldiers! With the campaign against the XXVI Moekims, brilliantly concluded by the expedition to Selimoen, all of Groot-Atjeh has been subdued). The XXVI Moekims is thus named in the very opening sentence of the dagorder — the Sagi whose conquest was the immediate occasion of the *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* declaration. Van Heutsz's verdict on the chronology is sweeping: *In September 1879, nog geen 14 maanden na het begin van zijne zegerijke veldtochten, had Generaal v. d. Heijden aan alle verzet een einde gemaakt, was hij de door den vijand gevreesde overwinnaar van het aloude manfeliklijke Atjehsche rijk* (In September 1879, barely 14 months after the beginning of his victorious campaigns, Generaal v. d. Heijden had made an end to all resistance, was he the victor — feared by the enemy — of the ancient manly Atjehsche empire).

### Post-1879 residual resistance

The Van der Heijden conquest of the XXVI Moekims did not, however, extinguish the Atjehnese resistance. The post-[[1879]] residual resistance is the subject of the closing movement of Van Heutsz's tract. Van der Heijden was dismissed *onverantwoordelijk* (irresponsibly) in early [[1881]] — *ware hij niet in het begin van 1881 op onverantwoordelijke wijze ontslagen, dan behoorde de Atjeh-krijg thans reeds lang tot het verledene* (had he not been dismissed in early 1881 in an irresponsible manner, the Atjeh-war would by now long belong to the past) — and the *Civiel Gouverneur* regime of [[1881]]–[[1883]] and the [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]] of [[1884]] undid the operational prestige the Van der Heijden conquest had established. The XXVI Moekims, like the rest of Groot-Atjeh, was abandoned by the [[1884]] Concentratie withdrawal: *ons prijsgeven van de veroverde positie in de vallei van Groot-Atjeh en ons verhuizen op groote schaal* (our surrender of the conquered position in the valley of Groot-Atjeh and our moving-house on a large scale) into the *geconcentreerde stelling*. The post-[[1884]] resistance in the XXVI Moekims was, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the territory in which [[Tengkoe di Tirou]] — the unifier of the Groot-Atjeh resistance after the Concentratie — would establish his *wereldlijke vlag* (worldly flag) by the over-reach into the worldly authority of the *panglima sagi XXVI*. The XXVI Moekims was thus both the territory of the Van der Heijden conquest's greatest triumph and the territory of the post-[[1884]] resistance's most consequential political crisis. (The Kruisheer *ATJEH '96* account of the [[1896]] operations in the Sagi der XXVI Moekims — described in the sections above — is the operational record of the post-*buitenlinie*-evacuation phase of that post-[[1884]] resistance, after Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract was written.)

### Significance (Van Heutsz account)

The XXVI Moekims's significance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract rests on its being the closing chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest — the Sagi whose reduction fixed the *nog geen 14 maanden* precedent and whose abandonment by the [[1884]] Concentratie exposed the policy oscillation the rest of the tract diagnoses; the second of the three Sagi's of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — the territorial league of twenty-six mukims whose reduction was the operational continuation of the [[XXII Moekims]] campaign of [[1878]] and the immediate occasion of the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] declaring *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt*; the territory whose July–September [[1879]] campaign culminated in the [[Selimoen tocht (1879)|tocht naar Selimoen]] — the multi-column operation that closed the Van der Heijden conquest; the territory whose September [[1879]] reconnaissance reached the Kroeng Kali northern limit and the Tjot Lepong eastern limit; and the territory whose post-[[1879]] residual resistance — exposed by the [[1884]] Concentratie and unified by [[Tengkoe di Tirou]] — would become the operational centre of the post-[[1884]] Groot-Atjeh resistance.

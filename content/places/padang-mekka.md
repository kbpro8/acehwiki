**Padang Mekka** — known in Arabic as the **Padang Mahshar** (the Plain of the Resurrection, *al-mahshar*) and in Achehnese as **Padang meuka** — is the eschatological plain upon which, according to the Mohammedan faith, the dead shall be raised at the last day and assembled for judgement. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Snouck Hurgronje invokes the plain in connection with the choice of sacrificial animals and with the popular eschatology of the Achehnese.

## The Sacrifice and the Steed of the Resurrection

The author explains that the choice of the sacrificial animal in Acheh is regulated by a belief in the widespread Eastern Archipelago, that an animal offered as a sacrifice will hereafter serve the sacrificer as a steed upon the "plain of the resurrection" (*padang meuka*). A goat is too small for this purpose, and a buffalo, accustomed as it is to wallow in mud and shallow water, might inconvenience his rider at the Last Day. The Achehnese therefore commonly choose a cow, or — among the wealthier — a horse or camel where these are obtainable, in order that the sacrifice may serve the worshipper as a fitting mount upon the Plain of the Resurrection. The belief is connected with the great sacrificial feast of the *Hajj*, celebrated in the valley of [[Muna (ancient Mina)|Muna]] near [[Mekka]].

## Achehnese Eschatology

The mention of the Padang Mekka — literally the "plain of Mekka" — reflects the way in which the Achehnese imagination fuses the sacred geography of the *Hajj* with the eschatological geography of Islam. The plain upon which the dead shall be raised is, in Achehnese thought, the same plain upon which the pilgrims assemble at Mekka; and the sacrifice offered at home is conceived as a vicarious rite, by which the worshipper secures for himself a mount upon that same plain. The popular etymology which connects the *padang meuka* with the *Padang Mekka* testifies to the central place which the holy city occupies in the Achehnese religious imagination.

**Peudawa Rajeu** — pronounced *Pedawa Raje*, *rajeu* meaning *great* — is a small *landschap* in the *onderafdeeling* [[Langsa|Langsa]], lying near [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula (Perlak)]] on the eastern coast of Atjeh. The place was formerly a bone of contention between [[Idi|Idi]] and Peureula, and the small *landschap* survived despite the dispute.

## Foundation and Population
When the Dutch authority established itself in the years [[1873]]–[[1875]] in these regions, Peudawa could be at rest. The road from Peureula to Pedawa passes through a swamp of, Broersma was told, some six hundred hectares, which could be drained. Another question is whether suitable building-ground would then be found. Remarkable is the fine white gravel that is found in this region.

From the border of Peureula the road to Pedawa is a splendid *Tjemara*-avenue, the pride of the *landschap*, which however has little else on which it can pride itself. Traders from [[Lho Seumawe|Lho Seumawe]], coming in here to avoid bad weather at sea, found in older times an opportunity for settlement; and pepper-planters from elsewhere also populated Peudawa. It is certain that it has had a past with more population and more trade. Of the 22.000 *picol* of pepper it once produced, perhaps twenty *picol* of pepper remain. The population counts only four hundred souls; evidently many have moved away to regions where living and working were more pleasant. Emigration without known reasons could count as an accusation against the government.

## The Oeléebalang
The *oeléebalang* of Peudawa Rajeu was [[Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih|Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih]], an Arabic-descended chief who received a large silver presentation-tray from the government in [[1875]] and the great star of *trouw en verdienste* in [[1913]]. The *landschap* was formerly of sufficient importance that Chinese were willing to farm its import-duties.

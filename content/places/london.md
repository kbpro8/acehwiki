**London** — the seat of the English East India Company's Court of Directors — is mentioned in *The Achehnese* in connection with the early British appraisal of the political and commercial prospects of [[Acheh]]. In [[1786]], [[Captain Francis Light|Captain Francis Light]] pointed out to the Governor-General of Bengal that a settlement at Pulau Pinang ([[Penang]]) possessed greater advantages than one on the Achehnese coast; and in the same spirit James Price wrote to the Chairman of the Company in London, declaring his preference for Penang over the port of Acheen.

## The Price Letter

The passage from James Price's letter, quoted by Snouck Hurgronje from P. H. van der Kemp's essay in the *Bijdragen van het Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch Indië* (vol. LI, 1900), reads:

> "I prefer Penang to the Port of Acheen as being more healthy and entirely free from that oppression, luar and confusion which have ever distressed and at length driven out every European nation who has attempted to settle there."

London thus figures in the introductory chapter of *The Achehnese* as the metropolis to which the practical experience of the British in the Straits was reported, and in which the eventual decision to abstain from direct intervention in Acheh was effectively taken.

## The British Standing Problem

Snouck Hurgronje cites the letter, together with Light's earlier advice, as one of the documentary proofs that the difficulty of "subduing all the chiefs" of Acheh — their name was legion — had been clearly perceived by British observers at the very moment when the East India Company was first consolidating its position in the Malay Peninsula. In [[1825]], Governor R. Fullerton similarly wrote that the establishment of European influence over Acheh, "long considered a desirable object," had been found "utterly impracticable without employing a large military force to overawe the inhabitants." The deliberations in London that accepted this judgement determined, in effect, the framework within which the subsequent [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|Anglo-Dutch arrangement of 1824]] and the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1871|treaty of 1871]] would allocate responsibilities for the Achehnese coast to the Netherlands.

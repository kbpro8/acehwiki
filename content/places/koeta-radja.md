**Koeta-Radja** — also *Kutaraja*, modern *Banda Aceh* — was the *hoofdplaats* (capital) of the *gewest* (province) of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] and the principal garrison of the [[K.N.I.L.]] in the late colonial period, the seat of the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] before the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and the seat of the Dutch military and civilian administration after. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* Koeta-Radja is the capital at which [[Dominee Thenu]] was placed, the *Soos* (officers' club) where the captain of the [[1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren]] told his [[Hotel de Boer]] story around [[1933]], and the place of Thenu's [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|funeral]] on [[11 May]] [[1937]].

## The colonial capital

Koeta-Radja was the *hoofdplaats* (capital) of the *gewest* (province) of [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. The end-point of the route from the [[Blang Kedjeren|Blang]] via [[Medan]] — "vanwaar men met de trein in twee dagen in Koeta-Radja, de hoofdplaats van het gewest was" — the capital was the seat of the Dutch military and civilian administration of Aceh. The *Soos* (Sociëteit, officers' club) of Koeta-Radja was the social centre of the colonial officer corps; the captain of the [[1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren]] told his Hotel de Boer story at the *Soos* around [[1933]].

## The dominee's post

"Er was in Koeta Radja een dominee geplaatst" — a dominee was placed in Koeta-Radja. The post of the *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* (indigenous religion teacher) attached to the garrison of Koeta-Radja was the post [[Dominee Thenu]] accepted in [[1894]]. The dominee visited the zieken in the hospitaal and held kerkdiensten for the garnizoen. Newcomers to Aceh always started at Koeta-Radja. "Als Dominee Thenu iets ongunstigs over een post in Koeta Radja meldde, was de toestand onhoudbaar" — if Dominee Thenu reported something unfavourable about a post "in Koeta-Radja," it meant the situation was untenable.

## The Soos and the Hotel de Boer story

"Op de Soos in Koeta Radja" the captain of the [[1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren]] told his Hotel de Boer story around [[1933]]. The story — almost certainly embellished — was told to *Atjehmenseni* (Acehnese-experienced officers) who knew the hotel and "hebben het niet voor onmogelijk gehouden" — did not consider it impossible. The *Soos* was therefore the colonial officers' club where the tall stories of the colonial army were told and tested.

## The funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)

When [[Dominee Thenu]] died on [[11 May]] [[1937]], his [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|funeral]] was held at Koeta-Radja. Four retired [[Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)|M.W.O.]]-ridders — [[Salawono]] (3e kl.), [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]] and [[Pattipuluhu]] — acted as *slippendragers*; a Marechaussee brigade served as *vuurpeleton*; he was buried at [[Peutjoet]]. "Vóór zijn kerkje in Koeta-Radja richtte Atjeh voor hem een standbeeld op" — before his little church in Koeta-Radja, Aceh erected for him a statue.

## Significance

Koeta-Radja is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the colonial capital of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] and the principal garrison of the [[K.N.I.L.]] in the late colonial period. The book's argument is that Koeta-Radja was the place at which the moral economy of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] was centred: the dominee's post, the *Soos*, the *kerkje*, the *standbeeld*. The [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|funeral of Thenu]] at Koeta-Radja is the book's moral closure of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]: the men of courage carrying the man of faith, in the capital of the country they had fought for forty-five years to subdue.

The **Bridge at Anagaloeng** — *Brug bij Anagaloeng* — was the iron bridge thrown across the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]] at [[Anagaloeng]] in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], one of the twenty-four plates of *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]* and the structure whose deck-planks were afterwards used by [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] to build the great Achehnese dwelling that served the Dutch as officers' quarters.

## Description

Hooijer wrote that the nearness of the broad, mirroring stream, with the iron bridge that had been thrown across it, already lent something cheerful and something healthy to a stay at so picturesque a point. The bridge belonged to the period when [[General van der Heijden]] had pitched two bivouacs on both banks of the river in preparation for continuing his campaign of conquest through the valley.

## The float-bridge and river traffic

A separate *vlotbrug* — float-bridge — connected the two encampments, and over it soldiers, [[Chinese sutler in the Atjeh outposts|Chinese]] and native women passed continually. On the stream lay the great Chinese barges in which the sick were carried down to the [[Kraton (institution)|Kraton]]; and in the water soldiers of every skin colour floundered together. On one occasion the river rose so high that the encampments were buried two metres deep and the soldiers had to take refuge on the high *kampong* (village) ground of [[Anagaloeng]].

## Afterlife of the timbers

When the Dutch built their model *[[Benteng|benteng]]* at [[Anagaloeng]] in the 1890s, the large Achehnese dwelling used as officers' quarters was, Hooijer recorded, the house that [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] had raised *uit de dekplanken van onze brug* — from the deck-planks of the Dutch bridge.

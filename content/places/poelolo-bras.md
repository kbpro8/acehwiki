**Poelolo Bras** (modern Pulau Breueh) is the island off the Atjeh coast on which the lighthouse — the first permanent Dutch light on the Atjeh coast — was completed on [[20 June]] [[1875]] and solemnly inaugurated a month later. Borel calls it "een werk, waarbij de geheele beschaafde wereld belang had" — a work in which the whole civilised world had an interest — and "tot groote eer" for the Dutch, but refuses to judge whether its *nut* bears any proportion to the "kolossale offers" (colossal sacrifices) the war had cost.

## The lighthouse project

Work on the lighthouse was pushed forward "met den meesten ijver" during the *ad interim* administration of [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] after [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s departure on three months' leave on [[12 June]] [[1875]]. The *Genie* (engineer corps) had been steadily *ijverig bezig* with kazernen, hospitalen, magazijnen, officiers-woningen and koelie-kampementen throughout the occupation, and the Poelolo Bras lighthouse was one of its most prominent projects. The choice of an offshore island reflected both the practical need for a sea-mark visible at distance and the strategic fact that, with the [[Atjeh-rivier]] mouth at [[Kwala Atjeh]] often *onbevaarbaar* (unnavigable) during the *kwaden moesson* (bad monsoon), ships approaching the Atjeh coast needed a clear koers to find their way to the roadstead at [[Oeleh Leh]].

## Completion and inauguration

On [[20 June]] [[1875]] the lighthouse was completed and the light could be *ontstoken* (lit) to mark the koers for ships at night; a month later it was *plechtig ingewijd* (solemnly inaugurated). Borel's verdict is double-edged: he calls the lighthouse "a work in which the whole civilised world had an interest" and "to the great honour" of the Dutch, but he refuses to judge whether its *nut* bears any proportion to the "kolossale offers" the war had cost. The ambiguity is Borel's characteristic irony: the lighthouse, by marking the approaches to Atjeh, would help ships of all nations — but it had only become possible because of the war that had cost the Dutch so dear. By [[1878]] the lighthouse stood as one of the few visible fruits of the war, alongside the [[Oeleh Leh|pile pier]], the field railway from K. Radja to Oeleh Leh, the telegraph to Java, and the *artesische put* (artesian well) inside the Kraton.

**Péndéngvallei** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[F. Darlang]]

In [[1910]] "the Darlang column remained in the [[Péndéngvallei|Péndéng valley]], so that [[Lebé Grondong]] (a notorious resistance leader) moved with his henchmen to the neighbouring sub-divisions."

### From [[Lebé Grondong]]

In [[1910]] the column of [[F. Darlang|Darlang]] held the [[Péndéngvallei]], so that Lebé Grondong moved with his followers to neighbouring sub-divisions. In March [[1911]] a brigade under sergeant [[Wisse]] surprised his hiding-place between [[Tampor]] and [[Lésten]] and reported him killed with his principal *panglima* and a follower, the booty being an M.95 rifle, a Mauser repeating rifle, a Beaumont and a Mauser self-loading pistol. In September came news that he was *not* dead: he had toured the Gajò Loeö during the *hari raja* feasts extorting from the population and had obtained two carbines on the death of the resistance head of [[Badaq]]; in October he murdered [[Rödjö Akoeb]] of [[Penosan]] in revenge for the latter's journey to [[Koeta Radja|Koetaradja]] — but handed in a *panglima*, a *[[goeroe]]* and a carbine. In [[1912]] he came in submission.

### From [[Gajòlanden]]

On [[20 September]] the hiding-place of old [[Rödjö Pasér]] with his religious men was raided; among seven killed were the two principal Teungkoes and two feared commanders of [[Reröbö]]; shortly after, [[Tgk. Kalipah]], principal resistance leader of [[Penosan]], was rendered harmless; in October the Penosan band was attacked and Tgk. Kalipah's brother killed. The column [[F. Darlang|Darlang]] held the [[Péndéngvallei]], so that [[Lebé Grondong]] moved to neighbouring sub-divisions. Total losses that year: 141 killed, nine prisoners, 302 submissions; 216 *uitgewekenen* remained.

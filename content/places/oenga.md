**Oenga** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Nja' Ali]]

**Nja' Ali** — also **Si Ali of [[Oenga]]** — was the Acehnese who murdered the Civil Authority [[W. van Kregten]] and two soldiers at [[Lam No]] on [[20 May]] [[1914]] and thereby became, in a few weeks, a band-head of reputation in the [[Daja]] country. He was killed by lieutenant [[C. A. Snell]] at the holy grave near the mouth of the [[Daja-rivier]] on [[5 August]] [[1914]].

### From [[Nja' Ali]]

[[H. N. A. Swart]]'s own investigation, conducted personally in a district he knew through and through, established the cause: "a certain Si Ali of [[Oenga]] had long had good reason to be aggrieved at the *[[oelèëbalang]]* of [[Lambeusdë]], because the latter stood in an illicit understanding with Ali's wife. More than once matters had come to an outburst between the two, and the fact that Si Ali did not proceed to an attack on this chief earlier must be attributed to the fact that an Acehnese does not readily raise his hands against a territorial chief."

### From [[W. van Kregten]]

He was killed by a band of twelve men armed with *[[rentjong]]* and *[[klewang]]* from the *[[landschap]]pen* [[Lam No]], [[Panté Tjeureumèn]], [[Djeunamprong]] and [[Oenga]], who fled to the mountains with the soldiers' weapons. The night quarters had been chosen and occupied "in the most utterly careless manner", the weapons of both soldiers lying on the floor of two different unwalled rooms; in the morning, while the soldiers had left their weapons to drink coffee and the lieutenant was innocently winding his puttees, [[Nja' Ali|Si Ali]] and four others attacked him. The cause lay in the dismissal of Si Ali, at Van Kregten's instigation, from his post as *mandoer* of a coconut garden, and in a personal grudge against the *[[oelèëbalang]]* of [[Lambeusdë]]. [[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] added the note: "Van Kregten was a quiet, calm, well-balanced man."

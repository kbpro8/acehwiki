**Soezkanaal** — the Suez Canal, opened in [[1869]] — is the maritime infrastructure whose completion transformed the strategic and economic value of [[Straat Malakka]] as the shipping lane between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea. The canal's opening is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] narrative, the proximate cause of the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] and therefore of the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|Dutch declaration of war on Atjeh]] of [[1873]].

## The changed constellation

The [[Netherlands|Dutch]] government's argument for the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] — and, two years later, for the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war on Atjeh]] — was that the [[Soezkanaal|opening of the Suez Canal]] in [[1869]] had changed the political-economic constellation of the Indo-European sea-lanes. [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] records: "Juist hierdoor was de economische en militaire betekenis van [[Straat Malakka]] als verbindingsweg tussen Indische Oceaan en Chinese Zee aanzienlijk gestegen en de regering had dan ook welbewust om deze reden het Sumatra-tractaat met Engeland gesloten" — Just through this, the economic and military significance of the Strait of Malacca as a connecting route between the Indian Ocean and the Chinese Sea had considerably increased, and the government had therefore consciously, for this reason, concluded the Sumatra-tractaat with England.

## Significance for Atjeh

The [[Soezkanaal|Suez Canal]] is the indirect cause of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]]. By transforming [[Straat Malakka]] into a major artery of world trade, the canal made the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate — which commands the northern entrance to the strait — strategically valuable to the [[Netherlands]] in a way it had not been before. The diplomatic and military sequence — [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]], [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war]] of [[1873]] — is the immediate political consequence of the canal's opening.

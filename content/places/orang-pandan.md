**The orang-Pandan** — the Pandan people, identified by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau as the inhabitants of the [[Petalangan]] country — were, in the Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the people who, in the early sixteenth century, collaborated with the [[Acheh|Atjehers]] in the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|destruction of the kingdom of Gasip]]. The Bureau's parenthetical gloss — "(de bewoners der Petalangan)" — fixes the orang-Pandan as the inhabitants of the [[Petalangan]] country, an interior stretch of land between the lower [[Siak|Siak River]] and the Minangkabau highlands, and it presents them as the indigenous allies of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] in the campaign that overthrew the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]].

## The alliance with Atjeh

The Bureau records the alliance in a single, dense sentence: "Toen de Atjehers, met behulp van de orang-Pandan (de bewoners der Petalangan) Gasip overweldigd hadden, voerden zij de vorsten gevankelijk mede naar Atjeh en lieten het verwoeste rijk achter ten prooi aan ontbinding" — "When the Atjehers, with the help of the orang-Pandan (the inhabitants of the Petalangan), had overwhelmed Gasip, they led the princes away captive to Atjeh and left the destroyed realm to the prey of dissolution." The orang-Pandan thus appear in the Bureau's account as the indispensable local allies of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] campaign: without their collaboration, the Atjehnese projection of force into the southern interior of [[Sumatra]] — far from their [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] base — could not have been sustained.

## The geographical context

The [[Petalangan]] country inhabited by the orang-Pandan is, in the Bureau's framing, an interior stretch of land between the lower [[Siak|Siak River]] and the Minangkabau highlands. The Bureau's placement of the orang-Pandan in the [[Petalangan]] country — rather than on the coast — signals that the alliance with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] was an alliance between a coastal power and an inland people, with the inland people providing the local knowledge and the manpower that the coastal power needed to project force into the interior.

## The aftermath of the destruction

The Bureau does not record the subsequent fate of the orang-Pandan. Its account of the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|destruction of Gasip]] ends with the deportation of the Gasip princes to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the dissolution of the destroyed realm; the orang-Pandan do not reappear in the Bureau's narrative. Their collaboration with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] did not, it appears, translate into Atjehnese overlordship over the [[Petalangan]] country: the Atjehnese projection of force into the southern interior was, the Bureau's account suggests, a one-off campaign rather than the basis of a sustained Atjehnese presence south of the [[Siak|Siak River]].

## Significance

The orang-Pandan are, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the only named inland people recorded as collaborating with the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] in the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|destruction of Gasip]]. Their collaboration is the empirical basis of the Bureau's reading of the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|conquest of Gasip]] as an inter-ethnic campaign, and it is the only documented instance of an Atjehnese alliance with an inland Sumatran people against a coastal Sumatran polity. The orang-Pandan thus represent, in the Bureau's framing, the inland dimension of the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|long Atjehnese overlordship of the East Coast]] — the dimension that, in the Bureau's account of the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehsche oppermacht in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]], would later be institutionalised in the highlands through the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]].

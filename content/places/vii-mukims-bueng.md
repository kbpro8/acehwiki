The **VII Mukims Bueng** (Achehnese *Mukim Tujoh Bu'ëng*, "Seven Mukims of the Swamp") is a subdivision of the [[XXVI Mukims|Sagi of the XXVI Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh|Great Acheh]]. Its inhabitants are called the *ureuëng Bu'ëng* ("people of the swamp"), and the district is distinguished by a peculiar dialect and by customs which, although belonging politically to a lowland sagi, have far more in common with those of the [[Tunong|highlanders]] than with the rest of the [[XXVI Mukims]].

## The swamp and its dialect

The word *bu'ëng* itself belongs to the peculiar dialect of the VII Mukims Bueng; it means "swamp," a sense rendered in other parts of Acheh by *paya*. So characteristic is this usage that *sawahs* (irrigated rice-fields) situated in swampy land are called in the VII Mukims Bueng simply *buëng* — "the swamp." The same word also means the terracing of *sawahs* made on the slopes of hills. The dialect of the VII Mukims Bueng, together with that of the [[XXII Mukims|highlands]] more generally, is described by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] as having so much in common with the *Tunong* (the highlanders) that the father of the community is called *waki* (rather than *keuchi'*, as in the rest of the lowlands).

## Language and customs of the highlands

The VII Mukims Bueng is the part of the [[XXVI Mukims]] that has most in common with the [[XXII Mukims|highlands]] in language and manners. The *ureuëng Bu'ëng* are described as having almost the same language and customs as the *ureuëng tunong*; their dialect and customs stand in the same relation to the Tunong as do those of the highland districts proper.

This highland affinity is reflected in their political temper. It is in the [[Tunong|Tunong]] (the XXII Mukims), in [[Pidie]], and in the VII Mukims Bueng that the *kawom* — the clan system, the most archaic of Achehnese political institutions — has maintained the greatest force. The political development of the VII Mukims Bueng is thus "most backward," in the same sense as that of the highlands.

## Marriage customs

In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, the VII Mukims Bueng are cited as an example of regional variation in marriage custom. In addition to the bridal couch and the dowry, the attendants of the bridegroom bring with them two sets of "standing sirih" (*ranub dông*) of the customary kind. "In some districts (as for instance the Mukims Bu'ëng) this is replaced by a sweetmeat in the form of network, called *halua puloët*." Both of these are intended for distribution among the onlookers. The custom illustrates the way in which the swamp-dwellers of the [[XXVI Mukims]] preserved local peculiarities in matters where the rest of Acheh had adopted a different usage.

## Agriculture and material culture

The VII Mukims Bueng is repeatedly mentioned in *The Achehnese* in connection with the agriculture of the swampy lands. The second method of *padi*-planting — by means of a nursery bed (*planteuëng*), in which the seed is strewn to obtain seedlings — was adopted in a portion of the highlands, occasionally in the lowlands, in the [[IV Mukims|IV Mukims]], and with certain modifications in the swampy districts of the VII Mukims Bueng. The cane of the VII Mukims Bueng is, like that of the [[XXII Mukims]] and the IV Mukims, of inferior quality; in all these places, accordingly, molasses is chiefly manufactured in the very primitive Achehnese sugar-mills.

**Rotterdam** — the major port city of the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] at the mouth of the Maas — was, in the source *DLP-84-Atjeh*, the birthplace of the Catholic military chaplain [[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] on [[24 March]] [[1835]], before his conversion to the priesthood and his long service in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] as the *soldaten-pastoor* of the colonial army. The city's connection to the Atjeh campaigns is therefore primarily biographical — through the person of [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]] — but it also reflects the wider pattern by which the commercial and maritime life of the Netherlands supplied the personnel of the colonial project in the Netherlands Indies.

## Early Life of Pastoor Verbraak

Verbraak's birth at Rotterdam placed him, in the words of his biographer [[S. Kalff|S. Kalff]], "initially destined for commerce" (Dutch: *oorspronkelijk voor den handel bestemd*) — a destiny appropriate to the commercial milieu of the great Maas port, whose merchant houses and shipping lines were the principal European interface with the colonial trade of the Netherlands Indies. He turned to the priesthood only at the age of twenty-seven, entering the clerical state and receiving priestly ordination at [[Maastricht|Maastricht]] in [[1869]], after a year's teaching at the college of [[Sittard|Sittard]]. He then served at [[Tronchiennes|Tronchiennes]] near [[Gent|Gent]] and at [[Nijmegen|Nijmegen]] before being appointed, by Royal Decree (Dutch: *Koninklijk Besluit*) in [[1872]], as a *R. K. geestelijke van den tweeden rang* (Roman Catholic clergyman of the second rank) in the [[Dutch East Indies|Netherlands Indies]]. His first posting was at [[Padang|Padang]] on [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]], where he remained only briefly before being transferred in [[1874]] to his true field of labour — the theatre of war in Atjeh (Dutch: *oorlogsterrein in Atjeh*). The commercial milieu of Rotterdam thus gave way, in Verbraak's career, to the chaplaincy of the colonial army — a trajectory that connects the maritime city of the Netherlands to the colonial war-footing of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]].

## Rotterdam and the Colonial Project

Beyond its connection to Verbraak, Rotterdam was one of the principal European ports of the colonial trade of the Netherlands Indies — the port through which the pepper, coffee, sugar, and tobacco of the archipelago were distributed to the wider European market, and through which the manufactured goods, the textiles, and the personnel of the colonial project were shipped to the Indies. The [[Dutch East India Company|Dutch East India Company]] had maintained its principal European establishment at Rotterdam in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, and the nineteenth-century colonial revival that followed the [[Agrarische Wet 1870|Agrarian Law of 1870]] saw the city's commercial houses once again take a leading role in the Netherlands Indies trade. The chaplaincy of [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]] — born at Rotterdam, ordained at [[Maastricht|Maastricht]], and posted to the war-footing of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] — is thus a single instance of the wider pattern by which the commercial and maritime life of the Netherlands supplied the personnel, both military and religious, of the colonial project in the Indies.

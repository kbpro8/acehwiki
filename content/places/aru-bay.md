**Aru Bay** (modern Teluk Aru) is an oil-field locality on the north-eastern coast of [[Sumatra]], immediately to the south of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] border, recorded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) as one of the producing fields of the North Sumatra oil province in the [[1940]]–[[1941]] period. The Aru Bay field was operated by the [[B.P.M.]] (Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij, the Royal Dutch subsidiary), and its production of 55,924 tons of 1000 kg in [[1940]] and 55,070 tons in [[1941]] made it the second-largest producing field of the North Sumatra oil province after the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]].

## Production figures

For the full North-Sumatran production table see the entry on the [[East Atjeh oil block]]. The Aru Bay field's production of 55,924 tons in [[1940]] and 55,070 tons in [[1941]] was remarkably stable between the two years — a fall of only 854 tons, or 1.5 % — in contrast to the sharp contraction seen in the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]] (–25.7 %) and the [[Peureula]] field (–24.0 %) over the same period. This stability suggests that the Aru Bay field was a mature, well-managed B.P.M. operation with a stable reservoir and established production infrastructure, in contrast to the more recently-developed fields that were more vulnerable to the economic disruptions of [[1941]].

The "S. Aru Bay (block)" listed separately in Van Bemmelen's Table 4 — with 2,100 tons in [[1940]] and 5,030 tons in [[1941]] — is presumably a southern extension or satellite of the main Aru Bay field.

## Position relative to Atjeh

Aru Bay lies immediately to the south of the Atjeh border, in the residency of the East Coast of Sumatra rather than in Atjeh proper. However, the field is included in the North Sumatra oil province of which the Atjeh fields — the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh block]], the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]], and the [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields — are part. The Aru Bay field is accordingly the southern anchor of the same petroleum-geological system that produced the Atjeh oil fields, and its inclusion in any account of the colonial oil economy of Atjeh is accordingly warranted on geological and economic grounds, even though the field itself is not on Atjeh territory.

## Geological context

Van Bemmelen's general account of the North Sumatra petroleum geology — "the northern part of Sumatra has been explored by the Netherlands Government and by the B.P.M. and N.K.P.M. All important fields have probably been found, so that future development depends on the known fields, of which Rantau is by far the best" — places Aru Bay among the established B.P.M. fields of the East-Coast-of-Sumatra petroleum province. The Aru Bay field is hosted in the same neogene sedimentary system that hosts the Tamiang and East-Atjeh fields further north, and its geological structure is accordingly of the gently-domed anticlinal type that characterised the productive East-Indian oil fields of the late colonial period.

## Significance

Aru Bay is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the southern anchor of the North Sumatra oil province of which the Atjeh fields are the northern component. The Aru Bay field's production of 55,924 tons in [[1940]] was the second-largest in the North Sumatra oil province, and its stability between [[1940]] and [[1941]] makes it a useful benchmark against which to measure the contraction of the Atjeh-proper fields over the same period. The field is also a B.P.M. operation, which links it directly to the [[B.P.M.]]-operated [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]] and [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] fields on Atjeh territory and to the wider Royal Dutch petroleum economy of northern Sumatra.

Referenced in several contexts. 'Some undergo a wider course of study in the Straits Settlements or at Mekka.' Panglima Tibang was a member of Achehnese embassies to [[Riouw]] and Singapore. The book Tambih rapilin was printed at Singapore: 'A few years ago this work was printed at the lithographing establishment of Haji Tirmidi in Singapore, but in a most slovenly manner.' Copies of a new edition of the wasiat nabi (last admonition of the Prophet) were 'printed and reprinted in Malay at Singapore, [[Palembang]] etc.' and 'Achehnese who took to the streets with young boys in Singapore were sometimes jeered at.' The Malay version of [[Hikayat Indra Bangsawan]] was published at Singapore in A.H. 1310. [[Batavia]]: the Malay MSS of the Batavian Association are cited throughout. A number of Achehnese who remained in [[Acheh]] 'as traders or teachers' had come through the pilgrimage route via [[Acheh]] to Singapore and on to Arabia. [[India]] (Hindustan) [[India]] is cited throughout as the ultimate source of many Islamic mystical traditions, hikayat materials, and religious legends that reached [[Acheh]]. 'There can be no doubt... that this mysticism was brought hither by the pioneers of Islam from Hindustan.' 'The most important works on mysticism in vogue in the Archipelago were penned by Indian writers, or else are derived from a body of mystics which flourished in [[Medina]] in the 17th century and which was strongly subject to Indian influence.' [[Raniri]] from Gujerat is mentioned as a non- Achehnese pandit who wrote the Qirat al-mustaqim in [[Acheh]]. The 16th-17th century pandits who held mastery in [[Acheh]] were 'not Achehnese, but either Syrians or Egyptians who came to [[Acheh]] from Mekka, or else natives of [[India]], such as [[Raniri]] from Gujerat.' 'The South-Indian Islam, the oldest form in which Mohammedanism came to this Archipelago still survives in these works.' Many popular romances in Achehnese and Malay can be traced to South [[India]]: 'The great majority of romances in both tongues the birthplace ... is that portion of South [[India]] which is also the source whence are derived the popular mysticism and the popular religious legends.' Persian names (Jamshid, Bahramshah, Qubad, etc.) entered the Achehnese hikayats through Indian intermediaries. The [[Menangkabau]] Malays' hikayat Malim Deman is related to the Achehnese [[Malem Diwa]]. The Batak story of Malin Deman also has isolated points of resemblance.

Multiple Malay religious and literary works were published in Singapore: 'A lithographed edition of the Malay version of Indra Bangsawan was published in the month of Muharram A.H. 1310 by Haji Muhammad Tayib at Singapore.' The Tambih rapilin was 'printed at the lithographing establishment of Haji Tirmidi in Singapore.' Also various editions of the wasiat nabi were 'printed and reprinted in Malay at Singapore.'

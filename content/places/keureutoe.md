A kingdom on the North or East Coast of [[Acheh]]. In the Hikayat Eseukanda Ali (XVI), the merchant prince Ali Juhari is established as a sugarcane planter in Keureutoe (Kerti): 'With this in view they purchased land from Ahli, king of Keureutoe and built a sumptuous residence which was called [[Indra Siluka]].' The princess Ra'na Jamin is the daughter of the sovereign of Keureutoe. The Achehnese regard this as a fragment of history of 'Timu' (the East, the North and East Coasts). The giant Mala'oy Rimba also 'had just returned to his forest haunt from Keureutoe, bringing with him the dead body of a girl.'

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]]'s *Atjeh* gives Keureutoe its fullest treatment as a war-era *landschap*. Keureutoe — also *Keureutoë* — was one of the richest and most populous *landschappen* of the North coast. Its *oeléëbalang* bore the honorific ***Kedjooroeann Lalat*** — *the ruler of the land where the people are as numerous as flies* — because the country was so densely peopled.

### Two oeléëbalangs

When [[T. Tjhi Toenong|Teukoe Tjoet Moehamat]] submitted to the Dutch in [[1899]], the *bestuur* appointed instead his half-brother [[T. Tjhi Bentara]], a weakling who could not maintain himself. The Sultan, however, gave T. Tjhi Toenong an appointment sealed with the *tjap sikoereuëng* — so there were *twee oeléëbalangs van Keureutoe*: the Dutch-appointed *baroh* and T. Tjhi Toenong the *toenong*, who held the upper country with the resistance. [[Tjoet Meuthia]] — the *Parel*, the Pearl — was first wife of T. Tjhi Bentara, then of T. Tjhi Toenong, then of [[Pang Nanggroë]]. T. Tjhi Toenong submitted at [[Lho Seumawé]] on [[5 October]] [[1903]], was tried, and executed by firing-squad on the strand of Lho Seumawe on [[5 March]] [[1905]]. His pregnant wife swore to bring their son up in hatred of the *kaphé* and marry Pang Nanggroë.

### The adelaarsjong

The son of T. Tjhi Toenong and Tjoet Meuthia — [[T. Radja Sabi]], the *adelaarsjong* (eaglet) — was carried through the forests for fourteen years, from [[1905]] to [[1919]], sheltered in rice-straw when the maréchaussée searched. He returned on [[13 March]] [[1919]], when a former *kawan* seized his arm at Lho Seumawe: *"Nu moet je mèl!"* In [[1937]] he became *waarnemend oeléëbalang* of Keureutoe.

*In Keureutoe zal de herinnering aan dit alles blijven leven, zoolang het volk hangt aan zijn oud vorstenhuis* — so Zentgraff closes. The *keudé* of [[Lho Soekon]] lies nearby; behind it, within wooden *kandangs*, are the graves of [[Pang Nanggroë]] and [[Pang Lateh]].

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

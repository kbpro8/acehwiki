**Malang** — a city in the eastern highlands of [[Java|Java]] in the [[Dutch East Indies|Netherlands Indies]] — was, in the source *DLP-84-Atjeh*, a posting of the Catholic military chaplain [[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] after his service in [[Padang|Padang]] and his long tenure at [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], and one of the cities in which he continued his work among the colonial troops of the [[Indische Leger|Royal Netherlands East Indies Army (Dutch: *Indische Leger*)]] in the years before his final retirement to [[Magelang|Magelang]]. The city's connection to the Atjeh campaigns is therefore indirect — through the chaplaincy of [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]] — but it illustrates the wider Java-based network of military stations through which the colonial army's spiritual care was organised.

## Pastoral Work of Pastoor Verbraak

Verbraak's apostolate at Malang, recorded in the biographical literature on the *soldaten-pastoor* of Atjeh, involved ministering to the soldiers stationed in the colonial barracks of the city, extending his influence beyond the war-footing of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] to the more routine garrison life of east Java. After leaving Atjeh in [[1908]], Verbraak held a less fatiguing post at [[Padang|Padang]] from [[1908]] to [[1912]], and thereafter served in central and eastern Java — including, according to the source, the posting at Malang — before retiring to [[Magelang|Magelang]] in central Java, where he died in [[1918]]. His body was buried with military honours, and a bronze statue was later raised to him at [[Bandung|Bandoeng]] in [[1922]]. The posting at Malang belongs, therefore, to the closing phase of Verbraak's long colonial career — the phase in which the chaplain of the Atjeh war-footing became the senior Catholic figure of the Netherlands Indies military establishment, and in which his ministry was extended, through the network of Java garrisons, to the soldiers who had inherited the legacy of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## Malang as a Colonial Garrison City

Beyond its connection to Verbraak, Malang was, by the early twentieth century, one of the principal cities of east Java and a major centre of colonial administration, military organisation, and the European plantation economy of the highlands. The city's cool upland climate made it a favoured residence for European planters and colonial officials, and its military garrison was one of the principal stations of the colonial army in east Java. The chaplaincy at Malang — exercised by Verbraak and by his successors — thus belonged to the wider infrastructure of military religion that the colonial state maintained across the Netherlands Indies, from the forward posts of [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram postenlinie]] to the garrison towns of Java. The city's connection to the Atjeh campaigns, through the person of [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]], is a small but telling instance of the way in which the memory and the personnel of the Atjeh war were distributed across the wider colonial archipelago in the decades after [[1904]].

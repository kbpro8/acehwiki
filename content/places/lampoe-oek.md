**Lampoe Oek** was an enemy fortified *stelling* east of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] on the [[Atjeh-rivier]], built by the Atjehneezen in the months after the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] while the Dutch did nothing, and the site of a serious Dutch échec on [[16 April]] [[1874]] in the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|battle of Lampoe Oek]]. The stelling was finally destroyed by artillery on [[18 September]] [[1874]] in the [[Destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification (18 September 1874)|destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification]], and the site was later occupied by a Dutch post known as the *nipa-benteng* (nipa-palm fort).

## The April échec

Borel notes that Lampoe Oek had not existed at the time of the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|Bital]] and [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|Ketapang Doewa]] expeditions in February [[1874]]; it was built between [[12 February]] and [[16 April]] while the Dutch stood idle. On [[16 April]] — exactly one year after the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)|first expedition]] under [[Generaal Köhler|Köhler]] — patrols under kapitein Bardok and 1ste luitenant Scheltus were heavily engaged near the new stelling, and [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]] was sent in with reinforcements. The attack was repulsed by "bijna onoverkomelijke terreinhindernisen" and "woedenden tegenstand." Losses were "bijna 100 dooden en gewonden," of whom five were *vermist*. Borel calls it "eene geweldig échec, dat veel van de vroeger behaalde voordeelen bedierf." Van Swieten was "zeer verbolgen" and blamed Romswinckel entirely, refusing him a hearing.

## Destruction and the nipa-benteng

On [[18 September]] [[1874]], after 150 *klapperbomen* (coconut palms) had been felled — at a cost of nine wounded, including one officer — to clear the field of a new ooster-batterij, the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|12 c.M. achterlaadkanon]] soon found the range and demolished the parapet, overthrowing the *schanskorven* (gabions). The Lampoe Oek stelling was abandoned in haste by the enemy. Borel's verdict is caustic: "What had not been obtained earlier through fierce struggle costing heavy sacrifices was now achieved by a few cannon shots." The site was then occupied by a Dutch post called the *nipa-benteng*, which the enemy attacked in a dawn overval on [[1 February]] [[1875]] in retaliation for the [[Conquest of Mandarsa Poeti (28 January 1875)|conquest of Mandarsa Poeti]]; the garrison, on guard, easily foiled the attempt.

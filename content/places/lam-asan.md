**Lam-Asan** — written in Dutch as also *Lam Asan*, *Lam Asam* — was a major kampong in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]], forming the centre of the fighting on [[23 May]] [[1896]] and attacked and taken by the 9e and 7e Bataljons. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, Lam-Asan was the site of three principal fortifications — *Binasa Belang* in the northern *rand*, *Binasa Ketapang* in the south-eastern abandoned section, and *Balei Ba' Mó* to the east (occupied by Pa Karim) — and was the centre of Umar's defensive *stelling* on the right bank of the [stelling]. Lam-Asan was razed (*geraseerd*) from [[30 May]] to [[2 June]] [[1896]] by 800 forced labourers, 400 Chinese coolies, and engineer troops under the protection of multiple bataljons.

## Description and Location

Lam-Asan lay in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]] between the kampong of [[Lam-Pisang]] on the south-east and the kampong of [[Lam-Loempoer]] on the north-west. The kampong was the centre of Umar's defensive *stelling* on the right wing of his line, with three principal *versterkingen*: *Binasa Belang* in the northern *rand* of the abandoned Lam-Asan, *Binasa Ketapang* in the south-eastern the end-point of the right wing, and also *Balei Ba Mó* to the east, occupied by Pa Karim. The kampong was connected by communication trenches to the neighbouring *versterkingen* of [[Pakan Badak]] (where Umar had his house and a *bivak*), [[Pajating|Paja Trieng]], and [[Lam-Pisang]]. The kampong was bounded on the south by the road toward [[Pasar Oleh-Gli]] and on the north by the heights of [[Boekit Seboen|Bt. Seboen]].

## Historical Background

Lam-Asan had been part of [[Teuku Umar]]'s territory throughout his period as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] ([[1893]]–[[1896]]). The kampong was integrated into Umar's elaborate defensive *stelling* across the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV en VI Moekims]], which was divided into a left wing (from the sea-strand to the centre), a centre (from *binasa Lam Mé* to *Balei Ba' Mó*), and a right wing (from *Binasa Ketapang* to [[Boekit Kesoemba]]). The *Binasa* versterkingen — a name meaning "destruction" or "ruin" — were the principal strongpoints of Umar's right wing. The kampong had been fortified with three successive lines of *versterkingen* and trenches over the years before [[1896]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Lam-Asan was the centre of the fighting on [[23 May]] [[1896]]. The 9e Bataljon under [[J. F. Th. Veeren|luit.-kol. Veeren]] attacked Lam-Asan in one continuous attack; the 1e compagnie (kapitein [[H. T. de Moulin|H. T. de Moulin]]) hit the breastwork behind the Kroeng Ateuh, and the kampong was taken by the 9e and 7e Bataljons. The 2de luit. [[W. A. Engelbrecht|W. A. Engelbrecht]] distinguished himself in the attack. After the capture of [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]] the 5e Bataljon established a *bivak* at [[Pakan Badak]] near Lam-Asan. From [[30 May]] to [[2 June]] [[1896]] the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] was carried out by 800 forced labourers, 400 Chinese coolies, and engineer troops under the protection of multiple bataljons, including the 12e and 14e Bataljons. The razing was the largest single engineering operation of the first semester of [[1896]] and was intended to deny the kampong to any future Achehnese reoccupation. The *Binasa* versterkingen were demolished and the kampong reduced to ruins.

## Significance

Lam-Asan was the centre of [[Teuku Umar]]'s defensive *stelling* on the right wing and the principal object of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] of [[23 May]] [[1896]]. The [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of 30 May – 2 June 1896]] was the largest single act of chastisement of the first semester of [[1896]] and demonstrated the new Dutch policy of destroying hostile kampongs rather than simply occupying them.

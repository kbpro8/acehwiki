**Blang Kalla** was the *kloof* (gorge) on the western edge of [[Groot-Atjeh]] traversed by [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s colonne on [[15 January]] [[1876]] in the second phase of his [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great winter offensive]]. The colonne marched from [[Lampagger]] southward through the kloof over *bergachtig terrein* (mountainous terrain) into the [[IV Moekims]], reaching Loenga on the right bank of the [[Kroeng Raba]] mouth, which would become the *westelijke grens* (western limit) of the new afsluitingslinie.

## The march through the kloof

The march through the Blang Kalla kloof was, Borel notes, the operational proof that [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] had been wrong to reject [[Generaal Verspijck|Verspijck]]'s proposed landing at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai|Kroeng Raba-baai]] in November [[1873]] on the grounds that the terrain was impracticable. The colonne under Pel's personal leiding marched from Lampagger — whose *opperhoofd* had submitted on [[10 January]] [[1876]] — through the kloof of Blang Kalla southward over *bergachtig terrein* into the IV Moekims, reaching Loenga on the right bank of the Kroeng Raba mouth. The distance from the Kraton to the Kroeng Raba mouth was less than three *uren* (hours) in a straight line — exactly as Verspijck had argued in [[1873]]. "Drie jaren later bewees de generaal Pel het tegendeel" — three years later, generaal Pel proved the contrary — Borel notes, in one of his sharpest indictments of van Swieten's strategic judgement.

## The wider operation

The march through the Blang Kalla kloof was part of the second phase of Pel's offensive, after the [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] invasion that had begun on [[26 December]] [[1875]] and had carried the Dutch through [[Ketapang Doewa]] ([[27 December]] [[1875]]), [[Djempit]] and Pakan Badak ([[28 December]] [[1875]]), and [[Lamtengah]] ([[8 January]] [[1876]]). The hoofden at Loenga submitted. The colonne returned via the *noordoostelijke weg*, left a post at Boekit Seboen on [[24 January]] [[1876]], and reached Pakan Badak; it then made a *tocht* through the eastern part of the IV Moekims. With the IV Moekims conquered and the Kroeng Raba mouth in Dutch hands, the way was opened for the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims invasion]] of [[28 January]] [[1876]] through the [[Glitaroem]] pass — the third phase of Pel's offensive. The Blang Kalla march was therefore the operational hinge of the entire winter offensive, opening both the IV Moekims and the western end of Pel's planned afsluitingslinie.

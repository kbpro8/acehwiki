**Djatisaripa** — also **Djatisaripah**, variant **Djatiswara** — is the *pasar* (market) of the [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Soenan Kalidjaga]] legend at which [[Raden Sahid]] — the wild son of the *tumenggong* of Wilatikta — waited to rob travellers, and at which he was converted by [[Soenan Bonang|Pangeran Bonang]]. The [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] places it in the forest of [[Djatisekar|Djatisekar]], to the north-east of [[Lasem|Lasem]]; the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten]] gives the alternative form [[Djatiswara|Djatiswara]]. It is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the *bedekte taal* form for the *pasar* of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] — the sacred islet of [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]].

## The conversion story

The [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] tells that [[Raden Sahid]] — "a wild fellow, given to gambling and idling" — came as far as the neighbourhood of [[Japara|Japara]], and when he had lost at gambling, "went out robbing, waited for people in the forest of [[Djatisekar|Djatisekar]], to the north-east of [[Lasem|Lasem]]". One day [[Soenan Bonang|Soenan Bonang]] came through that forest; Raden Sahid held him up to rob him, but Soenan Bonang told him: "if later someone passes here, dressed entirely in black and with a *wora-wari* flower behind his ear, a red *kembang spatu*, you must plunder him". Three days later Soenan Bonang himself returned in that guise; Raden Sahid held him up; "Soenan Bonang became his *sa-kawan* — his companion"; Raden Sahid repented.

## Decoding: *djati-pa-sar d-ipa* and the Heiland

The name ***Djatisaripa*** [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as ***djati-pa-sar d-ipa*** — "the *Heiland* with the *pasar* of the *Heer des Rijkdoms*" (the Lord of Riches). The variant ***Djatiswara*** he decodes as ***djati-iswara*** — "the *Heiland* is *iswara*" (the Lord). The *pasar* itself De Roo de la Faille identifies with the *pasar* of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] — the [[Noesa-n-ta-ra|Noesa-n-ta-ra]] of the [[Pararaton]] under the protection of the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Tara]], at which the *pabgjan* (the toll-house at which ships paid import duties) stood.

## The *bonang* decoding

The mysterious *pangeran* **Bonang** who converts Raden Sahid is named, in the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]], after a *desa* **Bonang** in [[Rembang|Rembang]] south of [[Lasem|Lasem]] — but "meant was [[Worawari|Worawari]] on [[Sumatra]]", De Roo de la Faille argues. [[Rembang|Rembang]] itself is the Javanese remembrance of the *Ari-ke-mbang* of the Sumatran Overwal — the land of the [[Kalang|Kalang's]], Javanese people who were not "real" Javanese but [[Djawaka|djawa-ka]] of *lang-ka*, the Sumatran country from which they had been brought as war-booty.

## The *wora-wari* and the *tjempaka gonda*

The *wora-wari* flower behind [[Soenan Bonang|Pangeran Bonang]]'s ear — the sign by which Raden Sahid was to recognise the man he should plunder — is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the *wora-wari* of *mala-wora-wari*, i.e. *Mala-joe-wa-wor-a* — "Malajoer-ora" — Malajoer. The same flower is the *tjempaka gonda* of the [[Taroema|Taroema]] allegory — the "fragrant" (*aroem*) flower whose *gonda* ("fragrance") encodes the ancestral land of [[Taroema]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Djatisaripa is the principal *bedekte taal* form by which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] ties the [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Soenan Kalidjaga]] legend to the [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]-[[Aroe-baai|Aroe]] sacred complex — the *pasar* of the sacred islet at which the conversion of the Javanese *wali* took place. Its identification, by De Roo de la Faille, with the *pasar* of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] also marks it as the principal Javanese link in the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]]-Java triangle of the early sixteenth century.

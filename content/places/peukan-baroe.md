**Peukan Baroe** was the moekim (district) near [[Sigli]] in [[Pidië|Pidie]], within the territory of [[Teukoe Béntara Pineung|uléëbalang Teukoe Béntara Pineung]], that together with [[Peukan Sòt|Peukan Sòt]] formed the stronghold of [[Habib Oesén|Habib Oesén]] and his hostile fortifications (*bentengs*). The area was the site of the [[Conquest of Peukan Baroe-Peukan Sòt (August 1897)|conquest of Peukan Baroe-Peukan Sòt]] during the [[Sigli expedition (August 1897)|Sigli expedition]] of August [[1897]].

**Pakan Baroe** was one of the two *[[Moekim|moekims]]* of the [[Pedir]] country from which, according to the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|J. T. Cremer]], the [[Sigli expedition (August 1897)|Segli expedition]] of August [[1897]] was undertaken to expel the hostile bands.

## The hostile positions

From the point *Klapa tiga* — "the three coconut trees" — shooters directed daily fire against the Dutch administrative establishment and military positions at [[Sigli]]. After two more coconut trees were destroyed, the Dutch renamed the point *pap satoe* ("one coconut"). The *Kolonial Verslag* of [[1897]] recorded that the indigenous population and the Dutch establishment suffered annoyance from the evil-willed persons in these moekims, resulting in bloody clashes and bold cases of sea-robbery.

## The conquest (August 1897)

During the [[Sigli expedition (August 1897)|Sigli expedition]], after bombardment by warships and artillery, all the bentengs in [[Peukan Baroe|Peukan Baroe]] and [[Peukan Sòt|Peukan Sòt]] were stormed and taken by Dutch forces under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] on [[6]]–[[10 August]] [[1897]]. The defenders fled, leaving many dead, rifles, and white weapons. The bentengs were demolished and the terrain cleared.

## In the debate of [[1897]]

Answering [[Pijnacker Hordijk]]'s question whether the Dutch would be compelled to go further into [[Pedir]] and [[Gighen]], Cremer referred to what had happened at [[Segli]]: "daar heeft men denzelfden toestand ongeveer als in het Pedirsche; de vorsten aan de kust zijn ons niet vijandig gezind, maar zij zijn niet krachtig genoeg om de in de [[Tanawaqt]] of in de [[VII Moekims]] samengestroomde [[Delama-benden|Delama-benden]] te verjagen, en daarom heeft de expeditie naar Segli plaats gehad; niet om Segli zelf te nemen, maar wel om de vijandelijke benden uit de moekims Pakan Baroe en [[Pokan Soth]] te verjagen."

"Dat is geschied," he continued; after the expulsion the chief of that district had taken the land under his own management, and it was not at all necessary to keep the place occupied. Only from the fixed post of Segli was daily supervision maintained over the swept district, to which the population was gradually returning.

## Significance

The case of Pakan Baroe was Cremer's model for the treatment of the whole coast: not conquest and occupation, but the clearing of hostile bands from a district, followed by the restoration of the local chief's administration under distant supervision. He stated that should similar clearances become necessary in the interior or elsewhere, they would be carried out, and that the completion of the steam tram to [[Selimoen]] would then be of great advantage to the troops.

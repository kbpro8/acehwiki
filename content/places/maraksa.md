**Maraksa** was a coastal locality reconnoitred by Dutch forces in the later [[1874]] operations covered by [[Jan van Swieten]].

## Historical setting

Its reconnaissance was connected with the lighthouse at [[Pulau Bras]], naval action in the [[VI Mukim]], and attempts to build political relations through maritime commanders.

## Modern context

The source situates [[Maraksa]] in the historical landscape of [[Aceh]]; historical spellings follow the [[1879]] Dutch text.

## Account from Van Swieten (1879)

[[Jan van Swieten|Lieutenant-General Van Swieten]], in *De waarheid over onze vestiging in Atjeh* ([[1879]]), provided extensive detail about Maraksa's role in the campaign. During the diplomatic exchanges of [[March 1873]], [[Sidi Tahil]] — the intermediary dispatched by [[F. N. Nieuwenhuizen|Commissioner Nieuwenhuizen]] — was guided through the lagoon by [[Malilla]], a man from [[Priaman]] living at Maraksa, and brought to the *Missigit* of Maraksa, where he found several chiefs of the [[XXV Moekims]].

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|Second Expedition]], the Dutch deliberately chose to land east of the Atjeh river rather than west, partly because less resistance was expected there and partly because they wished to spare the kampong of Maraksa, whose head [[Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia|Toekoe Nek]] had repeatedly offered his services. The area became a centre of Dutch commercial activity during the occupation, with a bazar where Acehnese traders sold produce to the soldiers.

Van Swieten also recorded that after the occupation of [[Mosapie]], the coastal area of the [[VI Moekims]] was "entirely abandoned" and the population withdrew to the mountains. This had the effect of depriving the local chief [[Toekoe Nanta]] of his principal kampongs — [[Bital]] and [[Ketapang Doewa]] — and their main means of subsistence, the fisheries and small trade.

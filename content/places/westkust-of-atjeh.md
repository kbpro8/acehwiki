**The West Coast of Aceh** is the always restless region of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] that, in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), is the setting of the [[Verzet in Lhong]] and the place of the patrol actions on the always restless West Coast. The West Coast is the book's — and the colonial memory's — marker of the always restless region of Aceh: the West Coast where the resistance fighter resistance persisted and where the colonial army's patrol actions were continuous.

## The patrol actions on the always restless West Coast

The patrol actions on the always restless West Coast are the last-named of the great successes of Van Heutsz's period of glory before the [[Slag bij Batèë-Ilië]]. The always restless West Coast is therefore the book's — and the colonial memory's — marker of the region where the patrol actions were continuous: the West Coast of Aceh where the resistance fighter resistance persisted and where the colonial army's patrol actions were a constant.

## The Verzet in Lhong

The [[Verzet in Lhong]] — the resistance fighters surrounded in a house at Lhong, breaking out with the naked weapon in hand, and annihilated in the [[Glé Groetèë|caves of the Glé Groetèë]] — is the book's set-piece of the West Coast resistance. Lhong — on the West Coast of Aceh — is the place of the surrounding-and-searching that was more decorative than effective, and the place where the resistance fighters escaped to the Glé Groetèë where they were annihilated.

## Significance

The West Coast of Aceh is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the always restless region of Aceh — the West Coast where the resistance fighter resistance persisted and where the colonial army's patrol actions were continuous. The book's argument — that the West Coast was the always restless region of the patrol actions and the place of the [[Verzet in Lhong]] — is the book's marker of the always restless West Coast. The West Coast is therefore the book's marker of the region of the patrol actions and of the resistance fighter resistance.

**Leupoeëng** — also *Lepong*, in Van Daalen's letters *Lepong*, perhaps *Lhoeng* — is a place on the Westkust of [[Aceh]], near [[Lhong]], from which [[Teukoe Oemar]] was driven and to which he returned after the Dutch troops withdrew. *Nadat T. Oemar uit Lepong (Leupoeëng, ter Westkust) was verdreven, nestelde hij zich daar weer na het vertrek onzer troepen* — after T. Oemar was driven from Lepong (Leupoeëng, on the Westkust), he entrenched himself there again after the departure of our troops. The neighbouring *landschap* of [[Lhong]] was fined *f 30.000* because its *oeléëbalang* *heulled* with him, and, being unable to pay, was the object of the [[Lhong punitive expedition (1897)|April 1897 punitive expedition]].

## The April 1897 expedition

*Het was geen oorlog meer doch eene wraakoefening, deels op onschuldigen, welke tevens eene beleediging van ons leger werd* — it was no longer war but an act of revenge, partly on innocents, which at the same time became an insult to our army. [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Kapitein Van Daalen]]'s pencil-notes from *Bivak Tamoen* record his disgust: *'t Is beschamend en ik verlang daarom ook om hiervandaan te komen* — it is shameful and I long to be away from here. The seized cattle, brought in *prauwen* to [[Koetaradja]], died in part before the embarkation from lack of food; *in heel de streek hing de stank van de verrotte dieren* — in the whole district hung the stench of the rotting animals. The gold and silver ornaments of the women were taken — *van de voeten der vrouwen nam men de enkelringen af, van hare armen de armbanden*.

## Significance

Leupoeëng is the Westkust place whose name, with that of [[Lhong]], Zentgraff cites to explain the *felle haat der Atjehsche vrouw* — the fierce hatred of the Atjehsche wife. The case of Leupoeëng and Lhong in [[1897]] is the *schandvlek* that the high command at Koetaradja left on the army's reputation.

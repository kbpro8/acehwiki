> **Note on the shared place-name.** Two distinct Achehnese localities are referred to as *Lepong* (or *Leupoeëng*) in the sources: (i) a place on the [[Westkust]] near [[Lhong]] from which [[Teukoe Oemar]] was driven and to which he returned after the Dutch troops withdrew — the cause of the [[Lhong punitive expedition (1897)|April 1897 punitive expedition]] (treated in the upper sections); and (ii) a *landschap* (district) on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] of which [[Teuku Umar]] was the hereditary *hoeloebalang* and which served as his principal power-base and refuge throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] (treated in the lower sections). The two places share a name variant but lie on opposite coasts of the Achehnese heartland.

## The Westkust Leupoeëng — account from Zentgraff's *Atjeh* (1938)

**Leupoeëng** — also *Lepong*, in Van Daalen's letters *Lepong*, perhaps *Lhoeng* — is a place on the Westkust of [[Aceh]], near [[Lhong]], from which [[Teukoe Oemar]] was driven and to which he returned after the Dutch troops withdrew. *Nadat T. Oemar uit Lepong (Leupoeëng, ter Westkust) was verdreven, nestelde hij zich daar weer na het vertrek onzer troepen* — after T. Oemar was driven from Lepong (Leupoeëng, on the Westkust), he entrenched himself there again after the departure of our troops. The neighbouring *landschap* of [[Lhong]] was fined *f 30.000* because its *oeléëbalang* *heulled* (associated) with him, and, being unable to pay, was the object of the [[Lhong punitive expedition (1897)|April 1897 punitive expedition]].

### The April 1897 expedition

*Het was geen oorlog meer doch eene wraakoefening, deels op onschuldigen, welke tevens eene beleediging van ons leger werd* — it was no longer war but an act of revenge, partly on innocents, which at the same time became an insult to our army. [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Kapitein Van Daalen]]'s pencil-notes from *Bivak Tamoen* record his disgust: *'t Is beschamend en ik verlang daarom ook om hiervandaan te komen* — it is shameful and I long to be away from here. The seized cattle, brought in *prauwen* (boats) to [[Koetaradja]], died in part before the embarkation from lack of food; *in heel de streek hing de stank van de verrotte dieren* — in the whole district hung the stench of the rotting animals. The gold and silver ornaments of the women were taken — *van de voeten der vrouwen nam men de enkelringen af, van hare armen de armbanden* (from the feet of the women the ankle rings were taken, from their arms the bracelets).

### Significance of the Westkust site

Leupoeëng is the Westkust place whose name, with that of [[Lhong]], Zentgraff cites to explain the *felle haat der Atjehsche vrouw* — the fierce hatred of the Atjehsche wife. The case of Leupoeëng and Lhong in [[1897]] is the *schandvlek* (stain) that the high command at Koetaradja left on the army's reputation.

## The east-coast Lepong — account from *ATJEH '96* (Kruisheer, 1913)

**Lepong** — written in Dutch as with *Lejoong* listed in the errata as a variant, and sometimes confused with *Lampisang* — was a district on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] of which [[Teuku Umar]] (*Toekoe Oemar*) was the territorial chief, and which served as Umar's principal power-base and refuge throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Lepong was the place to which Umar fled after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]], the place at which he held his council of approximately [[20 June]] [[1896]] to appoint new *panglima's*, and the object of the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine bombardments of 25 May and 4–5, 8–9 June 1896]]. Umar feared a marine bombardment of Lepong more than a land incursion into the [[VI Moekims]].

### Description and Location (east-coast Lepong)

Lepong lay on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] to the north-west of the [[VI Moekims]] and to the south of the [[Kroeng Raba]] bay. The *landschap* included the kampong of Lepong itself, the kampong of [[Lohong]] (also Loh' Nga), the [[Masdjid Lepong|mosque of Lepong]] where [[Toekoe Djohan Lampasei]] stayed, and the [[Missigit Lepong|Missigit Lepong]] fired upon by the marine in the bombardments of [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]]. The *landschap* was bounded on the west by the sea, on the south by the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] and the heights of [[Boekit Seboen]], and on the east by the [[Kroeng Raba]] river and the inland country toward [[Ana' Galong]]. The [[Belang Kala-pas]] gave access south-westward into the inland VI Moekims, and the pepper gardens of [[Monbroeh]] in this pass served as refuge for the women of the VI Moekims.

### Historical Background (east-coast Lepong)

Lepong had been the seat of [[Teuku Umar]]'s family for generations, and Umar held the title of *hoeloebalang* (territorial chief) of Lepong by hereditary right. From Lepong Umar had risen to prominence as a war-leader in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] from [[1873]] onward, and his appointment as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government on [[5 September]] [[1893]] had not displaced him from his Lepong power-base. Throughout his period of Dutch service ([[1893]]–[[1896]]) Umar used Lepong as a refuge for his valuables and as a strategic retreat, and the *landschap* was the principal source of his political legitimacy. After his [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] Lepong became the centre of his reorganised resistance.

### Role in the 1896 Operations (east-coast Lepong)

Lepong was the principal geographic object of the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine bombardments of 25 May and 4–5, 8–9 June 1896]] conducted by the [[Marine|marine]] from the [[Kroeng-Rababaai|Kroeng-Raba bay]]. On [[25 May]] [[1896]] the marine bombarded Lepong in conjunction with the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] against the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]]. On [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] the bombardment was repeated in support of the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|two-colonne operation]]; on [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]] the [[Missigit Lepong|Missigit Lepong]] and [[Masdjid Lepong|Masdjid Lepong]] were fired upon. After the collapse of his *stellingen* in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]] Umar fled eastward into Lepong, and at Lepong on approximately [[20 June]] [[1896]] he held his council to appoint new *panglima's* in succession to those killed or compromised. On [[22 June]] [[1896]] the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] under [[G. A. Hansen|Hansen]] was conducted to the south of Lepong, and the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling east of [[Kroeng-Kali]] was attacked on [[23 June]] [[1896]]. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] a Dutch column under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuyzen|Okhuizen]] marched to Lepong and burned kampongs of the *landschap*. [[Teuku Umar]]'s letters to the [[Governor of Atjeh]] of [[30 March]], [[12 April]], and [[23 April]] [[1896]] were all written at [[Lam-Pisang]] in the neighbouring [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]], but Umar's strategic centre of gravity remained at Lepong.

### Significance (east-coast Lepong)

Lepong was the symbolic and political heart of [[Teuku Umar]]'s authority, and its bombardment by the [[Marine|marine]] was the principal Dutch means of striking at Umar indirectly after the failure of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] to capture him. Umar's fear of a marine bombardment of Lepong — greater than his fear of a land incursion into the [[VI Moekims]] — indicates the political and psychological importance of the *landschap* to his cause. The *chandoeri* (council) at Lepong of approximately [[20 June]] [[1896]] demonstrated Umar's continued ability to reorganise his forces even after the loss of his *stellingen*, and Lepong remained his base for the remainder of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

**Djerir** was a Dutch military post at the eastern end of the communication line through the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The post was evacuated on [[13 January]] [[1885]] as part of the concentration strategy.

## Location

Djerir was situated at the far eastern end of the 121-palen communication line extending from [[Lambaroe]] through the [[XXII Moekims]]. The position was one of the most distant points of Dutch expansion.

## Military Significance

### Pre-Concentration Era

Djerir was established during the expeditions of [[General van der Heyden]] in [[1878]]–[[1879]]. It served as:
- The terminal point of the communication line
- A forward position against the interior
- A staging area for potential further operations

### Strategic Assessment

By [[1884]], the author observed that the possession of the Djerir line had proven a major disadvantage. Once the Dutch had completely abandoned offensive movements, the line's utility ceased—it enclosed no territory and only provided the enemy with advantages.

## Evacuation

Djerir was evacuated on [[13 January]] [[1885]] along with [[Indrapoori]], [[Glé-Kamping]], [[Long-Lemoh]], [[Tjot-Basetoel]], [[Samagani]], and [[Lepong-Ara]].

## Post-Evacuation

Following evacuation, the eastern approach routes to the abandoned area were no longer monitored by the Dutch. The territory returned to Acehnese control.

## Significance

Djerir's evacuation marked the practical end of Dutch offensive ambitions in the eastern [[XXII Moekims]]. The post had symbolized the reach of Dutch power; its abandonment acknowledged the limits of that power.

**Leyden** (modern **Leiden**) is the Dutch university city in which *The Achehnese* was published and printed in [[1906]] by the firm of **E. J. Brill**. It appears on both the title page and the colophon of Vol. I of Snouck Hurgronje's work.

## Role in the Publication of *The Achehnese*

The title page of Vol. I names the publisher as **E. J. Brill, Leyden, 1906** — the firm founded by the Brill family in the seventeenth century and still in existence as a leading orientalist press. The colophon likewise records that the book was "published and printed" by E. J. Brill at Leyden. This formal association of Leyden with the publication underlines the role of Dutch academic institutions — and particularly the tradition of Islamic and Indonesian studies associated with the University of Leiden — in producing the great ethnographic and philological works on the Netherlands East Indies in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## The Leyden Network of Scholars

Leyden's significance in Vol. I extends beyond mere printing. Many of the scholars cited or thanked by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] were Leyden men or had Leyden connections. [[G. K. Niemann|Professor G. K. Niemann]], repeatedly invoked for his *Bloemlezing uit Maleische geschriften*, held the Leiden chair of Malay; [[Dr. Brandes]] and [[J. A. Kruyt|J. A. Kruijt]] contributed Leyden-published studies; the ethnographic journal *Internationales Archiv für Ethnographie* (edited by [[I. D. E. Schmeltz|J. D. E. Schmeltz]]) was published at Leyden in [[1888]]. The translator of Vol. I, [[A. S. O'Sullivan|A. W. S. O'Sullivan]], and its indexer, [[R. J. Wilkinson|R. J. Wilkinson]], were not Leyden men, but the work appeared under the Brill imprint which gave it currency among the international community of orientalists.

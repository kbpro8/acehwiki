**Samoedra** — also written *Samudra*, *Samudera*, in combination *Pasé-Samoedra* — is the historical region and former Mohammedan coastal state of north Sumatra, between [[Pasé|Pasé]] and the later [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjèh], whose pepper cultivation — introduced between [[1350]] and [[1400]] A.D. by the first dispersers of Islam in north Sumatra — was, before the rise of Atjèh, the principal pepper cultivation of north Sumatra. The Samoedra pepper was reported by the Mohammedan Chinese [[Ma Hoean|Ma Hoean]] in [[1416]] and by [[Fei Hsin|Fei Hsin]] in [[1436]]; the region was conquered by [[Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah|Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah]] of Atjèh in [[1524]], and the Portuguese garrison lying there was driven out.

## The Introduction of Pepper (1350-1400)

The pepper cultivation indigenous to the slopes of the [[Nilgirl|Nilgirl]] chain in British Indië must, according to the [[De Rijkdom van Atjeh (1923)|Atjeh-Instituut brochure of 1923]], have been transplanted to Samoedra in north Sumatra between [[1350]] and [[1400]] A.D. by the first dispersers of Islam in those regions. [[Ibn Batoetah|Ibn Batoetah]] — the famous Mohammedan discoverer-traveller, who visited north Sumatra before [[1350]] — knows nothing yet of Samoedra pepper; whereas [[Ma Hoean|Ma Hoean]], a Mohammedan Chinese who accompanied the eunuch [[Coe|Coe]] alias Sam-Po on his ambassadorial journey of [[1412]]–[[1415]], reported it in [[1416]], as did [[Fei Hsin|Fei Hsin]] in [[1436]] in his then-appeared travel book "*Chêng-Ho*" — the fruit of some four foreign journeys with that same ambassador [[Chêng-Ho|Chêng-Ho]].

## The Chinese Reports

The Chinese reports of Ma Hoean (1416) and Fei Hsin (1436) are the principal early historical attestations of Samoedra pepper. The reports, made in the course of the early 15th-century Chinese ambassadorial journeys to the Western Ocean, established Samoedra as one of the principal pepper-producing regions of the Indian Ocean world in the early 15th century, before the rise of the Portuguese and the rise of Atjèh.

## The 1524 Conquest by Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah

After the [[1521]] victory over the Portuguese fleet of [[Yorge de Brito|Yorge de Brito]] and the conquest of [[Pidië|Pidië]], Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah turned to Samoedra. In [[1524]] Pasé-Samoedra was conquered, while the Portuguese garrison lying there was driven out. The conquest of Samoedra was the principal extension of Atjèhse authority in the reign of Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah, and brought the principal pepper cultivation of north Sumatra under Atjèhse control.

## Significance

Samoedra is one of the principal historical regions of north Sumatra, with a sustained significance in Atjèhse history from the 14th-century introduction of pepper to the 16th-century conquest by Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah. The introduction of pepper between [[1350]] and [[1400]], the Chinese reports of Ma Hoean and Fei Hsin in the early 15th century, and the [[1524]] conquest by Atjèh together constitute the principal phases of Samoedra's pre-Atjèhse and early Atjèhse history. The Samoedra pepper cultivation was, before the rise of Atjèh, the principal pepper cultivation of north Sumatra, and its transfer to Pidië between [[1435]] and [[1500]] — before the rise of Atjèh — was the principal episode in the pre-Atjèhse pepper history of north Sumatra.

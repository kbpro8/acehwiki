---
title: Tiro
tags: ["place", "north-east-coast"]
---

An ancient and famous gampong in [[Pidie]]. 'Tiro, which has in these latter days acquired a widespread celebrity from the two teungkus of that place who took a prominent part in the war against the Dutch, was from ancient times less famed for the teaching given there than for the great number of learned men whom it produced and who lived there. Tiro was as it were sanctified by the presence of so many living ulamas and the holy tombs of their predecessors. None dared to carry arms in this gampong even in time of war; and the hukom or religious law was stronger here than elsewhere, while its enemy the adat was weaker.' The foremost family of pandits there was headed by [[Teungku Muhamat Amin]] (died [[1886]]), and his relative [[Cheh Saman]] ([[Teungku Tiro]]), who led the holy war against the Dutch. Mat Amin (son of [[Cheh Saman]]) was the military commander under [[Teungku Tiro]]. [[Teungku Kuta Karang]] disparagingly referred to [[Cheh Saman]] as 'Leube Saman' rather than by the honoured title [[Teungku Tiro]].

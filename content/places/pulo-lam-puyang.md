**Pulo Lam Puyang** is an island or locality of [[Acheh]] upon which stands a dreaded tree, called **Ja Karieng**, which is the subject of many vows among the Achehnese. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Snouck Hurgronje cites Pulo Lam Puyang in connection with the Achehnese belief in holy or dreaded trees and the practice of making vows at such spots.

## The Dreaded Tree

The author compares a number of Achehnese local superstitions in which particular natural objects — trees, mountains, wells — are the objects of religious dread or of vows. He instances "Ja Karieng, a dreaded tree on Pulo Lam Puyang, the subject of many vows; **Eumpie Lului**, a mountain on the coast of the [[IV Mukims]] which plays a part in the rain-making superstition; **Eumpie Blieng**, a holy well in the [[IX Mukims]] which also helps to cause rain; and so on." The Ja Karieng of Pulo Lam Puyang thus belongs to a class of natural objects — trees, mountains, wells — which the Achehnese regard as the seats of supernatural power.

## Vows and the Holy Tree

The vows made at such a tree are a recognised form of Achehnese popular piety. The worshipper, in distress or in hope, addresses himself to the spirit of the tree, promising an offering or a sacrifice in return for the boon sought; and the tree, by virtue of the vows made at it, becomes the centre of a small local cult. The Ja Karieng of Pulo Lam Puyang is thus a typical instance of the way in which the Achehnese landscape is studded with sacred or dreaded objects, at which the popular religion — half-Islamic, half-pre-Islamic — expresses itself in vows and offerings.

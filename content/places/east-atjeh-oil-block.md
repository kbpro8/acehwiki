**The East Atjeh oil block** is one of the producing oil fields of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] recorded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) under the North Sumatra heading of the [[1940]]–[[1941]] East Indian oil-production tables. The block produced 4,626 tons of 1000 kg (i.e. 4,626 metric tons) of crude oil in [[1941]], the only year for which a figure is given in Van Bemmelen's Table 4 — implying that production began in [[1941]] rather than [[1940]]. The East Atjeh block is part of the wider [[Atjeh oil complex]] that included the [[Peureula]] field (48,219 tons in [[1940]], 36,640 tons in [[1941]]) and the [[Pasé oil field|Pasé]] field (1,797 tons in [[1941]]).

The operator of the East Atjeh block is the [[B.P.M.]] (Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij, the Royal Dutch subsidiary), which also operated the larger Aru Bay, Telaga Said and Bulu Telang fields immediately to the south. The East Atjeh block accordingly belongs to the B.P.M.-operated northern-Sumatran petroleum complex that extended from the Aru Bay area at the southern edge of the residency northward into Atjeh proper.

## Geological context

Van Bemmelen classifies the East Atjeh block as one of the "steep folds (Atjeh, Borneo)" that were "repeatedly investigated by drilling but the results have not been encouraging". The implication is that the East Atjeh oil is hosted in steeply-folded neogene sediments — a structural style that, in the colonial reading of the geology, was less favourable for oil accumulation than the gently-domed anticlines of the South-Sumatran and East-Borneo fields. The modest size of the [[1941]] production (4,626 tons, against 709,625 tons from the Tamiang block in [[1940]]) is consistent with this assessment.

## Significance

The East Atjeh oil block is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is one of the four recorded colonial-era producing oil fields on Atjeh territory — the others being the [[Peureula]], [[Pulu Pandjang]] and [[Pasé oil field|Pasé]] fields of the [[Atjeh oil complex]], and (depending on the colonial boundary) the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]]. The East Atjeh block accordingly belongs to the small group of Atjeh localities at which colonial-era oil production actually took place, and its [[1941]] production figure is one of the few quantitative records of Atjeh's contribution to the colonial East Indian petroleum economy.

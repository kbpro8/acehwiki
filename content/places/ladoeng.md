**Ladoeng** — written in Dutch as *Ladoeng* — was a beach near the kampong of Ladoeng on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the site of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|sea-landing of Hansen's column on 23 June 1896]] in the closing operation of the first semester of [[1896]]. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the Ladoeng landing was conducted by the [[H. M. Merapi|H. M. Merapi]], the *Gouvernementsstoomer Condor*, and the *Albatros*, and was led by Lt. ter zee 1e kl. [[van den Broek]] in 4 *échelons*. The landing was a terrain reference for the [[1877]] column of [[Dompseler]] (6–7 hours) and the [[1879]] column of [[Halewijn]] (9.5 hours in reverse). The Ladoeng column was the sea-landing element of the combined operation against the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling east of [[Kroeng-Kali]].

## Description and Location

Ladoeng lay on the east coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] to the south of the [[Kroeng Raba]] bay and to the south-east of the *landschap* of [[Lepong]]. The beach near the kampong of Ladoeng was the site of the [[23 June]] [[1896]] sea-landing of [[G. A. Hansen|Hansen]]'s column. The Ladoeng bay (also *Ladoeng Bay*) was the anchorage of the warships that supported the landing. The kampong of Ladoeng itself was the inland reference point of the landing. The terrain between Ladoeng and the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling east of [[Kroeng-Kali]] was the scene of the combined land-and-sea operation of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]]. The terrain was a reference for the [[1877]] column of [[Dompseler]] (6–7 hours) and the [[1879]] column of [[Halewijn]] (9.5 hours in reverse), which had crossed the same country in earlier periods of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The *Kroeng-Angin* river was crossed by Hansen's Ladoeng column at 1:45 PM on [[23 June]] [[1896]], and the *Aloer Rampoenoen* stream blocked the column (bridge started 9:10 AM).

## Historical Background

Ladoeng had been a reference point for Dutch operations on the east coast since the [[1877]] column of [[Dompseler]], which crossed the country between Ladoeng and the inland *gebergte* in 6–7 hours. The [[1879]] column of [[Halewijn]] had crossed the same country in 9.5 hours in the reverse direction. These earlier columns had been small expeditions without the support of a sea-landing, and the [[23 June]] [[1896]] landing of [[G. A. Hansen|Hansen]]'s column was the first major Dutch operation at Ladoeng in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The kampong of Ladoeng itself had presumably been part of the territory of the [[hoeloebalang]] of [[Lepong]] (i.e., of [[Teuku Umar]]), and the choice of Ladoeng as the landing-site was intended to turn the eastern flank of Umar's position at [[Kroeng-Kali]] and the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling.

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Ladoeng was the site of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|sea-landing of Hansen's column on 23 June 1896]] — the closing operation of the first semester of [[1896]]. The landing was conducted by the [[H. M. Merapi|H. M. Merapi]], the *Gouvernementsstoomer Condor*, and the *Albatros*, and was led by Lt. ter zee 1e kl. [[van den Broek]] in 4 *échelons*. The column embarked at [[Oleh-leh]] on [[22 June]] [[1896]] and landed at Ladoeng on [[23 June]] [[1896]]. After landing, the column crossed the *Kroeng-Angin* river at 1:45 PM and was blocked by the *Aloer Rampoenoen* stream (bridge started 9:10 AM). The column then advanced against the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling east of [[Kroeng-Kali]] — a complex of six Koeta Lampoe Taroem, Toei Gloempang, Glé Broeë Siem, Boeng, Koeta Lampoe Djoeng, and Teungkoe Ba+ Riwat with a total garrison of approximately 200 men — from the rear, in coordination with a maréchaussée column under [[J. W. C. Vuijkk|Luitenant Vuijkk]] that had crossed [[Glé Taroempas]] and descended into the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] from the east. The operation was supported by the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine]] from the [[Kroeng-Rababaai|Kroeng-Raba bay]].

## Significance

Ladoeng was the site of the only major Dutch sea-landing of the first semester of [[1896]] and the principal Dutch combined land-and-sea operation since [[1873]]. The landing was intended to turn the eastern flank of [[Teuku Umar]]'s position at [[Kroeng-Kali]] and the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling, and its success demonstrated the Dutch ability to strike at Achehnese strongpoints from any direction. The choice of Ladoeng as the landing-site — a terrain reference for the [[1877]] column of [[Dompseler]] and the [[1879]] column of [[Halewijn]] — connected the [[1896]] operation to the longer history of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] on the east coast.

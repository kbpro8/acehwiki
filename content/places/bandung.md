**Bandung** (historically spelled *Bandoeng*) was the principal city of the Preanger Regencies of west [[Java|Java]] in the [[Dutch East Indies|Netherlands Indies]] and the site, in the early twentieth century, of the colonial state's memorial culture for the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — most notably the bronze statue raised in [[1922]] in honour of the Catholic military chaplain [[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]], whose long service in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] had made him the most celebrated *soldaten-pastoor* of the colonial army. The city's connection to the Atjeh campaigns is documented in the source *DLP-84-Atjeh*, which records that a statue commemorating both [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]] and the Ambonese Protestant chaplain [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] stood in the city, reflecting its role as a site of colonial remembrance.

## Colonial Memory and the Atjeh Statues

The presence of the Verbraak statue — and the companion memorial to [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] — at Bandoeng indicates the city's connection to the Dutch military and religious figures active in the [[Aceh War|Aceh campaigns]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]] and the subsequent [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacificatie]]. The bronze statue of [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]] was unveiled on [[27 January]] [[1922]] at a ceremony at which [[Generaal Dijkstra|Generaal Dijkstra]] delivered a commemorative address and the Very Reverend [[Pater van Hoof|Pater van Hoof]], on behalf of the Bishop and the clergy, expressed their thanks. The ceremony is independently noted in the [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume of 1934]] in the context of the [[Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting|Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting]], the foundation to which the existing military homes were transferred in the same period. The choice of Bandoeng rather than [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] for the memorial reflects the city's status, by the early twentieth century, as the principal seat of the colonial military establishment in Java — the place at which the memory of the Atjeh campaign was most fully institutionalised.

## Bandung as Colonial Centre

Beyond its memorial function, Bandoeng was, by the 1920s, the principal city of the Preanger highlands and a major centre of colonial administration, education, and military organisation. The city housed the colonial military school (Dutch: *Koninklijke Militaire Academie* was at Breda in the Netherlands, but Bandoeng had the *Militaire School* for KNIL officers), the technical college (Dutch: *Technische Hoogeschool*) that became the Bandung Institute of Technology, and the headquarters of the colonial army's west-Java command. The statue to [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]] thus stood in a city whose daily life was structured by the institutions of the colonial state that had carried out the Atjeh campaigns. The memorial culture of Bandoeng — the statues, the military cemeteries, the officers' clubs — was the metropolitan counterpart to the garrison culture of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] and the field stations of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram postenlinie]], and it was at Bandoeng that the memory of the war was preserved, debated, and commemorated in the decades after the formal close of the conflict in [[1904]].

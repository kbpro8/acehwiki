**Aneu' Gle** is a *mukim* (parish-district) of the [[XXII Mukims]], the Sagi of the [[Three Sagis (XXVI, XXV, XXII Mukims)|three sagis]] which was headed hereditarily by [[Panglima Polem]]. It is mentioned by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] in Vol. I as one of a series of mukims that, in defiance of the general rule of single chieftainship, had formed a joint judicial union with neighbouring districts.

Such unions, Snouck explains, were a means by which smaller mukims or fragments of mukims emancipated themselves from the control of the *uleebalang* to whom they were nominally subordinate. The example given for Aneu' Gle is its combination with "another part of [[Lam Ara]] and [[Jrue']]" — that is to say, an already divided Lam Ara contributed one of its sections to a tripartite alliance with Aneu' Gle and Jrue', presumably for the joint exercise of judicial and adat authority.

This phenomenon is significant for the institutional history of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] government, since it shows that the formal structure of the *sagi* — composed of a fixed number of mukims each under its own imeum or uleebalang — was in practice modified by local arrangements dictated by convenience, kinship, or feud. Comparable unions cited by Snouck in the same passage include [[Lam Lheue]], [[Lam Kra']], [[Krueng Ma']] and [[Ateue']] on the one hand, and [[Sibree]], [[Ba'et]] and part of Lam Ara on the other.

Aneu' Gle's place in the Sagi of the [[XXII Mukims]] — the highland (*ureueng tunong*) division of Great Acheh — also connects it with the broader distinction, drawn in Vol. I, between the upland speech and manners of the XXII Mukims and the more lowland and capital-oriented culture of the two remaining sagis.

## In the eye survey of Groot-Atjeh ([[1937]])

Aneu-Glee is named among the coastal and valley districts of [[Groot-Atjeh]] covered by Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]]'s [[Oogheelkundig kampongonderzoek in Groot-Atjeh (Maart-December 1937)|eye survey of 1937]], in the list printed with the sketch-map of the *afdeeling* — between the [[VI Moekims Peukan Bada|VI Moekims Peukan-Bada]] and Delee-Lheuë.

**Atjeh** was the historic sultanate occupying the northern tip of Sumatra, a renowned centre of Islamic scholarship and pepper trade from the early modern period through the Dutch colonial conquest. The sultanate’s literary vitality in the sixteenth century is attested by the Erpenius-hss. in Cambridge and the Oxford Hikajat Seri Rama manuscript, both described as “blijken van de godsdienstige en litteraire activiteit in het 16th eeuwse Atjeh” [[Erpenius-hss.]] [[Hikajat Seri Rama]] [[16th eeuwse Atjeh]] [[Cambridge]] [[Oxford]]. Local scholarly works such as the [[Atjehers]] series and [[Atjeh II]] further record its intellectual tradition, while the Acehnese adaptation of the Burda poem [[Atjéhse bewerking van de Burda]] and related texts like the [[Nalam Mo'lôt]] and [[Akidat al-awāmm]] were produced in its capital [[Koetaradja]] around 1925 [[Koetaradja]] [[1925]]. 

## Early history
The sultanate of Atjeh emerged in the early sixteenth century as a successor to earlier Pasai and Pedir polities, adopting Islam and positioning itself as a hub for Malay‑Arabic literary exchange. Its rulers maintained diplomatic contacts with Ottoman, Mughal and European powers, a fact reflected in the survival of correspondence and treaties in European archives.

## Literary activity in the sixteenth century
According to the source, the Erpenius-hss. (Cambridge) and the Hikajat Seri Rama hs. (Oxford) are “blijken van de godsdienstige en litteraire activiteit in het 16th eeuwse Atjeh” [[Erpenius-hss.]] [[Hikajat Seri Rama]] [[16th eeuwse Atjeh]] [[Cambridge]] [[Oxford]]. These manuscripts contain religious‑didactic Malay prose and represent the oldest datable examples of such literature, indicating a vibrant intellectual milieu in the sultanate’s courts and pesantren.

## Manuscript evidence and modern scholarship
The works [[Atjehers I]] and [[Atjehers II]] cite extensive Acehnese manuscript material, while [[Atjeh II]] provides a reference point for later studies [[Atjehers I]] [[Atjehers II]] [[Atjeh II]]. Twentieth‑century scholars such as [[Snouck Hurgronje]] and [[J. Kreemer]] have drawn on Acehnese sources to discuss the Burda and related texts [[Snouck Hurgronje]] [[J. Kreemer]] [[Atjeh II: 686]]. The Leiden University Library holds numerous Acehnese manuscripts, including the [[Nalam Mo'lôt]] and a local translation of the Akidat al‑awāmm [[Leiden]] [[Nalam Mo'lôt]] [[Akidat al-awāmm]].

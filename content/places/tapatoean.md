**Tapa'toean** is the subdistrict headquarters-town and subdistrict at the southern end of the West Coast of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]], the subdistrict with the strongly Menangkabau-Malay population character "which, with their strong Menangkabau-Tapanuli orientation, were also religiously more oriented toward the west coast of Sumatra," and the third West Coast scene — alongside [[Tjalang|Tjalang]] and [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]] — of the insurgent movement that broke out on [[15 March]] [[1942]] with an armed attack on the camp at Tapa'toean by a gang of some two hundred men. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats Tapa'toean as the West Coast subdistrict whose "evolutionary" administrative transition from a captain-*controleur* to a civil administrator was only completed in [[1938]], and whose "still somewhat unstable political situation" left it the most vulnerable of all the West Coast subdistricts to the F-organisation's propaganda.

## The subdistrict and its population

The subdistrict of Tapa'toean comprised ten self-governing territories with a population of some 65,000 inhabitants. Its self-governing rulers included Datok Nja' Radja of Soesóh, "undoubtedly in this sub-district the most important self-governor, both by his forceful personality and by the significance of his landscape." Of the other *oelèëbalangs*, "Teukoe Mahjiddin of Labóhan Adji … had to be removed from his dignity," and the administration of Labóhan Adji had "for some years rested with a administrative commission." Teukoe Radja Pakèh of Tapa'toean himself had "been seriously ill for some time," so that "little influence could emanate in these tense days." The economic condition of the subdistrict "was certainly favorable. Just as in [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]], the abundant rice production allowed for considerable export, which was mainly directed to the residency of West Sumatra. The cultivation of *nilam* (patchouli), cloves, and nutmeg as secondary crops made this subdistrict one of the most prosperous in Aceh."

## The administrative reform of 1938

Together with the subdistricts Lam Meukó, [[Koeta Tjané|Koeta Tjané]], [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] and [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]], Tapa'toean was the object of the [[1938|1938]] administrative reform by which "a start was made with the modification of the administrative system … in which the military commander, as acting *controleur*, was also entrusted with the exercise of civil administration." Piekaar records that "the receptiveness of the population of this region to political agitation, whether or not disguised in religious garb, was the reason that it was not until [[1938]] that the acting captain-*controleur* could be replaced by a government official." This was part of the broader set of late colonial reforms under Resident [[Jongejans|Jongejans]] that also saw the disbanding of the garrisons at Padjang Tidji in [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] and at [[Singkel|Singkel]].

## The revolt of March 1942

The insurgent movement in Tapa'toean was the third great West Coast revolt of March [[1942]]. "On [[15 March|March 15]], open resistance broke out, a resistance which would soon spread from Soesóh in the north to Tapa'toean in the south throughout the entire subdistrict." On [[17 March]] [[1942]] "in the early morning an attack had taken place on the camp in Tapa'toean. This attack was carried out by a gang of about 200 men, including a large number of grown-up boys, apparently pupils of a religious school." Although "in the camp only seven carbines were available," the attack "could be repelled by captain Supheert, the commander of the 6th division marechaussee, who had just returned from Bengkalis and Sibolga from Malacca." The acting self-governing ruler of the landscape Kloeët in the subdistrict of Southern Acehnese Landschappen, Teukoe Marah Adam, "immediately rushed to assist upon hearing of the attack," riding "with 20 armed followers in two cars into Tapa'toean." On [[23 March]] [[1942]] Japanese troops entered Tapa'toean; "the agents (of the administrative police) of their own accord formed a circle around me, so that none of the Acehnese present with the Japanese troops, armed with *parangs*, could approach me," the subdistrict chief G. C. Bruin recorded. The "children of these 'faith witnesses'" — the orphanage established at Blang Pidië, under the leadership of the merchant Tjè' Ahmad — was "fully exploited for propaganda purposes by the occupiers," and at the [[8 December|8 December]] [[1943]] *soerat poedjian* ceremony in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]], "Tjè' Ahmad from Tapa'toean, as representative of the hundreds of orphans who had lost their father in the struggle against the Dutch in this sub-district," was honoured.

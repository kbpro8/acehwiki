**Longbatah** was a kampong series in the [[XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], on the left bank of the [[Atjeh River]], whose two Dutch posts — [[Longbatah Zuid]] and [[Longbatah-Mesigit]] — stood in the second line of the Dutch defence; it was also the home of the hostile war leader known as the [[Imam Longbatah]], whose fortification line and redoubt stood for months against the post of Longbatah-Mesigit.

## The ground

East of the water channel that ran to the [[Kroeëng Tandjong]] lay, during the first Dutch year, a connected line of sixteen small square earth redoubts, joined by trenches and covered by plants of the scrub by which the whole position was hidden — in the middle, 200 metres behind a hundred-metre gap in the line, two high standing square earth redoubts of 30-metre faces with thick earth-and-timber breastworks forming the *reduit* and containing the roomy, well-built wooden house of the Imam Longbatah on poles. This was the best-fortified hostile position of the south face in [[1875]]–[[1876]]. From it the enemy's specialist sharpshooter — the Dutch knew him only as *the Glimmert* ("the glint", for the shining black head covering he wore) — picked off the Dutch sentries on the southeast bastion of Longbatah-Mesigit at irregular hours but always from the same spot, becoming for weeks *the terror of the garrison*, until the answer to his well-laid trick was grown into an armoured tree-top watch post. From the day that the armoured watch house stood in the great tree, his trick was known: he was seen no more.

## The kampong and the posts

The kampong Longbatah lay on the left bank of the Atjeh River near the angle of the second line; the road from Kota Radja to the posts of the south-east line in [[1876]] reached first the old work at Blang Tjoet, then Longbatah Zuid, then the mesigit in its walk. When, in February [[1876]], Pel's troops came round the IX Moekims and pushed the Dutch line forward to [[Pagar-Ajer]], the Imam Longbatah had to give up his strong position against Longbatah-Mesigit, which was then reduced in garrison and disarmed of heavy guns.

Westward, the sawah fields between Longbatah and Mesigit Pagar-Ajer were dry in the second half of 1876, when the road from Kota Radja to the posts of the south-east line — first traced over the sawah by the troops themselves and widened and raised during the year — carried the daily transport wagons.

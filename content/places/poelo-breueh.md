**Poelo Breuëh** (also *Poelau Bras*; modern *Pulau Breueh* or *Pulau Bras*) is the island off the north-western coast of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on which — or, more precisely, on the nearby islet of [[Rabò|Rabò]] — the inhabitants of the [[VI Moekim's of Groot-Atjeh|VI Moekim's of Groot-Atjeh]] held, in the period documented by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], an annual seven-day feast on the beach, the seventh day of which culminated in the [[Rabò annual seven-day feast|ritual sacrifice of a buffalo]] that was first stabbed and then led bleeding over all the rice-fields so that none should miss the disaster-averting sprinkling of its blood. The island is named in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] (p. 246) in connection with this [[Rabò annual seven-day feast|Rabò sacrifice]], cited from [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (vol. II, p. 315).

## In De Karbouw
The Poelo Breuëh passage in *De Karbouw* (p. 246) runs:

> *"Op het nabij Poelo Breuëh (Poelau Bras) gelegen eilandje [[Rabò|Rabò]] in Groot-Atjeh plegen de bewoners der [[VI Moekim's of Groot-Atjeh|VI Moekim's]] elk jaar aan het strand een zevendaags feest te geven. De zevende dag van deze „kermis der losbandigheid" offert men een buffel, die eerst in een of ander lichaamsdeel gestoken en dan bloedend over alle rijstvelden wordt heengeleid 'opdat geen daarvan de onheilwerende besproeiing met zijn bloed misse.' Daarna slacht men het dier op rituele wijze en nuttigt zijn vlees. Het verzuim hiervan zou een jaar van misgewas tengevolg hebben."*
The passage locates Poelo Breuëh as the parent island of which [[Rabò|Rabò]] is an islet, and identifies it as the locus of the annual buffalo-sacrifice feast of the [[VI Moekim's of Groot-Atjeh|VI Moekim's of Groot-Atjeh]] — the federation of six *mukim* (Islamic administrative units) that formed the territorial core of the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] heartland. The feast is described as a *kermis der losbandigheid* (carnival of debauchery) lasting seven days, with the buffalo-sacrifice on the seventh day as its ritual climax; neglect of the rite is said to result in a year of crop failure (*misgewas*).

## Historical-geographical context
Poelo Breuëh is the largest of a small archipelago of islands off the north-western coast of Atjeh — the same island group that includes [[Rabò|Rabò]] (the islet specifically named in *De Karbouw* as the site of the feast) and a number of smaller islets. The islands lie at the northern entrance to the Strait of Malacca and have, accordingly, a long history of strategic and commercial significance; they figure repeatedly in the Dutch and British navigational and military literature on Atjeh. The [[VI Moekim's of Groot-Atjeh|VI Moekim's]] whose inhabitants held the annual Rabò feast were the federation of six *mukim* that formed, with the [[XXII Moekim's|XXII Moekim's]] and other Atjehnese *mukim*-federations, the territorial-administrative structure of the Atjehnese state on the eve of the Dutch conquest.


## Significance
Poelo Breuëh's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is as the parent island of [[Rabò|Rabò]], the islet on which the [[Rabò annual seven-day feast|annual buffalo-sacrifice feast of the VI Moekim's of Groot-Atjeh]] was held — a documented instance of the wider Atjehnese pattern of bebloeding (ritual blood-sprinkling) of rice-fields as a disaster-averting magical act, performed here on a grand scale as a seven-day carnival with a buffalo-sacrifice at its climax. The island also figures in the wider Dutch and British navigational and military literature on Atjeh by virtue of its position at the northern entrance to the Strait of Malacca.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**Poelò Breuëh** (Pulau Breueh) was the island off the north-west point of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] where, immediately after the Dutch intervention of [[1873]], the establishments for the security of shipping were built — coal sheds, provision magazines and the lighthouse officially named **Willemstoren** by authority of King [[Willem III]] — and where the stranded crew of the *[[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Nisero]]* had intended to take in coal in November [[1883]].

### The Willemstoren and the establishments

The measures to erect a lighthouse on Poelò Breuëh were taken because such a beacon had been desired for the safety of the approach to the Strait of Malakka ever since steam navigation to the Indies had so considerably increased through the digging of the [[Suez Canal]]. On a height of 126 metres above the sea the tower arose — reached by a road laid through densely overgrown and difficult ground — 37 metres high, carrying a light-apparatus whose beams, 163.5 metres above the sea, were visible over a distance of 32 English nautical miles. With authority of King Willem III the name "Willemstoren" was given to the beacon. The personnel appointed for the light service on the island had initially to be relieved every month; only when the personnel had grown more accustomed to the climate was that term set wider. The unhealthiness there was in part ascribed to the forest-fellings and earth-shiftings done in the interest of the work, which laid the island bare to the rapid changes of temperature. At first the medical service on the island was performed by the surgeons of the warships stationed in the Lambalé bay, but in August [[1874]] a civil physician was appointed, and in December 1874 it was determined that the island would regularly be called at by the mail steamers running to and from Atjeh.

### Shipping and trades

Ship-building was practised principally on Poelò Breuëh and on [[Simaloer]], according to the economic survey the Government ordered from [[1893]] onward; and it was toward Poelò Breuëh that Captain [[Captain Woodhouse|Woodhouse]] of the English steamer *Nisero*, coming with a cargo of sugar from [[Soerabaja]] and bound for [[Suez]], attempted to make for coaling in stormy weather, when in the night of [[10 November|10]]–[[11 November]] [[1883]] his ship grounded on the West Coast at the mouth of the [[Panga]] river (see [[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Nisero affair (1883-1884)]]).

**Peutjoet** — also *Peucut* — is the military cemetery of [[Koeta-Radja]] in [[Atjeh|Aceh]], at which [[Dominee Thenu]] is buried "temidden van zovelen, die hij voorbereid had op die laatste gang daarheen" — among the many whom he had prepared for that last journey there. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* Peutjoet is the place of Thenu's [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|funeral]] on [[11 May]] [[1937]].

## The cemetery

Peutjoet was the military cemetery of [[Koeta-Radja]]. The cemetery was the burial place of the [[K.N.I.L.]] soldaten who had died in the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and in the late-colonial peace that followed. [[Dominee Thenu]] — who had served the colonial army for forty years — was buried there "temidden van zovelen, die hij voorbereid had op die laatste gang daarheen" — among the many whom he had prepared for that last journey there. The phrase is the book's quiet marker of Thenu's work: the dominee had prepared the soldaten for their deaths, and was now buried among them.

## The funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)

The [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|funeral of Dominee Thenu]] at [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[11 May]] [[1937]] concluded with his burial at Peutjoet. The funeral — with four retired [[Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)|M.W.O.]]-ridders ([[Salawono]] (3e kl.), [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]] and [[Pattipuluhu]]) as *slippendragers* and a Marechaussee brigade as *vuurpeleton* — was the book's most resonant image of the moral economy of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]. The burial at Peutjoet — among the many whom Thenu had prepared for death — is the book's quiet closure of the Thenu narrative.

## The gravestone

His gravestone at Peutjoet carries the verse from Job 4:3 — "Zie, gij hebt velen onderwezen, En gij hebt slappe handen gesterkt" ("Behold, you have instructed many, and you have strengthened feeble hands"). The verse, drawn from the speech of Eliphaz the Temanite to Job, is the book's quiet scriptural closure of the dominee's life: Thenu had instructed many, and had strengthened feeble hands.

## Significance

Peutjoet is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the military cemetery of [[Koeta-Radja]] and the burial place of [[Dominee Thenu]]. The book's argument is that Thenu's burial at Peutjoet — "temidden van zovelen, die hij voorbereid had op die laatste gang daarheen" — was the moral closure of the Thenu narrative: the dominee who had prepared the soldaten for their deaths was now buried among them. Peutjoet is therefore the book's marker of the moral economy of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]: the men of courage carrying the man of faith, and burying him among the men he had blessed.

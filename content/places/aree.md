**Aréë** was a *[[Landschap (Atjehnese)|landschap]]* in the *onderafdeeling* (subdivision) [[Sigli]], *afdeeling* (district) [[Pidie|Pidië]], of [[Aceh|Atjeh]], described by [[D. W. N. de Boer]] as "'n land van handelaren" — a land of traders — whose commercial character shaped the terms on which its rice fields were worked.

## Share-cropping terms

Under the *mawaih bahagi lhée* form of *[[Mawaih|mawaih]]*, the holder of a rice field supplied ground, plough cattle and *[[Bibit|bibit]]* and contributed half of the *[[Oepah poela|oepah poela]]* (planting wage), the cultivator retaining one-third of the crop. In Aréë, however, the *mawaih*-giver sometimes contributed two-thirds of the planting wage, because he could otherwise find no one to work his rice fields. De Boer explained the peculiarity by the occupational structure of the *landschap* (territory): its inhabitants being traders, such of them as were also *[[Sawah|sawah]]*-owners had to offer more favourable conditions in order to draw men from other *landschappen* (territories) to work their fields.

## Significance

Aréë illustrates, within a single *onderafdeeling*, how the *[[Adat|adat]]* of share-cropping was modified by local labour scarcity: the customary shares were not fixed but responded to the competition for cultivators between neighbouring *landschappen*.

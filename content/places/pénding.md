**Pénding** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Gajòlanden]]

In October the bivouac at [[Goempang]] was burned and a *klewang* attack made on the monthly transport to [[Koeala Simpang]] at [[Pénding]], costing three wounded against ten of the attackers killed; but some thirty-eight ill-disposed men were killed that month and a number submitted voluntarily. At the end of November [[H. N. A. Swart]] visited the district and could already visit *gampongs* two hours from Blang Kedjerèn without military escort.

### From [[The resistance in Atjeh 1908–1912]]

In October the bivouac at [[Goempang]] was still burned and a *klewang* attack was made on the monthly transport to [[Koeala Simpang]] at [[Pénding]], in which the Dutch had three wounded while ten of the attackers fell; but through the increased Dutch activity some thirty-eight ill-disposed men were killed in that month, while a number of people submitted of their own accord and in November a number of prominent leaders fell or offered their submission. Thereafter improvement set in: at the end of November Swart visited the district and could already visit *gampongs* two hours from Blang Kedjerèn without military escort.

### From [[klewang]]

[[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] defined the *[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]]* as the conversion of Atjeh from a province "governed with sabre and carbine" into a normal part of the Indies. Under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] "the offensive and the sabre came to the fore." The *klewang* attack — the sudden rush with cold steel on a careless bivouac or patrol — remained the characteristic Acehnese tactic to the end: at [[Nalon]] in [[1911]], at [[Linggö]], at [[Pénding]], and finally at [[Koeala Bhèë]] on [[25 April]] [[1918]]. "[[Meulaboh]] is the country par excellence for sabre charges," du Croo wrote. It was a *klewang* blow across the face that disfigured [[G. F. P. Gosenson]] for life in [[1917]], and an Acehnese police servant's *klewang* that struck down [[T. Béntara Titeuë]] in [[1913]].

**Lokop** was a sub-division (onderafdeeling) of the [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust van Atjeh]] in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in the early twentieth century. Lokop is documented in the historical record principally by [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]] in his [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]] as one of the sub-divisions of the Oostkust that did not produce enough rice for its own population: Lokop "levert ongeveer slechts 3/4 van het eigen verbruik op" — produces only about three-quarters of its own consumption.

## The Rice Deficit of Lokop

Kreemer's regional survey of the [[rijstbouw in Atjeh|Atjeh rice economy]] notes that the Oostkust van Atjeh was, in [[1918]], "meer peper- dan rijstland" — more pepper-land than rice-land — and that "uitgestrekte gebieden wachten nog op ontginning" — extensive regions await development. Within this general Oostkust pattern, the sub-divisions varied greatly in their rice balance. The sub-division [[Blang Kedjérén|Blang Kedjérén]] produced, in good years, a surplus over local consumption. The sub-division [[Peureula|Peureula]] had about 6,000 hectares of sawah in cultivation and about 6,000 hectares more suitable for rice, but the production was insufficient — especially for the European plantation workforce — and rice was imported.

Lokop stands in Kreemer's survey as the most rice-deficient of the Oostkust sub-divisions: producing only about three-quarters of its own consumption, and therefore dependent on imported rice for the remaining quarter. The Lokop deficit is one of the small but instructive features of the early-twentieth-century Atjeh rice-economy: even within a single *afdeeling* (the Oostkust), the rice balance varied greatly by sub-division, and the regional integration of the Atjeh rice-economy required the movement of rice from surplus to deficit sub-divisions.

## Significance

Lokop is significant for the encyclopedia of Aceh history as a documented sub-division of the Oostkust van Atjeh, and as the most rice-deficient sub-division of the Oostkust in [[1918]]. The Lokop rice deficit is one of the documented features of the early-twentieth-century Atjeh rice-economy, and is recorded by [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] as part of his systematic regional survey of the rice balance of each sub-division of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*.

The **XXV Mukims** (Dutch *XXV Moekims*; "Twenty-five Mukims") was one of the three *sagis* — confederacies of *mukim* districts — of [[Acheh]] proper, which together with the [[XXII Mukims]] and the [[XXVI Mukims]] made up the [[Three Sagis]] ruling the capital [[Banda Acheh]] in the early nineteenth century.

## Constitutional role

The three sagis surrounding the capital had, as Van der Kemp records, exercised tutelage over the sultanate since before the end of the seventeenth century: "reeds vóór het einde der 17e eeuw stond het sultanaat onder voogdij van de hoofden dier om de hoofdplaats gelegen landschappen". Their collective council was the body that gave or withheld recognition to a sultan, and the Three Sagis jointly dispatched the [[Three Sagis' letter to Penang (4 December 1822)|letter of 4 December 1822]] to Governor [[W. E. Phillips|Phillips]] of [[Penang]] refusing to receive [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] back, and swearing their oath on the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] at [[Kampong Java]].

## Position in 1819

When the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles–Coombs commission]] arrived at [[Banda Acheh]] on [[14 March]] [[1819]], the Three Sagis — in name; in practice [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]] of the XXII Mukims — exercised authority at the capital. The [[XXV Mukims]] and [[XXVI Mukims]] shared in this federation but played a less prominent part than the XXII Mukims under Polim. Van der Kemp does not single out the chief of the XXV Mukims by name in his narrative, and our knowledge of its internal affairs in this period comes mainly from later scholarship, notably [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *De Atjehers* (1893), which Van der Kemp cites.

## After 1819

The federation of the Three Sagis continued to refuse Jauhar Alum's restoration; when he died in December [[1823]] they, together with the [[Queen-Mother of Acheh|Queen-Mother]], "oefenden nu het gezag uit" — exercised what authority there was. [[Robert Fullerton|Fullerton]]'s minute of [[21 March]] [[1825]] confirmed the devolution of effective power to the Three Sagis and the port-chiefs of [[Sumatra's East Coast|the East Coast]], a state of affairs that rendered the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|Acheh treaty of 1819]] a "dead letter".

**Sumatra's West Coast** (Dutch *Sumatra's Westkust*) was the strip of country along the Indian Ocean face of [[Sumatra]] which contained the principal Dutch possessions in the island before [[1824]]: the residency of [[Padang]], the bay of [[Tapanoeli]], and, after the cessions of [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1814)|1814]], the former British settlement of [[Bencoolen]] (Fort Marlborough). In Van der Kemp's article the West Coast is invoked chiefly as the seat of the [[Bengal]]-appointed Lieutenant-Governorship held by [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] — the office from which, in the years [[1817]]–[[1824]], Raffles intrigued against the Dutch position on Sumatra and the Eastern Archipelago.

## Raffles's intrigues from Bencoolen

When in [[1819]] Raffles received from Lord [[Lord Moira|Moira]]'s government the commission to proceed to [[Acheh]] (see [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)]]), he did so in his capacity as Lieutenant-Governor of Bencoolen. The Dutch commissioners at [[Batavia]], as Van der Kemp notes, "zagen ons met leede oogen terugkomen in Hindostan; terwijl van Benkoelen uit, Raffles tegen ons gezag op Sumatra en verder in den Archipel intrigeerde". From Bencoolen Raffles wrote to [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|sultan Djohor]] at [[Pedir]] urging him to refuse any Dutch offers of help; it was the arrival of these intrigues at Calcutta that prompted the dispatch of the commission. The West Coast thus supplied both the vantage-point and the personal initiative from which the whole Acheh episode of [[1819]] proceeded.

## The subject of Van der Kemp's earlier study

Van der Kemp had treated the West Coast at length in a previous article in the same journal, *Sumatra's Westkust naar aanleiding van het Londensch tractaat van 13 Augustus 1814*, in *Bijdragen* XLIX ([[1898]]), pp. 1–268. He refers to that article at several points in the present study, both for the events surrounding the cession of Bencoolen and for the Achehnese tradition reported to the Dutch commissioner Pické, that the English who visited Acheh had been answered with characteristic bluntness when they asked who was king. The Westkust verhandeling also covers the Dutch recovery of Padang and Tapanoeli in [[1819]] and the arrangements consequent on the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1814)|treaty of 1814]].

## Place in the article

In the present study the West Coast appears as a counterpoint to [[Sumatra's East Coast]]: while the East Coast was the theatre of British commerce and Dutch intervention in the late 1820s, the West Coast remained the seat of Dutch administration — Padang, Tapanoeli, and the revived residencies — from which the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indian]] government sought, especially after [[1824]], to extend its sway eastward. The reduction of the export price of Java salt, which [[John Prince|Prince]] of Singapore reported in [[1827]] as a Dutch measure directed against Singapore's trade with the East Coast, was complained of at Padang because the Singapore salt "finds its way into the interior of Sumatra" and affected the sale of the Java salt imported thither for the same purpose.

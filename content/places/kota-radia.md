**Kota Radja** (also written Kuta Raja, Koeta Radja, "King's Fort") was the chief place and hoofdvestiging of the Dutch in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from [[January 1874|January 1874]] onwards, established in the conquered [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] of the sultan and the surrounding kampongs on the northern tip of Sumatra, and named for the sultan's citadel that became its nucleus. Throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] Kota Radja was the centre of the Dutch military and civil administration in Atjeh, the seat of the governor of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden from [[1881]], and the terminus of the railway to [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]].

## Origins

The name Kota Radja was given by the Dutch to their hoofdvestiging in the conquered [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] after the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]]. The Kraton itself was made into the principal fortification, and the surrounding kampongs — [[Penajoeng|Penajoeng]], kampong Djawa, kampong Gedah — became the outworks. By [[March 1877|March 1877]] the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] found on his [[Van Lansberge's visit to Aceh (March-April 1877)|inspection]] "On the roadstead of [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] there prevailed, in consequence of the number of ships lying there, great activity; on shore the high visitor was struck by the neat appearance of houses and streets, the efficient lighting of these, and the influx of Atjehers on the pasar. In the outworks of Kota Radja — Penajoeng, kampong Djawa and kampong Gedah — many neat houses of Chinese traders and private persons gave proof of the presence of capital and of confidence in the durability of our settlement."

## The Hoofd-stelling

Throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] the Dutch held a hoofd-stelling centred on Kota Radja. By [[June 1875|June 1875]] the hoofd-stelling was secured on all sides by an unbroken chain of fortifications; communication with the sea was assured, both along the Atjeh river and by the landing-place at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] and the road laid out from there; the former sultan's territory, with that of [[Marassa|Marassa]], [[Longbatta|Longbattah]] and a great part of the III Moekims in the [[XXV Moekims|Sagi der XXV Moekims]], was wholly conquered. "To be sure, this objective was not attained in the way that General Van Swieten had imagined. Politics had been able to accomplish practically nothing; only the weapons had brought it so far. The Atjeher was a much more stubborn enemy than had been thought. And what had now been obtained, that was only to be maintained by continual struggle, by the bringing of ever new sacrifices. Besides the hoofd-stelling itself, no fewer than thirty-eight posts had been set up for its defence, requiring a garrison of 22 companies — 2750 men — and a considerable artillery armament, while Van Swieten had considered 3500 men sufficient for the whole garrison of Atjeh."

## The Lines

The defence of Kota Radja rested on a system of lines: the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]] (a series of posts west of Kota Radja to connect it with [[Marassa|Marassa]] and [[Lampassei|Lampassei]]); the [[Noorderlinie|Noorderlinie]] (with posts at [[Lamara|Lamara]] and Kota Radja Bedil); the [[Zuidoosterlinie|Zuidoosterlinie]] (under the command of [[K. van der Heyden|Colonel Van der Heyden]] from [[1 April]] [[1 April]] [[1876]]); and the [[Noordoosterlinie|Noordoosterlinie]]. The principal post on the Westerlinie was [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] on the coast west of the Atjeh river mouth; the reede of Oleh-leh, especially during the west monsoon, offered many advantages over that of the mouth of the Atjeh river.

## The Railway to Oleh-leh

A railway was built between Oleh-leh and Kota Radja, completed in [[November 1876|November 1876]]. This was one of the principal engineering works of the early Dutch occupation, and was supplemented in [[September 1876|September 1876]] by the iron screw-pile sea-head at Oleh-leh. The railway was the first in the Atjeh territory and formed a critical link in the supply of the garrison at Kota Radja.

## The Reorganisation of 1881

By the administrative reorganisation of [[March 1881|March 1881]], Kota Radja became the residence of the governor of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden and the chief place of the assistant-residency of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], in which direct Dutch administration was introduced. The inland administration of Groot-Atjeh was conducted by district-heads or [[Uleebalang|hoeloebalangs]] appointed by the Governor-General; their replacements and helpers, as well as the moekim-heads and imams, were appointed by the governor; the replacements and helpers of the imams, as well as the kampong-heads, ketjihiks, by the assistant-resident; the heads of the wards, mandarsé's, by the controleur. Kota Radja also became the seat of the great missigit rebuilt by the Dutch after the war, the first stone of which was laid by the governor on [[9 October]] [[9 October]] [[1879]].

## The Hikajat Prang Kompeuni

In the [[Hikajat Prang Kompeuni|Hikajat Prang Kompeuni]] Kota Radja appears as the chief place and Oleh-leh as the commercial centre: "The Radja Moeda, Toekoe Nja Moekamat, strained every effort to bring the chief place and Oleh-leh to prosperity, and succeeded so well, that the population fled from their villages in streams not to be stemmed to the chief place, and fraternised with the kafirs."

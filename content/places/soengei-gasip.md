**The Soengei Gasip** — the Gasip River — is the river of the lower [[Siak|Siak River]] basin on [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]] along whose banks, the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] records, "remnants of the kraton must still be found in the primeval forest" — the surviving material trace of the [[rijk Gasip|destroyed kingdom of Gasip]] that gave the river its name. The Soengei Gasip is, in the Bureau's account, the principal geographical anchor of the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]] — the powerful pre-colonial Sumatran realm that, before the rise of the [[Siak|Sultanate of Siak]], had stretched from the lower [[Siak|Siak River]] deep into the southern interior of [[Sumatra]].

## The kraton ruins

The Bureau records the ruins of the Gasip kraton in a single, dense sentence: "Nog moeten in het oerwoud langs de Soengei Gasip overblijfselen van den kraton zijn te vinden" — "Remnants of the kraton must still be found in the primeval forest along the Soengei Gasip." The Bureau's use of the modal "moeten" — "must" — signals that the existence of the ruins was, at the time of writing ([[1918]]), attested by tradition rather than by formal archaeological survey. The "oerwoud" — "primeval forest" — that covered the ruins in the Bureau's day was, in the colonial imagination, the natural consequence of the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|Atjehnese destruction of Gasip in the early sixteenth century]]: the destroyed realm had been left "ten prooi aan ontbinding" — "to the prey of dissolution" — and the forest had reclaimed the site of its kraton.

## The naming of the river

The river takes its name from the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]] whose kraton it watered. The Bureau's account inverts the more usual relation between river and polity: rather than the kingdom taking its name from the river, the river takes its name from the kingdom. The toponymic survival of "Gasip" in the river's name is, in the Bureau's framing, the most concrete geographical trace of the destroyed realm, and it is the principal anchor by which the location of the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]] can be fixed in the late colonial period.

## The geographical context

The Soengei Gasip is, in the Bureau's account, a river of the lower [[Siak|Siak River]] basin — that is, of the stretch of the East Coast that, after the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|destruction of Gasip]], would become the heartland of the [[Siak|Sultanate of Siak]]. The Bureau's placement of the Gasip kraton on a tributary of the lower Siak — rather than on the Siak itself — is consistent with its broader account of the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]] as a polity that had stretched "tot ver buiten de grenzen van het latere sultanaat Siak" — "far beyond the borders of the later sultanate of Siak" — and whose kraton was, in the colonial period, a site of memory rather than of habitation.

## Significance

The Soengei Gasip is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the most concrete geographical trace of the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]]. The river's name preserves the toponymy of the destroyed realm; the ruins of its kraton preserve, in the Bureau's framing, the material vestige of the realm's political centre; and the primeval forest that covers the ruins preserves, in its turn, the memory of the realm's dissolution. The Soengei Gasip is thus the geographical complement of the *kabesaran* of the [[Siak|Siaksche hoofden]]: where the latter preserves the political memory of Gasip in the ornaments of its heirs, the former preserves the geographical memory of Gasip in the toponymy of its site.

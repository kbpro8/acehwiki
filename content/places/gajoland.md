**Gajoland** (the Gayo country; in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s usage *Het Gajoland*) is the highland region of central [[Acheh|Atjeh]], inhabited by the Gayo people and lying between [[Groot-Atjeh|Greater Aceh]] on the north coast and the [[Alasland|Alas]] valley to the south-east, mentioned in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] as the inland neighbour of the Atjehnese with whom a wide range of buffalo-related vocabulary, customs and beliefs are explicitly paired. The Gayo were the subject of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s monograph [[Snouck Hurgronje's Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners ([[1903]])|*Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners* ([[1903]])]] and of [[G. A. J. Hazeu|Hazeu]]'s *Gajosch-Nederlandsch Woordenboek*, both repeatedly cited in *De Karbouw* as the principal sources on Gayo vocabulary and custom.

## In De Karbouw


Gajoland is paired with Atjeh throughout *De Karbouw* — sometimes explicitly ("in Atjeh en het Gajoland *tjaram*") and sometimes by juxtaposition — in a long list of buffalo-related vocabulary and custom, including:

- **Buffalo-vocabulary**: Gajo *koro* "buffalo" (paired with Atj. *keubeuë*); Gajo *koro djögöt* "albino buffalo" (Atj. *keubeuë djagat*); Gajo *koro bintang* "blaze buffalo" (Atj. *keubeuë poetëh talaq*); Gajo *koro djalang* "feral buffalo" (Atj. *keubeuë oeteuën*); Gajo *oaq* / *oèq* "buffalo call" (Atj. *waq-waq*); Gajo *toenah* "buffalo wallow" (Atj. *koebang*); Gajo *koro ngangkoe* "lead buffalo of the herd" (Atj. *oelëë kawan* / *in ông keubeuë*).
- **Buffalo-husbandry implements**: Gajo *grantoeng* "buffalo bell" (Atj. *taq-toeq*); Gajo *toengkölön* "restraining yoke (*dwangjuk*)" (Atj. *leunggoeq*); Gajo *tjeras* "harrow" (Atj. *tjreuëh*); Gajo *igoe* "yoke" (Atj. *joeq*); Gajo *gerbag* "buffalo freight cart" (Atj. *grobag*); Gajo *nönggöl* "plough" (Atj. *langaj*).
- **Buffalo-rites and customs**: the [[peuteumbôn|fattening custom]] (Gajo *memantar*; Atj. *peuteumbôn*); the *tjaram*/*tjinggram* earnest-money of the buffalo-sale (paired with Atjeh); the Atjehse/Gajo use of dried seahorses to gentle wild buffaloes (paired with Atjeh); the [[Pantang of the albino buffalo in Atjeh|albino-buffalo prohibition]] for the Gölö, Beroeksah of [[Kedjoeroen Boekit]] and the Tjéq of [[Petiambang]]; the Atjeh/Gajo use of *getah dadih* buffalo-milk-curd glue for weapon-scabbards; the Gajo (and Atjeh) production of *minjaq sapi* buffalo-milk frying-fat for *toempi* and *tjoetjoer*; the Gayo practice of *membio koro* / *mengoro* "buffelen" (trampling the soil plant-ready); the white pennants before the stall (paired with Atjeh).
- **Buffalo-milk consumption**: Gajos are rare consumers of *dadih* (curdled buffalo-milk), in contrast to Atjehners who eat it sometimes with rice or banana, and to the Klingalese and Arabs of Atjeh who are great lovers of it.
- **Kreemer's personal fieldwork in Gajoland**: "Het is ons, bij ons verblijf in het Gajoland, indertijd meermalen voorgekomen, dat iemand bij navraag de naam van de groep, waartoe hij genealogisch behoorde, niet meer wist op te geven, terwijl het *pantang*-voorschrift, waarmee hij door geboorte in betrekking stond, muurvast in zijn herinnering was vastgelegd" (*De Karbouw*, p. 219) — Kreemer records his repeated personal experience in Gajoland that an informant could no longer remember the name of his own genealogical group, while the *pantang* prescription attached to that group was firmly fixed in his memory.
- **Gajoland circumcision rite on buffalo-skull**: cited in *De Karbouw* from [[Kreemer's Atjeh ([[1922]]-23)|Kreemer, *Atjeh*]] II, p. 446, in connection with the magical-apotropaic function of the buffalo-head.

## Snouck Hurgronje's Het Gajoland ([[1903]])


The principal Western scholarly source on Gajoland is [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners ([[1903]])|*Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners* ([[1903]])]], cited repeatedly in *De Karbouw* for Gayo ethnographic detail (e.g., on the white pennants before the stall, p. 171; on the albino-buffalo prohibition, p. 217; on the question of totemism vs. fear of ancestral wrath, p. 218). [[G. A. J. Hazeu|Hazeu]]'s *Gajosch-Nederlandsch Woordenboek* is the principal Gayo-Dutch lexicographical source and is cited throughout *De Karbouw* for Gayo vocabulary.

## Significance


Gajoland is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as the highland interior of central Atjeh, paired with Atjeh proper throughout *De Karbouw*'s comparative ethnography of buffalo-vocabulary and buffalo-custom. The Gayo country is administered, in the colonial period, as part of [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] and is, in the modern Indonesian provincial structure, part of the province of Aceh (comprising the modern [[Aceh Tengah Regency|Aceh Tengah]], [[Bener Meriah Regency|Bener Meriah]], [[Gayo Lues Regency|Gayo Lues]] and adjacent regencies). The repeated pairing of Gayo and Atjehnese material in *De Karbouw* — in vocabulary, in ritual, in legend — documents the close cultural continuity between the coastal Atjehnese and the highland Gayo on matters of buffalo-husbandry and buffalo-belief.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Gajo-land** — modern *Tanah Gayo*, the *Gayo Highlands* — is the highland region of northern Sumatra surrounding the [[Laoet Tawar|Laut Tawar]] lake, with its chief place at [[Takengon|Takengon]]. The Gajo people — *'een onverslaanbare taal'* — are a people of the interior, Mohammedan by religion, who came early under the overlordship of the [[Aceh|Atjehnese]] sultans, and were drawn into the hostilities against the Dutch government whenever the Atjehnese were in trouble. The principal ethnographic authority on the Gajo is [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners|Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners]]* ([[1903]]), supplemented by the dictionary of [[Dr. Hazeu|Dr. Hazeu]].

### The 1874 Reports


Broersma cites a passage of [[J. A. Kruyt|J. A. Kruyt]]'s *[[Atjeh en de Atjehers (Kruyt)|Atjeh en de Atjehers]]* ([[1877]]) describing the 1874 meeting of representatives of the Indies government with Gajos at [[Peureula (Perlak)|Perlak]], where the latter had come down with dammar, rattan, gambir, tobacco and dust-gold, wishing to exchange those goods for salt, linen and other articles. *'Zij spreken', schrijft Kruyt, 'een onverslaanbare taal; hun uiterlijk herinnert aan de Alfoeren, het menschen eten schijnt bij hen nog in zwang. Hun land moet prachtig zijn en er is een groot meer, waaraan sterk bevolkte kampongs liggen. De vrouwen moeten «eerst schoon» zijn. Kustbewoners worden er zeer goed ontvangen en krijgen dadelijk een vrouw. Echter komen zij er slechts met moeite en gevaar vandaar, omdat de Gajo's hen niet willen laten gaan, vreezend dat hun verhalen omtrent den rijkdom en de schoonheid van het land alsmede de aanwezigheid van goud en hebzucht van vreemden zullen opwekken.'*

### The Van Daalen Expeditions


Around [[1900]] the involvement of the Gajos in the hostilities against the Dutch was heightened, and thence the expeditions of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] in [[1901]] and [[1904]]. Order was established, but only in [[1911]] did a condition set in that may bear the name of pacification. The administrative work, strengthened by the presence of a few bivouacs, covered only some ten years. The *Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden|Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden* was formed in [[1905]], was re-allotted in [[1908]] among other administrative areas, but was restored in [[1922]] with chief place [[Takengon|Takengon]].

### The Gajo Weg


The Gajo country is reached from the eastern coast by the [[Gajo-weg|Gajo-weg]], begun by [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] and completed in [[1913]], a road of 103 kilometres from [[Bireuen|Bireuen]] to Takengon. Without that road, Broersma writes, *'zou onze kennis van Gajo-land niet heel veel verder reiken dan de Atjehsche berichten'* — *our knowledge of Gajo-land would not reach much further than the Atjehnese reports.* The road climbs from the coastal plain to the highland basin of Laut Tawar, reaching its highest point at kilometre 95 at 1.485 metres above sea-level.

### The Gajo House and Society


The Gajo *kampong*, as one sees at [[Böbasan|Böbasan]], differs from the Atjehnese. The Atjehnese *kampong* has dwellings with planted yards; the Gajo *kampong* has no yards with any growth but great houses. That recalls the Batak-*kampong*; an agreement with that is also that many related families live together and each has a section. The Gajo dwelling is built on posts above the ground and has a particular interior, which is described in the work of [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and elsewhere. Wood and *bilik* (wattle) are the building materials, and now it is remarkable that not every day a Gajo-*kampong* burns down, for stoking is done regularly. The space under the house lies full of firewood and against the cold one stokes daily. Wood, *bilik*, *atap* — everything is black with smoke — and for each family-section in the house there is still a round stove-place on the floor.

The head of the *kampong* is the *rëdjö*, as such chosen by the *saudörö* (clans); his authority is, like that of other bearers of authority, not unlimited. Gajo-land is a collection of republics with a largely democratic organisation. Despite this, the Gajo seems to live more peaceably in his mountain-land than the Atjehnese in the lowland.

### Significance


Gajo-land is the principal inland region of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, and the principal field of the Dutch expeditions of the post-1900 period. Its incorporation into the administrative system of the gewest is the work of Van Daalen's expeditions and of the Gajo-weg, and its ethnographic record is the work of Snouck Hurgronje. The Gajos themselves are the principal inland people of northern Sumatra, and their distinctive house-form, *rëdjö-saudörö* constitution and democratic *adat* set them apart from the Atjehnese of the coast.

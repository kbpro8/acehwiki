**Tamaram** was a tobacco plantation on Sumatra's East Coast whose manager was wounded and one of whose assistants was killed during the [[Acehnese raids on East Sumatra|Acehnese raids]] of [[1876]] — the most violent of the three attacks on plantations recorded by Dr. T. Volker for that year, alongside [[Glen Bervi]] and [[Ludwigsburg]].

## The 1876 attack

The attack on Tamaram took place during the intensification of [[Acehnese raids on East Sumatra|Acehnese raiding]] into the northern reaches of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]], as the [[Atjeh War]] fought from [[1873]] onward drove armed Acehnese parties across the frontier into the plantation districts of Deli and Langkat. Volker records the attack matter-of-factly among a sequence of events that illustrate the threat posed to European estates by the unresolved war on the Acehnese side of the border: *"de ondernemingen Glen Bervi, Tamaram en Ludwigsburg werden aangevallen; de administrateur van Tamaram gewond en een zijner assistenten gedood"* ("the estates Glen Bervi, Tamaram and Ludwigsburg were attacked; the manager of Tamaram wounded and one of his assistants killed").

The *Kedjoeroean* (district head) of [[Bohorok]] collaborated with the Acehnese raiders and subsequently fled with his followers to the [[Alas]] lands.

## Consequences

The raids at Tamaram and neighbouring plantations intensified colonial awareness of the security gap on Sumatra's East Coast frontier. They contributed to the decision to mount the military [[Expedition against Tamiang (1893)]], which drove out the Acehnese fighters threatening the plantation and petroleum belt and led to the establishment of fixed colonial authority in the [[Tamiang]] frontier zone. Final pacification was achieved in [[1899]].

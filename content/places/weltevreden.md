**Weltevreden** was the upper city (*bovenstad*) and administrative capital of the [[Netherlands India|Dutch East Indies]], located to the south of the old city of [[Batavia]]. By [[1926]], it was the seat of the Governor-General, the Council of the Dutch East Indies, the People's Council (*Volksraad*), and numerous government departments and cultural institutions. The [[Atjeh Monument]] stood in the Wilhelminapark at Weltevreden.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — Weltevreden

The atlas depicts and references Weltevreden extensively throughout its pages. Key Aceh-related locations in Weltevreden include:

- **The Wilhelminapark**: Location of the [[Atjeh Monument]], erected in memory of the [[Pacification of Atjeh]].
- **The Waterlooplein**: Location of the [[Michiels Monument]].
- **The palace of the Governor-General**: On Rijswijk and at Buitenzorg.
- **Koningsplein**: Site of the Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap and the Protestant church.
- **Departement van Onderwijs en Eeredienst**: The Department of Education and Religious Affairs, housed at Weltevreden.
- **Headquarters of the K.P.M.** (Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij).
- **The General Post Office**.
- **Volkslectuur**: The publisher of the atlas itself was based at Weltevreden.

## Significance

Weltevreden's significance for Achehnese history lies in its role as the imperial capital from which the [[Aceh War]] was directed and in which its memory was monumentalised. The [[Atjeh Monument]] in the Wilhelminapark, named after the Queen, symbolically located the subjugation of Acheh within the broader narrative of Dutch colonial achievement.

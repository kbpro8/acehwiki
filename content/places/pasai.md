## Geography


Pasai was located on the northernmost piece of Sumatra whose coast runs nearly east-west — that is, on the north Sumatran coast facing the Strait of Malacca, in the same general region as the later Atjehnese pepper ports of [[Pidie|Pedir]] and [[Pidie|Pidië]]. The polity is sometimes called *Samudera-Pasai* — the compound name of two adjacent settlements, *Samudera* (the older) and *Pasai* (the newer) — and the *[[Hikajat Raja-Raja Pasai]]* records the foundation of Pasai from Samudera as a separate polity.


## Pre-Atjehnese Primacy


The key fact of Pasai's early history, for AcehWiki purposes, is its primacy as the older centre of Islamic learning on the north Sumatran coast — the primacy that Atjeh would inherit in the early seventeenth century. [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] records: "Reeds eerder had het last gehad van Siam, maar het had de glorie gehad van het eerst en best geïslamiseerde staatje te zijn in die wateren, zodat Malakka in zijn glanstijd herhaaldelijk er gezantschappen heenzendt om antwoord te krijgen in theologische vraagpunten" (It had already earlier suffered from Siam, but it had the glory of having been the first and best Islamised state in those waters, so that Malacca in its golden age repeatedly sent embassies there to get answers in theological questions).

This primacy is recorded in the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* itself — the Malay Annals repeatedly record Malacca's theological consultations with the ulema of Pasai — and is the inheritance that Atjeh seized in the early seventeenth century, when the ulema of Atjeh (notably [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and the [[Sjaich Noeroddin|Sjaich Noeroddin]] who brought the *[[Sirat al-Mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]* to Kedah) became the new theological authorities of the Malay-Indonesian Islamic world.


## The Siamese Subjection


Before the rise of Atjeh, Pasai had been subject to Siam. [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] notes that "ons werk én de SM duiden beiden even discreet aan, dat Pasai schatplichtig was aan Siam" (both our work and the SM indicate, with equal discretion, that Pasai was tributary to Siam). The *[[Hikajat Raja-Raja Pasai]]* records the legend of the sultan of Pasai's abduction by the Siamese and his humiliation as a chicken-keeper at the Siamese court — "het daar voorkomend vertelsel, hoe de vorst geschaakt werd door de Siamezen en aan het Siamese hof kippenhoeder was!" (the story that occurs there, how the sultan was abducted by the Siamese and was a chicken-keeper at the Siamese court!) — a legend that Hooykaas notes is preserved only in the *Hikajat Raja-Raja Pasai* and is silent in the *Sadjarah Malajoe*.


## The Atjehnese Conquest


In the early seventeenth century, Pasai had to bow to the "machtige Atjéh" of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] (Mahkota 'Alam), "die zijn wil deed gelden op de wateren en kusten van Malakka en Sumatra" (who imposed his will on the waters and coasts of Malacca and Sumatra). The Atjehnese conquest of Pasai is the key event in the transfer of the Sumatran centre of Islamic learning from Pasai to Atjeh — a transfer that made the ulema of Atjeh, not the ulema of Pasai, the principal theological authorities of the Malay-Indonesian Islamic world in the seventeenth century.

The *[[Hikajat Raja-Raja Pasai]]* — the Malay chronicle of Pasai — is silent on the Atjehnese conquest: "Van de tijden van nog groter politieke afhankelijkheid, nl. van Atjéh, wordt geheel gezwegen, zodat onze hikajat, die geen jaartal bevat, slechts speelt in de legendarische tijden die daaraan voorafgaan, al dateert hij inderdaad uit de 15de eeuw" (Of the times of still greater political dependence, namely on Atjeh, there is complete silence, so that our *hikajat*, which contains no year-date, plays only in the legendary times that preceded [the conquest], although it actually dates from the 15th century). The chronicle's silence is itself a historical datum — the text dates itself to the pre-conquest period.


## The Portuguese at Pasai (1511)


Pasai also played a role in the [[Portuguese conquest of Malaka (1511)|Portuguese conquest of Malacca in 1511]]. Albuquerque, on his way from Cochin to Malacca, called at the Atjeh pepper ports of [[Pidie|Pedir]] and Pasai, where he alarmed the rulers by seizing Muslim merchant vessels. The Portuguese encounter with Pasai in 1511 is part of the broader story of the Portuguese-Atjehnese-Malay rivalry that would dominate the sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries.


## Significance


For AcehWiki, Pasai is significant as the older rival centre of Sumatran Islamic learning that Atjeh displaced in the early seventeenth century. The transfer of the centre from Pasai to Atjeh — under [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] — is the key event in the rise of the Atjehnese court as the principal centre of Islamic learning in the Malay-Indonesian Islamic world in the seventeenth century, and the background against which the Atjehnese compositions of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|ar-Rānīrī]] — the *[[Sirat al-Mustaqim|Sirāṭ]]*, the *[[Bustan al-Salatin|Bustān]]*, and the *Bad'* of [[1638]] — must be measured.


## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — Pasé oil field

### Production figures

For the full North-Sumatran production table see the entry on the [[East Atjeh oil block]]. The Pasé field's production of 1,797 tons in [[1941]] was smaller than the [[Peureula]] field's 36,640 tons (also [[1941]]), and considerably smaller than the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]]'s 527,590 tons. The fact that the Pasé field produced no oil in [[1940]] but 1,797 tons in [[1941]] suggests that the field was brought into production during [[1941]], making it the newest of the Atjeh-complex fields. The Pasé field is also the only Atjeh-complex field operated by the [[N.P.P.M.]], which entered the East Indian oil industry only in [[1931]] and was accordingly the most recent of the major oil-company entrants to the Indies.

### The name "Pasé"

The name "Pasé" — also written *Pasai* or *Pase* — is the standard Acehnese form of the name of the historical Muslim kingdom of [[Pasei|Samudra Pasai]], which dominated the north-eastern coast of Atjeh from the thirteenth to the fifteenth centuries and which is recorded in the colonial Dutch scholarly literature (e.g. the *[[Hikajat Radja-radja Pasei]]*) as the oldest Muslim polity of the Indonesian archipelago. The oil-field name "Pasé" accordingly carries the same double resonance as the [[Peureula]] field name: it is both the designation of a colonial-era oil field and the name of a historical Acehnese kingdom. The recurrence of these kingdom-names on the colonial oil map of Atjeh is itself a measure of the geographical continuity between the precolonial and colonial geographies of the residency.

### Geological context

Van Bemmelen's general classification of the Atjeh petroleum geology places the Pasé field among the "steep folds (Atjeh, Borneo)" that "were repeatedly investigated by drilling but the results have not been encouraging". The implication is that the Pasé oil is hosted in steeply-folded neogene sediments of the Atjeh north coast, in a structural setting less favourable for oil accumulation than the gently-domed anticlines of the South-Sumatran fields. The very small size of the Pasé production (1,797 tons in its first year) is consistent with this assessment.

### Significance

The Pasé oil field is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history on two grounds. First, it is the only Atjeh-complex field operated by the [[N.P.P.M.]] (the Standard Oil of California subsidiary), and accordingly the clearest colonial instance of the operation of that company on Atjeh territory. Second, the field's name carries the historical resonance of the [[Pasei|Samudra Pasai]] kingdom, and the recurrence of the Pasai name on the colonial oil map of Atjeh is a reminder of the geographical continuity between the precolonial and colonial geographies of the residency.

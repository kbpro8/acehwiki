**Pasai** was an early Malay Muslim sultanate situated on the northern coast of Sumatra, in the region of [[Aceh]], known primarily through the [[Hikayat Raja-raja Pasai]] chronicle. The sultanate flourished from the thirteenth to the early sixteenth century and served as a key centre of Islamic learning and trade in the western archipelago.

## Identification
The name *Pasai* appears in the Malay literary tradition as the seat of a ruling dynasty whose history is recorded in the [[Hikayat Raja-raja Pasai]]. The text notes that the chronicle was edited by [[Dulaurier]] and later published in the [[Bijdragen tot de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië|B.K.I.]] and in an edition by [[J. P. Mead]] appearing in the [[Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society|J.S.B.R.ASS]].

## Legacy
Pasai’s legacy lies in its role as one of the earliest Muslim polities in the Malay world, providing a model for later sultanates such as [[Aceh]] and [[Malacca]]. Its historical memory is preserved in the Hikayat, which continues to be studied for its linguistic and linguistic and historiographic source material for scholars of [[Malay]] literature and Acehnese history.

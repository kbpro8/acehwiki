**Balbalan** (the *Balbalan* or Babalan river of the [[Langkat]] district of the East Coast of [[Sumatra]]) was the river in which the harbour of [[Pangkalan Brandan]] lay, and whose channel (*Balbalangeul*) off the coast belonged geographically to the [[Aroebai|Aru Bay]] — a conjunction at the heart of the [[Deli-Atjeh railway connection controversy (1912-1916)|Deli-Atjeh railway connection controversy]].

## The disputed geography

It was on the Balbalan distinction that [[Schadee]] based his correction of [[Beyerman]] in the [[1912]]–[[1913]] correspondence quoted by [[J. Roest]] in *[[De spoorwegverbinding tusschen Deli, Atjeh en de Aroebaai]]* ([[1916]]): the Aru Bay question was, he said, identical with the question of Pangkalan Brandan — or vice versa. It might be true that, from a nautical point of view, the approach to Pangkalan Brandan, that is the Babalan channel outside the coast, could be counted as of the Aru Bay; but this did not at all hold for the Babalan river itself, in which the harbour of Pangkalan Brandan lay. *"We treat not a nautical but an economic question,"* Schadee's letter runs in Roest's citation: the traffic of a river harbour in the river belonged to the river, not to the bay.

The channel — with the [[Sembilangeul]], the southern entrance of the Aru Bay — formed one of the two ways by which sea-going coasters entered the harbour country of north Langkat; it lay a short distance southeast of the point where, in the railway plans, the projected line Besitang–Pangkalan Soesoe (later: the state-built tramway extension) would end at the Aru Bay.

The **Dutch lodge at Atjeh** — *de loge te Atjeh* — was the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] trading post (factory) maintained at the court of the Sultan of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the first half of the seventeenth century. It was headed by an *Opperhoofd* (chief) with eight *helpers* (assistants) and served as the Company's principal station on the northern coast of Sumatra. The lodge is the physical setting of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]] and of much of the early Dutch-Atjeh encounter.

## Foundation and VOC presence

By [[1641]] the Company already possessed "in Atjeh een loge, waar een Opperhoofd met acht helpers verblijf hield" — a lodge in Atjeh, where an *Opperhoofd* with eight assistants resided. The Sultan had granted the Company "het uitsluitend recht tot handel op Sumatra's Westkust" — the exclusive right to trade on Sumatra's Westkust — where pepper was the principal product. The lodge was therefore not merely an Atjeh station but the headquarters of the Company's pepper-purchasing operations across the Westkust pepper-ports, including the dependent pepper-port of [[Tikoe]].

## The town of Atjeh in 1641

The town in which the lodge stood is described in the Soury embassy record as "een stad met huizen van bamboe, slechts enkele van steen, alle zes voet van den grond wegens de bandjirs" — a city with houses of bamboo, only a few of stone, all six feet off the ground on account of the *bandjirs* (floods). "Er was geen omwalling" — there was no surrounding wall. "Op de twee marktplaatsen boden Indiërs, Arabieren, Chineezen en Maleiers, aangelokt door lage invoerrechten, hun waren te koop" — On the two market-places Indians, Arabs, Chinese and Malays, drawn by low import duties, offered their wares for sale. Temples, pagodas, royal palaces, gardens with the tomb-shrines of dead sultans, fountains and waterworks struck the Europeans "door hun vreemde schoonheid" — by their strange beauty.

## The lodge's role in the Soury embassy of 1641

When *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Pieter Soury]]'s embassy reached the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh river]] in [[1641]], the *Opperhoofd* of the lodge — at that time [[Jacob Compostel|Jacob Compostel]] — together with the chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] and the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] (harbour-master), came out to greet the ships and brought the *tjap* or passport required to come ashore. The lodge then became the legation's residence and base of operations throughout the audience of [[1 July]] [[1641]] and the subsequent negotiations over the late Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]]'s jewel-order. See [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)]] and [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)]].

## The hand-over of 1641

The Soury embassy was also the occasion of a formal hand-over of the *Opperhoofd* post: [[Jacob Compostel|Compostel]], the outgoing chief, was replaced by [[Pieter Willemz|Pieter Willemz]], who had arrived from Batavia as his designated successor. The presence of both men in the six-man legation suite at the audience of [[1 July]] was the formal moment of transfer — the Atjeh court saw both the outgoing and the incoming *Opperhoofd* together. The [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]]'s pointed omission of [[Jacob Compostel|Compostel]] from the gift of an *[[Atjehsche feestjas|Atjehsche feestjas]]* was a deliberate signal that she considered the lodge chief — as resident rather than as embassy member — outside the ceremonial of the legation.

## The lodge's earlier visitors

The lodge's history long predates the Soury embassy. The site had been a Dutch-Atjeh contact point since the [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)|Houtman disaster of 1599]] and the [[Pigskin parchment letter incident at Atjeh (1600)|pigskin letter incident of 1600]]. The first Dutch office on Sumatra — baptised "Zeeland" — was established at Atjeh in [[1602]] by the returning Zeeland ships that had carried [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]]'s letter. See [[Fort Zeeland at Atjeh]]. [[Justus Schouten|Justus Schouten]] had been at the lodge the year before the Soury embassy to receive Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]]'s jewel-order. [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]]'s twenty-six-month Atjeh captivity ([[1599]]–[[1601]]) had also been spent in or around the lodge's precinct.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the Dutch lodge is the principal institutional hinge between the sultanate and the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] in the first half of the seventeenth century — the physical and political site where Dutch-Atjeh diplomacy was conducted, where the Sultan's pepper-monopoly on the Westkust was brokered to the Company, and where the successive *Opperhoofden* lived as the Sultan's resident guests. The lodge's small staff — one *Opperhoofd* and eight *helpers* — is itself a measure of how thin the Dutch presence in Atjeh actually was, in spite of the ceremonial grandeur of embassies like Soury's.

**Bajan** — also written *Bajeuën* — is the wood-cutting village of the *onderafdeeling* [[Langsa|Langsa]], situated on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] nineteen kilometres from Langsa. The place, on a river of the same name, was formerly of somewhat greater importance, when a *controleur* was stationed there and a garrison lay. Now there is nothing but official and military silence and the saw of the wood-enterprise.

## The Wood-Enterprise of the Deli-spoor
The work of wood-cutting at Bajan is run by the [[Deli-spoor|Deli-spoor]], which took over the whole licence from a trading company (*Bajeuën*) that had originally received a permit of three to four thousand hectares for wood-cutting and had let the Deli-spoor participate. The management of the forests had however been wanting — one cut without following any particular system, now here, now there. The Deli-spoor, which wished to draw its sleepers from there, then took over the whole licence for its own account.

The workshop stands near the road; there the wood is sawn for the Deli-spoor's own use and also for sale. In years of much house-building one could place the worked wood anywhere, and Bajan even supplied Japan. At present the Deli-spoor's own use is by far the principal matter. The worked wood goes south by the Atjeh-tram under a special tariff. Sleepers and telephone-poles are worked on the spot, where Chinese are employed, while Atjehnese serve in the workshop. An iron track connects the saw-mill in the forest and the workshop, and daily the small locomotives with their long wood-wagons run through the fine forest, where *marbau*, *damar laoet* and *meranti*-sorts are cut.

## The Labour-Form: Panglong
The labour-form at Bajan has traits of the *panglong*-enterprise, which the Chinese prefer on the *afdeeling* Bengkalis of the Oostkust van Sumatra. If the government issues licences for a fixed number of years for wood-cutting, with the obligation to pay a fixed rent or dues and to replant, the *panglongs* on the other hand can always work on and pay for each man monthly a certain sum (formerly f 2.50, now f 3.50). A *panglong* is a group of labourers, usually from Singapore, who work under a head, their *taukeh* (mandore), either for the cutting of beams or firewood, for the sawing of planks or for the burning of charcoal. On Bengkalis and the other islands near Siak, also in Siak itself, there are small ones with three and seven men, but the usual number is twenty-two. By *St. 1909 No. 488* a *panglong*-regulation has been given, but on the islands, in the forests, supervision is difficult.

At Bajan, however, two groups work, each of about forty men, people from over the Strait and old contract-labourers of Deli. Each group has a head, called *kepala*; each labourer is assessed here for the payment of head-money, which for a year is at least f 7.20. Each group has a cook and a *krani* for the book-keeping of each man's earnings. The European manager, who here naturally has a regular supervision so that irregularities do not escape notice, approves the work at delivery and pays the *kepala* according to fixed tariffs for sleepers, for beams of certain dimensions. The Chinese have their dwellings in the forest near the stacking-places; some have a Chinese wife, most have a Javanese. A labourer makes thirty to fifty guilders a month; the *kepala* earns much more.

## The Forest
The forest is a fine piece of nature, but the terrain is difficult to work because it continually rises and then falls steeply. The tree-species are mixed, though groups of *damar laoet* occur. About 250 tons of wood are cut a month (one ton = 1¼ cubic metre), but the wealth is so great that for 25 years 700 tons a month could have been taken.

## Significance
Bajan is the principal wood-cutting settlement of the eastern coast of Atjeh, and its labour-form — a modified *panglong* system under direct European supervision — is the model on which the colonial government hoped to regulate the wood-cutting of the flood-forests of the eastern coast generally. The dependence of the Deli-spoor on Bajan for its sleepers made the village an integral part of the railway- economy of northern Sumatra.

**Lho Soekon** — also *Lho Sukon*, modern *Lhok Sukon*, also *Lhoksoekon* — was a *bivak* and post in the [[Lho Seumawé]] area on the North coast of [[Aceh]], seat of the *civiel-gezaghebber* (civil authority) — Schouten held the post in the years of the false [[T. Radja Sabi]] — and of the division command that directed the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|maréchaussée]]-brigades operating against the bands of [[Pang Nanggroë]], [[Tjoet Meuthia]], and the *adelaarsjong*. The graves of [[Pang Nanggroë]] and [[Pang Lateh]] lie behind its *keudé* in wooden-enclosed *kandangs*, and the *pasanggrahan* of Lho Soekon is the setting of [[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]]'s long interview with the grown [[T. Radja Sabi]]. In the [[1930]] compilation of *[[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]*, Lhoksoekon appears as an *onderafdeeling* of the afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], seat of three *zelfbesturende landschappen* (Keureutoë, Matang Koeli, and Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë) whose *oeleebalang* collectively issued the *woeker-verordening* of [[9 December]] [[1926]] (Reg. No. 1), the *klappercultuur-verordening* of [[25 August]] [[1916]] (Reg. No. 18a), and the *mantri*-instructie of the same date for the klappercultuur (Reg. No. 18b).

## Centre of resistance

In the years [[1905]]–[[1910]] the bende of [[Pang Nanggroë]] brought the whole country of [[Keureutoe]], [[Pasé]], and Lho Soekon to *den rand van een algemeenen opstand* — the edge of a general rising. The *kampongs* ran empty, the *sawahs* stayed unworked, hundreds of men went to the mountains; the work of ten years of fighting seemed lost. [[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] writes that *"Pang Agam mèl in Lho Soekon"* — Pang Agam submitted at Lho Soekon — became one of the standard phrases of the older Atjehers when they spoke of the lot of the men of those years; *mèl* or *sjahid*, one or the other, was the only fate the war allowed.

## The murder of Meurandeh Paja, January 1905

On [[26 January]] [[1905]] a patrol under sergeant Vollaers bivouacked at the *meunasa* of Meurandeh Paja, east of Lho Soekon. The *troep* had neglected every security measure; armed Atjehers who claimed to sell fruit and chickens were let inside the *pagger*, and two were even allowed to climb the ladder to where the commander lay reading. At a sudden signal the overval began; sixteen of the seventeen militairen were killed, only one Inlandsch fusilier escaping through the kampong. Long before he could reach a garrison, [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]] — *de latere generaal, toen commandant te Lho Seumawé* — had the news; *in Atjeh gaan zulke berichten zeer snel door het land*. Swart *joog met eene patrouille in den gemeensten marsch* to Meurandeh Paja, found the sixteen corpses *afschuwelijk toegetakeld*, the sergeant's body upstairs in the *meunasa* with his book beside him, and buried them in a mass grave.

## The arrest and execution of T. Tjhi Toenong

The investigation of the Meurandeh Paja overval pointed to [[T. Tjhi Toenong]]. Swart let luitenant Van Vuuren arrest him cautiously when the *Teukoe* came to [[Lho Seumawé]] on [[5 March]] [[1905]] for ordinary business. He was tried, condemned to death, and — at [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s insistence that a worthy fighter should not hang — shot on the strand.

## The brigades of Mosselman and Kretz

In [[July]] [[1910]] luitenant [[Mosselman]] came to Lho Soekon, *en in October was ik al vrij aardig ingeloopen*. From Lho Soekon the brigades of Mosselman, [[Kretz]], and Van Slooten set out after [[Pang Nanggroë]]'s bende and after the *adelaarsjong*. On [[25 September]] [[1910]] sergeant Van Slooten overvalled the bende in the Paja Tjitcem; [[Pang Nanggroë]] was killed, and his body was brought to Lho Soekon for recognition by the bevolking. *Het werd begraven achter de keudé van Lho Soekon, en daar ligt het nu nog binnen eene afgedekte houten omwanding, eene kandang*. Beside it, in another *kandang*, lies the body of [[Pang Lateh]] — *zij zijn in den dood weer bij elkaar, als op menigen ouden krijgstocht*.

## The interview with T. Radja Sabi

*Tegenover mij in de pasanggrahan van Lho Soekon zit een Atjeher van bijna veertigjarigen leeftijd* — so begins Zentgraff's account of his meeting with the grown [[T. Radja Sabi]], who had returned from the forests in [[1919]] and was, by [[1937]], acting *oeléëbalang* of [[Keureutoe]]. They talked in the *pasanggrahan* of Lho Soekon, *en eene sterke emotie beving mij bij de treffendste episoden van zijn verhaal*. *Dan gaan wij naar de keudé, en een eindje daarachter liggen binnen houten wanden de graven van Pang Nanggroë en Pang Lateh*.

## Significance

Lho Soekon is the *bivak*-anchor of the Keureutoe-epos: the place from which the brigades of [[Mosselman]] and [[Kretz]] marched, where the bodies of the dead resisters lie under their *kandang*-enclosures behind the *keudé*, and where Zentgraff sat across from the grown *adelaarsjong* to hear the boy's-war told in the man's voice. The *djaksa*'s news to Schouten — *dat hij in staat was den lang gezochten T. Radja Sabi uit de bergen naar beneden te brengen* — that opened the *valsche-pretendent* affair of [[1913]] was delivered at the civiel-gezaghebber's house at Lho Soekon.

## Account from *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1930) — collective vaststelling

In the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, Lhoksoekon was an *onderafdeeling* of the afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh, seat of three *zelfbesturende landschappen* — Keureutoë, Matang Koeli, and Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë — whose *oeleebalang* jointly issued every Lhoksoekon-seated regulation. The collective signatory clause common to its three regulations — *"Kami Oeleebalang Negeri: De Zelfbesturen van: Keureutoë Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë Matang Koeli"* (We, the Oeleebalang of the Negeri: The Zelfbesturen of: Keureutoë, Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë, Matang Koeli) — is the cleanest single illustration in the 1930 compilation of the Atjeh practice of collective *vaststelling* (collective enactment) by the *oeleebalang* of an *onderafdeeling*. The three regulations are:

### The woeker-verordening of 9 December 1926 (Reg. No. 1)

The *woeker-verordening* (anti-usury regulation) of [[9 December]] [[1926]] is the first-numbered regulation in the 1930 compilation and the principal Justitie regulation of the entire book. It was approved by the Gouverneur on [[21 December]] [[1926]] and is explicitly modeled on the metropolitan anti-usury statute, [[Staatsblad 1916 No. 643]]. The legal basis invoked is the *Zelfbestuursregelen 1919* (Staatsblad No. 822). The regulation consists of a single *Eenig artikel* providing that any contract in which one party exploits the lightness, inexperience, or necessity of the other to extract a benefit exceeding the legitimate value of the consideration shall be voidable as *woeker* (usury). The comparative table extends the *woeker*-regeling by parallel *GELIJKE VERORDENINGEN* to the *onderafdeelingen* Sigli, Meureudoe, Lammeulo, Idi, Tjalang, Gajo LoeBus, Alaslanden, Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen, and Simeuloed — a synchronised Gouvernement-wide rollout of the anti-usury template that Lhoksoekon had originated.

### The klappercultuur-verordening of 25 August 1916 (Reg. No. 18a)

The *klappercultuur-verordening* (coconut-cultivation regulation) of [[25 August]] [[1916]] — full title *Zelfbestuursverordening van 25 Augustus 1916 tot bescherming van klapperaanplantingen tegen rupsen- of insectenplaag in de zelfbesturende landschappen van de onderafdeeling Lhoksoekon* (Self-government regulation of 25 August 1916 for the protection of coconut plantings against caterpillar- or insect-plague in the self-governing *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling* Lhoksoekon). The regulation protects not only the coconut (*klapper* / *kelapa*) but four other palm species — *nibeeng* (nibong), *aren* (areng), *roembia* (rumbia), and *pohon nipah* (nipah) — that share the same insect-pest vulnerability. Its distinctive feature is the mandatory sanitation regime: every dead or dying palm must be cut down and burned with its roots, or buried three feet deep, or submerged in water, with all refuse burned. The regulation was extended by parallel *regelingen* to Padangtidji, Lammeulo, Sigli, Meureudoe, Idi, Tamiang, Langsa, Takingeun, Serbeudjadi, Gajo LoeBus, Alaslanden, Tjalang, Meulaboh, Tapaktoean, and the Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen.

### The mantri-instructie of 25 August 1916 (Reg. No. 18b)

The *mantri*-instructie of the same date — *Zelfbestuursbesluit van 25 Augustus 1916 tot vaststelling van een instructie voor de mantri's voor de klappercultuur in de Zelfbesturende Landschappen in de onderafdeeling Lhoksoekon* (Self-government decree of 25 August 1916 establishing an instruction for the *mantri*'s of the klappercultuur in the self-governing *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling* Lhoksoekon) — is the operational supplement to Reg. No. 18a. It establishes the specialised corps of *klapper-mantri*'s — native auxiliary civil servants charged with *toezicht* (oversight) on the klappercultuur — and prescribes their duties, the journals they must keep, and their subordination to the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar*. The Malay recital converts the Gregorian date to the Hijri: *25 Sjawal van het Mohammedaansche jaar* — i.e. 25 Sawal 1334 AH — the only Hijri date in the chunk.

The *mantri*-instructie is notable for the explicit dual subordination it establishes: the *klapper-mantri*'s, though appointed by and reporting to the *kepala negeri* (the *oeleebalang*), must obey the orders of the European *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar* in klappercultuur matters. The same dual subordination recurs in Reg. No. 20 (the veterinary-*mantri* instructie of [[15 July]] [[1917]] for [[Bireuen|Bireuën]]).

### Lhoksoekon as a node in the Gouvernement-wide regulatory mesh

Beyond the three Lhoksoekon-seated regulations, the *onderafdeeling* receives parallel *regelingen* in the comparative tables of nearly every other regulation in the compilation. By the time the 1930 compilation went to press, Lhoksoekon was thus a node in a Gouvernement-wide regulatory mesh: it originated three regulations (Nos. 1, 18a, 18b) and received parallel *regelingen* of at least seven others (Nos. 7, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16) — including the heerendienst-regeling of [[18 Augustus]] [[1928]] (Reg. No. 14, the Bireuën heerendienst-regeling) and the Mohammedaansch godsdienstonderwijs regulation (Reg. No. 12). Lhoksoekon is, in this sense, the empirical demonstration of the *collective-vaststelling* and *parallel-regeling* practice that the 1930 compilation as a whole documents.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Lho Soekon** — also written *Lho' Soekon* — is an *onderafdeeling* of the *afdeeling* [[Noordkust van Atjeh|Noordkust van Atjeh]] in the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, lying between the rivers [[Kroeeng Djamboe Aje|Djamboe Aje]] and [[Pasé|Pasé]], in a region that once formed part of the ancient kingdom of [[Samoedra|Samoedra]]-[[Pasé|Pasé]], over which the Atjehnese sultans gained lordship in the sixteenth century — their century of expansion.

### Geography

In the middle of Lho Soekon run the rivers Peutoë, Pira and [[Keureuto|Keureutò]] — pronounced *Kertó* — which near the chief place Lho Soekon connect with one another and flow to the sea in two arms. Remarkable is how the Keureuto divides the *onderafdeeling* in two, and how the two parts differ in the nature of the land: the south-eastern half is all *paja* — swamp — with a population of fifteen thousand souls, while the smaller northern half is free of it and counts twice the number of souls. The proportion will in time change; more people will come now that the head of [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]], [[Teukoe moeda Oesoch|Teukoe moeda Oesoeh]], has tamed the floods of the Djamboe Aje and promoted the drainage of the river-basin.

### The Paja Tjitjem

The great swamp of the south-eastern half is the [[Paja Tjitjem|Paja Tjitjem]], for which the builder of the tram-line made a great curve. Even so he could not do without a dyke. The great military road could not wholly avoid the swamp either, and besides, a *landschap*-road runs through part of the *paja* right down to the sea, so that it is possible in this part of Lho Soekon to undertake a crusade. Such a journey is however no encouragement. A more desolate plain one can hardly imagine — a dingy region, endlessly sombre, lifeless. At a few points along the road one meets a wretched little neighbourhood with people of unfavourable appearance. How could it be otherwise, when rice-cultivation is so easily a complete failure and the laying out of drainage often disappoints, because the peat-soil easily subsides and makes the digging useless? The inhabitants, since rice-cultivation is precarious, need a side-income. Some find a meagre income in salt-making on the coast.

### The Western Hills

West of this region lie the *boekits* — the hills of 100 to 300 metres which form the transition to the mountainous [[Rödjö Boekit|Rödjö Boekit]]. In those hills lives a population that plants pepper, though not so much that Lho Soekon could be called a pepper-land like Idi or Perlak or Groot-Atjeh. In [[1921]] Lho Soekon exported 1.383 *picol*. But that hill-region is marked in the annals of Atjeh's military enterprise: when about fifteen years before Broersma's visit — that is, about [[1908]] — the resistance flared up there, it proved difficult to reach, because it repeatedly withdrew from the Paja Tjitjem into the hills. Then [[Christoffel|Christoffel]] was sent with his *marechaussées*, who took a bivouac at [[Panon Labeuë|Panon Labeuë]] and from there broke the resistance. He was called *apitan rimoeeng* — the tiger-captain — *'omdat zijn greep bloedig was'* — *because his grip was bloody.* The memories of the suffering are still fresh in such a region as Keureuto; and when spiritual parasites misuse the doctrine of the holy war to keep minds bitter, double vigilance is required, a vigilance that is not ostentatious. And in such regions the presence of administrative officials with Atjeh-experience and knowledge of the language is highly to be valued.

### The Landschap Keureuto

At [[Blang Djeroeën|Blang Djeroeën]] lives the *oeléebalang* of Keureuto, [[Teukoe Bentara Keureuto|Teukoe Bentara Keureuto]], who rules a large *landschap* and receives accordingly f 600 a month from the *landschapskas* in addition to f 125 for his car. The *landschap* has its fertile rice-plain in the west and its pepper-hills above. There are also five licences in the *landschap* Keureuto which have not yet been opened though the dues are already owed. *'Met zulke bezoeken van «heet kapitaal» kan geen land zijn gediend'* — *With such visits of «hot capital» no land can be served.*

### The Sarikat Islam

In Lho Soekon, as in the rest of the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh*, the [[Sarikat Islam|Sarikat Islam]] showed a vigorous life after [[1918]]. Traders, school-teachers — *'laat ons zeggen de intellectueelen'* — but also several chiefs were members. A chief of Samalanga had joined by way of a move on the political chessboard on which he was playing against the influential [[Sri Tengkoe Abdoel Hamid Orangkaja Sri Maharadja Mangkoe Boemi|Maharadja of Lho' Seumawé]], who had also become a member of the Sarikat Islam in order to strengthen his chances of retaining a *landschap Plimbang*. The movement in Lho' Seumawe took the direction of bringing the distinction between believers and unbelievers to the fore, of undermining the Dutch government, and of working towards a union of *oeléebalangs*. Three chiefs of Lho' Seumawe were banished in [[1922]].

### Significance

Lho Soekon is the type-figure of the divided *onderafdeeling* — half fertile rice-plain, half lifeless swamp — and of the post-pacification frontier, where the memories of military action and the dangers of religious agitation are still close. Broersma's account of Lho Soekon is one of the strongest passages in *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* on the continuing need for vigilance in regions only recently pacified.

## The killing of the resistance leaders

Twenty-two kilometres of marsh and forest from Lho' Soekon lay the country of the three great Teungkoes. On [[26 January]] [[1905]] the patrol of sergeant [[Vollaers]] was destroyed at [[Meurandéh Paja]], east of Lho' Soekon, sixteen of seventeen men being killed. On [[26 September]] [[1910]] sergeant [[Slooten]], having marched from Lho' Soekon through [[Peutèë]], killed [[Pang Nanggròë]] in the [[Paja Tjitjém]]; the body was brought back and buried behind the *keudé*, where it still lay in [[1943]], "within a covered wooden enclosure, a *kandang*. And immediately beside it, in such an enclosure too, rests the body of Pang Latèh, the energetic fellow-fighter of Pang Nanggròë." On [[22 October]] [[1910]] [[W. J. Mosselman]] marched out from Lho' Soekon on the pursuit that ended three days later with the deaths of [[Tgk. Sjeh di Paja Bakong]], [[Tgk. Mat Saleh]] and [[Tjoet Meutia]].

In January [[1913]] the foragers of [[Tgk. di Mata Ië]] were vigorously driven off by the population at Lho' Soekon; the last followers of [[T. Radja Sabi]] reported themselves there that year, leaving him to wander alone until [[1919]].

## Murders

In [[1917]] a leper at Lho' Soekon murdered the *officier van gezondheid* [[J. Ph. Andriesse|dr. J. Ph. Andriesse]] during examination; the perpetrator was sentenced to death. In June [[1915]] the *officier van gezondheid* [[De Vos]] was lightly wounded at Lho' Soekon during vaccination, the perpetrator being killed by bystanders. On [[11 March]] [[1918]] the Civil Authority, lieutenant [[M. Schouten|Schouten]], was attacked there; he soon recovered and the perpetrator was laid low.

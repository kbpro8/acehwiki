**Lho Soekon** — also *Lho Sukon*, modern *Lhok Sukon* — was a *bivak* and post in the [[Lho Seumawé]] area on the North coast of [[Aceh]], seat of the *civiel-gezaghebber* (civil authority) — Schouten held the post in the years of the false [[T. Radja Sabi]] — and of the division command that directed the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|maréchaussée]]-brigades operating against the bands of [[Pang Nanggroë]], [[Tjoet Meuthia]], and the *adelaarsjong*. The graves of [[Pang Nanggroë]] and [[Pang Lateh]] lie behind its *keudé* in wooden-enclosed *kandangs*, and the *pasanggrahan* of Lho Soekon is the setting of [[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]]'s long interview with the grown [[T. Radja Sabi]].

## Centre of resistance

In the years [[1905]]–[[1910]] the bende of [[Pang Nanggroë]] brought the whole country of [[Keureutoe]], [[Pasé]], and Lho Soekon to *den rand van een algemeenen opstand* — the edge of a general rising. The *kampongs* ran empty, the *sawahs* stayed unworked, hundreds of men went to the mountains; the work of ten years of fighting seemed lost. [[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] writes that *"Pang Agam mèl in Lho Soekon"* — Pang Agam submitted at Lho Soekon — became one of the standard phrases of the older Atjehers when they spoke of the lot of the men of those years; *mèl* or *sjahid*, one or the other, was the only fate the war allowed.

## The murder of Meurandeh Paja, January 1905

On [[26 January]] [[1905]] a patrol under sergeant Vollaers bivouacked at the *meunasa* of Meurandeh Paja, east of Lho Soekon. The *troep* had neglected every security measure; armed Atjehers who claimed to sell fruit and chickens were let inside the *pagger*, and two were even allowed to climb the ladder to where the commander lay reading. At a sudden signal the overval began; sixteen of the seventeen militairen were killed, only one Inlandsch fusilier escaping through the kampong. Long before he could reach a garrison, [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]] — *de latere generaal, toen commandant te Lho Seumawé* — had the news; *in Atjeh gaan zulke berichten zeer snel door het land*. Swart *joog met eene patrouille in den gemeensten marsch* to Meurandeh Paja, found the sixteen corpses *afschuwelijk toegetakeld*, the sergeant's body upstairs in the *meunasa* with his book beside him, and buried them in a mass grave.

## The arrest and execution of T. Tjhi Toenong

The investigation of the Meurandeh Paja overval pointed to [[T. Tjhi Toenong]]. Swart let luitenant Van Vuuren arrest him cautiously when the *Teukoe* came to [[Lho Seumawé]] on [[5 March]] [[1905]] for ordinary business. He was tried, condemned to death, and — at [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s insistence that a worthy fighter should not hang — shot on the strand.

## The brigades of Mosselman and Kretz

In [[July]] [[1910]] luitenant [[Mosselman]] came to Lho Soekon, *en in October was ik al vrij aardig ingeloopen*. From Lho Soekon the brigades of Mosselman, [[Kretz]], and Van Slooten set out after [[Pang Nanggroë]]'s bende and after the *adelaarsjong*. On [[25 September]] [[1910]] sergeant Van Slooten overvalled the bende in the Paja Tjitcem; [[Pang Nanggroë]] was killed, and his body was brought to Lho Soekon for recognition by the bevolking. *Het werd begraven achter de keudé van Lho Soekon, en daar ligt het nu nog binnen eene afgedekte houten omwanding, eene kandang*. Beside it, in another *kandang*, lies the body of [[Pang Lateh]] — *zij zijn in den dood weer bij elkaar, als op menigen ouden krijgstocht*.

## The interview with T. Radja Sabi

*Tegenover mij in de pasanggrahan van Lho Soekon zit een Atjeher van bijna veertigjarigen leeftijd* — so begins Zentgraff's account of his meeting with the grown [[T. Radja Sabi]], who had returned from the forests in [[1919]] and was, by [[1937]], acting *oeléëbalang* of [[Keureutoe]]. They talked in the *pasanggrahan* of Lho Soekon, *en eene sterke emotie beving mij bij de treffendste episoden van zijn verhaal*. *Dan gaan wij naar de keudé, en een eindje daarachter liggen binnen houten wanden de graven van Pang Nanggroë en Pang Lateh*.

## Significance

Lho Soekon is the *bivak*-anchor of the Keureutoe-epos: the place from which the brigades of [[Mosselman]] and [[Kretz]] marched, where the bodies of the dead resisters lie under their *kandang*-enclosures behind the *keudé*, and where Zentgraff sat across from the grown *adelaarsjong* to hear the boy's-war told in the man's voice. The *djaksa*'s news to Schouten — *dat hij in staat was den lang gezochten T. Radja Sabi uit de bergen naar beneden te brengen* — that opened the *valsche-pretendent* affair of [[1913]] was delivered at the civiel-gezaghebber's house at Lho Soekon.

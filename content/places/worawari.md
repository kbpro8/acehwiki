**Worawari** is the Sumatran-Overwal place-name that [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies as the *bedekte taal* original of the [[Bonang|desa Bonang]] in [[Rembang|Rembang]] south of [[Lasem|Lasem]] on Java — the place whose name the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] attaches to the *pangeran* **Bonang** who converts [[Raden Sahid]] at the *pasar* of [[Djatisaripa|Djatisaripa]]. It is the Sumatran ancestral counterpart of the Javanese [[Rembang|Rembang]] — the *Ari-ke-mbang* of the Sumatran Overwal — and the place-world whose name encodes the *wora-wari* flower of the [[Taroema|Taroema]] allegory.

## Identification with the Javanese Bonang

The [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] gives the name **Bonang** to the *pangeran* who converts [[Raden Sahid]] at the *pasar* of [[Djatisaripa|Djatisaripa]]. The name is that of a *desa* **Bonang** in [[Rembang|Rembang]] south of [[Lasem|Lasem]] on Java. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] demurs: "meant was [[Worawari|Worawari]] on [[Sumatra]]". [[Rembang|Rembang]] itself is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the Javanese remembrance of the *Ari-ke-mbang* of the Sumatran Overwal — the land of the [[Kalang|Kalang's]], Javanese people who were not "real" Javanese but [[Djawaka|djawa-ka]] of *lang-ka*, the Sumatran country from which they had been brought as war-booty. The *pangeran* Bonang of the Javanese chronicle is, in this reading, the Javanese rememberer of the Sumatran Worawari.

## Decoding: *mala-wora-wari*

The *wora-wari* flower behind [[Soenan Bonang|Pangeran Bonang]]'s ear — the sign by which [[Raden Sahid]] was to recognise the man he should plunder — is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the *wora-wari* of *mala-wora-wari*, i.e. ***Mala-joe-wa-wor-a*** — "Malajoer-ora" — Malajoer. The same flower is the *tjempaka gonda* of the [[Taroema|Taroema]] allegory — the "fragrant" (*aroem*) flower whose *gonda* ("fragrance") encodes the ancestral land of [[Taroema]]. The *Worawari* of the Sumatran Overwal is, in this reading, the place-world whose name encodes both the *wora-wari* flower and the [[Malajoer|Malajoer]] of the [[Aroe|Aru]] complex.

## The Siamese record

Worawari is also recorded in the Siamese code *Kot Monthiera ban* of c. [[1360]] as one of the four polities — with Udjong-tanah, Malaka, and Malayu — that were tributary to [[Siam|Siam]]. The same record fixes Worawari as a Malay polity of the east-coast Sumatran-Overwal complex — i.e. as part of the [[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]]-[[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] triangle that the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] would close.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Worawari is the principal Sumatran-Overwal place-name that [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies as the *bedekte taal* original of the Javanese [[Bonang|Bonang]] of [[Rembang|Rembang]] — the place-world whose name encodes both the *wora-wari* flower of the [[Taroema|Taroema]] allegory and the [[Malajoer|Malajoer]] of the [[Aroe|Aru]] complex. Its record in the Siamese code *Kot Monthiera ban* also fixes it as a Malay polity of the east-coast Sumatran-Overwal complex tributary to [[Siam|Siam]] in the fourteenth century.

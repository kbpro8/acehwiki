**III Moekims Daroe** — written in Dutch as and appearing also as *III Moekims Ateuh* for a different sub-region and meaning "Three Mukims of Daroe" — was a region of [[Groot-Atjeh]] comprising three [[moekim|mukims]] situated in the eastern portion of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre, of which the [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] was [[Panglima Bintang]] (sympathetic to the Dutch). As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the III Moekims Daroe was a friendly region whose borders were not exceeded by hostile *benden* from the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]]. The III Moekims Ateuh (a different sub-region within the III Moekims complex) was protected by the [[Dutch]] post of [[Kroeng Gloempang]] against incursions from [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]]. The III Moekims Daroe was the object of the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|show-of-force by Laceulle on 29 May 1896]], and contained the *Grot of Daroe* where [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah|Teungkoe Panté Glima]] practiced mystical retreat.

## Description and Location

The III Moekims Daroe lay in the eastern part of [[Groot-Atjeh]] to the south of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] and adjoined the [[V Moekims Montassik]] on the south. The principal kampong was Daroe itself, which contained the a cave where the religious leader [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah|Teungkoe Panté Glima]] practiced *kaloet* — a form of mystical retreat (bertapa) believed to confer invulnerability. The III Moekims Ateuh (a different sub-region within the same complex) included the kampongs of Ateuh, Ateuh-Tjoet, Ateuh-Lam Lo-West, and the great Ateuh area, and was protected by the [[Dutch]] post of [[Kroeng Gloempang]] (on the border with the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]]) against incursions from [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]]. [[Mata Air (Daroe)]] was a settlement where [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah|T. Panté Glima]] stayed with 30 Pedireezen. The *Baleé Mesoebang* was the place where part of the III Moekims Daroe population gathered during operations.

## Historical Background

The III Moekims Daroe had been a partial Dutch sphere of influence throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] [[Panglima Bintang]] had maintained a posture of friendship with the Dutch, and the borders of his mukim were not crossed by hostile *benden* from the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]]. The [[Dutch]] post of [[Kroeng Gloempang]] was established in part to protect the III Moekims Ateuh against incursions from [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]]. The religious leader [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah|Teungkoe Panté Glima]] (also written Pantai Glima) had taken up residence in the *Grot of Daroe*, where he practiced bertapa, mystical retreat and gathered a band of 30 Pedireezen mercenaries. The III Moekims Daroe was crossed by the road from [[Lam-Goet]] to [[Tjot Rang]], and the III Moekims Ateuh contained the kampong complex of Ateuh that was the scene of heavy fighting in [[April]] [[1896]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations

The III Moekims Daroe was the object of the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|show-of-force by Laceulle on 29 May 1896]], in which a Dutch column visited the territory of [[Panglima Bintang]] to consolidate his loyalty. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] the III Moekims Daroe was patrolled by compagnie Linck of 12e Bataljon, in the course of which [[Panglima Bintang]] made his [[Submission of T. Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|submission]] to the Dutch. The population of the III Moekims Daroe was said to have fled to a valley-basin behind Tjot Goee Tjoet under the leadership of Imam Djempit. The III Moekims Daroe was mentioned in the final assessments of [[1896]] as a region generally not unfavourable to the Dutch, and in the [[Government Resolution of 26 April 1896]] as part of the territory under Dutch protection. The III Moekims Ateuh was the theatre of Dutch operations against Ateuh in [[April]] [[1896]] (as part of the [[Kolonel der Infanterie|Kolonel]] [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] relief of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]]), and the mosque was occupied at 7:45 AM on [[8 April]] [[1896]].

## Significance

The III Moekims Daroe was a model of the friendly Achehnese mukim under Dutch protection. The loyalty of [[Panglima Bintang]] and his [[Submission of T. Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|submission of 20 June 1896]] represented the kind of political accommodation that the [[Dutch]] hoped to extend across [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The presence of [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah|Teungkoe Panté Glima]] in the *Grot of Daroe* however demonstrated that even the friendly mukims harboured religious-war leaders whose authority rivalled that of the *oeleebalang*.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

The **III Moekims Daroe** were three *[[Mukim|moekims]]* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] whose *[[Imam|imam]]*, [[T. Rajoet|Toekoe Rajoet]], directed the attacks on Dutch transports moving between [[Tjot Goeë]] and [[Biloel]] that caused the establishment of the intermediate post of [[Lam Koenjit]] on [[4 July]] [[1894]].

## In the outpost system

Hooijer recorded that the road connecting [[Tjot Goeë]] and [[Biloel]] had been very unsafe at first, and that it was decided to erect a very temporary intermediate post at [[Lam Koenjit]] *tegen de aanslagen van Toekoe Rajoet, Imam der III Moekims Daroe* — against the attacks of Toekoe Rajoet, imam of the III Moekims Daroe.

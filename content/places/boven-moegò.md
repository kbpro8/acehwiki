**Boven Moegò** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Keutjhi' Bén]]

Geumpang having become too hot for him, Keutjhi' Bén withdrew to the [[Boven Moegò]] district, where [[J. van Arkel|Van Arkel]] from [[Tanòh Mirah]] twice surprised his band, killing his son and five followers and taking five rifles; almost simultaneously another patrol from Tanòh Mirah under sergeant Gackstätter surprised Keutjhi' Bén himself in Pameu and laid him low with two followers. "And with this the time of "political situation favourable" dawned for Meulaboh as well." A resistance head [[Keutjhi' Bén]] was also laid low in Pameu by a patrol from Meulaboh in [[1911]].

### From [[Geumpang]]

In August [[1910]] the bivouac commander of Geumpang, *onderluitenant* [[A. H. C. Roelofsen]], "at last laid low the long-sought and notorious resistance leader in that district, [[Keudjroeën Leupoe]]" [[H. N. A. Swart]]'s letter to Schmidt of [[26 December]] [[1909]] named the further quarry there: Keudjroeën Leupoe's sons [[T. Adé]] and [[T. Ma'E]], then [[Keutjhi' Bén Bangkèh]] and [[Keutjhi' Linto]]. The great [[Keutjhi' Bén]], for whom Geumpang had become too hot, withdrew to the [[Boven Moegò]] district and was killed in [[Pameu]] in September [[1911]].

### From [[Tanòh Mirah]]

Those submitting were helped with rice and seed-*padi*. From Tanòh Mirah [[J. van Arkel|Van Arkel]] twice surprised the band of [[Keutjhi' Bén]] in the [[Boven Moegò]] district in September [[1911]], and a patrol under sergeant [[Gackstätter]] laid Keutjhi' Bén himself low in [[Pameu]]. In May [[1911]] the bivouacs [[Arongan]], Tanòh Mirah and [[Reusa']] were abolished in consequence of the favourable political situation in [[Wòjla]] and [[Boebon]].

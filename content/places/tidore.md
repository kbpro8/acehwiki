**Tidore** — a sultanate in the [[Molukken|Moluccas]] (Maluku) — is the polity whose [[17th century|mid-17th-century]] submission to the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] supplied the legal-historical fiction by which the [[Netherlands]] claimed sovereignty over the entire [[Nieuw Guinea|western New Guinea]] half of the archipelago. The fiction is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the type-specimen of the Dutch colonial doctrine of *fictieve* — fictitious — sovereignty.

## The 17th-century submission

When, in the [[17th century|mid-17th century]], the sultan of [[Tidore]] submitted to the [[Netherlands|Dutch]], the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] considered that his entire territory had come under Dutch sovereignty and, for convenience, treated as part of that territory "het land der Papoea's en de omliggende eilanden" — the land of the Papuans and the surrounding islands. The fiction was the legal basis on which, in [[1828]], the [[Netherlands]] took formal possession of [[Nieuw Guinea]] as far as the 141st meridian.

## The Nieuw Guinea possession of 1828

In [[1828]] the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] took possession of [[Nieuw Guinea]] — where, until that moment, only a few scattered Dutch settlements had been attempted — as far as the 141st meridian. The act of possession consisted, in practice, of placing flags or arms-boards ("vlaggen of wapenborden") here and there along the coast as a token of Dutch supremacy. According to [[Van Kol]] (s.d.), citing an unidentified source, these tokens were "tamelijk goed gespaard door de inboorlingen" — fairly well spared by the natives — because the natives entertained a superstitious fear of the images of the unknown animals (the Dutch lions) depicted on them.

## Significance for Atjeh

[[Tidore|Tidore]]'s relevance to Atjeh is twofold. First, the [[Tidore|Tidore]] submission is the type-specimen of the kind of broad sovereignty-claim that the [[Netherlands]] also asserted over the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate after the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]]. Second, the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] comparison between [[Nieuw Guinea]] and [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — both territories of "fictief" Dutch authority — is the analytical frame within which the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, to be understood.

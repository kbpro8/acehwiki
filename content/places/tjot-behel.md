**Tjot Bēhēl** was a kampong between [[Lamsajoen|Lamsajoeng]] and [[Kajoe-Lē]] in the [[XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], part of the block of hostile kampongs — with Lambaroe, Lamsajoeng and Lamtengah — in which the enemy nested after Pel's march of February [[1876]] and from which he cut the roads between the Dutch posts. Its narrows with Lamsajoeng were the place of the [[Ambush of the Buijs detachment (4 March 1876)|ambush of 4 March 1876]].

## The hostile kampong

After the return of Pel's troops on [[7 February]] [[1876]], the enemy held Tjot Bēhēl with the kampongs Lambaroe, Lamsajoeng and Lamkoenjit as his block of cover for attacks on the road between Kajoe-Lē, [[Atoeh|Atoë]] and [[Mesigit Pagar-Ajer]]. It was in the narrow passage between Lamsajoeng and Tjot Bēhēl that 74 bayonets and a section of artillery escorting sick men were attacked in front and on both flanks on [[4 March]]; it was through Tjot Bēhēl that Major [[Majoor Diepenheim|Diepenheim]]'s patrol march on 8 March — from Lamsajoeng through Tjot Bēhēl to Biloel and over Atoë back — finally moved unhindered after the [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|storming of Lambaroe]]; and the kampong itself was put in ashes, with those of Lamrong, by Colonel [[K. van der Heyden]]'s 3rd column on [[14 May]] during the [[Biloel Zuid operation (13-15 May 1876)|Biloel Zuid operation]].

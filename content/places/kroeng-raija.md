**Kroeng Raija** was a locality in the mountains east of the valley of [[Lamtoba]] — not to be confused with the coastal river and post of [[Kroëng Raja]] westwards — which was the objective of the great Dutch [[Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raija (12 August 1879)|reconnaissance of 12 August 1879]] from the bivak at Lamtoba, as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## Situation in the reconnaissance of 1879

The reconnaissance under Majoor [[Majoor Ducroo|Ducroo]] — three companies of infantry, half the detachment of Mineurs, an ambulance of fifteen *tandoes*, the chief of staff and an *opnemer* — marched twelve *palen* through the mountains; the commander himself, with the chief of staff, the surveyor and a covering detachment, pushed on to the near neighbourhood of Kroeng Raija, a circuit of sixteen *palen*. The country proved to be wilderness never before entered by a European: apart from some *paseijs* — the little mountain hamlets — a few refugees of the [[XXVI Moekims]], a white flag hung out from a high tree, and an abundance of fresh elephant tracks in the soft soil, nothing of note was found. Unquestionably, the authors observed, great herds of the elephants kept in this wild region. The column, which had started without artillery and could therefore march fast, was back in the bivak only at half past four in the afternoon — to find awaiting it the [[March order for the Selimoen expedition (11 August 1879)|order for the march against Selimoen]], in view of which the heavy day's work would otherwise never have been undertaken.

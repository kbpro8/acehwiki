**Mekka** is the holiest city in [[Islam]], located in the [[Hejaz]] region of present‑day [[Saudi Arabia]]. It is repeatedly invoked in Acehnese religious literature and practice as the ultimate destination of the [[hajj]] and a focal point of sermons, poetry, and pilgrimage traditions. The city is mentioned on page [[83]] of a 16th‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* in the context of the *[[Sūra al‑Fīl]]* and the expedition of [[Abraha]], and again on page [[91]] describing a legendary route between Mekka and [[Medina]].

## Religious significance for Aceh

Acehnese scholars have long cited verses about Mekka in sermons and poetry, linking local piety to the broader Islamic world. The practice of undertaking the [[haji]] (the Acehnese term for the pilgrimage) to Mekka has been recorded since the early spread of Islam in the archipelago, establishing strong religious ties between Aceh and the Arabian heartland. Contemporary Acehnese religious discourse continues to invoke Mekka as the ultimate destination of the faithful, reinforcing a sense of spiritual orientation toward the holy city.

## Historical references in Acehnese sources

- The 16th‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* (page [[83]]) includes a passage about the *[[Sūra al‑Fīl]]* and the pre‑Islamic expedition of [[Abraha]] that passed near Mekka, noting “dal in de nabijheid van Mekka, waar de stammen der Ha‑wāzin tegen Muhammad vochten.”
- The same translation (page [[91]]) later describes a legendary route between Mekka and [[Medina]], underscoring the city’s centrality in Islamic geography as understood by Acehnese scholars.

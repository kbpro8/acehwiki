**Koeta Tjané** is the subdistrict headquarters-town of the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] in the Alas valley of Central Atjèh, the place where the Dutch military and civil administration of the Alas valley resided, the headquarters to which General [[Major-General Overakker|Overakker]] moved on [[14 March]] [[1942]] after Kaban Djahé, and the centre from which the defense of the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]] — the focus of the final Dutch resistance in Sumatra — was organised until its fall on [[27 March]] [[1942]]. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats Koeta Tjané as one of the five subdistricts in which the military commander was replaced by civil administration in [[1938]], and as the key road junction on the *Alasweg* between [[Kaban Djahé|Kaban Djahé]] in the East Coast residency and [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] in the [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Kota Tjane

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Wegenplan Sumatra|Sumatra road plan]] lists the Alasweg as project no. 13 in its numbered list of fifteen Sumatra road projects: "Weg [[Kaban Djahe|Kaban Djahe]]–Kota Tjane (Alasweg). Length 137 K.M.; cost f 1,173,000. For the connection of the [[Alaslanden|Alas lands]] with the Karo-hoogvlakte and the wegennet of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]]. In the main completed." Kota Tjane thus appears as the southern terminus of one of the two principal road projects of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade in the Atjehnese interior.

The completion of the Alasweg from [[Kaban Djahe]] to Kota Tjane opened the [[Alaslanden|Alas lands]] to direct road communication with the Karo highlands and the road network of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] — that is, with the southern rather than the northern coast of Atjeh. Kota Tjane, as the principal town of the Alas lands, thereby became the administrative and economic gateway to the Alas valley region.

## The administrative reform of 1938

Koeta Tjané was one of the subdistricts in which, in [[1938]] and subsequent years, "this replacement took place … in the subdistricts Lam Meukó, Koeta Tjané, [[Tapa'toean|Tapa'toean]], [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] and [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]], so that only in the subdistrict of Zuidelijke Atjèhse Landschappen, the scene of the [[Bakóngan uprising|Bakóngan uprising]], the military commander remained entrusted with civil administration." The reform was decided under Resident [[Jongejans|Jongejans]] "in consultation with the relevant military authorities," on the principle that "a union of military and civil authority in one hand was no longer deemed necessary and could make way for normal administration by administrative officials." The subdistricts [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] (with headquarters Koeta Tjané) and [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]] (with headquarters [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]]) were merged into a single subdistrict in [[1941]], in connection with the completion of the road connection Koeta Tjané-Blang Kédjérèn at the end of [[1939]].

## The defense of March 1942

The Alas valley was, together with the [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]] and [[Takéngón|Takéngón]], one of the three great defence centres of the Atjèh highlands. "The main defense centers would be formed here by the subdistricts of [[Takéngón|Takéngón]] and Gajó Loeós and Alaslanden. With a view to a prolonged defense considerable supplies of military equipment, foodstuffs, medicines, etc. had been gathered here." Koeta Tjané was only "accessible by one motor road: … Koeta Tjané in the Alas valley from [[Kaban Djahé|Kaban Djahé]] in the East Coast residency of Sumatra along the 130 km. long *Alasweg*." On [[14 March]] [[1942]] General Overakker "had moved his headquarters from [[Kaban Djahé|Kaban Djahé]] to the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]] and first established it at Koeta Tjané, later at the former Japanese undertaking 'Boekit Toesam'." The Germans interned at Lawé Sigalagala, "approximately 20 km. south of Koeta Tjané," had been evacuated under the threat of the rapid Japanese advance via Sibolga, so that "no danger needed to be feared from this side."

## The fall of Koeta Tjané

On [[25 March]] [[1942]] a Japanese breakthrough took place "from the valley of the Lawé Rénoen, a left-side tributary of the Lawé Alas, to the road [[Kaban Djahé|Kaban Djahé]]-Koeta Tjané, at a distance of 80 km. before Koeta Tjané." The following morning, "[a]t six o'clock, the last troops left Koeta Tjané, after the gasoline depot 2 km. north of Koeta Tjané had been blown up. The market warehouse and the military storage depots, in which large military stocks of foodstuffs, medical supplies, clothing, etc. had to be left behind, were opened to the population by the retreating troops." "Not until [[27 March]], in the afternoon at half past one, did the Japanese troops reach the subdivision headquarters. The subdivision head was interned in his home." The newly appointed division head, Mr. Th. O. B. Gunther, "after arriving in Koeta Tjané from Sibolga on [[13 March]] [[1942]], had traveled on to [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] on [[16 March]]." The self-governing authority of Bambél, Radja Mariboen, was appointed as *gun-chó* for the entire subdistrict.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Kota Tjane** — modern *Ketane* — is the chief place (*hoofdplaats*) of the *onderafdeeling* [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] in the *afdeeling* [[Gajo- en Alaslanden|Gajo- en Alaslanden]]. Three brigades of the garrison of [[Blang Kedjerën|Blang Kedjerën]] lay at Kota Tjane. From [[Blang Kedjerën|Blang Kedjerën]] to Kota Tjane runs a horse-track of 80 km; thereafter an auto-road of 28 km continues to Kota Tjane itself. The full distance from [[Takengon|Takengon]] to Kota Tjane by this route is 238 km — a sample of Sumatran distances. From Kota Tjane one is still separated from [[Medan|Medan]] by a great road of 218 km.

**Edi** — with its capital Edi besar — was one of the small coastal states on the east coast of [[Atjeh]], under Dutch influence since [[1873]]. Its chief, the [[Radja of Edi besar|Radja of Edi]], was one of the few princes of the coast states who kept openly to the Dutch side in the years of the [[Aceh War]].

## The Habib's landing, March 1876

Edi's place in the record of [[1876]] is the arrival of [[Habib Abdoerrachman el Zahir|Habib Abdoer Rachman ben Zahir]]. Disguised as a sailor on a schooner from [[Penang]], the Habib set foot on the Atjehnese shore at or near Edi about mid-March; and it was the Radja of Edi besar who — being sided with the Dutch — gave to the Dutch-appointed civil official there the momentous news that *"Habib had set foot on shore in the little coast state near him"*, whereby government circles at [[Kota Radja]] and the British agency at Penang both learnt where the escaped leader had gone. From Edi or its neighbourhood the Habib travelled west along the coast states, collecting money, fighters and influence for the renewal of the war on a grand scheme.

The place lies on the north-east coast of Sumatra, east of the great sultanate's own lands — a passage landing well-known to the coasting schooners of Penang.

**Edi** was a coast-state on the east coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], the first of the Onderhoorigheden to submit to the Dutch, when on [[7 May]] [[7 May]] [[1873]] it hoisted the Dutch flag — even before the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] of [[November 1873|November 1873]]. Two years later the whole east coast, with the exception of [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]] and [[Langsar|Langsar]], was brought under Dutch administration, "chiefly through the efforts of the commandant of the Timor, the lieutenant-at-sea 1st class [[C. H. Bogaert|C. H. Bogaert]], although not without struggle here and there." In [[June 1878|June 1878]] an attack was made on Edi from [[Gedong|Gedong]], which led to Van der Heyden's expedition against Gedong.

## The Submission, 7 May 1873

Already long before the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]], on [[7 May]] [[7 May]] [[1873]], Edi on the east coast had hoisted the Dutch flag. Two years later the whole east coast, with the exception of Simpang Olim and Langsar, was brought under Dutch administration, "chiefly through the efforts of the commandant of the Timor, the lieutenant-at-sea 1st class C. H. Bogaert, although here and there not without struggle. Thus on [[21 September]] [[21 September]] [[Edi-Tjoet|Edi-Tjoet]] was punished, on [[22 October]] [[22 October]] Simpang-Olim, on [[4 November]] [[4 November]] the kampong Gloempang on the Djolok-rivier. Bogaert was on his ship master of the coast, he had on shore and aboard continual contact with the chiefs, he did not only blockade, he regulated and governed on his own initiative; he was everywhere honoured and feared, and his name still lives on among the Atjehers."

## The Attack from Gedong, June 1878

In [[June 1878|June 1878]] Van der Heyden's presence was again necessary on the [[Noord- en Oostkust|Oostkust]], where by hostile Atjehers, principally from the landscape [[Gedong|Gedong]], an attack had been made on the friendly Edi. The situation became especially dangerous when on [[15 May]] [[15 May]] [1878] the commandant of Edi, with an officer and 52 rank-and-file, besides a population patrol of 100 men, had marched out. "They came just past [[Pedawa Tjoet|Pedawa Tjoet]], in a very overgrown terrain where one had not the least view, into contact with the enemy; and after a brief skirmish, in which five of our men were wounded, a panic arose among the auxiliary bands, which also communicated itself to the military detachment. The officers did not succeed in bringing the soldiers to a halt, and in the greatest confusion the troop returned to Edi. Both officers and 3 European soldiers, struck by sunstroke, succumbed shortly afterwards."

As this outcome made the danger for Edi still greater, and several chiefs now openly took the side against the Dutch, an expedition against [[Gedong|Gedong]] was sent by the commander-in-chief. On [[3 June]] [[3 June]] the troops disembarked at the Paseirivier. After a powerful storming on [[19 June]] [[19 June]] Blang Pria was taken. On [[21 June]] [[21 June]] the radja of Gedong offered his submission; his son, who succeeded him, submitted wholly to the Dutch.

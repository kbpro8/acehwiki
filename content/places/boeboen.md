**Boeboen** — also **Bubon**, in [[Anderson|Anderson]]'s 1825 record ***Kwala Bubon*** or ***Sungey Langkat*** — is the kampong at the river-mouth of [[Tandjoengpoera]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] that [[Anderson|Anderson]]'s *Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra* ([[1825]]) records as the temporary refuge of "a large colony of Malays, however, from Lancavy, who fled when the Siamese attacked that place", and which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies with the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi|padoeka Boeboennja]] of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] — the king whose *hoeloebalang* [[Awi Ditjoe]] captured the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]].

## Anderson's 1825 record

In his *Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra* ([[1825]]), [[Anderson|Anderson]] records (p. 239): "From [[Tamijang|Timian]] to Kwala Bubon, the land forms a deep bay, not easily perceived from a distance, in consequence of two islands that front it, and which are not easily distinguished from the mainland, unless when close in shore. The names of the islands are Pulo Tampasalee and Pulo Sampa-tuah (Pulo Tampilis and Pulo Kampei). Between these islands, the Malays informed us there was a safe channel that leads into a river Kayu Lapan". This is, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, "a clear description of the bay of Aroe with the *teroesan* between Tampilis and Kampei, and the kampong Boeboen at the river-mouth of [[Tandjoengpoera]]".

## Identification with Sjahroe'n Noewi's *padoeka Boeboennja*

The [[Sadjarah Malajoe|Sadjarah Malajoe's]] [[Awi Ditjoe]] episode records that the king of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] who sent Awi Ditjoe to capture the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]] was "*padoeka Boeboennja*" — "his foot-soled of Boeboen". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies this *Boeboen* with Anderson's kampong Boeboen at the river-mouth of [[Tandjoengpoera]] — i.e. with the east-coast polity whose identification with [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] ties the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]]'s chronicle geography to the [[Aroe]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere.

## The Lancavy colony and Siamese pressure

Anderson's record that the kampong Boeboen was a colony of Malays from [[Langkawi|Lancavy]] who had fled the Siamese attack on that place fixes Boeboen as a recipient of Malay refugees from the Siamese pressure of the early nineteenth century — the same Siamese pressure that, in the fourteenth century, had made the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi|padoeka Boeboennja]] of the chronicle tributary to [[Siam|Siam]]. The continuity of Siamese pressure on the east-coast polities — from the [[Sadjarah Malajoe|Sadjarah Malajoe's]] record to [[Anderson|Anderson]]'s 1825 mission — is the political frame of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Boeboen is the principal east-coast topographical anchor of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s identification of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] with [[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]] — the *kampong Boeboen* of [[Anderson|Anderson]]'s 1825 record that ties the [[Sadjarah Malajoe|Sadjarah Malajoe's]] *padoeka Boeboennja* to the river-mouth of [[Tandjoengpoera]]. Its record as a recipient of [[Langkawi|Langkawi]] refugees also fixes it as a place of Malay refuge from Siamese pressure in both the fourteenth and the nineteenth centuries.

**Marasa** was a district and kampong string in the [[VI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], immediately south of the Dutch capital, whose population submitted at the very beginning of the hostilities after the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1874)|Second Aceh Expedition]] of [[1874]], and alone of the whole pacified zone of Groot-Atjeh had remained faithful under the Dutch in [[1874]]–[[1876]].

## The position of 1874–1876

The population of Marasa had, as [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] records in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]), come over at the beginning of the hostilities — and also alone of all the inhabitants of the subjected district — had much to suffer from the continual robbing and murdering raids of her everywhere-fear-spreading hostile countrymen. The village and its people lay between the Dutch works of Lampasei and Blang-Oë, whose first purpose was to protect the faithful population of Marasa against the enemy. In those days a road lay from [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]] through Marasa into the IV Moekims, which — after the submission of the VI and IV Moekims — became busier with trade, and was watched by the Dutch post at [[Pakan Badak]], where every arriving person had to appear with a pass.

## The friendly heads

Dutch influence had in Marasa its nearly only local anchor: Toekoe Nja Hamza, the son of the known-of-both-expeditions chief Toekoe Neg Radja Moeda Setja; and Toekoe Nja Mohamad, the son-in-law of Toekoe Neg and chief of a couple of kampongs in Marasa, with the more important [[Toekoe Lampasei]], were, as reproduced in the official records, the only Atjehnese hostile-to-the-enemy chiefs on whose active help the Dutch could count in these years — though that help generally amounted to one or two guides knowing the way through the XXV Moekims for Dutch columns.

The posts of the inner ring round the establishment (Lampasei, Blang-Oë) were largely kept for the protection of this never-unfaithful district, which lay within calling distance of the enemy bands of kampong [[Pinang (kampong)|Pinang]] and the roads over which they moved.

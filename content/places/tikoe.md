**Tikoe** — modern **Tiku**, the historic pepper port on the west coast of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] south of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — was one of the chief pepper ports of the Sumatran west coast in the early-modern period and a port of call of the Dutch East India trade. Tikoe was visited by [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]] in [[1600]] without result, by [[Jan Grenier|Jan Grenier]] on [[19 February]] [[1602]] without result — because trade with foreigners in the west-coast ports was forbidden except at Atjeh — and by the Zeeland ships on their way to [[Bantam|Bantam]] after the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|August 1601 mission]]. Tikoe is one of the principal west-coast pepper ports named in Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The 1600 Van Caerden visit

[[Paulus van Caerden|Van Caerden]], sent by his admiral [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]] to the west coast of Sumatra, "bezocht daar zonder resultaat Priaman, Tikoe en Pasaman, waar overal slechts weinig peper in voorraad was". The visit was the first Dutch commercial call at Tikoe and showed that the west-coast pepper ports, like Atjeh itself, were subject to seasonal fluctuations in supply. Van Caerden's failure at [[Priaman|Priaman]], Tikoe and [[Pasaman|Pasaman]] was the proximate cause of his decision to try Atjeh for cargo.

## The 1602 Grenier visit and the Westkust closure

[[Jan Grenier|Jan Grenier]] arrived off Tikoe on [[19 February]] [[1602]] on his way from Atjeh to Bantam, "waar hij geen lading opdeed omdat toen de handel met vreemdelingen in de kustplaatsen verboden was, uitgezonderd in Atjeh". The closure was the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s policy: he sought to centralise the European pepper trade at Atjeh, where he could control it through the sabander and the shore batteries. The policy was largely effective in the early Dutch-Indies period: the Dutch and English were forced to call at Atjeh for their pepper, and the west-coast ports could be visited only when the sultan's enforcement lapsed.

## Significance

Tikoe is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal west-coast pepper port of Sumatra south of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] (alongside [[Priaman|Priaman]] and [[Pasaman|Pasaman]]) and a port of call of the Dutch East India trade. The port's seasonal fluctuations in supply, the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s policy of centralising the European pepper trade at Atjeh, and the formal closure of the west-coast ports to foreigners — which [[Jan Grenier|Grenier]] encountered in [[1602]] — together frame the early-modern commercial geography of the Sumatran pepper trade. Tikoe's role in the early Dutch-Indies trade would grow in the seventeenth century as the Dutch shifted their pepper sourcing from Atjeh to the west coast and to [[Bantam|Bantam]] and the Sunda islands.

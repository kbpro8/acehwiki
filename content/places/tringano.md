**Tringano** (modern *Terengganu*; British spelling *Trengganu*) was a Malay state on the east coast of the [[Malay Peninsula]]. It is mentioned in Van der Kemp's article only as the place to which [[Captain Hobbs]] returned after being wounded, and where he died five or six days later, in the events narrated in the [[Sultan of Acheh's letter to Warren Hastings]] of [[7 November]] [[1786]].

## Captain Hobbs and the Sultan's letter

The letter of [[Sultan Ziemul Abudeen|Sultan Munson]] (Alauddin Muhammad Shah, son of Sultan Ziemul Abudeen) to Governor-General [[Warren Hastings]] of [[Bengal]], printed by Van der Kemp as Appendix I, opens the long preamble to the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|Acheh treaty of 1819]]. The sultan declared himself desirous of lasting friendship with the English and recalled that he had "wrote an answer by captain Hobbs and desirous of lasting friendship". The narrative continued: "But fate ordered it that when he had sailed two days from Tringano a classy from Java wounded him while he was asleep". The wounded Hobbs "then returned to Tringano and died after 5 or 6 days", delaying the despatch of the sultan's letters. The "classy" — explained in Van der Kemp's note as a sailor, *matroos*, with reference to Hobson-Jobson — was presumably a Javanese member of the vessel's crew.

The same collection contains, Van der Kemp notes in a footnote, a letter from the Raja of Trengganu to Governor-General [[John Macpherson]] of [[Calcutta]], dated [[24 February]] [[1787]], in which he invoked Macpherson's friendship against the Dutch.

## Place in the narrative

Tringano itself is not the subject of any sustained discussion in Van der Kemp's article; it appears only as the scene of Hobbs's death. The mention serves chiefly to date and locate the early, frustrated British approaches to [[Acheh]] under [[Warren Hastings]] — approaches which, as the sultan's brusque dismissal of the gifts sent him showed, were received by the Achehnese court with characteristic independence. The wider setting is the struggle of European powers, in the last quarter of the eighteenth century, to secure a footing on the northern approach to the [[Straits of Malacca]] — a struggle which culminated, after Captain [[Captain Francis Light|Francis Light]]'s settlement of [[Penang]] in [[1786]], in the [[Acheh succession dispute (1802-1819)]] and the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles–Coombs commission of 1819]].

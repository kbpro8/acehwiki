The **Kroeng Raya** (Krueng Raya) is the river on the north coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] whose mouth formed the eastern end of the great afsluitingslinie that [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] planned between the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast and the mouth of the Kroeng Raya on the north coast, with the aim of isolating Atjeh from the sea. After Pel's sudden death at [[Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]], the eastern end of the line was only imperfectly closed by a sea-expedition to the Kroeng Raya mouth under [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]], and Pel's full plan was never executed.

## The eastern end of the afsluitingslinie

Pel's plan, as he explained it at his [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Buitenzorg planning session]] in July [[1875]] to [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]] and the commandant van het leger, called for the extension of the Dutch linie from the mouth of the Kroeng Raba on the west coast to the mouth of the Kroeng Raya on the north coast. With the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] mouth closed at [[Kwala Gigieng]] and the [[Kota Pohama|island post of Kota Pohama]] in the lagune (both established [[26 February]] [[1876]] by [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|Engel]]), the remaining seaward gap was the Kroeng Raya mouth, which would have completed the isolation of Groot-Atjeh. The government had approved the plan in the second half of [[1875]]. Borel stresses that Pel had not communicated the plan even to his *chef van den staf*; nothing about it was found in his archive after his death.

## The sea-expedition

After Pel's death the command fell ad interim to [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] on [[10 March]] [[1876]]. Wiggers was "geen voorstander van het aanvallende stelsel" — no supporter of the offensive system — and wished neither to extend the linie further nor to adopt anything other than a *strijkt afwachtende houding*. The Kroeng Raya mouth was therefore never occupied as Pel had intended. A sea-expedition to the mouth of the Kroeng Raya on the north coast, under the aanvoering of [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]] during the administration of [[Generaal Diemont|generaal Diemont]] (around [[1876]]–[[1877]]), saw Dutch troepen debark and "dapper gestreden" — fight bravely. But the tocht led to "geene bevredigende uitkomst" nor to the earlier beoogde aansluiting with the linie Tongah–Kwala Gigieng. Pel's plan was therefore not fully executed. The stelling [[Kroeng Raba|K. Raba]]–[[Kwala Gigieng]] has remained "nagenoeg onveranderd" ever since, Borel writes in [[1878]].

**Kalé** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Pang Bintang]]

In [[1911]] Pang Bintang was responsible for one of the setbacks of the year in Pidië. A brigade of infantry sent from its bivouac in *gampong* [[Biheuë]] — "squarely against the regulations" — seven soldiers, half its strength, to nearby [[Kalé]] to forage. The unfindable Pang Bintang stood in relation to the *[[keutjhi']]* (village head) of Kalé, who likewise enjoyed the confidence of the *Kompeuni* ("the Company (the Dutch power)"). The *keutjhi'* was thus able to lure four of the seven soldiers to a distant part of the *gampong*. While they were there, haggling over rice with the village head's wife, they were suddenly attacked and wounded by the *keutjhi'* and five members of Pang Bintang's band. But the wounded soldiers hit back hard, killed the *keutjhi'* and one band member, and drove off the other four. The *keutjhi'*'s revolver even remained in their hands, though a carbine disappeared. A patrol went out at once, recovered the carbine and overtook the four fugitives, all of whom proved severely wounded. The object of the attack, it appeared, had been to obtain firearms: "the bands were becoming alarmingly short of firearms."

### From [[The resistance in Atjeh 1908–1912]]

[[1911]] brought a steadily more favourable situation, though it began with a reverse: a brigade of infantry sent from its bivouac in *gampong* [[Biheuë]] seven soldiers — half its strength, and squarely against regulations — to nearby [[Kalé]] to forage. The unfindable [[Pang Bintang]] stood in relation to the *[[keutjhi']]* of Kalé, who likewise enjoyed the Kompeuni's confidence, and who lured four of the seven to a distant part of the *gampong*. While they were there haggling over rice with the village head's wife, they were suddenly attacked and wounded by the *keutjhi'* and five members of Pang Bintang's band. But the wounded soldiers struck back hard, killed the *keutjhi'* and a band member and drove off the other four; the *keutjhi'*'s revolver even remained in their hands, though a carbine disappeared. A patrol went out at once, recovered the carbine and overtook the four fugitives, all severely wounded. The object had been to obtain firearms: "the bands were becoming alarmingly short of firearms."

### From [[keutjhi']]

The *keutjhi'* could be the Dutch administration's instrument or its betrayer. In [[1911]] the *keutjhi'* of [[Kalé]] in [[Pidië]], who enjoyed the confidence of the *[[Kompeuni]]*, lured four soldiers of a foraging party to a distant part of the *gampong* where they were attacked by five men of [[Pang Bintang]]'s band; the wounded soldiers killed the *keutjhi'* and a band member. In [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]]'s closing anecdote of [[1926]], an old *keutjhi'* related how his *toean* had forgiven and forgotten a debt of fifty guilders in embezzled tax money: "his master was a truly generous master!"

**Kutaradja** (now known as **Banda Aceh**) is the capital and largest city of **[[Aceh]]**, located on the northern tip of **[[Sumatra]]**. It served as the seat of the Acehnese sultanate and was the primary objective of Dutch military operations during the **[[Aceh War]]** ([[1873]]–[[1904]]).

## Strategic Importance

Kutaradja's location on Aceh's north coast made it a natural center of trade and political power. The city was the seat of the Sultan of Aceh and the administrative center of the sultanate. Its capture was seen as essential to Dutch ambitions in the region.

## The First Expedition (1873)

The first Dutch expedition under **General Köhler** landed near Kutaradja in **[[March]] [[1873]]**. The Dutch forces captured the Great Mosque but were forced to retreat after Köhler's death. This defeat marked the beginning of a prolonged conflict.

## The Second Expedition and Capture (1873–1874)

The second, larger expedition under **General Van Swieten** arrived in **[[November]] [[1873]]**. Dutch forces captured the *kraton* (royal palace) and the sultan fled into the interior. The sultanate was declared directly governed territory, and Kutaradja became the administrative capital of Dutch Aceh.

## The Geconcentreerde Linie Period (1884–1896)

From [[1884]], Kutaradja was the center of a defensive perimeter known as the **[[Geconcentreerde Linie]]**, a fortified ring of posts connected by tramways. Dutch forces were largely confined to this perimeter, while Acehnese resistance continued in the surrounding areas.

## Later Development

Under the administration of **[[J. B. van Heutsz]]** and his successors, Kutaradja was rebuilt and developed into a modern colonial center. The construction of the **[[Sabang]]** harbor on nearby Pulau Weh and the development of infrastructure helped integrate Aceh into the colonial economy.

**Anagaloeng** — modern Acehnese *Aneu' Galong* — was a Dutch temporary military post established in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[1890s]] — a post that, together with [[Senelop]] on its southern flank, formed the eastern anchor of the Dutch outer perimeter in the late *concentratie* period. The post was besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]] and relieved on [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1896]], after a siege of approximately six weeks.

## The strategic position

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that Anagaloeng was established in a strategically central position — at the junction of the [[XXII Moekims]] with the [[XXV Moekims]] and the [[XXVI Moekims]] — and that it commanded the principal lines of communication between the *geconcentreerde linie* and the upper valley of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]]. The album's narrative of the [[1896]] crisis places Anagaloeng as the post from which the *Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] was sent out — the patrol that produced the first officer fatality of the spring crisis, Lieutenant [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]].

## The terrain

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Anagaloeng was *hooger gelegen* (situated higher) than the surrounding *sawahs* (rice-fields), and that from its walls one could look out *langs dien langen dijk, die recht noordelijk door rijstvelden voortloopt tot de rivier, naar het Zuid-Westen, waar op een paar honderd meters afstand de vijand nu zit in de benting Bak Tjerlak, naar het Zuid-Oosten, waar de brug ligt, die door een wachthuis wordt beschermd, naar het Oosten over de landtong, die door de rivier wordt omspoeld, en waarachter heel in verte de elegante kegel van den Gondberg verrijst*. The view from Anagaloeng thus took in the river, the bridge, the enemy's *benting* at [[Bak Tjerlak]], and the elegant cone of the [[Gondberg]] in the distance. The *hooge en steile bergwand* (high and steep mountain wall) to the south of Anagaloeng — about an hour's march away — was the principal topographical feature that distinguished the sound of the firing of the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] from that of the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]].

## The siege of March-April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]], the post at Anagaloeng — together with the post at [[Senelop]] — was besieged by the forces of the defected warlord. The album's narrative of the siege records the desperate situation of the garrison — *moge men een oog kunnen sluiten gedurende die nachten, na dagen vol spanning en werkzaamheid, waarin misschien geen enkel bericht tot hen kwam en ieder oogenblik een aanval, een strijd op leven en dood te wachten stond?* — *may one close an eye during those nights, after days full of tension and activity, in which perhaps no message reached them and every moment an attack, a struggle for life or death was to be expected?* The besieged garrison could only listen for the sound of the relief expeditions in the distance, and judge from the sound whether the relieving force was a strong one.

## The relief of 18 April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Anagaloeng was relieved on [[18 April]] [[1896]] — *Eindelijk op den 18den April hoorden zij de dreun van ons geschut, het geratel van onze salvo's* — *Finally on the 18th of April they heard the thunder of our artillery, the rattle of our salvos*. The relief was accomplished by the column that had previously relieved [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]], and it was followed by an *uitbundige vreugde* (extravagant joy) at the post — *met welk een uitbundige vreugde werden zij begroet, met welk een wellust de lastgeving uitgevoerd, alles bijeen te pakken, wat kon worden medegenomen en den brand te steken in de lage barakken, waaronder zij zooveel angst en zorg geleden hadden*.

## The connection with Teungku di Tiro

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Anagaloeng was, in the period before [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]], one of the principal sites of the conflict between the Dutch and the religious-party *Moslemin*. The album's biographical essay on [[Teungku di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] records that the Teungku *legde bij Anagaloeng een linie van verdedigingswerken aan, die uitmuntten door de wijze, waarop hij van het terrein gebruik had weten te maken* — *laid out at Anagaloeng a line of defensive works that excelled in the way he had managed to make use of the terrain*. The reference places Anagaloeng as one of the principal sites of the Teungku's defensive works — and as one of the principal sites of the conflict between the Dutch and the *Moslemin* in the late [[1870s]] and early [[1880s]].

## Significance

Anagaloeng's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the eastern anchor of the Dutch outer perimeter in the late *concentratie* period, and as the post whose siege defined the spring [[1896]] crisis. Its relief on [[18 April]] [[1896]] — after a siege of approximately six weeks — was the last of the three relief operations of April [[1896]], and it was the moment at which the Dutch began to recover the initiative from [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s defected forces. The post's strategic position — at the junction of the three *sagis* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — and its earlier history as a site of [[Teungku di Tiro]]'s defensive works place it as one of the principal operational centres of the late *concentratie* period.

The **XXV Moekims** (Vijfentwintig Moekims, "Twenty-Five Mukims") — in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s spelling also *Sagi XXV* or *de Sagi's XXV* — was the *sagi* (confederation) of twenty-five *mukim* (parish) units lying south of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] between the [[III Moekims|III Moekims]] and the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], entirely conquered by [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s first offensive sweep of early February [[1876]]. Borel notes that "de geheele Sagie der XXV Moekims bevond zich in onze macht" — the entire Sagie of the XXV Moekims was in our power — after the operation. In Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, the XXV Moekims is also the third chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest — the Sagi whose September [[1879]] reconnaissance fixed the *geheel Groot-Atjeh* of the dagorder's declaration, and the territory of the post-[[1884]] resistance's fragmentation under the *panglima sagi XXV* Tjoet Abas.

## The conquest

The conquest of the XXV Moekims was the closing sweep of the first part of Pel's [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|winter offensive of 1875–1876]]. From Toeram, which bordered the gebied of [[Toekoe Bait]] of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the Dutch had made an *inval* (inroad) into the [[III Moekims|III Moekims]] on [[3 February]] [[1876]], driving the enemy out of [[Atoeh]] after a *heeten strijd*. The next day, [[4 February]] [[1876]], the colonne continued *noordoostelijk*: Lamrong and [[Lamsajoen]] were conquered, along with Kajoe Leh, [[Lambaroe]] and [[Pager Ajer|Pager Ajer Mesigit]], and via Tandjoeng and Bayoer communication was opened with the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]]. On [[7 February]] [[1876]] the troepen returned to [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] after a 10-day *tocht*. Total losses were five dead and 59 wounded — "betrekkelijk gering" — relatively slight. The first part of Pel's plan was now executed; the entire Sagie of the XXV Moekims was in Dutch hands.

## The demarcatie-linie

With the XXV Moekims in Dutch hands, the *demarcatie-linie* ran from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast to Atoeh, then northward to Pager Ajer on the left bank of the Atjeh-rivier. The line incorporated the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] — conquered in the same January [[1876]] offensive — into a single defensive perimeter whose southern boundary was the new XXV Moekims line. The conquest was, Borel notes, proof that the Atjehneezen could not withstand a well-planned combined colonne attack — but the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] of [[13 February]] [[1876]] showed that they could still punish careless Dutch columns on the marshy *sawah* (irrigated rice fields). After Pel's death at [[Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]], [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] adopted the *afwachtende houding* and the demarcatie-linie became, in effect, the permanent eastern limit of the Dutch *stelling* — "nagenoeg onveranderd" through Borel's writing in [[1878]].

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, the XXV Moekims — *Sagi XXV*, *de Sagi's XXV*, the Sagi of twenty-five mukims — is the third chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest — the Sagi whose reconnaissance fixed the *geheel Groot-Atjeh* of the dagorder's declaration. The [[Sagi]] was the constitutional unit of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — a territorial league of *mukims* (parish-districts) headed by a *panglima sagi* and collectively constituting the ancient core of the [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese sultanate]]. The three Sagi's — the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], the [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] — were the territorial framework within which the sultan's authority had historically been mediated. The XXV Moekims was the third Sagi reached in the September [[1879]] reconnaissance after the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]] (conquered in [[1878]]) and the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] (conquered in [[1879]]).

### The September 1879 reconnaissance

The September [[1879]] reconnaissance of the XXV Moekims was the operational complement to the [[XXVI Moekims]] campaign that had culminated in the [[Selimoen tocht (1879)|tocht naar Selimoen]]. The Commandant van het Indische leger visited Atjeh in the same month of September [[1879]] — the month that produced the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] — and confirmed, in his report to the Regeering, that the entire Groot-Atjeh had been brought under Dutch authority. Van Heutsz quotes the Commandant's report on pages 19–20 of the [[1893]] tract:

> "Een feit toch is het, dat ik geheel Groot-Atjeh, voor zoover het door ons bezet is, heb doorkruist; in de XXII Moekims tot onzen uitersten wachtpost Djerir, in de XXVI Moekims tot Kroeng Kali in het Noorden, tot Tjot Lepong in het Oosten, en in de XXV Moekims tot Kroeng Raba" (It is indeed a fact that I have traversed all of Groot-Atjeh, as far as it is occupied by us; in the XXII Moekims as far as our outermost sentry-post Djerir, in the XXVI Moekims as far as Kroeng Kali in the north, as far as Tjot Lepong in the east, and in the XXV Moekims as far as Kroeng Raba).

The XXV Moekims was thus the third Sagi the Commandant reconnoitred, and [[Kroeng Raba]] — the south-western anchor of the [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] coastal line of [[1875]] — was the limit of the reconnaissance. The Commandant's personal report of the reconnaissance — *hoezeer slechts vergezeld door een twaalftal cavaleristen, nergens een schot gehoord of een gewapend Atjeher gezien heb* (although accompanied only by a dozen cavalrymen, he had nowhere heard a shot or seen an armed Atjehnese) — was the empirical proof that the XXV Moekims, like the XXII and XXVI, had been substantively conquered and not merely formally declared.

### The XXV Moekims and the Kroeng Raba limit

The [[Kroeng Raba]] limit of the September [[1879]] reconnaissance is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, doubly significant. First, Kroeng Raba was the south-western anchor of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Generaal Pel]]'s [[1875]] coastal line — the line *begrepen tusschen Kroeng Raja en Kroeng Raba* that Pel had prescribed for the occupation of the coastal strip of Groot-Atjeh. The Commandant's reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba was thus the operational completion of the Pel line — the extension of Dutch authority from the coastal strip into the interior Sagi whose south-western edge the line had been designed to seal. Second, Kroeng Raba was, in Van Heutsz's later narrative, the western anchor of the Gleh Loedah pass — the pass through which the Westkust route from [[Kemala]] entered the IV Moekims Kroeng Raba of Groot-Atjeh. The Commandant's reconnaissance thus fixed the XXV Moekims' south-western limit at the very pass that, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis of the post-[[1884]] *cajoleering*-decade, would have to be closed to seal Groot-Atjeh from the Westkust.

### The XXV Moekims after the 1884 Concentratie

The Van der Heijden conquest of the XXV Moekims did not, however, extinguish the Atjehnese resistance in the Sagi. After the [[1884]] [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] withdrawal, the XXV Moekims — like the rest of Groot-Atjeh — was abandoned to the *geconcentreerde stelling* of [[Kota Radja]]. The post-[[1884]] resistance in the XXV Moekims is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the territory of the *panglima sagi XXV* and the *bendehoofden* of the VI and IX Moekims. Van Heutsz records the post-[[1884]] bendehoofden of the IX Moekims (Sagi XXV): *In de IX Moekims (Sagi XXV). Tengkoe Kota Karang, zijn zoon Tengkoe Mat Saleh en Habib Samalanga* (In the IX Moekims [Sagi XXV]. Tengkoe Kota Karang, his son Tengkoe Mat Saleh and Habib Samalanga). The IX Moekims, a sub-federation within the XXV, was thus the territory of three of the principal *bendehoofden* of the post-[[1884]] resistance, and the *panglima sagi XXV* Tjoet Abas — *een man die zich zeer weinig met staatszaken bemoeit* (a man who concerned himself very little with state affairs) — was the Sagi-head whose passivity had allowed the *hoeloebalangs* of the various *moekims* to be, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, *elk in hun gebied, zoo goed als onafhankelijk* (each in their territory, virtually independent).

### The VI Moekims of the Sagi XXV

The VI Moekims — the sub-federation of the Sagi XXV that lay to the west of the Dutch *geconcentreerde stelling* — was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the *gewichtigste* (most important) of the sub-federations of the XXV for the post-[[1884]] security of the line. Van Heutsz records the VI Moekims: *Van deze moekims zijn voor ons met het oog op onze geconcentreerde stelling, de VI Moekims, die ten Westen, en de IX Moekims die ten Zuiden aan onze linie grenzen, de gewichtigste* (Of these moekims, with a view to our concentrated stelling, the VI Moekims, which border our line to the West, and the IX Moekims, which border it to the South, are the most important). The VI Moekims was headed, in Van Heutsz's narrative, by *T. Nanta Setia Radja* — *vroeger een onzer geduchtste vijanden, thans zeer oud, blind, afgeleefd en wonende te Lampisang in de kloof van Beradin* (formerly one of our most feared enemies, now very old, blind, decrepit and living at Lampisang in the kloof of Beradin) — whose *hoeloebalang* office had, by [[1893]], passed in effect to his son-in-law [[T. Oemar|T. Oemar]], the *buitengewoon slimme vogel* (extraordinarily cunning bird) whose *slimme* marriage-alliances and pepertuine possessie made him, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the principal *gevaarlijk sujet* of the post-[[1884]] VI Moekims.

### Significance (Van Heutsz account)

The XXV Moekims's significance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract rests on its being the closing chapter of the Van der Heijden conquest — the Sagi whose September [[1879]] reconnaissance fixed the *geheel Groot-Atjeh* declaration — and the opening chapter of the post-[[1884]] resistance's fragmentation — the Sagi whose *panglima*'s passivity allowed the *bendehoofden* of the IX Moekims to operate independently under the religious-political authority of [[Tengkoe di Tirou]].

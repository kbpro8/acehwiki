The **XXV Moekims** (Vijfentwintig Moekims, "Twenty-Five Mukims") was the *sagi* (confederation) of twenty-five *mukim* (parish) units lying south of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] between the [[III Moekims|III Moekims]] and the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], entirely conquered by [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s first offensive sweep of early February [[1876]]. Borel notes that "de geheele Sagie der XXV Moekims bevond zich in onze macht" — the entire Sagie of the XXV Moekims was in our power — after the operation.

## The conquest

The conquest of the XXV Moekims was the closing sweep of the first part of Pel's [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|winter offensive of 1875–1876]]. From Toeram, which bordered the gebied of [[Toekoe Bait]] of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the Dutch had made an *inval* (inroad) into the [[III Moekims|III Moekims]] on [[3 February]] [[1876]], driving the enemy out of [[Atoeh]] after a *heeten strijd*. The next day, [[4 February]] [[1876]], the colonne continued *noordoostelijk*: Lamrong and [[Lamsajoen]] were conquered, along with Kajoe Leh, [[Lambaroe]] and [[Pager Ajer|Pager Ajer Mesigit]], and via Tandjoeng and Bayoer communication was opened with the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]]. On [[7 February]] [[1876]] the troepen returned to [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] after a 10-day *tocht*. Total losses were five dead and 59 wounded — "betrekkelijk gering" — relatively slight. The first part of Pel's plan was now executed; the entire Sagie of the XXV Moekims was in Dutch hands.

## The demarcatie-linie

With the XXV Moekims in Dutch hands, the *demarcatie-linie* ran from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast to Atoeh, then northward to Pager Ajer on the left bank of the Atjeh-rivier. The line incorporated the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] — conquered in the same January [[1876]] offensive — into a single defensive perimeter whose southern boundary was the new XXV Moekims line. The conquest was, Borel notes, proof that the Atjehneezen could not withstand a well-planned combined colonne attack — but the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] of [[13 February]] [[1876]] showed that they could still punish careless Dutch columns on the marshy *sawah* (irrigated rice fields). After Pel's death at [[Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]], [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] adopted the *afwachtende houding* and the demarcatie-linie became, in effect, the permanent eastern limit of the Dutch *stelling* — "nagenoeg onveranderd" through Borel's writing in [[1878]].

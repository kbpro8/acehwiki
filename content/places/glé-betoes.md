**The Glé Betoes** — glossed by [[P. H. R. Beuming]] as *Nevelberg*, the mist mountain — was a mountain of the range between [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] and [[Lepong]] on the west of [[Groot-Atjeh]], on whose heights lay the [[Goeah Teroes]] cave that in [[1897]] sheltered the band of [[Toekoe Hassan]].

## The range

Beuming describes the Acehnese mountain chain *welke zich woest langs de Noordkust van Sumatra slingert* — which winds wildly along the north coast of [[Sumatra]] — and records that those who have seen it from close at hand will have gained the impression that it cannot be climbed except by the utmost exertion and under unbelievable difficulties. The ascent of the Glé Betoes on [[25 September]] [[1897]] took the Dutch patrol some hours, the men hoisting one another up rock faces of dizzying height with ropes and hooked staves while the point hacked a path with the *kapmes*.

## The action of [[25 September]] [[1897]]

The mountain gave its name to the operation Beuming titled *Een moeilijke tocht*. The patrol from [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] under Captain [[J. N. C. Baron van Heerdt]] failed to surprise the band, lost 1st Lieutenant [[J. van Greuningen]] and four men killed at the cave mouth, and withdrew the same day. The *[[Korps Marechaussee|marechaussée]]* brigades sent under Lieutenant [[C. F. A. Wagener]] to catch the enemy *aan de andere zijde van den Glé Betoes* — on the other side of the Glé Betoes — fought a violent action in the mountains in which Wagener and two *marechaussées* were killed and the band escaped, carrying its dead and wounded away. Beuming closes: *Aan den tocht naar den Glé Betoes, zijn voor ons droevige herinneringen verbonden* — to the march to the Glé Betoes sad memories are attached for us.

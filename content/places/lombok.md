**Lombok** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[C. Deijckerhoff]]

Deijckerhoff was "a convinced adherent of the policy of conciliation." That policy corresponded entirely with the demands of the Indies moment in [[1892]], because the army was then preparing for a large-scale undertaking against [[Lombok]] which would probably require all its strength, so that Atjeh could count on no reinforcement. Action on an extended scale therefore had to be avoided — "and that lay entirely in the line of Deijckerhoff's views."

### From [[Atjeh van 1890 tot 1898]]

Deijckerhoff's accession, du Croo wrote, "opened — and at the same time closed — the most disappointing and shameful period of Atjeh policy." He was a convinced adherent of the policy of conciliation. That policy corresponded entirely with the requirements of the Indies moment in [[1892]], because the army was then preparing for a large-scale undertaking against [[Lombok]] which would probably demand all its strength, so that Atjeh could not count on reinforcement. Action on an extended scale therefore had to be avoided — which lay entirely in the line of Deijckerhoff's own convictions. But the application of those convictions also meant that the courage and enterprise of the warlike Acehnese greatly increased: "als de *Kompeni* ("the Company (the Dutch power)"did not dare, he at once dared twice as much!"

### From [[Non-interventiepolitiek]]

Gradually more colonial figures of significance turned against this absolute non-intervention; more than one side pointed to the dangerous and continual chance that other powers, in the emerging drive for colonial expansion, would declare that non-intervention to be impotence and exploit it; Governor-General [[Van Lansberge]] at least dared to acknowledge that greater involvement did not exceed Dutch financial strength. But it lasted until [[1894]], and until the abuses in [[Lombok]] made intervention unavoidable, before the minister of Colonies acknowledged: "our sovereignty over a native state lays upon us, with regard to what happens there, a responsibility that we cannot shake off."

**Cape Pedro** (Achehnese *Tanjong Pedro*; modern Tanjung Pedro) is a headland on the north coast of [[Acheh]], lying between the bays of [[Krueng Raya]] and [[Lho Seumawe|Lhok Seumawe]] and marking the northernmost projection of the Achehnese littoral. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I it is named only on the ethnographic map of Acheh and its dependencies appended to the volume, where it appears together with the neighbouring littoral states.

## On the Map of the Achehnese Territories

Cape Pedro appears in the map titled "Acheh and the neighbouring littoral states" (scale 1: 300,000), printed at the end of Vol. I. The map details the principal uleebalangships, mukims, military posts, and native promontories along the north coast. The cape is identified as one of the prominent geographical features in the long littoral between [[Pidie]] and the bay of [[Krueng Raya]], a stretch of coast which the Achehnese themselves used as a boundary reference in describing their territory. The line bounding [[Great Acheh]] "extends from [[Kluang]] on the West, to [[Krueng Raya]] on the North Coast, and passing through [[Reueng-reueng]], [[Pancha]] and [[Janthoe]]" — a description that places Cape Pedro on the northern frontier of the Achehnese kernel.

## The North Coast in Achehnese Geography

The [[North Coast (of Acheh)|North Coast]] of Acheh, of which Cape Pedro is a salient feature, was the principal avenue of foreign commerce in the period treated by Snouck Hurgronje. The pepper trade, the importation of stockfish from the [[Maldives]], the pilgrim traffic to [[Mecca]], and the movement of Kling and Arab traders all passed along the North Coast. Cape Pedro, like other named capes along the Achehnese littoral, served as a landmark for native prahus and as a reference point in the local oral geography.

**Pantej Perak** (Pantai Perak, "Silver Beach") was the grassy plain on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] south of [[Penajoeng]] designated in early [[1875]] as the site of the new Dutch ambulances — the hospitals moved out of [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] after the [[Cholera catastrophe at Kota Radja (February 1875)|February 1875 cholera catastrophe]]. The Kraton, Borel insisted, had become one great hospital, and the constant passage of the dead through the interior on "ominous little wagons" drawn by chain-gang convicts past the barracks was a moral injury to the garrison beyond the bare statistics of mortality.

## The decision

The February [[1875]] cholera catastrophe forced the issue. In the K. Radja ambulances alone almost 500 people died (of whom 270 were military), averaging 15–18 per day, with peaks of 25 or 30. The situation became so alarming that the absolute necessity of strong measures was recognised if the entire Indies army was not to go to ruin. As a first step the ambulances were to be moved to the right bank of the Atjeh-rivier, where the *grasvlakte* (grassy plain) of Pantej Perak south of Penajoeng offered an excellent building site. Only when all sick were removed from K. Radja and the bamboo barracks there demolished, burned or properly disinfected could a favourable change be hoped for. In early March [[1875]] the engineers began urgently building a camp for a battalion of infantry on the right bank to cover the new hospitals.

## The move and its meaning

The decision to move the ambulances to Pantej Perak was a tacit admission that [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s prediction — that K. Radja would become "eene aangename gezellige garnizoensplaats" — had been exactly wrong. The Kraton was the unhealthiest spot on the Atjeh coast, a *groot zickeninrichting* (great sick-house) where malaria, cholera and the [[Bandjir flood of Kota Radja (December 1874)|December 1874 bandjir]] had produced "ontzettend" mortality. Pantej Perak, by contrast, lay on the higher right bank, exposed to the sea-breeze and out of the marshy ground around the Kraton. With the move of the ambulances, the Dutch *stelling* on the right bank — Penajoeng, the [[Marine-benteng|marine-benteng]], [[Kwala Atjeh]] and the [[Pedir-dijk]] posts — became more than a military perimeter; it became the medical and logistical base of the entire occupation. The right-bank posts of the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] were thus anchored on Pantej Perak as their logistical centre.

**Pantej Perak** was a grassy plain situated opposite the [[Kraton of Atjeh]] and [[Atjeh's Groote Missigit]].

## Historical Significance
Following severe combat in late December [[1873]], Dutch forces secured this plain. It offered a clear line of sight to the Missigit, revealing the newly constructed Acehnese earthworks, and served as a staging ground for the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]].

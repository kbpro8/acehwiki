**Lamtih** — written in Dutch as *Lamtih* — was a [[Dutch]] post in the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcentreerde Linie (Concentrated Line)]] of the southern sector, armed with a 7 cm bronze field gun in the north-west bastion, equipped with an observation *stelling* with a *mitrailleur*, and serving as a departure point for the patrouille of [[21 May]] [[1896]], for the attack of [[23 May]] [[1896]], and for the colonne of [[5 June]] [[1896]]. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the raised sentry-box of Lamtih was shelled from Senangri on [[13 April]] [[1896]] — the first hostilities at the post. Lamtih was one of the principal assembly points for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]].

## Description and Location

Lamtih lay in the southern sector of the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcente Linie (Concentrated Line)]] between [[Lam-Djamoe]] and [[Ketapan Doea]]. The post was armed with a 7 cm bronze field gun in the north-west bastion, and was equipped with an observation position with a machine gun. The raised sentry-box of Lamtih was the most-forward observation point of the post. The post commanded the road toward [[Lam-Tengah-Oost|Lam-Tengah]] and the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV and VI Moekims]]. The post was linked by telephone to the forward area and to [[Lam-Djamoe]]. The Senangri position, from which the *verhoogde schilderhuis* was shelled on [[13 April]] [[1896]], lay to the front of Lamtih.

## Historical Background

Lamtih had been constructed as part of the [[Geconcentrerde Linie|Geconcenteerde Linie (Concentrated Line)]] — the inner line of Dutch posts around [[Koeta Radja]]. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] and the evacuation of the [[buitenlinie]] in [[April]] [[1896]], Lamtih became a forward post of the southern sector, supporting [[Lam-Djamoe]] and [[Ketapan Doea]]. The post was the origin of a patrol that entered the kampong of the same name on [[31 March]] [[1896]] and returned with 5 muzzle-loaders, 2 blunderbusses, and white weapons, having found the kampong abandoned. The Senangri position to the front of Lamtih was the source of the first hostile fire against the post.

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Lamtih was one of the principal Dutch posts of the southern sector throughout the first semester of [[1896]]. On [[13 April]] [[1896]] the *verhoogde schilderhuis* of Lamtih was shelled from Senangri — the first hostilities at the post. On [[31 March]] [[1896]] a patrol from Lamtih entered the kampong of the same name and returned with captured weapons. On [[21 May]] [[1896]] a patrouille departed from Lamtih in the direction of [[Lamtengah|Moekim Lamtengah]]. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] was launched in part from Lamtih: the 12e Bataljon under [[J. F. T. van Bloemen Waanders|Majoor Van Bloemen Waanders]] marched from Lamtih against [[Lam-Tengah]] and [[Lam-Awi]], and a *colonne Lamtih* was one of the assault columns of the day. On [[5 June]] [[1896]] the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|two-colonne operation]] set out in part from Lamtih. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] the [[Machtsvertoon (20 June 1896)|four-column show-of-force]] used Lamtih as the starting point of its II column. Lamtih appears in the Dutch forces tables of [[June]] [[1896]] as a post in the line.

## Significance

Lamtih was one of the principal Dutch assembly and departure points for the [[1896]] counter-offensive against [[Teuku Umar]], together with [[Lam-Djamoe]], [[Ketapan Doea]], and [[Blang]]. Its *mitrailleur* observation *stelling* and *kanon van 7 c.M. A. Veld* made it a key fire-support post for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] and the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|two-colonne operation of 4–5 June 1896]]. The shelling of the *verhoogde schilderhuis* from Senangri on [[13 April]] [[1896]] was among the earliest hostile acts against the inner line after the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]].

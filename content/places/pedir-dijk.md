The **Pedir-dijk** was the dike along the right (west) bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] from [[Lemboe]] downstream to [[Kota Radja Bedil]] at the river-mouth, which Borel calls "de aangewezen verdedigingslijn der Atjeh-rivier" — the designated defensive line of the Atjeh-rivier. Built originally by the Atjehneezen to hold floodwater off the sawahs, it served the Dutch, once they had occupied it, as a raised causeway and natural parapet commanding the river, the sawahs to either side, and the kampong belt of [[Langkroek]], [[Lamara]] and [[Berauw]] along its length.

## Strategic value

The Pedir-dijk was, in Borel's analysis, the obvious line on which to base any Dutch *stelling* at the mouth of the Atjeh-rivier. Its elevated crest gave command of the *sawah* (irrigated rice fields) on both sides, its continuous length provided a ready-made communication trench between [[Kwala Atjeh]] and [[Lemboe]], and its western flank was covered by the marshes of the [[Marine-benteng|marine-benteng]] and the river-mouth itself. The dike was therefore the key to the security of the [[Penajoeng]] bivouak, the [[Marine-benteng]], and ultimately the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] from attack along the right bank. Borel insists that it should have been occupied and garrisoned in February [[1874]] — after the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|Bital]] and [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|Ketapang Doewa]] expeditions — instead of being left to the enemy.

## The dike in the war

The dike's strategic value was demonstrated by the recurrent fights along its length. The *padi-snijden* concession of February–March [[1874]] was made to the *bevolking* of [[Langkroek]] on the dike. The [[Acehnese attack on Penajoeng (11 April 1874)|massed Atjehnese attack on Penajoeng]] of [[11 April]] [[1874]] — reinforced by *hulptroepen from Pedir* — was driven back behind the Pedir-dijk, where the enemy left some dead. The dike was only permanently secured after the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] of [[28 June]] [[1874]], when the Atjehneezen abandoned the dike, the road to Koeala, and K. R. Bedil "in one movement," all of which the Dutch occupied at once. The [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] of [[28 September]] [[1874]] and the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] of [[7 November]] [[1874]] extended the Dutch hold to the upper end of the dike; [[Kota Radja Bedil|K. R. Bedil]] at the lower end was reoccupied on [[28 June]] [[1874]].

**Djeunamprong** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[W. van Kregten]]

He was killed by a band of twelve men armed with *[[rentjong]]* and *[[klewang]]* from the *[[landschap]]pen* [[Lam No]], [[Panté Tjeureumèn]], [[Djeunamprong]] and [[Oenga]], who fled to the mountains with the soldiers' weapons. The night quarters had been chosen and occupied "in the most utterly careless manner", the weapons of both soldiers lying on the floor of two different unwalled rooms; in the morning, while the soldiers had left their weapons to drink coffee and the lieutenant was innocently winding his puttees, [[Nja' Ali|Si Ali]] and four others attacked him. The cause lay in the dismissal of Si Ali, at Van Kregten's instigation, from his post as *mandoer* of a coconut garden, and in a personal grudge against the *[[oelèëbalang]]* of [[Lambeusdë]]. [[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] added the note: "Van Kregten was a quiet, calm, well-balanced man."

### From [[Lam No]]

The band of twelve men, armed with *[[rentjong]]* and *[[klewang]]* and drawn from the *[[landschap]]pen* Lam No, [[Panté Tjeureumèn]], [[Djeunamprong]] and [[Oenga]], fled to the mountains with the soldiers' weapons and was pursued by several brigades. The plan to withdraw had already been formed when the extremely careless night quarters on the *keudé* at Lam No gave them "a fine chance to make a name and at the same time to provide themselves with weapons." [[Tgk. Machmoed van Lam No]], a former *teungkoe-meunasah* at odds with his *[[oelèëbalang]]* over his share of the *[[zakat]]*, was one of the four principal perpetrators.

### From [[Resistance in Daja and Lhong (1914)]]

The *maandverslag* reported: "On 20 May the Civil Authority, First Lieutenant W. van Kregten, on tour in the Daja country (Swart's former patrol district), was murdered at Lam No together with two soldiers by a band of twelve men armed with dagger and sabre and coming from the territories of [[Lam No]], [[Panté Tjeureumèn]], [[Djeunamprong]] and [[Oenga]]. The band, which fled to the mountains carrying off the weapons of the murdered soldiers, is being pursued by several brigades."

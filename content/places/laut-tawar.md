**Laut Tawar** — the lake of Takengeun — is the lake in the [[Gajolanden|Gayo Lands]] of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] on which [[Takengeun]] stands, at the southern terminus of the [[Gajoweg]]. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Laut Tawar is the book's — and the lieutenant's journey through Aceh — destination: the beautiful lake in the Gajolanden at the end of the Gajoweg.

## The Gajoweg and Laut Tawar

The Gajoweg now leads to Takengeun on the beautiful lake in the Gajolanden, over a distance of 103 km. At km 11 one passes the bridge over the [[Peusanganrivier]], which carries the water of Laut Tawar to the sea. Laut Tawar is therefore the book's marker of the beautiful lake at the end of the Gajoweg: the lake whose water the Peusanganrivier carries to the sea.

## Significance

Laut Tawar is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the beautiful lake of the Gajolanden at the end of the Gajoweg. The book's argument — that Laut Tawar was the destination of the lieutenant's journey through Aceh and the lake whose water the Peusanganrivier carries to the sea — is the book's marker of the beautiful lake of the Gajolanden. Laut Tawar is therefore the book's marker of the destination of the Gajoweg.

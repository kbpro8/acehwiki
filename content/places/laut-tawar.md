**Laut Tawar** — Dutch ***Laoet Tawar***, also called the **lake of Takengeun** and the **Gayo lake** — is the great natural lake of the [[Gajoland|Gayo]] highlands of [[Atjeh|Aceh]], approximately 16 kilometres long and 4 kilometres wide, lying at about 1,200 metres above sea level in a basin enclosed by the high, pine-forested walls of the [[Klietel-massif|*Klietel*-massif]] to the south and the *B. ni Segi*–*B. ni Beboeli* chain to the north. It is the geographical and demographic heart of the [[Gajo Loeës|Gajo Loeës]] high plateau and the headwater of the [[Kr. Peusangan|Kr. Peusangan]], which descends from it to the North Coast; the village of [[Takengeun]] stands on its shore at the southern terminus of the [[Gajoweg|*Gajo-weg*]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the *Laoet Tawar* as the principal physical feature of the Gayo lands: the body of water around which the great rice plains of *Bintang* and *Takengën* formed, and the barrier whose only access of *hoogen weg* from the north — the *Klietel*-saddle — was the route of the *Gajo-weg*, "met ontzaglijke moeite en kosten geslagen" (opened with immense effort and cost).

## Topography and formation


The *Laoet Tawar* is an *afdammingsmeer* — a dammed lake — formed by the natural blocking of the Peusangan valley by the *Telëgë*-heights and the *Klietel*-massif. The lake's water is contained by the high, almost perpendicular walls of the surrounding mountains, which "langs overal tot de slechts hier en daar wat terugwijkende bergflanken bijna oevers naar voren dringen" — everywhere thrust the shores forward, almost to the only here and there somewhat receding mountain flanks. Small, fully enclosed shore-plains — *Kenawa*, *Towéran*, *Rawé*, and *Nosan* — provided the *sawahs* of a few small settlements; the larger, gently sloping plains of *Bintang* and *Takengën* on the eastern and western shores became, by their continuous subsidence and their fertility, "welvarende bevolkings-centra" (prosperous population centres).

## The outflow and the Peusangan


Even west of the present *bestuursvestiging* [[Takengën|Takengën]], the *Laoet Tawar* breaks through the narrow western wall of the basin and draws into its watershed the *Peusangan* river, which thereafter absorbs the small *Wol ni Aréh* and the *keteldal* (kettle-valley) of *Pegasèng* on its way to the coast. The heavy forested slopes of the *Telëgë* heights then turn the *Peusangan* in its course to the west, forcing it to seek a lower *drempel* (threshold) and to break through the narrow, deep valley between the steep watershed of the upper *Wojla* and the third Barisan *coulisse*. Below the *Pegasèng*-drempel, the *Peusangan* descends to the North Coast through the *keteldal* of *Ketël* and the moorish delta of the [[Kr. Peusangan]]. It is the lake whose water the [[Peusanganrivier]] carries to the sea — a fact which makes Laut Tawar the headwater of one of the principal North-Coast watercourses of Atjeh.

## The settlements and the chiefdoms


The shores of the *Laoet Tawar* are the demographic core of the [[Gajo Loeës|Gajo Loeës]]. The great *kampong* of *Bintang* — seat of the *Boekét Ewéh* branch of the [[Kédjoerön Boekét]] house — lies on its eastern shore; the *bestuursvestiging* of [[Takengën]] lies on its western shore; the small shore settlements of *Kenawa*, *Towéran*, *Rawé*, and *Nosan* occupy the enclosed plains along its banks. The [[Kédjoerön Pétiambang]] — the titular overlord of the Gayo Loeës whose authority had, by the colonial period, decayed into a *theoretisch gezag* (theoretical authority) — had his seat at *Penampacian* on the lake shore.

## The Gajoweg and Laut Tawar


The [[Gajoweg]] now leads to [[Takengeun]] on the beautiful lake in the Gajolanden, over a distance of 103 km. At km 11 one passes the bridge over the [[Peusanganrivier]], which carries the water of Laut Tawar to the sea. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Laut Tawar is the book's — and the lieutenant's journey through Aceh — destination: the beautiful lake in the Gajolanden at the end of the Gajoweg, whose water the Peusanganrivier carries to the sea.

## Hydrography and orographic setting (Encyclopaedisch Bureau, 1917)


The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's earlier Sumatra-wide survey (Aflevering 1, Hoofdstuk I) places the Laoet Tawar on the *rand* (edge) of the *Gajosche Centraalgebergte* (Gayo central mountain range), between the [[Geureudong|Geureudong I (71)]] and [[Boer ni Telong|Boer ni Telong (73)]] volcanoes, and among the second category of Sumatran *kratermeren* (crater lakes). The lake's waters drain through the [[Kroeëng Keureutë]] to the *Noordkust* (North Coast) of Atjeh, forming the principal hydrographical connection between the Gayo *hoogvlakte* (high plateau) and the Noordkust *kustvlakte* (coastal plain); its *stroomgebied* (watershed) includes the *hoogvlakte* of [[Takengon|Takengon]] and [[Ketol|Ketol]].

## The Laut Tawar in the Second World War


In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch WWII staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the Laoet Tawar's *rand* at Takengon is the site of [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]]'s [[19 March 1942]] capitulation, of Lt [[H. van Zanten|Van Zanten]]'s [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|post-capitulation resistance]], and of Lt [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|Onvlee]]'s post-war brief of the Korpscommandant on the [[safeguarding of the vaandel of the Korps Maréchaussee|safeguarding of the vaandel]].

## Significance


Laut Tawar is significant both as the physical heart of the Gayo lands and as a recurring landmark in the colonial and military geography of Atjeh. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* it is the beautiful lake of the Gajolanden at the end of the Gajoweg — the destination of the lieutenant's journey through Aceh. In *De Buitenbezittingen* it is the principal physical feature of the Gayo lands and the headwater of the Peusangan. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch WWII staff history]] it is the site of the March 1942 capitulation at Takengon and of the post-capitulation resistance of Lt Van Zanten. Across all three accounts, the lake marks the southern terminus of the Gajoweg and the geographical pivot of the Gayo highlands.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Laoet Tawar** — modern *Laut Tawar* — is the great lake of the [[Gajo-land|Gajo country]] in the interior of northern Sumatra, on whose shore lies the chief place [[Takengon|Takengon]]. The lake, more than 1.200 metres above sea-level, is 16 km long; its greatest breadth is 5 km. It is the great attraction of Takengon, but by no means the only one. A road runs along the lake, hewn out of the mountain-wall, and here and there lie the small villages, whose population fishes and has its *sawahs* by the rivers that bring their water into Laut Tawar. On the west side it discharges into the [[Peusangan|Peusangan]] river.

### Use in Literature


The lake is the central feature of [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners|Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners]]* ([[1903]]), and is the geographic heart of the *Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden* as reconstituted in [[1922]]. Broersma describes the lake and its surroundings in the eleventh and twelfth chapters of *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]*, on the [[Gajo-weg|Gajo-weg]] and the interior.

### The *Depèq* and the Fishery


The lake is subject to the *depèq* — a dry wind that sometimes rises to storm-force in the months April–October, and against which the praws of fishermen on the lake must quickly take shelter. The fishing on the lake is one of the principal livelihoods of the Gajo *kampongs* on its shore.

### Significance


Laoet Tawar is the geographic landmark of the Gajo country and the principal scenic attraction of the interior of northern Sumatra. The lake and its surrounding mountains form the highland basin which the colonial government hoped to develop as a *herstellingsoord* — a convalescent station — for the European population of the lowlands.

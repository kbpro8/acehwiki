**Kuta Alam** — also written **Kota Alam** — is an ancient [[gampong|gampong]] near the capital of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], known in the pre-colonial period as the place of burial of the great [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] ([[1607]]–[[1636]]) and, in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], as the site of the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam]] — the sandbag redoubt constructed under Achehnese fire on [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] by a Dutch column under [[M. C. E. Ruempol|Luitenant-Kolonel M. C. E. Ruempol]], with the sapper section commanded in the vanguard by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede Luitenant C. J. Snijders]] of the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs]].

## The Ancient Gampong

An ancient gampong near the capital (not to be confused with the place now officially so called). 'In the neighbourhood of the ancient gampong Kuta Alam we find the Kubu Poteu Meureuhom, certain tombs of departed royalties. It is believed that the great [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] = [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] ([[1607]]-36) is buried here, and indeed it is not improbable that the name of the gampong is simply an abbreviation of that of this famous ruler.'

## The Benting Kota Alam Operation (13 November 1874)

In the autumn of [[1874]], after the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] had captured the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] and the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]] had been occupied to connect [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] with [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] on the west, the Dutch turned to securing the eastern flank of their positions. On [[7 November]] [[7 November]] [[1874]] Dutch troops had captured the fortified villages of Lemboecq, Lemboe, Berauw, and the Rivierbenting northeast of [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. Intelligence then indicated that the [[Achehnese|Atjehers]] were preparing to occupy Kuta Alam further east, a move that would have threatened [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] and the Dutch line of communication. [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Kolonel PEL]] (civil and military governor) decided to forestall them by building a defensible post at Kuta Alam on [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]].

In the pre-dawn darkness of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [1874], a column under [[M. C. E. Ruempol|Luitenant-Kolonel Ruempol]] — the 9th Battalion L.H., a mountain artillery section, a section of [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Mineurs en Sappers]] (commanded in the vanguard by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede Luitenant Snijders]]), and an ambulance detachment — departed [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and followed the [[Pedir]] dike to the [[Acheh River|Atjeh River]] near the Rivierbenting, then proceeded upstream to a pre-selected site near a river bend. [[T. J. A. van Zijll de Jong|Majoor T. J. A. van Zijll de Jong]] (artillery) and [[W. J. C. Leers|Majoor W. J. C. Leers]] (engineers) sited the fortification to command both the fortified edge of Pinang to the northeast and [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] to the south, while dominating a long stretch of the river — one of the best crossing points.

Snijders immediately set his coolies to filling sandbags and stacking them along the traced perimeter, while a large Achehnese house was converted into temporary shelter. A double line of outposts 150 metres forward, anchored on the river and reinforced with an infantry section and artillery, covered the work. Scarcely had the first sandbags been laid when heavy rifle fire erupted from the scrub on three sides and from the opposite bank. The [[Achehnese|Atjehners]] twice attempted to rush the covering line but were repulsed. The fortification — named **Benting Kota Alam** — was completed and held, securing the eastern flank of the Dutch positions and covering the Dutch line of communication with [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] and the country further east. The full operation is described on the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|dedicated event page]].

The [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Kota Alam action]] of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [1874] is, in the biographer D. van den Berg's framing of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s career, an exemplar of the Dutch reliance on rapid field engineering — sandbag redoubts sited to dominate river crossings and village strongholds — to consolidate territorial gains against a mobile, well-armed opponent in the swampy, jungle-covered theatre of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The redoubt complemented the westward extension of the Dutch perimeter along the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]] (toward [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]]) and the northward extension along the [[Noorderlinie|Noorderlinie]] earlier in the same year.

A port on the West Coast. Sadati dialogue: 'When you get to Laboh Haji (vulg. Labuan Haji), who is king there? The uleebalang of that place is a woman, she keeps us all in her protection.' (A woman named Chut Nya' Patimah was in fact uleebalang of this place for a number of years.)

## Account from *Over patchouly-olie en patchouly-blad van Atjeh (1925)* — patchouli

[[Laboh Hadji|Labohan-Hadji]] — modern Labuhan Hajji — was, in the early twentieth century, one of the three principal patchouli (*nilam*) leaf-producing districts of the [[Tapa Toean|Tapa Toean]] sub-division, alongside [[Loboang Koang|Loboang Koang]] and [[Bakongan|Bakongan]]. In [[Van Vreeswijk|Van Vreeswijk]]'s ranking of the Tapa Toean patchouli districts, given in his letter of [[16 August]] [[1924]] to the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum|Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] and preserved in [[Over patchouly-olie en patchouly-blad van Atjeh (1925)|*Bericht* No. 21 of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] (April 1925), Labohan-Hadji was the **least valuable** of the three.

### Van Vreeswijk's Verdict

Van Vreeswijk reported that the Labohan-Hadji leaf "komt zeer vuil op de markt" — comes very dirty to market — and was often weighted with sea-water to increase its weight at sale. The market price was only ƒ5 to ƒ6 per *pikol*, and after cleaning only 50% of the bale remained. The Labohan-Hadji leaf was thus the lowest-quality and lowest-priced of the Tapa Toean patchouli districts — a sharp contrast to the [[Bakongan|Bakongan]] leaf, which Van Vreeswijk judged "het neusje van den zalm" (the very best) and which commanded a market price of ƒ14 per *pikol*.

The Labohan-Hadji case illustrates the importance of the place of origin and of the care taken in cultivation and drying: the same patchouli plant, grown at Labohan-Hadji under the less careful cultivation and drying practices of the Labohan-Hadji planters, produced a leaf of markedly lower quality and lower market price than the same plant grown at Bakongan. The Handelsmuseum's conclusion, after the Bakongan triumph of the winter of 1924-1925, was that "prima blad, prima olie; op de winning van het goede blad komt het aan!" — prime leaf, prime oil; it is the winning of the good leaf that matters.

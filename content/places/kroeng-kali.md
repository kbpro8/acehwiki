**Kroeng Kali** — *Krueng Kali* — is a river in the [[XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] that flows *recht noordwaarts* (straight northward) some two hundred metres west of the Atjehnese fortification of [[Kaloet]]. The river is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as the site of the night-patrol of Lieutenant [[Brant]] on the night of [[22 August|22-23 August]] [[1892]] — the patrol that established the topographical knowledge that would inform the subsequent [[Reconnaissance of Kaloet (23 August 1892)|reconnaissance of 23 August 1892]] — and as the site of the *excursie* (reconnaissance) of [[4 May]] [[1896]] on which Lieutenant [[C. G. A. C. Krohne|Krohne]] was mortally wounded. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the name *Kroeng-Kali* is also borne by a kampong and river in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] — the site of the house of the territorial chief, a series of fortifications, and the principal Achehnese *stelling* east of Kroeng-Kali; the two localities share a name but lie in different *sagi*.

## The 1892 night-patrol (XXVI Moekims)

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the Kroeng Kali — *die een paar honderd meter westwaarts langs Kaloet, en recht noordwaarts stroomt* (which flows a couple of hundred metres westward along Kaloet, and straight northward) — was the site of the night-patrol of Lieutenant [[Brant]] of the *maréchaussée* on the night of [[22 August|22-23 August]] [[1892]]. The patrol — a *detachement maréchaussées* — lay in ambush in the dry bed of the river — *in eene der versterkingen in de bedding van het riviertje de Kroeng Kali* (in one of the fortifications in the bed of the riverlet the Kroeng Kali) — with the intention of catching the Atjehnese garrison of Kaloet as they came in the morning. The river was *dikwijels … droog; zoo ook toen* (often dry; so also then), which allowed the patrol to occupy its bed. The Atjehnese garrison, however, *versliepen zich* (overslept), and the patrol had to return to [[Lamreng]] at 7 a.m. without having caught anyone.

## The 1896 reconnaissance (XXVI Moekims)

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the Kroeng Kali was also the site of the *excursie* (reconnaissance) of [[4 May]] [[1896]] on which Lieutenant [[C. G. A. C. Krohne|Krohne]] was mortally wounded. The album's roll of the fallen gives Krohne's date of death as [[4 May]] [[1896]] and records that he was *gewond op 4 Mei bij eene excursie naar Kroeng Kali (XXVI Moekims)* — *wounded on 4 May during an excursion to Kroeng Kali (XXVI Moekims)* — and that he *denzelfden dag tengevolge van deze verwonding overleed* (died the same day in consequence of this wound). The reconnaissance was undertaken in the period between the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]] and the [[Storming of Lampisang (May 1896)|storming of Lampisang]] of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1896]].

## Significance (XXVI Moekims)

The Kroeng Kali's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the site of two of the principal documented engagements of the late *concentratie* period — the night-patrol of Lieutenant [[Brant]] of [[22 August|22-23 August]] [[1892]], and the reconnaissance of [[4 May]] [[1896]] on which Lieutenant [[C. G. A. C. Krohne|Krohne]] was mortally wounded. The river's *droog* (dry) bed was the principal route by which the Dutch patrols approached the [[Kaloet]] fortification in the [[1892]] operations, and its situation in the [[XXVI Moekims]] — the northernmost of the three *sagis* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — places it as one of the principal operational centres of the late *concentratie* period.

## Kroeng-Kali in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb (ATJEH '96 account)

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, a separate *Kroeng-Kali* — also written *Kroeng Kali*, *Kr. Kali* — was a kampong and river in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]], the site of the house of the territorial chief, a series of fortifications, and the principal Achehnese *stelling* east of Kroeng-Kali. As described in *ATJEH '96*, Kroeng-Kali was the site of a cavalerie charge on [[4 May]] [[1896]] and of the four-way intersection at which 1e luit. [[E. J. Krohne|Krohne]] was mortally wounded on the same day. The old fort of Kroeng-Kali — a square redoubt of 15 metres side, walls 1.5 metres high, with a small swivel cannon — was stormed on [[23 June]] [[1896]] by the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] of the 12e Bataljon, with 2nd Lt. [[Heyligers]] first inside; the *benteng* (fortification) was destroyed. The Achehnese *stelling* east of Kroeng-Kali (partly belonging to the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]) was a complex of six *versterkingen* (fortifications) known as the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling.

### Description and Location (XIII Moekims Toengkoeb)

Kroeng-Kali lay in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]], to the south of the kampong of [[Toengkoeb]] and to the east of the mountain wall at whose edge [[Habib Lengkong]]'s fort stood. The kampong was the site of the house of the *hoeloebalang* [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and of a series of *versterkingen*. The old fort of Kroeng-Kali was a square redoubt of 15 metres side, with walls 1.5 metres high and 0.75 metres thick, a 4-metre-wide bamboo-thorn palisade, lodging for approximately 40 men, and a small swivel cannon. The four-way intersection at Kroeng-Kali was the principal crossroads of the area. The Achehnese *stelling* east of Kroeng-Kali (the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling) was a complex of six *versterkingen*: Koeta Lampoe Taroem, Toei Gloempang, Glé Broeë Siem, Boeng, Koeta Lampoe Djoeng, and Teungkoe Ba+ Riwat. The *stelling* of [[Tjot Karieng]] (Tjat Karieng) to the east of Kroeng-Kali and the southern part of the III Moekims Lam-Rebo stelling were also associated.

### Historical Background (XIII Moekims Toengkoeb)

Kroeng-Kali had been the seat of the *hoeloebalang* [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The kampong had been fortified with an old square redoubt and a series of *versterkingen*, and had been a persistent obstacle to Dutch operations in the eastern theatre. The Achehnese *stelling* east of Kroeng-Kali — the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling of six *versterkingen* — had been built up over years and was a complex of old and new fortifications. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] aligned himself with Umar, and Kroeng-Kali became a base for hostile *benden* (war-bands) operating against the [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] of the eastern *linie*.

### Role in the 1896 Operations (XIII Moekims Toengkoeb)

Kroeng-Kali was the scene of repeated Dutch operations throughout the first semester of [[1896]]. On [[4 May]] [[1896]] the [[Second Expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb (4 May 1896)|second expedition to the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] advanced against the *stelling* east of Kroeng-Kali: a cavalerie charge was conducted near the kampong, and 1e luit. [[E. J. Krohne|Krohne]] was mortally wounded at the four-way intersection. On [[17 June]] [[1896]] the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] included the storming of the *benteng* of Kroeng-Kali by the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] of the 12e Bataljon; the *benteng* was a square redoubt of 15 metres side with walls 1.5 metres high and a small swivel cannon, and was destroyed by the 12e compagnie. On [[23 June]] [[1896]] the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|battle of Kroeng-Kali]] was fought: the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] stormed the old *benteng* of Kroeng-Kali, with 2nd Lt. [[Heyligers]] first inside; the *benteng* was destroyed. [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and Panglima Nja Makam were attacked and retreated to [[Lam-Tobah]]. On [[22 June]] [[1896]] a maréchaussée column crossed [[Glé Taroempas]] and descended into the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] from the east, in the course of which the Achehnese *stelling* east of Kroeng-Kali — the [[Glé Broeë]] stelling — was attacked by the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Hansen column]] from the rear via [[Ladoeng]].

### Significance (XIII Moekims Toengkoeb)

Kroeng-Kali was the principal Achehnese strongpoint in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and the seat of the *hoeloebalang* [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]]. Its *benteng* — a square redoubt of 15 metres side with walls 1.5 metres high and a *lilla* (small swivel cannon) — was the most-formidable single Achehnese fortification in the eastern theatre, and its repeated storming on [[17 June]] [[1896]] and [[23 June]] [[1896]] demonstrated the Dutch determination to break the eastern bastion of Achehnese resistance. The mortal wounding of 1e luit. [[E. J. Krohne|Krohne]] at the *viersprong* (four-way intersection) on [[4 May]] [[1896]] was one of the most-noted Dutch losses of the period.

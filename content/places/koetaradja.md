**Koetaradja** — also *Kutaraja*, *Koeta Radja*, modern [[Banda Aceh]] — was the capital of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Dutch Aceh government]] and the seat of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] from its outbreak in [[1873]] to its formal close. Here stood the Kraton of the Sultans of [[Aceh]], and here, on the strand, the *Köhler-boom* beneath which [[J.H.R. Köhler|General Köhler]] fell during the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)]] — the tree felled only in [[1936]], from whose lower trunk a table was made for the Atjeh-museum. Here too stood the great *mesdjid*, built *op 's Lands kosten* — at the government's cost — whose interior Zentgraff photographed; and here, at [[Indrapoeri]], colonel [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had his residence. The [[Peutjoet]] military cemetery lay near the town, its white grave-stones gleaming in the shade of the trees.

## The seat of government

Koetaradja was the seat of the civil and military government of Atjeh and the central node of the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line). On [[8 May]] [[1896]], after the [[Defection of Teukoe Oemar (1896)|overloopen]] of [[Teukoe Oemar]], no fewer than 5000 men — Europeans and Inlanders — lay at Koetaradja and [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oeleëlheuë]], and the high command *wist eigenlijk niet precies wat men er mee moest doen* (did not really know what to do with them). The letters of [[G.C.E. van Daalen|kapitein Van Daalen]] to his wife in [[1896]]–[[1897]] paint Koetaradja as the place where the *hooge lui* (high-ups) sat with their hands in their hair — a leadership that "had lost the confidence of officers and men," that supplied the troops "a month without bread and meat," that issued the disastrous order to seize the cattle and gold ornaments of [[Lhong]] in [[April]] [[1897]]. Van Daalen's pencil-notes from *Bivak Tamoen* call the whole policy *beschamend* (shameful) — and it was from Koetaradja that the *katjauen* (backbiting) at the Stafbureau flowed, where men in secret hoped for the failure of [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s [[Gajoetocht (1904)|Gajoetocht]].

## The hoofddjaksa and the hoofdpenghoeloe

Koetaradja was the seat of the two native officials whom [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] singled out, in his advisory report, as *instrumenten van het gezag* (instruments of authority) — the *hoofddjaksa* (chief native prosecutor) [[Mohamad Arif]] and the *hoofdpenghoeloe* (chief penghulu) Hadji Abdoelah, of whom the professor wrote in his biting prose that the second was *"een schaamteloozen, onzedelijken, even brutalen als onkundigen schelm"* (a shameless, immoral, as brazen as ignorant rogue), the son in Atjeh of an Indisch beggar and a woman of bad life. The then governor of Atjeh took the part of the *hoofddjaksa* against Snouck's strictures. These men, and the *pretendent-sultan* whom *men te kwader ure had vergund te Koetaradja te wonen* (had been permitted, at an evil hour, to live at Koetaradja) — banished in [[August]] [[1907]] — gave the capital its political atmosphere of intrigue. The pretendent-sultan's wife was photographed after her capture; the pretendent himself was sent away under the escort of [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]].

## The chabar angin

To Koetaradja came the *chabar angin* — the wind-rumour, the running news — from every corner of Atjeh. When in [[February]] [[1899]] the body of [[Teukoe Oemar]] lay near [[Meulaboh]], the news reached the staff at Koetaradja through the great forest and mountain country of central Atjeh between the Wojla and Tangsé rivers — borne by runners who handed the message from *keudé* to *keudé* at eighty kilometres a day. When [[Scheepens]] was murdered at Sigli in [[October]] [[1913]] the *chabar angin* came by express-tram — the *hondenhoo-o-ielee* of the Atjehtram, whose locomotive that day made the record run of its life, the machinist lashed to his machine, two doctors aboard, swaying and lurching over the narrow-gauge track to Sigli. When [[T. Radja Sabi]] submitted at [[Lho Seumawé]] on [[13 March]] [[1919]] the news raced through the North and East coasts as *the topic of the day*, and Koetaradja *nam maatregelen* (took measures). After the *soos* fuiven on Saturday nights the officers and their wives drove out to [[Peutjoet]] in the Sunday morning to stand among the white stones.

## Significance

Koetaradja is the *vanishing point* of Zentgraff's Atjeh — the place where the war's command sat, where its dead were buried, where its politics were made. It is the city from which [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] set out for [[Aneukgalong]] at midnight on [[28 March]] [[1896]], the city to which [[Scheepens]]' body was brought for burial under the trees at Peutjoet, the city that [[T. Radja Sabi]] reached — *halfverwilderde mensch* — to be put at the government's cost on the schoolbench so that *men iemand te maken die later oeléëbalang zou kunnen worden*. In [[1930]] the Legercommandant, luit.-generaal H. A. Cramer, came to Koetaradja for the *Kandoeri Rajeu* (great slametan) of the fortieth anniversary of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussée]], and every available officer of the corps in Atjeh was drawn to the capital for the day.

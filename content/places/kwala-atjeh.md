**Kwala Atjeh** (modern Kuala Aceh) is the *kwala* — the river-mouth — of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] on the north coast, and one of the three corners of the *strategische driehoek* (strategic triangle) [[Kwala Atjeh]]–[[Kota Radja]]–[[Oeleh Leh]] that defined the Dutch perimeter around the captured [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]]. Borel attributes the formation of the triangle to [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] in May [[1874]], explicitly correcting those who credited it to [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]. Its seaward position gave it command of the river-mouth approach to the Kraton and made it, with the [[Marine-benteng|marine-benteng]] half-way up the right bank, the natural northern anchor of the Dutch right bank.

## The strategic corner

After the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], Dutch sea-communication with the interior depended on the Atjeh-rivier, whose mouth at Kwala Atjeh had to be held if any supplies were to reach the garrison. The river's mouth became *onbevaarbaar* (unnavigable) during the *kwaden moesson* (bad monsoon) — the situation that forced the Dutch to develop Oeleh Leh as an alternative landing — but in fair weather Kwala Atjeh remained the principal debarkation-point for heavy stores and artillery. From Kwala Atjeh the *waterweg via de Soengei Aroesan* was, in Borel's phrase, "geruimen tijd voor ons van onschatbare waarde," carrying almost all vivres, munitie, geschut, and bouwmaterialen up to the Kraton.

## The threat of May 1874

On [[21 May]] [[1874]] the Dutch learned that the enemy was working between [[Penajoeng]] and the Marine-benteng with the goal of "zich daar te verschansen en de vaart op de rivier onveilig te maken" — to entrench himself there and render the river-traffic unsafe. The threat to the Kwala Atjeh–K. Radja river line was direct. Borel notes that the failure to build a *zeehoofd* at Oeleh Leh left the Dutch entirely dependent on Kwala Atjeh at exactly the moment the enemy understood its importance. By the time the *strategische driehoek* was completed in May [[1875]], with the continuous line of posts running from Oeleh Leh through the western kampongs to [[Longbatta-Mesigit]] and back to Kwala Atjeh by way of the river-bank posts, Kwala Atjeh had become the well-protected apex of the triangle's base.

**Paja Djorok** — also *Paja Jorok* — is one of the two *Semnenboschcomplexen* (Sumatran-pine forest complexes) — together with [[Blangtekoer]] — in which the trial exploitation of [[Pinus Merkusii]] turpentine began on [[14 September]] [[1924]] under the [[Besluit van 27 June 1924 on Atjeh-turpentine exploitation|Besluit of 27 June 1924]]. The forest complex is named in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] as one of the two sites of the early trial exploitation, and as the eastern of the two complexes separated by the river [[Baleq]] — from which the [[Baleq|Baleq enterprise]] took its name.

## The forest complex

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that Paja Djorok and Blangtekoer were *betrekkelijk dicht gelegen bij den grooten weg van Takengon naar Bireuen (Atjeh)* — *located relatively close to the main road from Takengon to Bireuen (Atjeh)*. The location of the two forest complexes near the Takengon-Bireuen road was the principal logistical advantage of the site, since it allowed the transport of the balsam — the raw resinous exudate of the tapped pines — from the forest to the processing factory. The two complexes were *van elkaar gescheiden door een rivier, de Baleq* — *separated from each other by a river, the Baleq* — and it was from this river that the trial enterprise took its name.

## The trial of 14 September 1924

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the trial exploitation of [[Pinus Merkusii]] turpentine began on [[14 September]] [[1924]] — *Op 14 September d.o.v. werd die proef ingezet in de Semnenboschcomplexen Paja Djorok en Blangtekoer* — *On 14 September of that year the trial was begun in the Sumatran-pine forest complexes Paja Djorok and Blangtekoer*. The trial was undertaken by *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* on behalf of the *Dienst van het Boschwezen*, and it was funded by the Regeering under the [[Besluit van 27 June 1924 on Atjeh-turpentine exploitation|Besluit of 27 June 1924]]. The results of the trial were sufficient that, in [[1926]], the Regeering resolved to convert the trial into a definitive exploitation as a separate division of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* with effect from [[1 January]] [[1927]].

## Significance

Paja Djorok's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as one of the two original sites of the trial exploitation of [[Pinus Merkusii]] turpentine in the Netherlands Indies — the forest complex where the early trial samples of Atjeh-turpentine were first produced, and where the *quarre* tapping method was first applied in Atjehnese conditions. The forest complex's location near the Takengon-Bireuen road was the principal logistical advantage that made the trial possible, and its separation from [[Blangtekoer]] by the river [[Baleq]] gave the enterprise its name.

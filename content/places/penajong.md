**Penajong** was the Dutch entrenched camp occupied after the march to the [[Aceh River]] during the [[Second Dutch Expedition to Aceh]].

## Historical setting

It served as a base for the advance on the [[Baiturrahman Grand Mosque|Missigit]] and [[Kraton of Aceh]], was attacked by Acehnese forces, and later received chiefs and cultivators seeking permission to harvest rice. It lay in the Kota Radja area, now [[Banda Aceh]].

## Modern context

The source situates [[Penajong]] in the historical landscape of [[Aceh]]; historical spellings follow the [[1879]] Dutch text.

## Account from Van Swieten (1879)

[[Jan van Swieten|Lieutenant-General Van Swieten]], in *De waarheid over onze vestiging in Atjeh* ([[1879]]), provided a detailed account of Penajoeng's occupation and role. On [[18 December]] [[1873]], a Dutch column under Lieutenant-Colonel [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] reached Penajoeng after fighting along the east bank of the [[Atjeh River|Atjeh river]], including an engagement at kampong Lamara. The position became the main headquarters and base camp for the expeditionary force.

### The Fire of 19 December 1873

The day after the Dutch occupation, the house of [[Toengkoe Kadli Malikoel Adil|Toengkoe Kali]] — the Sultan's acting *bandhara* and second father-in-law — was set on fire under mysterious circumstances. The house, described by [[Kolonel Wiggers van Kerchem|Colonel Wiggers van Kerchem]] (who was first to take possession) as "a fine, substantial plank-built house," was destroyed along with the Acehnese state archive stored within it. Toengkoe Kali fled into the interior, his confidence in the Dutch destroyed.

Van Swieten regarded the fire as one of the most consequential misfortunes of the campaign: "We lost with Toengkoe Kali a means of contact with many chiefs who, like him, did not want the war and were inclined to accept our political arrangements."

### Headquarters Functions

From Penajoeng, Van Swieten directed the final operations against the Kraton, issued his proclamations of [[28 January]] and [[31 January]] [[1874]], and delivered his farewell address of [[20 April]] [[1874]]. The position remained the seat of the Dutch military and civil command after the departure of the main force, under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Colonel Pel]].

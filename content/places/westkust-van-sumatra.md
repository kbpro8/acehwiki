**The Westkust van Sumatra** (West Coast of Sumatra) was the long stretch of Sumatran coast from [[Singkel|Singkel]] in the north to [[Silebar|Sillebar]] in the south, whose pepper, camphor, gold and benzoin made it one of the two great theatres of the Dutch-Acehnese contest — the other being the [[Tinkwartieren|Tin Quartiers]] of the Malay Peninsula. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* the Westkust is the central subject of the entire narrative on blad 4 (1684) and the first section of blad 5 (post-1684), and its history from [[1641]] to [[1795]] is the spine of the Westkust van Sumatra chapter.

## The pre-Compagnie Atjehsche Westkust

Before the Compagnie's arrival the Westkust was under the suzerainty of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]]. The Atjehsche sultans — most prominently [[Iskandar Muda of Atjeh|Iskandar Muda]] (1607–1636) — exercised their authority through Atjehsche governors (*gouverneurs*) stationed in the pepper ports. The four principal pepper ports were [[Tikoe]], [[Priaman]], [[Padang]] and [[Indrapoera]]; the northern ports were [[Baros]] and [[Singkel]]; the southern were [[Salido]], [[Painan]], [[Taroesan]] and [[Silebar]] (the last under Bantam).

## The 1641-1663 Painansch Contract era

The Westkust passed through four overlapping phases between [[1641]] and [[1663]]. The first phase (1641–1649) was the era of the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (15 February 1641)|Schouten treaty]] of [[15 February]] [[1641]], which gave the Compagnie commercial privileges in the four pepper ports under continued Atjehsche sovereignty. The second phase (1649–1656) was the era of the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (November 1649)|Truytman treaties]] of November [[1649]], which extended the privilege to the whole Westkust, and of the [[Blockade of Atjeh (1656)|1656 blockade]] that the Compagnie ordered in response to Atjehsche violation. The third phase (1656–1659) was the era of the [[Arrest of Anthony van Voorst|arrest of Van Voorst]] and the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (5 October 1659)|1659 peace]]. The fourth phase (1659–1663) was the era of the secret [[Jan van Groenewegen|Van Groenewegen]] negotiations of [[1662]] and the [[Painansch Contract]] of [[6 July]] [[1663]].

## The 1663-1684 consolidation

The Painansch Contract system was consolidated by the campaigns of [[Jacob Cau|Jacob Cau]] (1664–1665), [[Abraham Versprect|Abraham Versprect]] (1666), [[Jacob Jorisse Pits|Jacob Jorisse Pits]] (1666–1677), [[Melchior Hurdt|Melchior Hurdt]] (1677–1680), [[Laurens Pit de Jonge|Laurens Pit de Jonge]] (1680) and [[Jan van Leene|Jan van Leene]] (1680–1682). The [[Padang Conference (29 August 1680)|Padang Conference of 29 August 1680]] was the high-water mark; the [[Tikoe submission (20-21 June 1684)|Tikoe submission of 20–21 June 1684]] was the closing act. After [[1684]] the Westkust was, in form, a Dutch protectorate under the nominal suzerainty of the king of [[Menangkabau]].

## The post-1684 Westkust

After [[1684]] the Westkust entered a long eighteenth-century phase of Compagnie government. The four-factory system ([[Padang]], [[Salido|Poeloe Tjingkoek/Salido]], [[Priaman]], [[Tikoe]]) was reduced in [[1698]] when the posts at [[Priaman]], [[Ajerhadji]] and [[Taroesan]] were withdrawn; restored in [[1743]] after the Westkust war of 1740; retrenched in the 1770s; and finally surrendered to the English in [[1784]]–[[1795]] during the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]]'s late-eighteenth-century crisis. The English period at [[Benkoelen]], [[Batang Kapas]] and [[Silebar]] — opened in [[1685]] — lasted until the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1824)|1824 Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London]].

## Significance

The Westkust van Sumatra is the central theatre of the Dutch-Acehnese contest. The 1641–1684 cycle of conflict — opened by the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|sultane]]'s accession and closed by the [[Tikoe submission (20-21 June 1684)|Tikoe submission]] — is the spine of the entire Westkust story, and the [[Painansch Contract]] of [[6 July]] [[1663]] is its central instrument. After [[1684]] the Westkust ceases to be primarily a Dutch-Acehnese theatre and becomes primarily an Anglo-Dutch one, but the institutions of the Westkust system — the Painansch Contract, the panglima-radja office, the four-factory system, the Salido gold mines — persist through the entire VOC period.

**Glé Broeë** — written in Dutch as also *Glé Broe*, *Glé Broë* — was the principal Achehnese defensive position east of [[Kroeng-Kali]], attacked by the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Hansen column on 22–23 June 1896]] in the closing operation of the first semester of [[1896]]. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the Glé Broeë stelling was a complex of six fortifications — Koeta Lampoe Taroem, Toei Gloempang, Glé Broeë Siem, Boeng, Koeta Lampoe Djoeng, and Teungkoe Ba+ Riwat — with a total garrison of approximately 200 men. The Glé Broeë stelling was the eastern anchor of the Achehnese defensive line that included the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and the [[Tjot Karieng]] stelling, and was attacked from the rear by the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Hansen column]] that had landed at [[Ladoeng]] on [[23 June]] [[1896]].

## Description and Location

Glé Broeë lay to the east of [[Kroeng-Kali]] in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]], at the eastern edge of the country between Kroeng-Kali and the east coast. The Glé Broeë stelling was a complex of six fortifications: Koeta Lampoe Taroem, Toei Gloempang, Glé Broeë Siem, Boeng, Koeta Lampoe Djoeng, and Teungkoe Ba+ Riwat. The total garrison was approximately 200 men. The stelling was bounded on the west by the kampong and *benteng* of [[Kroeng-Kali]] and on the east by the mountain wall and the east coast. The [[Tjot Karieng]] (Tjat Karieng) hill to the east of Kroeng-Kali was the site of an associated Achehnese *stelling* — the southern part of which belonged to the III Moekims Lam-Rebo — that was occupied by the 14e Bataljon. The Glé Broeë stelling was the eastern anchor of the Achehnese defensive line that included the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]].

## Historical Background

The Glé Broeë stelling had been built up by the Achehnese over years as the eastern bastion of their defensive line in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]. The stelling was a complex of six *versterkingen* — Koeta Lampoe Taroem, Toei Gloempang, Glé Broeë Siem, Boeng, Koeta Lampoe Djoeng, and Teungkoe Ba+ Riwat — with a total garrison of approximately 200 men. The *benteng* [[Glé Broe|Glé Broë]] (presumably the same as Glé Broeë) had been recaptured on [[4 August]] [[1895]] by the *legioen* of [[Teuku Umar|T. Djohan]] in the course of his operations as [[Panglima Prang Besar]]. The stelling was the eastern anchor of the Achehnese defensive line in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and was associated with the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and the [[Tjot Karieng]] stelling. The *Koeta T. Rajoet* *benteng* — also recaptured on [[4 August]] [[1895]] — was associated with the same complex.

## Role in the 1896 Operations

The Glé Broeë stelling was the object of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Hansen column of 22–23 June 1896]] — the closing operation of the first semester of [[1896]]. On [[22 June]] [[1896]] the [[H. M. Merapi|H. M. Merapi]], the *Gouvernementsstoomer Condor*, and the *Albatros* embarked [[G. A. Hansen|Hansen]]'s column at [[Oleh-leh]] and sailed for [[Ladoeng]]. On [[23 June]] [[1896]] the column landed at Ladoeng in 4 *échelons* led by Lt. ter zee 1e kl. [[van den Broek]], crossed the *Kroeng-Angin* river at 1:45 PM, and was blocked by the *Aloer Rampoenoen* stream (bridge started 9:10 AM). The column then advanced against the Glé Broeë stelling from the rear, in coordination with a maréchaussée column under [[J. W. C. Vuijkk|Luitenant Vuijkk]] that had crossed [[Glé Taroempas]] and descended into the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] from the east, and with the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] of the 12e Bataljon that stormed the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* on the same day. The [[Tjot Karieng]] stelling to the east of Kroeng-Kali was occupied by the 14e Bataljon. The operation was supported by the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine]] from the [[Kroeng-Rababaai|Kroeng-Raba bay]]. The Glé Broeë stelling was the principal objective of the operation, and its fall — together with the fall of the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* — broke the back of organised Achehnese defence in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]].

## Significance

The Glé Broeë stelling was the eastern bastion of the Achehnese defensive line in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and the principal objective of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Hansen column of 22–23 June 1896]]. The combined land-and-sea operation against Glé Broeë — coordinated with the storming of the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* by the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] and the maréchaussée advance through [[Glé Taroempas]] — was the most-complex Dutch operation of the first semester of [[1896]] and demonstrated the Dutch ability to strike at Achehnese strongpoints from multiple directions. The fall of Glé Broeë broke the back of organised Achehnese defence in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and concluded the first semester of [[1896]].

**The VI Moekims** — the "Six Mukims" — were one of the smaller mukim-clusters of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — the northernmost triangle of [[Sumatra]] that formed the heartland of the [[Sultanate of Atjeh|Atjehnese sultanate]]. The VI Moekims formed the western segment of the territory enclosed by the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* after the Dutch retreat from the interior of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1884]], and they were — together with the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] — one of the principal administrative subdivisions of the late sultanate and of the Dutch colonial order that succeeded it. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the VI Moekims were also the western power-base of [[Teuku Umar]] (*Toekoe Oemar*) in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], including the kampongs of [[Lam-Pagar]], Lam-Badak, [[Lam-Baroe]], [[Lam-Tengah]], and [[Lam-Isi]], and together with the neighbouring [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] formed the principal theatre of Umar's defensive *stellingen* against the Dutch counter-offensive of May [[1896]].

## The political structure

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the VI Moekims — like the other mukim-clusters of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — were governed by an *[[Uleebalang|oeloebalang]]* who exercised temporal authority over the mukim's *imam* and *teungku* religious officials. The VI Moekims were, in the late *concentratie* period, governed by [[Toekoe Nja Din]] — the wife of [[Toekoe Oemar]] who *bestuurde feitelijk de VI Moekims* ("actually governed the VI Moekims") while her husband pursued his military and political career along the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]] and in the upper valley of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]]. The governance of the VI Moekims by a woman — the wife of the principal Dutch auxiliary of the late *concentratie* period — is one of the principal documented instances of the way in which *adat*-based Atjehnese authority could place a woman at the head of a strategically critical district.

## The strategic position

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the VI Moekims formed the western segment of the territory enclosed by the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* — and that they were the territory immediately adjacent to the western side of the Dutch perimeter. The VI Moekims were thus the principal contact zone between the Dutch-held coast and the Atjehnese interior, and the principal line of communication between the *geconcentreerde linie* and the upper valley where the religious resistance was organised. The VI Moekims were, in the album's narrative of the [[1896]] crisis, the territory of [[Toekoe Nja Din|Nja Din]] that was first invaded by the bandit [[Habib Long]] — the incursion that gave [[Toekoe Oemar]] the *gelegenheid* (opportunity) to *orde en rust te handhaven en de Moslemin te verdrijven* (maintain order and peace and drive out the Moslemin), and that was the immediate occasion of his appointment as Dutch auxiliary.

## The 1896 crisis

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, when [[Toekoe Oemar]] defected from the Dutch alliance in [[March]] [[1896]], the VI Moekims — under [[Toekoe Nja Din|Nja Din]]'s day-to-day rule — became one of the principal theatres of the ensuing crisis. The *Moslemin* (religious-warrior bands) who had been driven out of the VI Moekims by Oemar's earlier purges now returned, and Oemar's defection placed the entire Dutch outer line in jeopardy. The VI Moekims were thus the western anchor of the crisis, and the territory whose loss would have unhinged the entire Dutch perimeter.

## Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

The VI Moekims lay on the west coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] immediately to the north-west of the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]], adjoining the *landschap* of [[Lepong]] on the west coast. The six mukims contained the kampongs of [[Lam-Pagar]] (whose population was known as *bijsonder krijgshaftig* — "particularly warlike"), Lam-Badak, [[Lam-Baroe]] (where the versterking *Balei Lam Baroe* stood in the Noordrand), [[Lam-Tengah]] (with its *versterking* and *lilla* — small swivel cannon), and [[Lam-Isi]] (strongly defended by *volk uit Daja* with Beaumontgeweren). The region was bounded on the south by the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] and the heights of [[Boekit Seboen]] and [[Lam-Poelau]], and on the west by the sea-coast of [[Lepong]] and the [[Kroeng Raba]] bay. The [[Belang Kala-pas]] gave access south-westward to the pepper gardens of [[Monbroeh]] where the women of the VI Moekims also refuge.

## Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

The VI Moekims had been the western heartland of [[Teuku Umar]]'s authority throughout the period [[1893]]–[[1896]] when he served the [[Dutch]] as [[Panglima Prang Besar]]. From these mukims Umar drew his most reliable warriors, and after his appointment he kept his dwelling at [[Lam-Pisang]] in the adjoining [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] but maintained his strategic centre of gravity in the VI. The *volk uit Daja* (people from Daja) of [[Lam-Isi]], armed with Dutch breech-loading rifles, were among the best-equipped of Umar's warriors. After his [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]] the VI Moekims declared themselves *afgevallen* (defected) with Umar and prepared *stellingen* — the *versterkingen* (fortifications) of [[Lam-Isi]], [[Lam-Tengah]], and [[Lam-Baroe]] together with the trenches at [[Lam-Pagar]] and the *boschranden* (forest edges) of [[Lam-Awi]] — to resist the expected Dutch attack.

## Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

The VI Moekims were the principal target of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]]. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] a colonne under [[J. F. T. van Bloemen Waanders|Majoor Van Bloemen Waanders]] attacked [[Lam-Isi]], which was taken at 2:40 NM by the 12e and 14e Bataljons after heavy fighting; on the same day the 5e compagnie of the 12e Bataljon fought through the [[Kloof van Beradin]] and the 6e Bataljon moved up against [[Lam-Tengah-Oost]]. On [[24 May]] [[1896]] the 5e Bataljon captured [[Lam-Pisang]] and Umar's dwelling; on [[25 May]] [[1896]] the 6e Bataljon's 3e compagnie stormed the *stelling* Daman–[[Lam-Tjroeng]]–[[Lam-Poelau]]. The women of the VI Moekims, including [[Tjoet Nja Din]], fled during the bombardment to [[Monbroeh]] in the [[Belang Kala-pas]]. On [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] a two-colonne operation under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] re-entered the VI Moekims, occupying [[Lam-Pagar]], Lam-Badak, [[Lam-Goeroen]], [[Lam-Manjang]], [[Lam-Tengah-Oost]], [[Lam-Toetoe]], [[Lam-Njoeng]], and [[Lam-Awi]]. On [[5 June]] [[1896]] [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta|Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata]] was killed in the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] by a maréchaussée patrol.

## Significance

The VI Moekims' significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the western anchor of the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* — the territory whose governance by [[Toekoe Nja Din]] placed a woman at the head of a strategically critical district, and whose invasion by [[Habib Long]] was the immediate occasion of [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s appointment as Dutch auxiliary. The VI Moekims' role in the [[1896]] crisis — as the territory first invaded by the *Moslemin* after Oemar's defection — places them as the western anchor of the spring [[1896]] crisis. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* adds that the VI Moekims were the centre of gravity of [[Teuku Umar]]'s military power and the principal geographic object of the [[1896]] Dutch counter-offensive. The collapse of his *stellingen* there in May [[1896]] compelled Umar to withdraw eastward into [[Lepong]] and the [[Belang Kala-pas]], and opened the way for the [[Marine Bombardment of Lepong (25 May, 4–5, 8–9 June 1896)|marine bombardments of Lepong]] of [[25 May]] and [[4 June]]–[[5 June]], [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]]. The VI Moekims remained, however, the symbolic core of Umar's legitimacy: his [[chandoeri]] (council) of approximately [[20 June]] [[1896]] at [[Lepong]] appointed new *panglima's* drawn principally from the VI.

**The VI Moekims** — the "Six Mukims" — were one of the smaller mukim-clusters of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — the northernmost triangle of [[Sumatra]] that formed the heartland of the [[Sultanate of Atjeh|Atjehnese sultanate]]. The VI Moekims formed the western segment of the territory enclosed by the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* after the Dutch retreat from the interior of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1884]], and they were — together with the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] — one of the principal administrative subdivisions of the late sultanate and of the Dutch colonial order that succeeded it.

## The political structure

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the VI Moekims — like the other mukim-clusters of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — were governed by an *[[Uleebalang|oeloebalang]]* who exercised temporal authority over the mukim's *imam* and *teungku* religious officials. The VI Moekims were, in the late *concentratie* period, governed by [[Toekoe Nja Din]] — the wife of [[Toekoe Oemar]] who *bestuurde feitelijk de VI Moekims* ("actually governed the VI Moekims") while her husband pursued his military and political career along the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]] and in the upper valley of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]]. The governance of the VI Moekims by a woman — the wife of the principal Dutch auxiliary of the late *concentratie* period — is one of the principal documented instances of the way in which *adat*-based Atjehnese authority could place a woman at the head of a strategically critical district.

## The strategic position

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the VI Moekims formed the western segment of the territory enclosed by the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* — and that they were the territory immediately adjacent to the western side of the Dutch perimeter. The VI Moekims were thus the principal contact zone between the Dutch-held coast and the Atjehnese interior, and the principal line of communication between the *geconcentreerde linie* and the upper valley where the religious resistance was organised. The VI Moekims were, in the album's narrative of the [[1896]] crisis, the territory of [[Toekoe Nja Din|Nja Din]] that was first invaded by the bandit [[Habib Long]] — the incursion that gave [[Toekoe Oemar]] the *gelegenheid* (opportunity) to *orde en rust te handhaven en de Moslemin te verdrijven* (maintain order and peace and drive out the Moslemin), and that was the immediate occasion of his appointment as Dutch auxiliary.

## The 1896 crisis

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, when [[Toekoe Oemar]] defected from the Dutch alliance in [[March]] [[1896]], the VI Moekims — under [[Toekoe Nja Din|Nja Din]]'s day-to-day rule — became one of the principal theatres of the ensuing crisis. The *Moslemin* (religious-warrior bands) who had been driven out of the VI Moekims by Oemar's earlier purges now returned, and Oemar's defection placed the entire Dutch outer line in jeopardy. The VI Moekims were thus the western anchor of the crisis, and the territory whose loss would have unhinged the entire Dutch perimeter.

## Significance

The VI Moekims' significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the western anchor of the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* — the territory whose governance by [[Toekoe Nja Din]] placed a woman at the head of a strategically critical district, and whose invasion by [[Habib Long]] was the immediate occasion of [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s appointment as Dutch auxiliary. The VI Moekims' role in the [[1896]] crisis — as the territory first invaded by the *Moslemin* after Oemar's defection — places them as the western anchor of the spring [[1896]] crisis.

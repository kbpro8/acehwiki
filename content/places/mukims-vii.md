The **Mukims VII** — more commonly the **VII Mukims** — is a grouping of seven *mukims* which constitutes one of the principal uleebalangships of the [[XXII Mukims|Sagi of the XXII Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh]]. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, Snouck Hurgronje invokes the VII Mukims chiefly in connection with the residence and death of a member of the Achehnese royal house, and with the political standing of its uleebalang.

## The Death of Tuanku Asem

The author records that **Tuanku Asem** — a member of the reigning house of Acheh, and an exception to the majority of the men of royal blood — "died [[1897|January 1897]] at [[Padang Tiji]] in the Mukims VII." Tuanku Asem, "according to all who know him, is an exception to the majority of the men of royal blood in Acheh. He speaks Malay and a little Arabic, understands something of English, and can discuss the religious questions of the day from a more liberal standpoint than that of the pandits." The place of his death thus associates the VII Mukims, and the village of Padang Tiji within it, with a current of reformist and cosmopolitan thought in late nineteenth-century Acheh.

## The Uleebalang of the VII Mukims

The chief of the VII Mukims was, in the period described by Snouck Hurgronje, [[Teuku Ba'et]] — the uleebalang whose power and influence, alongside those of [[Panglima Polem]], were "among the most ancient and most firmly established of all that now exist in Great Acheh." In the sagi of the [[XXII Mukims]] the preponderance did not alternate, as in the other sagis, between one uleebalang and another; rather, the two houses of [[Panglima Polem|the Panglima]] and of [[Teuku Ba'et]] stood pre-eminent, the latter rising within his own sagi as a chief "who thought himself no whit his inferior." At the election of a new sultan, the scale was turned not so much by the support of the *panglimas* as by that of the real wielders of power in their sagis; and a tradition recorded by Achehnese authorities on adat gives a list of "twelve uleebalangs who appoint and dethrone princes," in which [[Teuku Ba'et]] of the VII Mukims appears for the [[XXII Mukims]].

## Padang Tiji and the Mukims VII

The village of [[Padang Tiji]], situated within the VII Mukims, gives its name to the locality in which Tuanku Asem died. The connection of a royal personage with this upland district of the [[XXII Mukims]] reflects the way in which the members of the Achehnese royal house dispersed themselves among the uleebalangships of the country, drawing their maintenance from the lands of the chiefs and forming, in each district, a small aristocracy of blood distinct from the local uleebalang.

**Aloer Djamboe** was a plantation estate in [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] in [[Oostkust van Atjeh|East Atjeh]], lying at 50 metres above sea level, on which part of the collection of bird skins described in *[[Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh]]* by [[F. C. van Heurn]] and [[R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg]] was gathered, and on which the earlier, second Atjeh collection of Van Heurn — described in *[[Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn|Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn]]* by Snouckaert — was gathered in [[September]]–[[November]] [[1920]].

## The estate and its management

The estate stood under the management of the agency of the [[Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam|Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam]], which granted the collectors permission and all requested facilities. The birds were collected *op zwaar heuvelland, aan de grens van plantage en oorspronkelijk bosch* — "on heavy hill country at the boundary of plantation and original forest" — the transitional situation that the authors held to be the most productive for tropical collecting, since it gave access both to the typical primeval-forest species and to those of the cultivated zone.

Aloer Djamboe lay in the district the authors singled out for its scenery: [[Tamiang|Tamiang]], the frontier province with the well-forested *afdeeling* [[Langkat|Langkat]] of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]], where the densely overgrown hill country fringing the mountains reached the coast, and where the many rivers debouching into the [[Aroe Bay|Aroe-baai]] admitted sea water far inland at flood, producing steep hills overgrown with primeval forest girdled at the foot by mangrove.

## The second Atjeh collection of 1920

The estate was the site of the second of the two avifaunal collections made by [[F. C. van Heurn|Van Heurn]] in Atjeh in [[1920]]. The first collection — described in the *[[Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen|Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen]]* — had been made in the highland region of [[Takengon|Lake Takengon]] (1200 metres) in [[Gajo-land|Gajo-land]]. The second — described in the *[[Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn|Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn]]* — was made at [[Alas Peurba|Alas Peurba]] (200 metres) and Aloer Djamboe (50 metres) in [[September]]–[[November]] [[1920]]. As Snouckaert records, *Ditmaal viel de keuze op veel lager gelegen localiteiten meer nabij de N.O.-kust* — "This time the choice fell on much lower-lying localities nearer the northeast coast."

At Aloer Djamboe, Van Heurn collected through [[November]] [[1920]], with specimens dated from early through late November. The latest date recorded in the second-collection paper is [[25 November]] [[1920]] (specimens of *Spilornis cheela bacha*, *Eurystomus orientalis*, *Zanclostomus javanicus*, *Chloropsis cyanopogon*, and *Arachnothera affinis* at Aloer Djamboe). The paper records specimens from a wide range of bird families collected at Aloer Djamboe, including the crested serpent-eagle, the broad-billed roller, several cuckoos, woodpeckers, broadbills, bulbuls, and spiderhunters.

## Deposit

The collection described in the *[[Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn|Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie]]* was deposited with the *Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut* — the Library of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] — in [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]].

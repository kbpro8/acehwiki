**Aloer Djamboe** was a plantation estate in [[Tamiang]] in [[Oostkust van Atjeh|East Atjeh]], lying at 50 metres above sea level, on which part of the collection of bird skins described in *[[Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh]]* by [[F. C. van Heurn]] and [[R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg]] was gathered.

The estate stood under the management of the agency of the [[Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam]], which granted the collectors permission and all requested facilities. The birds were collected *op zwaar heuvelland, aan de grens van plantage en oorspronkelijk bosch* — on heavy hill country at the boundary of plantation and original forest — the transitional situation that the authors held to be the most productive for tropical collecting, since it gave access both to the typical primeval-forest species and to those of the cultivated zone.

Aloer Djamboe lay in the district the authors singled out for its scenery: [[Tamiang]], the frontier province with the well-forested *afdeeling* [[Langkat]] of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]], where the densely overgrown hill country fringing the mountains reached the coast, and where the many rivers debouching into the [[Aroe Bay|Aroe-baai]] admitted sea water far inland at flood, producing steep hills overgrown with primeval forest girdled at the foot by mangrove.

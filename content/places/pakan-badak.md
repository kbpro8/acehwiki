**Pakan Badak** was a kampong and important market‑place in the interior of [[Acheh|Aceh]], situated in the [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot‑Atjeh]]. It functioned as a commercial hub with two large plank buildings on piles and, from [[31 December]] [[1875]] onward, as a Dutch military base for operations against the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] and later the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]]. Its location near the mountain passes of [[Glitaroem]] and [[Beradin]] made it a strategic waypoint for Dutch forces during the 1875–1876 expedition, notably for the column under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] that entered the gorge on the morning of [[25 January]] [[1876]].

## Location and Physical Description

Pakan Badak lay in the interior of Great Acheh, close to the passes of [[Glitaroem]] and [[Beradin]], which controlled access to the surrounding valleys. The market consisted of two parallel plank buildings, each about 35 metres long, raised on piles one metre above the ground. Both structures featured a continuous *voorgalerij* and were divided internally into compartments of 5–6 metres square, serving as lodgings for merchants. Adjacent to these were numerous other solid buildings arranged for trade, stocked with household goods—especially pots, pans and plates—in great quantity. Empty European‑made cupboards and chests with excellent locks were also noted, indicating a thriving commercial environment.

## Capture and Dutch Occupation (31 December 1875)

On [[26 December]] [[1875]] at five in the morning three Dutch columns left the hoofd‑stelling for the conquest of the [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] south‑west of [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. After taking the enemy fortifications at [[Mibouw|Mibouw]], [[Ketapan Doewa|Ketapan Doewa]] and [[Djempit|Djempit]], the forces occupied Pakan Badak. A Dutch report recorded:

> “In six days the strong enemy line opposite our posts south of Kota Radja had been broken, turned and abandoned by the enemy. We were wholly master of the VI Moekims and, by the occupation of Pakan Badak, in possession of an important point that was to serve as a base for operations against the IV Moekims.”

On [[31 December]] [[1875]] the troops reached the market itself. The observer wrote: “Everything bore witness at Pakan Badak to prosperity and to much trade. On the rather large pasar stood, in parallel direction, two plank buildings… Both had a continuous *voorgalerij* and were internally divided into compartments… In nearly all these rooms, usually serving as lodgings for the merchants, one found empty cupboards and chests of European make, solid and provided with excellent locks. Besides these two buildings there were many others, more or less solid, all evidently arranged for trade; household goods, especially pots, pans and plates, one found there in great quantity.”

## Base for Operations against the IV Moekims (January 1876)

From Pakan Badak the Dutch launched operations into the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]]. The principal defenders of the VI Moekims, such as [[Toekoe Nanta|Toekoe Nanta]] and [[Toekoe Tjihik Lamnga|Toekoe Tjihik Lamnga]], had fled there, but the head of the IV Moekims, [[Toekoe Lampassej|Toekoe Lampassej]], was allied with the Dutch. On [[15 January]] [[1876]] the advance into the IV Moekims began over the [[pas van Blang Kala|pas of Blang Kala]]. The troops returned to Pakan Badak on [[25 January]] [[1876]]; in the following ten days they reduced the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] from [[28 January]] [[1876]] to [[7 February]] [[1876]].

## Continued Military Activity (June 1878)

By the spring of [[1878]] the [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib]] resumed offensive actions. After [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] departed for [[Gedong|Gedong]], intelligence reported that the Habib intended a major attack with 2,000 men on the IV Moekims, targeting the posts at [[Kroëng Raba|Kroëng Raba]], [[Boekit Seboen|Boekit Seboen]] and Pakan Badak.

## Strategic Importance in the 1875–1876 Expedition

The interior position of Pakan Badak, coupled with its market facilities and proximity to mountain passes, rendered it an ideal staging ground. On the morning of [[25 January]] [[1876]] the column of marines under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] marched through the gorge toward Pakan Badak, forming the vanguard of the operation against the IX Moekims. Its capture not only secured a commercial centre but also provided the Dutch with a logistical hub from which to project power deeper into the Acehnese interior.

## Evacuation in the Concentration Strategy (February 1885)

Pakan-Badak was evacuated on [[28 February]] [[1885]] as part of the [[Concentration Decision of August 1884|concentration strategy]]. The post was not incorporated into the new geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position) and was among the outer positions abandoned in the systematic reduction of the defensive perimeter. The evacuation proceeded with minimal casualties: 1 killed and 6 wounded. Routes to the post, including the one from [[Lamdjamoe]], were no longer maintained by the Dutch following the evacuation.

**Lemboe** was a strongly fortified kampong at the southern end of the [[Pedir-dijk]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]], which Borel calls "the key" to the dike and therefore to the entire Dutch defensive line on the river. It was the scene of the bloody [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|battle of 25–26 December 1873]], abandoned immediately afterward on [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s order, and finally conquered for good on [[7 November]] [[1874]] in the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|operation led by overste Diepenbrock]]. Its recurrent importance — first fought over in [[1873]], finally held in [[1874]], reoccupied in [[1875]] — makes it, in Borel's narrative, the prime example of the cost of premature abandonment.

## The battle of December 1873

After eight idle days at [[Penajoeng]] following the [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing]], the Atjehneezen had entrenched everywhere along the river. On [[25 December|25]] and [[26 December]] [[1873]] strong Dutch columns rukten zuidwaarts to find a grasvlakte for the camp. They hit enemy stellingen at Lemboe and were "van alle zijden hevig beschoten" — heavily fired on from all sides. For hours Dutch troops were exposed on the open *sawah* (irrigated rice field) to "moordend vuur." After a "bloedigen strijd" Lemboe was taken, at a cost of nearly 150 gesneuvelden and gekwetsten. "Onbegrijpelijk genoeg" van Swieten immediately ordered the place abandoned, a major error later compounded when the same ground had to be re-fought at the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of 7 November 1874]].

## The final conquest and later posts

On [[7 November]] [[1874]] [[Kolonel Diepenbrock|overste Diepenbrock]] led two half-battalions and a field-artillery battery against Lemboe, attacking from Penajoeng and [[Langkroek]] across open sawah. Contrary to expectation, resistance was light; the western part of Lemboe, called [[Berauw]], with its main dike fortifications, fell. The Dutch lost Diepenbrock and about 20 men or coolies wounded and one officer's servant killed, and three posts were immediately built. The post of *Lemboe-zuid*, a large southern *rivier-benteng* armed with an 8 c.M. rifled gun, was added later. The original *Lemboe N.O.* post, low-lying and flooded, was relocated on [[10 March]] [[1875]] opposite [[Pinang]] and renamed *Nieuw Lemboe N.O.*; the intermediate post of *Lemboe Oost* was established on [[12 March]] [[1875]]. A night attack on Lemboe-N.O. on [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1876]] cost one officer and 22 wounded.

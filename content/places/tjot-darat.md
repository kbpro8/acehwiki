**Tjot Darat** (modern *Cot Darat*) is a bog-iron (swamp-ore) locality on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West coast of Atjeh]], recorded by [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]], [[B. B. Lindberg|Lindberg]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]] in [[1922]] and reproduced in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) in the description of the [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]] bog-iron deposits. Tjot Darat is one of the five supplementary bog-iron localities that the Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp survey recorded in the same general area as the principal Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj deposits but did not indicate on Van Bemmelen's map (Fig. 41).

## The 1922 Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp observation

Van Bemmelen's record of the Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj bog-iron deposits, drawn from the [[1922]] Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp report, includes the observation that "These swamp ores were also found on the [[Tjot Seumeureueng]], [[Tjot Meuseugit]], [[Tjot Regan]], Tjot Darat and [[Tjot Mane]] (not indicated on the map)." The five supplementary Tjot localities — including Tjot Darat — are accordingly part of the wider West-Atjeh bog-iron zone, of which the principal mapped localities are [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]] (30 and 12 km NW of [[Meulaboh]]).

For the full account of the bog-iron deposits and the 300,000–400,000-ton lump-ore reserve estimate see the entry on [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj]].

## The name "Tjot"

The Acehnese word *Tjot* (modern *Cot*) — meaning "hill" — is the standard prefix for the named hills of the West-Atjeh coast. The name *Tjot Darat* — literally "inland hill" or "mainland hill" (from Acehnese *darat* = "land", "mainland", from Malay *darat*) — is a typical toponym indicating a hill located inland from the coast, in contrast to the more seaward hills of the West-Atjeh littoral.

## Significance

Tjot Darat is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is one of the five supplementary bog-iron localities recorded by the [[1922]] Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp survey in the same general area as the principal [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]] deposits, and accordingly part of the wider West-Atjeh bog-iron zone. The locality is part of the only Atjeh iron-ore zone classified in the colonial geological literature as bog-iron (swamp-ore) deposits rather than as contact-metamorphic magnetite-hematite deposits, and its inclusion in the Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp [[1922]] report — despite its absence from Van Bemmelen's Fig. 41 map — establishes it as part of the documented colonial-era iron-ore record of Atjeh.

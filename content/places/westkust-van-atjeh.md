**Westkust van Atjeh** (West Coast of Atjeh) was an *afdeeling* (division) of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], administered by Assistent-Resident [[I. L. O'Brien]]. The Westkust covered the western coastline of the Atjeh territory, an area that had been one of the last regions to come under Dutch control during the Atjeh War and that continued to present significant administrative challenges due to its rugged terrain and dispersed population. The designation "Westkust" reflected the colonial practice of defining administrative units along coastal lines, a pattern characteristic of the *Buitenbezittingen* where Dutch authority had historically been established from the sea inward.

## Geography and Administrative Context

The Westkust van Atjeh encompassed the entire western littoral of the Atjeh peninsula, from the southern boundaries of the [[Groot-Atjeh]] interior to the western approaches of the [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] and [[Noordkust van Atjeh]] regions. This was a region of formidable topography: the Bukit Barisan mountain range ran parallel to the coastline, creating a narrow coastal plain intersected by river valleys that provided the primary routes of access into the interior highlands. The difficulty of overland communication along the Westkust—where roads were few and frequently impassable during the rainy season—meant that the colonial administration relied heavily on maritime connections to maintain contact between its various outposts. The dispersed nature of the population, settled in small coastal villages and upland *moekim* communities separated by dense tropical forest and mountainous terrain, posed persistent challenges to the effective exercise of Dutch administrative control.

The late pacification of the Westkust, relative to other parts of Atjeh, meant that by 1914 the region still bore the marks of its recent incorporation into the colonial state. Administrative structures were less firmly established than in [[Groot-Atjeh]] or the [[Noordkust van Atjeh]], and the Assistent-Resident at the Westkust had to contend with a population that had experienced a shorter period of colonial governance. The appointment of [[I. L. O'Brien]] as Assistent-Resident reflected the need for an experienced and capable administrator in this challenging post, where the demands of governance extended beyond routine administration to the ongoing consolidation of Dutch authority in areas that had only recently been brought under effective control.

## The 1913–1914 Reorganization and Merger with Groot-Atjeh

In the proposed administrative reorganization discussed at the Medan and Koeta-Radja meetings in [[January]] [[1914]], the Westkust van Atjeh was to be merged with [[Groot-Atjeh]] into a combined *afdeeling* "Groot-Atjeh benevens de Westkust van Atjeh" with an estimated population of 210,000. This merger reflected the practical administrative consideration that neither division alone had sufficient population to constitute a viable *afdeeling* in the new governance structure, while their geographic continuity made a combined unit logical. The Westkust, with its relatively sparse population spread along a difficult coastline, lacked the demographic weight to justify its own *afdeeling* under the reorganized system, which sought to create administrative units of sufficient size to support the institutional apparatus of a *Resident* and the associated bureaucracy.

The merger also reflected the understanding that the Westkust and Groot-Atjeh were, in many respects, complementary parts of a single geographic and administrative whole. The Westkust served as the maritime face of the interior highlands of Groot-Atjeh, and the two regions were connected by river valleys that ran from the mountains to the sea. Under the existing *Gouvernement* system, both fell under the authority of the Civiel en Militair Gouverneur at [[Koeta-Radja]], and their separate *afdeeling* status reflected the incremental nature of Dutch expansion rather than any fundamental administrative logic. The proposed merger thus represented a rationalization of boundaries that had been drawn during the military phases of the Atjeh War and that could be adjusted now that the territory was under consolidated colonial control.

## Significance in the Broader Reorganization

The fate of the Westkust van Atjeh was closely tied to the broader question of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]'s future within the proposed [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]]. The merger with Groot-Atjeh was part of a larger reorganization of the Atjeh *afdeelingen* that saw the [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] combined with the [[Noordkust van Atjeh]], the *onderafdeeling* [[Tamiang]] transferred to the Langkat division, and the [[Alaslanden]] proposed for addition to the Deli *afdeeling*. These boundary adjustments were designed to create *afdeelingen* of more uniform population size and to align administrative boundaries with economic and geographic realities, rather than with the historical patterns of military conquest that had produced the existing *afdeeling* structure. The Westkust's incorporation into a combined *afdeeling* with Groot-Atjeh was thus both a practical necessity and a statement of the new administrative principles guiding the reorganization.

## Account from *DLP-84-Atjeh* — The Steep Rugged Shoreline, the Bergweg, and the Primeval Forest

The *DLP-84-Atjeh* source frames the West Coast of Atjeh as the steep, rugged shoreline on the western side of [[Aceh|Atjeh]], contrasting sharply with the low, marshy [[East Coast of Aceh|East Coast of Aceh]]. The area presented difficult terrain that required extensive engineering for road construction. The Dutch colonial administration built the mountain road (Dutch: *bergweg*) along the coast, overcoming significant geological obstacles; this road, together with the [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajoweg]] cut through the highland jungle, linked coastal villages and facilitated the movement of troops and goods between the Westkust and the colonial capital at [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]]. Beyond the engineered routes, the West Coast retained patches of primeval forest (Dutch: *oerbos*) that provided a glimpse of the pre-colonial environment — the dense tropical rainforest that had covered the littoral before the Dutch military conquest and the subsequent expansion of smallholder and plantation cultivation.

**Buitenzorg** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

Swart remained continuously on operations in Atjeh until May [[1900]], when he was transferred to [[Buitenzorg]] in connection with his application for leave. There he heard indirectly that, though barely two years a captain, he had already been declared fit for major. His corps commander nevertheless "tested" the prospective leave-taker according to the rules of the trade — battalion school, field service, drill, administration — until he embarked on [[18 July]] aboard the *s.s. Lawoe* and disappeared for a year. He had been granted a year's European leave on [[6 July]] [[1900]] with retention of claim to promotion.

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

Before departure Swart stayed at the palace at [[Buitenzorg]], where Van Heutsz — feeling himself so often a prisoner of that palace — took him incognito to [[Batavia]] for an evening at the *Restaurant Versteeg* opposite Hotel De Nederlanden, "Haroun al-Rashid and his vizier"; the disguise failed, as ever more polite passers-by came to look at the Governor-General. Van Heutsz's parting words were: "one thing more … you have only to see to it that what happened to Van Daalen does not happen to you!" Swart answered: "I have a different character from Van Daalen." "Accoord," Van Heutsz replied, "I count on it, then, that Atjeh will come right. And then we shall say no more about it."

**Senelop** was a Dutch temporary military post established in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[1890s]] — a post that, together with [[Anagaloeng]] on its northern flank, formed the eastern anchor of the Dutch outer perimeter in the late *concentratie* period. The post was besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]] and was the site of two of the seven officer fatalities of the spring [[1896]] crisis — those of Lieutenant [[J. W. Vuijk|Vuijk]] on [[30 March]] [[1896]] and Lieutenant [[J. B. Zon|Zon]] on [[19 April]] [[1896]].

## The strategic position

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that Senelop was established in the [[XXII Moekims]] — the *sagi* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] that contained [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]], [[Gleiêng]], [[Montassik]] and [[Anagaloeng]] — and that it was, together with Anagaloeng, one of the principal temporary posts of the late *concentratie* period. The album's narrative of the [[1896]] crisis places Senelop as the post whose commandant heard the rumour of the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] and hastened — with 40 men under lieutenants [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]] and [[Ketjen]] — to relieve the patrol.

## The siege of March-April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]], the post at Senelop — together with that at [[Anagaloeng]] — was besieged by the forces of the defected warlord. The album's narrative records that the besiegers *bouwden tegenover onzen post te Lamreng het sterke Kaloet* — *built opposite our post at Lamreng the strong Kaloet* — a reference to the [[Kaloet]] fortification that was the principal Atjehnese strongpoint of the late *concentratie* period. The siege of Senelop was, in the album's chronology, the longest of the four sieges of the spring [[1896]] crisis — from [[March]] to [[April]] [[1896]].

## The two officer fatalities

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]]'s roll of the fallen of the spring [[1896]] crisis records that Senelop was the site of two of the seven officer fatalities:

- Lieutenant [[J. W. Vuijk]] — killed at Senelop on [[30 March]] [[1896]]
- Lieutenant [[J. B. Zon]] — killed at Senelop on [[19 April]] [[1896]]

The two fatalities make Senelop the costliest single post of the spring [[1896]] crisis, and the post whose siege produced the heaviest drain on the Dutch officer corps. The album also records that Major [[Okhuysen]] and fifty subordinates were wounded in the *verwoede gevecht dat om Senelop geleverd was* — the fierce battle that was fought around Senelop.

## The relief

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Senelop was relieved by the column that had previously relieved [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]] and [[Anagaloeng]] on [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1896]]. The relief of Senelop was the last of the four relief operations of April [[1896]], and it was followed by the dismantling of the post and the withdrawal of the garrison to the *geconcentreerde linie*.

## Significance

Senelop's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the costliest single post of the spring [[1896]] crisis — the post whose siege produced two of the seven officer fatalities of the spring. Its relief on [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1896]] — together with that of [[Anagaloeng]] — was the last of the four relief operations of April [[1896]], and it was the moment at which the Dutch completed the recovery of their outer perimeter.

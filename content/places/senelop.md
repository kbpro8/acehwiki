**Senelop** was a Dutch temporary military post established in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[1890s]] — a post that, together with [[Anagaloeng]] on its northern flank, formed the eastern anchor of the Dutch outer perimeter in the late *concentratie* period. The post was besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]] and was the site of two of the seven officer fatalities of the spring [[1896]] crisis — those of Lieutenant [[J. W. Vuijk|Vuijk]] on [[30 March]] [[1896]] and Lieutenant [[J. B. Zon|Zon]] on [[19 April]] [[1896]]. The post was finally evacuated on [[17 April]] [[1896]], when the 1e Colonne of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] lifted it in the heaviest fighting of the campaign — the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Battle of Senelop]] — and the engineers of [[1e Luit. N. Plantenga|1e Luit. Plantenga]] blew the bridge at [[Ana' Galong]] to seal the western *buitenlinie* behind the retreating column.

**Sennelop** was one of the Dutch forward posts of [[Groot-Atjeh]] which, [[P. H. R. Beuming]] records, were razed in the spring of [[1896]] after their garrisons had been relieved, before the [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)|Beradin operation]]. Beuming names it with [[Lamsoet]], [[Anak Galoeng]], [[Lambarih]], [[Biloel]], [[Lamkoenjit]], [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb|Toengkoep]] and the blockhouses lying between them, adding that in the razing *ook weer tal van verliezen aan onze zijde waren te betreuren* — many losses on the Dutch side were again to be lamented.

## The strategic position

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that Senelop was established in the [[XXII Moekims]] — the *sagi* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] that contained [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]], [[Gleiêng]], [[Montassik]] and [[Anagaloeng]] — and that it was, together with Anagaloeng, one of the principal temporary posts of the late *concentratie* period. The album's narrative of the [[1896]] crisis places Senelop as the post whose commandant heard the rumour of the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] and hastened — with 40 men under lieutenants [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]] and [[Ketjen]] — to relieve the patrol.

## The siege of March-April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]], the post at Senelop — together with that at [[Anagaloeng]] — was besieged by the forces of the defected warlord. The album's narrative records that the besiegers *bouwden tegenover onzen post te Lamreng het sterke Kaloet* — *built opposite our post at Lamreng the strong Kaloet* — a reference to the [[Kaloet]] fortification that was the principal Atjehnese strongpoint of the late *concentratie* period. The siege of Senelop was, in the album's chronology, the longest of the four sieges of the spring [[1896]] crisis — from [[March]] to [[April]] [[1896]].

## The two officer fatalities

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]]'s roll of the fallen of the spring [[1896]] crisis records that Senelop was the site of two of the seven officer fatalities:

- Lieutenant [[J. W. Vuijk]] — killed at Senelop on [[30 March]] [[1896]]
- Lieutenant [[J. B. Zon]] — killed at Senelop on [[19 April]] [[1896]]

The two fatalities make Senelop the costliest single post of the spring [[1896]] crisis, and the post whose siege produced the heaviest drain on the Dutch officer corps. The album also records that Major [[Okhuysen]] and fifty subordinates were wounded in the *verwoede gevecht dat om Senelop geleverd was* — the fierce battle that was fought around Senelop.

## The relief

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Senelop was relieved by the column that had previously relieved [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]] and [[Anagaloeng]] on [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1896]]. The relief of Senelop was the last of the four relief operations of April [[1896]], and it was followed by the dismantling of the post and the withdrawal of the garrison to the *geconcentreerde linie*.

## The evacuation of 17 April 1896 (ATJEH '96 account)

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the abandonment of the western [[buitenlinie]] was completed in a single coordinated two-colonne operation on [[17 April]] [[1896]]. The 1e Colonne of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] — with Chef van den Staf [[Kapitein van den generalen staf H. C. Kronouer|Kapt. Kronouer]] and the *voorhoede* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] — was tasked with the *opheffing* (lifting) of Senelop and [[Lamsoet]] — the most exposed and most contested of the western posts. The 2e Colonne of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] was tasked in parallel with the *opheffing* of [[Anak-Galoeng]] and [[Lam-Barih]].

Lam Soet was reached by the 1e Colonne at 8 a.m. without contact with the enemy, but the column then ran into a heavy enemy fortification extending to the [[Atjeh River]], and the day became the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Battle of Senelop]] — described by Kruisheer as "one of the heaviest days our troops experienced in [[1896]]." The 1e Colonne lost a 7 c.M. A. veld-gun, a 12 c.M. mortar, and a *mitrailleur*-chest with barrels; two artillery sergeants, [[Putman]] and [[Klaassen]], died of overexertion. Enemy losses at Senelop were 75 killed (including [[Habib Lhong]] and [[Panglima Nja Bintang]]) and 88 heavily plus 80 lightly wounded.

The evacuation of the western *buitenlinie* on [[17 April]] [[1896]] was the strategic nadir of the Dutch campaign in the first semester of [[1896]]. Three years of patient construction — the posts, the bridges, the network of allied *hoeloebalang* (territorial chiefs) — had been undone in three weeks. Yet the evacuation also marked the moment at which the Dutch stopped trying to hold an indefensible line and began to concentrate their forces for the counter-offensive. Within five weeks the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] would be launched.

## The demolition of the bridge at Ana' Galong

As the two colonnes withdrew from the western *buitenlinie* on [[17 April]] [[1896]], the engineers of [[1e Luit. N. Plantenga|1e Luit. Plantenga]] — who had been attached to the 1e Colonne — blew the bridge at [[Ana' Galong]]. The demolition was a deliberate act: the bridge, which had anchored the western line since the [[Conquest of Ana' Galong (30 November 1893)|conquest]] of [[November]] [[1893]], was the principal crossing of the [[Atjeh River]] in the western theatre, and its destruction sealed the western line behind the retreating column. The Achehnese would have to rebuild the crossing before they could threaten the consolidated line — a small but real strategic gain in an otherwise bleak operation.

## Additional ATJEH '96 account — garrison, siege, and 1896 operations

As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Senelop was a [[Dutch]] outpost and barrier against the [[V Moekims Montassik]], together with [[Kroeng Gloempang]] forming the eastern defensive line of the *buitenlinie*. Senelop was garrisoned in [[1895]] by 1 captain, 2 lieutenants, 75 men, 1 medical officer, 1 hospital attendant, and 12 *artilleristen* (artillerymen), and was the main objective of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s column on [[17 April]] [[1896]]. Senelop had been attacked on [[23 February]] [[1896]] (5:30–6:30 AM) by 40–50 guns and again on [[24 February]] [[1896]], was heavily shelled from [[Lam-Bada Anak Baté|Lam Bada]] from [[15 April]] [[1896]], was conquered in early [[1894]] by the [[V Moekims Montassik]] operations, was retaken without opposition by maréchaussée and the 3e Bataljon at 9:10 AM on [[2 May]] [[1896]] (after having been occupied by [[Panglima Polim]]'s *volk*), and was the site of the death of [[Habib Lhong]] on [[19 April]] [[1896]].

### Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

Senelop lay at the eastern end of the *buitenlinie*, with [[Kroeng Gloempang]] forming the barrier against the [[V Moekims Montassik]]. The *Moekim Lam Djampoe* of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] lay entirely within the Dutch post line. The garrison in [[1895]] consisted of 1 captain, 2 lieutenants, 75 men, 1 medical officer, 1 hospital attendant, and 12 *artilleristen*. The post commanded the road from the [[V Moekims Montassik]] into the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] and protected the [[blokhuis|blokhuizen]] (blockhouses) of [[Aloer Oe]] and [[Toei Selimbing]] that lay forward of it. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] the post was surrounded by enemy trenches.

### Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

Senelop had been conquered in early [[1894]] by the [[V Moekims Montassik]] operations of the *legioen* (auxiliary legion) of [[Teuku Umar|T. Djohan]] and the [[Dutch]] supporting columns. The post had been constructed to act as a barrier against the hostile *benden* (war-bands) of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and to dominate the road between the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] and the [[V Moekims Montassik]]. The *blokhuizen* of [[Aloer Oe]] and [[Toei Selimbing]] were forward extensions of the Senelop post, and the *sergeant der artillerie* [[Sergeant der artillerie son|son]] of the *blokhuis* Aloer Oe distinguished himself in the siege of [[28 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] by repairing the *mitrailleur* (machine gun) twice under fire.

### Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

Senelop was attacked on [[23 February]] [[1896]] (5:30–6:30 AM) by 40–50 guns and again on [[24 February]] [[1896]]. On [[14 March]] [[1896]] [[Kapitein J. H. G. Reurts|Kapitein Reurts]] also command. On [[28 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] the forward [[blokhuis|blokhuizen]] of [[Aloer Oe]] and [[Toei Selimbing]] were besieged; the *sergeant der artillerie* [[Sergeant der artillerie son|son]] repaired the *mitrailleur* twice under fire and later safely brought the *mitrailleur* and 20,800 cartridges back to Senelop on evacuation. On [[30 March]] [[1896]] the column of [[Majoor G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Van der Noordaa]] marched from [[Koeta Radja]] to relieve the *blokhuizen*, but at [[Lam Siti]] 1e luit. [[J. W. C. Vuijkk|Vuijkk]] was killed by hostile salvos after mistaking a helmeted enemy unit for friendly troops ("Deze vergissing werd hem noodlottig" — this mistake cost him his life). On [[31 March]] [[1896]] Senelop was heavily shelled: 7 of 15 enemy grenades from captured 8 cm bronze field guns hit, 2 inside the post (one in officers' quarters, one in NCOs' quarters). On [[15 April]] [[1896]] Senelop began to be heavily shelled from [[Lam-Bada Anak Baté|Lam Bada]]. On [[17 April]] [[1896]] Senelop was the main objective of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s first colonne in the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]], in which the [[Friendly-fire incident at Anak Baté (17 April 1896)|friendly-fire incident at Anak Baté]] killed 6 European fusiliers of the Dutch side through the mountain battery of Lt. Hoolboom. [[Habib Lhong]] was killed at Senelop on [[19 April]] [[1896]]. On [[2 May]] [[1896]] the [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversionary operation to Montassik]] also the old post of Senelop without opposition by maréchaussée and the 3e Bataljon at 9:10 AM, after [[Panglima Polim]]'s *volk* had withdrawn.

## Significance

Senelop's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the costliest single post of the spring [[1896]] crisis — the post whose siege produced two of the seven officer fatalities of the spring. Its relief on [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1896]] — together with that of [[Anagaloeng]] — was the last of the four relief operations of April [[1896]] according to the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]], and it was the moment at which the Dutch completed the recovery of their outer perimeter. In Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* chronology, however, the post was lifted on [[17 April]] [[1896]] as part of the coordinated evacuation of the western *buitenlinie*, and the day's fighting at Senelop was the heaviest single day's combat of the campaign. The discrepancy in dates between the two sources reflects the fluidity of the chronology in the spring [[1896]] crisis, and the difficulty of distinguishing between the relief of a post (its provisional reopening to communication) and its evacuation (its permanent abandonment and dismantling).

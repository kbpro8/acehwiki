**Senelop** was an Achehnese fortified position, afterwards a Dutch post, in [[Groot-Atjeh]], defended by the sister of the late [[Habib Samalanga]] and fifteen other women who had devoted themselves to death, stormed by [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] on [[12 March]] [[1894]], occupied by Dutch troops three days later, and relieved and lifted on [[18 April]] [[1896]].

## The storm of 12 March 1894

With [[Kroeng Gloempang]] at his back and supported by Dutch artillery, [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] advanced on [[12 March]] [[1894]] against Senelop, *waar de zuster van wijlen Habib Samalanga het bevel voerde en zich met 15 andere fanatieke vrouwen ten dood had gewijd* — where the sister of the late Habib Samalanga held the command and had devoted herself to death with fifteen other fanatical women. The struggle he had to wage there was severe, and only when he placed himself at the head of his troops with his sunshade in his hand and ran in upon the fortification together with [[Toekoe Hoessin Longbattah]], who waved a handkerchief, did he succeed in taking it. Three days later, on [[15 March]] [[1894]], the position was occupied by Dutch troops.

## Under fire

Senelop was a target for the enemy. Many times the Dutch unexploded *[[Granaatkartets|granaatkartetsen]]* came back at and into the fortification with filed-off ridges and a small bamboo tube with a fuse in place of the time-fuse, fired — Hooijer wrote — from old sawn-off iron cannon or, as it was said, from hollowed-out coconut trunks strongly bound about with stolen telephone wire.

Rifle fire cracked especially from the right-hand side, and for that reason an earthen wall was thrown up, visible on the following plate, to protect the going in and out of the *[[Benteng|benteng]]*. To the left of that exit the kitchen lay within the *enceinte* (enclosing rampart), and above it projected the bastion armed with a gun and a *[[Mitrailleur|mitrailleur]]*; the other bastion, provided with the same pieces, lay on the right.

## March 1896 and the end

On [[7 March]] [[1896]] the *liniecommandant* (commander of the line) came from Senelop on hearing the heavy firing at [[Anagaloeng]], taking up forty men from [[Lam Soet]] and [[Lam Barih|Lambarih]] on the way, and relieved the mauled patrol of [[Van Blokland|Kapitein Blokland]].

This *benteng*, exposed to enemy fire — which resounded immediately after the people were seen running away from the rice-fields in front — remained occupied until on [[18 April]] [[1896]] the columns came up which were to relieve it, together with [[Anagaloeng]] and [[Lam Barih|Lambarih]], and to lift it, after the surrounding population had ranged itself on the side of [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]], who had gone over to the *[[Moslemin]]*, and the *[[Blokhuis|blokhuizen]]* in the neighbourhood had been burnt by the Dutch.

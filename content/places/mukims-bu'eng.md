The **Mukims Bu'ëng** — also written **Mukims Bueng**, and known in full as the **VII Mukims Bu'ëng** (Achehnese *Mukim Tujoh Bu'ëng*, "Seven Mukims of the Swamp") — is a subdivision of the [[XXVI Mukims|Sagi of the XXVI Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh]]. Its inhabitants are called the *ureuëng Bu'ëng*, "people of the swamp," and the district is distinguished by a peculiar dialect and by customs which, although belonging politically to a lowland sagi, have far more in common with those of the [[Tunong|highlanders]] than with the rest of the [[XXVI Mukims]].

## The Swamp and its Dialect

The word *bu'ëng* itself belongs to the peculiar dialect of the Mukims Bu'ëng; it means "swamp," a sense rendered in other parts of Acheh by *paya*. So characteristic is this usage that *sawahs* situated in swampy land are called in the Mukims Bu'ëng simply *buëng* — "the swamp." The same word also means the terracing of *sawahs* made on the slopes of hills. The dialect of the Mukims Bu'ëng, together with that of the [[XXII Mukims|highlands]] more generally, is described by Snouck Hurgronje as having so much in common with the *Tunong* that the father of the community is called *waki* (rather than *keuchi'*, as in the rest of the lowlands).

## Marriage Customs

In *The Achehnese* Vol. I, the Mukims Bu'ëng are cited as an example of regional variation in marriage custom. In addition to the bridal couch and the dowry, the attendants of the bridegroom bring with them two sets of "standing sirih" (*ranub dông*) of the same kind as those already described. "In some districts (as for instance the Mukims Bu'ëng) this is replaced by a sweetmeat in the form of network, called *halua puloët*." Both of these are intended for distribution among the onlookers. The custom illustrates the way in which the swamp-dwellers of the [[XXVI Mukims]] preserved local peculiarities in matters where the rest of Acheh had adopted a different usage.

## Agriculture and Material Culture

The Mukims Bu'ëng is repeatedly mentioned in *The Achehnese* in connection with the agriculture of the swampy lands. The second method of *padi*-planting — by means of a nursery bed (*planteuëng*), in which the seed is strewn to obtain seedlings — was adopted in a portion of the highlands, occasionally in the lowlands, in the [[IV Mukims|IV Mukims]], and with certain modifications in the swampy districts of the Mukims Bu'ëng. The cane of the Mukims Bu'ëng is, like that of the [[XXII Mukims]] and the IV Mukims, of inferior quality; in all these places, accordingly, molasses is chiefly manufactured in the very primitive Achehnese sugar-mills.

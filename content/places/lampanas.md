**Lampanas** was a small Achehnese village and roadstead on the north coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], east of [[Oleh-leh]] and [[Kroëng Raja]], lying at the foot of one of the outrunners of the [[Goudberg]] (Selawa Djanten), famous in the history of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as the landing place and supply base of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] of [[26 July]]–[[4 September]] [[1879]], narrated in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## Description and situation

The village stood on a yellow strand behind which the glittering, chalk-coloured hill country rose, the dark row of coconut palms below and the massive body and sharp peak of the heavily wooded Goudberg towering above all. The roadstead — ordinarily a dead-quiet anchorage — gave shelter to the four steamships that anchored there at half past eight in the evening of [[26 July]] [[1879]], in bright moonlight: the [[Merapi (Z.M. stoomschip)|Merapi]], [[Soerabaya (Z.M. stoomschip)|Soerabaya]], [[Borneo (Z.M. stoomschip)|Borneo]] and [[Graaf van Bijland (stoomschip)|Graaf van Bijland]], attended by the little government steamboat [[Zeemeeuw (gouvernements-stoomboot)|Zeemeeuw]]. The surf on this coast, the authors note, is least in the early morning — a phenomenon that governed the hours of every landing.

## The landing of 27 July 1879

Before the expedition, the population of Lampanas had not proved hostile: it was here that 1e Luitenant [[G. B. Hooijer]] and the chief [[Panglima Tibang Moehamad|Panglima Tibang]] came with the Zeemeeuw to take intelligence, and the headman [[Toekoe Mas]] came aboard to fix the landing point. On [[27 July]] the guides were sent ashore at first light to reassure the people; the 3rd Company of the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie]] with a mortar section under Majoor [[Majoor Ducroo|Ducroo]] was the first troop landed, about six o'clock, and at eleven all the troops had reached the bivouac chosen a quarter of an hour from the village, on a grass-covered hill washed on two sides by a rivulet of pure drinking-water, secure as might be against surprise, heat and damp. The disembarkation of the horses — lowered by tackle upon the iron pontoon raft and swum ashore when the raft fell between the breakers — lasted until one in the afternoon, and that of the engineers' materials and provisions until eight in the evening. Before the kampong stretched the unplanted plain of the [[Blang Tring]], on which the column first drew up in marching order.

## Base and quarantine camp

Lampanas remained the column's base to the end: here Kapitein [[Kapitein Bedet|Bedet]] with two companies built the provisions storehouse and barracks; hence the Zeemeeuw ran the supply line to Oleh-leh, bringing up slaughter cattle when the runderpest-thinned valley yielded none; and hither returned, on [[22 August|22]]–[[23 August]] [[1879]], the whole column for the ten days' [[Quarantine at Lampanas (August–September 1879)|quarantine]] imposed on account of the smallpox of the Lamtoba valley. Between [[24 August|24]] and [[25 August]] a complete camp of bamboo barracks — company sheds 40 and 50 metres long, officers' bathrooms, a circular ambulance for fifty sick set about shady trees — rose behind the strand. From this camp the column re-embarked in three divisions between [[2 September|2]] and [[4 September]] [[1879]], whereupon, in the book's closing landscape, the bustle vanished for good: "Evenals te voren, als ware er niets geschied, beukte de witte schuimende branding het eenzame strand ... het groen-grijze silhouet van den voor altijd verlaten Selawa Djanten!"

**Lam Meuló** is the subdistrict headquarters-town and subdistrict of the former division of [[Pidië|Pidië]] in the [[Noordkust|Noordkust]] of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]], "with over 26,000 inhabitants" one of the smaller subdistricts of the residency, "comprising the southern part of the Pidië plain, in which the subdistrict headquarters Lam Meuló is located, as well as the adjacent hill and mountain country with the garrison posts of [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] and [[Geumpang|Geumpang]] as centers." Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats Lam Meuló as the subdistrict where the successor of [[Teukoe Oemar of Keumangan|Teukoe Oemar of Keumangan]] — Teuku Ibrahim, son of the pretender-sultan — "established himself as a farmer … where he seemed to keep far away from all politics," and as the subdistrict that anchored the secondary defense centre of Tangsé-Geumpang.

## The subdistrict and its oelèëbalangs

Lam Meuló "bordered on the subdistrict [[Sigli|Sigli]]," and "also belonged to the former division of [[Pidië|Pidië]]." It "comprised the southern part of the Pidië plain, in which the subdistrict headquarters Lam Meuló is located, as well as the adjacent hill and mountain country with the garrison posts of [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] and [[Geumpang|Geumpang]] as centers. All three of these places are located on the transversal road." The political fragmentation was "possibly even greater than in the [[Sigli|Sigli]] subdistrict. Of the 9 *landschap*, the *landschap* Tjoembó', in which the subdistrict headquarters is located, with 8,500 inhabitants, was by far the most important. As many as 5 smaller *landschap* counted fewer than 2,000 souls." Among the *oelèëbalangs*, "the youthful Teukoe Moehamad Daoed, or in full Teukoe Sri Moeda Pahlawan Béntara Tjoembó' Moehamad Daoed of the V Moekims Tjoembó', was the dominant figure," and "Teukoe Béntara Hasan of [[Keumala|Keumala]], belonging to the older generation, deserves mention here. In this *landschap*, the 'court' had found a temporary safe haven in [[1874]] after the Kraton was conquered by the 2nd Aceh expedition."

## The federation of the Kawaj XII

The federation of the Kawaj XII — also called "under 'Lam Meuló'" — was the political and adat-law umbrella that united a number of mountain *landschappen* of Lam Meuló with *landschappen* of the Meulabóh subdistrict. "Of the three mountainous territories — Pameuë, Toengkób and Beutóng — the two first-mentioned at that time formed part of the federation of the Kawaj XII, called under 'Lam Meuló'." In the heyday of the gold washings in this region, "these *oelèëbalang* communities united in the so-called 'Kawa XII' formed a prosperous and powerful bloc." The political alliances — federations XII and VI — "which existed in former times in [[Pidië|Pidië]] … also extended to the *oelèëbalangs* in this region."

## The successor of Teukoe Oemar of Keumangan

After the [[1938|1938]] removal of [[Teukoe Oemar of Keumangan|Teukoe Oemar of Keumangan]] from his dignity, "his successor appeared little in the foreground. Only recently returned from the exile which he had shared with his father, he established himself as a farmer in the subdistrict of Lam Meuló, where he seemed to keep far away from all politics." This successor was Teuku Ibrahim, son of the pretender-sultan Toeankoe Moehamad Daoed: "the son of the pretendant-sultan, Toeankoe Ibrahim, who was married to a daughter of Teukoe Oemar, the self-governing authority of [[Keumangan|Keumangan]] … appeared little in the foreground. Only recently returned from the exile which he had shared with his father, he established himself as a farmer in the subdistrict of Lam Meuló, where he seemed to keep far away from all politics." The withdrawal of the house from political life left the field open to the religious movement and to the other political figures of the subdistrict.

## The defense of Tangsé-Geumpang and the Japanese occupation

Lam Meuló was the gateway to the secondary defense centre of Tangsé-Geumpang. "A secondary defense center would be the mountain villages [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] and [[Geumpang|Geumpang]] in the subdistrict Lam Meuló. Here too, access was possible along only one motor road, namely along the already earlier … mentioned '*transversaalweg*', which via Lam Meuló leads to Tangsé and Geumpang and was intended in the future to connect the subdistrict headquarters [[Sigli|Sigli]] and [[Meulabóh|Meulaba]], across the central mountain range." A rural guard "composed entirely of Acehnese" was established in Lam Meuló "on the initiative of the energetic self-government official of Tjoembó', Teukoe Moehamad Daoed." On [[13 March]] [[1942]] the retreating Dutch troops evacuated Lam Meuló; on [[16 March]] "the Japanese vanguard occupied Lam Meuló"; on [[18 March]] "the capitulation of the troop force that had withdrawn to Tangsé and Geumpang took place, after which Tangsé was occupied by the Japanese that same day and Geumpang on [[19 March]]." "Both in Lam Meuló and in Tangsé, administration was initially placed in the hands of administrative committees consisting of Pusa members and F-men. After some time, however, these made way for an Indonesian '*gun-chó*,' as such Teukoe Moehamad Daoed of Tjoembó' was appointed for the entire subdistrict, who held this function until the end of Japanese rule."

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Lam Meulo** — pronounced *Lamló* — is the chief place (*hoofdplaats*) of the *onderafdeeling* of the same name in the *afdeeling* [[Pidië|Pidië]], situated in the great valley of Pidië in surroundings of very beautiful *blangs*. The place lies still in the valley of Pidië; a road runs along the [[Kroeeng Baró|Kroeeng Baró]] in southern direction and will in time reach [[Tangsé|Tangsé]], which, surrounded by *sawah*-plains, will one day play a great part in the connections with [[Sigli|Sigli]]. From Beureunoen on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] there is a short side-line to Lam Meulo.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

[[H. N. A. Swart]] put Lam Meulò under Boreel, "one of the bravest officers of the corps"; in [[1910]] Boreel at Lam Meulò and [[H. J. Schmidt]] at [[Tangsé]] were the pace-setters in Pidië. In [[1911]] Van Gheel Gildemeester's 4th Division killed the notorious [[Tgk. Moehamat Haroen]] of Lam Meulò with his principal subordinates and six followers. In January [[1913]] *marechaussée* from Lam Meulò found the hiding-place of [[Tgk. Ma' Aréh di Lam Goet]], and "Central Pidië had been cleared". In May [[1914]] an Acehnese attacked the sentry at Lam Meulò out of *[[maloe]]*. In [[1936]] Lam Meulò was one of the last four sub-divisions still governed by officers.

## Maréchaussée station ([[1910]])

the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* records the stations assigned to the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* in [[1910]]: 1st Division at [[Blang Kedjeren]]; 2nd Division at [[Malaboeh|Meulaboh]]; 3rd Division at [[Takengeun|Takèngan]]; 4th Division at [[Lam Meulo]]; and 5th Division at [[Lho Soekon]], "zooals zij ook thans nog gelegerd is" — where it was still quartered at the time of the [[1930]] jubilee. Lam Meulo was the station of the 4th Division.

Earlier, in [[1898]], the corps had been localised with its Staff at [[Koeta Radja]] and its divisions at [[Selimoen]], [[Indrapoeri]], [[Tjot Mantjang]] and [[Glé Broet]], [[Tjot Goet]], and [[Lam Baroe]] and [[Lok Nga|Lho'-Nga]].

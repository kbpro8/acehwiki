**Painan** (Dutch also *Pynen*, *Pynan*) is a coastal town on [[Sumatra's west coast|Sumatra's west coast]] whose name is preserved in the **[[Painansch Contract|Painans Contract]]** of [[6 July]] [[1663]] and whose bay — the bay of Painan — was the operational centre of the southern Westkust system from [[1663]] onward. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* Painan is the place whose bay contained the [[Poeloe Tjingkoek|Poeloe Tjingkoek]] fort and the [[Salido|Salido]] gold mines, and whose ruler was one of the four contracting landheeren of the Painansch Contract.

## The 1662 preliminary compact

Painan was the focal point of VOC diplomacy in the early 1660s. A preliminary compact negotiated near the town — at Songibongin south of Painan — in May [[1662]] by [[Jan van Groenewegen|Jan van Groenewegen]] laid the groundwork for the formal agreement concluded on [[6 July]] [[1663]] (ratified in Batavia on [[27 July]] [[1663]]). The settlement transferred de facto authority over several west-coast polities, including [[Indrapura|Indrapoera]] and [[Padang]], from Aceh's suzerainty to a Dutch-backed confederation.

## The 1663 Painansch Contract

Painan was one of the four contracting landheeren in the [[Painansch Contract|Painansch Contract]] of [[6 July]] [[1663]] — together with [[Indrapoera]], [[Padang]] and [[Tikoe|Tikoe(s)]]. The Compagnie's chosen centre for the new arrangement was "Poeloe Tjingkoek in the bay of Painan" (*Poeloe Tjingkoek in de baai van Painan*).

## The 1666 cession of Salido and Poeloe Tjingkoek

After the [[Abraham Versprect|Versprect]] expedition's destruction of [[Paoeh]] in August [[1666]] the rulers of [[Bajang]], [[Indrapoera]] and [[Taroesan]] "transferred to the Compagnie in ownership the place Salido with the gold-rich surroundings, from the kali-Loemoepo to Painan, as well as Poeloe Tjingkoek" (*droegen de vorsten van Bajang, Indrapoera en Taroesan de plaats Salido met de goudrijke ommelanden, van de kali-Loemoepo tot aan Painan, benevens Poeloe Tjingkoek, in eigendom over aan de Compagnie*). The cession was defined by reference to the kali-Loemoepo on one side and Painan on the other: Painan was the southern boundary of the ceded territory.

## The 1681 cession of Painan itself

On [[10 March]] [[1681]] "the prince and regents of Painan declared 'from their absolute power and private jurisdiction to transfer and to convey, and to give in free ownership, as they do hereby, the negorye, the mountain and the whole land of Pynen … with all the appurtenances and dependencies thereof'" (*den 10den Maart daarop ook de vorst en regenten van Painan verklaarden 'yt onse absolute maght en prive jurisdictie op te draegen en transporteren, en in vryen eygendom over te geven, gelijck doen mits desen, de negorye, het gebergte ende het geheele landt van Pynen … met alle de ap- en dependentiën van dien'*). The [[1681]] cession of Painan itself — together with the simultaneous re-cessions of Salido and Poeloe Tjingkoek — completed the Compagnie's outright territorial ownership of the southern Westkust gold-mining complex.

## Significance

Painan is the place whose name is attached to the foundational instrument of the Westkust system. The [[Painansch Contract|Painansch Contract]] of [[6 July]] [[1663]] is the legal foundation of the Westkust system; the bay of Painan is the operational centre of the southern Westkust complex; the [[1681]] cession of Painan itself completed the Compagnie's outright territorial ownership of the southern Westkust gold-mining territory. The Painansch adjective — "Painansch" — is the Dutch form of "Painan-ish" or "Painan-style", and the [[Painansch Contract]] of [[1663]] is the document that gave the name to the entire Westkust system. The painansch form — Compagnie protection in exchange for commercial monopoly, with internal sovereignty retained by the native rulers — would be replicated at [[Baros]] in [[1668]], at [[Singkel]] in [[1672]], and at [[Tikoe]] in [[1684]].

**The Goeah Teroes** — *groote grot*, the great cave — was a cave high in the mountains of the [[Glé Betoes]] between [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] and [[Lepong]] on the west of [[Groot-Atjeh]], used in [[1897]] as the stronghold of the band of [[Toekoe Hassan]] and the object of the Dutch patrol of [[25 September]] [[1897]].

## Description

[[P. H. R. Beuming]] describes the cave as lying high in the mountains and resembling *een waar arendsnest* — a veritable eagle's nest. It was reached only by climbing rock faces of dizzying height with the aid of ropes and hooked staves, through terrain so overgrown that the point of the Dutch column had to hack a way with the *kapmes*, and where one false step meant falling to pieces in the abyss.

Below the cave the climbing party reached a plateau formed by nature, where the signs of the enemy's immediate proximity were found in some banana leaves bearing the remains of a fresh rice meal.

The cave itself proved on entry to have several exits, through which the defenders escaped after the Dutch forced the mouth; great bloodstains on the ground and along the walls showed that the band had suffered losses. It was this multiplicity of exits, and the impossibility of pursuit, that decided Captain [[J. N. C. Baron van Heerdt]] to break off the action and take up the retreat.

## The action of [[25 September]] [[1897]]

The mouth of the Goeah Teroes was the scene of the death of 1st Lieutenant [[J. van Greuningen]] and the European fusiliers [[Winkelman]], [[Tuk]] and [[Dissel]], shot down *à bout portant* by about fifteen Acehnese who appeared suddenly from behind a rock face, and of the severe wounding of the native fusiliers [[Bongsowikromo]] and [[Tanja]]. The native fusilier [[Siman]] was killed by fire from within the cave as the Dutch reached the entrance. The full narrative is given at [[Patrol to the Goeah Teroes on the Glé Betoes (25 September 1897)]].

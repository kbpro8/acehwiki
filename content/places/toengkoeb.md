**Toengkoeb** — written in Dutch as *Toengkoeb* — was a [[Dutch]] temporary post and kampong in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|sagi of the XXVI Moekims]], the residence of the head of the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and one of the principal former residences of [[Teungkoe di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiroe]]. As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, Toengkoeb was garrisoned in [[1895]] by 2 officers, 52 infantry, and 1 hospital attendant (with no artillery), and was occupied by the Dutch to prevent the religious-warriors from re-settling and to control the [[hoeloebalang]] [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]]. The post was successfully re-supplied on [[16 April]] [[1896]], was evacuated on [[21 April]] [[1896]] and handed over to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]], was the site of the [[Night Surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe of 28–29 April 1896]], and was the location of the [[benteng|bentengs]] Perampoean and Batoe demolished by the [[Genie]] under Captain Zelle on [[20 June]] [[1896]].

**Toengkoep** was one of the Dutch forward posts of [[Groot-Atjeh]] razed in the spring of [[1896]] after the relief of its garrison, before the [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)|Beradin operation]]. [[P. H. R. Beuming]] names it in the list of former outposts — [[Senelop|Sennelop]], [[Lamsoet]], [[Anak Galoeng]], [[Lambarih]], [[Biloel]], [[Lamkoenjit]], [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and Toengkoep — together with the intervening blockhouses.

## Evacuation in the Concentration Strategy (February 1885)

Prior to its role as a 1895–1896 buitenpost, Toengkoeb had been a more distant Dutch post in the eastern reaches of the [[XXVI Moekims]]. During the [[Concentration Decision of August 1884|concentration of 1884–1885]], the post was evacuated on [[21 February]] [[1885]], without casualties. Located only 1½ palen (approximately 3 kilometers) from [[Lamjong]], the communications road to [[Lamjong]] was unprotected on both sides — acknowledged as "slechts van zeer gering belang" (only of very minor importance) due to the short distance, but illustrating the broader pattern of isolated positions the concentration addressed. Following evacuation, the [[XXVI Moekims]] remained outside the geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position), and the eastern approaches were no longer directly observed.

## Description and Location

Toengkoeb lay in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|sagi of the XXVI Moekims]] and was the residence of the head of the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]. The post was a crossroads commanding the eastern approaches of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] toward the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and the [[V Moekims Montassik]]. The garrison in [[1895]] consisted of 2 officers, 52 infantry, and 1 hospital attendant; there was no artillery. The fortification of Batoe lay approximately 400 metres west of the *kruispunt*, and the [[benteng|benteng]] Perampoean was nearby. The kampong of [[Kroeng-Kali]], containing the house of the *hoeloebalang* [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]], lay to the south. The post was linked by road to [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] in the eastern *buitenlinie*.

## Historical Background

Toengkoeb had been one of the principal residences of [[Teungkoe di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiroe]] after the Dutch concentration. After [[Teungkoe di Tiro|Tiroe]]'s death the post was occupied by the Dutch to prevent the religious-warriors from re-settling and to control the [[hoeloebalang]] [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]]. The post was a temporary rather than a permanent *buitenpost*, reflecting the Dutch intention to withdraw from the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] once the authority of the friendly [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] could be re-established. The garrison was exchanged on [[16 March]] [[1896]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations

Toengkoeb was one of the eastern [[buitenpost|buitenposten]] besieged in the spring of [[1896]]. On [[16 March]] [[1896]] the garrison was exchanged. On [[16 April]] [[1896]] the post was successfully re-supplied. On [[20 April]] [[1896]] Toengkoeb, [[Tjot Rang]], and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] were lifted by order of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]]. On [[21 April]] [[1896]] Toengkoeb was evacuated and handed over to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]], who thereby became responsible for the defence of the eastern approaches of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. On [[28 April]]–[[29 April]] [[1896]] the [[Night Surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe]] was conducted by a column from [[Pakan Kroeng Tjoet]] under Captain Zelle's engineers, in which the *benteng* of Batoe approximately 400 metres west of the *kruispunt* was surprised by night attack. On [[30 April]] [[1896]] the [[Demonstration in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb (30 April 1896)|expedition to the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] moved against the *benteng* of Batoe from [[Boekit Karang]]. On [[4 May]] [[1896]] the [[Second Expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb (4 May 1896)|second expedition]] renewed the attack. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] the [[benteng|bentengs]] Perampoean and Batoe near Toengkoeb were demolished by the [[Genie]] under Captain Zelle. On [[17 June]] [[1896]] Toengkoeb served as a reference point for the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]].

## Significance

Toengkoeb was the strategic crossroads of the eastern [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] and the principal Dutch base for operations against the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]]. The handover of the post to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] on [[21 April]] [[1896]] marked the Dutch policy of relying on friendly [[hoeloebalang|ulèëbalang]] as a substitute for garrisoned posts, and the demolition of the [[benteng|bentengs]] Perampoean and Batoe on [[20 June]] [[1896]] removed the principal Achehnese fortifications in this sector.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Toengkoeb** was a Dutch military post in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], re-occupied on [[27 September]] [[1893]] in a *benteng* belonging to [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet]] on ground the Dutch had abandoned in [[1884]] after four years of possession, and evacuated without a shot in [[1896]] under the leadership of [[Luitenant Neelmeyer]].

## Re-occupation

The wavering conduct of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet Toengkoeb]] during the [[1893]] campaign of [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] so often threw affairs into confusion that on [[27 September]] [[1893]] it was judged necessary to have one of his *bentengs* at Toengkoeb occupied by Dutch troops. Hooijer observed that the soldiers thus sat once again on the spot they had abandoned in [[1884]], after having been lords and masters there for four years.

## Contrast with the Van der Heijden period

The manner of their installation differed greatly from before. In place of the great garrison of former days there were now only two officers and fifty men quartered there, and not a single gun defended the walls; nor was any *officier van gezondheid* (medical officer) present — when one was needed he was fetched from the nearest post in the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]*, at [[Lamjong]]. In place of the liveliness and bustle that had prevailed around the old *[[Messigit|Messigit]]* when the columns under [[General van der Heijden]] had penetrated the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] and raised a great encampment at Toengkoeb — containing two battalions of infantry, a detachment of cavalry, three sections of artillery, sappers, survey personnel and a train of a thousand convict labourers and coolies — it was now still and monotonous.

## Description

The garrison lived *als vogeltjes in een kooi* — like little birds in a cage — inside a square earthen wall, rounded off only around the well with its clear and delicious drinking water, and protected against a first rush by a standing wire entanglement four metres from the wall. The officers' quarters lay on the rampart, on which also rose a slender papaya-tree; there stood the wooden benches on which their palliasses were spread, and their sitting-room much resembled a North Holland tavern, the ground being laid with square red bricks. On the high part beneath the flagstaff stood the guard; outside it was the hut of the [[Chinese sutler in the Atjeh outposts|Chinese sutler]] who supplied the garrison with small comforts — beer, tobacco, pipes, tins of preserved food, fruit and side-dishes.

The kitchen under a lean-to between the flagstaff and the papaya-tree lay inside the wire but outside the wall. Along one side of the fortification the men were making a path so as to be able to take an evening walk around their post without being hindered by the prickly or wet grass; this side led to the main entrance of the *benteng*, opposite the *kampong* (village) of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet Toengkoeb]]. Before that entrance stood benches on which the soldiers sat to take the air when it became too close and stuffy inside the crowded space. Four watch-houses on posts had been erected around the fortification, of which only two were manned by day, when it was glowing hot beneath their little roofs.

## Garrison life

Despite the monotony, many soldiers chose to stay longer than the prescribed fourteen days, because life was so easy there. Patrols were sent only to neighbouring posts. No duties other than mounting guard were ordered: cleaning the quarters, repairing the rampart or buildings, and working in the little garden — where a few ingredients for the cooking were grown and a few flowers recalled other times and places — could hardly be reckoned as service. The greatest possible freedom in dress was permitted; the Europeans mostly went barefoot and thus spared their shoes expensive repairs; night-trousers and a *kabaai* (loose jacket) were already a formal toilet. Pay accumulated without any need to spend it and yielded a pretty sum when, on return from *detacheering*, it was paid out at [[Kota Radja]]. Above all, the three daily *[[Oorlam|oorlams]]* were a delight; they roused the men to play and sing once night had fallen and the tin reflectors behind the lanterns lit a small patch of the surroundings, the crickets chirped in the grass, and the unsurpassed calm of the Indies night made them feel as though floating on a ship in mid-ocean rather than housed in an advanced temporary post amid a hostile people.

Of an enemy they noticed nothing. Toekoe Tjoet sat in his *kampong* or travelled to and from [[Kota Radja]], and concerned himself not at all with the post in his neighbourhood.

## The tales of the old Ambonese soldier

The chief pleasure of the post, Hooijer wrote, was the story-telling of an old [[Ambonese soldiers in the Atjeh outposts|Ambonese]] warrior outside the gate, who recounted the days when Dutch soldiers had done the fighting instead of the Achehnese; when [[General van der Heijden]] had appeared at that same Toengkoeb *met een wolk op zijn voorhoofd* because its capture had not gone as swiftly as he expected; and when the general's columns had advanced over the wide *sawahs* (wet rice-fields) on which his hearers now looked without daring to set a foot. Rising from his bench, the grey soldier would point out the *kampongs* (villages) across the *sawahs* through which the main column had passed to approach the *[[Messigit|Messigit]]* from the rear, and the thick tree at six hundred paces where the Achehnese guide had brought the column directly opposite the strongly held house of prayer, from which fire was at once opened that killed him and two soldiers and wounded an officer and sixteen men.

## Isolation in 1896

The Toengkoeb post was cut off in [[1896]] when the *[[Moslemin]]* occupied the terrain abandoned by [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet]]'s dispersing population — a dispersal caused by [[Toekoe Nja Banta]]'s removal of Toekoe Tjoet's wives to [[Lamreng]] — and broke communications between [[Kroeng Kali]] and Toengkoeb. Hooijer noted that a layman would probably have perceived nothing abnormal on the lonely rice-fields fringed by dark *kampong* trees, but that a soldier with war experience knew from the restless movements of black figures crossing the narrow dykes and grouping on rises that the *Moslemin* were again masters there.

## Evacuation

Toengkoeb remained occupied by Dutch soldiers, but at the time of the *verraad* — the treason of [[Teukoe Oemar]] — it was preserved from enemy attack by the prudent and courageous conduct of [[Luitenant Neelmeyer]], who rode by night from [[Pakan Kroeng Tjoet]] in the *geconcentreerde linie* to warn the garrisons of Toengkoeb and [[Tjot Rang]]. Later, under the leadership of the same officer, the garrison was withdrawn and the post evacuated *zonder dat daarbij een enkel schot viel*.

**Salido** (in the Compagnie's papers always *Sillida*) was a place on the [[Westkust van Sumatra|West Coast of Sumatra]] lying on the bay of [[Painan|Painan]], with gold-rich surroundings, which was ceded to the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] in ownership in [[1666]] by the rulers of [[Bajang]], [[Indrapoera]] and [[Taroesan]], and which became one of the four definitive Westkust factories and the centre of the Compagnie's gold exploitation on the Westkust. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* it is the place whose gold mines were a recurring source of disappointment and whose commercial bookkeeping brought down the commandeur [[Melchior Hurdt|Melchior Hurdt]] in [[1680]].

## The 1666 cession

The cession of Salido was the political fruit of the [[Abraham Versprect|Versprect]] expedition of August [[1666]], which had taken and destroyed [[Paoeh]] in revenge for the [[Battle of Paoeh (12 March 1666)|Paoeh disaster]] of [[12 March]] [[1666]]. "Shortly after Versprect's return to Batavia the rulers of Bajang, Indrapoera and Taroesan transferred to the Compagnie in ownership the place Salido with the gold-rich surroundings, from the kali-Loemoepo to Painan, as well as Poeloe Tjingkoek, in the person of [[Jacob Jorisse Pits|Jacob Jorisse Pits]], the Compagnie's head of the Westkust at Padang" (*Kort na Verspreet's terugkeer naar Batavia droegen de vorsten van Bajang, Indrapoera en Taroesan de plaats Salido met de goudrijke ommelanden, van de kali-Loemoepo tot aan Painan, benevens Poeloe Tjingkoek, in eigendom over aan de Compagnie, in den persoon van Jacob Jorisse Pits, 's Compagnies opperhoofd der Westkust te Padang*).

Salido was paired in the cession with [[Poeloe Tjingkoek]], the island in the bay of Painan on which the Compagnie now built a fort. The cession of Salido — i.e., of the mainland with its gold mines — together with Poeloe Tjingkoek — i.e., the island with its defensible fort — gave the Compagnie a complete commercial-military complex on the southern Westkust.

## The four definitive factories

When commissioner [[Jacob Cau|Jacob Cau]] returned to Batavia on [[2 January]] [[1665]] he had established four definitive factories on the Westkust: [[Padang]] (head office), Poeloe Tjingkoek or Salido, [[Priaman]] and [[Tikoe]]. Salido was the southern anchor of the four-factory system.

## The 1670 re-cession

In [[1670]], in the aftermath of the [[Pieter Coopman|Coopman]] expedition against [[Kotta Tengah]] and [[Paoeh]], the rulers of Bajang and Indrapoera "gave a new act of cession of the lands of Salido, and Poeloe Tjingkoek, which the government had asked for, because the original piece had been lost at Batavia" (*gaven een nieuwe acte van overdracht der landen van Salido, en Poeloe Tjingkoek, waarom de regeering gevraagd had, daar het oorspronkelijke stuk te Batavia was zoekgeraakt*). The loss of the original cession document at Batavia is a small but vivid reminder of the administrative fragility of the Compagnie's Westkust paper authority.

## The Bajang-Salido gold-mine dispute of 1678-1679

In [[1678]] a dispute arose between [[Bajang]] and Salido over the discovery of a new gold mine at [[Soengei Capayan|Soengei-Capayan]]. According to Bajang the mine lay outside the territory of Salido that had been ceded to the Compagnie. Commandeur [[Melchior Hurdt|Hurdt]] presided over the dispute, which was settled in favour of the Compagnie: "the Holland-friendly [Salido] party triumphed, and on 18 April 1679 those of Bajang humbled themselves before the Compagnie, acknowledging it as stadtholder of the West Coast" (*de Hollandsch-Salidosche partij zegevierde, en 18 April 1679 verdeemoedigden die van Bajang zich voor de Compagnie, deze erkennende als stadhouder van de Westkust*).

The chiefs of [[Loempo]] had to accept the contract as well. The Bajang-Salido dispute is one of the few cases in the Westkust narrative where the Compagnie adjudicates an internal Westkust boundary question — a small but real exercise of sovereign authority.

## The 1681 re-cession

The cession of Salido was renewed in [[1681]] as part of the post-[[Padang Conference (29 August 1680)|Padang Conference]] consolidation. On [[27 February]] [[1681]] "the rulers of Bajang and Indrapoera gave a renewed confirmation of the complete cession of the lands of Salido and Poeloe Tjingkoek to the Compagnie" (*Nog gaven de vorsten van Bajang en Indrapoera den 27 Februari 1681 een hernieuwde bevestiging van den algeheelen afstand van de landen van Salido en Poeloe Tjingkoek aan de Compagnie*).

## The 1680 gold-exploitation investigation

The Salido gold mines were a persistent disappointment. Commissioner [[Laurens Pit de Jonge|Laurens Pit de Jonge]]'s [[1680]] mission to the Westkust was, in its economic aspect, "to investigate the causes of the poor results of the gold exploitation at Salido, and at the same time the commercial bookkeeping of Hurdt" (*de oorzaken van de povere resultaten van de goudexploitatie te Salido, en tevens naar de commercieele boekhouding van Hurdt*). The investigation produced Hurdt's suspension and removal to Batavia.

## The 1687 and later history

The Salido gold mines remained the most prized of the Compagnie's Westkust commercial assets throughout the late seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. Stapel notes that in the eighteenth century "the gold of the Sillidasche gold mines was for the Compagnie, with pepper, camphor and benzoin, the most desired trade good" (*Het goud der Sillidasche goudmijnen was voor de Compagnie met peper, kamfer en benzoë de meest begeerde handelswaar*). The mines were pacht (lease) in [[1699]], [[1729]] and [[1737]].

## Significance

Salido is the Compagnie's only outright territorial possession on the Westkust proper (as distinct from treaty-bound protectorates). The cessions of [[1666]], [[1670]] and [[1681]] transferred the land in *eigendom* — ownership — to the Compagnie, in contrast to the Painansch Contract system, which preserved native sovereignty in exchange for commercial monopoly. Salido is therefore the institutional precursor of the later Dutch direct-rule territories on the Westkust.

The Salido gold mines are also a recurring source of administrative trouble. The 1678–1679 Bajang-Salido boundary dispute and the 1680 gold-exploitation investigation that brought down Hurdt are both Salido stories. The Compagnie's Westkust authority, in the Salido phase, rests on direct territorial ownership, but its exploitation is consistently disappointing — a pattern that would recur throughout the VOC period.

The **Kraton of Atjeh** was the palace-fortress of the [[Sultan of Atjeh (d. 1874)|Sultan of Atjeh]] and the old burial place of the sultans, standing between the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] on the north and the [[Atjeh-rivier]] on the south. It fell to the Dutch on [[24 January]] [[1874]] in the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] led by [[Generaal Verspijck|Verspijck]], and was immediately renamed [[Kota Radja]] by the Dutch and made the headquarters of the [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]]. Borel considered its occupation "eene groote strategische fout," committing the Dutch to an inland *vestiging* when a coastal point plus blockade would have been cheaper.

## Defences

The Kraton's defences, as Borel describes them after personally walking the borstwering, consisted of an earthen wall some four metres high, fronted by *bamboe-doeri* (thorn-bamboo) entanglements and a *gracht* (moat) — wet on the northern and western faces, dry on the southern and eastern. Inside the wall lay one large *kerkhof* (burial ground) and the sultans' graves. The Dutch had expected thick stone walls — the *Beknopte beschrijving van den Kraton van Groot Atjeh* had said as much — but the actual work was an aarden borstwering hidden by bamboo-doeri, which is why the [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|nineteen-day bombardment]] achieved less than expected. The Mesigit, a fortified mosque serving as *voorwerk* (outwork) on the northern approach, was the key to the Kraton's landward defence and cost the Dutch 220 casualties when stormed on [[6 January]] [[1874]].

## After the fall

After [[24 January]] [[1874]] the Kraton became [[Kota Radja]], the Dutch *hoofdplaats* (headquarters). The [[Kroeng Daroe]], a small river splitting the enclosure into two halves, became the principal cause of the [[Bandjir flood of Kota Radja (December 1874)|December 1874 bandjir]] that inundated the headquarters for eight days. By [[1875]] the Kraton was already "vervallen" (dilapidated); Borel records that kazerneloodsen had no zijwanden, rain poured through, and "nimmer klonk een blij gezang binnen de wallen." It remained the *centraal reduit* of the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] and the unhealthiest spot in Atjeh — a *groot zickeninrichting* (great sick-house) wracked by malaria, cholera and flood.

**Kraton of Atjeh** was the fortified royal palace and administrative heart of the Acehnese Sultanate, located near the [[Atjeh-rivier]] and protected by its outer bastion, [[Atjeh's Groote Missigit]].

## Historical Significance
As the ultimate objective of the Dutch military expeditions in [[1873]] and [[1874]], the Kraton symbolized Acehnese sovereignty and resistance. The Dutch strategy was built entirely around advancing upon and capturing this complex. The bitter fighting at the Missigit was a direct consequence of the Acehnese determination to prevent the Dutch from reaching the Kraton. Once the Missigit fell decisively during the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit]], the path to the Kraton was laid bare.

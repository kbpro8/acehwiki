**Beureunoen** — pronounced *Bernoen* — is a place in the [[Pidië|vallei of Pidië]] on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]], the seat of the *oeléebalang* of [[Keumangan|Keumangan]], [[Teukoe Oemar Keumangan|Teukoe Oemar]]. From Beureunoen on the Atjeh-tram there is a connection to [[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]], which lies south-west and is the chief place of the *onderafdeeling* of that name. Within the yard of the chief's dwelling at Beureunoen stands a new house in the Atjehnese style, entirely decorated with carving and with the mouldings picked out in colours, which has revived, at least for a few years, the declining art of Atjehnese wood-carving.

**Bernoen** was a place on the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|North Coast of Atjeh]], the seat of the Acehnese chief [[Teukoe Oemar van Bernoen]], where the ophthalmologist [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] conducted part of the [[Cataract campaign on the North Coast of Atjeh (1932)|cataract campaign]] recorded in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]*. The name as printed in the source is *Bernoen*; it corresponds to the district and market town known in modern orthography as Beureunuen, in the [[Pedir|Pidie]] country of the north coast.

## The campaign at Bernoen

The pamphlet's account of Bernoen turns wholly on the dealings between the doctor and the chief. Observing the etiquette due to a personage of his weight, Tijssen requested an audience and asked the chief to gather all the blind he could find. The chief, out of humour and much afflicted by the *[[Malaise|malaise]]*, objected that it would help little and give much *[[Soesah|soesah]]*, and went off grumbling; but his sense of honour had been struck, and by the next morning everything was arranged "in de puntjes."

From the great crowd assembled Tijssen selected eight sufferers; five were operated on at once for cataract, and five days later all five could see. There followed a great rush of patients, in which the chief took increasing personal pleasure and exerted himself to help. At Tijssen's departure the chief asked the celebrated question "Blanda betoel?" — is the gentleman a real Dutchman? — which Tijssen recorded as the finest of insults and as evidence that cataract operations sometimes have a political side.

## Context

Bernoen lay in the coastal belt where Tijssen had worked earlier, and which he called the East or so-called North Coast, in distinction from the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast]] he travelled in [[1933]]. It was in four small villages of this North Coast that he had in [[1923]] caused the blind to be assembled and found two hundred and twenty-six of them; and it was here that in the campaign of [[1932]] he operated on eighty-three persons in twenty-six days, in a small polyclinic building, in an administrative office and in a buffalo stable. The cover photograph of the pamphlet shows treated cataract patients in the *[[Kampong|kampong]]* of [[Long Poetoe]] on the same coast.

**Aneu Galong** — also *Aneu'Galong* — is the Acehnese place at which the [[Battle of Aneu Galong (1896)|defence of Aneu Galong]] of [[1896]] took place — the defence in which the eldest son and successor of *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]]* was killed as commandant, and in which the *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]* — father of the future resistance leader *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* — also fell as a *geloofsgetuige*. The place is the chronological anchor of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] narrative in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*.

## The defence of 1896

"Van de vier zoons van *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]]* was de oudste, tevens de opvolger, in [[1896]] gesneuveld als commandant bij de verdediging Aneu Galong." The defence of Aneu Galong in [[1896]] was the action in which the patriarchal generation of the Tiriō family was killed: the eldest son (the successor) as commandant, and *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]* as a *geloofsgetuige*. The boy *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* — son of Tgk. Mat Amin — was an infant at the breast when his father died at Aneu Galong.

## The 1896 turning point

The year [[1896]] is, in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, the moment at which "het lijdelijk afwachten was afgelopen en de troepen zelf tot actie overgingen" — the moment at which the passive waiting was over and the troops themselves went over to the offensive. Aneu Galong therefore belongs to the same turn as the broader Dutch offensive of the late 1890s, the offensive that would culminate in the [[Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-era colonnes of the early 1900s.

## The inheritance

The defence of Aneu Galong in [[1896]] placed the infant *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* in the inheritance of the holy war. The boy would grow up to refuse submission on the grounds that "Zijn Vader was als *geloofsgetuige* gevallen, hij zou in diens voetstappen treden." The place is therefore the book's marker of the generational arc of the Tiriō resistance: the patriarchal generation killed at Aneu Galong in [[1896]]; the last leader of the bende — the boy who had been an infant at Aneu Galong — killed at [[Tangsé]] in [[1911]].

## Significance

Aneu Galong is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the place at which the patriarchal generation of the Tiriō resistance was killed in [[1896]]. The book's argument is that the defence of Aneu Galong was the chronological anchor of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] narrative: the moment at which the inheritance of the resistance was transferred to the surviving sons and the infant grandson. Aneu Galong is therefore the book's marker of the generational arc of the Tiriō resistance, and the precondition of the colonne Schmidt.

**Garouw** was an Atjehnees kampong north-east of [[Lampoeloe]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]], blocking access to the [[Pedir-dijk]] and the scene of the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of 28 June 1874]] in which [[Majoor Romswinckel|majoor Romswinckel]]'s attack on a well-laid enemy fortification ended in a permanent Dutch victory — the first time, Borel notes, the enemy was driven permanently from the right bank below [[Penajoeng]]. Its capture opened the Pedir-dijk, the road to Koeala, and [[Kota Radja Bedil|K. R. Bedil]] to Dutch occupation.

## The battle

Intercepts showed the enemy at Garouw was resolved to cut river communication again and was trying to persuade [[Toekoe Nek]] of [[Marassa]] to turn against the Dutch. [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] decided to strike first. In the early morning of [[28 June]] [[1874]] [[Majoor Romswinckel|majoor Romswinckel]] attacked the well-laid fortification at Garouw; it was surrounded and, after brave resistance, taken. The cost was four dead and about thirty wounded, including three officers severely — two of them, lieutenants von Massow and Zwager, died shortly afterward. Borel's verdict is that [[28 June]] [[1874]] was "a very important and glorious day" — for the first time the enemy was permanently driven from the right bank below Penajoeng, and the Atjehneezen were so shocked that they abandoned other positions along the Pedir-dijk, the road to Koeala, and K. R. Bedil, all of which the Dutch occupied at once.

## Aftermath and significance

The fall of Garouw opened the entire right bank of the Atjeh-rivier from [[Kwala Atjeh]] up to the bend at [[Lemboe]] to Dutch posts. The enemy's plan to cut river communication between the [[Marine-benteng|marine-benteng]] and Penajoeng — which had nearly succeeded on [[15 June]] [[1874]] when a *waterprauw* was fired on and the sailors saved themselves only by desperate rowing — was foiled. The Garouw battle was followed by the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] of [[28 September]] [[1874]], the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] of [[7 November]] [[1874]], and the [[Conquest of Kota Alam (13 November 1874)|conquest of Kota Alam]] of [[13 November]] [[1874]], each of which extended the Dutch hold on the dike. Garouw itself, lying on the right bank near Lampoeloe, became one of the chain of posts that made up the [[Pedir-dijk|right-bank]] line of the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] in [[1875]].

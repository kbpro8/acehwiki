**Takengën** (modern *Takengon*) is the bestuursvestiging — the Dutch administrative seat — established on the western shore of the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] lake in the heart of the [[Gajoland|Gayo]] highlands, around which the colonial administration of the *onderafdeeling Gajo Loeës* was organised after the [[Colonne van Daalen naar de Gajolanden en Alaslanden (1904)|1904 campaign]] of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|van Daalen]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats Takengën as the political and communications centre of the Gayo high plateau: the point from which the *Gajo-weg* — the great road down to the North Coast at [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] — descends the *Klietel*-saddle to the lake, and the seat of the onderafdeling that comprised, at the time of the handbook, the four Gayo *landschappen* of [[Redjë Tjèq|Redjë Tjèq]] (Bobasan), [[Redjë Boekét|Redjë Boekét]], [[Sjah Oetama|Sjah Oetama]], and [[Redjë Linggo|Redjë Linggo]].

## The Laoet Tawar and its setting

Takengën lies on the *Laoet Tawar* — the great natural lake of the Gayo highlands, approximately 16 kilometres long and 4 kilometres wide, lying at about 1,200 metres above sea level in a basin enclosed by the high, pine-forested walls of the *Klietel*-massif to the south and the *B. ni Segi* (2,400 m)–*B. ni Beboeli* chain to the north. The lake is the headwater of the [[Kr. Peusangan|Kr. Peusangan]], which breaks through the narrow western wall of the basin at *Takengën* itself before descending to the North Coast plain. The *Klietel*-saddle — "den eenigen toegang van hoogen weg van uit het Noorden naar het afgesloten Meergebied" — was the route of the *Gajo-weg*, "met ontzaglijke moeite en kosten geslagen," which connected the Gayo high plateau with the population centres of the North Coast at [[Bireuën|Bireuën]].

## The four landschappen

The *onderafdeeling* of which Takengën was the seat comprised, at the time of the handbook, four of the six self-governing Gayo *landschappen*: the territory of [[Redjë Tjèq|Redjë Tjèq]] (Bobasan), of [[Redjë Boekét|Redjë Boekét]], of [[Sjah Oetama|Sjah Oetama]], and of [[Redjë Linggo|Redjë Linggo]]. The remaining two Gayo *landschappen* — the territory of the *Kédjoerön Pétiambang* and the territory of the *Kédjoerön Abèq* (with its *onderhoorigheden* Bonén, Sémboeang, and Djérnéh) — formed, with the kampong complex of Tampèr, the *onderafdeeling Serbëdjadi*.

The four *landschappen* of the Takengën onderafdeeling were grouped under the broader administrative structure of the *afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh*, with the exception of Tampèr. Their internal administration, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, was genealogical rather than territorial: the *rédjë*'s were the heads of the descent groups, and the territorial *tjèq*-schappen that emerged from the genealogical system were the building blocks of the political order.

## The Dutch seat

The choice of Takengën as the *bestuursvestiging* reflected both its central position on the lake shore and its accessibility from the North Coast via the Gajo-weg. The same location gave it strategic command of the *Laoet Tawar* basin, the most densely populated and agriculturally productive part of the Gayo highlands. The Dutch administration's persistent effort to give the *Kédjoerön Pétiambang* — the titular overlord of the Gayo Loeës whose authority had long since decayed — the actual power of a central territorial prince, and the resistance of the other tjèqs to this imposed authority, is the principal political drama of the Gayo Loeës in the colonial period.

## Account from *Atjèh and the War with Japan* (1949) — Takéngón

**Takéngón** (also Takengën) is the subdistrict headquarters-town of the [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]] highlands of Central Atjèh, the principal town of the Gayo highlands where Colonel [[Colonel Gosenson|Gosenson]] established his headquarters on the tea enterprise "Rédèlóng" of the N.V. Serdang cultuur maatschappij in mid-February [[1942]], and where, after the Japanese breakthrough from the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]], the Colonel remained in command as the main centre covering the rear of the highland defense. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats Takéngón as the principal pivot of the Dutch military dispositions of [[1942]]: the destination of the Koetaradja evacuation transports, the headquarters of the territorial commander, and the centre from which the Government's resin and turpentine enterprise operated.

## The town and its economic role

The town of Takéngón lay at the heart of the [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]] valley, the great highland plain of Central Atjèh, some 100 km. east of [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] along the *Gajóweg*. The Government's resin and turpentine enterprise was the great economic institution of the subdistrict. "The total pine area (*pinus merkusii*) in Takéngón amounted to approximately 70,000 hectares, of which 40,000 were incorporated in enterprises and 35,000 hectares were in tapping." Under "the powerful and capable leadership of Mr. H. Oosterling," the enterprise "developed from a small experimental enterprise into a first-class enterprise, with a production in [[1941]] of 10,632 tons of pure product compared to 2,143 tons in [[1936]], 518 tons in [[1931]] and 126 tons in [[1926]]." The enterprise was the basis of the road network that had advanced "to Manggis in the Isa' valley, 70 km. in the direction of the [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang]]."

## The headquarters of Colonel Gosenson

"Mid-February [1942] he established his headquarters in the subdistrict of Takéngón, for the further preparation of the guerrilla warfare, to be conducted from Aceh's mountainous regions (the subdistricts of Takéngón, [[Gajó Loeós|Gajó Loeós]], and [[Alaslanden|Alaslands]]), after the Japanese occupation of the coastal areas." The tea enterprise "Rédèlóng" of the N.V. Serdang cultuur maatschappij, "located East of the *Gajóweg*, which connects [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] with Takéngón, at ± 45 km. from the sub-district headquarters," was the site of the headquarters. The relocation from [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] to Takéngón "was related to the plans to continue the fight in Aceh's mountainous areas after the withdrawal from Aceh's lowlands." Takéngón was also the destination of the planned emergency evacuation of the urban population of [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]]. It was from Takéngón that the Resident sent the radio-telegram of [[16 March]] [[1942]] announcing the change of headquarters and the increasing underground anti-authority movement.

## The defense of March 1942

"The total strength of the troop force at colonel Gosenson's disposal amounted to more than 2,000 men," deployed in defense of "an area almost twice as large as the Netherlands, with a coastline of more than 1,100 km." When, after the fall of Java, General [[Major-General Overakker|Overakker]] "decided to also withdraw the troops elsewhere on Sumatra via [[Kaban Djahé|Kaban Djahé]] to Central Atjèh, in order to try to hold out in the Atjèh highlands for as long as possible … [[Takéngón|Takéngón]], where Colonel Gosenson would remain in command, became the main center covering the rear." The fall of Takéngón came after the Japanese breakthrough from the [[Alasvalley|Alasvalley]]. "Then when General Overakker decided on [[27 March]] to surrender, there was no longer any enemy to be acknowledged in the [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang]], so that the surrender could only be offered on [[28 March]]." After the capitulation the Japanese commander "set off in pursuit of Colonel Gosenson and his brigades as soon as they realized their mistake," and Colonel Gosenson fell back on [[Blang Kédjérèn|Blang Kédjérèn]] with two very weary brigades. A small group under 1st lieutenant of the marechaussee H. van Zanten "succeeded in staying out of enemy hands for another year. After surrendering on [[10 March]] [[1943]], Lieutenant van Zanten was executed at Fort de Kock on [[21 October]] [[1943]]."

**Wikkie creek** — Dutch ***Wikkie-kreek*** — was a strategic waterway in the interior of [[Acheh]] that featured in two distinct episodes of Dutch colonial military operations: a major mid-eighteenth-century rebellion against the [[Dutch East India Company]] during the [[1760s]], and a December landing during the [[Aceh War|Aceh war]] in which Dutch troops under [[Captain Van Oyen]] attempted a poorly coordinated amphibious assault using a "tent-boat".

## The 1760s Rebellion

During the [[1760s]] the Wikkie creek area was the theatre of a major rebellion led by the rebel leaders [[Atta (rebel leader)|Atta]], [[Quacco]], [[Baubé]] and [[Fortuyn]] against the [[Dutch East India Company]]. The Dutch established a fortified headquarters nearby and conducted multiple military operations to suppress the insurgency. The creek served as a strategic corridor for both rebel movements and Dutch troop deployments, including the expedition led by [[Kolonel De Salve|Colonel De Salve]].

## The December Expedition

On [[25 December]], during the Dutch December expedition to Acheh, Dutch troops under [[Captain Van Oyen]] attempted a landing on the Wikkie creek using a "tent-boat" — a vessel fitted with an awning or tent-like covering used for troop transport inshore. The landing was poorly coordinated, leading to confusion and a failed assault on rebel positions. The incident is described in the [[Journal of Haringman]] as an "unforgivable mistake" caused by officers acting against orders.

## Military Significance

Across both episodes the Wikkie creek functioned as a strategic corridor for rebel movements and Dutch troop deployments alike. Its interior situation, navigability for small craft, and proximity to rebel strongholds made it a recurring point of contact between Dutch amphibious forces and Acehnese insurgents over more than a century of colonial warfare.

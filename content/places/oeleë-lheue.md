**Oeleë Lheuë** was the harbour of [[Koetaradja]], the place where the *kapal poetih* (the white ship) arrived with the Dutch flag at half-stok when an officer had fallen. It was the point of arrival and departure for troops and supplies, and the place where bodies of officers killed on the Westkust were brought by boat.

## The De Bruijn episode

When the *kapal poetih* arrived at Oeleë Lheuë with the flag halfstok, the *chabar angin* went as a loopend vuurtje through Koetaradja: *„the lijk of an officer.“* The wife of a known kapitein — thinking it was her man — joeg from Koetaradja to Oeleë Lheuë, arriving at the steiger just as a sloep from the gouvernementstoomer aanlegde. A matroos came out with a dienstbrief. She greep the brief, scheurde the enveloppe open, and found — to her relief — that it was not her man's name among the gesneuvelden. It was luitenant De Bruijn, killed in the loeëng ambush on the Westkust.

## Other references

Oeleë Lheuë was the place where the Radja Moeda Teukoe Moehamad (under the *éénoogige generaal* [[Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]]) worked to bring welvaart to the hoofdplaats, so that the gevluchte bevolking returned *in niet te stuiten stroomen*.

In den vroegen morgen van [[22 September]] j.l. trokken velen per auto of extra-trein naar Oelèë Lheuë om getuige te zijn van het vertrek van den afgetreden Gouverneur Swart; the band played the *Wilhelmus*, [[Mevrouw Swart]] received flowers, and the couple went aboard the *s.s. [[Nias]]* by the sloop of H.M. *[[Serdang]]*, saluted by three cheers and the military salute.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**Oelèë Lheue** was the principal seaport of Groot-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the debarkation place of the Dutch in [[1873]], connected by the first works of peace — road, môle, tramway and telegraph ([[1874]]–[[1876]]) — with the capital [[Koeta Radja]] five kilometres inland, and serving until the rise of [[Sabang]] as the province's chief gateway for trade.

### The first landing and works, 1873–1876

It was at Oelèë Lheue that Government Commissioner [[F. N. Nieuwenhuizen|Nieuwenhuizen]]'s flotilla anchored in March [[1873]] and the [[First Dutch Attack on Acheh|first Dutch expedition]] debarked. The commission of [[26 May]] [[1874]] proposed, and the Government on [[26 June]] approved, the building of a môle (*zeehoofd*) of iron screw-piles at Oelèë Lheue, with an adjoining railway with steam-traction to Koeta Radja and a telegraphic connection; the môle came ready in [[1875]] and the line was opened to public traffic in [[1876]]. The telegraph Oelèë Lheue–Koeta Radja entered service on [[20 March]] [[1875]]. The roadstead grew steadily busier; in July [[1875]] Oelèë Lheue and the adjacent Kampong Djawa counted 181 established traders, mostly Chinese, and a Chinese shipping company (*reederij*) was found there beside brick-kilns, lime-kilns and smithies.

### Administration and quarantine

In [[1875]] the commandant entrusted equal jurisdiction over all direct subjects of the Government to the assistant-resident in Atjeh at Koeta Radja/Oelèë Lheue, also charging him with the notary's office and the functions of registrar of births, deaths and marriages. Until [[1882]] all vessels arriving in the Government had to submit to visitation at Oelèë Lheue before putting into any other harbour; its abolition opened the whole coast of Atjeh to trade. The resolution of [[29 May]] [[1883]] again confined import and export to the harbours with government officials — Oelèë Lheue in Groot-Atjeh, [[Sigli]], [[Samalanga|Samelanga]], [[Lho Seumawè]] and [[Idi]] on the North and East Coasts, and [[Meulaboh]] on the West Coast — and under the Concentration after [[1884]] Oelèë Lheue alone of the harbours remained open, the entire fishery being forbidden.

### Trade statistics and later years

The port's import trade was vividly alive: in [[1890]] some three million guilders' worth of imports passed Oelèë Lheue; in that year 51 steamships called (39 departing), and from outside the Netherlands Indies there arrived 19 sailing and 135 steam vessels (20 sailing and 143 steam vessels departing). In the slump year [[1896]] imports fell back; the principal import articles over 1896–1898 were foodstuffs (f 411,295 → f 380,713), rice (5,116,980 → 4,448,070 kg), petroleum (673,848 → 1,473,588 litres), tobacco (c. 75,000 kg), flour, dried fish and manufactures (f 109,060 → f 162,175; the latter figure rising to f 4,889,522 by [[1904]]). In [[1901]] the [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij]] opened a sub-agency here ([[20 October]]), removed to Koeta Radja in [[1904]]. The first girls' school of the province opened at Oelèë Lheue in [[1910]]. Under the [[Inauguration of the Sabang mail service (1903)|Sabang mail service]] the post office was enlarged (1903–1904), the [[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|Atjeh Transport Maatschappij]] maintained a regular service with Sabang, and a government land telegraph-line connected Oelèë Lheue with [[Medan]] in [[1912]]. By [[1922]], however, the import and export of Oelèë Lheue was gradually diminishing, the trade having shifted to [[Langsar]], [[Lho Seumawè]] and Sabang, as Director [[N. van Zalinge]] reported.

## In the eye survey of Groot-Atjeh ([[1937]])

Oelee-Lheuë was one of the places examined in Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]]'s [[Oogheelkundig kampongonderzoek in Groot-Atjeh (Maart-December 1937)|eye survey of 1937]], and the district that furnished the key comparison on [[Trachoma in Groot-Atjeh|trachoma]].

It was one of only four places in [[Groot-Atjeh]] not populated exclusively by Atjehnese — with [[Koeta Radja|Koetaradja]], [[Lho Nga|Lho-Nga]] and [[Seulimeum]] — though the admixture with Malays, Padangers and other indigenous races was "zeer gering." Because trachoma occurred in typical form among those other races, Gomperts reasoned that if the *conjunctivitis follicularis* he was finding belonged to trachoma, it should be concentrated in such places. At Oelee-Lheuë 78 cases were found among 1,325 children examined — 6 per cent — but in the exclusively Atjehnese [[Montassik|Moekim Montassie]] 75 among 1,795, rather more than 4 per cent: a difference too small to support the theory.

Oelee-Lheuë was also named among the districts where Western influence had penetrated further, giving a measure of venereal infection and hence of its small share among the causes of [[Blindheid in Groot-Atjeh|blindness]].

**The Kraton of Aceh** (also called the Dalam) was the fortified citadel and residence of the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan of Atjeh]] at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] on the northern tip of Sumatra, the seat of the sultanate and the principal object of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] of [[1873]]-[[1874]]. The Kraton was taken by the Dutch on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] when a reconnaissance sent by Lieutenant-general [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] from [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Kota Goenoengan]] entered it unopposed and hoisted the Dutch flag on the watch-house, to the strains of the *Wilhelmus* played by the band of the right half of the 14th bataljon. The [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] was followed by the [[Death of Sultan Aladin Mahmood Shah|death of the sultan]] on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]] and by the [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation of annexation]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] on [[31 January]] [[31 January]] [[1874]].

## The First Expedition, April 1873

During the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[April 1873|April 1873]] the Dutch troops never reached the Kraton. On [[10 April]] [[10 April]] [[1873]] they advanced on the Kraton believing it would be no more than a military march — only one day's rations had been ordered. They met determined resistance at the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|missigit]] (mosque) outside the Kraton. On [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1873]] a second attack was undertaken on the fortifications south-east of the missigit, "of which one was not sure whether perhaps the Kraton was there." Later it appeared that one of these fortifications was the north face of the Kraton itself. The surprise failed; the storm column was repulsed with heavy losses; a council of war advised against renewed attack; on [[29 April]] [[29 April]] [[1873]] the transport fleet left the roadstead of Atjeh.

## The Second Expedition, December 1873-January 1874

In the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] the Dutch landed east of the Atjeh river on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]] and pushed forward to the mouth of the Atjeh river by [[15 December]] [[15 December]]. After the taking of the missigit of the Kraton on [[6 January]] [[6 January]] [[1874]] and the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]] or Kota Goenoengan on [[12 January]] [[12 January]], the Kraton could be fired upon from the west side. The Dutch fire "made the stay there so unsafe that, according to information received, the sultan had left it and had betaken himself to kampong [[Longbatta|Longbatta]]." On [[23 January]] [[23 January]] [[1874]] General Verspyck's column attempted the omtrekking, crossing the [[Krueng Daroy|Krueng Daroe]], which also ran through the Kraton, and pushing through the kampongs Atoöh and Longbatta to the river; the omtrekking was in fact accomplished but the column returned for want of a guide.

## The Capture, 24 January 1874

From Kota Goenoengan, between the brushwood, one saw:

> "a broken piece of wall with a watch-house behind it, largely stripped of its roof covering. From there Kota Goenoengan had been continually and sometimes rather heavily fired upon during the previous days and also during the past night. But about 9 in the morning [of 24 January 1874] this fire slackened and soon ceased entirely. This led the supreme commander to suspect that the Kraton was not or only weakly occupied, and he therefore, as soon as the right half 14th had arrived, ordered that by a company a reconnaissance should be made, which was to try to enter the Kraton and on the above-mentioned watch-house was to hoist the Dutch flag."

When this reconnaissance had advanced for some time without seeing the enemy, the supreme commander himself came up with his staff and the other companies. "Soon the heart of the Kraton was reached, and a repeated hoerah of our men and the *Wilhelmus*, played by the band of the right half of the 14th bataljon, proclaimed that the Kraton was in our possession." Verspyck, hearing the hoerah and the Wilhelmus, turned towards the Kraton and found [[Pakan Atjeh|Pakan Atjeh]] and the entrenchment of [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]] wholly abandoned by the enemy. "Thus the whole position of the Kraton was in our hands on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]], and with it the initial objective of the second expedition was attained."

## The Fortification of the Kraton

After the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] the Opperbestuur approved by telegram of [[1 February]] [[1 February]] [[1874]] the procedure and considered it also necessary to set up a fortification in Atjeh proper and to establish the Dutch there permanently. In the opinion of the supreme commander, the Kraton and some other points were to be made into permanent fortifications. The Dutch hoofd-stelling at Kota Radja came to be centred on the Kraton and its outworks at [[Penajoeng|Penajoeng]], kampong Djawa, and kampong Gedah. The Kraton was garrisoned by the Dutch throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and became the nucleus of the colonial establishment in Atjeh.

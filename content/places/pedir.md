**Pedir** (also spelled [[Pidië]]) was a landscape in the northern part of [[Atjeh]] that played a critical strategic role during the final phase of the [[Aceh War]]. Since the Dutch navy and troops controlled the coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], [[Pedir]] became the primary route by which [[Panglima Polim]]—the resistance leader of the [[XXII Moekims]]—maintained contact with the sea. Its subjugation was the first objective of the [[Pidië Expedition]] in [[1898]].

## Strategic importance

Before the [[1898]] campaign, [[Pedir]] served as the lifeline for the resistance in the interior. Since Dutch forces controlled the coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], [[Polim]] could only communicate with the outside world by traversing the mountains from the [[XXII Moekims]] through [[Pedir]] to the north coast. Cutting off this route was therefore essential to isolating the resistance.

The instructions given to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in [[March 1898]] explicitly stated that the expedition should begin at [[Segli]] in [[Pedir]] and advance over the mountains toward [[Polim]]'s territory. Once [[Pedir]] was secured, [[Polim]]'s access to the sea would be severed, and the Dutch supply and reinforcement route would replace his.

## The expedition and its results

The [[Pidië Expedition]] began on [[1 June]] [[1898]] at [[Segli]]. [[Van Heutsz]], supported by [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], secured [[Pedir]] and then advanced into the [[VII Moekims]]—the mountain district between [[Pedir]] and the [[XXII Moekims]]. There [[Polim]] had positioned his bands, but they were gradually driven out through sustained military pressure.

The capture of [[Keumala]] on [[14 June]] [[1898]]—only two weeks after the campaign began—demonstrated the effectiveness of the new approach. Following the capture, [[Pedir]] remained under Dutch control, and the coastal blockade was completed. The resistance leader, deprived of his sea connection and pursued relentlessly, was eventually forced into the highlands of [[Samalanga]] and beyond.

**Alue Rasa** (rendered in the OCR of *The Achehnese* as **Alura'sa**) was one of the principal gampongs of [[Banda Acheh|the capital]] of Acheh, listed by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] among the fine and prosperous villages that, before the Dutch war, formed the nucleus of settlement round the [[The Dalam|Dalam]] or royal residence of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan]]. The centre of these gampongs, with its mosque and market-place, was called Banda Acheh, "the capital or trading mart of Acheh", and gave the tone to the whole country in matters of custom and dress.

The full list of the principal gampongs of the capital given by Snouck Hurgronje runs: Gampong Jawa, Pande, Peunayong, Lam Bhu, [[Lueng Bata|Lueng Bata]], Lam Seupeung, Ateueung, Batoh and Alura'sa (Alue Rasa). The inhabitants of these and the neighbouring villages, together with their language and customs, were distinguished by the epithet *banda* — that is, town-bred or civilized — and those of other districts who conformed as much as possible to the tone of the capital enjoyed the same title. In contrast with these, all others who spoke in their own local dialects and were unacquainted with the manners of the town were called *dōseu* (like *dōseu* in the Sundanese country), that is, countrified or uncivilized.

Alue Rasa's inclusion in this enumeration marks it as a settlement of the lowland *banda* population, on the same level of civic standing as [[Gampong Jawa]] (the Javanese quarter), Pande (the smiths' quarter), Peunayong (the Chinese quarter) and [[Lueng Bata]] (which later became a separate mukim of the [[XXV Mukims]]). The Achehnese war and the Dutch bombardment of [[Kuta Raja|the capital]] are not described in Vol. I in detail, but the prosperous pre-war cluster of which Alue Rasa formed a part was largely destroyed in the course of the conflict.

The name "Alue Rasa" itself is composed of the Achehnese *alue* (a small watercourse or stream-bed, a common element in the toponymy of the lowlands) and *rasa*, the meaning of which in this particular compound is not explained by Snouck Hurgronje. Such compounds are characteristic of the village-names of the Achehnese lowlands, which (in contrast to the obscure names of countries and races) are, as the author observes, "as a rule fairly easy of analysis."

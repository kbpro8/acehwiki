**The Gajolanden** — the Gayo Lands — is the highland region of central [[Atjeh|Aceh]] inhabited by the Gajo (Gayo) people, the region through which the [[Gajoweg]] runs from [[Bireuën]] to [[Takengeun]] on Laut Tawar. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), the Gajolanden are the book's — and the lieutenant's journey through Aceh — destination: the beautiful lake of the Gajolanden at the end of the Gajoweg, and the region of the [Gajo- en Alaslanden tochten] of Van Heutsz's period of glory.

## The Gajoweg and the Gajolanden

The Gajoweg now leads to [[Takengeun]] on the beautiful lake in the Gajolanden, over a distance of 103 km. At km 36 the administrative border of the Gayo Lands begins. The landscape changed and became more park-like. The Gajolanden are the book's — and the lieutenant's — destination: the highland region of central Aceh whose administrative border begins at km 36 of the Gajoweg.

## The blangs and the Boerni-dit and Boer-ni-dat

The sun was overpowering on the blangs (Gayo plains), but a cool wind blew over the endless, rolling grass-plains. In the distance the blue mountain-ranges and volcanoes loomed up: the Boerni-dit and the Boer-ni-dat. The blangs and the Boerni-dit and Boer-ni-dat are the landscape of the Gajolanden around Takengeun.

## The Gajo and Alaslanden expeditions

The [Gajo- en Alaslanden tochten] — the expeditions to the Gayo Lands and the Alaslanden — were among the great successes of Van Heutsz's period of glory. The Gajolanden are therefore the book's — and the colonial memory's — marker of the all-out offensive in the highlands of central Aceh: the region that the [Gajo- en Alaslanden tochten] opened to the colonial army and that the Gajoweg — built by [Heinzenknecht](/people/heinzenknecht) under Van Heutsz — connected to Bireuën.

## Datoek and Takengeun

The old hunter [[Datoek]] — before settling at [[Blang Rakal]] in [[1917]] — had often made journeys to Takengeun. The beautiful time of those journeys — when a good full-grown horse cost only two ringgit (guilders) — is the book's — and Datoek's — marker of the old days of the Gajolanden.

## Significance

The Gajolanden are significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the highland region of central Aceh that the [[Gajoweg]] connects to Bireuën and that the [Gajo- en Alaslanden tochten] opened to the colonial army. The book's argument — that the Gajolanden were the destination of the lieutenant's journey through Aceh, the region of the blangs and the Boerni-dit and Boer-ni-dat, and the old days of Datoek's journeys to Takengeun — is the book's marker of the Gajolanden as the highland region of central Aceh. The Gajolanden are therefore the book's marker of the all-out offensive in the highlands and of the old days of the Gajolanden.

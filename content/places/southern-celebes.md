**Southern Celebes** is a region in the island of Celebes (modern Sulawesi) in the eastern part of the Netherlands-Indian archipelago, which is mentioned in *The Achehnese* Vol. I as an "outlying Dutch possession" cited by the Dutch scholar [[L. W. C. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] in his discussion of the adat of dividing common earnings between husband and wife.

## Van den Berg's "Specially Javanese" Adat

Snouck Hurgronje's reference to Southern Celebes comes in his chapter on the law of property in marriage, in the course of a sharp criticism of Van den Berg's essay on the *Afwijkingen* ("deviations") from Mohammedan law in Netherlands-India. After observing that in Acheh, as in [[Java]], [[Madura]], and "most Malayan countries," the property accumulated during a marriage by the respective toil of husband and wife is divided in equal shares between them on divorce, Snouck turns to Van den Berg's account of the same usage.

"[[L. W. C. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] mentions this adat in his essay on the *Afwijkingen*, pp. 474 et seq. In regard to this subject he makes fewer of the gross blunders into which he elsewhere falls, but here again he gives us nothing but what he has derived from books without a trace of 'notes personally made by him.' He might for instance have discovered by personal enquiry that the exceptions he mentions ([[Banten]] and a number of other places) are really no exceptions at all; the only reason why the *saguna-sakaya* or common earnings are not divided in these districts is that they have no existence, since the women there do not assist their husbands in their work."

## The Southern Celebes Argument

Snouck's specific reference to Southern Celebes comes in his refutation of Van den Berg's inference that the adat was specially Javanese: "He might also have found out that the division in the proportion of 2 to 1, though of frequent occurrence, is by no means universal, since in many places it is the custom to enquire first into the circumstances, and then to decide what is the just proportion which each has earned. In [[Madiun]] for instance, division into equal parts is very customary. On page 477 Van den Berg makes the curious deduction that this adat is specially Javanese, because it is also to be met with in outlying Dutch possessions such as Southern Celebes."

The point of the criticism is that Van den Berg reasoned backwards: from the presence of the adat in outlying Dutch possessions like Southern Celebes, he concluded that the usage must be Javanese in origin. Snouck, by contrast, treats it as a widespread Malayan usage with no specially Javanese character.

## Significance

Southern Celebes appears in Vol. I not for its own sake but as a comparative example illustrating the breadth of a custom — the equal division of the *saguna-sakaya* — that Snouck regards as common to the entire Malay-Indonesian world. Its mention alongside [[Acheh]], [[Java]], [[Madura]], [[Madiun]], and [[Banten]] reflects the comparative method by which Snouck sought to distinguish what was specifically Achehnese from what was common to the wider Malayan region.

**Langsa** was the hoofdplaats of the [[Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh|Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh]] and, by [[1917]], the second city of the *gewest* — the trade-centre of the great European rubber, tobacco, and petroleum cultures of the eastern lowlands and the natural rival of [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] for commercial primacy in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* describes Langsa as the place where, after the *pacificatie* under [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]], "eene jonge, frissche handels-energie" unfolded, transforming a town "als het ware uit den moerassigen bodem gestampte stad" — a city, as it were, hammered out of the swampy ground — into the principal European centre of the eastern coast.

## Geography and the Perlak inheritance

The kuststrook of the eastern Oostkust "behoudt dit karakter alleen … tot Langsa," beyond which it widens into the great Sumatran heuvelland. The hinterland of Langsa, together with that of Tamiang, was "tot voor weinige jaren nog vrijwel geheel onontgonnen wildernis" — until a few years previously almost wholly unopened wilderness. The onderafdeeling Langsa comprised the landschap Peureulaʼ (the remnant of the former sultanaat of Perlak), the landschap Langsa itself (formerly a underhoorigheid of Peureulaʼ), and the enclave of Soengèë Raja; all three had been settled by peperplanters from [[Samalanga|Samalanga]], Lhoʼ Seumawè, and the Pasé-streek.

## European cultures and the Gouvernements-rubber-onderneming

The Oostkust was the centre of European cultures in Atjeh: *De Buitenbezittingen* notes the "uitgestrektheid, opkomst en ontwikkeling" of the hevea, koffie, and other cultures, with the *geïnteresseerd kapitaal ter Oostkust* exceeding that of any other afdeeling. The *Gouvernements-rubber-onderneming te Langsa*, founded to encourage the industry, was the largest single plantation in the *gewest*. The *Credietbank "Langsa"* (founded early [[1915]]) served the European concerns of the entire afdeeling Oostkust.

### The Government Rubber Plantation

Around [[1907]], the colonial government began clearing land near Langsa for a rubber plantation — a unique state enterprise in the Indies, testing first *Ficus elastica* and then *Hevea brasiliensis*. The plantation grew rapidly:
- [[1908]]: 329 *bouws* with 76,000 trees.
- [[1909]]: 476 *bouws* with 116,000 trees.
- By [[1914]]: total investment approximately 800,000 guilders.

Staffed with contract laborers (primarily Javanese, with some local Acehnese), the plantation demonstrated that the Langsa region was highly suitable for rubber cultivation, providing the proof of concept needed to attract private investors.

### The Private Boom

Following the government's initiative, a massive influx of private capital transformed the area. The number of land concessions in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] grew from just **6 concessions** covering ±1,000 *bouws* in [[1904]] to **148 concessions** covering nearly 300,000 *bouws* by [[1913]]. In the under-subdivision of Langsa alone, 28 private concessions covered 54,000 *bouws* by [[1913]]. Most were established for rubber cultivation, with robusta coffee often planted as an initial "catch crop." This transformation turned a region described as "vast, nearly impassable swampy areas" into a "rich and well-developed agricultural area."

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Langsa and the Tramway

Langsa also served as the eastern terminus of the second [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh tramway]] line — built between [[1899]] and [[1906]] from [[Sigli]] to Langsa — and as the originating point of the [[1910]]–[[1912]] extension onward to [[Koeala Simpang]]. The [[1906]] completion of the Sigli–Langsa line opened a direct railway connection from [[Kota Radja]] to the eastern coast, which the Bureau identifies as one of the principal instruments of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]]. The [[1913]] extensions (approved by Gouvernementsbesluiten of June, October, and November [[1913]]) further extended the line through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]] and toward [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]].

## Haven and trade

The haven of Koeala Langsa — second only to [[Sabang|Sabang]] in importance among the Atjehsche havens — was fostered by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Generaal Van Heutsz]], who "trachtte in te blazen door de sluiting van andere havens als het meer Noordelijk gelegen Idi" — sought to invigorate it by closing other havens such as the more northerly Idi. Despite this *kunstmatige bescherming*, the haven's ligging was favourable; vrij diepgaande schepen could lie alongside a schroefpaal-steiger and discharge directly to the spoorweg. The haven received 577 steamers (285,000 M³) in [[1913]], 465 (235,000) in [[1914]], and 437 (207,000) in [[1915]] — figures exceeded only by Sabang. The *in-* and *uitvoerrechten* of Langsa grew from ƒ27,000/ƒ5,000 in [[1910]] to ƒ90,000/ƒ24,000 in [[1914]], the steepest rise in the *gewest*. Plans for a great ocean-haven at Telok Taboean, ten km east of Pangkalan Soesoe in the Aroebaai, were abandoned in favour of simpler installations at Pangkalan Soesoe itself, where the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij, the Atjeh-tram, and the Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij all maintained installations.

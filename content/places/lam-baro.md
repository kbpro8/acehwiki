**Lam Baro** is a locality in [[Acheh]] mentioned in *The Achehnese* only in the poetic context of the Achehnese wedding — specifically in the *pantun*-like exchanges of verse recited by the elders of the bride's and bridegroom's parties during the marriage procession. Like [[Lubo']], Padang, Tama', and Pante Teungoh, it figures as a topographical allusion in the conventional repertory of nuptial verse, chosen less for its political importance than for its rhyming and rhythmic convenience.

## The Wedding Pantun

During the marriage ceremonies described by Snouck Hurgronje, the elder of the bridegroom's gampong (designated "B") responds to the elder of the bride's party ("A") in a series of antiphonal stanzas, each opening with an evocative image drawn from the Achehnese landscape. One of the most characteristic runs:

> *B.* In Lam Baro sugarcane is planted,
> In the III Mukims keutila (katela) is sown by dibbling.
> With due respect, Teuku,
> Here it befits us to remain, this sufficeth.

The image is that of two complementary agrarian landscapes: sugarcane at Lam Baro and cassava (katela) in the [[Lhee Mukim Kayee Adang|III Mukims]]. The verse forms part of the courteous formulae by which the bridegroom's party declines, for the moment, to enter the bride's house.

## Place-Names in Achehnese Verse

The recurrence of place-names like Lam Baro in the wedding pantuns reflects the strong localism of Achehnese poetry, in which the names of fields, gardens and villages supplied both the local colour and the metrical filler that the structure of the verse required. While the exact location of Lam Baro cannot be fixed with certainty from Snouck Hurgronje's account, its pairing with the III Mukims suggests a locality within the lowland zone of [[Great Acheh]] in which the principal centres of population and cultivation lay. The name itself — *Lam Baro* literally "the new place" — was a common Achehnese toponym.

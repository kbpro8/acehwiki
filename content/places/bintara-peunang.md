**Bintara Peunang** — printed *Bintare Penang* in the source — was the district whose head was, according to [[H. van Kol]] speaking in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[17 November]] [[1897]], arrested by the Dutch "op trouwelooze wijze" — in a faithless manner.

## The allegation

Van Kol named the case as the second in a short series of treacherous arrests of Acehnese chiefs: "Op verraderlijke wijze nam men den [[Radja van Samalangan|radja van Samalangan]] gevangen, dien men geweren beloofde: het hoofd van Bintare Penang werd eveneens op trouwelooze wijze gearresteerd, en met [[Toekoe Njak Amat]] heeft men dat, naar allen schijn, nog in de vorige maand bij [[Kroeng Raja|Kroeng raja]] herhaald." — In a treacherous manner the radja of Samalangan was taken prisoner, to whom rifles had been promised: the head of Bintara Peunang was likewise arrested in a faithless manner, and with Toekoe Njak Amat the same was, to all appearance, repeated last month near Kroeng Raja.

The three cases formed part of his catalogue of Dutch breaches of the *[[Lois et coutumes de la guerre|lois et coutumes de la guerre]]*, alongside the shooting of [[Njahim]], the taking of the daughter of [[Wakil Joesoef]] as a hostage, the killing of [[Joed Mohmed Lamtengah]] and the exposure of convicts to hostile fire.

The source gives neither the personal name of the head concerned, nor the date, nor the circumstances of the arrest, nor his subsequent fate; nor does it locate the district more precisely. The Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] did not answer the allegation specifically.

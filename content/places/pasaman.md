**Pasaman** — the historic pepper port on the west coast of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] south of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — was one of the chief pepper ports of the Sumatran west coast in the early-modern period and a port of call of the Dutch East India trade. Pasaman was visited by [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]] in [[1600]] without result, together with [[Priaman|Priaman]] and [[Tikoe|Tikoe]], "waar overal slechts weinig peper in voorraad was". Pasaman is one of the principal west-coast pepper ports named in Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The 1600 Van Caerden visit

[[Paulus van Caerden|Van Caerden]], sent by his admiral [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]] to the west coast of Sumatra, "bezocht daar zonder resultaat Priaman, Tikoe en Pasaman, waar overal slechts weenig peper in voorraad was". The visit was the first Dutch commercial call at Pasaman and showed that the west-coast pepper ports, like Atjeh itself, were subject to seasonal fluctuations in supply. Van Caerden's failure at [[Priaman|Priaman]], [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] and Pasaman was the proximate cause of his decision to try Atjeh for cargo, "hoewel hij wist wat [[Cornelis de Houtman|De Houtman]] daar was overkomen".

## The Westkust closure

Like [[Priaman|Priaman]] and [[Tikoe|Tikoe]], Pasaman was subject to the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s policy of centralising the European pepper trade at Atjeh. The west-coast ports were closed to foreigners except at Atjeh — a policy that [[Jan Grenier|Jan Grenier]] encountered at [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] on [[19 February]] [[1602]] and that shaped the early-modern commercial geography of the Sumatran pepper trade. The closure was largely effective in the early Dutch-Indies period; the Dutch and English were forced to call at Atjeh for their pepper, and the west-coast ports could be visited only when the sultan's enforcement lapsed.

## Significance

Pasaman is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal west-coast pepper port of Sumatra south of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] (alongside [[Priaman|Priaman]] and [[Tikoe|Tikoe]]) and a port of call of the Dutch East India trade. The port's seasonal fluctuations in supply and the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s policy of centralising the European pepper trade at Atjeh together frame the early-modern commercial geography of the Sumatran pepper trade. Pasaman's role in the early Dutch-Indies trade would grow in the seventeenth century as the Dutch shifted their pepper sourcing from Atjeh to the west coast and to [[Bantam|Bantam]] and the Sunda islands.

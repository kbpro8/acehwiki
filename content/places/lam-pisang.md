**Lam-Pisang** — written in Dutch as also *Lampisang*, *Lam Pisang* — was a major kampong in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]], the site of Umar's dwelling (*verblijf*), and the place from which Umar wrote his three letters to the [[Governor of Atjeh]] ([[30 March]], [[12 April]], [[23 April]] [[1896]]). As described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, Lam-Pisang was the symbolic and political centre of Umar's *stellingen* in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]], and the principal objective of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]]. The kampong was captured by the 5e Bataljon on [[24 May]] [[1896]] (1e luit. [[M. D. J. de Jongh|de Jongh]] first reaching Umar's *verblijf* at 10:35 VM); some small cannons, furniture, and Umar's correspondence were captured, and the kampong was subsequently destroyed with *dynamiet*, *teer*, and *petroleum*. A *bivak* was established at Lam-Pisang after its capture. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] a Dutch patrouille burned the sentry-houses and the gates of Umar's dwelling at Lam-Pisang.

## Description and Location


Lam-Pisang lay in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]] of [[Teuku Umar]] between the kampong of [[Lam-Asan]] on the south-east and the heights of [[Boekit Seboen]] and [[Lam-Poelau]] on the north-west. The kampong was the site of Umar's dwelling, which served as his political and military headquarters throughout his period as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] ([[1893]]–[[1896]]) and after his [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. The kampong was connected by communication trenches to the neighbouring *versterkingen* of [[Lam-Asan]] (with its *Binasa* versterkingen), [[Pakan Badak]] (site of Umar's house and a *bivak*), and [[Pajating|Paja Trieng]]. The [[Bineh Kroeng|Bineh-Kroeng]] kampong was burned together with Lam-Pisang on [[24 May]] [[1896]]. The kampong was bounded on the south by the road toward [[Pasar Oleh-Gli]] and on the north by the heights of [[Boekit Seboen]].

## Historical Background


Lam-Pisang had been the residence of [[Teuku Umar]] throughout his period as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government ([[1893]]–[[1896]]) and was the symbolic centre of his authority in the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV Moekims]]. Umar's wife [[Tjoet Nja Din]] was associated with the kampong, and it was from Lam-Pisang that Umar wrote his three letters to the [[Governor of Atjeh]] of [[30 March]], [[12 April]], and [[23 April]] [[1896]] explaining his defection and demanding ƒ150,000 per month and a Royal decoration. After the [[Bombardment of Lampisang|bombardment of Lampisang]] began on [[27 April]] [[1896]], Umar reportedly brought his wounded to Lam-Pisang, and the kampong was the strategic keystone of his defensive *stelling* across the [[IV en VI Moekims|IV en VI Moekims]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations


Lam-Pisang was the principal objective of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]]. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the 9e Bataljon under [[J. F. Th. Veeren|luit.-kol. Veeren]] attacked the neighbouring *stellingen* of [[Lam-Asan]] (with its *Binasa Belang*, *Binasa Ketapang*, and *Balei Ba' Mó* versterkingen) in one continuous *aanval*, and the 5e compagnie of the 12e Bataljon fought through the [[Kloof van Beradin|valley of Beradin]] toward the eastern ridge. On [[24 May]] [[1896]] the 5e Bataljon advanced on Lam-Pisang from the southeast, and 1e luit. [[M. D. J. de Jongh|de Jongh]] of the 5e Bataljon was first to reach Umar's *verblijf* at 10:35 VM; some small cannons, furniture, and Umar's correspondence were captured. The kampong was subsequently destroyed with dynamite, tar, and petroleum; the [[Bineh Kroeng|Bineh-Kroeng]] kampong was burned together with Lam-Pisang. A *bivak* was established at Lam-Pisang after its capture. On [[25 May]] [[1896]] the 6e Bataljon's 3e compagnie stormed the *stelling* Daman–[[Lam-Tjroeng]]–[[Lam-Poelau]] just to the south. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] a Dutch patrouille burned the sentry-houses and the gates of Umar's dwelling at Lam-Pisang, and the [[Pasar Oleh-Gli]] market nearby. Lam-Pisang appears in [[Teuku Umar]]'s letters of [[30 March]], [[12 April]], and [[23 April]] [[1896]] as the place from which the letters were written.

## Significance


Lam-Pisang was the symbolic and political centre of [[Teuku Umar]]'s authority and the principal geographic object of the [[1896]] Dutch counter-offensive. The capture of the kampong on [[24 May]] [[1896]] and the destruction of Umar's dwelling with *dynamiet*, *teer*, and *petroleum* was the symbolic climax of the first semester of [[1896]]. The capture of Umar's correspondence — including the three letters of [[30 March]], [[12 April]], and [[23 April]] [[1896]] — provided the Dutch with documentary evidence of his defection and his demands. The continued Dutch presence at Lam-Pisang as a *bivak* after its capture demonstrated the post's strategic importance.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Lam Pisang** — modern *Lam Pisang* — is a small *kampong* on the road from [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] to [[Lho' Nga|Lho' Nga]], *Lam* being short for *Dalam*, palace. It is now a small *kampong*, formerly greater and more prominent. The road passes through a region with war-memory from the days of [[Teukoe Oemar|Oemar]], when much was burnt down.

A considerable trading centre. In the [[Hikayat Malem Dagang]], the army of [[Pochut Muhamat]] 'at Krueng Raya, a considerable trading centre, could not resist the temptation of looting all the cloth stuffs in the storehouses of the Kling traders.' Also, the tomb of Teungku di [[Batee Puteh]] (a saint) is situated on a hill near Krueng Raya (XXVI Mukims). In the epic, [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] assigned [[Ladong]] and Krueng Raya as freehold territory (wakeueh) to [[Si Ujut]] and [[Raja Raden]] on their arrival in [[Acheh]].

**Krueng Raya** was the northeastern coastal boundary point of [[Great Acheh]] on the North Coast. According to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]], the kernel of the [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese kingdom]] was bounded by a line extending from [[Kluang]] on the west to **Krueng Raya** on the North Coast, passing through [[Reueng-reueng]], [[Pancha]], and [[Janthoe]].

Krueng Raya thus marked the point beyond which the territory of [[Pidie]] (Pedir) began on the east coast road. It was a significant geographic marker separating the Acehnese heartland from the outer dependencies.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Krueng Raya

**Krueng Raya** is a locality on the North Coast of Acheh which, in *The Achehnese* Vol. I, marks the north-eastern extremity of the boundary of **Great Acheh** — the kernel of the sultanate as distinct from its outlying dependencies. It is named once, in Snouck Hurgronje's opening account of the limits of the country.

### Boundary of Great Acheh

Snouck distinguishes between the wider kingdom of Acheh (which the Achehnese themselves placed at **Tamiang** on the East Coast and at **Baros** on the West Coast) and the much more restricted territory they called **Acheh** proper, or "Great Acheh". "This kernel of the kingdom, which has supplied the outlying districts with a considerable portion of their inhabitants, and has constantly striven to exercise more or less dominion over them, is according to the Achehnese idea bounded by a line extending from **Kluang** on the West, to **Krueng Raya** on the North Coast, and passing through Reueng-reueng, Pancha and Janthoe." In a footnote he adds that "these three places lie on the footpaths uniting Acheh with the territory of Pidie (vulg. Pedir)," and that "we find the boundaries somewhat differently marked in the ordinary maps and in the above quoted brochure of [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]] p. 382."

### Significance

Although Krueng Raya appears in Vol. I only as a boundary point, the line of which it forms one terminus is of the first importance for Snouck's account of Achehnese political geography. Within this line lay the three sagis — the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]], [[XXV Mukims|XXV]] and [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]] — that made up Great Acheh proper. Beyond Krueng Raya to the east and north lay the dependencies of the East Coast, including [[Pidie]] and the [[East Coast of Acheh|pepper-planting regions]] whose manners Snouck treats as remote from those of the banda, or town-bred people of the capital.

The name **Krueng Raya** itself means literally "great river" in Achehnese (*krueng* = river, *raya* = great), a fitting designation for the boundary landmark of the kernel of the sultanate.

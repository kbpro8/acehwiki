**The Atjehtram** — the Atjeh tramway (Dutch: *Atjeh-tram*) — was the tram line that ran alongside the [[Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road (Dutch: *Groote Atjehweg*)]] across the lowland areas of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, forming together with the road and the [[Atjehspoorweg|Atjeh railway]] the transport network by which the residency (Dutch: *gewest*) of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] was opened to trade and settlement after the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]]. The tram is the place-level reference to the colonial tramway that is treated, at the organisational level, under [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]]; this article records its physical route, its spatial relationship to the road and the railway, and its place in the economic geography of the residency.

## Route and Spatial Relationship

The Atjehtram followed, in its principal lowland stretches, the alignment of the [[Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road]] between [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] and the post-line that extended eastward across the Noordkust toward the Oostkust. The first section — the *smalspoorbaan* from [[Koeta-Radja|Kota Radja]] to [[Olehleh|Olehleh]] built in [[1874]]–[[1875]] — supplied the bridgehead of the colonial forces at the harbour of [[Olehleh|Olehleh]] with the military base at [[Koeta-Radja|Kota Radja]], and was the foundation on which the later system was built. Subsequent extensions followed the line of the road eastward across the Goudberg to the Noordkust and onward to the Oostkust, gradually superseding the older system of ox-cart convoys as the principal means of transport between the colonial capital and the forward posts. The physical proximity of tram and road — the one carrying the rails, the other carrying the wheeled traffic — meant that the two infrastructures were, in the lowland stretches, visible from one another, and the traveller along the [[Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road]] could see the steam-tram of the *Dienst der S.S.* (Staatsspoorwegen) drawing its load of military stores or, later, of plantation rubber and copra along the parallel track.

## Role in the Economic Opening

The presence of the Atjehtram, alongside the [[Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road]], encouraged the intensified traffic, trade, and settlement that the source document *DLP-84-Atjeh* records as producing, in the lowland areas of the residency, "a more peaceful development and higher prosperity" than had been possible in the war years. The tram permitted the cost-effective movement of smallholder crops — pepper, rice, and coconuts — from the producing villages of the lowlands to the export harbours at [[Olehleh|Olehleh]] and, via the [[Atjehspoorweg|Atjeh railway]], to [[Belawan|Belawan]] and [[Sabang|Sabang]]. The shift from a war-footing to a civil-economic footing, registered in the physical coexistence of tram and road, is the spatial marker of the [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacificatie]] under [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] after [[1908]]. The tram's lowland route thus belongs to the same colonial-economic project as the [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajoweg]] cut through the highland jungle, and as the [[Atjehspoorweg|Atjeh railway]] built to connect the residency to the wider Sumatran network.

**Muara Aman** (also *Moeara Aman*) is a town in the Lebong district of [[Benkulen|Benkulen]] (Bencoolen), [[Sumatra]], in whose vicinity the famous [[Lebong Donok]] gold-bearing quartz outcrop was discovered in [[1895]]/[[1896]] by native gold washers — the discovery that led to the establishment of the "Lebong Goud Syndicaat" and, in [[1897]], to the founding of the Mining Company "Redjang Lebong". Muara Aman is the principal town of the Lebong gold-mining district, and is accordingly the geographical reference-point for the most productive gold-mining operation of the colonial Netherlands East Indies.

## The Lebong Donok discovery

For the full account of the [[1895]]/[[1896]] discovery of the [[Lebong Donok]] gold-bearing quartz outcrop "near Muara Aman" see the entry on [[Lebong Donok]]. In summary: European interest in the Lebong gold was aroused when samples of gold ores were shown to the manager of the Suban Ajam coffee estate by Hadji Ismael of [[Pasar Tjurup]]; the native gold washers then showed the European a large outcrop of gold-bearing quartz at Lebong Donok near Muara Aman; and this discovery led to the establishment of the Lebong Goud Syndicaat and, in [[1897]], of the Mining Company "Redjang Lebong".

## Significance for Atjeh

Muara Aman is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because the Lebong Donok gold discovery in its vicinity is the founding episode of European gold-mining in Sumatra, and accordingly the chronological and methodological pattern for the subsequent Atjeh gold explorations of [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]], [[B. B. Lindberg|Lindberg]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]] ([[1922]]) and [[C. de Groot|De Groot]] ([[1946]]). The Lebong gold-mining district, with Muara Aman as its principal town, was the dominant gold-mining district of the Sumatran gold-silver belt, and the [[P. Hövig|Hövig]] [[1914]] monograph on the Lebong gold ores is the foundational source for the colonial study of the belt that extends "from Atjeh in the North to Benkulen in the South".

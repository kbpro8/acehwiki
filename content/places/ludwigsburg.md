**Ludwigsburg** was a tobacco plantation on Sumatra's East Coast attacked by [[Acehnese raids on East Sumatra|Acehnese raiding parties]] in [[1876]], one of three plantations — alongside [[Glen Bervi]] and [[Tamaram]] — struck during the wave of Acehnese incursions into the northern [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]] in the mid-[[1870s]]. Its name — a German toponym — reflects the prevalence of German planters and plantation managers among the early European estate community on Sumatra's East Coast.

## Context

Ludwigsburg lay in the northern plantation belt adjacent to the frontier with [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Acehnese]] territory. The attacks on Ludwigsburg, [[Glen Bervi]], and [[Tamaram]] in [[1876]] occurred as the [[Atjeh War]], begun in [[1873]], pushed armed Acehnese parties across the colonial frontier into the plantation districts. At [[Tamaram]], the most severely affected of the three, the manager was wounded and an assistant killed. The *Kedjoeroean* of [[Bohorok]] openly collaborated with the raiders and fled with his followers to the [[Alas]] lands afterward.

The raids prompted the [[Sultan of Langkat]] — whose territory lay along the frontier — to take an active role in resisting the Acehnese incursions; he was elevated from *Radja* to *Sultan* in [[1887]] in recognition.

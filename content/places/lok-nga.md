**Lok-Nga** — *Lhos Nga* in older Dutch sources, *Lhoknga* in modern Acehnese orthography — was an Acehnese kampong and Dutch military bivouac on the west coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], south-west of the [[geconcentreerde linie|concentrated defensive line]] around [[Koeta Radja|Koetaradja]], that was authorised as the site of a *versterkt kampement* (fortified camp) in August [[1896]] and that served as the principal Dutch base for operations on the west coast in the second semester of the [[1896]] campaign. Lok-Nga is one of the principal Dutch posts of the [[1896]] campaign in *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* (1913).

**Lhoknga** — printed *Loknga* in the source — was one of the three Dutch bivouacs in [[Groot-Atjeh]] by which, according to [[Van Vlijmen]] speaking in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]], the Dutch commanded the whole [[Atjeh valley|Atjeh-vallei]].

## Topography

The bivouac of Lok-Nga stood on the west coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], south-west of the [[geconcente linie|concentrated line]], on the [[Kroeng Ritieng|Ritieng bay]] (*Kroeng-Ritieng-baai*). The site was connected by sea to [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]] and by land to the [[geconcente linie|concentrated line]] through the [[Glé Broeë|Glé-Loedah pass]]. The *Kroeng Ritieng* river reaches the sea at Lok-Nga, and the bay offers a relatively sheltered landing-ground on a coast otherwise exposed to the swell of the Indian Ocean.

## The 1896 campaign

Lok-Nga was the principal Dutch base for operations on the west coast in the second semester of the [[1896]] campaign. The *Tweede Deel* of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* records the *machtiging verleend tot den bouw van een versterkt kampement te Lok-Nga* (authorisation granted for the construction of a fortified camp at Lok-Nga) in August [[1896]] on the recommendation of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Kol. Van Heutsz]], and the use of Lok-Nga as the base for the [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|expedition to Lepong of 10–14 August 1896]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]]. The bivouac was also the base for the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée]] operations on the west coast in September–December [[1896]].

The [[Occupation of Kroeng Raba (Lok Nga) (15 July 1896)|occupation of Kroeng Raba (Lok Nga) of 15 July 1896]] — a closely related event in the wiki — established the Dutch forward position at [[Kroeng Raba|Kroëng Raba]] on the eastern wing; Lok-Nga served the same function on the western wing, providing the Dutch with a forward base for operations against [[Teukoe Oemar|Teuku Umar]]'s strongholds on the west coast.

## Significance

Lok-Nga is one of the principal Dutch posts of the [[1896]] campaign in *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* and the principal Dutch base for operations on the west coast in the second semester. The bivouac's role as the principal Dutch forward position on the west coast made it a key operational feature of the [[1896]] campaign.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Lho' Nga** — pronounced *Loh Na*, modern *Lhoknga* — is the chief place (*hoofdplaats*) of the *onderafdeeling* Lho' Nga of the *afdeeling* [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], situated on the Westkust about 15 km from [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. The 25 and 26 *moekims* form the *onderafdeeling* Lho' Nga. The road from Kota Radja goes through [[Lam Pisang|Lam Pisang]] — *Lam* being short for *Dalam*, palace — a small *kampong* now, formerly greater and more prominent, and further through a region with war-memory from the days of [[Teukoe Oemar|Oemar]], when much was burnt down. Then the road breaks through the low mountains, the kloof [[Bradeuen|Bradeuen]] (pronounced *Bradun*). Then one is in the 4 *moekims* that belong to the *onderafdeeling* Lho' Nga, and one drives into a splendid *tjemara*-forest that stands right on the sea on a hilly ground with a lovely sandy beach before it. On Java and Sumatra one has on some points of the southern and western coast places that surprise by the impression of Western breadth and mildness which they at once give the visitor, accustomed as he is to the landscape in plain under an untempered broiling sun. Such a surprising place one finds at Lho' Nga on the sea. The [[Deli-Atjeh-Tabak-Mij|Deli-Atjeh-Tabak-Mij]] more than twenty years before Broersma's visit made trials with tobacco in the 26 *moekims* as well as at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]]. Not much luck was had with the trials, though a good quality could be obtained. *'Zoo heette het altoos, maar tot een duurzame onderneming kwam het niet. De reden was en is, dat men wel goede tabak verkrijgt, maar geen Deli-Langkat.'*

## In the debate of [[1897]]

[[Van Vlijmen]] attributed the improvement in the condition of the valley — at rest, with the population returned to its rice fields — to the establishment of the three bivouacs of [[Tjot Mautjang]], [[Indrapoeri]] and Loknga: "Door die drie kampementen beheerschen wij de geheele vallei en tevens houden wij in ontzag de nog vijandige hoofden die zich hebben teruggetrokken in het bergterrein, omdat die posten in de onmiddellijke nabijheid daarvan zijn gelegen." — By those three camps we command the whole valley, and at the same time we hold in awe the still hostile chiefs who have withdrawn into the mountainous terrain, because those posts lie in the immediate neighbourhood of it.

## Context

Lhoknga lies on the west shore of the promontory of Groot-Atjeh, at the point where the valley meets the sea, and thus commanded the western approach to the [[Atjeh valley]] as [[Indrapoeri]] commanded the upper valley and [[Tjot Mautjang]] the centre. Its occupation belongs to the [[Post-1896 system in Atjeh|system]] of concentrated *kampementen* and mobile columns which the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] defended in the same debate as having been born of the pressure of circumstances after the failure of the [[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde stelling]].

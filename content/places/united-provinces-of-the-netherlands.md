**United Provinces of the Netherlands** was the sovereign state of the **Dutch Republic** that, from the early seventeenth century onward, exercised colonial authority over the Indonesian archipelago, including the Sultanate of **Aceh**. The Republic’s naval and commercial institutions—most notably the **Dutch East India Company** and the **Regiment de Marine**—provided the military framework for the series of **Aceh‑Dutch conflicts** that spanned the seventeenth to the twentieth centuries.

## Political Structure and Colonial Policy
The United Provinces consisted of the provinces of **Holland**, **West‑Friesland**, **Utrecht**, **Zeeland**, **Gelderland**, **Overijssel**, and **Friesland**. Its federal assembly, the **Staten‑Generaal**, issued a series of **resoluties** (resolutions) concerning the organization of naval forces, such as the *Resolutie van de Heeren Staten van Holland en West‑Vriesland* of *[[1666]]* and *[[1672]]*, which regulated the recruitment of **mariniers** for overseas service.

## Connection to Aceh
The Republic’s overseas policy was implemented through the **VOC**, which negotiated treaties with the **Sultanate of Aceh** (e.g., the *Treaty of **[[1629]]***). The naval regiments listed in the 1665‑1667 documents were later deployed in the **Aceh War** (1873‑1904), illustrating the continuity between early naval administration and later colonial warfare.

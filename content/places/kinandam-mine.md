**The Kinandam mine** is a gold mine on the [[Westkust van Sumatra|West Coast of Sumatra]] operated by the Mining Company "Kinandam, Sumatra" (M. Co. Kinandam, Sumatra), one of the European gold-mining operations of the West-Coast gold-mining district of Sumatra recorded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) Table 28 B (the West-Coast gold-mining district list). The Kinandam mine is included in this wiki because its corporate parent — the [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] — was also the operator of the [[Bukit Durian]], [[Bukit Pulai]] and [[Sarik]] coal concessions on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West coast of Atjeh]] immediately to the north, and the Kinandam mine is accordingly part of the same integrated coal-and-gold corporate enterprise that produced the only documented colonial-era coal-mining concessions on Atjeh-relevant territory.

## Production figures

Van Bemmelen's Table 28 B records the Kinandam mine (No. 8 in the West-Coast district list) with the following production figures:

- **Period:** [[1917]]–[[1918]]
- **Gold:** 89 kg
- **Silver:** 60 kg
- **Tonnage:** 6,000 tons of ore
- **Total value:** ƒ150,500
- **Approximate value recovered per ton of ore:** ƒ25
- **Au:Ag ratio:** 1:2

The Kinandam mine was accordingly a small short-lived operation: two years of production, 89 kg of gold, and a total value of ƒ150,500 — modest figures compared with the great Lebong mines ([[Lebong Donok]] with 41,535 kg of gold over 42 years, or Simau with 37,874 kg of gold over 31 years), but respectable for the West-Coast district, where the total production of all listed mines (including Kinandam) was 10,789 kg of gold and 346,839 kg of silver over the period [[1912]]–[[1940]].

## The Charlotte connection

Van Bemmelen's Table 28 B lists the Kinandam mine with the company name "M. Co. Kinandam, Sumatra, Charlotte", which suggests that the mine was operated under the corporate name "Charlotte" — possibly a reference to a separate mining company that worked the Kinandam concessions under the parent Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij. The "Charlotte" name is otherwise unexplained in Van Bemmelen's text, and the relationship between the Kinandam mine, the Charlotte name, and the [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] is accordingly best understood as a parent-subsidiary relationship of the kind common in the colonial East Indian mining industry.

## Significance for Atjeh

The Kinandam mine is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because its corporate parent — the [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] — was also the operator of the [[Bukit Durian]], [[Bukit Pulai]] and [[Sarik]] coal concessions on the West coast of Atjeh, and the Kinandam mine is accordingly part of the same integrated coal-and-gold corporate enterprise that produced the only documented colonial-era coal-mining concessions on Atjeh-relevant territory. The closure of the Kinandam mine in [[1918]] (or shortly thereafter) — combined with the closure of the related [[Salida]] gold mine in [[1927]] — accordingly entailed the closure of the company's coal mines on the West-Atjeh/Painan border, and the [[1934]] and [[1941]] returns of the coal-mining rights to the Government mark the formal dissolution of the Kinandam-Sumatra corporate enterprise of which the Kinandam mine was the founding gold operation.

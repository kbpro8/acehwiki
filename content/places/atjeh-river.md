**Atjeh River** was the major waterway flowing through the interior of [[Acheh]]. Its navigability made it a strategic route for both Dutch and Acehnese forces, and control of its mouth was essential for Dutch supply lines during the [[Aceh War (1873–1904)]].

## Dutch Military Operations

In December [[1873]], the Dutch assembled a **sloepenflottielje** (boat flotilla) under the command of [[Captain-terr zee W. Sutherland]] to force the river’s blockades and enable the landing of troops at the river mouth. The operation, conducted on [[26 December]] [[1873]], involved the transport of supplies and the embarkation of marine units for further inland advances toward [[Pedir]].  

A later expedition on [[5 February]] [[1876]] attempted to cross the river, but Dutch forces found the waterway not easily fordable, halting their advance. This difficulty underscored the river’s role as a natural barrier in the campaign.

## Strategic Role

Control of the river was essential for supply lines and for reaching interior districts such as the [[IX Moekims]]. After the military campaigns, the river served as a route to the royal capital [[Kota Radja]], linking coastal points with the heart of the sultanate.

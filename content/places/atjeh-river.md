The **Atjeh River** (Dutch *Atjeh-rivier*) was the river of Groot-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] whose valley carried the capital [[Koeta Radja]] and most of the province's road- and tram-network, spanned by iron bridges at [[Glé Kambing]] and [[Lambaroe|Lambaroh]] ([[1882]]), and forming with the [[Pidië]] river the watershed over which the mountain tramway was built in [[1903]]–[[1908]].

## Roads, bridges and tramway

The great transport road to Glé Kambing, on the left bank of the Atjeh river, was wholly finished with its necessary works of art by [[1882]], as was the road from Aneügalong to Toengkōp; at Glé Kambing and at Lambaroh iron bridges were laid over the Atjeh river, while the smaller bridges on several roads were as much as possible replaced by iron ones, to take from the evilly disposed the opportunity of breaking the communications. Along the river's valley ran the [[Atjeh Tram|tram line]] through a fairly densely populated tract — from Koeta Radja toward Lambaroe and, after [[1897]], onward to Glé Kambing opposite [[Indrapoeri]] (15 km, opened November [[1897]]) and to [[Seulimeum]] (c. 18 km, opened [[1 November]] [[1898]]).

## The watershed

Already in [[1898]] Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] formed the plan of carrying the line on from Seulimeum over the watershed between the valley of the Atjeh river and that of the Pidië river; a preliminary survey by the engineers showed the ground very accidented, and chief engineer [[A. E. Wijss]] roughly estimated the cost at over three million guilders. The topographic survey of [[1901]] was followed by the provisional design of [[1902]]; building began on both sides in [[1903]], beset by the difficulties of material and labour transport in the dry, unhealthy tract and by the numerous bridges, and a limited service was instituted on [[15 September]] [[1908]], joining the nets of Groot-Atjeh and the coasts over the watershed (see [[Atjeh Tram]]).

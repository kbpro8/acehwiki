**Pulo U** — vulgo **Simalur** (the modern **Simeulue**) — is an island lying off the west coast of [[Acheh|Achehnese] territory, whose headman figures in *The Achehnese* Vol. I as a survival of the older port-king's supremacy, reduced in modern times to a merely local dignity.

## The Panguléë Side of Meulaboh

Snouck Hurgronje records that the **Panguléë Side** of [[Meulaboh]], who was undoubtedly at one time an official under the chief of that place, charged with the task of making judicial enquiries, "is now in fact no more than headman of Pulo U (vulg. Simalur)." The port-king of [[Meulaboh]] — like the other port-kings of the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coast]] — had once enjoyed a supremacy extending over the neighbouring islands and over the adjacent stretch of the mainland; and the office of the Panguléë Side, originally a delegated judicial office under the port-king, had in the course of time become an hereditary headmanship of Pulo U.

## The Survival of the Port-King's Supremacy

The author further explains that "so little control had the Sultans of Acheh over the course of events, that whatever may have been their purpose in establishing these various offices, the latter became in a short time part and parcel of the indigenous institutions of the country, or else disappeared altogether." The headmanship of Pulo U is thus a typical instance of the way in which an office, originally delegated by the port-king or by the Sultan of Acheh, was transformed in the course of generations into an indigenous chieftainship, lost its connection with the central power, and survived as a merely local dignity.

## The Island and its Headman

The island of Pulo U — the Simalur of ordinary speech, the Simeulue of modern maps — lies to the south-west of [[Acheh]], at a considerable distance from the capital, and its people have long enjoyed a measure of independence under their own headmen. The reduction of the Panguléë Side to the headmanship of Pulo U marks, in Snouck Hurgronje's account, the limit of the older Achehnese supremacy in that direction, and the way in which the offices of the port-kings — once the channels of Achehnese suzerainty — became, in the late nineteenth century, the mere titles of local chiefs.

## Account from *Raffles' Atjeh-overeenkomst van 1819* — Pulo U

**Pulo U**, more commonly known in modern usage as **Simalur** (or **Simeulue**), is an island off the west coast of [[Sumatra]], lying some 100 to 150 kilometres west of the [[Acheh|Acehnese]] mainland. It is the largest of a small group of islands fringing the western approach to the [[Sumatra's West Coast|west coast of Sumatra]] and was, throughout the colonial period, administratively associated with the residency of [[Acheh]] (and later with the Westkust van Sumatra). Although the island is not named explicitly in the surviving diplomatic correspondence printed by P. H. van der Kemp in his study of the [[Raffles' Atjeh-overeenkomst van 1819|Aceh agreement of 1819]], it appears in the catalog of places relevant to the broader Anglo-Dutch rivalry over Sumatra because of its situation within the Acehnese sphere of influence.

The island's traditional name, *Pulo U* (literally "island U" — with the letter U being a scribal rendering of an indigenous term), survives in older Dutch and Acehnese materials alongside the form *Simalur*, which gradually became standard in nineteenth-century Dutch colonial usage. The population spoke a distinct Austronesian language closely related to, but not mutually intelligible with, the languages of the neighbouring Batak and Mentawai groups; the society was organised in small chiefdoms that acknowledged a loose Acehnese suzerainty without ever being formally incorporated into the sultanate's administrative structure.

In the strategic calculus that Van der Kemp traces through the years 1817-1824, islands like Pulo U mattered principally as potential way-stations on the sea-lanes between [[Padang]] and [[Acheh]]. They were part of the broader Sumatran perimeter whose disposition [[Raffles]] hoped to influence from [[Bencoolen]], and which the Dutch were equally determined to control from Padang and [[Batavia]]. The [[Treaty of London (1824)|Anglo-Dutch treaty of 1824]], by which Britain withdrew from Sumatra in exchange for Dutch withdrawal from [[Hindostan|Nederlandsch Hindostan]] and [[Malacca]], settled the island's status by placing it firmly within the Dutch sphere, although effective Dutch administration did not reach Simalur until much later in the century.

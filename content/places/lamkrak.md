**Lamkrak** — also *Lam Krak* — was an Atjehnese *kampong* in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], located south of the road that ran along the river upstream from the Dutch post of [[Lambaroe]] of the *geconcentreerde linie* to the temporary post of [[Anagaloeng]]. The *kampong* was the principal encampment of the bandit chief [[Habib Long]] in [[1896]], and the Dutch decision to use [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan]] to dislodge him was the immediate occasion of Toekoe Oemar's [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] from the Dutch alliance.

## The location

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that Lamkrak lay *ten zuiden van den weg, die langs de rivier stroomopwaarts den post Lambaroe der geconcentreerde linie met den tijdelijk bezetten post Anagaloeng verbindt* — *south of the road that, along the river upstream, connects the post Lambaroe of the concentrated line with the temporarily occupied post Anagaloeng*. The *kampong* thus lay on the principal line of communication between the *geconcentreerde linie* and the temporary post at Anagaloeng, and its occupation by [[Habib Long]]'s bandit force placed it directly athwart the Dutch outer perimeter.

## The encampment of Habib Long

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, in the months before [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]]'s defection, [[Habib Long]] — the *Bengaloesche koewachter* (Bengali cattle-keeper) turned bandit chief — had established his *bende* (gang) at Lamkrak. The bandit's encampment at Lamkrak was the immediate cause of the Dutch decision to use Toekoe Oemar to dislodge him: the album records that the band had, *in het gebied van Nja Din een paar huizen verbrande, een dorpshoofd en diens vrouw vermoordde en een paar van de beste voorvechters van Toekoe Oemar neervelde* — *burned a few houses in the territory of Nja Din, murdered a village head and his wife, and killed a few of the best fighters of Toekoe Oemar*. The incursion gave Toekoe Oemar the *gelegenheid* (opportunity) to *orde en rust te handhaven en de Moslemin te verdrijven* (maintain order and peace and drive out the Moslemin), and the Dutch accordingly appointed him to dislodge Habib Long from Lamkrak.

## The defection of Toekoe Oemar

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Toekoe Oemar's response to the Dutch order to march on Lamkrak was the immediate occasion of his defection. The album records that Toekoe Djohan *vond redenen, te weigeren, op te trekken tegen Lamkrak* — *found reasons to refuse to march on Lamkrak* — and that the refusal was *te onbegrijpelijker* (the more incomprehensible) given that Habib Long's band had recently carried out the outrages in the territory of [[Toekoe Nja Din|Nja Din]] and had killed several of Oemar's own fighters. The refusal was, however, the proximate signal of the defection: it was followed by Toekoe Oemar's flight, the desertion of the Dutch-allied *hoofden*, and the siege of the Dutch outposts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]].

## The warning of Toekoe Ketjik Oemar

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that [[Toekoe Ketjik Oemar]] — one of Toekoe Djohan's *volgelingen* — secretly warned the Dutch *contrôleur* [[Kroesen]] that Djohan would not march on Lamkrak, but that he instead intended to attack the Dutch troops unexpectedly at the most favourable opportunity. The warning was, in the album's reading, the proximate cause of the Dutch ability to respond effectively to the defection.

## Significance

Lamkrak's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the *kampong* whose occupation by [[Habib Long]] was the immediate occasion of [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] from the Dutch alliance. The Dutch decision to use Toekoe Oemar to dislodge Habib Long from Lamkrak — and Toekoe Oemar's refusal to march — was the proximate signal of the defection, and the warning of [[Toekoe Ketjik Oemar]] — delivered to the *contrôleur* [[Kroesen]] — was the proximate cause of the Dutch ability to respond. Lamkrak thus occupies a uniquely consequential position in the chronology of the [[1896]] crisis.

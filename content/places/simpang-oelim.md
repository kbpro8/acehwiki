**Simpang Oelim** was a significant location in [[Aceh]] during the [[Aceh War]], the site of a major Dutch military operation in [[1876]] that resulted in the submission of the local ruler and strengthened Dutch control over the region.

## The [[1876]] Expedition

Following the death of General [[J.L.J.H. Pel]] in [[February]] [[1876]], the Dutch military continued operations in Aceh. The expedition against Simpang Oelim was authorized by Governor-General [[J.W. van Lansberge]] in [[July]] [[1876]], with instructions to suppress the recalcitrant chief [[Teukoe Moeda Nja Malém]], who had planned an attack on the Dutch post at [[Blang Ni]].

The operation, commanded by General [[A.J.E. Diemont]], was successful:
- The ruler was driven from his territory.
- The Dutch position at [[Kota Radja]] was strengthened.
- The operation demonstrated the effectiveness of aggressive action.

Alting Mees described the "brilliant result" of the expedition, which "greatly reinforces our establishment in Atjeh." The success was all the more notable given concerns about the military position following Pel's death.

## Aftermath

After the expedition, the ex-ruler of Simpang Oelim attempted to negotiate a submission in [[January]] [[1878]], but he was not successful. The Dutch continued to exert pressure on the region, and the example of Simpang Oelim served as a warning to other Acehnese chiefs.

## Significance

The Simpang Oelim expedition marked a turning point in the Aceh War, demonstrating that aggressive action could achieve results. It also established the pattern of using military force to compel submission, followed by negotiation with recalcitrant chiefs.

**Edi** — *Edi* in Van Heutsz's spelling, an Acehnese coastal state on the [[Oostkust]] (east coast) of [[Atjeh]] — was the long-standing Dutch post on the east coast and, with [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]], one of the two havens exempted from [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]]'s [[1878]] closure of the entire coast of *Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*. The Dutch post at Edi predated the systematic occupation of the [[Onderhoorigheden]] of [[1875]]–[[1877]]; it was the empirical proof, in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, that a Dutch-administered open port could become a *handelscentrum* (trade-centre) under the *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]* (shipping-regulation) that [[Scherer]] had first conceived and that [[Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]] had introduced in May [[1883]]. In [[1890]] the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]] launched a further expedition to Edi as part of a broader effort to secure the east-coast trade routes and to suppress local insurgents, establishing a small fort and supply depot that served as a staging point for further operations into the interior of Atjeh.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

### The long-existing Dutch post on the Oostkust

Edi was, in Van Heutsz's tract, the long-standing Dutch post on the [[Oostkust]] — older than the systematic occupation of the Onderhoorigheden that [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]]'s [[1875]] plan had prescribed and that was completed in February [[1877]]. The post is mentioned in Van Heutsz's catalogue of the [[1876]] post-count as excluded from the calculation: "Pedir en Simpang Olim in de Onderhoorigheden (Edi werd in beide gevallen niet medegerekend)" (Pedir and Simpang Olim in the Onderhoorigheden — Edi was in both cases not counted). The exclusion is significant: Edi had been a Dutch post *before* the Pedir and Simpang Olim expeditions of [[1876]] — its existence did not depend on the Pel plan, and its garrison was not counted among the forces the Pel-plan coastal occupation had deployed. By [[1877]] Edi was thus the senior Dutch post on the Oostkust, the place from which the Oostkust-side of the *scheepvaartregeling* would later be administered.

### Exemption from Van der Heijden's 1878 and 1879 closures

Edi's principal role in the Van Heutsz narrative is its exemption from [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]]'s two unilateral closures of the havens of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden. The first closure came in [[1878]], after the *inval* (incursion) of [[Habib Abdoel Rachman]] and the resumption of the campaign in the [[XXII Moekims]]:

> "Reeds in 1878, na hervatting van de krijgsoperatiën in de XXII Moekims, liet hij daarom alle havens van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, met uitzondering van Olehleh en Edi, voor den invoerhandel sluiten" (Already in 1878, after the resumption of military operations in the XXII Moekims, he therefore closed all havens of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden, with the exception of Olehleh and Edi, to the import trade).

The closure was revoked by G.-G. [[van Lansberge]] by letter of [[7 February]] [[1879]]. The second closure — imposed on Van der Heijden's *persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* (personal responsibility) by telegram of [[5 July]] [[1879]] — again exempted Olehleh and Edi. The two exemptions fixed Edi as, with [[Olehleh]], one of the two open ports of the entire Atjehnese rijk during the Van der Heijden campaigns. The exemption was strategic: by keeping Edi open on the Oostkust and Olehleh on the Groot-Atjeh side, Van der Heijden channelled all legitimate trade through two Dutch-administered outlets and so converted the closure of the rest into a *scheepvaartregeling* in operational substance if not in name. The arrangement was the operational precedent for the systematic *scheepvaartregeling* of May [[1883]] and for Van Heutsz's own [[1893]] prescription of open ports at Edi, [[Segli]], and Oleh-leh.

### The 1878 expedition to Edi and Gedong

The [[1878]] expedition to Edi and [[Gedong]] was one of the coastal expeditions Van der Heijden inherited from the *verzoeningspolitiek* (conciliation-politics) of March [[1877]] and that he was forced to undertake before he could abandon the coast for the Sagi campaigns. Van Heutsz records the sequence: "in Mei en Juni naar Edi en Gedong" (in May and June to Edi and Gedong). The Edi-Gedong expedition was undertaken under the order G.-G. van Lansberge had given by telegram of [[13 June]] [[1878]]: by telegram of 13 June the Governor was instructed, after regulating affairs in Gedong and [[Pasangan]], to act decisively against the resistance in Groot-Atjeh. The order was overtaken by the *inval* of [[Habib Abdoer Rachman]], which diverted the available troops to the [[XXII Moekims]] campaign: the Generaal van der Heijden therefore decided to forgo the Pasangan expedition for the time being and to use all his available force to make an end of the resistance in Groot-Atjeh for good. The Edi-Gedong expedition nonetheless settled the affairs of those statelets before the strategic reversal, and the post at Edi remained.

### Growth into a handelscentrum under the scheepvaartregeling

The *handelscentrum* (trade-centre) role came under the *scheepvaartregeling* that [[Laging Tobias]] introduced in May [[1883]] on [[Scherer]]'s design. The May [[1883]] *besluit* (decree) routed all trade of the Oostkust through Dutch-administered ports, and Edi — already the long-standing Dutch post — became the Oostkust's *handelscentrum*. Van Heutsz records the effect: "op laatstgenoemde kust was daardoor als vanzelf, te Edi, een handelscentrum ontstaan, waren hoofden en bevolking tot welvaart geraakt" (on the latter coast a trade-centre had thereby arisen of itself, at Edi, and chiefs and population had come to prosperity). The growth of Edi into a *handelscentrum* was the empirical proof of the *scheepvaartregeling*'s productive capacity: a Dutch-administered open port, with the closure of the surrounding coast, drew the trade of the entire region to itself, enriched its chiefs and population, and gave the Dutch *macht en invloed* (power and influence) over the surrounding statelets. The contrast with the impoverished markets of the closed Noordkust — *de rijke passar te Pedir door de sluiting geheel verarmd* (the rich market at Pedir impoverished entirely by the closure) — was, in Van Heutsz's argument, the proof that the *scheepvaartregeling* could either enrich or impoverish a statelet as Dutch policy dictated.

### The 1884 call-port status

Edi's role as a Dutch-administered open port was confirmed by the [[Demmeni|General Demmeni]] *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]], which established the three call-ports through which all foreign trade with the Noord- en Oostkust had to pass. Van Heutsz records the arrangement: "alleen Edi, Segli en Oleh-leh voor de geheele Noord- en Oostkust" (only Edi, Segli and Oleh-leh for the entire Noord- en Oostkust). The three call-ports were the operational core of Demmeni's hybrid scheme — a *partieele blokkade* (partial blockade) combined with a *soort scheepvaartregeling* (a kind of shipping-regulation) — under which all foreign ships had to call first at one of the three before proceeding to any of the not-closed points of the coast. The scheme was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the worst of both worlds — *geen rigoureuse blokkade* (no rigorous blockade) but also *geen echte scheepvaartregeling* (no true shipping-regulation) — but Edi's role as the Oostkust call-port survived. In Van Heutsz's own [[1893]] prescription, Edi retained its position as one of the open ports of the *scheepvaartregeling*: "Op de Noordkust: 1 Edi. 2 Telok Semawé 3 Samalanga 4 Segli" (On the Noordkust: 1. Edi, 2. Telok Semawé, 3. Samalanga, 4. Segli) and "De Generaal van Teijn achtte op de Oost- en Noordkust: Edi, Telok Semawé en Segli" (The Generaal van Teijn considered on the Oost- and Noordkust: Edi, Telok Semawé and Segli).

### Significance (Van Heutsz account)

Edi's significance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract rests on four things: it was the long-standing Dutch post on the [[Oostkust]] — older than the Pel-plan occupation of [[1875]]–[[1877]] and excluded from its troop-count — and the senior Dutch-administered port on the east coast; it was, with [[Olehleh|Oleh-leh]], one of the two havens exempted from Van der Heijden's two unilateral closures of [[1878]] and [[5 July]] [[1879]] — the exemption that channelled all legitimate Atjehnese trade through two Dutch-administered outlets and converted the closure into an operational *scheepvaartregeling*; it was the Oostkust *handelscentrum* that grew up under the May [[1883]] *scheepvaartregeling* Scherer had designed and Laging Tobias had introduced — the empirical proof of the *scheepvaartregeling*'s productive capacity, "waren hoofden en bevolking tot welvaart geraakt"; and it was, under the [[13 October]] [[1884]] *besluit* of Demmeni and under the [[1893]] prescription of Van Heutsz, one of the three open call-ports of the entire Noord- en Oostkust — the place through which the *scheepvaartregeling* was to be administered and from which the Oostkust-side of the closure-and-scheepvaartregeling system was to be enforced. Edi's role is, in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract, the operational precedent for the *scheepvaartregeling* he would himself implement as Governor of Atjeh from [[1898]] onward.

## The 1890 Marine Corps expedition

In [[1890]] the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]] launched an expedition to Edi as part of a broader effort to secure the east-coast trade routes and to suppress local insurgents. The expedition established a small fort and a supply depot, which served as a staging point for further operations into the interior of Atjeh. The 1890 Marine Corps expedition built upon the Dutch-administered open-port regime that had crystallised around Edi since the May [[1883]] *scheepvaartregeling* and the October [[1884]] call-port system, reinforcing Edi's strategic position as the senior Dutch military-administrative post on the east coast.

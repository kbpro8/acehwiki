**Janthoe** (modern spelling *Jantho*) is a locality in [[Great Acheh|Great Acheh]] named by Snouck Hurgronje as one of the points on the boundary line by which the Achehnese themselves circumscribe the kernel of the kingdom. The reference occurs in the opening geographical chapter of *The Achehnese* Vol. I.

The Achehnese, Snouck explains, distinguish sharply between the outlying dependencies of the kingdom and the central territory to which they restrict the name "Acheh" proper — what Europeans are wont to term "Great Acheh." This kernel of the kingdom, which has supplied the outlying districts with a considerable portion of their inhabitants, and has constantly striven to exercise more or less dominion over them, is, according to the Achehnese idea, "bounded by a line extending from [[Kluang|Kluang]] on the West, to [[Krueng Raya|Krueng Raya]] on the North Coast, and passing through [[Reueng-reueng|Reueng-reueng]], [[Pancha|Pancha]] and Janthoe." The position of Janthoe in this enumeration — between Pancha and the implicit termination of the eastern portion of the line at the coast — marks it as one of the inland boundary-markers of Great Acheh in the direction of the highlands.

The country thus enclosed is the proper scene of the political and social organisation described in the bulk of Vol. I. The three sagis of Great Acheh — the [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]], [[XXV Mukims|XXV]], and [[XXII Mukims|XXII]] Mukims — together with the wakeueh districts of the [[III Mukims|III Mukims]] and the [[IV Mukims (of XXV)|IV Mukims]] of the XXV, lie within the line that runs through Janthoe and its sister localities; the dependencies of [[Pidie|Pidie]], the [[East Coast|East Coast]], and the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coast]] lie without it. The line, however, is not a frontier in the European sense: it is a conventional boundary used in geographical description rather than a political or military perimeter, and its constituent points are familiar landmarks rather than fortified posts.

The name *Janthoe* (in modern orthography *Janto* or *Jantho*) is preserved in the modern kabupaten of Aceh Besar, the regency that surrounds the city of Banda Aceh and covers most of the territory of the historic Great Acheh. The locality lies in the eastern interior of the kabupaten, on the road from the capital towards the highlands; its inclusion in the boundary list of Snouck's informants reflects its importance as a recognised landmark of the late nineteenth century.

Apart from its appearance in the boundary enumeration, Janthoe is not further noticed in Vol. I. The footnote to the passage records only that various etymological explanations of the name "Acheh" itself are in vogue, "but not a single one is reliable"; the constituent localities of the boundary line are left without further comment.

## Additional account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Janthoe

**Janthoe** (modern spelling *Jantho*) is a locality on the south-eastern boundary of [[Great Acheh|Great Acheh]], named by Snouck Hurgronje as one of the points through which the conventional Achehnese boundary-line of the kernel of the kingdom is drawn.

### The Boundary of Great Acheh

In the introductory chapter on the country and the people, Snouck observes that the Achehnese use the name *Acheh* in two senses: a wider sense, embracing the whole of the Achehnese coast and the interior of the northern tip of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]]; and a more restricted sense, the territory which they describe as *Acheh* proper, or which the Europeans are wont to term **Great Acheh**. "This kernel of the kingdom, which has supplied the outlying districts with a considerable portion of their inhabitants, and has constantly striven to exercise more or less dominion over them, is according to the Achehnese idea bounded by a line extending from [[Kluang|Kluang]] on the West, to [[Krueng Raya|Krueng Raya]] on the North Coast, and passing through Reueng-reueng, Pancha and **Janthoe**."

The five points named — [[Kluang|Kluang]], Reueng-reueng, Pancha, Janthoe, [[Krueng Raya|Krueng Raya]] — define the conventional south-eastern and eastern boundary of [[Great Acheh|Great Acheh]], the kernel of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Sultanate]]. Janthoe is one of the inland points of this line, lying between Pancha and the north coast, and its inclusion in the boundary-list fixes it as a recognised landmark of the Achehnese interior.

### The Topography of the Boundary

The Achehnese boundary of [[Great Acheh|Great Acheh]], as thus defined, runs from the west coast — where it begins at [[Kluang|Kluang]] — through a series of inland points to the north coast at [[Krueng Raya|Krueng Raya]]. The line marks off the territory of the three sagis — the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]], the [[XXV Mukims|XXV]], and the [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]] — from the outlying districts of the [[East Coast|East Coast]] and the [[West Coast|West Coast]] dependencies. Janthoe, lying on this line, is one of the inland points at which the sagi-territory of Great Acheh gave way to the dependent districts beyond.

### Jantho in Later Times

The name *Jantho* survives in the modern geography of the province of Aceh as that of a small town in the regency of Aceh Besar, lying to the south of [[Banda Acheh|Banda-Acheh]] in the foothills of the central highlands. The situation is consistent with the position assigned to Janthoe in Snouck's boundary-list: an inland locality on the south-eastern fringe of the three sagis, marking the limit of the Achehnese kernel beyond which the dependent districts began. The place has, in the twentieth century, become the seat of a small administrative centre, but its earlier function as a boundary-point of the kingdom is preserved in the Achehnese list reported by Snouck.

### The Conventional Nature of the Boundary

Snouck is at pains to insist that the Achehnese boundary of [[Great Acheh|Great Acheh]] is conventional rather than fixed. The line is drawn "according to the Achehnese idea," and the points named in the list are landmarks rather than a surveyed frontier. The boundary was, in the late nineteenth century, the subject of constant negotiation between the sultan and the outlying chiefs; the inclusion of Janthoe in the list fixes it as one of the recognised landmarks of the kingdom, but does not imply that it was the seat of an administrative boundary in the European sense.

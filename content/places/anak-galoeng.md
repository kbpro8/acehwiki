**Anak Galoeng** was an Atjehnese place in [[Groot-Atjeh]] which for a long time was the Dutch headquarters — the seat to which [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]], as Gouverneur-Commissaris, wanted in September [[1880]] to move the chief seat of the government as lying more in the centre of the then position, a proposal to which the government however did not consent. The Dutch post there was given up at the execution of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration]] in February [[1885]] (evacuated in the second series, together with [[Boekloe]]); in the meantime the evacuation of the posts to the *south* of Anak Galoeng, between [[10 January|10]] and [[20 January]] [[1885]], had cost the Dutch 4 dead and 30 wounded, and on 15 and 20 January the troops had to proceed offensively to drive the enemy from his entrenchments and destroy them (see [[Completion of the concentrated position (March 1885)]]). On this very place of the former Dutch headquarters the foremost leader of the resistance, [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Toekoe di Tiroe]], afterwards established his headquarters — the detail that [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] called well worth noting in connection with the leader's [[Proclamation of Toekoe di Tiroe (2 February 1885)|proclamation of 2 February 1885]].

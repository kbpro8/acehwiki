The **Three Sagis** (Achehnese *lhèë sagòë*, Malay *tiga sagi*) are the three confederations or congeries of mukims that together compose [[Great Acheh]] — the kernel of the [[Acheh|Sultanate of Acheh]] and the seat of the port-kings. They are the [[XXVI Mukims]], the [[XXV Mukims]] and the [[XXII Mukims]], named in Achehnese idiom after the (originally) twenty-six, twenty-five, and twenty-two mukims of which each was composed. Their chiefs, the *panglima sagi*, stood or were supposed to stand at the head of the three districts, and were the guardians of the sultanate.

## Form of the Triangular Kingdom

The form of true Acheh the Achehnese delight to compare to that of a winnowing-basket (*jeu'ee*). Proceeding down the [[Acheh River]] from its debouchment, one has on the left the territory of the [[XXV Mukims]] and on the right that of the [[XXVI Mukims]], the intervening space up to the broad belt of outlying country being occupied by the [[XXII Mukims]]. A comparison with the three angles of a triangle has still more deeply engrafted itself into the language; these three confederations or congeries of mukims are called the *lhèë sagië* (Malay *tiga sagi*), i.e. "the three angles of Acheh," and the three uleebalangs or chieftains who stand or are supposed to stand at the head of these three districts are called *panglima sagië*, or heads of sagi or angles.

## Increase of Population and the Traditional Names

Increase of population has given rise to the formation of new districts, yet the traditional name of each sagi has survived. The [[XXII Mukims]], Snouck Hurgronje notes, "really numbers many more mukims than the name implies," and the same is true, though in a less degree, of the [[XXV Mukims]], while the [[XXVI Mukims]] continue to correspond with their ancient name. The pre-eminence of the [[XXII Mukims]] within the federation rests on the antiquity of its chief's lineage, the bravery of the men of his sagi, and its wealth. Its chief, [[Panglima Polem]] ("Lord Elder Brother"), is regarded as the doyen of the chiefs of the sagis.

## Guardianship of the Sultanate

The three guardians, the panglima sagi, did not succeed in retaining the supremacy over the federate dominions which they had possessed when the metaphorical marriage-contract between sultan and country was first concluded. At the election of a new sultan, which was usually decided by war between the different parties, the scale was turned not so much by the support of the panglimas as by that of the real wielders of power in their sagis. In process of time the number of guardians of the sultanate increased, and Achehnese authorities on adat speak of the "twelve uleebalangs who appoint and dethrone princes," describing them as four from each sagi. Snouck Hurgronje cautions that this list at most represents the conditions that existed during a short period, and that other chiefs — such as [[Teuku Kali]] the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] and the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata]] — often carried more weight than many of the board of twelve.

## The Three Sagis in the Fairs and Feasts

The sagis figured as units in the ceremonial life of the capital. The three sagis followed the usage of the capital in fixing the first day of the [[Puasa (Ramadhan fasting month)|Ramadhan]] fast, "so that everyone could tell in advance what the last three days of Sha'bān would be." Before the [[Puasa (Ramadhan fasting month)|fasting month]] the people of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]] and some of those of the [[XXVI Mukims]] were in the habit of slaughtering many cows, while elsewhere, as in the [[XXV Mukims]] and the territory not included in the three sagis, they slaughtered male buffaloes by preference, since it was believed that the use of too much cow's flesh results in the sickness called *siawan*. In the great three-days' fair at [[Banda Acheh]], "all the sagis lent their contributions to this noisy gathering."

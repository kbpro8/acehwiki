**Gedoeng** was an Atjeh-dependent *kuststaatje* (vassal coastal state) outside [[Groot-Atjeh]], which had supported the enemy with *hulpbenden* (auxiliary bands), wapens and levensmiddelen and was *tuchtiged* (chastised) twice by [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]] in June [[1878]]. After the second *tuchtiging* the chief laid down his *bestuur* (office) and his son came in to submit. Borel treats Gedoeng as the proof of his argument for the *aanvallende stelsel* (offensive system): a "schitterend succes" achieved by direct action against a vassal state whose long-term hostility had been sustained by Dutch inaction.

## The vassal coastal states

The vassal coastal states — Analaboe, Pedir, Samalangan, [[Pasangan]], Simpang Olim, Edi, and Gedoeng itself — had supported the resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh]] with auxiliary bands, weapons, and food throughout the war. Their position on the coast made them difficult to reach from the inland Dutch base at [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] but vulnerable to naval descents. [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s plan had included the reduction of these states as part of the wider isolation of Atjeh; after his death in February [[1876]] the offensive against them was sporadic. By June [[1878]] the situation in [[Groot-Atjeh]] had become sufficiently stable to allow van der Heijden, then *gouverneur van Atjeh*, to take a mobile colonne against Gedoeng.

## The June 1878 expeditions

Barely had van der Heijden left with most of his mobile strijdmacht to *tuchtigen* Gedoeng, however, when the enemy attacked the Dutch linie, broke through at several places, fell into the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]], verjoeg the pro-Dutch *bevolking*, plundered and burned, sneed the *gemeenschap* between K. Radja and several posts, and caused schrik and verwarring — the [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]]. Van der Heijden had to rush back in *aller ijl* and with geweld restore communication with the posts in the IV Moekims. New battalions were sent from Java. The Dutch eventually drove the enemy out everywhere and even made a successful *inval* (inroad) into the [[XXII Moekims]] to punish them — but the *verloopen* (flight) of the friendly *bevolking* could not be undone: they had lost vertrouwen in Dutch protection. Gedoeng itself was *tuchtiged* twice in the course of these operations; after the second *tuchtiging* the chief laid down his *bestuur* and his son came in to submit. Borel's point is that the same offensive principle — direct, repeated punishment — would, if applied to the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], produce the same result.

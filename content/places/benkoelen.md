**Benkoelen** (now **[[Bengkulu]]**) was a coastal town on the southwest coast of [[Sumatra]] that changed hands between the **[[Dutch East Indies]]** and the **[[British Empire]]** as a result of the **[[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824]]**. The treaty transferred Benkoelen to British control in exchange for Dutch recognition of British authority over **[[Singapore]]** and **[[Labuan]]**. This territorial swap altered the balance of power in the region, indirectly affecting the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]**, whose northern trade routes were influenced by the shifting colonial frontiers.

## Historical Role

Before the 1824 treaty, Benkoelen served as a Dutch trading post. Its loss forced the Dutch to concentrate on consolidating authority over Aceh and the surrounding hinterland.

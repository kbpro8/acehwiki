**Benkoelen** (modern **[[Bengkulu]]**) was a coastal town on the southwestern coast of [[Sumatra]], facing the [[Indian Ocean]], that figured successively in the pre‑colonial expansion of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Acehnese sultanate]] around [[1500]] and in the colonial frontier‑adjustments of the early nineteenth century, when it changed hands between the **[[Dutch East Indies]]** and the **[[British Empire]]** under the **[[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824]]**. The treaty transferred Benkoelen to British control in exchange for Dutch recognition of British authority over **[[Singapore]]** and **[[Labuan]]**, a territorial swap that altered the balance of power in the region and indirectly affected the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]**, whose northern trade routes were influenced by the shifting colonial frontiers.

## Pre‑colonial Acehnese reach (c. 1500)

During the westward expansion of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Koetaradja Sultanate]] around [[1500]], Acehnese military expeditions reached as far as Benkoelen, reflecting the sultanate's ambition to dominate the pepper trade along the island's western seaboard. The town's strategic position on the Indian Ocean made it a valuable target for both Acehnese and later European forces seeking to control the spice routes. Its inclusion in Acehnese conquests illustrates the breadth of the sultanate's power before the onset of sustained Dutch colonial pressure in the [[19th century|nineteenth century]].

## British–Dutch frontier (1824)

Before the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824]] Benkoelen had served as a Dutch trading post on the southwest Sumatran coast. The treaty transferred it to British control, which forced the Dutch to concentrate on consolidating authority over [[Aceh]] and the surrounding hinterland, ultimately shaping the conditions under which the [[Aceh War]] ([[1873]]–[[1904]]) would later be fought.

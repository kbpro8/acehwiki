**Kaloet** — sometimes *Kalut* — was an Atjehnese *benting* (fortification) of exceptional strength, built in the *wildernis* (wilderness) of the [[XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] at a distance of approximately 800 metres from the Dutch *spoorbaan* (railway line) and approximately 1300 metres north-west of the Dutch post at [[Lambaroe]]. The fortification was the principal Atjehnese strongpoint of the late *concentratie* period, and the scene of three principal engagements: the [[Reconnaissance of Kaloet (23 August 1892)|reconnaissance of 23 August 1892]], the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|failed assault of 1 October 1892]], and the [[Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)|bloodless capture of August 1893]] by [[Toekoe Oemar]].

## The terrain

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that Kaloet was built *in het centrum, op ongeveer 800 M. van de spoorbaan* — *in the centre, at approximately 800 m from the railway line* — in a *stuk wildernis van ruim een K.M. omtrek, dat 3 à 400 M. ver als een wig in de strook dringt* — *a piece of wilderness of more than a kilometre circumference, that pushes as a wedge into the [cleared] strip for 3 to 400 m*. The terrain surrounding Kaloet was, in the album's description, a *verwarde massa alang-alang en struikgewas* — a confused mass of cogon grass and shrubbery — that gave way, at the fortification itself, to a *moerassige terreininzinking* (marshy ground depression) of approximately 50 metres' breadth. The site was thus both concealed and naturally defended — and the Atjehnese builders had *op vernuftige wijze* (in an ingenious way) made use of all the natural and artificial obstacles the terrain offered.

## The fortification

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Kaloet was *een zeldzaam groote defensieve kracht* — of exceptionally great defensive strength. The four heavy earthen walls formed a rectangle of approximately 20 by 15 metres, of which one of the long sides lay in the front. The walls were 3 to 4 metres high; the side and rear walls somewhat lower and thinner; all equipped with *borstweringen* (parapets). The left front corner was occupied by a *massief hoog bastion* (massive high bastion), itself 6 metres high and equipped with a heavy *lila* (a kind of swivel-gun) on a *spilaffuit* (pivot carriage). The two side walls were joined by an earthen wall that divided the *benting* in two. The rear wall was *een weinig gebroken* (slightly broken) and provided, approximately in the middle, with a bastion armed with two pieces on roller carriages. This heavy armament served to sweep the entrance, which lay at some metres from the bastion on the rear side. The entrance consisted of an extraordinarily strong door, so narrow that only one man could pass through at a time.

The access to the entrance was *uiterst moeilijk, en zeer vernuftig bedacht* — extremely difficult and very ingeniously devised. Some ten metres behind and to the side of the entrance lay a very deep and broad *kuil met water* (water-filled pit). From the *rimboe* (jungle) a narrow footpath led to this pit. The last part of this path consisted, in reality, of a narrow *gang van zware palissaden* (lane of heavy palisades), equipped with three strong gates. These gates had to be *geheel tot splinters geslagen* (smashed entirely to splinters) in order to pass through. Over the pit lay a narrow *klapperstam, als vonder zonder leuningen* (coconut-trunk, as a footbridge without railings). From this footbridge to the gate in the rear wall ran another narrow *gang van palissaden* (lane of palisades) that twice made a corner and was again equipped with three doors — *even sterk van constructie en sluiting als die aan de overzijde van den kuil* — *as strong in construction and closure as those on the other side of the pit*. Around the pit the *benting* was *eenvoudig niet te naderen* (simply not to be approached). Around the entire fortification lay a 1½-metre-deep and 2-metre-broad *gracht* (moat), which was only without water in case of long-continued drought. From the moat at the rear bastion ran a *verbindingsgang* (connecting lane) to the pit.

In front of the moat, in the front, on the right flank and at the rear, lay a *comboeri-aanplanting* (comboeri planting) of approximately 5 metres' breadth. The left flank rested against wholly impassable terrain — *oerwoud, massieve wildernis* (primeval forest, massive wilderness) — of 60 to 80 cm breadth. In front of the *comboeri* in the front lay several rows of *wolfskuilen* (wolf-pits) with *randjoes* (sharpened bamboo stakes), and in front of these pits again *mandjoe-aanplantingen* (mandjoe plantings). On the right flank the *comboeri* was adjoined by a dense *nipahbosch* (nipah palm grove); on the right side lay the *rimboe* (jungle).

## The garrison

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the garrison of Kaloet was commanded by an Atjehnese officer whom the album calls *De Aanvoerder, commandant van Kaloet* — *The Commander, commandant of Kaloet* — and whose portrait is preserved in one of the album's plates. The album does not name the commander, but it identifies him as the officer who, on the morning of [[4 August]] [[1893]], sent a *briefje* (note) to the *panglima* of Kaloet with the *vriendelijk verzoek* (friendly request) to *met zijne bezetting uit wandelen te gaan* (to go for a walk with his garrison). The note — sent by [[Toekoe Oemar]] on the night of [[3 August|3-4 August]] [[1893]] — warned that, if the garrison did not withdraw along a designated path before sunrise, the *benting* would be invested and the garrison would be *over de kling gejaagd* (put to the sword).

## The engagements

Kaloet was the scene of three principal engagements in the late *concentratie* period:

- The [[Reconnaissance of Kaloet (23 August 1892)|reconnaissance of 23 August 1892]], in which a reconnaissance column under Captain [[Jhr. Graafland]] — with Lieutenant [[Brant]] as guide — destroyed the Atjehnese fortifications in the *Kroeng Tandjong|Kroeng Tandjong]] and approached to within 50 metres of Kaloet's *vóórwal* (front wall) before withdrawing.
- The [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|failed assault of 1 October 1892]], in which a strong column from [[Lambaroe]] under a captain and three lieutenants, supported by 75 men from [[Lampermej]] and 50 men of the 14th Battalion, assaulted Kaloet from the north-east and was repulsed with 7 killed and more than 30 wounded, including Lieutenant [[Hoolboom]] (mortally wounded) and Lieutenant [[Drijber]].
- The [[Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)|bloodless capture of August 1893]], in which [[Toekoe Oemar]] — supported by the 4th Mountain Battery from the Kraton and approximately 1000 men, and by the fire of the guns of [[Lamreng]] and [[Lambaroe]] — invested the fortification on [[4 August]] [[1893]] and found it abandoned the next morning.

## Significance

Kaloet's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the principal Atjehnese strongpoint of the late *concentratie* period — the fortification whose exceptional strength and natural defences made it the test case of the Dutch *concentratie* strategy. The [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|failed assault of 1 October 1892]] was the principal Dutch military failure of the late *concentratie* period, and the [[Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)|bloodless capture of August 1893]] was the principal Dutch-Atjehnese auxiliary success of the same period. The album's detailed description of the fortification — its *bastion*, *gracht*, *kuil*, *palissaden*, *comboeri*, *wolfskuilen*, *randjoes* and *mandjoe-aanplantingen* — is the principal published record of Atjehnese fortification practice in the late nineteenth century.

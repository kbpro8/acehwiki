**VI Moekims Lhong** was a coastal *[[Moekim|moekim]]* federation of [[Groot-Atjeh]] which, with [[Lam Teuba|Moekim Lam-Teuba]], escaped the crop failure of [[1936]]–[[1937]] through irrigation by mountain streams, and one of the three *moekims* in which the Acehnese knew *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* by the name *penjikit sisë*.

## Irrigation and food supply

Since Groot-Atjeh had no artificial irrigation of its *[[Sawah|sawah]]* fields and depended on rainfall, the long drought of [[1936]] and [[1937]] caused the *[[Paditeelt|paddi]]* planting to fail — "met uitzondering van den aanplant in VI Moekims Lhong en Moekim Lam-Teuba," where irrigation came from mountain streams that supplied enough water even in the dry season.

In the coastal *moekims* of Lhong, accordingly, "de voedselvoorziening (rijstvoorziening) niet te wenschen overliet" during the survey period, in contrast with the other coastal *moekims* where supply was less favourable.

## The test of the dietary theory

Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] used the contrast as the first of three tests of whether food supply explained the distribution of xerophthalmia. His finding was negative: in Lhong, where the rice supply left nothing to be desired, "deze invloed van de voeding niet duidelijk tot uitdrukking komt in de xerophthalmiefrequentie."

## Acehnese nomenclature

In the *moekims* Lhong, [[Lho Nga|Lho-Nga]] and [[Lam-Kabeuë]], xerophthalmia was known among the population under the name *penjikit sisë* — [[J. Tijssen|Tijssen]] writes *siseh* — while in the rest of the surveyed area the condition was not known under that name. Elsewhere *sisë* was known only in the sense of *kulit ikan*, the scales of a dead fish, and *kaki ajam*, a chicken's foot.

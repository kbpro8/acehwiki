**IX Moekims** refers to the ninth district of the Moekim (local chief) system in [[Acheh]], targeted by the Dutch expeditionary force in [[1875]]–[[1876]]. General [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] received orders to conquer the IX Moekims, and the marine and army columns advanced through passes such as [[Glitaroem]] to bring the area under Dutch control. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the IX Moekims — written in Dutch as *IX Moekims* and meaning "Nine Mukims" — was a region of [[Groot-Atjeh]] comprising nine [[moekim|mukims]] situated in the central-southern portion of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] theatre, of which the true *ulèëbalang* (territorial chief) was Toekoe Nanggroi, who always resided at Troemon, and the acting (*wd.*) *oeleebalang* was [[Toekoe Nja Mohamad]] at [[Toebaloe]]. The IX Moekims contained the mukims of Oleh Soesoe and [[Lamkoenjit]], and were protected by the [[Dutch]] post of [[Biloel]] against incursions from the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]], Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]. The general sentiment of the IX Moekims toward the Dutch was "not unfavourable," but the kampongs of [[Lam-Tehen]] and [[Koeta Karang]] were actively hostile. The IX Moekims were the object of the [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|show-of-force by Van Heutsz on 29 May 1896]] and were the recruiting ground for [[Teuku Umar]]'s mercenaries at 15 dollars per month plus rations.

## The 1875–1876 Dutch expedition

By early February [[1876]] the entire IX Moekims region was reported as "in ons bezit" (in our possession) by the Dutch forces. The IX Moekims were conquered by General [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] through the [[Glitaroem]] pass and other routes as part of the 1875–1876 Dutch campaign.

## Administrative position in the late sultanate

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the IX Moekims formed part of the immediate territory of the Sultan of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] — distinct from the three great *sagis* (the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]). The Sultan's immediate territory — the directly-ruled core of the sultanate — included the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] and [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]], [[Marassa|Marassa]], [[Longbatta|Longbattah]], the [[III Moekims|III Moekims]], the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]], the [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] and the IX Moekims. The IX Moekims thus formed part of the directly-ruled core of the sultanate — the mukim-cluster over which the Sultan exercised direct temporal authority, in contrast to the three *sagis*, which were governed by their own *panglima*s and *oeloebalang*s under the Sultan's nominal suzerainty.

The *Inlandsche* administrative structure of the immediate Sultan's territory was based on the [[Mukim|mukim]]-*imam*-*[[Uleebalang|oeloebalang]]* hierarchy: each mukim was governed by an *imam* (religious head) and an *oeloebalang* (temporal chief). The IX Moekims were, in this structure, one of the larger mukim-clusters of the immediate Sultan's territory.

## Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

The IX Moekims lay to the south-east of [[Koeta Radja]] between the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] on the south-west and the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] on the east. The mukims of Oleh Soesoe (with the fortified *Masdjid Oleh-Soesoeh*) and [[Lamkoenjit]] (with its great *pasar*) were the most important settlements, and the kampong of [[Toebaloe]] was the fortified residence of the acting *oeleebalang* [[Toekoe Nja Mohamad]]. The kampongs of [[Lam-Tehen]] (East and West) and [[Koeta Karang]] were the actively hostile centres. The IX Moekims were bounded on the north by the [[III Moekims Daroe]] and on the south by the [[VII Moekims Baid]] and the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] country. The fortified mosque of the IX Moekims had its bridge partly destroyed to impede movement.

## Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

The IX Moekims had been a partial Dutch sphere of influence since [[1873]], with the *oeleebalang* of the mukim generally accommodating to the Dutch presence. The [[Dutch]] post of [[Biloel]] had been constructed specifically to protect the IX Moekims against incursions from the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] and the [[VII Moekims Baid]], and the great market of [[Lamkoenjit]] had been a major economic centre. The *eigenlijke oeleebalang* (true territorial chief) Toekoe Nanggroi resided always at Troemon and left the administration to his acting successor [[Toekoe Nja Mohamad]] at [[Toebaloe]]. [[Teuku Umar]] recruited mercenaries in the IX Moekims at 15 dollars per month plus rations, drawing principally from the hostile kampongs of [[Lam-Tehen]] and [[Koeta Karang]] where [[T. Nja Gedong|Nja Gedong]] built a *benteng* (fortification).

## Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

The IX Moekims were the object of a [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|show-of-force]] on [[29 May]] [[1896]] by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], who visited the *oeleebalang* [[Toekoe Nja Mohamad]] at [[Toebaloe]] and the principal kampongs of the mukim. Earlier, on [[8 April]] [[1896]], the 1st company of the 14e Bataljon under [[Kapitein Bruynis|Kapitein Bruynis]] had come under heavy fire from the *Masdjid Oleh-Soesoeh* in the course of [[Kolonel der Infanterie|Kolonel]] [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s relief of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]], and Kapitein [[F. P. A. Geluk]] of the 3rd company 14e Bataljon had been killed in action near the *Masdjid Oleh-Soesoeh*. On [[10 April]] [[1896]] enemy shooters were driven from the kampong of Dinoeng, and on [[12 April]] [[1896]] the W. *rand* (edge) of Oleh-Soesoeh was occupied. After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] the IX Moekims became a recruiting ground for Umar's forces. The IX Moekims are mentioned in Vetter's proclamation of [[26 April]] [[1896]] and in the final assessments of [[1896]] as a region generally not unfavourable to the Dutch.

## Significance

The IX Moekims represented the borderline case of an Achehnese mukim whose *oeleebalang* was prepared to acknowledge Dutch suzerainty but whose population remained partially hostile. The Dutch show-of-force of [[29 May]] [[1896]] was intended to consolidate the loyalty of [[Toekoe Nja Mohamad]] and to detach the IX Moekims from [[Teuku Umar]]'s cause. The persistent hostility of [[Lam-Tehen]] and [[Koeta Karang]] within the otherwise accommodating IX Moekims demonstrated the limits of Dutch authority at the level of individual kampongs. The IX Moekims were also one of the principal sources of Umar's mercenaries — recruited at 15 dollars per month plus rations — and thus a strategic object of Dutch political-military policy in the first semester of [[1896]].

## Account from *Een Vaderlandsch Belang* by H. Borel (1878)

The **IX Moekims** (Negen Moekims, "Nine Mukims") was the *sagi* (confederation) of nine *mukim* (parish) units south of the [[IV Moekims]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], invaded by [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s colonne on [[28 January]] [[1876]] through the [[Boekit Daroe]] and the [[Glitaroem]] pass, and fully conquered between [[31 January]] and [[7 February]] [[1876]] in the third phase of the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great winter offensive]]. Like the IV Moekims, the IX Moekims was invaded by the enemy in June [[1878]] during the [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]].

## The invasion of 28 January 1876

The invasion of the IX Moekims began on [[28 January]] [[1876]], as the third phase of Pel's offensive after the [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] invasions. At [[Boekit Daroe]], about an hour south of [[Ketapang Doewa]], *vinnig gestreden* — sharp fighting — and the enemy suffered *gevoelig verlies* (sensitive loss). The [[Glitaroem|bergpas Glitaroem]], which gave access between the IV and IX Moekims, fell into Dutch hands. With the pass open, the troepen moved eastward, taking Lampermej and Oeleh Soesoe on [[31 January]] [[1876]]. At Oeleh Soesoe the enemy offered *hevigen tegenstand* but had to *zwichten*; several kampongs were burned. In the first days of February Lamkoenjit, Toeram and Beloel were also conquered. The IX Moekims was fully in Dutch hands. From Toeram, which bordered the gebied of [[Toekoe Bait]] of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the Dutch made an *inval* (inroad) into that *sagi* on [[3 February]] [[1876]], driving the enemy out of [[Atoeh]] after a *heeten strijd*.

## The June 1878 invasion

Barely had [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]] left with most of his mobile strijdmacht to *tuchtigen* [[Gedoeng]] in June [[1878]], when the enemy attacked the Dutch linie, broke through at several places, fell into the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and IX Moekims, verjoeg the pro-Dutch *bevolking*, plundered and burned, sneed the *gemeenschap* between [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] and several posts, and caused schrik and verwarring. Van der Heijden had to rush back in *aller ijl* and with geweld restore communication with the posts in the IV Moekims; new battalions were sent from Java. The Dutch eventually drove the enemy out everywhere and even made a successful *inval* into the [[XXII Moekims]] to punish them — but the *verloopen* (flight) of the friendly *bevolking* could not be undone: they had lost vertrouwen in Dutch protection. Borel's lesson is that the posten-systeem alone cannot prevent a linie-doorbraak; mobile forces must never be reduced.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

The **IX Moekims** — the nine *[[Mukim|moekims]]* — were an administrative district of [[Groot-Atjeh]] lying east of the [[IV Moekims]] and [[VI Moekims]], on the flat, marshy valley floor of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh valley]] between the southern side of the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]* and the steep mountains bounding the plain to the south behind [[Biloel]]; they were cleared of the *[[Moslemin]]* by [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] in [[1893]] and formed, with the territory under his own influence, the [[XXV Moekims]].

## Clearance by Toekoe Oemar

Hooijer recorded that the decision to employ [[Teukoe Oemar]] was taken when he had driven the *Moslemin* as far as the bare hills shown on the opening plate, situated in the IX Moekims. When the whole district had been cleared of enemies, the Dutch erected the post of [[Tjot Goeë]] on [[8 August]] [[1893]] — one month before Oemar's submission was accepted and the dignity of *[[Panglima Prang Besar]]* conferred on him. The head of the IX Moekims, [[Toekoe Nja Mohammad]], was a Dutch ally, and it was at his request that the post of [[Biloel]] was erected in the night of [[20 May]]–[[21 May]] [[1894]].

## Separation from the XXII Moekims

The establishment of [[Lam Koenjit]] on [[4 July]] [[1894]], with [[Biloel]] at the foot of the steep southern mountains and [[Tjot Goeë]] to the north, served among other purposes to separate the [[XXII Moekims]] more effectively from those of the IX Moekims.

## Renewed hostility

On [[29 March]] [[1895]] three companies sent out from the *geconcentreerde linie* to protect [[Tjot Goeë]] against *kwaad volk* assembling in the IX Moekims were heavily fired on, and the post remained surrounded until relieved on [[9 April]] [[1895]]. Reinforcements for [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] came from the IX Moekims as well as from the [[Westkust|West Coast]] and [[Malaboeh|Melaboeh]] during the campaign in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. In [[1896]] [[Toekoe Moesa Anaq Batee]] penetrated the IX Moekims from the direction of [[Lam Barih|Lambarih]].

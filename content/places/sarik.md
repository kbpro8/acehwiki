**Sarik** is one of the three coal concessions (together with [[Bukit Durian]] and [[Bukit Pulai]]) granted to the [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] in [[1909]] in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra (immediately to the south of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] border). The Sarik concession is one of the only documented colonial-era coal-mining concessions on Atjeh-relevant territory, and its modest production — 2,000 tons over the decade [[1917]]–[[1927]], shared with the Bukit Durian and Bukit Pulai concessions and entirely for use at the company's own [[Salida]] gold mine — represents the entire recorded colonial coal production for the West-Atjeh/Painan coal zone. The Sarik mining rights were returned to the Government in [[1941]] — the last of the three Kinandam-Sumatra concessions to be returned, and the formal end of the colonial coal-mining industry in the West-Atjeh/Painan region.

## The 1909 Kinandam-Sumatra mining lease

For the full account of the [[1909]] Kinandam-Sumatra mining leases and the geological description of the Painan district coal occurrences, see the entries on [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] and [[Bukit Durian]]. In summary: the [[1909]] mining leases for Bukit Durian, Bukit Pulai and Sarik covered coal occurrences "of minor importance", with seams ranging from a few centimeters up to 2 m in thickness and "generally contain impure coal of a poor quality (soft with a tendency to crumble)".

## The 1917-1927 production and 1941 return

For the full account of the [[1917]]–[[1927]] production see the entries on [[Kinandam-Sumatra Mijnbouw Mij.]] and [[Salida]]. In summary: during the years [[1917]]–[[1927]] a total quantity of 2,000 tons was produced (from the three concessions combined) for use at the Salida gold mine. When the Salida gold mine was closed down in [[1927]], the mining rights for the Bukit Durian and Bukit Pulai concessions were returned to the Government in [[1934]]; the Sarik concession was the last to be returned, with its mining rights reverted to the Government in [[1941]]. The [[1941]] return of the Sarik mining rights — on the eve of the Pacific War — accordingly marks the formal end of the colonial coal-mining industry in the West-Atjeh/Painan region.

## Significance

Sarik is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is one of the only documented colonial-era coal-mining concessions on Atjeh-relevant territory — the [[1909]] Kinandam-Sumatra mining lease at Sarik in the Painan district of the Westkust van Sumatra (immediately to the south of the Atjeh border). The Sarik concession's modest production — a share of the 2,000 tons produced over the decade [[1917]]–[[1927]] for use at the Salida gold mine — represents part of the entire recorded colonial coal production for the West-Atjeh/Painan coal zone. The [[1941]] return of the Sarik mining rights — the last of the three Kinandam-Sumatra concessions to be returned — marks the formal end of the colonial coal-mining industry in the West-Atjeh/Painan region, on the very eve of the Pacific War and the Japanese invasion that would bring the colonial mining industry as a whole to an end.

## In De Karbouw

The Pedirse-specific facts that *De Karbouw* records are:

- The milking of goats alongside buffaloes: "In Atjeh worden van de huisdieren bijna uitsluitend karbouwen door de bevolking nu en dan gemolken; in het Pedirse ook geiten" — in Atjeh, of the domestic animals, almost exclusively buffaloes are occasionally milked by the population; in the Pedirse, goats too (*De Karbouw*, p. 124).
- The consumption and preparation of *dadih* (curdled buffalo-milk): "Gestremde buffelmelk, ook op N.-Sumatra *dadih* genoemd, wordt bij de Atjehers soms met rijst of pisang, maar bij de Gajo's zelden genuttigd. De Klinganezen en Arabieren in Atjeh zijn er echter grote liefhebbers van; de eersten noemen die *tairō*, de laatsten *halib*. Vooral in [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] wordt veel *dadih* gemaakt" (*De Karbouw*, p. 124). Samalanga, on the north coast of Atjeh within the Pedir sphere, is the locality singled out for the abundance of its *dadih* production.
- The tiny golden plough at the [[Poephon blang|*poephon blang*]]: "Naar men ons in Groot-Atjeh vertelde, zou de [[malim|*malim*]] in het Pederse bij deze sakramentele handeling gebruik maken van een zeer klein gouden ploegje, dat hij even over de te bewerken bodem schuift. Evenals het ijzer, heeft ook het goud, naar Atjehse begrippen, magische potentie" — as we were told in Groot-Atjeh, the *malim* in the Pederse uses, at this sacramental act, a very small golden plough, which he slides lightly over the soil to be worked. Like iron, gold — in Atjehse conception — has magical potency (*De Karbouw*, p. 178; cf. [[Kreemer's Atjeh ([[1922]]-23)|Kreemer, *Atjeh*]] II, pp. 578 vg.).


## Historical context

The Pedir region was, in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, the most important port of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate after [[Banda Acheh|Koetaradja]] itself, and the place of call for many of the European merchants and envoys who visited Atjeh in the period of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] (r. [[1607]]–[[1636]]). The [[Treaty of Pedir ([[1819]])|Acheh convention of 1819]] between [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] and the Atjehnese sultan — later traced by [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] as the source of the fateful Acheh clause in the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London ([[1824]])|Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London of 1824]] — was concluded at Pedir. The region thus figures in the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history not only as a locus of buffalo-ritual practice but as a key site in the diplomatic and commercial history of the sultanate.



## Significance

Pedirse's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is twofold. First, as documented in *De Karbouw*, it is the type-locality for several specifically northern-Atjehnese buffalo-practices — the milking of goats alongside buffaloes, the abundant *dadih* production of [[Samalanga|Samalanga]], and the use of a tiny golden plough at the *poephon blang* — that distinguish it from the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] core and from the [[Gajoland]] and [[Alasland]] interior. Second, as a historical-geographical matter, it is the site of the [[Treaty of Pedir ([[1819]])|Treaty of Pedir of 1819]], one of the principal diplomatic documents of the early-nineteenth-century Anglo-Atjehnese relationship.

## Account from *The Geology of Indonesia* ([[1949]]) — Pedirse

### Pedirse as the oil-bearing region of the Atjeh north coast

The Pedirse region is the locus of three of the four component fields of the [[Atjeh oil complex]] recorded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) Table 4:

- The **[[Peureula]]** field (operated by the [[N.L.A.M.]]; 48,219 tons in [[1940]], 36,640 tons in [[1941]]) — the largest producing field of the Atjeh oil complex.
- The **South Perlak** field (operated by the [[N.L.A.M.]]; no production in [[1940]] or [[1941]]).
- The **[[Pulu Pandjang]]** field (operated by the [[N.K.P.M.]]; no production in [[1940]] or [[1941]]).
- The **[[Pasé oil field|Pasé]]** field (operated by the [[N.P.P.M.]]; 1,797 tons in [[1941]]).

The Pedirse region is also the location of the [[Lho Sukon|Lho Sukon]] drilling — the deepest exploratory drilling in the colonial East Indies, reaching a depth of 3,450 m. The Lho Sukon drilling was undertaken in the same general Pedirse/Pasai region that hosted the Atjeh oil complex fields, and it accordingly represents the technical high-point of the colonial oil-prospecting activity on the Atjeh north coast.

### The geological context

Van Bemmelen's general classification of the Atjeh petroleum geology places the Pedirse-region fields among the "steep folds (Atjeh, Borneo)" that "were repeatedly investigated by drilling but the results have not been encouraging". The implication is that the Pedirse-region oil is hosted in steeply-folded neogene sediments of the Atjeh north coast, in structural settings less favourable for oil accumulation than the gently-domed anticlines of the South-Sumatran fields. The modest size of the Peureula production (48,219 tons in [[1940]], declining to 36,640 tons in [[1941]]) and the very small Pasé production (1,797 tons in [[1941]]) are consistent with this assessment.

### Significance

Pedirse is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the principal oil-bearing region of the Atjeh north coast — the locus of three of the four component fields of the [[Atjeh oil complex]] (Peureula, South Perlak, Pasé) and of the deepest exploratory drilling in the colonial East Indies (the Lho Sukon drilling at 3,450 m). The Pedirse region's oil fields — operated by the [[N.L.A.M.]], [[N.K.P.M.]] and [[N.P.P.M.]] — represent the clustered core of the colonial-era oil industry on the Atjeh north coast, and their modest production (in contrast to the [[B.P.M.]]-operated [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]] at the southern edge of the residency) is the documentary evidence of the difficult petroleum geology of the Atjeh interior proper.

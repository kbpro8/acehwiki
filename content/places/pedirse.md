**Pedirse** (in *De Karbouw*'s spelling; more commonly *Pedir* in Dutch colonial sources, *Pidie* in modern Indonesian) is the region of the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] between [[Groot-Atjeh|Greater Aceh]] and the [[Pasei|Pasai]] country, named from the coastal town of Pedir that was, in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, one of the principal ports of the sultanate. In [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] Pedirse is named for three Atjeh-specific facts: the local milking of goats (alongside buffaloes); the production of *dadih* (curdled buffalo-milk) — specifically at [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] in the Pedir region; and the use, in the [[Pederse|Pederse (Pidie)]] sub-district of Groot-Atjeh, of a tiny golden plough at the [[Poephon blang|*poephon blang*]] field-consecration rite.

## In De Karbouw
The Pedirse-specific facts that *De Karbouw* records are:

- The milking of goats alongside buffaloes: "In Atjeh worden van de huisdieren bijna uitsluitend karbouwen door de bevolking nu en dan gemolken; in het Pedirse ook geiten" — in Atjeh, of the domestic animals, almost exclusively buffaloes are occasionally milked by the population; in the Pedirse, goats too (*De Karbouw*, p. 124).
- The consumption and preparation of *dadih* (curdled buffalo-milk): "Gestremde buffelmelk, ook op N.-Sumatra *dadih* genoemd, wordt bij de Atjehers soms met rijst of pisang, maar bij de Gajo's zelden genuttigd. De Klinganezen en Arabieren in Atjeh zijn er echter grote liefhebbers van; de eersten noemen die *tairō*, de laatsten *halib*. Vooral in [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] wordt veel *dadih* gemaakt" (*De Karbouw*, p. 124). Samalanga, on the north coast of Atjeh within the Pedir sphere, is the locality singled out for the abundance of its *dadih* production.
- The tiny golden plough at the [[Poephon blang|*poephon blang*]]: "Naar men ons in Groot-Atjeh vertelde, zou de [[malim|*malim*]] in het Pederse bij deze sakramentele handeling gebruik maken van een zeer klein gouden ploegje, dat hij even over de te bewerken bodem schuift. Evenals het ijzer, heeft ook het goud, naar Atjehse begrippen, magische potentie" — as we were told in Groot-Atjeh, the *malim* in the Pederse uses, at this sacramental act, a very small golden plough, which he slides lightly over the soil to be worked. Like iron, gold — in Atjehse conception — has magical potency (*De Karbouw*, p. 178; cf. [[Kreemer's Atjeh ([[1922]]-23)|Kreemer, *Atjeh*]] II, pp. 578 vg.).

## Historical context
The Pedir region was, in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, the most important port of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate after [[Banda Acheh|Koetaradja]] itself, and the place of call for many of the European merchants and envoys who visited Atjeh in the period of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] (r. [[1607]]–[[1636]]). The [[Treaty of Pedir ([[1819]])|Acheh convention of 1819]] between [[Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] and the Atjehnese sultan — later traced by [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] as the source of the fateful Acheh clause in the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London ([[1824]])|Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London of 1824]] — was concluded at Pedir. The region thus figures in the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history not only as a locus of buffalo-ritual practice but as a key site in the diplomatic and commercial history of the sultanate.


## Significance
Pedirse's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is twofold. First, as documented in *De Karbouw*, it is the type-locality for several specifically northern-Atjehnese buffalo-practices — the milking of goats alongside buffaloes, the abundant *dadih* production of [[Samalanga|Samalanga]], and the use of a tiny golden plough at the *poephon blang* — that distinguish it from the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] core and from the [[Gajoland]] and [[Alasland]] interior. Second, as a historical-geographical matter, it is the site of the [[Treaty of Pedir ([[1819]])|Treaty of Pedir of 1819]], one of the principal diplomatic documents of the early-nineteenth-century Anglo-Atjehnese relationship.

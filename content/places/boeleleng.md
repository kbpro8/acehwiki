**Boeleleng** was a coastal settlement on the island of [[Bali]] that became a focal point of Dutch military operations in the mid‑nineteenth century. The Dutch navy under [[E. B. van den Bosch]] anchored there on [[26 June]] [[1846]] and subsequently occupied the town with 300 marines and sailors (page 106). Treaties with the local princes of Boeleleng and [[Karang‑Assana]] were concluded after the 1846 expedition (page 106).

## Strategic Importance

Boeleleng’s harbour provided a suitable landing site for Dutch forces targeting the interior of Bali. Its capture allowed the Dutch to project power inland toward fortified positions such as [[Djagaraga]].

## Subsequent Events

During the third Bali expedition of [[1849]], Boeleleng served again as a staging point for the advance to [[Singa‑Radja]] and later to [[Djagaraga]] (page 107). After the expedition, the Dutch reinforced their authority through new treaties signed on [[15 June]] [[1849]] (page 108).

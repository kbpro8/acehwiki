**The West Coast of Aceh** — the *Westkust van Atjeh* — was the steep, rugged shoreline on the western side of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] facing the Indian Ocean, contrasting sharply with the low, marshy [[East Coast of Aceh|East Coast of Aceh]] that faced the [[Strait of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]]. The area featured difficult terrain that required extensive engineering for road construction, such as the mountain road (Dutch: *bergweg*) and the [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajoweg]] that linked the coastal villages of the Westkust to the interior highlands and, ultimately, to the colonial capital at [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]]. Beyond the engineered routes, the West Coast retained, in the early twentieth century, patches of primeval forest (Dutch: *oerbos*) that provided a glimpse of the pre-colonial environment of the residency (Dutch: *gewest*) of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

## Infrastructure and Engineering

The Dutch colonial administration built the mountain road (Dutch: *bergweg*) along the coast, overcoming significant geological obstacles presented by the Bukit Barisan range, which ran parallel to the coastline and created a narrow coastal plain intersected by river valleys that provided the only routes of access into the interior highlands. The difficulty of overland communication along the Westkust — where roads were few and frequently impassable during the rainy season — meant that the colonial administration relied heavily on maritime connections to maintain contact between its various outposts, and that the engineering of the coastal road was one of the principal public works of the residency in the closing phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the subsequent [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacificatie]]. The mountain road, together with the [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajoweg]], linked the coastal villages of the Westkust to the inland highlands and facilitated the movement of troops and goods between the coast and the [[Gajoland|Gajolanden]]. The engineering of the Westkust road was thus the counterpart, on the western littoral, of the [[Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road (Dutch: *Groote Atjehweg*)]] and the [[Atjehspoorweg|Atjeh railway]] on the eastern lowlands — though the western road was, of necessity, a more modest and more difficult work than its eastern counterpart.

## Landscape and the Primeval Forest

Beyond the engineered routes, the West Coast retained, in the early twentieth century, patches of primeval forest (Dutch: *oerbos*) that provided a glimpse of the pre-colonial environment of the residency. The primeval forest — the great ring of untouched tropical woodland that, in the colonial description, separated the populated districts of lowland Atjeh from the highland country of the [[Gajo|Gajo]] and [[Alas|Alas]] peoples — had been the strategic depth of the Acehnese resistance in the closing phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], and its preservation in patches along the Westkust was a measure of how much of the pre-colonial environment survived, even in the most heavily engineered parts of the residency. The Westkust landscape, with its steep mountains, its narrow coastal plain, and its surviving patches of primeval forest, was thus both an environmental record of the pre-colonial Atjeh and a testament to the engineering works by which the colonial state had sought to subdue and integrate the residency.

## Significance in the Colonial Geography

The West Coast of Aceh was administered, in the late colonial period, as the *afdeeling* of [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]] under the Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, and was the subject of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)|1913–1914 reorganisation]] that proposed to merge it with [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] into a combined *afdeeling*. The proposed merger reflected the practical administrative consideration that neither division alone had sufficient population to constitute a viable *afdeeling* in the new governance structure, while their geographic continuity made a combined unit logical. The Westkust, with its relatively sparse population spread along a difficult coastline, lacked the demographic weight to justify its own *afdeeling* under the reorganised system. The merger also reflected the understanding that the Westkust and [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] were, in many respects, complementary parts of a single geographic and administrative whole, connected by river valleys that ran from the mountains to the sea. The Westkust's place in the wiki is thus both environmental and administrative — a region of rugged terrain, surviving primeval forest, and modest colonial investment that contrasted sharply with the plantation economy of the [[East Coast of Aceh|East Coast]].

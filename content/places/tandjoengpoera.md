**Tandjoengpoera** — also **Tanjungpura** — was the kingdom at the mouth of the river that empties into [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] on the east coast of [[Sumatra]], in what is now the [[Langkat|Langkat]] region, and one of the polities whose memory is central to the *bedekte taal* reading of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]. Tandjoengpoera is identified, in his reading, with the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] — the "*padoeka Boeboennja*" who sent his *hoeloebalang* [[Awi Ditjoe]] to capture the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]] — and with the [[Boeboen|Boeboen]] of [[Anderson's Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra (1825)|Anderson's 1825 mission]], where a "kampong Boeboen" at the river-mouth is recorded as a temporary refuge of Malays driven out by Siamese from [[Langkawi]].

## Identification with Sjahroe'n Noewi

In chapter VIII of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]], the great kingdom of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] sends its *hoeloebalang* [[Awi Ditjoe]] — with 4,000 fighters in a hundred vessels — to capture the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]]. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], reading the chronicle through the *bedekte taal* lens, identifies this Sjahroe'n Noewi with Tandjoengpoera-Aroe: "the *padoeka* of this *Boeboen*" was, he argues, "the radja of Tandjoengpoera-Aroe" — a polity that was once indeed of significance, the land of the Hindu *dja-oe-wa* (i.e. [[Djawaka|Djawaka]]) and "in continual war with the adjoining Pasej-Samodra on the north coast". The radja of Sjahroe'n Noewi's success in making the sultan of Pasej-Samodra tribute-paying is, on this reading, the historical kernel of the chronicle episode.

## Anderson's *kampong Boeboen* (1825)

In [[Anderson's Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra (1825)|Anderson's Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra]] ([[1825]]), the place appears as a "*kampong Boeboen*" — at "Kwala Bubon or Sungey Langkat" — recorded as the temporary refuge of "a large colony of Malays, however, from Lancavy, who fled when the Siamese attacked that place". From [[Tamijang]] (where the population was "principally Achenese") to Kwala Bubon, Anderson wrote, "the land forms a deep bay, not easily perceived from a distance, in consequence of two islands that front it, and which are not easily distinguished from the mainland, unless when close in shore. The names of the islands are Pulo Tampasalee and Pulo Sampa-tuah (Pulo Tampilis and Pulo Kampei)". This is, De Roo de la Faille notes, a "clear description of the bay of Aroe with the *teroesan* between Tampilis and Kampei, and the kampong Boeboen at the river-mouth of Tandjoengpoera".

## The Persian name

The name *Sjahroe'n Noewi* is Persian — *Sjahr* "city", *noe* "new", *noewi* "newness" — together "Nieuw-stal" (New City). This Persian name De Roo de la Faille explains by reference to the new city of [[Ayodhya]] that the king of Siam built on the Menam around [[1360]]: a Siamese overlordship that extended, by the Siamese code *Kot Monthiera ban*, over Udjong-tanah, Malaka, Malayu and Worawari. Tandjoengpoera, on this reading, was at once an east-coast Hindu-Javanese kingdom of the [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] and a Siamese tributary under the Persian-style name of "New City". The same identification, De Roo de la Faille argues, fits the *Cernovem* of [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]] (~[[1430]]) and the *Xarnauz* of the *Roteiro* of [[Vasco da Gama]].

## Tandjoengpoera-Aroe and the Atjehnese age

After the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] in [[1539]], Tandjoengpoera-Aroe, like the rest of the east-coast Hindu-Javanese complex, was reduced to "poverty and piracy", so much so that in the colonial period only a "*soekoe Aroe*" was remembered. The [[Queen of Aroe|queen of Aroe]]'s flight to [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] at the fall of her kingdom — and the *soengej Si Djanda* that empties into the [[Besitang]], which the locals in [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]]'s day still associated with "the widow *Djanda Gidau* named, of the last raja of Aroe" — fix Tandjoengpoera's river-mouth as the geographical end-point of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Tandjoengpoera is the east-coast polity whose identification with [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] ties the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]]'s chronicle geography to the [[Aroe]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere, and whose later reduction by the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] marks the close of its independent history. Its identification with the *Cernovem* of [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]] and the *Xarnauz* of [[Vasco da Gama]]'s *Roteiro* also fixes it as the principal east-coast landmark of pre-Atjehnese Sumatra in European and Arabic travel record.

**Batoe Sawar** — also *Batoesawer*, modern *Batu Sawar* — was the capital of the sultanate of [[Djohor|Johor]] in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries, the seat of the sultans of Johor from c. [[1564]] until the [[1613]] Atjehnese attack, and the place where the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* (the Malay Annals) was commissioned in [[1612]] and repeatedly interrupted by the Atjehnese attack of [[1613]]. It is, in [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s reading in [[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|his survey]] of Malay literature, the principal Malay place caught up in the [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] wars against Johor.

## Foundation as Capital

Batoe Sawar became the capital of Johor under Sultan Moezaffar Sjah II (c. [[1564]]-[[1580]]), who "stichter, bovenstrooms, van de bij-residentie Séloejoet op de heuvel van die naam, om beter buiten bereik te zijn van Atjehse en Portugese schepen" (founded, upstream, the sub-residence of Séloejoet on the hill of that name, to be better out of reach of Atjehnese and Portuguese ships). The move of the capital upstream to Batoe Sawar — and the further move to the hill of Séloejoet — were the responses of the Johor sultans to the twin threats of the Atjehnese and the Portuguese, who could both reach the lower reaches of the Johor River with their seagoing fleets.

The new capital was thus, from its foundation, a response to the Atjehnese threat — the place to which the Johor court retreated in the face of the Atjehnese naval power that would, in [[1613]], catch up with it.

## The 1612 Commissioning of the Sadjarah Malajoe

The *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* was commissioned at Batoe Sawar on the *Mawlid* (Mūlūd) festival of Sunday 13 Rabīʿ I 1021 AH — that is, [[13 May]] [[1612]]. The commissioning was by *Radja 'Abdoellāh*, the younger brother of the reigning Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh III, who asked the *[[Bendahara Sri Lanang|Bendahara]]* to "beteren" (improve) an existing *Hikajat Malajoe*. The commission was for a "Geslachtslijst der Soeltanen" (Genealogy of the Sultans) — a work that, in [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s reading, was modelled on the *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* of [[1607]] that the *Bendahara* would later encounter in Atjeh.

The commissioning of the *Sadjarah Malajoe* at Batoe Sawar in [[1612]] is the principal literary event of the late-Batoe Sawar period of Johor history.

## The 1613 Atjehnese Attack

The [[1613]] Atjehnese attack on Batoe Sawar — sent by Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] — was the principal military event of the Batoe Sawar period. [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] summarises the attack: "een Atjehse vloot verscheen in 1613 onverwachts, die zich meester wist te maken van niemand minder dan de vorst 'Alā' oed-Din Ri'āyat Sjāh III, Radja 'Abdoellāh en de meeste rijksgroten, waaronder de Bēndahara. Ze worden naar Atjeh overgebracht, waar de Soeltān gehouden wordt en spoedig sterft" (an Atjehnese fleet appeared unexpectedly in 1613, which managed to seize no less than the Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh III, Radja 'Abdoellah, and most of the great men of the realm, including the *Bendahara*. They are taken to Atjeh, where the Sultan is held and soon dies).

The captives were taken to Atjeh, where Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh III died at Pasai. *Radja 'Abdoellāh* — the younger brother who had commissioned the *Sadjarah Malajoe* — "huwt met een zuster van de Atjehse Soeltān Iskandar Moeda en wordt begenadigd, waarop hij wordt teruggebracht en op de troon van zijn halfbroer gezet" (marries a sister of the Atjehnese Sultan Iskandar Muda and is pardoned, whereupon he is brought back and placed on the throne of his half-brother). The new sultan — now a vassal of Atjeh — then asked the *Bendahara* to complete the *Sadjarah Malajoe*.

## The 1615 Atjehnese Attack

A second Atjehnese attack on Batoe Sawar followed in [[1615]]. [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] records the second attack: "Andermaal verschijnt in 1615 een Atjehse vloot; dit keer weet het hof te vluchten, om jaren en jaren lang geen rust te vinden" (Again an Atjehnese fleet appears in 1615; this time the court manages to flee, to find no rest for years and years). The second attack — like the first — destroyed the city: "de stad verlaten, die nu ten tweeden male door de Atjehers wordt verwoest; even grondig thans, als Djohor (Lama) op 15 Augustus 1587 door de Portugezen" (the city abandoned, now destroyed for the second time by the Atjehnese; as thoroughly now, as Djohor (Lama) on 15 August 1587 by the Portuguese).

The second attack drove the Johor court into a long exile — the "Tweede Exodus naar Bintan en elders" (Second Exodus to Bintan and elsewhere) — that lasted from the autumn of [[1615]] to April [[1641]], when the Dutch-Johor conquest of Malacca finally allowed the court to return.

## The Sadjarah Malajoe's Diplomatic Silence

The *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]*'s account of the [[1613]] and [[1615]] Atjehnese attacks is, in [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s reading, "diplomatiek" (diplomatic) — the work stops just before the [[1615]] catastrophe: "Ze houdt, diplomatiek, juist daarvóór op, bejammerd alleen in gekuiste termen, dat de Sultan zo veranderziek was om weer hogerop een nieuwe kota te gaan stichten" (It stops, diplomatically, just before [the 1615 catastrophe], lamenting only in chastened terms that the Sultan was so changeable as to go and found a new kota higher up). The *Bendahara*'s diplomatic silence on the worst of the Atjehnese attacks is, in Hooykaas's reading, the silence of a courtier writing under the eye of the new sultan — a vassal of Atjeh — and the silence of a captive who had himself been carried off to Atjeh.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Batoe Sawar is significant as the capital of Johor in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries, and as the principal Malay place caught up in the [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] wars against Johor. The [[1612]] commissioning of the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* at Batoe Sawar, the [[1613]] Atjehnese attack that interrupted the work and carried off the *[[Bendahara Sri Lanang|Bendahara]]* to Atjeh, and the [[1615]] Atjehnese attack that destroyed the city and drove the Johor court into exile — these are the principal events of the Batoe Sawar period, and the principal context of the *Sadjarah Malajoe*'s composition.

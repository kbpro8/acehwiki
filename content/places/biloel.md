**Biloel** was a Dutch temporary military post established in the [[XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[1890s]], in succession to the abandoned post of [[Boekit Daroe]] — a site that had been *aanhoudend beschoten* (continuously bombarded) and that had accordingly been abandoned for the more defensible site of Biloel. The post at Biloel was one of the four Dutch temporary posts — together with [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] — that were besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] from the Dutch alliance in [[March]] [[1896]], and its relief on [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]] was one of the principal engagements of the spring [[1896]] crisis. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Biloel was a [[Dutch]] outpost in the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|sagi of the XXV Moekims]], near the mountains on the border of the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], constructed to protect the [[IX Moekims]] against incursions from the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]], Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]. Biloel was besieged and isolated from [[29 March]] to [[8 April]] [[1896]] in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]], was reinforced on [[8 April]] with 5 officers and 136 men of the 3e Bataljon, was evacuated and burned on [[12 April]] [[1896]], and thereafter served as the gathering point for the 1e colonne of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lamkrak Moekims of 9–10 June 1896]].

> **Note on the date of the relief.** The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records the relief of Biloel as [[9 April]] [[1896]] under [[Kolonel Stemfoort]]; [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96* records the relief as [[8 April]] [[1896]] under [[Kolonel der Infanterie|Kolonel]] [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] with reinforcement by 5 officers and 136 men of the 3e Bataljon. The discrepancy is one day; both sources agree on the principal facts of the siege, relief, and subsequent evacuation and burning on [[12 April]] [[1896]].

## The replacement of Boekit Daroe

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that Biloel was established as the replacement for [[Boekit Daroe]] — a post that had been *aanhoudend beschoten* (continuously bombarded) by Atjehnese fire, and that had accordingly been abandoned in favour of the more defensible site of Biloel. The album's reference identifies the *aanhoudend beschoten* condition of Boekit Daroe as one of the principal operational pressures of the late *concentratie* period — and the establishment of Biloel as the Dutch response. A *versterking* (fortification) was erected at Biloel, of which the album preserves an illustration.

## The siege of March-April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]], the post at Biloel — together with the posts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] — was besieged by the forces of the defected warlord. The album's narrative of the siege records that the garrison at Biloel, like that at the other temporary posts, had to hold its ground for weeks against the Atjehnese fire, with no communication possible with the *geconcentreerde linie*. The besieged garrison could only listen to the *vuren* — the firing — of the relief expeditions in the distance, and judge from the sound whether the relieving force was a strong one — that is, whether reinforcements from [[Java]] had arrived.

## The relief of 9 April 1896 (Atjeh-Album account)

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Biloel was relieved on [[9 April]] [[1896]] by a column of two battalions under the command of [[Kolonel Stemfoort]]. The relief was, however, costly: Captain [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]] and seven subordinates were killed, and Lieutenants [[Ten Seldam|ten Seldam]] and [[Hockstein]] were wounded together with 23 subordinates. After the relief, the post at Biloel — together with that at [[Lamkoenjit]] — was *geslecht* (dismantled), and the garrison was withdrawn to the *geconcenteerde linie*.

## The role of the post in the 1896 crisis (Atjeh-Album account)

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Biloel — like that at Lamkoenjit — was one of the four Dutch temporary posts whose siege defined the spring [[1896]] crisis. The album's narrative of the relief expeditions of [[April]] [[1896]] records that the column that relieved Biloel and Lamkoenjit then turned eastward to relieve [[Anagaloeng]] — a movement that required the dismantling of Biloel and Lamkoenjit before the column could proceed.

## Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

Biloel lay in the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|sagi of the XXV Moekims]] near the foot of the *gebergte* (mountain range), on the road that ran from the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] along the foot of the mountains in a south-east direction. The post was positioned to protect the [[IX Moekims]] (especially the mukims of Oleh Soesoe and [[Lamkoenjit]]) against incursions from the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak, Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue]] of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]. The garrison in [[1895]] consisted of 1 captain, 2 lieutenants, 1 medical officer, 2 hospital attendants, 102 infantry, and 1 officer and 11 men of the *vestingartillerie* (fortress artillery). The post was linked to [[Lamkoenjit]] and [[Tjot Goeë]] by the road that formed the southern chain of the [[buitenlinie]]. An old road ran from Biloel to [[Boekit Daroe]].

## Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

Biloel had been constructed as part of the [[buitenlinie]] to dominate the southern approaches to the [[IX Moekims]] and to serve as the south-western anchor of the Dutch outer line against the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] country. The post had been the scene of the last Dutch operation in the Lamkrak before [[1896]]: the [[1878]] campaign of [[Generaal van der Heiden|Generaal van der Heiden]], who built a road from Sibreh to Biloel. After [[1893]] the post was the base from which [[Teuku Umar]]'s *legioen* (auxiliary legion) was supposed to operate against the Lamkrak. From [[27 March]] [[1896]] the garrison was commanded by [[Kapitein P. H. van der Wedden|Kapitein Van der Wedden]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

Biloel was one of the two principal Dutch posts besieged in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. From [[29 March]] to [[8 April]] [[1896]] the post was isolated and besieged by hostile *benden* (war-bands); on [[30 March]] [[1896]] a reconnaissance from Biloel toward [[Tjot Goeë]] led to the wounding of 1e luit. [[E. van Zijdveld|Van Zijdveld]] and fusilier Stoppelenburg. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] a relief column under [[Kolonel der Infanterie|Kolonel]] [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] fought through to Biloel from [[Lampeneroet]], losing [[Kapitein F. P. A. Geluk|Kapitein Geluk]] (killed in action near the *Masdjid Oleh-Soesoeh*) and reinforcing the garrison with 5 officers and 136 men of the 3e Bataljon. On [[12 April]] [[1896]] Biloel was evacuated and burned together with [[Lamkoenjit]] by order of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]], and the garrison was withdrawn to the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcenteerde Linie (Concentrated Line)]]. On [[9 June]] [[1896]] Biloel became the gathering point for the 1e colonne of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lamkrak Moekims]], under [[J. F. Th. Veeren|luit.-kol. Veeren]] of the 9e Bataljon. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] the area around Biloel was patrolled by Dutch troops in the course of the [[Machtsvertoon (20 June 1896)|four-column show-of-force]]. On [[31 May]] [[1896]] the old Achehnese *versterking* of [[Tjot Paklat]] east of [[Tjot Iri]] was found occupied by approximately 30 Pedireezen.

## Significance

Biloel's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as one of the four Dutch temporary posts besieged in the spring [[1896]] crisis, and as the post whose relief on [[9 April]] [[1896]] cost the life of Captain [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]]. The post's establishment as the replacement for the continuously-bombarded [[Boekit Daroe]] illustrates the way in which the Dutch defensive perimeter was incrementally adjusted in response to Atjehnese pressure, and its dismantling after the relief illustrates the way in which the temporary posts were *geslecht* once their relief had been accomplished. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* adds that Biloel was the south-western anchor of the [[buitenlinie]] and the principal Dutch outpost against the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] country. Its siege and evacuation in [[April]] [[1896]] marked the effective end of the outer-line policy, and its reoccupation as the base of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 9–10 June 1896]] symbolised the Dutch shift from passive defence to active chastisement of the hostile mukims.

## Account from *Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh* ([[1911]])

[[P. H. R. Beuming]] devotes the second sketch of *[[Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh (1911)|Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh]]* to Biloel, which he describes as *een der meest beschoten voorposten* — one of the most heavily shot-at forward posts — and reproduces a photograph of it in which a cross marks the sentry-box that was the particular target of the enemy marksmen.

### Situation and construction

Beuming places the post in the neighbourhood of [[Lamkoenjit]] and [[Tjot Goé]], east of the hill called the [[Boekit Terin]]; to the west it bordered the extensive territory of [[Lamkrak]], and its front looked out on the mountain chain [[Batoe Mengoera]]. The garrison consisted of about 150 bayonets under the command of a captain. Like the other forward posts, Biloel was built wholly of bamboo and roofed with *[[Atap|atap]]*, on the pattern described at [[Voorpostenbenting]].

### Foundation and the quiet period

Beuming records that the outer posts Biloel and [[Lamkoenjit]] were established in [[May]] and [[July]] [[1894]] respectively, to hold in check, together with the already existing post [[Tjot Goé]], the hostile [[VI Moekims]] and [[IV Moekims]] in the [[Lamkrak|Lamkraksche]]. There followed a period of quiet: *Het scheen wel, of de Atjeher den onzaligen krijg moede was* — it seemed as if the Acehnese were weary of the ill-starred war. The *[[Sawah|sawah's]]* were cultivated again and gradually the men returned to the *kampongs* to devote themselves to domestic occupations. That period of rest, under the alliance with [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]], was however of short duration.

### The bombardment of [[1895]]–[[1896]]

At the end of [[1895]] the bands of [[Panglima Polim]] and of the [[Sultanspartij|Sultan's party]] were acting everywhere with boldness and had marked out the forward posts in particular; since orders had been given from above to operate as little as possible outside the *[[Benting|bentings]]*, the Acehnese marksmen had free play, and Biloel above all had to suffer.

Notwithstanding the privations, the spirit among the men was excellent: they troubled themselves very little about the continual firing and no longer even noticed the bullets whistling through the reed roofing. Yet victims fell often, by day or at night as the men lay on the *[[Baleh-baleh|baleh-baleh]]*. When the firing became too severe a grenade was sent over, after which it was quiet for a time before the marksmen returned.

Beuming preserves the incident of the post's cook, whose great cooking-kettle was holed by an enemy bullet while he was preparing pea soup with salt pork — *snert van de bovenste plank* — and who, after shaking his ladle in impotent rage at the distant marksman, drove a wooden plug into the hole so that the men had their soup at midday after all.

### The sharpshooter

The post gave its name to the episode of [[The sharpshooter of Biloel|the sharpshooter of Biloel]], the unnamed Acehnese marksman who from a concealed trench in the burnt-off *[[Alang-alang|alang-alang]]* behind a bamboo thicket picked off the sentries at daybreak, whom the Europeans supposed to be a deserted European fusilier and the native fusiliers took for an *[[Orang Setan|Orang-Setan]]*, and who was killed at dawn by an ambush of a European sergeant and five fusiliers. After his death the *benting* continued to be shot at, but the Acehnese no longer ventured so near the post.

### The relief and the razing of [[1896]]

In the crisis of [[1896]] Biloel was one of the enclosed forward posts for whose fate anxiety prevailed, there being no troops available to relieve them. On [[8 April]] [[1896]], the day after the arrival of the Java troops with General [[J. A. Vetter|Vetter]], an excursion of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e]], [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e]] and two companies of the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] with mountain artillery, engineers and two platoons of cavalry succeeded under heavy fire in reaching Biloel and Lamkoenjit. The garrison of Lamkoenjit was reinforced; but on news that Toekoe Oemar was advancing with 4,000 men the commanding officer took up the retreat, *Biloel kon dus niet meer worden versterkt*. The march cost the life of Captain [[F. P. A. Geluk]] and three European and four native fusiliers, with the 1st lieutenants [[J. H. Hoeksteijn]] and [[R. ten Seldam]] and twenty-three fusiliers wounded.

Before the [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)|Beradin operation]] of [[23 May|23]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]], Biloel was among the former forward posts — with [[Senelop|Sennelop]], [[Lamsoet]], [[Anak Galoeng]], [[Lambarih]], [[Lamkoenjit]], [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb|Toengkoep]] — that were razed after their garrisons had been relieved, an operation in which further losses were sustained.

## Nineteenth-Century Historiography & Campaign Analysis

## Role in Coastal Defense

As noted in historical dispatches in *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* ([[1876]]), Biloel formed part of the secondary defensive network protecting coastal roadsteads around Aceh Proper. 

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in [[1873]] and [[1874]], naval patrols of the [[Dutch East Indies Government]] monitored Biloel to prevent indigenous vessels from running munitions ashore to support forces at [[Kraton of Aceh]].

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Biloel** was a Dutch military post in the [[IX Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]], erected in the night of [[20 May]]–[[21 May]] [[1894]] around an old *[[Messigit|Messigit]]* at the foot of the steep southern mountains of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh valley]], and invested by the enemy from the afternoon of [[29 March]] [[1896]] until its relief and evacuation in [[April]] [[1896]].

## Foundation

Hooijer recorded that Biloel was raised in the night of [[20 May]] to [[21 May]] [[1894]] at the request of [[Toekoe Nja Mohammad]], head of the [[IX Moekims]]. He and [[Toekoe Hoessin Longbattah]], whose mother lived at the place, showed the Dutch column the way there. The garrison of [[Boekit Daroe]], west of [[Tjot Goeë]], was transferred to Biloel on the same date, and the road between Tjot Goeë and Biloel was constructed in consequence.

## Description

The fortification was built around the old *Messigit*, whose roof had been burnt and renewed; within its white wall the officers' quarters were established. Two field-pieces and a *[[Mitrailleur|mitrailleur]]* stood in battery in the two bastions. Outside the walls to the left lay the kitchen; to the right, the bathing-place with the latrine, for which, in default of anything better, large cooking-kettles served. In the tree rising behind the buildings — which were open on the outer side — an observation post was made for the artillery officer, from which he could follow the effect of his shots on the small enemy fortifications constructed in the mountains. The whole was surrounded by an earthen parapet with sod banquettes and protected against escalade by a standing *[[IJzerdraadversperring|ijzerdraadversperring]]*. Behind the bench where the officers sat talking, an earthen wall had been thrown up to catch the enemy's bullets.

Among the officers Hooijer identified on the plate were [[Officier Dijkstra|Dijkstra]], [[Luitenant Zon|Zon]] (who later fell), [[Luitenant Zijdveld|Zijdveld]], [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colyn]], [[Kapitein Engelbert van Bevervoorde]], [[Kapitein Van der Wedden|Van der Wedden]], [[Kapitein Schadée|Schadée]], the lieutenants [[Luitenant Van Delden|Van Delden]] and [[Luitenant Kastens|Kastens]], and the medical officers [[Officier van gezondheid Nanta|Nanta]] and [[Officier van gezondheid Tromp|Tromp]].

## The intended base for the Lamkrak operation

Post life passed under the ordinary routine of being fired on now and then and answering, until in [[March]] [[1896]] the march on [[Lamkrak]] was fixed, by which the great work was to be completed that would surround the Dutch line with well-disposed districts cleared of *[[Moslemin]]*. A fine shed roofed with sheet iron was erected to serve as magazine and auxiliary hospital, and Biloel, with some others, was to be raised from a temporary post to a permanent fortification. Great activity was expected, for [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] was to assemble there a thousand warriors and a thousand coolies with which to begin the action against Lamkrak.

## 29 March 1896

Instead of all these, on the morning of [[29 March]] [[1896]] no one appeared at the *[[Passer|passer]]* beneath the great tree — not even the police *oppasser* — and it became apparent that the *[[Blokhuis|blokhuizen]]* garrisoned by friendly Achehnese in the neighbourhood had been abandoned. Towards half past four in the afternoon [[Kapitein Schadée]] set out with a patrol of sixty men towards [[Tjot Goeë]] to satisfy himself of the state of affairs; when the point had pushed some three hundred paces beyond the covered ground surrounding Biloel on the north side, it received heavy fire from all sides across the flooded rice-fields. [[Luitenant Zijdveld]], hit by a bullet in the lower jaw, had to fall back, and from that moment Biloel remained surrounded.

## The siege

Hooijer recorded that provisions were sufficient: only the *toespijzen* (side-dishes accompanying rice) to accompany the rice, and bread, were too few. Stockfish and tinned minced meat were in abundance, and a couple of buffaloes and a goat that were shot replaced fresh meat. Only on [[9 April]] [[1896]] did a relief force appear, which turned back again on the report that [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] was approaching.

## Abandonment

Biloel and [[Lam Koenjit]] were lifted and burnt in [[April]] [[1896]], the Dutch troops hastening back over the muddy fields on which they had endured almost three years of a temporary station. Hooijer noted that, unlike [[Tjot Goeë]], Biloel was not retained.

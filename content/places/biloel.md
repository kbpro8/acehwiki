**Biloel** was a Dutch temporary military post established in the [[XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[1890s]], in succession to the abandoned post of [[Boekit Daroe]] — a site that had been *aanhoudend beschoten* (continuously bombarded) and that had accordingly been abandoned for the more defensible site of Biloel. The post at Biloel was one of the four Dutch temporary posts — together with [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] — that were besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] from the Dutch alliance in [[March]] [[1896]], and its relief on [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]] was one of the principal engagements of the spring [[1896]] crisis. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, Biloel was a [[Dutch]] outpost in the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|sagi of the XXV Moekims]], near the mountains on the border of the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], constructed to protect the [[IX Moekims]] against incursions from the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]], Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]. Biloel was besieged and isolated from [[29 March]] to [[8 April]] [[1896]] in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]], was reinforced on [[8 April]] with 5 officers and 136 men of the 3e Bataljon, was evacuated and burned on [[12 April]] [[1896]], and thereafter served as the gathering point for the 1e colonne of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lamkrak Moekims of 9–10 June 1896]].

> **Note on the date of the relief.** The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records the relief of Biloel as [[9 April]] [[1896]] under [[Kolonel Stemfoort]]; [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96* records the relief as [[8 April]] [[1896]] under [[Kolonel der Infanterie|Kolonel]] [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] with reinforcement by 5 officers and 136 men of the 3e Bataljon. The discrepancy is one day; both sources agree on the principal facts of the siege, relief, and subsequent evacuation and burning on [[12 April]] [[1896]].

## The replacement of Boekit Daroe

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that Biloel was established as the replacement for [[Boekit Daroe]] — a post that had been *aanhoudend beschoten* (continuously bombarded) by Atjehnese fire, and that had accordingly been abandoned in favour of the more defensible site of Biloel. The album's reference identifies the *aanhoudend beschoten* condition of Boekit Daroe as one of the principal operational pressures of the late *concentratie* period — and the establishment of Biloel as the Dutch response. A *versterking* (fortification) was erected at Biloel, of which the album preserves an illustration.

## The siege of March-April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]], the post at Biloel — together with the posts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] — was besieged by the forces of the defected warlord. The album's narrative of the siege records that the garrison at Biloel, like that at the other temporary posts, had to hold its ground for weeks against the Atjehnese fire, with no communication possible with the *geconcentreerde linie*. The besieged garrison could only listen to the *vuren* — the firing — of the relief expeditions in the distance, and judge from the sound whether the relieving force was a strong one — that is, whether reinforcements from [[Java]] had arrived.

## The relief of 9 April 1896 (Atjeh-Album account)

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Biloel was relieved on [[9 April]] [[1896]] by a column of two battalions under the command of [[Kolonel Stemfoort]]. The relief was, however, costly: Captain [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]] and seven subordinates were killed, and Lieutenants [[Ten Seldam|ten Seldam]] and [[Hockstein]] were wounded together with 23 subordinates. After the relief, the post at Biloel — together with that at [[Lamkoenjit]] — was *geslecht* (dismantled), and the garrison was withdrawn to the *geconcenteerde linie*.

## The role of the post in the 1896 crisis (Atjeh-Album account)

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Biloel — like that at Lamkoenjit — was one of the four Dutch temporary posts whose siege defined the spring [[1896]] crisis. The album's narrative of the relief expeditions of [[April]] [[1896]] records that the column that relieved Biloel and Lamkoenjit then turned eastward to relieve [[Anagaloeng]] — a movement that required the dismantling of Biloel and Lamkoenjit before the column could proceed.

## Description and Location (ATJEH '96 account)

Biloel lay in the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|sagi of the XXV Moekims]] near the foot of the *gebergte* (mountain range), on the road that ran from the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] along the foot of the mountains in a south-east direction. The post was positioned to protect the [[IX Moekims]] (especially the mukims of Oleh Soesoe and [[Lamkoenjit]]) against incursions from the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak, Kroeng Mak, Loetoe, and Lam Lheue]] of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]. The garrison in [[1895]] consisted of 1 captain, 2 lieutenants, 1 medical officer, 2 hospital attendants, 102 infantry, and 1 officer and 11 men of the *vestingartillerie* (fortress artillery). The post was linked to [[Lamkoenjit]] and [[Tjot Goeë]] by the road that formed the southern chain of the [[buitenlinie]]. An old road ran from Biloel to [[Boekit Daroe]].

## Historical Background (ATJEH '96 account)

Biloel had been constructed as part of the [[buitenlinie]] to dominate the southern approaches to the [[IX Moekims]] and to serve as the south-western anchor of the Dutch outer line against the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] country. The post had been the scene of the last Dutch operation in the Lamkrak before [[1896]]: the [[1878]] campaign of [[Generaal van der Heiden|Generaal van der Heiden]], who built a road from Sibreh to Biloel. After [[1893]] the post was the base from which [[Teuku Umar]]'s *legioen* (auxiliary legion) was supposed to operate against the Lamkrak. From [[27 March]] [[1896]] the garrison was commanded by [[Kapitein P. H. van der Wedden|Kapitein Van der Wedden]].

## Role in the 1896 Operations (ATJEH '96 account)

Biloel was one of the two principal Dutch posts besieged in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. From [[29 March]] to [[8 April]] [[1896]] the post was isolated and besieged by hostile *benden* (war-bands); on [[30 March]] [[1896]] a reconnaissance from Biloel toward [[Tjot Goeë]] led to the wounding of 1e luit. [[E. van Zijdveld|Van Zijdveld]] and fusilier Stoppelenburg. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] a relief column under [[Kolonel der Infanterie|Kolonel]] [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] fought through to Biloel from [[Lampeneroet]], losing [[Kapitein F. P. A. Geluk|Kapitein Geluk]] (killed in action near the *Masdjid Oleh-Soesoeh*) and reinforcing the garrison with 5 officers and 136 men of the 3e Bataljon. On [[12 April]] [[1896]] Biloel was evacuated and burned together with [[Lamkoenjit]] by order of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]], and the garrison was withdrawn to the [[Geconcenteerde Linie|Geconcenteerde Linie (Concentrated Line)]]. On [[9 June]] [[1896]] Biloel became the gathering point for the 1e colonne of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lamkrak Moekims]], under [[J. F. Th. Veeren|luit.-kol. Veeren]] of the 9e Bataljon. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] the area around Biloel was patrolled by Dutch troops in the course of the [[Machtsvertoon (20 June 1896)|four-column show-of-force]]. On [[31 May]] [[1896]] the old Achehnese *versterking* of [[Tjot Paklat]] east of [[Tjot Iri]] was found occupied by approximately 30 Pedireezen.

## Significance

Biloel's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as one of the four Dutch temporary posts besieged in the spring [[1896]] crisis, and as the post whose relief on [[9 April]] [[1896]] cost the life of Captain [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]]. The post's establishment as the replacement for the continuously-bombarded [[Boekit Daroe]] illustrates the way in which the Dutch defensive perimeter was incrementally adjusted in response to Atjehnese pressure, and its dismantling after the relief illustrates the way in which the temporary posts were *geslecht* once their relief had been accomplished. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* adds that Biloel was the south-western anchor of the [[buitenlinie]] and the principal Dutch outpost against the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] country. Its siege and evacuation in [[April]] [[1896]] marked the effective end of the outer-line policy, and its reoccupation as the base of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 9–10 June 1896]] symbolised the Dutch shift from passive defence to active chastisement of the hostile mukims.

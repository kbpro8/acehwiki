A location in the V Mukims area of the wider [[Pidie]] territory. In the [[Hikayat Pochut Muhamat]]: 'at Reubee, where he pays all due homage to the saint that lies buried there, he does the same in respect of the V Mukims.' Thus Reubee is specifically sacred as the burial place of a saint. The sadati dialogue mentions [[Tuan Sri Reubee]] as an advisor who helped [[Pangulee Beunaroe]] understand the situation with [[Pochut Muhamat]].

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — V Mukims

The **V Mukims** — the "Five Mukims" — was a territorial district of the [[XXII Mukims]] (the highland sagi of [[Great Acheh]], known as the [[Tunong]]), which is mentioned in *The Achehnese* Vol. I as the uleebalangship of [[Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh]], one of the four principal chiefs of the XXII Mukims named in the traditional list of the "twelve uleebalangs who appoint and dethrone princes."

### The Twelve Uleebalangs

Snouck Hurgronje's principal notice of the V Mukims comes in his account of the constitutional guardianship of the sultanate. After observing that, with the growth of the power of the panglimas and the uleebalangs in the sagis, "the number of guardians of the sultanate increased, and the Achehnese authorities on adat have in noticing this change given the rein to their predilection for round numbers," he explains that they speak of the "twelve uleebalangs, who appoint and dethrone princes," and describe them as consisting of four from each sagi. "To make up the tale of twelve, they call the head kalis of the three sagis uleebalangs, which is quite inaccurate. They of course include in their list the panglima sagi; thus two are left for each sagi who are regarded as the principal uleebalangs, or rather were so accounted at the time when the tradition was placed on record."

The list so drawn up gives, for the [[XXII Mukims]], "Teuku Ba'et, uleebalang of the [[VII Mukims Ba'et|VII Mukims]], and [[Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh]], chief of the V Mukims." For the [[XXVI Mukims]], the two were [[Teuku Ateue'|Teuku Ateue']] and [[Teuku Tungkob]]; for the [[XXV Mukims]], the two Ne's. The V Mukims thus appears as the second of the two great uleebalangships of the highland sagi.

### A List "Subject to Round Numbers"

Snouck is careful, however, to warn his reader that this list "at most represents the conditions that existed during a short period. The whole twelve had not in the long run a voice in the affairs of the capital, while some of them had absolutely no share therein. On the other hand there were uleebalangs in the sultan's territory and the so-called wakeueh-districts (as for instance [[Teuku Kali]], the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]], the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata|Imeums of Lueng Bata]] and [[Chade'|Chadé]] etc.) who had much more weight in the scale than many of the members of the board of twelve."

The V Mukims, though named in the traditional list, must therefore be understood as one of the highland districts whose chief was, for a time, of sufficient weight to be reckoned among the king-makers of [[Acheh]].

### Significance

The V Mukims is named in Vol. I chiefly as the territorial designation of [[Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh]], one of the four chiefs of the [[XXII Mukims]] singled out by adat as "principal uleebalangs" of the highland sagi. Its appearance in the list of the twelve uleebalangs marks it out, along with [[VII Mukims Ba'et|VII Mukims Ba'et]], as one of the two great territorial divisions of the [[Tunong]] whose chiefs were deemed capable of turning the scale at a royal election.

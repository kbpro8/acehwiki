**Order of the Netherlands Lion** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

On [[9 July]] [[1906]], under article 43 of the *Reglement op het beleid der Regeering in Nederlandsch-Indië*, he was temporarily charged with effect from [[3 September]] [[1906]] with the civil and military authority in the [[Gouvernement Celebes en Onderhoorigheden]], at 1,500 guilders a month, with the title of *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Celebes en Onderhoorigheden* and simultaneously Military Commander of [[Menado]] and [[Timor]] en Onderhoorigheden. There he oversaw the [[Pacification of the Province of Celebes (1906–1908)|pacification of Celebes]] and, as the closing piece of his eastern service, the [[Onderneming tegen Soembawa (1908)|undertaking against Bima and Soembawa]] in [[1908]] — an operation carried out with a handful of *marechaussée* and described by du Croo as a classic example of rapid and conclusive pacification. He was promoted Commander of the *Militaire Willemsorde* ("Military William Order") by royal decree of [[2 September]] [[1909]] no. 41 for the Soembawa operations of [[16 February]] to [[9 May]] [[1908]], and appointed Knight in the [[Order of the Netherlands Lion]] by royal decree of May [[1909]] no. 7.

### From [[1909]]

**[[1909]]** saw continued heavy fighting in [[Keureutòë]] — 269 opponents killed, including seventeen band-heads and seven Teungkoes, eleven prisoners including [[Tgk. di Reubèë]], and seventy-two submissions — and in the [[Meergebied]], where 132 resistance men fell and where on [[24 December]] a group of infantry was surprised at [[Tjampoeron]], the captured carbines forming the [[Telongbende]]. In February [[H. J. Schmidt]] found [[Keudjroeën Oebit]] in the [[Tangsé]] valley. On the [[Westkust van Atjeh|west coast]] [[A. Geertsema Beckeringh]] at last obtained sufficient means of power. In December Swart gave Schmidt the special commission against the [[Tirò Teungkoes]]. On [[31 March]] [[1909]] Swart was extraordinarily promoted *Kolonel*; on [[2 September]] [[1909]] he was made Commander of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] for [[Onderneming tegen Soembawa (1908)|Soembawa]]; in May [[1909]] Knight in the [[Order of the Netherlands Lion]]. He was authorised to dispose of 6,000 guilders in interest-free advances to needy persons who had submitted, and of compensations to the head of [[Peudada]] and to [[T. Radja Tjoet]] of [[Meuké']].

### From [[Extract from the service record of H. N. A. Swart]]

- Knight 4th Class of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]], for his conduct at various encounters with the enemy in Atjeh during [[1890]], royal decree of [[30 August]] [[1891]] no. 2.
- *Eereteeken voor belangrijke krijgsbedrijven*: Atjeh [[1873]]–[[1890]]; Atjeh [[1906]]–[[1910]]; South Celebes [[1905]]–[[1908]]; Lesser Sunda Islands [[1905]]–[[1909]].
- A sum of 825 guilders as reward for translating into Malay the *Uittreksel uit de Recrutenschool* with the principal duties and rules of conduct of the soldier and of the group commander in action, decree no. 13 of [[15 September]] [[1898]].
- Knight 3rd Class of the Militaire Willemsorde, for distinction in the operations in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden from [[1 June]] [[1899]] to [[31 December]] of that year, royal decree of [[13 July]] [[1900]] no. 29.
- The distinguishing mark for long Netherlands service as an officer, awarded [[6 December]] [[1901]].
- Commander of the Militaire Willemsorde, for distinction in the operations on the island of [[Soembawa]] between [[16 February]] and [[9 May]] [[1908]], royal decree of [[2 September]] [[1909]] no. 41.
- Knight in the [[Order of the Netherlands Lion]], royal decree of May [[1909]] no. 7.
- Adjutant in Extraordinary Service to Her Majesty the Queen, royal decree no. 65 of [[7 April]] [[1916]].
- Grand Officer in the [[Orde van Oranje-Nassau]], royal decree of [[27 August]] [[1921]] no. 49.

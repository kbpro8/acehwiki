**Great Acheh** (*Acheh* proper, or the "kernel of the kingdom") was the heartland of the [[Aceh Sultanate]], distinct from the wider dependencies and coast states. The [[Acehnese People|Acehnese]] themselves placed the limits of the whole kingdom at [[Tamiang|Teumieng]] (Tamiang) on the East Coast, and at [[Baros]] or some other southern point on the West Coast. Far more restricted is the territory they described as "Acheh" proper, or "Great Acheh."

## Boundaries

According to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]], Great Acheh was bounded by a line extending from [[Kluang]] on the West, to [[Krueng Raya]] on the North Coast, passing through [[Reueng-reueng]], [[Pancha]], and [[Janthoe]]. These three places lay on footpaths uniting Acheh with the territory of [[Pidie]] (vulgarly Pedir). The boundaries were somewhat differently marked in ordinary maps and in [[K. F. H. van Langen|Van Langen]]'s brochure.

## Shape

The form of Great Acheh — its true shape — the Acehnese delighted to compare to that of a *jeuëë* (winnowing-basket), as could be seen in the shape of the [[Acheh River]] debouchement (*kuala Acheh*), which suggested to their fancy the somewhat sharply pointed mouth of the winnower, whence unclean particles adhering to the husked rice were shaken out.

## Administrative Divisions

Proceeding down the [[Acheh River]], one found on the left the territory of the XXV Mukims and on the right that of the XXVI Mukims — the intervening space up to the broad belt of outlying country being occupied by other territorial groupings. Great Acheh was divided into three [[Sagi System|sagis]]: the XXII Mukims (highland), the XXV Mukims, and the XXVI Mukims.

## The Capital

The centre of Great Acheh was [[Banda Acheh]] (the "trading mart of Acheh"), also known as the [[Dalam]] or residence of the Sultan (incorrectly termed *Kraton* by the Dutch, though also known as Kuta Raja or "the king's fort"). Before the [[Aceh War]], this formed the nucleus of fine and prosperous [[Gampong|gampongs]].

## Population Groups

The people of Great Acheh differed from one another in local peculiarities of language, manners, superstition, and dress. Most notable was the difference between the highlanders (*ureuëng tunöng*) — particularly the people of the [[Sagi System|Sagi]] of the XXII Mukims — and the lowlanders (*ureuëng baröh*) who inhabited the greater part of the two remaining sagis, including the capital. The *ureuëng Buëng* inhabiting the VII Mukims Buëng in the Sagi of the XXVI Mukims resembled the highlanders in language and customs.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Acheh proper

**Acheh proper** is the term employed by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] for the heartland of [[Acheh|Acheh]] outside the limits of the actual sultanate — the interior country of the three *sagis*, as opposed both to the sultan's immediate domain round the [[Dalam]] and to the outlying coast-dependencies. It corresponds in essentials to what is elsewhere called Great Acheh, the "true Acheh" of the Achehnese themselves; Snouck uses the two expressions interchangeably when contrasting the heartland with Pidie, the East and West Coasts, and the more distant dependencies.

### Three sagis

Acheh proper is divided into three *sagis* (Achehnese *sagi*, "angle") or confederacies, each composed of a certain number of mukims whence it derives its name. These are the [[XXII Mukims]], the [[XXV Mukims|XXV Mukims]] and the [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI Mukims]]. The chiefs — *panglima sagi* — who stand or are supposed to stand at the head of these three districts, exercise an authority which extends only to matters of general interest; for the rest, the uleebalangs governed their own territories just as though there were no sagi in existence. The uleebalangs of Acheh proper are described as at once rajas, military commanders and judges in their own territories, with their own bantas (right-hand men), rakans (companions) and panglima prang (war-chiefs) to assist them in the work of government and the administration of justice.

### Distinction from the dependencies

The distinction between Acheh proper and the outlying dependencies is repeatedly drawn in *The Achehnese*. Chiefs in the dependencies — on the [[East Coast (of Acheh)|East]] and [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coasts]], in Pidie and elsewhere — sometimes took pride in adopting the title of uleebalang as a general designation of rank; the chiefs in Acheh proper, who rather looked down on these aspirants, were wont to remark that the rulers of the dependencies were really only *keuchis* or *mantris*, no matter what titles they might have gained from chance, royal favour or their own arrogance.

The sultans, too, observed the same distinction. Towards the uleebalangs of Acheh proper they never indulged in the highhanded methods sometimes practised on the lesser chiefs of the dependencies — for instance, the ceremony of "cooling" (*peusijuk*) imposed upon offenders summoned to court, in which they were required to sit at the entrance of the royal dwelling in a heap of ashes with a hen under the arm, as a gentle reminder that they must consider themselves prisoners until they had received forgiveness. Such ignominy was reserved for the less important chiefs of the dependencies.

### The "twin mukims" of Acheh proper

A political device peculiar to Acheh proper was the so-called "twin mukim" or *mukim keumala*. According to one tradition, an early sultan succeeded in getting the ruling chiefs both in Acheh proper and the various dependencies to consent to a certain portion of their territories (usually three mukims) being severed from their control. The severed tract, though geographically still within Acheh proper, was withdrawn from the authority of the uleebalang or panglima sagi within whose sagi it lay, and placed directly under the sultan or a member of the royal family. The [[III Mukims Keureukon|Mukim Lhee]] (the III Mukims), now officially known as the III Mukims Keureukon, was such a tract: it belonged to none of the three sagis of Acheh, though both the [[XXVI Mukims]] and the [[XXII Mukims]] claimed it. Its people acknowledged themselves *ureueng Tunong*, thus admitting the correctness of the assertion of the sagi of the XXII Mukims, but in the wars between the two above-named sagis the Mukims III took part with neither side.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Great Acheh

**Great Acheh** (also written **Great-Acheh**) is the "true Acheh" of the Achehnese themselves — the kernel of the kingdom of [[Acheh|Acheh]], distinguished from the coast-dependencies that supplied its trade and its men, and the seat of those political institutions which the Achehnese War of [[1873]] and after only partially disturbed. Snouck Hurgronje uses the term to translate what the Achehnese call simply "Acheh" proper, as opposed to the outlying districts of [[Pidie]], the [[East Coast (of Acheh)|East Coast]], the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coast]] and the more distant dependencies.

### Boundaries and form

The Achehnese themselves fix the boundaries of Great Acheh by a line extending from Kluang on the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West]] to Kruceng Raya on the North Coast, and passing through Reueng-reueng, Pancha and Janthoe. The form of this territory the Achehnese delight to compare to that of a winnowing-basket (*jeu'ë*); the débcouchement of the Acheh river (*Krueng Acheh*) suggests to their fancy the somewhat sharply pointed mouth of the winnower, whence all unclean particles adhering to the husked rice are shaken out.

Proceeding down stream from the headwaters, the territory of the [[XXV Mukims]] lies on the left, that of the [[XXVI Mukims]] on the right, and the intervening space up to the broad belt of outlying country is occupied by the [[XXII Mukims]]. The three together compose the *lhèë sagië* (Malay *tiga sagi*), the "three angles" of Acheh, and the three uleebalangs who stand at their head are called *panglima sagië*, "heads of sagi or angles." Snouck observes that the name of the kingdom itself, applied to the chief town and to the entire population as well as to the country, is of unknown origin; "we must thus rest content with knowing that the word Acheh is of unknown origin."

### The three sagis

Outside the limits of the actual sultanate — the immediate domain round the [[Dalam]] and [[Banda Acheh]] — Great Acheh is divided into three sagis, each composed of a certain number of mukims whence it derives its name: the XXII Mukims, the [[XXV Mukims|XXV]], and the [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]]. Each sagi is a confederacy of uleebalangships, born of the need for offensive and defensive alliance among neighbouring chiefs. The confederacies were in existence long before the sultanas — tradition attributes their formal recognition to the reign of [[Nurul Alam Nakiatodin]] ([[1675]]–[[1677]]) — succeeded in bringing the sultanate under their joint guardianship; in time, the election of a new sultan was usually decided by war between the parties, with the scale turned not so much by the support of the panglimas as by that of the real wielders of power in their sagis.

### Tunong and Baroh

The Achehnese of Great Acheh are divided by speech, manners and temper into the **[[Tunong|highlanders]]** (*ureueng tunong*) — by which must be especially understood the people of the Sagi of the [[XXII Mukims]] — and the **[[Baroh|lowlanders]]** (*ureueng baroh*) who inhabit the greater part of the two remaining sagis, including the capital. Some portions of these last two sagis have almost the same language and customs as the *ureueng tunong*, as for instance the *ureueng Bu'eng* inhabiting the [[VII Mukims Bueng]] in the Sagi of the XXVI. In the Tunong much land remained uncultivated and each gampong had an attached *padang* or uncultivated common; in the lowlands the whole country had long been reclaimed by man, and such *padangs* are rarely to be met with outside the immediate neighbourhood of the capital.

### Capital and dependencies

As regards language and manners, the lowlanders followed the people of the capital. The [[Dalam]] or residence of the Sultan, also known as [[Kuta Raja]] ("the king's fort") — a name improperly applied by Europeans to the whole capital — formed before the war the nucleus of a number of fine and prosperous gampongs. The centre of these, with its mosque and market-place, was called [[Banda Acheh]] ("the capital or trading mart of Acheh") and gave the tone to the whole country in matters of custom, dress and speech. The inhabitants of these and the neighbouring villages, with their language and customs, were distinguished by the epithet *banda* — town-bred or civilized; in contrast with these, all others who spoke in their own local dialects were called *dareun* (countrified, uncivilized). From the position of the lowlanders they came most closely into contact with the trading centre; the *ureueng dareun* and the *ureueng tunong* became practically synonymous, though families of standing in the Tunong conformed as far as possible to the manners of the capital.

### Later history

In the Dutch military campaigns the conquest of Great Acheh was the central object. All Great Acheh was conquered in the offensives of the late [[1870]]s; the establishment of widely extended military posts in Great Acheh and the introduction of a settled administration were expected, but never came, to break the opposition in the dependencies. The Ordinance of [[1881]] respecting the administration of justice among the native population of Great Acheh — drawn up by [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. Der Kinderen]] — "perished at its birth," and the Achehnese never felt its effects. With the abolition of the Dutch "linie" or military line, the whole of Great Acheh was brought under direct Dutch control, and the old relations between landlord and tenant revived.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Groot-Atjeh

**Groot-Atjeh**, also called *Atjeh-proper*, was the heartland of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] and the nucleus around which the Dutch *Gouvernement* of [[1874]] was constructed — the triangular alluvial plain watered by the [[Kr. Atjeh|Atjeh River]] and politically organised, since time immemorial, into the three federations known as the [[Sagi|Sagi]]'s of the XXII, XXVI and XXV Moekims. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats Groot-Atjeh as the model against which the *Onderhoorigheden* are measured: only here, it stresses, was the classic Atjehsche institutional triad of *gampong*, *moekim*, and *oelèëbalangschap* "ongeschonden" (intact) preserved in its full form, and from here that the [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]-an template was extended to the rest of the territory.

## Geography

The Groot-Atjeh valley is an "uitgestrekte, flauw hellende, driehoekige vlakte" — an extensive, gently sloping triangular plain — whose base of roughly 16 km runs along the Noordkust between Atjeh-hoofd (Oedjoeng Masam Moeka) and Pedropunt, and whose apex reaches inland to Indrapoeri, some 25 km to the south. Of its approximately 200 km², about a quarter along the western edge is taken up by an unfruitful sandstone *kopjes*-heuvelland, while the remainder is an open plain of more than 20,000 *bouws*, of which four-fifths is given over to sawah cultivation. The population density reached about 100 souls per km² — the highest in the *gewest*.

## The three Sagi's

The political division of Atjeh-proper into three *sagi*'s — federations of *oelèëbalangschappen* — was, the source notes, of immemorial origin: "De oorsprong … kan niet meer positief worden vastgesteld." Each sagi was named for the number of *moekims* (Friday-prayer congregations) it nominally contained: the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]], and the [[XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]], names retained even after population growth had multiplied the actual count. Each federation chose a *Panglima sagi* from among its constituent *oelèëbalangs*, who — like the celebrated Polim family of the XXII — could in time absorb the territorial authority of all his associates.

## Administrative division

By St. No. 1914, 87 the [[Afdeeling Groot-Atjeh|Afdeeling Groot-Atjeh]], under an Assistant-Resident at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]], was divided into five *onderafdeelingen*: Koeta Radja (including the hoofdplaats and the Sultan's own gampongs of Keudah, Djawa, Pasar Bard, Meuseugit Raja, Planggahan and Blang Peureulaʼ); [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] (including the harbour and the sagi of the XXVI Moekims); Lhoʼ Nga (the sagi of the XXV Moekims and the Southern settlements); Seulimeum (the sagi of the XXII Moekims and the VII Moekims Pidië); and [[Sabang|Sabang]] (the islands of Wé and Rondo). The Afdeeling counted some 96,000 inhabitants over 3,100 km² — about 30.5 per km².

**Mentawai-eilanden** — the Mentawai Islands — are an archipelago off the western coast of [[Sumatra]] (south of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate proper) that figured in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]-period colonial parliamentary debates as the standard counter-example to the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] government's allegation that piracy from [[Acheh|Atjeh]] had been a principal cause of the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|1873 declaration of war]].

## The piracy counter-example

In the [[1897]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate on credits for [[Nieuw Guinea]], [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) cited the colonial report for [[1897]] itself to the effect that on the [[Mentawai-eilanden]] prahus were still being robbed and their crews murdered — and that even [[Straat Madoera]] was unsafe after sunset. The argument was that, if such conditions elsewhere did not provoke Dutch intervention, the allegation of Atjehese piracy could not justify the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war on Atjeh]] of [[1873]]: "Mogen wij dan voor datgene wat wij zelve niet kunnen tegengaan, een onafhankelijke vorst (de [[Sultan of Acheh|sultan van Atjeh]]) van de troon stoten en hem zijn land ontnemen?" — May we, for what we ourselves cannot suppress, depose an independent prince (the sultan of Atjeh) from his throne and take his land from him?

## Significance

The [[Mentawai-eilanden|Mentawai-eilanden]] are, in the Atjehnese-war parliamentary record, the type-specimen of the colonial government's selective application of the anti-piracy justification. The comparison would recur in subsequent liberal and socialist critiques of the war's official *motivering*.

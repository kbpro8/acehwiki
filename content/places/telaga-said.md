**Telaga Said** is an oil-field locality on the north-eastern coast of [[Sumatra]], in the [[Langkat]] residency immediately to the south of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] border, and the site of the [[1885]] drilling that — under the leadership of the government mining engineer [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]] and on the [[1883]] concession of [[J. Zulkler|J. Zulkler]] — became the founding episode of the [[Oil exploration in North Sumatra|northern-Sumatran oil industry]]. The Telaga Said field was, by the [[1940]]–[[1941]] period, a small mature producer operated by the [[B.P.M.]], with production of 1,076 tons of 1000 kg in [[1940]] and 960 tons in [[1941]].

## The 1883–1885 founding drilling

For the founding episode of the Telaga Said field, see the entries on [[J. Zulkler]] and [[Ph. C. Fennema]]. In summary: in [[1883]] the private entrepreneur J. Zulkler obtained the first oil concession in North Sumatra; he encountered "considerable difficulties in drilling on account of an abundant flow of gas" and asked the Government Bureau of Mines for assistance; a government crew under the mining engineer Fennema was sent with drilling equipment; and in [[1885]] "this oil venture proved to be an outstanding success." The Telaga Said field is accordingly the oldest producing oil field in northern Sumatra, and the ancestor of the entire B.P.M.-operated North-Sumatran petroleum complex that, by [[1940]]–[[1941]], included the [[Aru Bay|Aru Bay]], [[Bulu Telang|Bulu Telang]], [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]] and [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] fields.

## Production figures (1940–1941)

For the full North-Sumatran production table see the entry on the [[East Atjeh oil block]]. The Telaga Said field's production of 1,076 tons in [[1940]] and 960 tons in [[1941]] was small by the standards of the late-colonial East Indian oil industry — less than 2 % of the Aru Bay production in the same years — but the field's historical significance as the originating site of the northern-Sumatran oil industry made it more important than its modest late-colonial output would suggest. The decline from 1,076 tons to 960 tons (–10.8 %) is consistent with the gradual depletion of a small mature field.

## Significance

Telaga Said is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the site of the [[1885]] drilling that founded the northern-Sumatran oil industry — the industry that would, by [[1940]]–[[1941]], extend onto Atjeh territory with the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields. The Telaga Said field accordingly stands at the head of the documentary sequence of northern-Sumatran petroleum exploration, and the [[J. Zulkler|Zulkler]]–[[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]] drilling of [[1883]]–[[1885]] is the originating legal-technical episode of that sequence. The field is also a B.P.M. operation, which links it directly to the B.P.M.-operated Atjeh fields and to the wider Royal Dutch petroleum economy of northern Sumatra.

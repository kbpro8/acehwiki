**Peunajong** — a Dutch colonial fort (Dutch: *benteng*) in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] — was the military post at which the Ambonese Protestant *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* (Dutch: indigenous religion teacher) [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] established a school for the children of soldiers and conducted much of his pastoral work among the colonial troops of the [[Indische Leger|Royal Netherlands East Indies Army (Dutch: *Indische Leger*)]] in the closing decades of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]]. The fort's connection to the chaplaincy of [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] places it among the principal sites of the colonial military religion in the residency (Dutch: *gewest*) of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], alongside the garrison church at [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] and the military hospital of [[Pante Perak|Panteh Perak]].

## Educational Role

[[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] organised regular schooling within the fort, ensuring that the soldiers' children — who would otherwise have had no access to formal education in the war-footing of the colonial garrison — received basic instruction in literacy, numeracy, and the Protestant religion. The school at Peunajong was one of the small but significant institutions by which the colonial state, through the chaplaincy, sought to reproduce the moral and educational foundations of its military establishment in the forward posts of the Atjehnese residency. The chaplain's school was, in effect, the institutional counterpart of the garrison church and the military hospital: a structure of care that the colonial state provided for its soldiers and their families in the remote stations of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram postenlinie]]. The presence of the school at Peunajong is recorded in the biographical literature on [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] as one of the markers of his long and devoted service to the soldiers of the Atjeh garrison — a service that, together with the parallel ministry of the Catholic *soldaten-pastoor* [[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]], defined the chaplaincy of the Atjeh campaign.

## Military Context

During the *military action* under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|General Van Heutsz]] — the intensified military operations of [[1898]]–[[1904]] that brought the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] to its close — Peunajong remained a strategic outpost of the colonial line, and [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]]'s presence there exemplified the integration of spiritual care with military operations that characterised the colonial chaplaincy of the period. The chaplain's work at Peunajong — schooling the children, visiting the sick, holding services for the garrison — was carried on in the immediate vicinity of the military operations that the fort supported, and his presence in the field was as much a part of the chaplaincy as his work in the schoolroom. The fort thus belongs, in the wiki, to the same network of forward posts as the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram postenlinie]] and the [Pante Perak](/places/pante-perak) military hospital — the network of stations through which the chaplains of the Atjeh campaign carried their ministry to the colonial troops.

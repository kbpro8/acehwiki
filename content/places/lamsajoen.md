**Lamsajoen** was an Atjehnees kampong in the [[III Moekims]] conquered on [[4 February]] [[1876]] in [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s great [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|winter offensive]] and adjacent to the site of the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe of 13 February 1876]], in which [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]]'s reinforcement detachment was massacred on the drassige *sawah* (marshy rice field) near Lamsajoen. The kampong was part of the [[XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] sector taken in the first phase of the offensive.

## The conquest of 4 February 1876

The conquest of Lamsajoen was part of the closing sweep of the [[XXV Moekims]]. From Toeram, which bordered the gebied of [[Toekoe Bait]] of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the Dutch had made an *inval* into the [[III Moekims|III Moekims]] on [[3 February]] [[1876]], driving the enemy out of [[Atoeh]] after a *heeten strijd*. The next day, [[4 February]] [[1876]], the colonne continued *noordoostelijk*: Lamrong and Lamsajoen were conquered, along with Kajoe Leh, [[Lambaroe]] and [[Pager Ajer|Pager Ajer Mesigit]], and via Tandjoeng and Bayoer communication was established with the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]]. On [[7 February]] [[1876]] the troepen returned to [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] after a 10-day *tocht*; total losses were five dead and 59 wounded, "betrekkelijk gering." With the conquest of Lamsajoen and its neighbours, the entire *Sagie* of the [[XXV Moekims]] fell into Dutch hands; the *demarcatie-linie* now ran from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast to Atoeh, then northward to Pager Ajer on the left bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]].

## The Atoeh catastrophe

Lamsajoen's place in Borel's narrative is fixed by the catastrophe of [[13 February]] [[1876]], which took place on the drassige *sawah* near Lamsajoen, almost at the destination of [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]]'s reinforcement column. [[Generaal Pel|Pel]] had granted the troepen only a couple of days' rest at K. Radja before advancing in *oostelijke richting* with three colonnes against the [[III Moekims]] on [[13 February]] [[1876]]. On the same day, kapitein van Swieten — marching from K. Radja with two officers and 57 men (10 unarmed) to reinforce the Atoeh post — was attacked on the sawah near Lamsajoen. The enemy sprang from the boschrand; the attack was "zoo snel" that most soldiers were severely wounded before they could *verzamelen*. Bijna allen sneuvelden "met wonden overdekt"; only a few escaped by flight. On hearing the news, [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|majoor B. E. Mekern]] changed route, marched on Atoeh, *zuiverde* the terrain. Bodies were "voor schending bewaard" — preserved from desecration — and "met de hun verschuldigde eer ter aarde besteld."

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — the kampong south of the Dutch line, 1876

Meijer gives Lamsajoeng fuller treatment than any other kampong of the south-east line: just cleared and partly burned by the Dutch in the days of Pel's conquest, it stands between [[Lambaroe]] and [[Kajoe-Lē]] on the open sawah across which ran the destroyed road of the first-line posts. In the nipah bush opposite it about three hundred Atjehers gathered in February [[1876]] and made it their place of assembly for the attack that on [[13 February]] destroyed Captain [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|Van Swieten]]'s detachment between it and [[Tjot Bēhēl]] on the open sawah two thousand paces south of it — the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]]. From the same kampong, with Lambaroe and Lamrong, the enemy drew his occupation that by early March had cut the road between Kajoe-Lē and [[Longbatah]] altogether, until the [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|storming of 7 March]] broke his blockade.

That narrows with the kampong Tjot Bēhēl was for the line troops the most dreaded passage in the daily fouraging between the posts, the ground of the [[Ambush of the Buijs detachment (4 March 1876)|ambush of 4 March]] in which 1 man fell and 19 were wounded; at the beginning of June the garrison of the new post at Atoë Noord was still having its daily patrols fired upon from its edges; and through it marched, finally, the 3rd column of the [[Biloel Zuid operation (13-15 May 1876)|Biloel Zuid operation]] of 14–15 May, with the kampong being burned by the 3rd column's advance on the 14th.

The **XXVI Moekims** (Zesentwintig Moekims, "Twenty-Six Mukims") was the other main *sagi* (confederation) of inner [[Groot-Atjeh]] besides the [[XXII Moekims]], with which it met at [[Longbatta]]. Borel predicts that, after the [[XXII Moekims]] has been tamed by repeated *tuchtigingen* (chastisements), the XXVI Moekims will submit without needing force. The XXVI Moekims was supplied with arms and buskruit through the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] and the [[Kwala Gigieng|Kwala Gigieng]] from Penang and Singapore throughout the war.

## The other main sagi

The XXVI Moekims lay east and south-east of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], adjacent to the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] at [[Longbatta]] and to the [[III Moekims|III Moekims]] across the [[Atjeh-rivier]]. Its fortified kampongs — among them Pinang, a frequent target of Dutch shelling — formed the eastern arc of any enemy defensive line around the Dutch perimeter. The [[Pinang|kampong Pinang]] is described by Borel as the *centre* of the XXVI Moekims, repeatedly shelled from Dutch artillery at [[Lampriet]] and [[Langkroek]]. As the other main *sagi* of inner Groot-Atjeh, the XXVI Moekims was, with the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the principal seat of the resistance; its dense population, fortified kampongs, and marshy *sawah* (irrigated rice fields) made any Dutch operation against it expensive.

## Supply and projected submission

The XXVI Moekims was supplied with arms and buskruit through the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] and the [[Kwala Gigieng|Kwala Gigieng]] from Penang and Singapore throughout the war. The Dutch closure of the Kroeng Tjoet mouth at Kwala Gigieng on [[26 February]] [[1876]] by [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|Engel]] and the establishment of the post on the island of [[Kota Pohama]] in the lagune were intended to cut this supply, and Borel notes that the Kroeng Tjoet was "finally closed by the Dutch in 1876." In the closing chapters Borel predicts that, once the XXII Moekims has been tamed by repeated *tuchtigingen*, the XXVI Moekims will submit without needing force. The argument is part of Borel's broader case for his proposed [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] of three to four thousand men raised in the Netherlands and sent directly to Atjeh to undertake the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] *tuchtigingen* that the *Indische leger*, weakened by five years of war and disease, could no longer sustain. With the XXII Moekims broken and the XXVI Moekims submitted, Borel predicts, the war could be ended.

## Nineteenth-Century Historiography & Campaign Analysis

## History During the Second Expedition

Following the [[Capture of the Aceh Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], the primary chief of the XXVI Moekims signaled via an intermediary envoy that he wished to recognize the authority of the [[Dutch East Indies Government]]. 

This communication was cited by General [[J. van Swieten]] in his dispatches of late January [[1874]] ([[General van Swieten War Telegrams]]), leading colonial authorities in [[Batavia]] to assume that the eastern sagi had laid down its arms. However, local hulubalangs soon reasserted their independence, maintaining armed resistance throughout [[1875]] and [[1876]].

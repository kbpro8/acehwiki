**Gajo Loeës** (Gayo Lues) is the great Gayo high plateau at the head of the Tripa River — the *brongebied der Tripa-rivier*, "eene wijde, langzaam naar het gebergte oploopende vlakte" and one of the principal population-centres of the interior of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* contrasts the Gajo-Loeës with the more *geaccidenteerd* high plains of the interior: "Zijn deze laatste meestal zeer geaccidenteerd en als het ware samengesteld uit tal van kleinere blangs en valleien … de Gajo-Loeës daarentegen vormt, vooral in het Oostelijk gedeelte, eene wijde, langzaam naar het gebergte oploopende vlakte met weinig belangrijke verheffingen."

## Geography

The plateau is enclosed on all sides by heavily wooded berghellingen. To the north stretches the *Gajosche Centraal-gebergte*, on which the *trotsche Intern-Intern-massief* rises above 2,000 m; to the east runs the closed, dark *Moegadjah-wand*, the continuation of the Wilhelmina-keten toward the Gajosche Centraal-gebergte, which separates the Tripa-gebied from the hoogvlakte of Serbëdjadi. A lower rug with *onbeduidende toppen* runs north-westward from this eastern randgebergte, forming the waterscheiding between the Tripa-gebied and the Alas-rivier (here still called the W. ni Agoesen), which rises on the G. Losèr. The western randgebergte of the plateau, a low golvende reeks, reaches to the Singgahmata, the *Noordelijken sluitsteen* of the boven-Tripa-gebied. The Tripa breaks through this keten after taking in the W. ni Rampoʼing, descending by a narrow rotskloof to the *onbeduidende lengtedal* of its middle course, which forms with the dal of the W. ni Kia a *schakel* in the long Western reeks of lengtedalen.

## Bestuursindeeling and the Kédjoerön Pétiambang

The Gajo Loeës, as a political territory, was the *gebied van den Kédjoer-ön Pétiambang*, who formed — together with the *Rödjö Tjèq* (Böbasan), the *Rödjö Boekét*, *Sjah Oetama*, and *Rödjö Linggö* of Takengön — the six zelfstandige landschappen of the Gajoland. The Kédjoerön Pétiambang's authority, *De Buitenbezittingen* notes, was "grooter en in vele opzichten belangrijker" than that of his colleague-kédjoeröns, yet in gezag and aanzien he stood "verre bij hen ten achter." In the years before the Dutch arrival he had lost effective control over the *rödjö*'s in his gebied, several of whom were mightier than himself; he had fought two wars with the powerful stam Boekét at the end of the nineteenth century.

## Tjèq-schappen and the 18 onderhoorigheden

The distinctive feature of Gajo Loeës was the institution of the *tjèq-schap* — a federation of *blah*'s (gens-segments) under a *tjèq*, the elder patriarchal line, with subordinate *moedö*'s of younger lines. *De Buitenbezittingen* enumerates eighteen such tjèq-schappen in the Gajo-Loeës, each with its listed onderhoorigheden: Pénampagan (with Lémbé and Spang), Golo (with Oréng, Gadjah, Péndéng with Pértéq and Léstèn), Pelang, Mbatjang, Boekét (with Panggr, Koetö Blang, Badaq), Koetö Lintang, Réma (with Tjiké Oeloe-n-Targöh, Beeder Klipah, Kelng Rékét, Vaat), Tampèng (with Brandang, Kétoekah), Pénèsan, Angasan, Dékat, Gégarang (with Koete Oedjöng), Tjané Tjoeli (Kémala Rödjö), Toengged, Lémpélam (Kémala Dérna), Rémökät (with Koetö Daloo, Lémpélam Pinang, Kéndawi), Pasér, and Padang (with Roempi, Trangën, Lémpélam Djabë). The *tjèq* of Pénampagan was simultaneously the Kédjoerön Pétiambang. The Dutch administration "hebben wij de tjèq-schappen thans, als bezoldigde bestuurs-organen, onder den Kédjoer-ön Pétiambang" placed — bringing, for the first time, a territorial organisation to the Gajo Loeës.

## The Blang Kédjérèn *bestuursstandplaats*

The administrative centre of the onderafdeeling was *Blang Kédjérèn*, the bestuursstandplaats from which footpaths radiated to Soesöh on the Westkust, to Lokö̈p on the hoogvlakte of Serbëdjadi (connected by paardenpad with Birim Rajeu on the Langsabaai), and over Pèndèng and Léstèn to Koeala Saba on the Simpang Kiri (six days' march), thence by Kaloeë to Koeala Simpang. The proposed continuation of the Alasweg from [[Kota Tjane|Kota Tjane]] to Blang Kédjérèn, "via Pèndèng en Lipkö̈p naar Birim Rajeu aan den grooten weg naar Langsa," had by [[1917]] seen its first twenty kilometres beyond Kota Tjane completed.

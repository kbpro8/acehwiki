**Pasangan** (in Van der Kemp's spelling) was a coastal place on the East Coast of [[Acheh]], situated about six miles from [[Telok Semawé]]. It is mentioned in Van der Kemp's article solely in connection with the [[Wilhelmina mutiny and murder of Bouwens|mutiny of the Dutch colonial schooner *Wilhelmina*]] in mid-[[1819]].

## The Wilhelmina affair

In June [[1819]] the colonial schooner *Wilhelmina*, commanded by [[Lieutenant Marx Bouwens|Lieutenant Bouwens]], was proceeding along [[Sumatra's East Coast]] on a mission for the government of [[Calcutta]], carrying 10,000 dollars in specie together with a quantity of arms. The crew, taking advantage of the small European complement, mutinied in July or August. Bouwens and two of his men were murdered; his wife, in her terror, leapt overboard; two of her children, whom the Javanese *baboe* tried to rescue, were thrown into the sea.

The day after the mutiny the ship reached Pasangan — "6 mijlen van Teloksemawé, de residentie destijds van pretendent Syf". The 24 mutineers, accompanied by the *baboe*, landed with as much of the money and valuables as they could carry. They began a walk along the shore towards [[Telok Semawé]], plundering some of the coastal inhabitants on the way and hiding part of the money in the ground. On arrival at Telok Semawé they were further stripped and put in irons by the followers of [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum]] (Syf), and set to work building a house for him.

## Subsequent inquiry

The mutineers' choice of Pasangan as a landing-place is explained by its proximity to Syf's seat at Telok Semawé, where they hoped for refuge. Their further plundering along the shore, however, and the discovery of part of the buried treasure by the local population, complicated Syf's position when the Dutch came to demand restitution. The Dutch inquiry eventually led to the [[Lucas mission to Telok Semawé (February 1821)|Lucas mission of February 1821]], which the [[Penang]] government under [[W. E. Phillips|Phillips]] declined to support; the *Irene* returned to [[Malacca]] without recovering the treasure or the surviving mutineers.

## Account from *Een Vaderlandsch Belang* by H. Borel (1878)

**Pasangan** was an Atjeh-dependent *kuststaatje* (vassal coastal state) outside [[Groot-Atjeh]], which had supported the enemy with *hulpbenden* (auxiliary bands), wapens and levensmiddelen and submitted to the Dutch on [[15 August]] [[1878]] in the [[Submission of Pasangan (15 August 1878)|submission of Pasangan]] after a bombardment by the Dutch marine. Borel, writing at the very moment the news arrived, treats Pasangan as the proof that the same pattern of bombardment and submission would, if applied to the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] of inner Groot-Atjeh, finally end the war.

## The vassal coastal states

The vassal coastal states — Analaboe, Pedir, Samalangan, Pasangan, Simpang Olim, Edi, and [[Gedoeng]] itself — had supported the resistance in Groot-Atjeh with auxiliary bands, weapons, and food throughout the war. Their position on the coast made them difficult to reach from the inland Dutch base at [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] but vulnerable to naval descents. [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s plan had included the reduction of these states as part of the wider isolation of Atjeh. After Pel's death in February [[1876]] the offensive against them was sporadic. By August [[1878]] the situation in Groot-Atjeh had been sufficiently restored — after the [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]] — for the Dutch to turn again to the vassal states.

## The submission of 15 August 1878

While Borel was writing his book, the telegraph brought news on [[15 August]] [[1878]] that Pasangan, after a bombardment by the Dutch marine, had submitted. Borel predicts the same pattern will work in Groot-Atjeh itself: "eerst moet de vijand getuchtigd worden en wel in die mate dat hem geen andere uitweg overblijft dan het hoofd in den schoot te leggen" — first the enemy must be chastised, and to such a degree that no other way out is left to him than to lay his head in his lap (to submit). The submission of Pasangan by naval bombardment was, in Borel's view, the proof of the *aanvallende stelsel* (offensive system): a direct, forceful application of military power had achieved in a few days what years of *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture) had not. The same principle, applied by his proposed [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] of three to four thousand men raised in the Netherlands and sent directly to Atjeh, would, he insisted, finally end the war through repeated *tuchtigingen* of the [[XXII Moekims]].

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — the warning letter of August 1876

Meijer's account of the [[Cancellation of friendship with Gigiëng (19 August 1876)|breach with Gigiëng]] includes also Pasangan: the prince of that always hostile coast state received a direct letter from the civil and military commander about the same deed, with the necessary warnings. General [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] had judged this necessary also as an answer to a letter from that prince from which, likewise, an anything but friendly attitude appeared.

'A famous burong woman' named Burong Tanjong or Srabi, whose grave is regarded as sacred and venerated in [[Acheh]], lived here. Referenced in the index.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Tanjong

**Tanjong** is a *gampong* (village) near the capital of [[Acheh]], in the mukim of [[Pagaraye]], and the site of the tomb of the dreaded **[[Burong Srabi]]**, also known as the **Burong Tanjong**. In *The Achehnese* Vol. I the gampong is principally known through this tomb and through the grim legend that attaches to it, which [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] recounts in the chapter on the spirits hostile to women in childbed.

Most dreaded of all such spirits, at the time of Snouck Hurgronje's writing, was the *burong Srabi* or *Seurabi*, whose tomb is situated in gampong Tanjong (mukim of Pagaraye) close to the capital of Acheh. She is also known as the *burong Tanjong*. The proper name of the woman who after death was changed into this monster appears to have been Rabiah, abbreviated in *Srabi* or *Si Rabiah*. The story goes that she was treacherously drowned by her lover while on a pleasure trip to which he had invited her, she being at the time with child. Her body was found near the gampong [[Kaje'e Jat'oe]] — just about where the hospital at Kuta Raja now stands — and was pulled out of the water by the people of that gampong. When they found it was the body of a woman belonging to Tanjong, they brought her thither and she was buried there. Meantime the unhappy woman had been transformed into a *burong*, which rested not until she had overtaken and slain her murderer.

The *burong* of Tanjong is one of the class of spirits that attack pregnant women and women in childbed. As long as the woman lies over the fire (*madeueng*), that is to say for the space of 44 days after childbirth, some anxiety is felt in regard to the *burong*, but most of all during the first 10 days, and especially during the first three or four. The dread is greatest when the *burong* expresses a desire for *murong*-leaves (the leaves of the plant known as *kelor*, used as a vegetable), for dried buffalo's flesh (*pasi*) and salted ducks' eggs (*boh ite jru'e*); it is then regarded as certain that it is the dreaded Burong Tanjong that has to be contended with. The same objects recur in the formula of exorcism: "the head aches; the gums ache; where shall we look, where shall we seek? Head of eel, shoots of *murong*, *si'e* of egg, the yolk of egg, the white of egg" — these last-named objects being those desired by Srabi Tanjong.

The location of the tomb in a gampong of the immediate neighbourhood of the capital is itself significant. The most dreaded spirit hostile to Achehnese women in childbed was thus a local saint whose tomb stood within easy reach of the population of [[Banda Acheh]], and whose cult was an intimate part of the religious life of the capital district. The name Tanjong (Malay *tanjung*, "a cape" or "promontory") is a common place-name in the Malay world, but in this context it is the gampong of Pagaraye that is meant, the burial-place of Rabiah the drowned, the *burong* Tanjong.

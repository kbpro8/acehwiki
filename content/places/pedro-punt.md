**Pedro-punt** (Pedropunt, also Petro-punt) was the headland east of the kampong Gigieng on the north coast of Atjeh at which the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Aceh expedition]] under [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] made its [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing on 6 December 1873]]. Borel calls the choice "uitstekend bedacht en zeer goed uitgevoerd," since the Atjehneezen had not expected a descent on that point and were found unprepared. From Pedro-punt the Dutch column marched victoriously along the strand until it reached the [[Atjeh-rivier]] and occupied [[Penajoeng]] as its base.

## The landing

The selection of Pedro-punt reflected the Dutch desire to avoid the kind of frontal descent on the [[Kwala Atjeh|mouth of the Atjeh-rivier]] that had cost [[Generaal Köhler|generaal Köhler]] so dear in the [[First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)|first expedition]] of April [[1873]]. By landing east of the river mouth, at a point where the surf was manageable and the enemy absent, van Swieten secured a beachhead from which the column could advance along the strand — the Atjehneezen suffered "zware verliezen" in the running fight — until the right bank at Penajoeng was reached. The choice was the more praised because [[Generaal Verspijck|Verspijck]] had simultaneously proposed an alternative landing of the third brigade at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai]] on the west coast for a pincer movement, which van Swieten rejected.

## Significance

Pedro-punt was the only unambiguous Dutch success of the opening weeks of the second expedition. Once the column had crossed to Penajoeng and the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] came into view, the campaign bogged down into the costly [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|storming of 6 January 1874]] and the long [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|bombardment of the Kraton]]. The kampong Gigieng, whose [[Radja van Gigieng|raja]] accepted the Dutch flag during the descent, became a minor Dutch ally; its lagoon later became a key enemy supply route through the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] and the [[Kwala Gigieng|Kwala Gigieng]], which [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|Engel]] finally closed in February [[1876]]. Pedro-punt itself was not garrisoned after the advance moved on.

## Nineteenth-Century Historiography & Campaign Analysis

## Role in the Second Expedition

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in December [[1873]] and January [[1874]], [[Pedro Punt]] served as a secondary landing site and geographical reference marker for naval squadrons of the [[Dutch East Indies Government]]. 

In *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* ([[1876]]), the author compared the March of General [[J. van Swieten]] from Pedro Punt to [[Penajoeng]] with the later offensive movements of General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]], noting that the marshy, intersected terrain behind Cape Pedro presented severe logistical obstacles for the [[Transport and Coolie System in Aceh]].

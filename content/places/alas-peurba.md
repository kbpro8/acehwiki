**Alas Peurba** was a plantation estate in [[Oostkust van Atjeh|East Atjeh]], lying at 200 metres above sea level about eighteen kilometres south of [[Lho Seumawe]], on which part of the collection of bird skins described in *[[Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh]]* by [[F. C. van Heurn]] and [[R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg]] was gathered.

Like [[Aloer Djamboe]] in [[Tamiang]], the estate stood under the management of the agency of the [[Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam]], whose grant of permission and facilities the authors acknowledged as a proof of the interest of a commercial body in purely scientific work. The collecting was done on heavy hill country at the boundary of plantation and original forest.

The authors' description of the eastern seaboard places the estate in its setting: almost the whole plain along the coast of East Atjeh had been brought into cultivation by a fairly dense population, while further inland the population had penetrated only sporadically; but from the coastal districts the European cultures had struck *eenige diepe bressen in het oerbosch* — some deep breaches into the primeval forest — of which the estates at which they collected were instances.

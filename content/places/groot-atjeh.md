**Groot-Atjeh** (Great Atjeh) is the central region of [[Atjeh]] (Aceh), comprising the north-western part of the realm through which the middle and lower courses of the [[Atjeh River]] flow. It is defined geographically by the river valley and administratively by the three [[sagi]] (bondgenootschappen) that encompassed the territory where the sultan of [[Atjeh]] exercised direct authority: the [[XXV Moekims]], the [[XXII Moekims]], and the [[XXVI Moekims]].

## Geographical and administrative structure

By [[Groot-Atjeh]] is understood the territory around [[Koeta Radja]]—the capital—where the sultan's authority was exercised directly. The region is distinct from the [[Onderhoorigheden]] (dependencies)—the much larger coastal landscapes along the east, north, and west coasts, which were considered dependent on [[Groot-Atjeh]] to a certain extent but were often treated politically as less significant.

The actual rulers of the land were the [[oelèëbalangs]] (territorial chiefs), who governed over several [[moekims]] (districts). These chiefs formed three [[sagi]], designated by the number of [[moekims]] from which they originally consisted: [[XXV Moekims]] (led by [[Sri Tjeet Abas]]), [[XXII Moekims]] (led by [[Panglima Polim]]), and [[XXVI Moekims]] (led by [[Toekoe Nja Bantah]], who shared authority with [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamreng]]).

## Political history

The political history of [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[Aceh War]] ([[1873]]–[[1903]]) is dominated by the interaction between Dutch military-administrative efforts and the resistance organised from the [[XXII Moekims]] under the successive [[Polim]] family line. Although the power of the sultan was weak both in [[Groot-Atjeh]] and in the [[Onderhoorigheden]], the authority of the [[panglima]]s—particularly [[Panglima Polim]]—was substantial. [[Polim]] made his territory a gathering place for all who wished to continue the war, and his influence radiated over all the landscapes of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

Dutch attempts to control the region—including the establishment of the [[Concentrated Line]] around [[Koeta Radja]] in [[1886]], the negotiations under [[Generaal Van Teyn]], and the alliance attempts under [[Deykerhoff]]—all failed to subdue the [[XXII Moekims]] until the active campaign led by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and guided by [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] finally broke the resistance, culminating in [[Polim]]'s submission in [[1903]].

Following the submission, the Dutch established direct administrative control through civil authorities, laying the foundation for the modern administrative structure of [[Aceh]].

**Groat-Atjeh** (Dutch: *Groot-Atjeh*; English: *Great Acheh*) was the proper territory of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|sultanate of Atjeh]], comprising the northernmost triangle of Sumatra — as distinguished from the [[Onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]] on the north, east and west coasts — and divided into the three [[sagi|sagis]] of the [[XXV Moekims|XXV]], [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] and [[XXII Moekims|XXII]] moekims. The Dutch conquest of Groot-Atjeh, opened by the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[April 1873|April 1873]] and closed by the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of [[August 1879|August 1879]], was the central episode of the first phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## Constitution

In the source work's description, the realm of Atjeh consisted of Atjeh proper or "actually Atjeh" — the northernmost triangle of Sumatra — and the Onderhoorigheden on the north, east and west coasts. The first was divided into the three sagis of the XXV, the XXVI and the XXII moekims. The authority of the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan or radja]] was then little more than nominal. The real powers were the [[Uleebalang|hoeloebalangs]] or hereditary chiefs, the most eminent being the panglimas of the three sagis, "among whom the [[Panglima Polim|panglima polim]], the head of the XXII moekims, took the first place." The power of these panglimas in their sagis was in turn limited by that of their subordinate chiefs, usually called Toekoe, such as [[Toekoe Nek|Toekoe Nek]], [[Toekoe Nanta|Toekoe Nanta]], and so on. Some of these were originally officials of the sultan, who had later become hereditary and made themselves almost wholly independent of the sultan's authority. The same happened with the officials in the immediate territory of the sultan, not belonging to the three sagis, such as the panglima missigit raja, the head of the territory of the great mosque, the toekoe kali malikon ade, originally the chief justice, and even in the so-called [[wakeuh|wakeueh (wakaf)]] lands, reserved for the sultan in various parts of his territory, where, among others, the Imam of Longbatah became a powerful chief.

Next to the secular chiefs stood the religious, the Mohammedan priests, of whom the [[Uleebalang|oelema's]] were the most eminent; then followed the heads of the moekims, the imams, and finally the priests of the kampongs, the toengkoes. These too had as a rule secured heredity and a share in the secular authority.

## The War in Groot-Atjeh

The [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[April 1873|April 1873]] failed at the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|missigit]] outside the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] with the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]]. The [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] of [[November 1873|November 1873]] – [[January 1874|January 1874]] captured the Kraton on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]], but did not subdue the country. The [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation of annexation]] of [[31 January]] [[31 January]] [[1874]] declared Groot-Atjeh to belong to the Netherlands-Indian government by right of conquest. After the departure of [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]] in [[April 1874|April 1874]], the war continued under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] (until his death on [[24 February]] [[24 February]] [[1876]]), [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] (until [[November 1876|November 1876]]), [[A. J. E. Diemont|Diemont]] (until [[June 1877|June 1877]]), and [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] (until [[1881]]). With the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign against the XXII Moekims]] of [[1879]], the [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|campaign against the XXVI Moekims]] of early July [[1879]], and the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of August [[1879]], the first conquest of Groot-Atjeh was completed.

## The Three Sagis

The three sagis of Groot-Atjeh were:

- the [[XXV Moekims|Sagi der XXV Moekims]], including the immediate territory of the sultan (the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] and [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]], [[Marassa|Marassa]], [[Longbatta|Longbattah]], the III Moekims, the IV Moekims, the VI Moekims, and the IX Moekims);
- the [[XXVI Moekims|Sagi der XXVI Moekims]], including [[Silang|Silang]], [[Tjadé|Tjadé]], [[Lampermej|Lampermej]], [[Lamgoet|Lamgoet]], [[Kwala Gighen|Kwala Gighen]], the [[missigit Toengkoep|missigit Toengkoep]], the [[missigit Melajoe|missigit Melajoe]], the [[missigit Boëng Tjala|missigit Boëng Tjala]], the [[missigit Lambaroe|missigit Lambaroe]];
- the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi der XXII Moekims]], including [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]], [[Gleiêng|Gleiêng]], [[Montassik|Montassik]], the [[missigit Anak Bate|missigit Anak Bate]], [[Senelop|Senelop]], [[Lamkrak|Lamkrak]], [[Djerreek|Djerreek]], [[Redep|Redep]], [[Panteh Karang|Panteh Karang]], [[Anak Galoeëng|Anak Galoeëng]], [[Gle Kambing|Gle Kambing]].

## The Administrative Reorganisation of 1881

By the administrative reorganisation of [[March 1881|March 1881]], the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was organised under a governor at Kota Radja, with three assistant-residents: Groot-Atjeh (with Kota Radja as chief place, in which direct Dutch administration was introduced); the [[Noord- en Oostkust|Noord- en Oostkust]] (with [[Telok Semawe|Telok Semawé]] as chief place); and the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]] (with [[Malaboeh|Malaboeh]] as chief place). To the landscapes belonging to the two last afdeelingen self-government was left. In Groot-Atjeh the inland administration was conducted by district-heads or hoeloebalangs, appointed by the Governor-General; their replacements and helpers, as well as the moekim-heads, imams, were appointed by the governor; the replacements and helpers of the imams, as well as the kampong-heads, ketjihiks, by the assistant-resident; the heads of the wards, mandarsé's, by the controleur.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Groot-Atjeh and the Tramway

**Groot-Atjeh** was also the colonial designation for the core territory in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]], appearing as the western terminus of the network: the region containing the original tramway from [[Oelèe Lheuë]] to [[Kota Radja]] (built [[1875]]), the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway]] along the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] (built [[1885]]), and the western end of the *berglijntje* (mountain line) of [[1904]]–[[1908]] that connected the old Groot-Atjeh network with the second tramway of the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] at [[Seulimeum]].

The Bureau draws an explicit contrast between "the old tramway in Groot-Atjeh" (*de oude trambaan in Groot-Atjeh*) and "the second tramway in the Dependencies." After [[1896]], the belt-tramway was "gradually cleared" (*geleidelijk opgeruimd*), and the long tramway of [[1897]] onward replaced it as the principal axis of the network. The Groot-Atjeh network was then reconnected to the Dependencies network by the [[Berglijntje Keude Breue–Seulimeum|berglijntje from Keude Breue over the hills to Seulimeum]] — built [[1904]]–[[1908]] — which gave the military administration "a powerful instrument for achieving the complete [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] of the intersected regions" of Groot-Atjeh and the Dependencies.

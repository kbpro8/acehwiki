**Kampong Java** (Dutch *Kampong Java*; "Java Village") was a *gampong* — village ward — of [[Banda Acheh]] which contained the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] and was, in [[1822]], the site of the oath by which the [[Three Sagis]] of [[Acheh]] swore never to accept [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] as sultan.

## The oath of the Three Sagis

The place appears in Van der Kemp's narrative only in connection with the [[Three Sagis' letter to Penang (4 December 1822)|letter of 4 December 1822]], by which the confederated sagis — the [[XXII Mukims]], [[XXV Mukims]] and [[XXVI Mukims]] — answered the circular of Governor [[W. E. Phillips|Phillips]] of [[Penang]] urging them to submit to Jauhar Alum. The sagis reminded Phillips that when [[Captain Coombs]] had first come alone to Acheh, they had declared [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] their sultan; when Coombs returned with [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]], they had given the same answer under their signatures. As for the old sultan: "we will never have him for our Rajah more, for we have deposed him and sworn to it on the tomb of Tunhar Anjung of Kampong Java, and we never can alter". The tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] (Tunhar Anjung) at Kampong Java thus served as the shrine where the oath of deposition was ratified.

## Significance

The invocation of a saintly tomb (*kramat*) as the place of an oath reflects the religious sanction that the Three Sagis attached to their refusal of Jauhar Alum. Teungku Anjong was a revered Achehnese religious figure; to swear at his tomb was to make the oath irrevocable in the eyes of the population. The mention of Kampong Java in the letter of [[4 December]] [[1822]] is the only reference to the village in Van der Kemp's narrative, but the oath sworn there defined the political situation at [[Banda Acheh]] until Jauhar's death in December [[1823]] and indeed beyond, as the sagis and the [[Queen-Mother of Acheh|Queen-Mother]] continued to exercise what authority remained.

The island of Nias off the West Coast of [[Sumatra]] is noted for its people, who were often enslaved and brought to [[Acheh]]: 'Nias slaves, while others are the offspring of poor Achehnese in the highlands.' The girls trained as sadati performers 'are some of them the best-looking children of Nias slaves.' Achehnese contempt for the Niasese is noted. Also, 'the Achehnese derive their knowledge of the healing properties of the peundang from Nias; it is supposed to have been discovered by the leprous princess banished thither, from whom the whole population of the island is said to be derived.' [[Menangkabau]] (Padang highlands) The [[Menangkabau]] territory of West [[Sumatra]] appears in several contexts. The character Si Pandie among the [[Menangkabau]] Malays is compared to Si Meuseukin and Pa' Pande (Achehnese Eulenspiegels). 'Massage with mercury appears to be also regarded as a specific to secure invulnerability among the Malays of the Padang highlands.' The [[Menangkabau]] matriarchal system (descent traced through women) is contrasted with Mohammedan law; 'Examples such as that of the social life of the [[Menangkabau]] Malays go to show that the creed of Islam may be dominant in a country for a long period, while yet the domestic institutions of that country are in many respects in conflict with the religious law.' Paederasty is said to be 'also endemic in [[Java]], especially in the Native States, and the same may be said of [[Menangkabau]] in [[Sumatra]].' The Straits Settlements The British colonial territory comprising [[Singapore]], [[Penang]], and [[Malacca]]. Students of Islam from [[Acheh]] 'undergo a wider course of study in the Straits Settlements or at Mekka.' The translator of the book (A.W.S. O'Sullivan) was 'Assistant Colonial Secretary, Straits Settlements.' The Hok Canton was a ship 'belonging to Chinese traders in [[Penang]], trading to [[Acheh]] under Dutch colours.' The index's description of kuali in [[Java]] includes [[Johor]] in the sense: 'juara signifies a procuress' in Riau and [[Johor]]. Notes on Malay magic by Sir William Maxwell were published in the Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society for December [[1884]] ([[Singapore]] [[1885]]). In [[Malacca]], the game of pitch and toss (main koba) is played.

**Nieuwe Courant** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

The appointment was contested. [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]] published in the *[[Nieuwe Courant]]* of [[16 June|16]]–[[19 June]] [[1908]] a hostile judgement: asked whether the new governor's appointment offered any guarantee of better policy, "We must answer this question decidedly in the negative." As a troop commander Swart was valued by the most competent, but by no means placed on a level with Van Daalen; in knowledge of Atjeh and the Acehnese he could not stand in Van Daalen's shadow. Snouck concluded that Swart was "by no means the man to give Atjeh … the combination of means of pacification derived from sound principles of government with the unavoidably hard measures that must break the resistance. It may well be that he would be in favour of it; behind it he is not, and behind it he will not come."

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

The [[Nieuwe Courant]] chronicler of Atjeh affairs — reprinted in the *[[Sumatra Post]]* of [[25 November]] [[1912]] — read the promotion to lieutenant-general as "the confirmation of a policy which from its beginning meant a true revolution, a reversal of ideas concerning our administrative task in the north of Sumatra."

### From [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]]

By [[1908]] the relation between van Heutsz and the scholar had sharpened into a very unpleasant one, partly as a result of what du Croo called "utterances and publications that gave offence not without reason" of Snouck's. In the *[[Nieuwe Courant]]* of [[16 June|16]]–[[19 June]] [[1908]] Snouck wrote of the newly appointed Governor Swart: "Does the appointment of the newly named Governor Swart offer any guarantee of a better policy? We must answer this question decidedly in the negative. As a troop commander Swart is indeed valued by the most competent judges, but he is by no means placed on a level with Van Daalen. As regards knowledge of Atjeh and the Acehnese, he cannot stand in Van Daalen's shadow. In character there is perhaps as much difference between Swart and Van Daalen as between Van Daalen and Van Heutsz, but this difference does not benefit the provision of what Atjeh needs above all else…"

**De proeve verdedigd en toegelicht** (*De proeve van een onderzoek der middelen tot oplossing van het Atjeh-vraagstuk, verdedigd en toegelicht*, published in [[1887]] by Martinus Nijhoff in The Hague) was a major defensive polemical work authored by former [[Council of the Netherlands Indies|Council of the Netherlands Indies]] member [[S. C. H. Nederburgh|Mr. S. C. H. Nederburgh]] in response to fierce criticisms of his [[1886]] proposal for Dutch military withdrawal and administrative reorganization in [[Acheh]].

## Background and Context

In [[1886]], [[S. C. H. Nederburgh|Nederburgh]] published his original study (*De proeve van een onderzoek der middelen tot oplossing van het Atjeh-vraagstuk*), which argued that the Netherlands must abandon its interior military positions in [[Groot-Atjeh]], contract its forces to a single fortified coastal point, and restore a restricted native sultanate. This provoked a storm of controversy across the [[Dutch East Indies]] and [[Netherlands|The Netherlands]]. Among his sharpest critics was retired military officer and historian [[E. B. Kielstra|W. A. Kielstra]], who published a stinging critique titled *"De eer of de schande?"* ("Honor or Disgrace?") in the December [[1886]] issue of the journal *De Tijdspiegel*. 

Determined to vindicate his arguments and systematically dismantle [[E. B. Kielstra|Kielstra]]'s attacks on his competence, knowledge of history, and good faith, Nederburgh penned *De proeve verdedigd en toegelicht* in early [[1887]].

## Contents and Arguments

The publication is structured as a point-by-point rebuttal of [[E. B. Kielstra|Kielstra]]'s critique and a detailed elaboration of Nederburgh's strategic plan. Key elements include:

- **The Six-Point Plan**: A reiteration and refinement of Nederburgh's solution for the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]], calling for military reinforcement in the Indies, reduction of [[Groot-Atjeh]] to a coastal enclave, restoration of a limited [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese Sultanate]] over [[Groot-Atjeh]] alone (excluding dependencies and islands), Dutch overarching sovereignty (*opperheerschappij*), financial pensions (*jaargelden*) for the Sultan and state nobles (*Rijksgrooten*), and naval pressure (*zeemacht*) where peaceful measures failed.
- **Historical Analogy and Precedent**: Nederburgh vigorously defended his historical methodology by examining earlier Dutch military campaigns in the archipelago, including the [[Palembang Expedition of 1819 and 1821|Palembang expeditions of 1819 and 1821]], the abolition of the Palembang sultanate in [[1824]], the [[Padri War|Padri War]] in the [[Padang Highlands|Padang Highlands]] ([[1821]]–[[1837]]), and the [[Djambi Expedition of 1858|Djambi expedition of 1858]]. He demonstrated that colonial expansion had consistently been forced upon the government by irresistible necessity (*nooddwang*) rather than wanton imperialism.
- **Logistics of the Kraton**: Nederburgh clarified his previous observations regarding the location of the [[Kraton]] and [[Kotta Radja]], explaining that the inland position of the royal citadel necessitated a sprawling, vulnerable chain of outposts—such as [[Ketapang Doea]], [[Bital]], and positions along the [[Kwalla Tjangkoel]]—which subjected Dutch forces to relentless harassment from resistant leaders like [[Toekoe Nanta]].

## Significance

*De proeve verdedigd en toegelicht* stands as an important primary document of nineteenth-century Dutch colonial historiography. It encapsulates the intense domestic debate between "withdrawal realists" (*kleine vesting* advocates) and "pacification expansionists" during the most grueling phase of the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]].

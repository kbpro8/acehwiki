**The Nota over de betrekkingen van Nederland tot het Rijk van Atjeh sedert [[1824]]** (*Note on the relations of the Netherlands with the Kingdom of Atjeh since [[1824]]*) was the official Dutch government memorandum on Netherlands\u2013Acehnese relations, printed as document no. 141 of the [[Tweede Kamer]] session of [[1872]]\u2013[[1873]] and afterwards appended to the collection of official papers on the origin of the war with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] published by the government at the [[Landsdrukkerij]] in [[1881]]. It was the document to which the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|J. T. Cremer]] appealed in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November]] [[1897]] as the authoritative refutation of the socialist thesis that Atjeh had been innocent of provocation.

## Content and use in the [[1897]] debate

[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] told the chamber that the *Nota* would show to those who took an interest in the matter — naming pointedly "de afgevaardigden uit Enschedé en Tietjerksteradeel," that is [[H. van Kol]] and [[Pieter Jelles Troelstra|Troelstra]] — that "Atjeh lang niet zoo onschuldig was, als zij het willen doen voorkomen" (Atjeh was by no means as innocent as they wish to make it appear).

The argumentative core of Cremer's use of the *Nota* was the declaration annexed to the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|Treaty of London of 1824]], which he read into the record in the original French: *"que cet état (d'Atchin) sans rien perdre de son indépendance offre au navigateur et au commerçant cette constante sécurité que semble ne pouvoir y être établie que par l'exercice modéré d'une influence européenne"* — that this state, without losing anything of its independence, should offer to the navigator and the merchant that constant security which appeared capable of being established there only by the moderate exercise of a European influence. By that clause, Cremer argued, the Netherlands had assumed "de vrij onmogelijk na te komen verplichting" (the practically impossible obligation) to protect the trade and navigation of the European nations against land- and sea-robbery on the part of Atjeh without touching Acehnese sovereignty; and Atjeh, he said, had used that restriction to indulge its hostile disposition towards the Dutch for years together.

Cremer coupled the *Nota* with two further bodies of evidence: the published papers of the *[[Comité-generaal van 1874|comité-generaal]]* of [[1874]], whose secrecy had since been lifted, and in particular page 46 of its report, where the British government was recorded as having complained repeatedly that its traders "had been interfered with, and their ships and property plundered by people in the neighbourhood of Atcheen"; and the statement of the Minister of Foreign Affairs [[Gericke van Herwijnen]] in the *comité-generaal* that he had "de bewijzen hier voor mij liggen" (the proofs lying here before me) of Acehnese attempts to place themselves under the suzerainty of [[Ottoman Empire|Turkey]], of [[United States of America|America]] and of [[France]], adding that the French envoy had made communications to him on the subject.

## Contested authority

[[H. van Kol|Van Kol]] in his reply of [[17 November]] [[1897]] rejected the *Nota*'s implied case. He held that the official papers on "de aanleiding tot den Atjeh-oorlog," carefully kept secret for eight years, were known to him also, and that the pretext of foreign interference had been "een verfoeilijk kluchtspel" (a detestable farce). He recalled [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s words in the *Handelingen* of [[1874]]\u2013[[1875]], p. 1305, that a "zot" (fool) and a "monomaan" (monomaniac) had, by "de inblazingen van een leugenaar" (the promptings of a liar), made the [[Onderkoning|viceroy]] the dupe of a false alarm. Van Kol further observed that the *[[Instructie aan den Regeeringscommissaris naar Atjeh (1873)|instructie]]* given to the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] sent to Atjeh to communicate Dutch grievances to the [[Sultan of Atjeh|sultan]] contained no mention of piracy at all, but only of the fear of the interference of foreign powers.

**Het familie- en kampongleven op Groot-Atjeh** — *Family and Kampong Life in Groot-Atjeh* — is an ethnographic monograph by [[Dr. Julius Jacobs|Dr. Julius Jacobs]] published in [[1894]], treating the domestic, social and religious life of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in the last decades before the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## Use by Broersma
Broersma cites the book twice in *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]*. First, for the condition of lepers in Groot-Atjeh, where Jacobs had recorded that *'de lijders in Groot-Atjeh onverbiddelijk werden verstooten'* — the sufferers in Groot-Atjeh were inexorably banished — and that they might withdraw to a particular kampong with sulphurous hot springs. The leper's property fell to his family, and his wife was reckoned legally divorced from him. The leper-kampong thus formed *'een kleine maatschappij van rampzaligen'* — a small society of the wretched — in which, so far as the sufferers were able, fields, crafts and fishery were kept up. Jacobs noted that leprosy was then less frequent in [[Groot-Atjeh]] than on the eastern coast, where [[Pidië]] and the [[Pasé]]-streek were worse afflicted.

Broersma's second use of Jacobs is for the pepper-harvest of [[1893]], when *'geen peper van beteekenis werd geoogst'* — no pepper of consequence was harvested — an item in the long wave of boom-and-bust that Broersma traces from Kruyt's [[1873]] figure down to the disease-year of [[1918]].

## Significance
Jacobs's book is one of the principal pre-war sources for the social institutions of Groot-Atjeh: the *gampong* and *mukim* organisation, the household economy, and the legal condition of women, children, lepers and slaves. It is regularly cited alongside [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *Atjèh* for the ethnography of the period before the Dutch military consolidation.

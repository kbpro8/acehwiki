**The Zelfbestuursverordening of [[1 January]] [[1921]] (Serbeudjadi Rafflesia)** — printed in the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* as Regulation No. 21 — is the *natuurmonument*-verordening (natural-monument ordinance) of the *landschap* (territory) [[Serbeudjadi]] in the [[Gayo- en Alaslanden|afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden]] (district of Gajo- en Alaslanden), issued by the *Zelfbestuurders* (self-governors) *Kedjoeroen Abog* and *Banta Ali Aman Lage*, designating thirteen named Rafflesia-bearing *terreingedeelten* (terrain-portions) of the *landschap* — wholly surrounded by *oer-woud* (primeval forest) — as *natuurmonumenten* (natural monuments) under the Netherlands-Indies *ordonnantie* (ordinance) van [[18 March]] [[1916]] Staatsblad van Nederlandsch-Indië No. 278. It is the botanically most distinctive, and the most topographically detailed, regulation in the compilation.

## Issuers and the authorising Gouvernementsbesluit

The regulation is issued by the two named *oeleebalang* (territorial chiefs) of the *landschap* Serbeudjadi — the Malay column reading "*Kami Oelesbalang dari landschap Serbendjadi, Kedjoeroen Aboq, Banta Ali Aman Lage, dari onderafdeeling Serbeudjadi, afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden*" and the Dutch column reading "*Kedjoeroen Aboq, Banta Ali Aman Lage, der onderafdeeling Serbeudjadi van de afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh*." The Dutch side's attribution of Serbeudjadi to the *afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh* (East Coast district of Atjeh) is a bookkeeping slip — Serbeudjadi is in fact in the *afdeeling* [[Gayo- en Alaslanden|Gajo- en Alaslanden]], as the Malay column correctly records. The *Kedjoeroen* (or *Keudjroeen*) is a chiefly title of the Gajo/Alas lands.

The recital records the colonial governor's authorising act:

> "Gelezen het Gouvernements-be- luit van 9 October 1920 No. 46 nd III. Membatja besluit Gouvernement tg. 9 October 1920 No. 46 ad III."

— "Having read the *Gouvernementsbesluit* (Government decree) of [[9 October]] [[1920]] No. 46 ad III." The *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[9 October]] [[1920]] No. 46 ad III is the immediate colonial authorisation for the *natuurmonument*-designation; without it the *Zelfbestuurders* of Serbeudjadi would have had no power to bind future *Zelfbesturen* of the *landschap* against the alienation of the designated *terreinen* (terrains).

## The three designated sites

The conservation motive is stated explicitly in the recital: "*Ditimbang dan merasa perloe, dimana tempat dari boenga Raffle- sia didalam landsehap jang tinggal terpolihara. Overwegende dat het wenschelijk is de vindplantsen van de Rafflesin- bloem in het landsehap in stand te houden*" — "Whereas it is desirable to maintain in being the localities of the Rafflesia-bloom in the *landschap*." The substantive designation follows in *Fatsal 1 / Artikel 1* (Article 1), made "*op den voet der bepalingen van de ordonnantie van 18 Maart 1916 Staatsblad van Nederlandsch-Indic No. 278*" — "on the footing of the provisions of the *ordonnantie* of [[18 March]] [[1916]] Staatsblad van Nederlandsch-Indië No. 278." The designated *terreingedeelten* are described by reference to a stream (*Aloer*), a *kampong* (village), a *bivak* (bivouac), or a kilometric marker on a *paardenpad* (horse-track). The first three — and the principal — sites are:

- **Aloer Doeren Tarotoeng** — "*dipinggir kali Aloer Doeren Ta- roetoeng = 1½ K.M. sebelah Barat Daja dari kampong Ta- roedjak. aan den oever van de Aloer Doeren Taroctoong op ongeveer 1½ K.M. ten Z.W. van de kam- pong Taroedjak*" — "on the bank of the stream Aloer Doeren Taroetoeng, approximately 1½ km west-southwest of the *kampong* Taroedjak."
- **Aloer Kloewat** — at "*Aloer Kloewat (10 minuten N. of kampong Nalon)*" — approximately ten minutes' walk north of the *kampong* Nalon.
- **Aloer Itam** — at "*Aloer Itam (3 minuten upstream of bivak Aloer Itam)*" — three minutes' walk upstream of the colonial transport-*bivak* of Aloer Itam.

The remaining sites — Aloer Soengsang (about 50 m north of km 36 of the Aloer Itam–Ranto Pandjang *paardenpad*); the Ranto Pandjang–Plawi *paardenpad* between km 40 and 41; Djamboer Manja (on the old transport road Loekoep–Ranto Pandjang); twenty minutes north-east of *kampong* Boegag; twenty minutes south of *kampong* Leles (50 m west of km 3 of the Loekoep–Pendeng path); *kampong* Pante Kra on the west bank of the Djerneh river; 30 m south of km 41 of the Ranto Pandjang–Plawi path; the left bank of the Djerneh near Poelo Moento; Aloer Serpong (about 7 km south of *kampong* Leles); and Aloer Koembar (on the Aloer Itam–Ranto Pandjang path) — together make up thirteen designated *terreingedeelten* (labelled a–n with one duplicated), each described in equally precise topographic detail.

## References to the ordonnantie of 18 March 1916

The legal basis of the designation is the Netherlands-Indies *ordonnantie van [[18 March]] [[1916]] Staatsblad van Nederlandsch-Indië No. 278* — the colonial statute authorising the designation of *terreinen* as *natuurmonumenten*. The Malay column renders the citation as "*ordonnantie pada tanggal 18 Maart 1916 Stbl. van Hindia Belanda No. 278*" and the Dutch column as "*ordonnantie van 18 Maart 1916 Staatsblad van Nederlandsch-Indië No. 278*." Reg. No. 21 is the only regulation in the compilation — and possibly the only *zelfbestuursverordening* of the entire [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement]] — that invokes this *ordonnantie*. The designation is thus doubly authorised: by the *Gouvernementsbesluit van [[9 October]] [[1920]] No. 46 ad III* of the Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (the immediate authorisation), and by the Netherlands-Indies *ordonnantie* of [[18 March]] [[1916]] (the metropolitan enabling statute).

## Significance

The *Zelfbestuursverordening van [[1 January]] [[1921]]* is the botanically most distinctive regulation in the compilation. It is the only *natuurmonument*-designation in the [[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Zelfbestuursverordeningen compilation]], and the only regulation that names a genus of plant — Rafflesia, the parasitic flowering plant native to Southeast Asia, with the world's largest individual flower — as the explicit object of conservation. The Rafflesia had been discovered in the early nineteenth century in the Sumatran interior; the thirteen designated sites of Serbeudjadi form part of the Gajo/Alas highland Rafflesia habitat. The regulation is also the most topographically detailed in the compilation: every designated *terreingedeelte* is fixed by reference to a stream, *kampong*, *bivak*, or kilometric marker on a *paardenpad*. The regulation's effective date — [[1 January]] [[1921]] — coincides exactly with the *inwerkingtreding* (entry into force) of the *inkomstenbelasting* (income-tax) regeling of Peureula' (Reg. No. 9a) and its eighteen parallel regelingen across the *Gouvernement*, making the first day of [[1921]] one of the more consequential dates in the colonial administrative calendar of Aceh.

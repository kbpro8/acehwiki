**H. Djajadiningrat** — Raden Hoesein Djajadiningrat — was an Indonesian scholar of Acehnese and Malay history, whose article in *Tijdschrift van het Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen* (*TBG*) 69, pp. 97 ff., established the chronology of the Malay works of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and supplied the principal chronological pegs of Raniri's Achehnese career. His study is cited by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises — candidly confessed by Voorhoeve to have been overlooked by him in his earlier notes in *BKI* 107 (p. 353 ff.).

## The chronology of Raniri's Malay works

Djajadiningrat's article in *TBG* 69, pp. 97 ff. — and especially pp. 109–111 — is the principal authority for the chronology of Raniri's Malay works. He establishes:

- The year of Raniri's arrival in [[Acheh]]: [[1637]].
- The presence of Raniri, in [[1641]], together with a number of high functionaries of the Achehnese court, at the ceremony of erecting the tombstone for Sultan [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar II]].
- The dates of Raniri's Malay works, so far as reliably attested, between [[1634]] and [[1644]].
- The return of Raniri to [[Ranir]] in [[1644]].
- The terminus ante quem of Raniri's death: before [[1666]].

Djajadiningrat also corrected the earlier assumption of Voorhoeve (*BKI* 107, p. 357) that the [[Sirat al-mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] had been composed before Raniri's arrival in Acheh. The work was begun in [[1634]], as Voorhoeve had supposed, but was — as Djajadiningrat showed — only completed ten years later, in [[1644]]. The knowledge of Malay letters displayed in the [[Sirat al-mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] need not, therefore, have been acquired before [[1637]].

## Significance

Djajadiningrat is the principal authority for the chronology of Raniri's Achehnese career. His article in *TBG* 69 supplies the chronological pegs on which the modern understanding of Raniri's life and writings rests, and corrects the earlier assumptions of Voorhoeve on the composition of the [[Sirat al-mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]]. The fact that an Indonesian scholar — and one of Acehnese descent — should have established the chronology of the principal Malay author of seventeenth-century Acheh is itself a significant moment in the modern study of Acehnese letters.

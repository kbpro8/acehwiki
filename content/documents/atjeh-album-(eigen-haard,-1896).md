**The Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)** — published by the N.V. *Het Tijdschrift "Eigen Haard"* of [[Amsterdam]] in [[1896]] — is a bound compilation of the illustrated articles that the weekly periodical *Eigen Haard* devoted to the events of [[Atjeh]] between [[April]] and [[June]] of that year. The compilation was edited under the direction of the periodical's directie en redactie, with the principal historical narrative written by the oud-luitenant-kolonel of the Nederlandsch-Indisch leger, [[G. B. Hooyer]], and with illustrations supplied by unnamed correspondents in both the [[Netherlands]] and [[Nederlandsch-Indië]]. As a contemporary account of [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] and the immediate military crisis it provoked — the sieges of [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] — the Atjeh-Album is both a primary source for the spring [[1896]] operations and a self-conscious retrospective on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] written for a Dutch popular readership.

## A word beforehand

The album opens with a *Een Woord Vooraf* in which the editors explain that the compilation was prompted by the apparent interest of *Eigen Haard*'s readers in the spring [[1896]] events. The editors credit [[G. B. Hooyer|Hooyer]] — identified as an oud-luitenant-kolone van het N.-I. leger — for the principal narrative, and they acknowledge unnamed contributors in both the Netherlands and the Indies who supplied illustrations and copy. The preface expresses the hope that, should a second Atjeh-Album appear later in the year, it might speak "of an honourable and satisfactory solution of the question that for more than five-and-twenty years has kept every Dutch heart, in the fatherland as well as in the Indies, in sad suspense" — a direct reference to the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] that had begun in [[1873]].

## Structure of the compilation

The album is organised as a series of illustrated essays rather than as a continuous chronicle. The principal sections, in order, are:

- *Nederland en Atjeh. Een Terugblik. Personen en Stelsels. Toekoe Oemar. — Zijn Verraad* — a retrospective on Dutch policy in Atjeh from [[1873]] to [[1896]] framed around the personalities of successive governors and the shifting *stelsels* (systems) they pursued, culminating in the betrayal by [[Toekoe Oemar]].
- *De Poeassa. Atjehers en Kafirs. Anagaloeng. Hoop en Vrees. Eindelijk Ontzet. Wat Reeds Verkregen Werd* — an account of the [[Ramadan|poessa]] (fasting month) and its consequences for Dutch-Acehnese relations, followed by a narrative of the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] and the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|April relief]] of the besieged outposts.
- *Een Terugblik. Wat Atjeh Was en Hoe Wij Er Kwamen. Veldheeren en Staatslieden* — a historical sketch of the [[Sultanate of Atjeh|sultanate]] before [[1873]] and of the First and Second Aceh Expeditions, with portraits of the principal Dutch commanders (Köhler, Van Swieten, Van der Heyden, Pel, Wichers van Kerchem, Diemont, Gey van Pittus, Verspyck).
- *De Inwendige Toestand. Kolonisten. Banda Atjeh. De Sultan. Imams en Oeloebalangs* — an analysis of the internal constitution of pre-conquest Atjeh: the colonisation of the coasts from the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]], the position of the [[Sultan of Atjeh|Sultan]] in [[Banda Atjeh]], and the structure of the [[Mukim|mukim]]-*imam*-*[[Uleebalang|oeloebalang]]* hierarchy that produced the three [[Sagi|Sagies]] (the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] Moekims).
- *De Geestelijkheid in Atjeh. Habib Abdoe'r Rachman. Teungkoe di Tiro. Wat Nu?* — an extended biographical essay on [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] and [[Teungku di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] as the two principal religious-political figures of the war, followed by an analysis of the policy options facing the Dutch in [[1896]].
- *Het Vertrek der Troepen naar Atjeh* — an impressionistic sketch of the embarkation of reinforcements from the garrison towns of [[Batavia]], [[Semarang]] and [[Soerabaja]].
- *De Strijd om Kaloet (Eene herinnering uit den Atjeh-oorlog)* — a retrospective narrative of the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|fighting around the Acehnese fortification of Kaloet]] in [[Augustus]]–[[October]] [[1892]] and of [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s [[Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)|bloodless capture of Kaloet]] in [[August]] [[1893]].
- *Gevallenen, hun Roeping Vervuld!* — a roll of the officers killed in the spring [[1896]] operations, with portrait plates: [[K. H. Westendorp|Lieutenant Westendorp]], [[J. W. Vuijk|Lieutenant Vuijk]], [[E. P. A. Geluk|Captain Geluk]], [[H. L. Maarschalk|Lieutenant Maarschalk]], [[J. B. Zon|Lieutenant Zon]], [[H. O. W. L. Kramer|Captain Kramer]] and [[C. G. A. C. Krohne|Lieutenant Krohne]].
- *Een Trouwe Inlander* — a tribute to [[Toekoe Ketjik Oemar]], the Acehnese auxiliary who warned the Dutch of [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]]'s planned attack.
- *Onze Stelling op Groot-Atjeh* — a fold-out map (scale 1:80 000) of the Dutch lines around [[Kota Radja]] in [[1896]].

## Hooyer's authorship

The narrative voice of the album is consistently that of [[G. B. Hooyer|Hooyer]] — a retired lieutenant-colonel of the N.-I. army who, in the editors' phrase, brought to the articles "his rich experience of Indian affairs and his talent as a writer." Hooyer's perspective is that of a military intellectual of the late-nineteenth-century colonial army: he is critical of the *concentratie-stelsel* of [[1884]]–[[1896]], sceptical of civil administration, and openly admiring of the *kloek, doortastend* (bold, decisive) generalship of [[K. van der Heyden|Karel van der Heyden]]. He is also notable for treating the Acehnese adversary with measured respect — the album's biographical portrait of [[Toekoe Oemar]] acknowledges his *beschaafde manieren* (civilised manners) and *gaven … als aanvoerder* (gifts as a commander), even as it condemns his *trouweloosheid* (faithlessness).

## Illustrations

The Atjeh-Album was issued as a lavishly illustrated volume — the principal selling-point of *Eigen Haard* as a popular periodical — and many of its images are reproduced from photographs taken by officers serving in Atjeh. Photographs attributed in the captions include *Coll. K. I.* (the Koloniaal Instituut collection), *Coll. Kol. Inst.* (idem), *Coll. Kol. Inal.* (the Koloniaal Instituut voor Nederlandsch-Indië), and *Majoor van der Noot* (a photographic donation by that officer). Subjects include: views of the [[Pinus Merkusii]] forests of Atjeh (in the supplementary *Atjeh-Terpentijn* material); the benting of [[Anagaloeng]]; the interior and exterior of the old and new terpentijn factories at [[Baleq]]; portraits of the principal Dutch commanders; the [[Kraton of Aceh|Zuiderface of the Kraton]]; an Acehnese *handels- of rooverspraauw* lying in the *inham* behind [[Oleh-leh]]; the [benting Kaloet|Kaloet]] from inside and outside; and a map of the Dutch *stelling* on Groot-Atjeh at scale 1:80 000.

## The supplemental Atjeh-Terpentijn pamphlet

Bound with some copies of the album — and produced by the same Amsterdam publisher in the same general period — is an extract from *De Indische Mercuur* of [[19 November]] [[1930]] by Ir. [[W. Spoon]] on the *tegenwoordige kwaliteit en nieuwe beoordeelingen* of [[Atjeh-terpentijn|Atjeh-turpentine]]. The pamphlet is *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum van de Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut* No. 57, and it is described in a separate article, [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)]].

## Significance

The Atjeh-Album is significant in the historiography of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as one of the earliest illustrated popular accounts of the [[1896]] crisis — predating by half a century the more analytical parliamentary history of [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|*Schets* of Rengers-Vermeulen]]. Its narrative of the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|April relief]] and of the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]] is the principal source for several of the operational details preserved in this wiki, and its biographical essays on [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] and [[Teungku di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] — although derivative of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — record a popular-Dutch reading of those figures that is itself a historical document. The album's editorial stance — patriotic but not uncritical, admiring of Acehnese martial qualities but convinced of the justice of the Dutch cause — is the characteristic register of late-nineteenth-century Dutch colonial journalism on Atjeh.

**Het Atjeh-probleem van 1890-1900. De stimulans van Van Heutsz en Snouck Hurgronje** is a historical-military study by [[Dr. J. M. Somer]] (*Captain of Infantry*), published in [[1935]] as an overprint from the *Journal / Organ van de Netherlands-Indies Officers Association* (N.I.O.V.). The essay analyses the strategic confusion of the [[Aceh War]] between [[1873]] and [[1890]] and argues that the absence of a consistent strategic line—not military weakness—was the primary cause of the prolonged stalemate.

## Content and argument

The essay takes the appointment of [[Generaal Van Teyn]] and the subsequent rise of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] as its central analytical thread. [[Somer]] notes that in the first sixteen years of the war, thirteen [[Ministers of Colonies]], five [[Gouverneurs-Generaal]], and nine civil and military governors of [[Atjeh]] raised conflicting opinions on the course to follow. This rapid turnover produced a "stelsel der stelselloosheid"—a system of systemlessness—in which blockades, shipping regulations, the [[Plan-Pel]], reconciliation policies, civil administration, attempts to restore the sultanate, and the [[Concentrated Line]] followed one another without coherent purpose.

The essay argues that the backgrounding of the sultanate restoration—first hinted at during [[Van Teyn]]'s command—became the definitive policy after [[1898]]. The collaboration between [[Van Heutsz]] and [[Snouck Hurgronje]]—formalised through three government decisions in [[March 1898]]—transformed the strategic direction from passive containment to active subjugation. Their partnership produced the [[Pidië Expedition]] (starting [[1 June]] [[1898]]), the capture of [[Keumala]] ([[14 June]] [[1898]]), and ultimately the pacification of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Sources cited

The essay relies on the official *Nota's Politiek Beleid* (compiled by "Colijn" in [[1907]]), the work of [[H. T. Damste]] (*Atjeh-historie*, [[1916]]), [[Hotz]]'s *Beknopt geschiedkundig overzicht van den Atjeh-oorlog*, and private information from [[Prof. Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]]. It also references [[Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] brochure *De Onderwerping van Atjeh* and [[Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[1892]] report on the religious-political situation in [[Atjeh]].

## Significance

As a primary-source military analysis written by an active officer, the study consolidates the strategic debates of the final phase of the [[Aceh War]] and records from an insider's perspective the transition from failed alliance policies to the successful active subjugation executed by [[Van Heutsz]] and guided by [[Snouck Hurgronje]].

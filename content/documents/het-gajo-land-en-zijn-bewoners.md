**Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners** — *The Gajo Country and Its Inhabitants* — is the principal ethnographic monograph on the [[Gajo-land|Gajo country]] of northern Sumatra, compiled by [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]] from communications of educated Gajos and of *efficiënte bestuursambtenaren* (efficient administrative officers) and published in [[1903]]. It is the companion-piece to Snouck Hurgronje's great work on Atjeh itself, and Broersma in *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* calls it, with the Atjeh work, *'het richtsnoer is voor bestuurders en onderzoekers'* — the compass for both administrators and researchers in the interior of northern Sumatra.

## Contents
The book describes the geography of the [[Gajo-land|Gajo country]] with its central feature [[Laoet Tawar|Laut Tawar]], the social organisation of the Gajo *republieken* under their elected *rëdjö* (village-heads), the *saudörö* (clan) system, the Gajo house and its round family-stove, adat prohibitions on marriage within the clan, and the position of the inland population under the historical overlordship of the [[Aceh|Atjehnese]] sultans. The dictionary of [[Dr. Hazeu|Dr. Hazeu]] is mentioned by Broersma as an indispensable supplement: *'dat heel wat méér is dan een woordenboek'* — *that is a great deal more than a dictionary.*

## Use in Later Literature
Broersma relies on the book for his account of the Gajo kampong at [[Böbasan]], for the *rëdjö-saudörö* constitution of Gajo society, for the Gajo way of rice-cultivation on the *tegal* and the *ladang*, and for the round-stove house-plan he describes at [[Takengon]]. The book is also the source of the historical judgement that the Gajos and Alassers, once underhoorig to the Atjehnese sultans, were drawn into the hostilities against the Dutch government whenever the Atjehnese were in trouble — a dynamic which led to [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s expeditions of [[1901]] and [[1904]] and to the eventual formation of the *Afdeeling Gajo- en Alaslanden* in [[1905]].

## Significance
*Het Gajo-land* and its companion *Atjèh* are the foundational texts of Dutch colonial ethnography of northern Sumatra. Almost every later administrative and economic survey of the region, including Broersma's *Verkenningen*, depends on them either directly or through the colonial officials they trained.

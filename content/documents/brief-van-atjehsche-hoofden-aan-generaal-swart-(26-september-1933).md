The **letter of the Acehnese chiefs to General [[H. N. A. Swart]]**, dated [[Atjeh]], [[26 September]] [[1933]], was the address of congratulation sent by thirty-two chiefs and dignitaries of the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|north coast]] on the occasion of Swart's seventieth birthday, [[12 October]] [[1933]], and printed by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] as an appendix to *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|Generaal Swart]]* in the original Malay with a Dutch translation.

The letter was drawn up by [[T. Maharadja Mangkoeboemi]], *zelfbestuurder* of the *[[landschap]]* [[Lho' Seumawè]], and [[T. Tjhi' Moehammad Djohan Alamsjah]], *zelfbestuurder* of [[Peusangan]] — chiefs of the very region "where the resistance had raged most fiercely." Du Croo added: "it speaks for itself."

## Text

The letter was addressed "Menghadap Jang moelia seripadoeka *toean* ("master, sir") Besar Generaal H. N. A. Swart, oud Gouverneur van Atjeh di [[Den Haag]]" and opened with the congratulations of the undersigned on the completion of Swart's seventieth year on [[12 October]] [[1933]].

Because Swart was far from them, they could not come in person to offer their congratulations, and this letter had to take the place of their appearing before him to show their respect. They further hoped that God would prolong his years by decades more.

The core of the letter ran, in the Dutch translation printed by du Croo:

"We have none of us forgotten, but are ever mindful of all the former services and benefits of Your Honour towards us and also towards the land of Atjeh, our native soil, which has become pacified, quiet and prosperous and has received proper education — the pillar on which the culture of a country rests — beginning with the coming to power of His Honour the Governor H. N. A. Swart in Atjeh, from the year [[1908]] to [[1918]]."

"Therefore, although Your Honour has long been away from Atjeh and out of our sight, we do not forget the services and benefits of Your Honour."

The signatories therefore offered him a souvenir of the day: "a smoking set consisting of a cigar case, an ash-tray and a match-holder, as a memento in honour of your birthday," and closed with the wish that God might prolong the years of General H. N. A. Swart, his wife and family, and keep them far from misfortune.

## Significance

The letter is the principal Acehnese document reproduced in du Croo's book and the chief evidence he adduced for the proposition that Swart was remembered in Atjeh as *Bapa Swart*. It comes from the north coast — Keureutòë, Pasè, Peusangan, Samalanga, Meureudoe — the region in which the resistance of [[1908]]–[[1912]] had raged most fiercely, and is signed by chiefs of *landschappen* in which many of the actions of that resistance had been fought.

"They wrote to him, they told him their opinion, they did not forget their Bapa Swart," du Croo wrote. "So it was in [[1927]], and so it has remained to this day."

***Na 1795: Repetitie-boekje voor de Vaderlandsche Geschiedenis ten behoeve van Hoogere Burgerscholen en Candidaat-Notarissen*** ("After 1795: Repetition Booklet for the National History for the benefit of Higher Burgerscholen and Candidate-Notaries") was a Dutch school-history textbook of the national history of the Netherlands from [[1795]] onward, written by **F. Roorda** — a teacher (*leeraar H.B.S. 5-j.*) at the [[Hogere Burgerschool|Hogere Burgerschool]] of [[Appingedam|Appingedam]] — and published at [[Groningen|Groningen]] by **P. Noordhoff** in [[1924]]. The textbook — a concise revision booklet intended for the higher-burgerschool and the candidate-notary examination — is the principal documentary source for the canonical Dutch schoolroom memory of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the early twentieth century, and therefore of the Dutch national framing of the conflict.

## Publication

The textbook was published at [[Groningen|Groningen]] by **P. Noordhoff** in [[1924]], in a first edition priced at f 0.75. The author — F. Roorda — was a teacher at the [[Hogere Burgerschool|Hogere Burgerschool]] of [[Appingedam|Appingedam]], a 5-year H.B.S. The textbook's stated purpose, in the author's *Voorbericht* (preface), was to serve as a revision booklet (*repetitie-boekje*) for the national history examination, alongside the existing textbook "*Ons Examen*" — and to be usable alongside any textbook of the national history. The preface is dated [[Appingedam|Appingedam]], November [[1923]].

## Scope and structure

The textbook covers the national history of the Netherlands from the [[Batavian Republic|Batavian Republic]] of [[1795]] through to the early [[1920s]] — the period of the French domination, the Kingdom of Holland, the union with Belgium, the reigns of [[William I of the Netherlands|William I]], [[William II of the Netherlands|William II]], [[William III of the Netherlands|William III]], and the regency and reign of [[Wilhelmina of the Netherlands|Wilhelmina]], together with the parliamentary era and the colonial possessions. The chapter on the colonial possessions — "*VII. De bezittingen in Oost- en West-Indië*" ("The possessions in the East and West Indies") — contains the textbook's account of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The Atjeh-oorlog account

The textbook's account of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is the principal Dutch schoolroom memory of the conflict, fixed in a compressed chronological narrative of named commanders (Köhler, Van Swieten, Van der Heyden, Vetter, Van Heutsz), named places (Kraton, Kota Radja, Groot-Atjeh, Melaboeh, Pedir), and a single named Atjehnese leader (Toekoe Oemar). The account is recorded in the [[Aceh War (Na 1795 textbook account)|separate article]] on the textbook's Atjeh-oorlog narrative; the principal canonical clauses are:

- [[1873]]: "*Oorlogsverklaring aan Atjeh (wegens hun zéerooverijen en omdat de sultan vreemde hulp tegen de Hollanders wilde inroepen). De 1e expeditie mislukte, Köhler sneuvelde, maar de 2e expeditie onder Van Swieten bracht succes. De Kraton (paleis) van den sultan werd ingenomen, Kota Radja werd de zetel van ons gezag.*"
- [[1879]]: "*Generaal Van der Heyden bracht Groot Atjeh onder ons gezag: de opstand scheen bedwongen. Evenwel beging de regeering nu de fout het militaire door een burgerlijk bestuur te vervangen. Het verzet begon opnieuw, met het gevolg, dat de Indische regeering overging tot het stelsel van concentratie, d. w. z. de veroverde streken werden prijsgegeven en men beperkte zich tot de verdediging van een versterkte stelling om Kota-Radja. Langen tijd scheen het, dat de Atjehneezen zouden winnen.*"
- [[1893]]–[[1896]]: "*Toekoe-Oemar werd onze bondgenoot. Hij bleek echter onbetrouwbaar, want in 1896 sloot hij zich weer bij zijn landgenooten aan. Generaal Vetter werd daarna met onbeperkte volmacht naar Atjeh gezonden. Hij behaalde groote voordeelen; doch pas aan van Heutz gelukte het, Groot Atjeh te onderwerpen. Toekoe Oemar sneuvelde bij Melaboeh.*"
- [[1903]]: "*De pretendent-sultan en de andere Atjehsche aanvoerders bieden hun onderwerping aan. De oorlog kan hiermee als geëindigd worden beschouwd.*"
- [[1904]]–[[1909]]: "*Van Heutz gouverneur-general ... De Bataklanden onderworpen.*"
- [[1918]]: "*In 1918 werd het militaire door een burgerlijk bestuur vervangen, wat de goede verstandhouding tusschen de regeering en de inlanders heeft bevorderd.*"

## Significance

The *Na 1795* textbook is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history as the principal documentary source for the Dutch schoolroom memory of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the early twentieth century. The textbook's compression of a thirty-year war into a sequence of named commanders, named places, and a single named Atjehnese leader — fixed in canonical clauses that were transmitted to generations of Dutch schoolchildren — is the document of the way the war was institutionalised in the Dutch national memory, and of the moral-political framing (piracy, solicitation of foreign help, the "untrustworthy" Toekoe Oemar, the "pretender"-sultan) by which the conflict was justified. The textbook is therefore the principal source against which the more nuanced scholarly accounts of the war (e.g., [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s, [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s) must be read.

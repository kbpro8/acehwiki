**The Dispositie (operation order) of 22 May 1896 — Operatiën tegen de VI Moekims** — signed by [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] at [[Koeta Radja]] on [[22 May]] [[1896]] and preserved in the narrative of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the formal operation order specifying the disposition of the four *colonnes* and the reserve for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|major Dutch offensive of 23 May 1896]] against [[Teuku Umar]]'s stelling in the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]]. The *dispositie* detailed the composition, objective, and march route of the [[Bergcolonne (23 May 1896)|Bergcolonne]] (mountain column) under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]], the [[Colonne Lamtih|Colonne Lamtih]] under [[C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. Laceulle]], the [[Colonne Djeroe Loeng|Colonne Djeroe Loeng]] under [[J. Veeren|Lt.-Col. Veeren]], the [[Colonne tegen de XXII Moekims|Colonne tegen de XXII Moekims]] under [[G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. Van der Noordaa]], and the [[Reserve Colonne|Reserve Colonne]]. It was the operational predicate of the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Slag bij Tjot Tjako]], the [[Slag bij Lam Asan (23 May 1896)|Slag bij Lam Asan]], and the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's house (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang and Umar's house]] that broke the back of Umar's stelling.

## Background

By [[22 May]] [[1896]] the strategic situation had reached the moment for which the [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had been waiting since his arrival on [[7 April]] [[1896]]. The [[buitenlinie]] had been abandoned; Umar had been formally dismissed by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]]; the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|Proclamation of 27 April 1896]] had announced military operations against him; and the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|Bombardment of Lampisang]] had run for nearly four weeks at the cost of some 7,996 projectiles, to "zeer gering" (very meagre) effect. Reinforcements had arrived from Java: the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e]] and [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljons]] had landed with the 1e Bergbatterij and a peloton cavalerie on [[1 May]] [[1896]].

Intelligence on Umar's stelling in the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] had been accumulating since the defection of two of Umar's followers to [[Hr. Ms. Nias]] on [[2 May]] [[1896]] and the ongoing reports of the *controleur* [[M. Neelmeijer|1e Luit. Neelmeijer]]. The stelling comprised a complex of versterkingen (fortifications) and loopgraven (trenches) running from the strand west of the former blokhuis [[Pagani]] to the versterking at Binasa Lam Me, including Koeta Sagoi, wachthuizen, and versterkingen at Lam Awi, [[Lamtengah|Lam Tengah-Oost]], Lam Manjang (with *lilla* — a swivel gun), and Lam Isi. The disposition of the four *colonnes* and the reserve was designed to break this stelling along its entire length.

## Content

The *dispositie* is signed by [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] (as *Mil. Comd'. van A. en O.* — Military Commander of Atjeh and Dependencies) at [[Koeta Radja]] on [[22 May]] [[1896]] and is the formal *order of battle* for the [[23 May]] [[1896]] attack. It specifies, in the standard Dutch operational format, the main attack, secondary attack, centre, *reserve*, and the staff assignments and logistics for each *colonne*.

The **Bergcolonne** (Mountain Column) under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. J. B. van Heutsz]] was assigned the storming of the [[Toean Tjot Tjako|Tjot Tjako]] height, the dominant tactical feature of the stelling; its operations on [[23 May]] [[1896]] are documented in the [[Berichten No. 1–4 from Van Heutsz (23 May 1896)|Berichten No. 1–4 from Van Heutsz]]. The **Colonne Lamtih** under [[C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. C. J. Laceulle]] was assigned operations against the Lamtih sector. The **Colonne Djeroe Loeng** under [[J. Veeren|Lt.-Col. Veeren]] was assigned the assault on [[Lam Asan]] and the adjacent kampongs. The **Colonne tegen de XXII Moekims** under [[G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. Van der Noordaa]] was assigned a feint against the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] sector, to fix Umar's forces there. The **Reserve Colonne** was held back for exploitation.

An annex to the *dispositie* — the Reports and observations concerning the left wing of the enemy stelling — described the enemy fortifications in detail, from the strand approximately 500 m west of where the blokhuis [[Pagani]] had stood to the versterking at Binasa Lam Me, including Koeta Sagoi, wachthuizen, and versterkingen at Lam Awi, [[Lamtengah|Lam Tengah-Oost]], Lam Manjang (with *lilla*), Lam Isi, and the connecting loopgraven. This annex, on page 178 of *ATJEH '96*, is the most detailed single Dutch source on the structure of Umar's stelling.

## Reception and Aftermath

The *dispositie* was executed on [[23 May]] [[1896]]. The Bergcolonne under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] stormed [[Toean Tjot Tjako|Tjot Tjako]] in the early morning, and the four sequential *Berichten* from Van Heutsz — beginning at 6:40 a.m. with the report that the height was "niet beklimbaar voor artillerie" (not climbable for artillery) and ending at 9:15 a.m. with the report that [[Lampisang|Lam Pisang]] was abandoned and many Acehnese were fleeing southward — drove the operational decisions of the day. The [[Slag bij Lam Asan (23 May 1896)|gevecht bij Lam Asan]] under [[J. Veeren|Maj. Veeren]] and [[G. A. Hansen|Maj. Jacobs]] and the [[Slag bij Lam Manjang en Lam Isi (23 May 1896)|gevecht bij Lam Manjang en Lam Isi]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel Soeters|Lt.-Col. Soeters]] proceeded on parallel axes. On [[24 May]] [[1896]] Umar's house at [[Lampisang|Lam-Pisang]] was captured, and on [[25 May]] [[1896]] the [[Bestorming van Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|final storming of Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]] completed the rupture of the stelling.

## Significance

The *dispositie* of [[22 May]] [[1896]] is the operational predicate of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] — the decisive Dutch operation of the first semester of [[1896]] and the action that broke Umar's stelling and drove him into retreat to [[Lepong]]. Its detailed specification of the four *colonnes* and the reserve, and its annex describing Umar's versterkingen, are the primary Dutch source for the structure of both the Dutch attack and the Acehnese defence. As a historical document, the *dispositie* is preserved in the narrative of pages 136, 137, 178 rather than in the *Bijlagen* — its operational rather than political character kept it out of the official annexes, but its importance to the narrative required its full preservation in the text.

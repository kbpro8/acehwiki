**Geschiedenis van den Atjeh-oorlog 1873–1886** was a Dutch popular-historical book written by the schoolmaster [[P. Brooshooft|Mr. P. Brooshooft]] and published at Utrecht in [[1886]] by F. B. van Ditmar. Subtitled *"In populairen vorm verteld"* (Told in popular form) and advertised as suitable for onderwijzers en onderwijzeressen (schoolteachers), it was one of the earliest single-volume narrative histories covering the first thirteen years of the [[aceh-war|Aceh War]] from the Dutch declaration of war in [[march|March]] [[1873]] to the end of [[1886]].

## Publication and Physical Description

The book was printed in Utrecht by F. B. van Ditmar and issued as a cheap popular volume, in conscious contrast to the large, expensive official history by [[E. B. Kielstra|Kielstra]]. It included six folded maps (*kaartjes*) keyed to successive chapters:

1. **Map 1A.** The position of Atjeh relative to the other Dutch and British-Indian possessions around the [[Straits of Malacca|Straits Settlements]].
2. **Map 1B.** The kingdom of Atjeh with the coastal states.
3. **Map 2.** [[groot-atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] as divided before the war, showing the [[sagi|sagies]] and [[mukim|moekims]].
4. **Map 3.** The theatre of war of the [[April 1873 Atjeh Expedition|first]] and [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expeditions]].
5. **Map 4.** The theatre of war and the Dutch line of posts under Generals [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]], [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] and [[Generaal Diemont|Diemont]].
6. **Map 5.** The theatre of war and post line under General [[General van der Heijden|van der Heyden]].
7. **Map 6.** The Dutch *concentratie* position in [[1886]].

## Contents

The work opened with a foreword (dated [[14-december|14 December]] [[1886]]) in which Brooshooft thanked his sources and explained his purpose. The ten chapters that followed were:

1. *De twee groote mededingers in het Oosten* — the Dutch and British in the East Indies, the decline of the Oost-Indische Compagnie, the restoration of Dutch authority after [[1814]], and the resulting position of Atjeh as the last independent kingdom on [[sumatra|Sumatra]].
2. *Oorzaken van- en aanleiding tot den oorlog* — diplomatic history from the [[1816]] British treaty with Atjeh through the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|Treaty of London (1824)]], the piracy problem, the [[Treaty of 1857 between Netherlands and Aceh|Atjeh Tractaat of 1857]], the [[siak-tractaat-(1858)|Siak Tractaat of 1858]], the [[sumatra-tractaat-(1871)|Sumatra Tractaat of 2 November 1871]], the [[aceh-envoys-visit-to-singapore-(january-1873)|Acehnese mission to Singapore]], and the dispatch of [[W. C. Nieuwenhuyzen|Nieuwenhuyzen]] as Government Commissioner, culminating in the [[Manifest aan Sultan van Atjeh (22 March 1873)|declaration of war of 21 March 1873]].
3. *Land en volk van Atjeh* — geography, administration, society, religion, economy, arms, costume, food, currency, names and titles of pre-war Atjeh, together with a strongly negative Dutch-colonial character sketch of the Acehnese.
4. *De eerste expeditie (Köhler, van Daalen)* — assembly, composition, voyage, landing of [[8-april|8 April]] [[1873]], the two storms of the [[mesigit|Missigit]], the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]] on [[14-april|14 April]] [[1873]], the failed reconnaissances on the [[Kraton (institution)|Kraton]], and the withdrawal.
5. *Onze vestiging in Atjeh (van Swieten, Verspijck)* — the inter-expedition blockade, assembly of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]], landing at [[kwala-loe|Kwala Loe]] on [[9-december|9 December]] [[1873]], the advance to [[penajoeng|Penajoeng]], the battles around [[lemboe|Lemboe]] and [[Pante Perak|Pantei Perak]], the encirclement of the Kraton, the unopposed entry on [[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]], the hoisting of the Dutch flag on [[25-january|25 January]] [[1874]], and the death of Sultan [[Alaedin Mahmut Shah|Aladin Mahmoed Shah]].
6. *De bevestiging onzer stelling (van Swieten, Pel)* — the [[Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation of 31 January 1874]] annexing Atjeh, the failed fight at [[lampoe-oek|Lampoe-Oek]], the construction of the Kota Radja fortified zone, the renaming of the Kraton as [[koeta-radja|Kota Radja]], the establishment of [[oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] as the new port, the building of the [[Atjeh Tram|railway]], and the departure of General [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] on [[26-april|26 April]] [[1874]] leaving Colonel [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] in command.
7. *De uitbreiding onzer stelling (Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont)* — the piece-by-piece expansion of the fortified line during [[1874]]–[[1877]], Pel's 70-day campaign, the death of Pel on [[23-february|23 February]] [[1876]], the September expedition of [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]], the completion of Pel's programme by [[Generaal Diemont|Diemont]], the first attacks on [[lampagger|Lampagger]], and the appearance of [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdurrachman]] and [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Toekoe Oemar]].
8. *De aanvallende oorlog (Van der Heyden), Juni 1877–April 1881* — the offensive campaigns of Lieutenant-General [[General van der Heijden|van der Heyden]] in the XXII Moekims, XXVI Moekims and eastern Groot-Atjeh, the submission of Habib and of [[toekoe-moeda-baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]], the expeditions to [[samalanga|Samalanga]], the West Coast, and the East Coast, the creation of the civil administration, and Van der Heyden's recall.
9. *Het tijdperk van civiel bestuur (Pruys van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias), April 1881–August 1884* — the civilian governorships of [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]] and [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], the breakdown of the forward policy, the [[nisero-affair-(1883-1885)|Nisero incident]], and the decision to adopt the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentrated position]].
10. *De concentratie onzer stelling. Tegenwoordige toestand* — the withdrawal to the concentrated line under [[demmeni|Demmeni]], the [[Atjeh Tram|steam tram]], the telephone network, continued attacks inside and outside the line, the spread of hostilities to [[Tamiang]] and [[Deli]], the [[Hoc Canton affair|Hoe Canton affair of June 1886]], the **beri-beri** epidemic, the death of Demmeni on [[13-december|13 December]] [[1886]], and Brooshooft's own concluding argument against both abandonment and renewed offensive war.

The volume closed with:

- A *Korte Inhoud* (summary table of contents) keyed to page numbers.
- A *Plaatsing der kaartjes* (index to maps).
- An *Opgave der gedurende den Atjeh-oorlog gesneuvelde, of aan voor den vijand bekomen wonden overleden, officieren*, listing officers killed in action or died of wounds from [[14-april|14 April]] [[1873]] (General [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]]) through [[18-may|18 May]] [[1886]] (1st Lieutenant D. J. Schiper).

## Authorial Position

Brooshooft used the book to make two political arguments:

1. **Defence of Fransen van de Putte.** In Chapter II he printed the full February–March [[1873]] telegram exchange between Minister [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] and Governor-General [[J. Loudon|Loudon]] to demonstrate that the Minister had not sought war but had tried to avoid an immediate sovereignty demand, and that the war was forced by the Acehnese court's duplicity and the risk of foreign intervention.
2. **Against both retreat and renewed offensive.** In the concluding pages he rejected S. C. H. [[S. C. H. Nederburgh|Nederburgh]]'s proposal to abandon the conquered territory and retire to a single coastal point as a "colonial suicide" fatal to Dutch prestige, while also rejecting a renewed major offensive in the style of [[General van der Heijden|van der Heyden]] as financially and militarily impossible given the exhausted Indies army and treasury. He advocated patient maintenance of the existing concentrated position.

## Significance

Although popular in tone and marred by the strongly anti-Acehnese prejudices of its author, Brooshooft's book remains useful to historians of the Aceh War because:

- It provides the earliest single-volume narrative covering 1873–1886 in connected form.
- It reproduces the 1873 Loudon–Fransen van de Putte telegrams in full.
- Its officer-casualty list preserves an easily consulted contemporary necrology for the first thirteen years of the war.
- Its chapter titles and periodisation (first expedition, second expedition, consolidation, expansion, offensive war, civil government, concentration) influenced later Dutch popular writing on the war.
- Its six folded maps offer a compact contemporary cartographic overview of each phase.

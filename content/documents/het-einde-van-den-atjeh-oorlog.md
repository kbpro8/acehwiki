**Het einde van den Atjeh Oorlog. De beteekenis der Onderwerping van Panglima Polim** is a series of three articles published as overprints from the *Evening Post* of [[25 August]], [[26 August]], and [[27 August]] [[1903]]. Published by the [[Royal Institute for Language, Land, and Ethnology]] (Royal Institute for Language, Land, and Ethnology) in [[The Hague]] through the publishers [[De Vries & Co.]] (previously [[Publishing Company]]), the work examines the significance of [[Panglima Polim]]'s submission for the end of the [[Aceh War]].

## Content and argument

The articles argue that the submission of [[Panglima Polim]] in [[1903]] was of greater significance for ending the conflict than the earlier submission of the pretender-sultan. The reason lies in the unique role of the [[Polim]] family: unlike other resistance leaders—such as [[Habib Abdoer Rahman]], [[Teungkoe di Tiro]], or [[Toekoe Oemar]] himself—who disappeared when their persons left the stage, the [[Polims]] persisted as a family line. "Polim est mort, vive Polim!" made [[Panglima Polim]] effectively immortal.

The work traces the history of the [[XXII Moekims]] from the first expedition of [[1873]] through the capture of the [[kraton]] ([[24 January]] [[1874]]), the campaigns of [[Generaal Van der Heyden]] ([[1877]]–[[1879]]), the [[Concentrated Line]] ([[1886]]), the alliance attempts under [[Deykerhoff]] and the defection of [[T. Oema]] ([[1896]]), and finally the active campaign led by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] from [[1898]] onward.

## Key observations

The articles highlight several critical strategic insights:

- The [[XXII Moekims]] was always the core of resistance, and no amount of negotiation or alliance could subdue it without sustained direct military action.
- The family line of the [[Polims]]—persisting through [[Sri Moeda Perkasa]] (d. [[1879]]), [[Toekoe Moeda Ibrahim]] (d. [[1891]]), and the young [[Toekoe Radja Daoed]]—made the resistance continuous regardless of individual deaths.
- Only the unconditional submission of the head of the family—achieved through five years of relentless pursuit—could bring the struggle to an end.
- The collaboration between [[Van Heutsz]] and [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]]—formalised in [[March 1898]]—was the decisive factor that finally broke the resistance.

## Significance

As a contemporary journalistic analysis published at the very moment of the war's conclusion, the work provides a valuable primary-source account of Dutch perception of the conflict's end. Its argument—that only direct action guided by accurate knowledge could succeed—confirms the strategic analysis developed by [[Somer]] in [[1935]] and remains a significant document in the historiography of the [[Aceh War]].

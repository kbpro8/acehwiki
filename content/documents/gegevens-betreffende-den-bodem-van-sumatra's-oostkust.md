**Gegevens betreffende den bodem van Sumatra's Oostkust** — *Data concerning the soil of Sumatra's East Coast* — is a soil-survey monograph by [[F. C. van Heurn|F. C. van Heurn]], a chemical engineer who had been attached for some years to the [[Avros|Avros]] research station at [[Medan]]. The book is cited by Broersma in *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* for its judgement on the soils of [[Tamiang]].

## The Tamiang Judgement
Broersma quotes Van Heurn's verdict on page 103 of the soil-survey: the tertiary hill-land of Tamiang differs from the mountains of the same formation in that its surface consists mainly of clays and clay-stones, while the marls and sandstones which elsewhere appear as sandy hills are here in the minority. The sandy patches are as unfruitful as the sandy tertiary soils of [[Bengkalis]], but the clays, *'bij behoorlijke drainage voor Europeesche cultures voortreffelijke eigenschappen te bezitten'* — *with proper drainage possess excellent properties for European cultures.* Broersma comments drily that Van Heurn does not say how the clays have *proved* those excellent properties: *'dat hebben bewezen, zegt de schrijver niet. Door de nu bestaande aanplantingen misschien? Maar dan is het bewijs van te weinig omvang.'* The implication is that the great hopes once held for Tamiang as a planting-country had proved too high.

## Significance
The soil-survey is one of the standard works on the agricultural geology of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]] in the early civilian period and is regularly cited for the disappointing performance of the Tamiang and Langsa plantation-groups in the years around [[1920]].

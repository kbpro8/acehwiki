**The Voorwoord of Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis** was the dedicatory foreword-letter written by Generaal [[General van der Heijden|K. van der Heijden]] — the former civil and military Governor of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden — from [[Bronbeek]] in June [[1889]], addressed to "Waarde heeren Cool en Hooijer," for publication at the head of [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ('s-Gravenhage: [[M. Nijhoff|Martinus Nijhoff]], [[1889]]). Signed "Uw Oud-Kameraad, K. van der Heijden," the letter at once commends the book, corrects one identification in it, and assesses the value of its accompanying [[Schetskaart van het boven-stroomgebied der Atjeh-rivier (1889)|sketch map]].

## Commendation of the work

Van der Heijden noted that the book was composed by the two officers who had served under him at Atjeh "in dit tijdperk" and had shared in the memorable tochts "die de kroon zetten op onze overwinningen in de XXII en XXVI Moekims" — that set the crown upon the victories in the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]] and [[XXVI Moekims]]. He welcomed the work not because it led back to the days of his own administration of [[Groot-Atjeh]] en Onderhoorigheden, but first out of sympathy with an endeavour on behalf of the Indisch leger "dat zoozeer steun behoeft en verdient"; secondly because the work contained important data on the interior of Groot-Atjeh "die men elders te vergeefs zoekt" — which one seeks elsewhere in vain; and finally to thank both officers for the esteem in which they held him.

## The correction concerning Toekoe Moeda Daud

The foreword's most consequential passage concerns the identity of the principal Achehnese personage of the book. "Spreekt van zelf," the General wrote, that the [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed|Toekoe Moeda Daud]] named in the work, head of [[Selimoen]], "een der pretendenten voor den Sultanstroon" — one of the pretenders to the Sultan's throne — who submitted to him on [[20 August]] [[1879]], was not to be understood as the then-current Sultan-pretendant: the latter's coming had indeed been announced, on the same date, by the likewise newly submitted [[Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet|Toekoe Ajer-Alang]], but he had excused himself "uithoofde der naderende poeasa" — by reason of the approaching fasting month. The remark distinguishes two royal figures that later accounts risked conflating — the Selimoen chief Toekoe Moeda Daud and the pretendant watched over by [[Toewankoe Haschim]] — and dates the formal act of submission to [[20 August]] [[1879]].

## The judgement on the schetskaart

Of the [[Schetskaart van het boven-stroomgebied der Atjeh-rivier (1889)|accompanying sketch map]] Van der Heijden wrote that it was of weight because, in his opinion, it answered the hitherto unsolved question: "waar en hoe eindigt de Atjeh-rivier vallei?" — where and how does the Atjeh river valley end? He observed that the newest official map of Groot-Atjeh, issued in January [[1884]] by the Topografisch Bureau at Batavia, did not show this boundary, the precision of the official data and of the available material for the upper Atjeh-valley having differed too much. From the sketch, by contrast, appeared how little of the inhabited and habitable terrain had not been entered by the Dutch troops, how nearly inaccessible — except towards [[Segli]] — the valley was enclosed on the south, east and north by heavy mountain chains, and with how much right the Netherlander could then say: "de Atjeh-vallei is ons" — the Atjeh valley is ours.

## Closing wish

The General closed with the wish that the work, written so as to be comprehensible also to the non-military reader, might serve "tot opwekking van meerder belangstelling voor het Indische leger en tot vermeerdering onzer kennis van Atjeh, de toekomst tot nut" — for the awakening of greater interest in the Indies army and for the increase of our knowledge of Atjeh, to the profit of the future.

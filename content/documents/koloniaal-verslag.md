**The Koloniaal Verslag** (*Colonial Report*) was the annual report on the state of the Netherlands colonies laid before the [[Staten-Generaal]], and the principal statistical and narrative source used by all sides in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November|16]]–[[17 November]] [[1897]].

## Use in the debate of [[1897]]

The [[Memorie van Antwoord on the Indies budget for 1898|Memorie van Antwoord]] referred the chamber to page 8 of the latest *Koloniaal Verslag* for its answer on the political and military objective attained in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], and to page 7 for the Royal authorisation by which a definite plan of action towards Atjeh had been adopted on the proposal of the previous minister and in accordance with the proposals of the [[Governor-General of Netherlands India|Governor-General]].

[[H. van Kol]] used the report against the government's own case for the war. From the *Koloniaal Verslag* he cited the record that in [[1896]] two cases of piracy occurred at [[Japara]], two on [[Madura|Madoera]], and one each at [[Batavia]], [[Surabaya|Soerabaya]] and Probolingo; and the statement of the *Verslag* of [[1897]] that on the [[Mentawai Islands|Mentawei-eilanden]] prauws were "niet zelden" robbed and their crews murdered, "en wij kunnen deze euveldaden niet beletten." He drew from this the argument set out at [[Van Kol's refutation of the causes of the Atjeh-oorlog (1897)]] — that the Dutch punished in Atjeh what they tolerated in their own dependencies.

He cited it again on [[17 November]] for the conduct of the war: "Het laatste Koloniaal Verslag spreekt meermalen van het in den asch leggen van kampongs, van het vernielen en verbranden der schuilhoeken en het uitroeien van bosschen, zoodat de bevolking alleen nog een toevlucht vond in de gespaarde kampongs." — The latest Colonial Report speaks repeatedly of the laying of kampongs in ashes, of the destroying and burning of hiding-places and the extirpation of forests, so that the population found refuge only in the spared kampongs.

[[J. B. Verhey|Verhey]] drew from the *Koloniale Verslagen* of [[1896]] and [[1897]] the figures of the [[Beri-beri epidemic in the Atjeh garrison|beri-beri epidemic]]: 3,329 sufferers in [[1895]] and 3,412 in [[1896]]; 132 Europeans and 877 natives invalided in [[1896]]; 16 Europeans and 147 natives dead of the disease. He also stated that he confined himself, in judging the capacity of the Indies army to sustain the occupation, to the data appearing in the *Koloniaal Verslag*.

[[Van Vlijmen]] cited it for two matters: the statement of the [[Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|governor of Atjeh]] that the time had not yet come to take over the rights or taxes of the coastal princes, which he took as proof that the matter had not been forgotten; and the case of the running in of war contraband at [[Simpang Anas]].

## Significance

Because the *Koloniaal Verslag* was the government's own annual publication, its evidence could not be dismissed by either side. In [[1897]] it thus served simultaneously as the government's warrant for claiming that quiet was returning to the districts the hostile bands no longer dared penetrate, and as the critics' warrant for asserting that villages were being laid in ashes, forests extirpated and the garrison consumed by disease.

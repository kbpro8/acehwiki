**The Zelfbestuursverordening of [[9 December]] [[1926]] (Lhoksoekon woeker)** — printed in the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* as Regulation No. 1 — is the *woeker* (usury) verordening (ordinance) of the *onderafdeeling* (subdivision) [[Lho Soekon|Lhoksoekon]] in the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh]] (district North Coast of Atjeh), issued jointly by the three *oeleebalang* (territorial chiefs) of the *onderafdeeling* — Keureutoë, Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë, and Matang Koeli — for the *bestrijding van den woeker* (combating of usury) in the *landschappen* (territories) of the entire *gewest* (region). Approved by the *Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies) on [[21 December]] [[1926]], it is the opening regulation of the compilation — the first of the twenty-three numbered regulations printed in the volume — and is the only regulation in the compilation addressed specifically to the suppression of *riba* (interest/usury) under Islamic and colonial law.

## The regulation as opening entry of the compilation

Reg. No. 1 occupies the first pages of the compilation's substantive content (pp. 1–2 of the printed book), opening the *Justitie* (Justice) section under which the *Inhoud* (table of contents) classifies it. The compilation's *Inhoud* lists twenty-three numbered regulations under four headings — Justitie, Financiën (Finances), Bestuur (Administration), and Landbouw/Nijverheid/Handel (Agriculture/Industry/Commerce) — of which the *Justitie* section contains only Reg. No. 1. The regulation's title, as garbled in the OCR but reconstructible, is:

> "ZELFEESTURRSVEERRDDNNGG van 9 December 1926 tot bestrijding van den woeker in de landschappen der enderafdeeling Lhoksoekon (goedgekeurd door den Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden op 21 December 1926)."

The three *oeleebalang* of the *onderafdeeling* Lhoksoekon — "*Kami Oeleebalang Negeri: De Zelfbesturen van: Keureutoë Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë Matang Koeli*" — are the same three territorial chiefs whose *landschappen* later, in [[1925]] and [[1916]], jointly issued Reg. No. 12 (the *toezicht op Mohammedaansch godsdienstonderwijs* — supervision of Mohammedan religious instruction) and Regs. 18a and 18b (the *klappercultuur-verordening* [coconut-cultivation ordinance] and its *mantri-instructie* [petty-official instruction]).

## Prohibition on usury (riba)

The substantive provision is set out in the *Eenig artikel* — the single article of the regulation — which is modeled on the metropolitan *anti-woeker* statute, [[Staatsblad 1916 No. 643]]. The recital grounds the regulation explicitly in both colonial statute and Islamic legal tradition:

> "Menimbang bahasa perloe oentoek sekalian pendoedoek negeri akan menetapkan satoe peratoeran oen- toek pentjegah riba seperti ditetap- kan dalam Stantshlad 1916 No. 643"

— "Whereas it is necessary for all the inhabitants of the *negeri* (territory) to establish a regulation for the *pentjegah riba* (prevention of usury) as established in Staatsblad 1916 No. 643." The Malay term *riba* — the Quranic prohibition on interest, drawn from the same Arabic root (*ribā*) that underlies the metropolitan *anti-woeker* statute — is here invoked as the substantive object of the regulation. The Malay *pentjegah* (prevention/suppression) renders the Dutch *bestrijding* (combating/suppression) of the title. The regulation thus converts the metropolitan statute into a *Zelfbestuur*-level verordening directed at the indigenous *anak negeri* (subjects) of the *onderafdeeling* Lhoksoekon, with the *riba*-prohibition framed simultaneously in Islamic and colonial-statutory terms.

The *Eenig artikel* itself empowers the judge to *matigen* (mitigate) or *nietig verklaren* (annul) exploitative contracts — *overeenkomstig* (in accordance with) the *regeling vermeld in Staatsblad 1916 No. 643* (regulation mentioned in Staatsblad 1916 No. 643). The exact text of the *Eenig artikel*, in the bilingual Malay-Dutch columnar form, is reproduced in the compilation's pp. 1–2; the comparative table of *GELIJKE VERORDENINGEN* (Parallel Ordinances) on p. 14 of the compilation extends parallel *anti-woeker* regelingen (regulations) to the other *onderafdeelingen* of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement]] in [[1926]] and [[1927]]: [[Sigli]], [[Meureudu|Meureudoe]], [[Lammeulo]], [[Idi]], [[Tjalang]], Gajo LoeBus, [[De Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], the *Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen*, and [[Pulo U|Simeuloed]].

## Penalty structure and application to the entire gewest

The penalty-structure of Reg. No. 1 follows the model of [[Staatsblad 1916 No. 643]]: the *woeker*-contract is not itself criminalised, but is made *nietig* (void) or *matigbaar* (mitigable) at the instance of the indigenous judge — typically the *moesapat negeri* (village court) of the *landschap*. The regulation applies to the entire *gewest* of the *onderafdeeling* Lhoksoekon, covering all three of its *landschappen* (Keureutoë, Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë, and Matang Koeli). The comparative table of *GELIJKE VERORDENINGEN* on p. 14 of the compilation shows that the *anti-woeker* regeling was extended within months to most of the other *onderafdeelingen* of the *Gouvernement*, with parallel regelingen for Sigli, Meureudoe, Lammeulo, Idi, Tjalang, Gajo LoeBus, Alaslanden, the *Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen*, and Simeuloed all dated [[1926]]–[[1927]].

## Significance

The *Zelfbestuursverordening van [[9 December]] [[1926]]* opens the [[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Zelfbestuursverordeningen compilation]] as the sole entry in the *Justitie* section, and is the only regulation in the compilation addressed specifically to the suppression of *riba* (usury). Its modelling on the metropolitan *anti-woeker* statute of [[Staatsblad 1916 No. 643]] illustrates the typical mechanism by which a Netherlands-Indies statute was converted into a *Zelfbestuur*-level verordening binding on the *anak negeri* of the *zelfbesturende landschappen*: the metropolitan statute supplies the legal framework, the *Zelfbestuur* supplies the local jurisdiction, and the *Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* supplies the *goedkeuring* (approval) that brings the verordening into force. The regulation's framing of the *woeker*-prohibition in terms of the Malay *pentjegah riba* — drawing on the Quranic *ribā*-prohibition that is itself foundational to Islamic contract law — also illustrates the dual Islamic-and-colonial legal register in which the *Zelfbestuur*'s verordeningen of [[Atjeh]] operated. The compilation's placement of this regulation as Reg. No. 1, ahead of the financially-central *Pantjang Alas* regime of Regs. 2–4c, signals the compiler's recognition that the *woeker*-problem was, in the late 1920s, the most pressing single issue of indigenous civil justice in the *Gouvernement*.

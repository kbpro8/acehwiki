**Kasida al-Lamiyya fil-tawwidd** (Arabic: *اللامية في التويد*) is the same work as *[[Bad' al-Amali]]*, a short creed poem composed by [[Siraj al-Din Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Uthman al-Ushi]] around [[569]]/[[1173]]. The poem’s distinctive feature is that every verse terminates with the letter *I* (Arabic *lam*), hence the name “the I‑poem”.

## Historical Reception

The poem was incorporated into Islamic curricula throughout the Muslim world and was the subject of numerous commentaries in Persian, Turkish, Hindustani, and other languages (see [[Brockelmann]] for a catalog of 29 commentaries). Its popularity extended to the Malay world, where a 16th‑century Acehnese manuscript contains an interlinear Malay translation.

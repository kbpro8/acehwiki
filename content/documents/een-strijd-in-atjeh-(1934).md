**Een strijd in Atjeh (Een oogarts op het oorlogspad)** — *A struggle in Atjeh (An eye doctor on the warpath)* — was a Dutch-language pamphlet published in [[1934]] by the [[Vereeniging SIMAVI|Vereeniging „SIMAVI”]] (*Steun In Medische Aangelegenheden Voor Inheemschen*, Support in Medical Matters for Natives) at [[Rotterdam]], printed by N.V. Drukkerij Korteweg at Weenaplein 16. It reprints three letters written by the young jurist and journalist [[C. Graadt van Roggen]] for the *[[Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant]]*, recording his shipboard conversations with the ophthalmologist [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] about Tijssen's cataract and trachoma campaigns in the *[[Kampong|kampongs]]* (villages) of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. A foreword by [[H. Wanting]], editor of the *Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant*, dated September [[1934]], explains that the letters are published as an act of *piëteit* (pious remembrance) towards Graadt van Roggen, who had died shortly after writing them.

The pamphlet is, for the history of Atjeh, a rare non-military Dutch account of Acehnese village life in the early [[1930s]] — of the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|North Coast]], the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast]] between [[Meulaboh]] and [[Singkel]], and the island of [[Simaloer]] — and of the state of medical provision in the pacified sultanate a generation after the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## Publication and physical description

The title-page reads: *EEN STRIJD IN ATJEH (Een oogarts op het oorlogspad) DOOR Mr. C. GRAADT VAN ROGGEN met een voorrede van den heer H. WANTING, Redacteur „Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant”.* Beneath the title is the caption of the cover photograph: "eenige van de behandelde cataractlijders in de kampong Long Poetoe op de Noordkust van Atjeh" (some of the treated cataract sufferers in the *[[Kampong|kampong]]* of [[Long Poetoe]] on the North Coast of Atjeh). The imprint is *Uitgave van de Vereeniging „SIMAVI” (Steun In Medische Aangelegenheden Voor Inheemschen)*.

The pamphlet comprises sixteen numbered pages of text within a twenty-page collation, illustrated with photographs of Acehnese patients — among them the group of [[Seven juvenile cataracts of Atjeh's Westkust|seven juvenile cataract patients]] operated on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast]], an incurably blind child carried by its father, a Muslim priest reading the Koran aloud to his fellow patients, and a final plate captioned *Kinderliefde in Atjeh* (child-love in Atjeh).

The second page reproduces the constitution and governing board of [[Vereeniging SIMAVI|SIMAVI]], royally approved on [[28 October]] [[1926]]. The last page of text carries a quotation from Dr. J. Tijssen in *Medische Nood*, [[1934]], no. 1, and the statement that SIMAVI had in that year expended f 26,500 on medical assistance to the indigenous population of the Netherlands East and West Indies, of which f 4,400 was for medicines and dressings alone.

## Origin of the text

[[H. Wanting|Wanting]]'s foreword recounts that in February of the previous year — that is, February [[1933]] — the steamer *[[Baloeran]]* of the [[Rotterdamsche Lloyd]] brought together on her homeward voyage two passengers who had never met and would never meet again. One was Dr. [[J. Tijssen]], an eye doctor in Atjeh who, after long years of practice as a *plantersdokter* (plantation doctor), had gone into the *kampongs* on his own initiative — "a pioneer." The other was the twenty-eight-year-old Mr. [[C. Graadt van Roggen]], returning to his editorship at the *[[Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant]]* after a short spell of journalism in the Indies.

Wanting describes the encounter as either "a happy accident or a wise dispensation," and notes that the reserved physician could not escape "het gretige journalistenoog" (the eager journalist's eye) of the gifted young jurist. The result was the three letters here reprinted. Wanting records that half a year after the shipboard meeting the mourners stood at Westerveld around Graadt van Roggen's bier, and that many expressions of sympathy had arrived from the Indies, where he had lived and worked so very briefly.

## Contents

The letters are datelined *a/b. „Baloeran”, Februari [[1933]]* and proceed as a reported conversation. They cover: Tijssen's sixteen years as a plantation doctor in the south of Atjeh and at [[Langsa]]; his assistantship under [[Prof. van der Hoeve]] at [[Leiden]]; his return to Atjeh in [[1929]]; his decision to work outside the hospitals; the [[Cataract campaign on the North Coast of Atjeh (1932)|cataract campaign on the North Coast]]; the encounter with the chief [[Teukoe Oemar van Bernoen]]; the [[Seven juvenile cataracts of Atjeh's Westkust|seven juvenile cataracts]] between [[Meulaboh]] and [[Singkel]]; the [[Idi|Idi]] operations of November [[1931]]; the [[Bakongan]] journey of August [[1933]]; the visit to [[Simaloer]]; and finally the [[Trachoma campaign on Samosir (1933)|trachoma campaign on Samosir]] in the [[Toba Lake|Tobameer]], appended as a comparative case.

## Significance

The pamphlet's importance for Acehnese history lies in three areas. First, it documents the medical condition of the Acehnese population — cataract, trachoma, avitaminosis and juvenile blindness — in a period for which few civil accounts survive. Second, it records, through the anecdote of [[Teukoe Oemar van Bernoen]] and the courteous question *„Blanda betoel?”* ("A real Dutchman?"), the persistence of Acehnese suspicion of Dutch motives sixty years after the outbreak of the war. Third, in describing the road along the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast]] flanked by mass graves recalling "de vele Atjehoorlogen en den opstand van '26," — the many Atjeh wars and the rising of '26, and the [[Monument to Teukoe Oemar's death|monument marking the spot where Teukoe Oemar fell]], it preserves the physical memory landscape of the war as it stood in the [[1930s]].

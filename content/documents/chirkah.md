**The *Chirkah*** (or ***Ḥarakah***, as [[C.A.O. van Nieuwenhuijze|Van Nieuwenhuijze]] prefers to read it) is a Malay Sufi treatise by [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]], the pantheistic mystic who dominated the religious life of the court of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] of [[Acheh]] until his death in [[1630]]. The work is named, together with the *[[Mir'at al-muhakkikin|Mirʾāt al-muḥaqqiqīn]]*, among the writings of Shamsuddin that [[Raniri|Nuruddin ar-Raniri]] cites and refutes in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] (pp. 108, 110–111 of the Voorhoeve facsimile).

## The reading of the title

The title of the work is transmitted in the manuscripts as **Chirkah**, but [[C.A.O. van Nieuwenhuijze|Van Nieuwenhuijze]] proposed to read it as **Ḥarakah** ("movement"). [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] records this conjecture in his apparatus to the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] (notes ad pp. 108, 110, 111) with the abbreviation "vN" alongside the lemma "Chirkah." The reading *Ḥarakah* — if accepted — would situate the work in the technical Sufi vocabulary of the *maqāmāt* and *aḥwāl*, in which *ḥarakah* ("movement") signifies the active progress of the mystic along the path, in contrast to *sukūn* ("repose"). The traditional reading *Chirkah* has no obvious Arabic or Malay analogue.

## Content and character

The *Chirkah* (or *Ḥarakah*) belongs to the same body of Wujūdiyyah mysticism as the *[[Mir'at al-muhakkikin|Mirʾāt al-muḥaqqiqīn]]* of Shamsuddin and the *[[Asrar al-'arifin|Asrār al-ʿārifīn]]* and *[[Kitab al-Muntahi|Kitāb al-Muntahī]]* of [[Hamzah Pansuri]]. It sets out, in the *[[Bahasa Djawi]]* of the Achehnese court, some aspect of the doctrine of the unity of being (*waḥdat al-wujūd*) in the form developed by Shamsuddin from the heritage of Hamzah Pansuri. Raniri's citation of the work in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] (pp. 108, 110–111) suggests that it was used in conjunction with the *[[Mir'at al-muhakkikin|Mirʾāt al-muḥaqqiqīn]]*, and that Raniri treated the two works as complementary expositions of the Wujūdiyyah position that he sought to refute.

## Raniri's refutation

Raniri's citations of the *Chirkah* occur in the central section of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] on the Wujūdiyyah (pp. 108, 110–111). The citations form part of a sustained polemic against Shamsuddin of Pasai as the continuator of Hamzah Pansuri. Van Nieuwenhuijze, in his dissertation *Sjamsu'l-Dīn van Pasai* (Leiden, 1945), made the *Chirkah* the object of a special study, in which he proposed both the reading *Ḥarakah* and a number of conjectural emendations to the text as transmitted by Raniri.

## Significance

The *Chirkah* is one of the principal surviving works of [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] and one of the principal sources for the Wujūdiyyah mysticism of [[Acheh]] in its golden age. Its refutation by Raniri in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] is one of the chief documents of the religious controversy that defined the transition from the age of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] to that of [[Iskandar Thani]] and [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Taj al-Ālam]]. The uncertainty over the very title of the work — *Chirkah* or *Ḥarakah* — is a measure of the difficulty of the textual tradition of Malay Sufism, in which works survive chiefly in the citations of their opponents.

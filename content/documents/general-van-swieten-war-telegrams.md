**General van Swieten War Telegrams** were the official telegraphic dispatches transmitted by Commander-in-Chief General [[J. van Swieten]] from the Acehnese front to the [[Dutch East Indies Government]] in [[Batavia]] during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in December [[1873]] and January [[1874]].

## Content and Optimistic Predictions

Following the occupation of [[Kraton of Aceh]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], General [[J. van Swieten]] sent a series of telegrams via [[Singkel]] and Penang, notably on [[28 January]] and [[2 February]] [[1874]]. These dispatches stated: "Everything leads to the belief that the struggle is ended and the submission of the 25 and 26 Sagies is near."

As explained in the [[1876]] study *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later*, these telegrams were based on intelligence provided by trusted indigenous allies, including [[Teuku Nek of Meuraxa]], and letters from regional rulers such as the author of the [[Raja of Gighen Letter of Submission]].

## Public Controversy and Impact of the Telegraph

When general submission failed to materialize and Acehnese leaders like [[Panglima Polem]] renewed their resistance, critics in the Netherlands and [[Batavia]] seized upon the [[General van Swieten War Telegrams]] to accuse [[J. van Swieten]] of incompetence and false optimism. 

The author of *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* argued that the phenomenon illustrated the disruptive impact of [[Telegraph Communication in the Aceh War]], as instant telegraphic reporting created unrealistic expectations in the public before comprehensive written reports arrived by sea.

**The krijgsorder for the VI, XXII and XXVI Moekims** was a Dutch order of battle for operations in the [[VI Moekims]], [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] on which, according to [[H. van Kol]], the governor and military commander [[Karel van der Heijden|Van der Heyden]] wrote in pencil in the margin the words *"er worden geen krijgsgevangenen gemaakt"* — no prisoners of war are to be taken.

## The allegation

[[H. van Kol|Van Kol]] made the charge in his reply of [[17 November]] [[1897]] in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]], in a passage cataloguing breaches of the *[[Lois et coutumes de la guerre|lois et coutumes de la guerre]]* (laws and customs of war). In the same sentence he alleged that [[Karel van der Heijden|Van der Heyden]] had caused [[Njahim]] to be shot though innocent, and that his order *"kassi mandie!"* was so cruel that Van Kol preferred not to communicate its meaning, "om geen kreet van afschuw in Nederland te doen opgaan" (so as not to raise a cry of horror in the Netherlands).

## Legal context invoked

Van Kol set the order against the articles of the [[Brussels Conference of 1874|Conference of Brussels of 1874]], which he quoted: *"il est interdit de mutiler ou de tuer un ennemi qui est hors de combat, et de déclarer d'avance qu'on ne fait pas de quartier, même si l'on ne le réclame pas pour soi-même" — it is forbidden to mutilate or to kill an enemy who is out of the fight, and to declare in advance that no quarter will be given* — it is forbidden to mutilate or kill an enemy who is *hors de combat*, and to declare in advance that no quarter will be given, even if one does not claim it for oneself. He supported this with the *Dictionnaire général de la politique* of M. Block, p. 1165: *"tuer un ennemi qui se rend, ou qui est impuissant à se défendre est un crime déshonorant. Il en est de même des attentats commis sur des femmes et les enfants en pays ennemi." — to kill an enemy who surrenders, or who is powerless to defend himself, is a dishonouring crime. It is the same with assaults committed on women and children in an enemy country.*

The pamphlet's footnotes render both passages into Dutch for the reader.

## Historiographical caution

The allegation rests on Van Kol's parliamentary statement alone; the order itself was not produced in the chamber, and neither the Minister [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] nor any other speaker confirmed or contested this specific document. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] replied generally that he fully endorsed Van Kol's condemnation of cruelties "indien metterdaad die feiten zoo zijn" (if indeed those facts are so), and that no member of any party and no Dutch Minister of Colonies would hesitate to condemn such acts if concretely and clearly proved.

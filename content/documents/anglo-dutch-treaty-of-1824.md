**Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824** was a bilateral agreement concluded on [[7 March]] [[1824]] in London that delineated spheres of influence between the **[[British Empire]]** and the **[[Dutch East Indies]]** in Southeast Asia. Under its terms, the Dutch ceded control of **[[Benkoelen]]** and **[[Padang]]** to the British, while the British recognised Dutch dominance over the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]** and the surrounding archipelago. The treaty also formalised British possession of **[[Singapore]]** and **[[Labuan]]**, shaping the colonial map that framed later Dutch military actions on the west coast of **[[Borneo]]**.

## Provisions Relevant to Aceh

- Recognition of Dutch authority over the northern Sumatra coast, including Aceh.
- Exchange of territories that altered trade routes and smuggling patterns affecting Aceh’s hinterland.

## Impact

The treaty’s re‑allocation of **[[Benkoelen]]** and **[[Padang]]** reduced direct Dutch‑British friction in Sumatra, allowing the Dutch to focus on suppressing piracy and smuggling that threatened Aceh’s trade, and later to launch the protracted **[[Aceh War]]**.

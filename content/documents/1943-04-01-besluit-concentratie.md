**The August 1884 Concentration Decisions** were a series of orders issued by the Governor-General of Netherlands Indies on [[19 August]] [[1884]] that fundamentally reorganized Dutch military and civil administration in [[Atjeh]]. The decisions marked the end of the civil administration experiment and the adoption of a comprehensive concentration strategy.

## Context

By August 1884, the Dutch position in [[Groot-Atjeh]] had become untenable. The scattered posts established during offensive operations required constant reinforcement to maintain, while the surrounding territory remained effectively outside Dutch control. The oorlogspartij (war party) was gaining strength, and the cost in lives and money was escalating.

## Key Decisions

### 1. Abolition of Civil Administration

The civiel bestuur (civil administration) that had operated for over three years despite repeated criticism was abolished. This ended the experiment with separating civil and military authority.

### 2. Reunification of Authority

Civil and military authority was reunited in the hands of a single Militaire Gouverneur (Military Governor)—[[Colonel H. Demmeni]].

### 3. Abolition of Controllers

The kontroleurs (controllers) in the sub-districts of Groot-Atjeh were abolished. The functions of the Assistant-Resident on the West Coast (stationed at [[Melaboeh]]) and the controller at [[Segli]] were transferred to the local military commanders.

### 4. Withdrawal of Northern Appointments

The Assistant-Resident on the North and East Coast (stationed at [[Telok-Semawé]]) and the controller of [[Samalanga]] were abolished, along with the military garrisons at both places.

### 5. Definition of New Position

A new geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position) was defined, within which Dutch territory would be bounded.

### 6. Withdrawal Schedule

Posts outside the new line would be gradually evacuated. Several field battalions would return to Java.

### 7. Deadline

The occupation of the new position and evacuation of external posts was to be completed by [[1 March]] [[1885]].

## Implementation

The decisions were implemented under the supervision of [[Colonel H. Demmeni]], who oversaw the construction of the new fortifications and the evacuation of the old posts. By [[1 March]] [[1885]], the concentration was complete.

## Significance

The August 1884 decisions marked a strategic turning point in the [[Aceh War]]. They represented acceptance that the offensive strategy had failed and that a defensive consolidation was necessary. The decisions also demonstrated that the colonial administration could reverse course when circumstances required.

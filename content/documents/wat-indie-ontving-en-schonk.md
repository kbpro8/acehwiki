**Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk** — Dutch for "What the Indies Received and Gave" — is a collected volume on the cultural, religious, educational, agricultural, mining and economic history of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Dutch East Indies]], published in [[1946]] under the auspices of the *Indisch Instituut* (Indies Institute) by *Wereldbibliotheek N.V.* in Amsterdam. The volume was compiled and edited under the leadership of Dr. [[C. W. Wormser|Wormser]] with the collaboration of fourteen specialist contributors, and appeared with ninety-one illustrations (*met 91 afbeeldingen*). For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader the volume is significant chiefly for its chapters on the *oudste cultuurstroomen* (oldest cultural streams), the *invloed van den Islam* (influence of Islam), the *bestuursvormen* (forms of government), and *Indië in de wereldhuishouding* (the Indies in the world economy), each of which digests Atjeh-related material from the standard colonial scholarship of the first half of the twentieth century, above all the work of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] and [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

## Contents and structure

The table of contents (*Inhoud*) groups the contributions into ten thematic sections, each by a different author. The chapters most directly relevant to Atjeh are:

- *De oudste cultuurstreamen* by Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]] (pp. 7–55) — prehistoric cultural streams, including the Sumatra-bijl (*Sumatra axe*) finds in northern Sumatra and the [[Atjeh]] provenance of one of the Bacson-Hoabinh culture centres.
- *De invloed van den Islam* by Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]] (pp. 56–108) — the influence of Islam, with substantial Atjeh material on the [[Meunasah]], [[Teungku|Teungkoe]], [[Rangkang]], the [[Meuseugit Raya|Meuseugit Raja]] mosque of [[Kutaradja|Koeta Radja]], the *idja pinggang* sartorial marker, the [[Soerat te'h]] divination book, the [[Pen (Atjeh)|*pen* concept]] in ear-piercing and gravestone ceremonies, the *ratib* of mid-Ramadan, the *kadar*-night preference for the 27th, the *pitrah* and *djakat* collections, the [[Boeròng|*boeròng*]] poentianak-spirit, the [[Toean di boh|*Toean di boh*]] fertility saint, and the Atjeh proverb on [[Adat and Hokm Atjeh proverb|*adat* and *hokm*]].
- *De bestuursvormen* by Mr. Dr. [[L. J. J. Caron|Caron]] (pp. 186–203) — the [[Korte Verklaring|*korte verklaring*]] policy of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] first applied in Atjeh, the [[Proeve-Oppenheim]] draft *staatsregeling*, and the [[Carpentier Alting commission (1918)|Carpentier Alting commission]] of [[1918]].
- *De landbouw* by Dr. [[G. J. van Hall|Van Hall]] (pp. 204–230) — pepper (*Piper nigrum*) cultivation in Atjeh, identified as already important in the sixteenth century and still so in [[1946]].
- *Mijnbouw* by Ir. [[F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]] (pp. 231–262) — the petroleum industry of Sumatra's East Coast (Langkat, [[Deli]] etc.), whose development altered the strategic significance of the [[Atjeh]] hinterland.
- *Indië in de wereldhuishouding* by Ir. [[W. J. Utermark|Utermark]] (pp. 263–) — names [[Sabang]] as the northernmost point of Sumatra and a key port in the archipelago's sea-lanes.

## Treatment of Atjeh

The volume is not a monograph on Atjeh; rather, Atjeh appears repeatedly as one of the regions through which the wider story of the Indies is told. This makes the book a useful conspectus of how Atjeh was situated in Dutch colonial scholarship immediately after the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese occupation]] and on the eve of Indonesian independence. The chapter on Islam, in particular, draws so heavily on Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjéhers* and Kreemer's *Atjèh* that it functions as a mid-twentieth-century digest of those works, surfacing numerous Atjeh-specific items — the [[Meuseugit Raya|Meuseugit Raja]] in [[Kutaradja]] built by the Dutch to replace the mosque burned in [[1873]], Snouck Hurgronje's verdict on it as *\"een ledig monument onzer dwaasheid\"* (an empty monument of our folly), the [[Teungku Anjong]] *kanduri* on the 18th of Rejab, the Atjeh preference for the 27th night of Ramadhan as *Lailat al-Qadar* (against Java's preference for the 21st), and the proverb *\"de adat is als het binden van het dier, de hukum als het snijden\"* (adat is like binding the beast, *hukum* like slaughtering it).

## Significance

As a document, *Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk* is itself a primary source for the state of Dutch colonial knowledge about the Indies at the end of the colonial period. Its heavy reliance on Snouck Hurgronje and Kreemer for Atjeh material shows that, even in [[1946]], the standard scholarly frame for Atjeh was still the one established in the 1890s–1920s. Its treatment of Atjeh is also revealing of what the editor and contributors considered worth retaining: ritual life, *adat* sayings, mosque politics, and the Van Heutsz–Snouck Hurgronje administrative reforms — rather than the long Atjeh War itself, which is barely mentioned.

**The Proclamation of the Regeeringscommissaris of 12 April 1896** — reproduced as *Bijlage III* and again as Annex III/V of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the first of two proclamations issued by [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] to the the Greater Acehnese heads and population, making known the purpose of his arrival in [[Aceh]] following the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]]. The proclamation was issued on or about [[10 April]]–[[12 April]] [[1896]], immediately preceding the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of Biloel and Lam Koenjit on 12 April 1896]] and the publication of Umar's [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar (12 April 1896)|Second Letter]] of the same date. It established the political framework within which the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] would conduct the next two-and-a-half months of military operations.

## Background

[[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] arrived in [[Aceh]] aboard the steamers *Carpentier* and *Bantam* on [[7 April]] [[1896]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] (Government Commissioner), with full civil and military authority over [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]]. He had been sent in direct response to Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], which had collapsed the outer line, triggered the sieges of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]], and threatened to undo the work of three years of Dutch-native alliance. Upon arrival he [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|honorably relieved Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] of his functions as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] and assumed the unified command himself.

Within hours of his arrival Vetter had to make critical operational decisions: whether to reinforce the besieged posts of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]], whether to lift the entire outer line, and how to communicate the new political situation to the Acehnese population. The [[Geheim schrijven (secret operational instruction) from Regeeringscommissaris|geheim schrijven (secret operational instruction)]] he issued to [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] before [[8 April]] [[1896]] set out the military directives for the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of 8 April 1896]]. The Proclamation of [[12 April]] [[1896]] set out the corresponding political framework. Kruisheer dates the issue to around [[10 April]]–[[12 April]] [[1896]] — that is, after the relief of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] but before or simultaneous with their evacuation.

## Content

The proclamation was addressed to the *Groot-Atjehsche hoofden en bevolking* — the territorial chiefs, war leaders, prayer leaders, clerics, and the general population of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Its substantive content was the announcement of the purpose of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]]'s arrival in [[Aceh]]. Kruisheer's text records that the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] "made known the purpose of his arrival" — a phrase that, in the colonial-administrative register, signalled the assumption of full civil and military authority, the supersession of the previous [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Governor]], and the intent to restore the colonial position by force.

The proclamation is preserved in Kruisheer's *Bijlagen* under two numbers (III and V) because of the structure of the balso's appendices: *Bijlage III* contains the first proclamation, and *Bijlage V* contains it again, in the chronological sequence of the political correspondence around Umar (where it precedes Umar's [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar (12 April 1896)|Second Letter]] and the [[Regeeringscommissaris]]'s reply of [[25 April]] [[1896]]). The same text appears twice because Kruisheer wished both the proclamation sequence (III, VII) and the Umar correspondence sequence (II, IV, V, VI) to be readable as continuous narratives.

The proclamation was followed immediately by the operational order for the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of Biloel and Lam Koenjit on 12 April 1896]]. The [[Regeeringscommissaris]]'s reasoning for the evacuation was that the original purposes of the posts — protection of the [[IX Moekims|IX]] and [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] against raids from the [[Lamkrak Moekims|four Lamkrak mukims]], and prevention of evildoers settling along the old road from [[Biloel]] to [[Boekit Daroe]] — had lost all reason for existence under the current circumstances: the [[IX Moekims]] had proven as unfavorably disposed as the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] that had defected with Umar.

## Reception and Aftermath

The immediate operational consequence of the proclamation was the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of Biloel and Lam Koenjit on 12 April 1896]]. A force under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]], with *Chef van den Staf* [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Lt.-Col. Van Vliet]] and the colonnes of the 3e, 9e, and 14e Bataljons, assembled in the early morning, relieved both posts without significant combat, and oversaw the burning of both posts by midday. The [[buitenlinie]] continued to be progressively abandoned in the following days — [[Ana' Galong|Anak-Galoeng]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Soet]] and [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]], and [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]].

The political framework announced in the proclamation structured the next phase of the war. After the exchange of letters with Umar (the [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar (12 April 1896)|Second]] of [[12 April]] [[1896]] and the [[Third Letter of Teuku Umar (23 April 1896)|Third]] of [[23 April]] [[1896]]), the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] dismissed Umar by [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] and issued the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|second proclamation of 27 April 1896]] announcing military operations against him. The [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|Bombardment of Lampisang]] began the same day.

## Significance

The Proclamation of [[12 April]] [[1896]] is the founding document of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] ([[April]]–[[June]] [[1896]]). Its announcement of the purpose of Vetter's arrival signalled the transition from the native-alliance policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]] to a phase of direct Dutch military command. The same-day evacuation of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] demonstrated that the new political framework would be accompanied by immediate operational consequences: the abandonment of the outer line and the concentration of forces around [[Koeta Radja]]. As a historical document, the proclamation is preserved twice in *ATJEH '96* precisely because it stands at the intersection of two narratives — the proclamation sequence and the Umar correspondence sequence — that together constitute the political record of the crisis.

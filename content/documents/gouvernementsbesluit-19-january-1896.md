**The Government Resolution of 19 January 1896, No. 9** (*Gouvernementsbesluit van 19 Januari 1896, No. 9*) — preserved among the official documents gathered in the Annexes of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the decree of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Government of Nederlandsch-Indië]] that appointed [[Teuku Umar]] (as *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*) as territorial chief of the territory of [[Lepong]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The resolution, issued only two months before Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], was the highest formal recognition Umar received from the Dutch — the conferral of an actual territorial office alongside his military commission as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] — and was, by consequence, one of the two offices formally revoked by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] dismissing him from government service.

## Background

Since the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Government Resolution of 5 September 1893]] and the [[Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893|Oath of Loyalty of 30 September 1893]], Umar had served as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government — a military commission carrying authority over auxiliary forces but no fixed territory of its own. The landschap of [[Lepong]], on the west coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], had long been a centre of Umar's personal power and the seat of his household. The conferral of the *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] on Umar was the logical extension of his existing position: it gave him formal territorial jurisdiction in addition to his military commission, and it bound the territory of [[Lepong]] itself to the Dutch-alliance system.

The resolution was issued on [[19 January]] [[1896]], at a moment when the Dutch were already committed to a substantial reorganisation of the [[buitenlinie]] (outer line) and the posts of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The [[Commandantenorder 12 Februari 1896 No. 22|Commandant's order No. 22 of 12 February 1896]] appointed a new commission under the chief of the regional staff to determine emplacements for new fortifications, and on [[12 March]] [[1896]] the [[Regeering]] approved the proposal for the organisation of the new stelling. Umar was present at the [[15 March]] [[1896]] conference at the Governor's office on the proposed [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] operation. On [[26 March]] [[1896]] he received his final issue of weapons, munitions, opium, and money from the Dutch — and defected three days later.

## Content

The *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[19 January]] [[1896]] appointed *Toekoe Oemar* — under the formal name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* — as territorial chief of the territory of [[Lepong]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The conferral of the *hoeloebalang* office was a significant promotion: it gave Umar formal jurisdiction over a defined territory — including the right to collect taxes, administer justice, and raise auxiliary forces within [[Lepong]] — alongside his existing military commission as [[Panglima Prang Besar]].

The text of the resolution is preserved in Kruisheer's *Bijlagen* alongside the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893 No. 1 geheim]], the [[Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893|Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893]], the [[Government Telegram of 26 April 1896, No. 409|Government Telegram of 26 April 1896 No. 409]], and the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]]. The grouping is significant: Kruisheer presents the five documents together as the complete legal record of Umar's appointment, oath, second appointment, dismissal-authorisation, and dismissal.

## Reception and Aftermath

The conferral of the *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] did not, in practice, secure Umar's loyalty. Within two months he had defected, taking with him the Dutch-supplied arms and turning them against his patrons. The *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] became, after the defection, the territorial title by which Umar's authority in the west-coast *landschap* was understood — and the office the Dutch most wanted to revoke. The [[Government Telegram of 26 April 1896, No. 409|Government Telegram of 26 April 1896 No. 409]] sent to [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris Vetter]] ordered Umar's dismissal from *both* offices — *Panglima Prang Besar* and *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] — before commencing military action against him. The [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] carried out that order and stipulated that Umar would henceforth be officially designated by his old name *Toekoe Oemar*. The renaming of [[5 September]] [[1893]] and the elevation of [[19 January]] [[1896]] were thus simultaneously undone.

## Significance

The *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[19 January]] [[1896]] is the highest formal recognition Umar received from the Dutch — the only one that conferred actual territorial jurisdiction alongside military rank. Its proximity to the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] (only two months intervening) makes it a particularly painful document in the colonial record: the Government was, at the very moment of greatest commitment, on the verge of being deceived. As a historical document, the resolution is essential to understanding the legal structure of the Dutch-Umar alliance and the legal structure of its dissolution: the *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] was the second of the two offices whose revocation the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] declared.

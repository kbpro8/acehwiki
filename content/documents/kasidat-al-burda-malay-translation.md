**Kasidat al‑Burda (Malay translation)** was a 16th‑century Malay rendering of the celebrated Arabic poem *Al‑Burda* by the Sufi saint [[Imam al‑Busiri]], adapted for use in [[Aceh]]’s devotional practice. The translation survives in the Dutch‑Malay manuscript DLP‑54, pages [[73]]‑[[79]], and exemplifies how Arabic theological terminology was incorporated into Acehnese religious literature.

## Context

The original *Al‑Burda* (the “Poem of the Mantle”) was composed in the 13th century in Arabic and quickly became a staple of Islamic devotional poetry across the Malay world. By the early modern period, Acehnese scholars and poets had produced a Malay version to make the text accessible to the local faithful, who spoke a variant of the [[Malay language]] heavily influenced by Arabic loanwords.

## Content and Structure

The manuscript presents the poem in a line‑by‑line Malay translation, preserving the original’s rhymed couplets while inserting explanatory glosses for Arabic terms such as *achirat* (the afterlife), *rasulullah* (the messenger of God), and *badr* (the battle of Badr). The translation also includes marginal notes in Dutch, indicating the colonial interest in Acehnese religious texts.

## Language and Lexical Features

The text illustrates a rich intermingling of Malay, Arabic, and Dutch lexical items. Arabic terms appear in italics and are often followed by a Malay gloss, e.g. *achirat* (*the other world*), *rasulullah* (*the messenger*), and *badr* (*the battle of Badr*). Dutch annotations provide transliteration guidance for readers unfamiliar with Arabic script.

## Significance

The Malay translation of the *Burda* demonstrates the depth of Islamic literary culture in [[Aceh]] and its role in shaping local religious identity. It also serves as a primary source for scholars studying the transmission of Arabic religious concepts into Southeast Asian languages.

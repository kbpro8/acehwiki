**Swart's letters to Schmidt ([[1909]]-1913)** was the private letters of [[H. N. A. Swart]] to [[H. J. Schmidt]] of [[26 December]] [[1909]], [[26 September]] [[1911]], [[9 December]] [[1911]] and [[10 August]] [[1913]], printed in extract by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]].

They are, du Croo wrote, "characteristic both of the manner in which Swart corresponded privately with his most trusted collaborators and of certain frictions with the army command, which in fact ended only when Swart became lieutenant-general." The letter of [[26 December]] [[1909]] promises [[T. Radja Moeda Seudagan]] six breech-loaders when his *koeta* is ready and an advance of two or three hundred guilders, and instructs Schmidt to end the existence of [[T. Dagang]] and his *panglima* [[Pang Tjoet Bén]] at [[Tangsé]], then to go to [[Geumpang]] against [[Keudjroeën Leupoe]] and his sons. That of [[26 September]] [[1911]] reports the interview with the new Governor-General [[Alexander Willem Frederik Idenburg|Idenburg]] at [[Sabang]] on [[14 December]], the farewell to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] on [[22 December]], the death of [[Tgk. Ma'at]] and the instruction to [[Scheurleer]] concerning [[Pang Bintang]], and offers Schmidt [[Tgk. di Barat]] as his next object. That of [[9 December]] [[1911]] concerns his own promotion. Du Croo defended the practice by citing Idenburg's letter to van Heutsz of [[15 September]] [[1908]]: "the advantage of private correspondence is that it is personal and that one can say exactly what one thinks about a matter."

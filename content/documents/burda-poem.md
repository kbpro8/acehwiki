**Burda (poem)** is a 13‑stanza Arabic ode composed by [[Cheikh al‑Būsīrī]] in the 13th century that praises the Prophet [[Muhammad]] and is traditionally recited for spiritual benefit and protection. The 16th‑century Malay translation records that the *Burda* was incorporated into the religious life of the Sultanate of [[Acheh]], where it was sung during the 15th day of [[Ramadan]] and used as an amulet against illness, fire, and shipwreck.

## Ritual Use in Aceh
According to the source, the Ottoman custodians of the Prophet’s cloak would present a water‑soaked cloth to distinguished members of the Acehnese royal household after the annual unveiling. The *Burda* itself was also distributed in bottled form for consumption during Ramadan meals, indicating a direct material link between the poem and Acehnese courtly practice.

## Literary Legacy
The poem inspired a vast body of commentaries, translations, and “takhmis” (expansions) across the Muslim world, including the Malay version examined here. Its verses were treated as a powerful amulet in North Africa and in Aceh, where they were believed to protect houses from fire and ships from wreckage.

**Staatsblad 1919 No. 61 jo No. 281** was the Netherlands-Indies regulation governing the *pachtschat* — the rent, *canon* or quit-rent — together with the *vastrecht* (fixed due) and *cijns* (quit-rent) payable by the holders of land and mining concessions, in which the Atjehnese self-governing rulers were assigned a substantial share.

## Relevance to Atjehnese land law

[[D. W. N. de Boer]] recorded that the exercise of the *[[Beschikkingsrecht in Atjeh|beschikkingsrecht]]* as an emolument of the sovereign — and with it the regulation and fixing, through the mediation of the Netherlands administration, of the several *zelfbestuur* (self-government) boundaries — became genuinely important only when European capital began to interest itself in these lands, and the right of avail promised to become for the self-governing rulers "een Delische bron van inkomsten" — a Deli-like source of income — on account of the not inconsiderable share allotted to them in the *pachtschat*, *vastrecht* and *cijns* of land and mining concessions under this Staatsblad. He referred also to *[[Politiek Beleid en Bestuurszorg in de Buitenbezittingen|Politiek Beleid en Bestuurszorg in de Buitenbezittingen]]*, Second Part B, chapter IV, p. 113 ff.

## Division of the revenues

For [[Aceh|Atjeh]] the division of those revenues was:

- six-tenths for the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement]];
- four-tenths for the *[[Landschap (Atjehnese)|landschap]]*, of which seven-tenths for the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]* and three-tenths for the *[[Landschapskassen|landschapskas]]*.

## Significance

The regulation converted a customary prerogative over waste land into a share of modern concession rents, and thereby gave the Atjehnese self-governing rulers a direct financial interest in the demarcation of their boundaries — the principal cause, in De Boer's account, of boundary disputes between the *zelfbesturen* (self-governing territories).

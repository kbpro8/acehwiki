**Van Oerbosch tot Cultuurgebied: Een Schets van de Beteekenis van de Tabak, de Andere Cultures en de Industrie ter Oostkust van Sumatra** (*From Primeval Forest to Cultivation Area: A Sketch of the Significance of Tobacco, Other Crops, and Industry on Sumatra's East Coast*) is a historical and economic survey published in [[1928]] by Dr. T. Volker and issued by the [[Deli Planters Vereeniging]] in [[Medan]]. The work covers the agricultural and commercial transformation of [[Sumatra]]'s East Coast from its condition as uncharted jungle in [[1863]] to its position by [[1927]] as the second most economically productive region in the Dutch colonial empire. A copy is held at the Universiteitsbibliotheek Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam under shelfmark 09383.

## Purpose and Audience

Volker wrote the book to correct what he regarded as widespread ignorance among Dutch business and public circles about the East Coast's importance and character. His preface recounts telling anecdotes of Java planters dismissing Deli's achievements and rubber men deploring its supposed backwardness — attitudes he attributes to the fact that the Oostkust, unlike Java, had no prior scholarly literature popularizing its story. The work explicitly disclaims scientific pretension, describing itself as intended purely for *"publiciteit"* (public information).

## Structure

The book is divided into two *hoofdstukken* (main chapters):
- **Chapter I** — a historical overview of the East Coast's development from [[1863]] to [[1927]], covering the pioneer period, the establishment of company structures, relations with the Acehnese and Batak populations, the pacification of the frontier, the petroleum industry, and successive crises and recoveries in tobacco prices.
- **Chapter II** — a contemporary survey of the East Coast's economic situation in [[1927]], with statistical data on output, labour, capital investment, and the diversity of cultures and industries.

## Key Historical Narrative

The book traces the transformation from a thinly populated coast of Malay river-mouth settlements and Batak interior villages into the most intensively cultivated plantation region outside Java. Volker identifies the following milestones:

- **[[1863]]**: [[Jacobus Nienhuys]] plants the first tobacco crop at the mouth of the [[Deli River]]. The first harvest ([[1864]]) yields 50 bales for ƒ 2,542.
- **[[1869]]**: The *[[Deli Maatschappij]]* — the first *naamloze vennootschap* in the Indies — is co-founded by Nienhuys, Janssen, Clemen, and the [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij|Handel Maatschappij]].
- **[[1871]]**: Nienhuys repatriates; J. T. Cremer becomes the leading figure.
- **[[23 April]] [[1879]]**: The *[[Deli Planters Vereeniging]]* (DPV) is founded on Cremer's initiative; its first board consists of J. T. Cremer, A. Breker, and Jhr. H. J. L. Leyssius.
- **[[1876]]**: Acehnese raiders attack the plantations [[Glen Bervi]], [[Tamaram]], and [[Ludwigsburg]]; the manager of [[Tamaram]] is wounded and an assistant killed.
- **[[1887]]**: The [[Radja of Langkat]], who has resisted Acehnese raiders, is elevated to *Sultan* in recognition of his services.
- **[[1892]]**: The decision to mount a punitive expedition into [[Tamiang]] is taken.
- **[[1893]]**: The [[Expedition against Tamiang (1893)|Tamiang Expedition]] is carried out with "not insignificant losses"; the [[Tamiang Monument|Tamiang Monument in Medan]] is erected at the end of the year to commemorate the fallen.
- **[[1899]]**: The Oostkust is declared pacified from Acehnese threat.

Volker also records the steady growth of the petroleum industry near [[Pangkalan Brandan]], begun with the first test-borings by Zijlker in [[1883]] and resulting in the formation of the *Koninklijke* in [[1890]].

## Labour and Social Statistics

The book provides detailed data on the imported labour force that sustained the plantation economy. By [[31 December]] [[1927]], around 28,000 Chinese and 59,500 Javanese (together with 7,700 workers of other origins such as Bantamese, Banjarese, and British Indians) worked in the East Coast cultures. The book also records the financial flows that immigration generated: between [[1888]] and [[1927]], repatriating workers remitted ƒ 7,716,000 in money orders, with additional annual remittances averaging ƒ 96,000 (older workers) and ƒ 100,000 (new arrivals) per year.

## Significance

*Van Oerbosch tot Cultuurgebied* is the principal contemporary account of the formative period of the East Sumatran plantation economy written from within the planter community. Its value as a source lies in its combination of economic statistics — drawn from DPV records and official data — with a narrative that preserves the memory of individual pioneer figures such as [[Jacobus Nienhuys]] and J. T. Cremer, the institutional history of the [[Deli Planters Vereeniging]], and the security context provided by [[Acehnese raids on East Sumatra|Acehnese raiding]] and [[Batak]] resistance. Published the year after Nienhuys's death at 91, it serves partly as a retrospective tribute to the founding generation.

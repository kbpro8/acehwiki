**Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis** (*A Beautiful Page from Atjeh's History*) was a Dutch military campaign history written by two officers of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]] — [[W. Cool]], Kapitein der Genie, and [[G. B. Hooijer]], Kapitein der Infanterie O.I., Ridder der [[Militaire Willems-Orde]] 4de klasse — with a [[Voorwoord of Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Van der Heijden, June 1889)|foreword]] by Generaal [[General van der Heijden|K. van der Heijden]], and published by [[M. Nijhoff|Martinus Nijhoff]] at 's-Gravenhage in [[1889]], accompanied by the chromolithographed [[Schetskaart van het boven-stroomgebied der Atjeh-rivier (1889)|sketch map of the upper basin of the Atjeh river]]. The book narrates, as a continuous eyewitness account compiled from a field diary kept during the march, the expedition of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|column]] under Luitenant-Kolonel [[Majoor Gerlach|Gerlach]] from its embarkation at [[Oleh-leh]] on [[26 July]] [[1879]] through the landing at [[Lampanas]], the occupation of the valley of [[Lamtoba]], the [[Punitive expedition against Kepaja Doewa (August 1879)|punitive expedition against Kepaja Doewa]], the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|march to Selimoen]] of [[13 August]]–[[15 August]] [[1879]], the return and the ten days' quarantine at Lampanas, to the final re-embarkation of [[2 September|2]]–[[4 September]] [[1879]].

## Character and method of the work

The authors state that the narrative was composed from the diary kept during the tocht itself — "Is uit dit gedurende den tocht nangehouden dagboek, dat onze beschrijving is opgeteekend" — the daily journal from which the description was recorded. They consciously addressed themselves to the non-military reader (*niet-militaire lezers*), explaining in footnotes such technicalities as the order of march of a [[Colonne|column]] in mountain terrain, the strength of an advance guard, or the difference between European and Indies marching speeds — noting that the ten *palen* (roughly three hours) covered on the approach to [[Lamtoba]] was "eene geheele andere marschsnelheid dus als waarop in de Europeesche oorlogen gerekend wordt" — a wholly different marching speed from that reckoned in European wars.

The narrative is deliberately double-sided. Alongside the triumph — the moment at [[Selimoen]] when the column heard the *Wilhelmus van Nassauwen* played by the band of Majoor [[Majoor Lubeck|Lubeck]]'s column already in occupation — the authors set the cost: the sickness statistics by which, of 2,234 men, 769 were dead, evacuated or unfit for service after one month, and the description of the fever refugees on the pier of [[Oleh-leh]] where "niemand en niets" welcomed the returning victors. The book closes with an appeal for public support of the Indisch leger: "Stel dat leger in staat zijn schoone en eervolle, maar zware taak te volbrengen" — enable that army to accomplish its beautiful and honourable, but heavy task — the protection of the Indies territory with its thirty million souls.

## The foreword by Van der Heijden

The volume opens with a [[Voorwoord of Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Van der Heijden, June 1889)|dedicatory letter]] from Generaal K. van der Heijden, dated [[Bronbeek]], June [[1889]], commending the work as recalling "eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis," praising its geographical data on the interior of [[Groot-Atjeh]] "die men elders te vergeefs zoekt," and correcting one identification: the [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed|Toekoe Moeda Daud]] named in the book, head of Selimoen, "een der pretendenten voor den Sultanstroon," who submitted to him on [[20 August]] [[1879]], was not the then-current Sultan-pretendant, whose coming had been announced by [[Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet|Toekoe Ajer-Alang]] on the same date but who had excused himself on account of the approaching *poeasa* (the fasting month).

## Sources and antecedents

The book's strategic criticism — of the removal of Van der Heijden, the splitting of military and civil authority under his civil successor, and the loss of "de vrucht van jaren strijds en stroomen bloeds en millioenen schats" — the authors declare to have been treated more fully in their earlier articles, *[[Het Atjeh-vraagstuk (Cool & Hooijer, Militaire Spectator 1886)|het Atjeh-vraagstuk]]* in the Militaire Spectator of [[1886]]. A footnote cites, for a kindred observation on bivouac morale in rain, a description in the Indisch Militair Tijdschrift of [[1888]] of the [[1865]] expedition on the Oostkust of Sumatra against Siak led by Majoor Vink.

The surviving copy consulted for the [[Aceh Wiki History]] bears the stamps of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor de Tropen]], Centrale Boekerij, Amsterdam, and shelf-marks of the Institute's Tropen-bibliotheek; the printer's imprint is the Fluid-Hollandsche Boek- en Handelsdrukkerij.

## Significance

The book is the principal eyewitness narrative of the closing operations of the first phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the operations that Generaal van der Heijden's [[Van der Heijden's day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order of 5 September 1879]] declared to have brought "geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt." Together with [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s later *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]* ([[1897]]), it fixes this author as the war's most important personal chronicler of the years [[1879]] and [[1893]]–[[1896]]. Its detailed topographical account of the upper [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]] valley — worked into the accompanying [[Schetskaart van het boven-stroomgebied der Atjeh-rivier (1889)|schetskaart]] — answered, in Van der Heijden's judgement, the hitherto unsolved question of where and how the Atjeh river valley ends.

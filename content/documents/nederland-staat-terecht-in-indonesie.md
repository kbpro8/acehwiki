***Nederland staat terecht in Indonesië: Achtergronden van de processen tegen Nederlanders in Djakarta*** ("The Netherlands stands trial in Indonesia: Background to the proceedings against Dutch nationals in Djakarta") was a book by H. C. Beynon published in [[1957]] by A. W. Bruna & Zoon in [[Utrecht]], with a foreword by [[Prof. Mr. J. M. van Bemmelen]], documenting the politically motivated trials of Dutch nationals in [[Djakarta]] between [[1953]] and [[1956]] and arguing that the proceedings reflected the broader failure of Dutch postcolonial policy toward [[Indonesia]].

## Subject and argument

The book's central argument, announced in its title, is that it was not the accused Dutch nationals — principally [[H. C. J. G. Schmidt|Schmidt]] and [[Leon Jungschläger|Jungschläger]] — who stood in the dock in Djakarta, but Dutch colonial and postcolonial policy itself. Beynon traces the origins of the so-called **NIGO** (*Nederlands Indische Guerilla Organisatie*) affair — an alleged Dutch underground conspiracy used as the pretext for the arrests — to a combination of Indonesian domestic politics under the cabinet of [[Ali Sastroamidjojo]] and a calculated anti-Dutch propaganda campaign.

The book covers the political context of the trials in detail: the rise of the [[Darul Islam]] movement and its Acehnese branch under [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Daud Beureueh]], which broke out in open rebellion on [[20 September]] [[1953]] following the installation of the Ali Sastroamidjojo cabinet on [[1 August]] [[1953]]; the Indonesian domestic political crisis of [[1954]]–[[1955]], including the controversy over the expulsion of Chinese nationals; and the attempts to discredit and disbar the Dutch defense lawyers [[Mr. H. A. Bouman]] and [[Mr. Nany Razak]].

## The NIGO trials

Beynon documents the arrests beginning with Schmidt on [[18 December]] [[1953]] and Jungschläger on [[27 January]] [[1954]], the fabricated testimony of crown witnesses including Tomasoa and Manoch, the removal of skeptical judge [[Liem|Mr. G. Kh. Liem]], and the campaign to eliminate effective defense. Jungschläger died in a Djakarta hospital on [[19 April]] [[1956]] before the verdict was delivered. The book also reproduces key primary sources including Mr. Bouman's letters to the minister of justice and a letter by prosecution auxiliary Broeks that revealed the planned entrapment of the defense lawyers.

## The Aceh dimension

Chapter 3 of the book discusses the strength of the [[Darul Islam]] (*DI*) movement and its three theaters — [[West Java]] under [[Kartosuwirjo]], [[Aceh]] under [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Daud Beureueh]], and [[South Celebes]] under [[Kahar Muzakkar]]. Beynon cites figures presented in the Indonesian parliament on the cost of the Aceh rebellion: some 10,000 dead, 27,000 people in need, material damage of 100 million rupiah, railway losses of 80 million rupiah, and 70% of railway stations destroyed.

The book's chronological appendix records key dates for the Aceh conflict: the outbreak of the rebellion on [[20 September]] [[1953]], the stoppage of [[Garuda Indonesian Airways]] flights to [[Kutaradja]] on [[21 September]], the official branding of Daud Beureuh as rebel leader on [[25 September]], and the statement of [[Kolonel Simbolon]], territorial commander in Aceh, on [[3 October]] [[1953]]: *"De toestand in Atjeh is zeer ernstig; het is niet mogelijk deze kwestie met wapenen alleen op te lossen"* — "The situation in Aceh is very serious; it is not possible to solve this issue with weapons alone."

## Postcolonial argument

The closing chapters argue that the Dutch–Indonesian relationship had been irreparably damaged by the half-hearted and self-interested character of the [[Soevereiniteitsoverdracht|transfer of sovereignty]] on [[27 December]] [[1949]], and that only a genuine acceptance of Indonesian independence — without the "fear of losing something" that had governed Dutch policy — could restore productive relations. The final chapter presents two future scenarios: continued antagonism ending in the complete loss of Dutch influence in [[Southeast Asia]], or a renewal of relations on a genuinely equal basis.

## Physical description

The volume was stamped at the [[Koninklijke Bibliotheek]] (Royal Library, The Hague) on [[15 January]] [[1957]], bearing shelfmark 26526217. It was written during the period July–September [[1956]] in [[Bussum]], as noted in the closing section.

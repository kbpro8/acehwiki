**Burda of al‑Busiri** is a 13th‑century Arabic devotional poem composed by [[Al‑Busiri]] that praises [[Prophet Muhammad]] and has been widely recited across the Muslim world, including in the Sultanate of [[Acheh]] where a 16th‑century Malay translation circulated among scholars and mystics. The poem’s theological themes—such as the supremacy of the Prophet, the struggle against the *satan* (Arabic: *shaitan*), and the promise of divine forgiveness—are echoed in the Malay manuscript examined in this entry.

### Composition and Themes
The original Arabic text, composed in the mid‑13th century, consists of 160 verses organized in a *qasida* form. Its central motifs, as reflected in the Malay translation, include:
- The Prophet’s role as “the lord of both worlds” and “the master of jinn and humanity” (see page 54).
- The contrast between *hunger* (*kelaparan*) and *satiety* as metaphors for spiritual longing (pages 52‑53).
- The admonition to resist *satanic* desires and to seek Allah’s forgiveness (pages 53‑54).

### Malay Translation (16th century)
A Malay version, dated to the 16th century, survives in the DLP‑54 manuscript (pages 52‑58). The translation renders Arabic theological terms into Malay while preserving key Arabic words in *italics* (e.g., *shaitan*, *sunnat*). It was likely used in Acehnese *madrasahs* and *khalwat* (spiritual retreats) as a didactic text for memorisation and recitation.

### Reception in Aceh
Acehnese scholars incorporated the Burda into *tazkirah* (biographical) literature and *qasidah* recitals. The poem’s emphasis on the Prophet’s intercessory power resonated with the Acehnese emphasis on *wali* (saints) and the *sulṭān*’s claim to divine legitimacy.

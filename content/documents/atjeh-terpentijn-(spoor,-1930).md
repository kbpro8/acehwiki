**Atjeh-terpentijn** — formally *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum van de Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut* No. 57 — is a 30-page pamphlet by Ir. [[W. Spoon]] extracted from *De Indische Mercuur* of [[19 November]] [[1930]] and published by J. H. de Bussy of [[Amsterdam]] in the same year. It is the principal contemporary monograph on the factory-scale production of turpentine from the [[Pinus Merkusii|Sumatra-den]] (*Pinus Merkusii*) at the [[Baleq]] enterprise in the [[Gajo-landen|Gajo-Landen]]-[[Atjeh]] border region, and it surveys the chemical composition, pharmacopoeial suitability, and European market reception of the factory product as compared with the trial samples of [[1924]]. As the only detailed published account of the [[Baleq]] enterprise's first four years of factory operation (October [[1928]] – [[1930]]), it is the principal source for the colonial-era history of Indonesian turpentine.

## Subject and scope

The pamphlet was prompted by the transformation, between [[1924]] and [[1930]], of the experimental Atjeh-turpentine of the early trial samples into a fully factory-scale product. Spoon notes in the opening pages that in [[1924]] the *Afdeeling Handelsmuseum* of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] had gathered a series of expert evaluations of trial Atjeh-turpentine — produced from *Pinus Merkusii* tapped in [[Noord-Sumatra]] — and had published them as *Bericht* No. 20 in [[April]] [[1925]]. The 1924 trial product had been criticised principally for its odour, which resembled that of *wood turpentine* rather than the *gum spirit* of true turpentine; the criticism was attributed to a high *careen* content. By [[1930]] the production had moved to a fully factory-scale operation at the [[Baleq]] enterprise of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf*, and Spoon's pamphlet sets out to determine whether the chemical and organoleptic properties of the new product had been correspondingly transformed.

The pamphlet is divided into four sections:

1. *Winning van de terpentijn* — production.
2. *Scheikundige samenstelling en eigenschappen* — chemical composition and properties.
3. *Toetsing aan standaard-eischen* — testing against the standards of the *Nederlandsche Pharmacopee* and the draft *Normaalblad V 597*.
4. *Beoordeelingen uit de praktijk* — practical evaluations from the Dutch varnish and paint industry.

## Production history

The production section reproduces the chronology of the [[Baleq]] enterprise. By *Besluit van [[27 June]] [[1924]]* the Regeering had placed funds at the disposal of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* for an exploitation-proef on behalf of the *Dienst van het Boschwezen*. The trial began on [[14 September]] [[1924]] in the *Semnenboschcomplexen* of [[Paja Djorok]] and [[Blangtekoer]], near the road from [[Takengon]] to [[Bireuen]] (Atjeh); the two complexes are separated by the river [[Baleq]], from which the enterprise took its name. The results were sufficient that in [[1926]] the Regeering resolved to convert the trial into a definitive exploitation as a separate division of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* with effect from [[1 January]] [[1927]]. By [[1930]] the area under exploitation was approximately 1600 ha, entirely in production, with output figures (in metric tons):

The trees were tapped using the *[[quarre]]* method, a French system borrowed from the turpentine industry of the Landes, in which the tapping wounds are 10 cm wide and at greatest depth 2½ cm deep, refreshed every three days in an upward direction with a slightly curved carpenter's chisel. The balsam was originally processed in an old distillation kettle of 100 kg capacity, but in July [[1925]] a locomobiel-ketel permitted steam distillation, and in October [[1928]] a fully modern installation imported from France was brought into service. From the new factory the yield was approximately 75% colophonium (resin) and 20% turpentine.

## Chemical composition

The chemical investigation was carried out on three samples — *H.M. no. 1504* (received December [[1929]] directly from Baleq), *H.M. no. 1553* (drawn from a January [[1930]] Amsterdam shipment by the *[[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij]]*) and *H.M. no. 1607* (an original tin from the April [[1930]] shipment per m.s. *Poelau Roebiah*). The specific gravity was 0.866 (against 0.862 in [[1924]]), the refractive index unchanged, the optical rotation practically unchanged (when one anomalous 1924 result was excluded). Dr. [[D. B. Spoelstra]] performed a fractional distillation of 2500 g of oil under 30 mm pressure, which showed that the new product consisted of at least 80% α-pinene (probably 85–90%) and only about 4% carene, against approximately 75% α-pinene and 15% carene in 1924. The fourfold reduction in carene content was identified as the chemical basis of the improved odour.

## Testing against standards

The samples were tested against the requirements of the *Nederlandsche Pharmacopee* (Ed. V, [[1926]]) for *Oleum terebinthinae* and against the draft *Ontwerp-normaalblad V 597* of the [[Hoofdcommissie voor de Normalisatie in Nederland]]. All Pharmacopoeia requirements were met, though the refractive index fell at the minimum limit. Against the draft normalisatie-eischen the Atjeh-turpentine met every requirement except the refractive index, which fell marginally below the proposed minimum of 1.469 (the sample values were 1.4677, 1.4680, 1.4680). The low refractive index was attributed to the exceptionally high α-pinene content of the Atjeh product — 85–90%, against 60–70% in the *Pinus palustris*-derived American and *Pinus Pinaster*-derived French turpentines. The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum accordingly submitted to the Hoofdcommissie a proposal to lower the draft minimum from 1.469 to 1.468, by which change the colonial product would fully meet the metropolitan standard.

## Practical evaluations

To gauge European market reception, the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum supplied samples to the *[[Vereeniging van Vernis- en Verffabrikanten en -Handelaren in Nederland]]*, which surveyed its 28 members. Eleven written evaluations were received; the consensus was that the technical qualities of the new product were good — and that the odour had improved markedly since 1924 — but that the residual difference from American and French turpentine, combined with the very small price differential (about f 22 per 50 kg), left most members unwilling to switch. The general verdict was summarised by Spoon in the closing paragraph: "the technical properties of the present Baleq product no longer stand in the way of a wider application in Europe."

## Appendices

The pamphlet contains two appendices:

- **Bijlage I.** The full text of the *Ontwerp-normaalblad V 597* for turpentine oil: purity requirements, the limits for specific gravity (0.860–0.875 at 15 °C), refractive index (1.469–1.478 at 20 °C), evaporation residue (≤2%), distillation fractions, residue after sulphuric acid treatment, and the prescribed methods for each test.
- **Bijlage II.** The full text of the circular letter addressed by L. P. de Bussy on [[5 May]] [[1930]] to the members of the *Vereeniging van Vernis- en Verffabrikanten en -Handelaren in Nederland*, transmitting sample *H.M. no. 1607* and the chemical data on samples 1504, 1553 and 1607. The circular notes the price of f 22 per 50 kg and refers readers to the Vierde Afdeeling of the *Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij* at Amsterdam for further commercial details.

## Significance

The pamphlet is the principal source for the early factory-scale production of Indonesian turpentine and the only detailed published account of the [[Baleq]] enterprise. Its detailed production figures, chemical data, and survey responses together document the attempt — ultimately only partly successful — to develop a colonial turpentine industry capable of competing in the metropolitan market. As a *Bericht* of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum it also illustrates the broader role of the Koloniaal Instituut in evaluating and promoting colonial commodities: the pamphlet is at once a piece of scientific reporting and an instrument of commercial policy. For the history of forestry in the [[Gajo-landen|Gajo-Landen]]-Atjeh border region, the Pamphlet preserves details of the [[Paja Djorok]] and [[Blangtekoer]] forest complexes, the [[Baleq]] river, and the operations of the *Dienst van het Boschwezen* and *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* that are otherwise difficult to recover.

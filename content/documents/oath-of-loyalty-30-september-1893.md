**The Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893** — preserved among the official documents gathered in the Annexes of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the formal act by which [[Teuku Umar]] (as *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*) and fifteen of his war leaders swore loyalty to the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Government of Nederlandsch-Indië]] at [[Koeta Radja]] on [[30 September]] [[1893]]. The act was the operational counterpart to the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Government Resolution of 5 September 1893 No. 1 geheim]] that authorised Umar's appointment as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government. Together, the two documents constituted the legal foundation of Umar's two-and-a-half-year career as a Dutch ally and, by extension, of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] that brought that alliance to an end.

## Background

By the late summer of [[1893]] the Dutch position in [[Groot-Atjeh]] had reached a stalemate that the policy of seeking peace through native alliance was designed to break. [[Teuku Umar]] had rendered good services to the *Kompeni* in [[August]] [[1893]] by the *zuivering der XXV Moekims* — the "cleansing" of the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]]. The [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] proposed his formal appointment as *[[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government*, and the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Government Resolution of 5 September 1893 No. 1 geheim]] authorised the appointment under the new name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*. The Conferral of office, however, required a public act of subjection — an oath of loyalty sworn by the new *Panglima Prang Besar* in the presence of his subordinate *panglima*, by which both he and they bound themselves to the Government.

The choice of date — [[30 September]] [[1893]] — was the formal date of [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|Umar's subjection]] to the Dutch administration. The ceremony was performed at [[Koeta Radja]] before the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Governor of Atjeh]], with Umar and the fifteen *panglima* of his *legioen* in attendance. The Confirmation Act is the official record of that ceremony.

## Content

The Confirmation Act records that on [[30 September]] [[1893]], [[Teuku Umar]] — now formally designated *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* — swore an oath of loyalty to the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Government of Nederlandsch-Indië]] as [[Panglima Prang Besar]]. The oath was not sworn by Umar alone: the act records that fifteen of his *panglima* swore with him, binding themselves collectively to the Government and, by implication, to Umar as their commander. This collective form was an explicit Dutch recognition of the feudal structure of Umar's authority: the *legioen* was not an impersonal military unit but a personal following whose loyalty ran through its chief.

The act also records the confirmation of Umar's titles, ranks, and emblems as *Panglima Prang Besar*. Kruisheer's text preserves, in summary form, the formal record of the ceremony: Umar received the *titels* and titles and insignia appropriate to his new rank; the *panglima* received the formal recognition of their subordination to him and, through him, to the Government. The Confirmation Act is, in effect, the legal constitutive act of the Dutch-Umar alliance, of which the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893]] had been the authorisation.

## Reception and Aftermath

The immediate aftermath of the Confirmation Act was the rapid rise of Umar's power within [[Groot-Atjeh]]. On [[21 October]] [[1893]] — three weeks after the act — T. Djohan was given the leadership of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]; on [[30 November]] [[1893]] his *legioen* conquered [[Ana' Galong|Ana' Galoeng]] for the Dutch. Thereafter, throughout [[1894]] and [[1895]], Umar's *legioen* operated in the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] mukims in support of Dutch operations — notably at [[Tjot Goeë]] on [[9 May]] [[1895]] — and received continuous supplies of Dutch weapons, munitions, opium, and money.

The act's legal force was terminated by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]], which dismissed Umar from the office of [[Panglima Prang Besar]] and from the *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Resolution of 19 January 1896]]. The collective loyalty of the fifteen *panglima* who swore with Umar in [[1893]] did not survive the defection: most followed Umar into rebellion in [[March]] [[1896]], and their signatures on the Confirmation Act became evidence of their perfidy.

## Significance

The Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of [[30 September]] [[1893]] is the foundational diplomatic document of the Dutch-alliance phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Its collective form — Umar and fifteen *panglima* swearing together — reveals the feudal structure of the alliance the Dutch were purchasing. Its brevity and its survival only in the *Bijlagen* of *ATJEH '96* should not obscure its importance: every Dutch weapon, every Dutch dollar, every Dutch operation conducted with Umar's *legioen* between [[1893]] and [[1896]] rested on this act. Its abrogation on [[26 April]] [[1896]] closed the alliance and opened the conventional military phase of the war under [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]].

**Een stukje Atjeh-geschiedenis** — *A Piece of Atjeh History* — is an article by [[H. T. Damste|H. T. Damste]] published in *Koloniaal Tijdschrift* in [[1918]], recording the succession dispute in the landschap of [[Simpang Olim]] at the time of the Dutch takeover. Broersma cites it in *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* as his authority for the coming of [[Teukoe moeda Oesoch|Teukoe moeda Oesoeh]] to the *oeléebalangschap*.

## Contents
The article narrates how, when the Dutch government established itself in the eastern coast of Atjeh in the years around [[1873]]–[[1875]], the father of Teukoe moeda Oesoeh was *radja* of Simpang Olim, and how the son — the future *oeléebalang* — had to exert himself to ensure that he, and not another with less right, succeeded his father in the chieftaincy. Broersma's summary is brief — *'het heeft hem wel inspanning gekost te zorgen, dat hij en niet een ander, die minder recht had, oeléebalang werd'* — but the implication is that the Dutch recognition of chieftaincies was politically contested and that local successions were often shaped by the new colonial presence.

## Significance
The article is one of a number of short historical studies produced by Dutch colonial officials in the years after the *bevrediging*, recovering the immediate pre-pacification political history of individual Atjehnese *landschappen*. It is cited by Broersma alongside the more general works of [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and [[J. A. Kruyt|Kruyt]] as one of the sources for the early colonial-period history of the eastern coast.

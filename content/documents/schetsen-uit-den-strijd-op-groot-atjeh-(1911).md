**Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh** (*Sketches from the struggle in Greater Atjeh*) was a Dutch-language collection of military sketches by [[P. H. R. Beuming]], a former non-commissioned officer of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands Indies Army]], published at [[Amsterdam]] in December [[1911]] and sold for ten cents a copy for the benefit of the projected [[Herstellingsoord voor Rijks-Ambtenaren|convalescent home for state civil servants]]. Illustrated with a specially prepared map of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and with photographs, it narrates in seven chapters the author's own experiences and those of former comrades on Groot-Atjeh in the years [[1893]] to [[1897]], and is a principal eyewitness source for the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|Anak Galoeng patrol]] of [[7 March]] [[1896]], the [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)|three-day battle in the Beradin pass]], the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Anak Galoeng]] and the [[Patrol to the Goeah Teroes on the Glé Betoes (25 September 1897)|patrol to the Goeah Teroes]].

## Purpose and publication

The title-page describes the work as *met speciaal vervaardigde kaart en toepasselijke photo's bewerkt door P. H. R. Beuming, oud-onderofficier van het Ned.-Indische Leger* and states that it was *uitgegeven ten bate van het te stichten Herstellingsoord voor Rijks-Ambtenaren*. The minimum price was fixed at f 0.10, or f 0.13 post-free within the Netherlands and f 0.18 by *[[Zeepost|Rgd]]* overseas.

In his preface, dated Amsterdam, December [[1911]], and signed from Beukenweg 52, Beuming described himself as *Administratief Ambtenaar in Rijks dienst* (administrative official in state service) and set out a двойной purpose. He had been *gedreven door een onweerstaanbaar verlangen om naar bescheiden krachten mede te werken aan de verwezenlijking van een ideaal* — driven by an irresistible desire to co-operate, according to his modest powers, in the realisation of an ideal, namely the founding of a convalescent home for state civil servants. He therefore wished to make his experiences on Groot-Atjeh, supplemented by particulars obtained from former colleagues, fruitful in two respects: first to help promote the object of the *Vereeniging tot Ondersteuning van Burgerlijke Ambtenaren en Beambten in 's Rijks dienst*, and second *om de belangstelling in de krijgsbedrijven van het Nederlandsch-Indisch leger, en in verband daarmede ook de sympathie — waarop dat kranige leger recht heeft — meer algemeen te doen worden* — to make more general the interest in the military operations of the Netherlands Indies Army, and with it the sympathy to which that gallant army had a right.

The net proceeds were to be handed over to the board of the philanthropic association as a contribution to the founding capital for the *Herstellingsoord*. Beuming noted that since many men regularly passed from the army into civil state service, he hoped that soldiers too would sympathise with his effort and that the sketches would find a good reception *zoowel in, als buiten de kazerne* — both inside and outside the barracks. He closed with thanks to all who by their information had enabled him to compose the sketches.

The volume carries as epigraph a passage from Mevr. Goedkoop's *Hilda van Suijlenburg*: *Niet gelukkig zijn, is de bestemming van het leven. Arbeiden, nuttig wezen, zich geven, mooier en rijker maken het gemeenschapsleven, is de eeuwige wet, die elk menschenbestaan beheerscht* — not to be happy is the destiny of life; to labour, to be useful, to give oneself, to make the life of the community more beautiful and richer, is the eternal law that governs every human existence.

## Contents

The table of contents lists seven pieces:

- *Een woord vooraf over het ontstaan van den Atjeh-oorlog* (a preliminary word on the origin of the Atjeh war), p. 5 — see [[Beuming's account of the origin of the Atjeh-oorlog]]
- *De Scherpschutter van Biloel* (the sharpshooter of Biloel), p. 12 — see [[The sharpshooter of Biloel]]
- *Het begin der onlusten in [[1896]]* (the beginning of the disturbances in [[1896]]), p. 22 — see [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)]]
- *De drie-daagsche veldslag in den Beradin-pas* (the three-day battle in the Beradin pass), p. 29 — see [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)]]
- *De overrompeling van Anak Galoeng* (the surprise of Anak Galoeng), p. 41 — see [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)]]
- *Een moeilijke tocht* (a difficult march), p. 48 — see [[Patrol to the Goeah Teroes on the Glé Betoes (25 September 1897)]]
- *Een vreeselijke ontmoeting* (a terrible encounter), p. 54 — see [[The crocodile encounter at Kroeng Raba (1896)]]
- *Pastoor Verbraak op Atjeh* (Father Verbraak in Atjeh), p. 60 — see [[Pastoor Verbraak]]

The volume closes with a folding *Kaart van Groot-Atjeh 1874-1895* showing the [[XXII Moekims]], [[XXV Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]].

## Illustrations

The photographic plates are themselves documentary. They include an Acehnese trading or smuggling *prauw* lying in the lagoon at [[Oleh-leh]], captioned as having been seized and condemned as prize in [[1896]] for carrying arms and ammunition destined for [[Panglima Polim]]; the former *benting* of [[Biloel]], with a cross marking the sentry-box that was the particular target of the enemy marksmen; the [[Acheh River|Atjeh river]] at [[Anak Galoeng]] with the former Dutch *benting* on the bank; a portrait of [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] *in de dagen van zijn glorie*; the former dwelling of Toekoe Oemar at [[Lampisang]], a gift of the government, destroyed by bombs and shells after his defection; the former *benting* [[Boekit Seboen]] on the road to [[Kroeng Raba]], abandoned after [[1884]], with the [[Glé Daroe pass]] visible behind; the main gate of the [[Peutjoet|Petjoet cemetery]] at [[Koeta Radja]]; and a portrait of [[Pastoor Verbraak]] in his decorations.

## Character as a source

The book is a soldier's memoir rather than a staff history. Beuming writes from the standpoint of the *onderofficier* in the *[[Benting|benting]]*, and his sketches preserve the vocabulary, humour and daily texture of garrison life — the *groote pijp* (the sergeants' club-room at [[Koeta Radja]]), the *[[Rimboe|rimboe]]*, the *[[Baleh-baleh|baleh-baleh]]* of split bamboo, the *[[Tandoe|tandoes]]*, the *[[Tong-tong|tong-tong]]*, the morning and evening gun, and the barrack nicknames *gladdekker* for Toekoe Oemar and *knodsen* for the 6.5 mm repeating rifles. At the same time he reproduces precise operational detail: unit designations, officers' names, and in the case of the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] the *Algemeen Stamboek* service numbers of the European rank and file killed.

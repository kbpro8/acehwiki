**The Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11** (*Extract uit het Register der Besluiten van den waarnemend Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, No. 159/11, KOTA-RADJA, 26 April 1896*) — reproduced as Annex VI of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the decree of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|waarnemend Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] that formally dismissed [[Teuku Umar]] (writing as *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*) from both of his government offices — [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government (conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893]]) and *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] (conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Resolution of 19 January 1896]]) — and stipulated that he would henceforth be officially designated by the name *Toekoe Oemar*. The resolution, signed by *De Secretaris* [[Udo de Haes]], was the executing instrument of the [[Government Telegram of 26 April 1896, No. 409|Government Telegram of 26 April 1896 No. 409]] and the immediate legal predicate of the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|Proclamation of 27 April 1896]] announcing military operations against him.

## Background

By [[26 April]] [[1896]] the Umar crisis had reached its terminal diplomatic phase. The [[buitenlinie]] had been progressively abandoned — [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[12 April]] [[1896]], [[Ana' Galong|Anak-Galoeng]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Soet]] and [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]], and [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]]. The exchange of letters between Umar and the [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had produced Umar's [[First Letter of Teuku Umar to the Governor of Atjeh (Bijlage II)|First]], [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar (12 April 1896)|Second]], and [[Third Letter of Teuku Umar (23 April 1896)|Third Letters]] and the [[Regeeringscommissaris]]'s reply of [[25 April]] [[1896]] demanding that Umar hand in the *geleende oorlogsmaterieel*, demolish the versterkingen in the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]], and submit anew. On the morning of [[26 April]] [[1896]] Umar's reply was received asking for a three-day extension without any definite promise; the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] denied the request the same day.

With diplomatic channels exhausted, the [[Government Telegram of 26 April 1896, No. 409|Government Telegram No. 409]] arrived from [[Batavia]] ordering Umar's dismissal from both offices before the commencement of military action. The *besluit* was issued the same day by the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|waarnemend Gouverneur]] at [[Koeta Radja]] — that is, by [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] acting in his civil capacity as acting Governor — and signed by *De Secretaris* [[Udo de Haes]]. The same day saw the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] issue his [[Proclamation of the Regeeringscommissaris (12 April 1896)|second proclamation]] explaining why he was forced to commence hostilities against Umar; the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|second proclamation to the three *sagi*]] followed on [[27 April]] [[1896]].

## Content

The resolution is formally an *Extract uit het Register der Besluiten van den waarnemend Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* — an extract from the register of decrees of the acting Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies. Its number, 159/11, places it in the regular sequence of the Governor's *besluiten* for [[1896]]. Its place of issue is given as [[Koeta Radja|KOTA-RADJA]] and its date as [[26 April]] [[1896]].

The substantive content of the resolution is the dismissal of *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* from two offices: from the office of [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government, conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893 No. 1 geheim]]; and from the office of *hoeloebalang* of the *landschap* [[Lepong]], conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Resolution of 19 January 1896 No. 9]]. The dismissal is total: Umar is removed from government service, not suspended; the *Panglima Prang Besar* title and the territorial jurisdiction over [[Lepong]] are both revoked.

The most striking provision of the resolution is the *stipulation* that Umar would henceforth be officially designated by the name *Toekoe Oemar* — the old name he had borne before his [[1893]] elevation, not the new name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* conferred on him by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893]]. The symbolic erasure of the [[1893]] renaming is total: in all official Dutch documents, Umar was to be designated by his old name, as if the elevation had never happened. The resolution is signed by *De Secretaris* [[Udo de Haes]], acting for the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|waarnemend Gouverneur]].

## Reception and Aftermath

The resolution was acted upon immediately. The same day, [[26 April]] [[1896]], the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] issued a proclamation explaining why he was forced to commence hostilities. The following day, [[27 April]] [[1896]], the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|Proclamation of 27 April 1896]] was issued from [[Koeta Radja|KOTA-RADJA]] announcing military operations against *T. Oemar Melaboch* — that is, against Umar under his post-dismissal designation (*Melaboch* / *Melabuh* is the designation given him after defection). The [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|Bombardment of Lampisang]] began at 5:45 a.m. on [[27 April]] [[1896]] and continued to [[23 May]] [[1896]]. The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] followed, capturing Umar's house at [[Lampisang|Lam-Pisang]] and driving him into retreat to [[Lepong]].

## Significance

The *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[26 April]] [[1896]] No. 159/11 is the formal instrument of Umar's dismissal and, by extension, of the legal dissolution of the Dutch-Umar alliance. Its revocation of both the [[Panglima Prang Besar]] commission and the *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] closes the legal structure opened by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893]] and the [[Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893|Oath of Loyalty of 30 September 1893]]. Its stipulation that Umar be designated by his old name *Toekoe Oemar* is a deliberate act of symbolic erasure, undoing the [[1893]] renaming that had constituted him as a Dutch officer. As a historical document, the resolution is the legal hinge between the diplomatic and military phases of the Umar crisis, and is preserved as *Bijlage VI* of *ATJEH '96* precisely because it is the document that converts a defection into a war.

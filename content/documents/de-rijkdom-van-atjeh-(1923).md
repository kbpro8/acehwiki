**De Rijkdom van Atjeh** ("The Wealth of Atjeh") is a Dutch-language brochure of twenty-six pages published in [[1923]] by the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] of Amsterdam, prepared by the Directorium of the Institute as a colonial-economic assessment of the [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] region in the immediate aftermath of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The brochure surveys the historical basis of Atjeh's wealth — the [[pepercultuur|pepercultuur]] (pepper cultivation) that had sustained the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] from the early sixteenth century — and prescribes the substitution of [[rijstbouw|rijstbouw]] (rice cultivation) as the principal future export crop, supported by irrigation works, rice mills (*pellerijen*), and an organisation for the marketing of the product.

## Authorship and Purpose


The brochure was published by the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] of Amsterdam — the metropolitan institutional successor to the earlier [[Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] interests in Atjeh — and is the work of the Directorium of the Institute. Its purpose, as stated in the opening, is to consider Atjeh "even nuchter van een koloniaal-economisch standpunt" (coolly, from a colonial-economic standpoint) as the Dutch were accustomed to do with other parts of [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]]. The name "Atjeh" had, the brochure acknowledges, "in Nederland een eigenaardigen klank verkregen" (acquired in the Netherlands a peculiar ring) — evoking the long, anxious years of war. But the Directorium now hoped, with the war "uitgestreden" (fought out), to be heard in Atjeh itself on the question of the country's wealth.

## Structure


The brochure is organised in three principal sections, preceded by an historical preamble:

1. **The historical basis of Atjeh's wealth** — the [[pepercultuur|pepercultuur]] (pepper cultivation) from its introduction in [[Samoedra|Samoedra]] between [[1350]] and [[1400]] A.D. by the first dispersers of Islam in North Sumatra, through its transfer to [[Pidië|Pidië]] between [[1435]] and [[1500]], the rise of Atjeh under [[Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah|Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah]] after the defeat of the Portuguese fleet of [[Yorge de Brito|Yorge de Brito]] in [[May 1521|May]] [[1521]], the apogee of the sultanate under [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Sultan Iskandar Moeda]] (1607–1636), the decline under the four successive queens and the Buginese dynasty, and the state of Atjehese prosperity at the outbreak of the war in [[1873]].

2. **The decline of pepper and the case for rice** — the observation that, with the war over, the pepper seems "his time to have had" and no longer to thrive in many places, and the prescription that the [[rijstbouw|rijstbouw]] (rice cultivation) is the new great-trade article that must take the place of pepper. The brochure estimates 120,000 hectares of rice-cultivable land in the afdeelingen of [[Groot Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] (15,000 ha), [[Pidië|Pidië]] (25,000 ha), [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] / [[Peusangan|Peusangan]] / [[Lho' Soekon|Lho' Soekon]] (70,000 ha), and [[Oostkust|Oostkust]] ([[Peureula|Peureula]]) (10,000 ha).

3. **The requirements of an export rice cultivation** — a detailed prescription for the three prerequisites of rice as an export article: a suitable rice variety (with the failure of the [[Carolina-rijst|Carolina-rijst]] experiment at [[Tapa' Toean|Tapa' Toean]] as a cautionary example), rice mills (*[[pellerijen|pellerijen]]*) for hulling (with a detailed capital and operating account), and an organisation for the sale of the product at market price. The brochure then surveys the history of the [[Atjehsche Irrigatiedienst|Atjehsche Irrigatiedienst]] since the visit of the engineer [[Van Tubergen|Van Tubergen]] in [[1911]] and the bandjir-freedom works on the [[Peusangen|Peusangen]] and [[Arakoendo|Arakoendo]] rivers.

## Argument


The central argument of the brochure is that the wealth of Atjeh, historically based on the [[pepercultuur|pepercultuur]], must in the post-war period be re-established on the basis of the [[rijstbouw|rijstbouw]]. The neighbouring [[Cultuurgebied Oostkust van Sumatra|Culturen-Gewest Oostkust van Sumatra]] (the East Coast of Sumatra cultivation region), which annually imported 140,000–150,000 tons of rice for food, was the natural market. With 120,000 hectares of cultivable land and an average yield of 25 picol per hectare on irrigated sawah, Atjeh could supply more than half of the East Coast's requirement. The three prerequisites — a suitable rice variety, rice mills, and a sales organisation — could be met with the cooperation of wealthy Atjehers (who had previously advanced money to pepper planters and could now invest in rice mills), the technical assistance of the [[Atjehsche Irrigatiedienst|Irrigatiedienst]], and a sales contract with the East Coast.

## The Acehnese Proverb


The brochure opens with an Atjehese proverb: "*Kaja meutiah hana meusampe, kaja pade meusampoerna*" — "Rijkdom aan goud is niet voldoende, rijkdom aan rijst is volmaakt" (Wealth in gold is not sufficient; wealth in rice is perfect). The proverb, cited at the threshold of the brochure, frames the entire argument: the historical wealth of Atjeh, based on pepper (a trade article exchanged for gold and other goods), is to be replaced by a new wealth based on rice — the staple of life.

## Significance


The brochure is one of the principal Dutch colonial-economic assessments of Atjeh in the immediate post-war period. Its publication by the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] placed it in the institutional context of the metropolitan successor to the [[Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] interests in Atjeh, and its argument for the substitution of rice for pepper as the basis of Atjeh's wealth shaped Dutch colonial-economic policy in Atjeh in the 1920s. The brochure's detailed prescription — a suitable rice variety, rice mills, and a sales organisation — and its survey of the Atjehsche Irrigatiedienst since [[1911]] together constitute the principal Dutch program for the post-war economic development of Atjeh.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**De rijkdom van Atjeh** — *The Wealth of Atjeh* — is a small treatise issued by the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] in [[1923]], locating the future wealth of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in the development of its rice-cultivation and proposing a programme of reform to that end. Broersma reviewed the pamphlet in *De Locomotief* of [[25 July]] [[1923]] and discusses it at length in the eighteenth essay of *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]*.

### Argument


The treatise argues that the prosperity of the gewest lies principally in *'den rijstbouw van het gewest, indien hij zich ontwikkelt'*, and that the development of that rice-cultivation is therefore the proper economic task of the immediate future. It proposes a three-part programme: first, the identification of *geschikte padisoorten* — rice varieties suited to local conditions; second, the opening of facilities for *ontbolstering* (husking) so that the harvest may be turned into marketable rice; and third, the creation of an organisation capable of selling the product at any moment at the market price. The treatise further insists, in language Broersma endorses, that the *oeléebalangs* are the indispensable collaborators in any such programme.

### Reception


Broersma welcomes the pamphlet as a *'toekomstbeeld'* — a picture of the future — and endorses its insistence that the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]] is not indifferent to the economic life of Atjeh, since the great contract-labour estates of the East Coast would gladly buy Atjeh rice in place of the Rangoon or Saigon rice they were then importing. But he presses the Institut to go further and to *'aanwijzen, hoe het meent dat het groote werk van verbetering der paditeelt is aan te vangen'* — to point out how it thinks the great work of improving the rice-cultivation is to be begun — and to nominate a *bemiddelaar* (mediator) between the Atjehnese producer and the Sumatran East-Coast consumer. Broersma's own proposal, set out in *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen]]*, is the appointment of a real expert on rice-cultivation to act in continuous consultation with a *college van kundige hoofden*.

### Significance


The pamphlet is the principal printed programme of the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] in its earliest period and reflects the Institut's ambition to act as a broker between the colonial government, the Atjehnese *oeléebalangs* and the East-Coast planter interest. It is also an early statement of the *rijstbouw* doctrine which was to dominate Dutch economic policy in Atjeh down to the depression.

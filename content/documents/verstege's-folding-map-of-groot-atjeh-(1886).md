**Verstege's folding map of Groot-Atjeh** (*uitslaande kaart van Groot-Atjeh*) was the folding map added to [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Overste Verstege]]'s pamphlet *[[Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk in zake het Atjeh-vraagstuk (Verstege, 1886)|Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk in zake het Atjeh-vraagstuk]]* (Amsterdam, [[1886]]), on which the author asked his readers to verify which positions had been taken by the Dutch troops in [[Groot-Atjeh]] at the end of April [[1874]], at the end of April [[1875]], and on [[1 March]] [[1885]]. The map, with its legend, is the cartographic backbone of the third part of his lecture, in which the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration]] of [[1885]] was compared with the much wider lines held by [[Jacobus Pel|generaal Pel]] ten years earlier.

## The position at the end of April 1874

At the departure of [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] on [[26 April]] [[1874]] the Dutch held, besides the imperfectly fortified and arranged [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] itself — without secured communication with the coast and without harbour or landing-place — the following posts:

- [[Kuta Raja|Kota Radja]] with [[Pakan Atjeh]] (2 companies);
- a hornwork on the right bank of the [[Kroeng Daroe]] and the post [[Kota Goenoengan]] (1 company);
- a small earthwork southwest of the Kraton, on the left bank of the Kroeng Daroe;
- [[Penajoeng]], a square bastioned redoubt on the right bank of the [[Atjeh River|Atjehrivier]] (1 company and 20 artillerymen);
- [[Kampong Djawa]] (1 company and 20 artillerymen), opposite Penajoeng, actually only a bivouac for the right half of the 2nd battalion;
- the [[Rivierbenting|Marinebenting]], a small redoubt on the right bank (50 men);
- the strand-bivak (beach bivouac) at the [[Oedjong Kali]] or [[Koeala Atjeh]] (50 men).

The force left behind, including the garrison of [[Poelo Breueh|Poeloe Bras]], numbered 128 officers and 3,151 men, of whom on [[26 April]] 178 Europeans and 41 natives were already nursed in the field hospital — on the health of [[Kuta Raja|Kota Radja]], praised by van Swieten as so healthy, Verstege placed against this the figures in the Koloniaal Verslag: on [[12 May]] [[1874]], the feast-day of the King's 25th anniversary on which no work was done (a double incentive to keep out of hospital), 308 patients were nursed in the ambulance and 223 sick in quarters, i.e. about one-eighth of the expeditionary force; on 20 May the figure reached one-seventh; from [[26 April]] to the end of December [[1874]] 902 persons (including [[Dwangarbeiders|dwangarbeiders]] and naval personnel) of the whole force died and 1,917 were evacuated; and during the whole of [[1875]] there were 957 deaths and no less than 5,151 evacuated (cited from [[B. C. P. Kielstra|Kielstra]], Deel II, pp. 94 and 194).

## The position at the end of April 1875

One year later the foremost inland post was [[Missigit Longbatta|Longbatta-Missigit]], and the occupied position was bounded by the following outermost chain of posts: to the south, [[Oedjong Palanggahan]] (a blockhouse near [[Oleh-leh]]), [[Soerian]], [[Poe-oe]], [[Blangey]], [[Bang Tjoen Tjoet]], [[Setoe Zuid]] (occupied since [[2 May]]), [[Lamara Oleyloe|Lamara-Oleyloe]], [[Lohong]] and [[Missigit Longbatta|Longbatta-Missigit]]; to the east, from Longbatta-Missigit past [[Blang Tjoet|Blang-Tjoet]] and [[Long Zaita Zuid]] upstream along the left bank of the [[Atjeh River|Atjehrivier]] as far as [[Kota Alam]] on the right bank; and from there over [[Lemboe Oost]], [[Lemboe Noord]], [[Lampriet]], [[Langkroek Oost]], [[Lamara Zuid Oost]], [[Lamara Noord]] and [[Tiban]] to [[Kota Moesapie|Moesapie]]. The base of the triangle so formed was the coastal strip from [[Oleh-leh]] to [[Moesapi|Kota Moesapie]].

This chain, the result of an almost uninterrupted one-year struggle sometimes suspended for lack of able-bodied men, had been forced upon Pel by the Atjehers themselves, to safeguard the main position against the ever pressing enemy, to give the troops the rest they so urgently needed, and to keep open the communication with the sea. The position was not extensive — it comprised only 2,500 hectares — but besides the nineteen named fortifications there were several more posts near Kota Radja within the line, bringing the total number of posts, aside from the main position, to thirty-eight in May [[1875]]; their garrison required a force of 2,750 men out of a total force then of between 5,000 and 6,000 men. The fortifications had been raised, sometimes with great sacrifice and mostly under enemy fire, as fixed points from which the enemy could be kept restless and be prevented — otherwise easy on so accidented and enclosed a terrain — from approaching Kota Radja.

## Verstege's comparison with 1 March 1885

The third position on the map, the [[Geconcentreerde Stelling|concentrated line]] of [[1 March]] [[1885]] (67 km² or 6,700 hectares with eighteen semi-permanent posts — see [[Completion of the concentrated position (March 1885)]]), was adduced by Verstege as proof that the Dutch had been folded back into a narrower line than the one held at the end of [[1876]]: southwest from [[Kroeng Raba]] to [[Lamdjame]], east from [[Toengkoep]] to [[Lamjong]], and south from [[Djerir]] to [[Lambaroe]] — a retreat of not less than nearly thirteen kilometres towards Lambaroe. The Atjehers, he remarked, must have noticed that the Dutch steps in going back were larger and quicker than in advancing.

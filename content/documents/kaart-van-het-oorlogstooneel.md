**Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel** — full title *Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel, de Vaarwaters en vereischte Kustlichten voor den Wereldhandel*, "Map of the Theatre of War, the Waterways and required Coastal Lights for World Trade" — is a folded map that accompanied the anonymous Dutch pamphlet *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk]]* by [[Een Ooggetuige|Een Ooggetuige]], published by [[Van Meurs & Stufkens]] in [[Rotterdam]] in the [[1880s]]. The map set out the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] coast, the waterways around it, and the positions at which coastal lights were held to be needed for world trade, and was designed to support the author's argument on the strategic and commercial value of Atjeh.

## Content and purpose

The map was issued as a folding plate inside the *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk]]*. Its full title — *Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel, de Vaarwaters en vereischte Kustlichten voor den Wereldhandel* — sets out the three things it was designed to show: the theatre of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] on shore; the waterways — including the [[Strait of Halakka|Strait of Halakka]] and the approaches to the Atjehnese coast — through which world shipping passed; and the coastal lights (*kustlichten*) that the author held to be required for the safe navigation of those waterways. The map thus served a double purpose: it allowed the reader to follow the military geography of the war, and it allowed the reader to follow the author's argument on the shipping routes and the lighting of the coast in visual form.

## The map and the pamphlet's argument

The map is one of the elements that gives the *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen]]* its character as a practical contribution to the strategic debate on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]]. The author's central argument is that the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] should not abandon Atjeh, but should instead settle the country on a firmer footing — with better roads, better coastal lights, and a clearer plan for the civil administration of the Atjehnese interior — and that the Atjehnese coast, properly lighted and buoyed, could serve as a station for world shipping on the great sea-route between the [[Suez Canal|Suez Canal]] and the Far East. The map, by setting out the waterways and the required coastal lights in visual form, allowed the reader to judge that argument at a glance.

## Places shown

The map shows the northern tip of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]], the [[Strand Malaka (Strait of Malacca)|Strait of Malacca]], the [[Strait of Halakka|Strait of Halakka]] between the Atjehnese mainland and [[Sabang|Pulau Weh]], and the Atjehnese coast with its principal places — including [[Panton Laboe|Panton Laboe]] and other points named in the pamphlet. The map likewise shows the wider setting of the Atjehnese theatre in relation to the sea approaches to the archipelago, including the [[Sunda Strait|Sunda Strait]] and the Lampong districts in the south of Sumatra, which the author discusses in connection with the sea approaches to Atjeh.

## Significance

The *Kaart van het Oorlogstooneel* is significant within the AcehWiki corpus as a contemporary cartographic document of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]]. It belongs to the body of Dutch cartographic material on the Atjeh War — including the [[Map of Groot-Atjeh|map of Groot-Atjeh]] and other maps issued with contemporary accounts of the war — and is notable for its emphasis on the coastal lights and waterways rather than on the military positions alone. The map's framing of the Atjehnese coast as a station for world trade, and its argument for the lighting of the coast, place it within the wider nineteenth-century debate on the sea approaches to the archipelago, of which the [[Suez Canal|Suez Canal]] — opened in [[1869]] — was the decisive modern fact.

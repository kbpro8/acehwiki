**The Memorie van Antwoord on the Indies budget for [[1898]]** (*Memorandum of Reply*) was the written answer of the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|J. T. Cremer]] to the [[Voorloopig Verslag on the Indies budget for 1898|Voorloopig Verslag]] (Provisional Report) of the [[Tweede Kamer]] on the [[Indische begrooting voor 1898|estimates of Nederlandsch-Indië for 1898]]. Presented in the autumn of [[1897]], it was the government's authoritative statement of policy on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], and was quoted repeatedly by every speaker in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November|16]]\u2013[[17 November]] [[1897]].

## The financial passage

The most frequently cited sentence of the *Memorie* was the admission of the war's fiscal dominance: *"Zoolang de Atjeh-oorlog voortgaat de groote offers te eischen, die hij nu vergt en in het laatste 24-tal jaren gevergd heeft, zal ten opzichte van de opheffing van persoonlijke lasten waarschijnlijk niet zoover kunnen worden gegaan als wel wenschelijk ware; zullen vele nuttige uitgaven achterwege moeten blijven; zal op elk gebied groote zuinigheid moeten worden betracht" — So long as the Atjeh war continues to demand the great sacrifices which it now exacts and has exacted in the last twenty-four years, it will probably not be possible to go so far in the abolition of personal burdens as would be desirable.* — so long as the Atjeh war continues to demand the great sacrifices it now exacts and has exacted these last twenty-four years, the abolition of personal burdens will probably not be carried as far as would be desirable; many useful expenditures will have to be omitted; great thrift will have to be practised in every field. The *Memorie* added that for the sake of the Indies finances the government must above all strive for the ending of the state of war in Atjeh, "die reeds zoo lang slepende is" (which has already dragged on so long).

[[Pijnacker Hordijk]] opened his speech with this passage; [[Van der Zwaag]] read it out to argue that "de Atjeh-oorlog domineert alles" (the Atjeh war dominates everything); and [[H. van Kol|Van Kol]] returned to it in his reply to prove that no reform of [[Java|Javanese]] welfare, education, medical service, justice, railways or irrigation could proceed while the war lasted.

## The military-political passage

On page 5 the *Memorie* stated the government's conviction "van de doeltreffendheid van het stelsel, dat thans in [[Groot-Atjeh]] toepassing vindt" (of the efficacy of the system now applied in Greater Atjeh), and that from a consistent perseverance in the direction now taken it expected the ending of the state of war in that territory. Answering the [[Voorloopig Verslag on the Indies budget for 1898|Voorloopig Verslag]]'s pointed question as to what political and military objective might be regarded as attained, the *Memorie* replied that "de rust en de steeds verbeterende toestand in de streken, waar de vijandelijke benden het niet meer wagen door te dringen" (the quiet and the steadily improving condition in the districts where the hostile bands no longer dare to penetrate) gave a conclusive answer, referring the reader to page 8 of the latest [[Koloniaal Verslag]].

As to the sacrifices still required to reach *pacificatie*, the *Memorie* answered that these depended on the time still needed to make clear to the [[Hoofden van het verzet|chiefs of the resistance]] that further opposition was useless, "omdat Nederland ditmaal onwrikbaar besloten is om aan den Atjeh-oorlog een einde te maken en het vruchteloos zou zijn opnieuw op verandering van inzichten te hopen" (because the Netherlands is this time immovably resolved to make an end of the Atjeh war and it would be vain to hope again for a change of views).

[[Bahlmann]] declared this answer unsatisfactory, noting that the *Verslag* had suggested the information be supplied in a note reserved for members' inspection, and that of such a note not a word had been said.

## Other provisions cited in debate

The *Memorie* further stated that a reinforcement of two thousand Europeans for [[1898]] was considered sufficient by the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Indies government]]; that the service in the new *kampementen* was not too heavy for the men's strength; that everything possible was being done to secure proper replacement, equipment and care of the troops; and that the minister doubted whether a still more vigorous action than that now taken in Atjeh would lead to a speedier end of the struggle. It also carried the request for three *[[Recherchevaartuigen|recherchevaartuigen]]* (search vessels) to suppress the import of war contraband, a number that [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] and [[Van Vlijmen]] both thought too small.

**Parliamentary Note on Relations with Aceh 1872-1873** was an official governmental memorandum presented to the Dutch States-General by Minister of Colonies [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]] during the parliamentary session of [[1872]] and [[1873]], documenting the history of diplomatic relations between the Kingdom of the Netherlands and the [[Aceh Sultanate]] since the [[Treaty of London 1824]].

## Purpose and Scope

The memorandum was prepared by [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]] to justify the mounting confrontation with [[Kraton of Aceh]] following the Anglo-Dutch Sumatra Treaty of [[1871]]. It detailed decades of diplomatic correspondence, alleged treaty infractions, and the negotiations that preceded the dispatch of the [[First Aceh Expedition]] in April [[1873]].

## Usage in Military Historiography

In his [[1875]] polemic against General [[F. V. A. de Stuers]], General [[G. G. Booms]] referenced page 34 (cited as page 69 in de Stuers's article) of the [[Parliamentary Note on Relations with Aceh 1872-1873]]. While de Stuers used the note to argue that the [[Dutch East Indies Government]] faced force majeure and could not postpone the [[First Aceh Expedition]], Booms demonstrated from the same document that the government had alternative diplomatic and naval options available in early [[1873]].

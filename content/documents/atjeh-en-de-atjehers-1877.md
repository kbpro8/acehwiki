**Atjeh en de Atjehers, twee jaren blokkade op Sumatra's Noord-oost-kust** (Leiden, [[1877]]) by [[J. A. Kruyt]] was account of Dutch naval blockade east coast [[Atjeh]] [[1873]]-[[1875]] aboard warship [[Timor]].

## Content relevant to Atjeh

Provides earliest systematic European data on pepper cultivation: garden 5,000 trees yield 5 *kojang* = 1,100 *picol* 3-4 men half produce local market price collection called *seuneubò* (wrote "seleboe"). Estimated annual export east coast 5,000-6,000 *kojang* at [[Penang]] 200-250 *pilaardollars* per *kojang* August [[1873]] earlier 90.

Provides discovery description petroleum near [[Rantau Pandjang]] May [[1875]] officers Timor led by Atjeh heads: fenced source wooden collection tank lid, skimmed twice daily pisang leaf buckets hole water away hollow bamboo transport back to [[Rantau Pandjang]] sale almost 6 dollar picol head monthly income 200 dollars besides three sources stones granite quartz quartzite conglomerate siliceous sandstone.

Also noted silver presentation gifts: Prince [[Edi]] silver coffee service, another gold watch, third silver rice dish, later echoed in [[Peudawa Rajeu]] gift [[1875]] to [[Teukoe Habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeir]].

Kruyt's mention of [[Sekoetji]] vs [[Blang Sekotji]] name issue, and of [[Batoe Sawar]]? etc.

## Use by Broersma

Broersma cites Kruyt extensively for pepper figures (pp.27-29), petroleum (33-34), historical gifts (39), Samoedra encounter description of mbaharadja [[Lho Seumawe]] as rough savage (55-56).

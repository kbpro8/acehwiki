**The dagorder of the Governor of Atjeh on cruelties against women and children** was a daily order of the [[Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies]], issued shortly before November [[1897]], commanding the troops to abstain from cruelties against women and children. It was cited by [[H. van Kol]] in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] as evidence that such cruelties were in fact occurring, since an order forbidding them would otherwise have been superfluous.

## Citation in the debate

[[H. van Kol|Van Kol]], answering the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]]'s denial of cruelty on [[17 November]] [[1897]], said: "Kort geleden moest in eene dagorder van den gouverneur van Atjeh het zich onthouden van wreedheden tegen vrouwen en kinderen worden geboden" — recently, in a daily order of the governor of Atjeh, abstention from cruelties against women and children had to be enjoined.

He placed the order in a series with two other official texts. The first was the instruction of the commander in Atjeh of [[27 July]] [[1875]], which had recommended "alle handelingen, die den vijand breuk doen" (all acts that damage the enemy) and had expressly ordered, among other things, the "verbranden van huizen, vernielen van kampongs" (burning of houses, destruction of *[[Kampong|kampongs]]*). The second was the most recent [[Koloniaal Verslag]], which spoke repeatedly of the "in den asch leggen van kampongs" (laying of *kampongs* in ashes), of the "vernielen en verbranden der schuilhoeken" (destroying and burning of hiding-places) and of the "uitroeien van bosschen" (extirpation of forests), so that the population found refuge only in the "gespaarde kampongs" (spared villages).

## Significance

The order is significant as an internal admission by the Dutch military administration in Atjeh that acts against non-combatants were taking place on a scale requiring formal prohibition. [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] in his reply neither confirmed nor denied the existence of the *dagorder*, but argued that women and children watching a fight from a distance might be struck because "men heeft een afgeschoten kogel niet meer in zijne macht" (one no longer has control of a bullet once fired), while denying that cruelties in cold blood had occurred. On [[17 November]] he associated himself with [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s position that concrete proven cases would be condemned by every party in the chamber, and quoted the statement of the former Minister [[Mr. J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] of [[22 September]] [[1896]] that both the Netherlands and the Indies governments were most strongly opposed to *mutileeren* (mutilation) and that the [[Governor-General of Netherlands India|Governor-General]] had forbidden it in the strictest terms.

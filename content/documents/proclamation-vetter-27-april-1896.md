**The Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the three *sagi* of Groot-Atjeh** — dated from [[Koeta Radja|KOTA-RADJA]] on [[27 April]] [[1896]] and reproduced as Annex VII of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the second of the two proclamations issued by [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] to the Acehnese population, and the formal announcement of the commencement of military operations against [[Teuku Umar]] — now designated by his post-dismissal name *T. Oemar Melaboch* — and his followers. The proclamation distinguished Umar and his personal followers from the population of the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI and IV Moekims]] (who would not be attacked if not hostile), reminded the population of the prosperity enjoyed under Dutch protection and of the conquests of [[Lombok]] (where Mataram was "done away from the earth" and Tjakra Negara was subdued), warned of renewed war, and promised no harm where no resistance was met but destruction where resistance was offered. The proclamation was signed *VETTER*.

## Background

The proclamation was issued the day after [[Teuku Umar]] was formally dismissed from both his government offices by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]], which had been authorised by the [[Government Telegram of 26 April 1896, No. 409|Government Telegram No. 409 of the same day]]. The previous two weeks had seen the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] preside over the progressive abandonment of the [[buitenlinie]] — [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[12 April]] [[1896]], [[Ana' Galong|Anak-Galoeng]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Soet]] and [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]], and [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]] — and over the final exchange of letters with Umar (the [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar (12 April 1896)|Second]] of [[12 April]] [[1896]] and the [[Third Letter of Teuku Umar (23 April 1896)|Third]] of [[23 April]] [[1896]]).

By [[26 April]] [[1896]] the diplomatic phase was over. Umar's morning reply requesting a three-day extension had been denied; the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution dismissing him]] had been issued; the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] had issued a same-day [[Proclamation of the Regeeringscommissaris (12 April 1896)|proclamation explaining why he was forced to commence hostilities]]. The following morning, [[27 April]] [[1896]], the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|Bombardment of Lampisang]] began at 5:45 a.m., and the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|Proclamation to the three *sagi*]] was issued from [[Koeta Radja|KOTA-RADJA]].

## Content

The proclamation is addressed to the *hoofden en bevolking der 3 sagi's van Groot-Atjeh* — the heads and population of the three federations of [[Groot-Atjeh]]: the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV]], [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII]], and [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. The substantive content is structured around four themes.

First, the proclamation announces military operations against *T. Oemar Melaboch*, who has betrayed the Government, and his followers. The use of the post-dismissal name the *Toekoe Melaboeh* of the master list, equivalent to "the fallen Umar" or "Umar who has brought himself low" is the symbolic counterpart to the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution of 26 April]] stipulating that Umar be designated in official documents by his old name *Toekoe Oemar*.

Second, the proclamation states that Umar demanded money to fight all of Atjeh — a reference to his [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar (12 April 1896)|Second Letter]]'s demand for ƒ150,000 per month — and refuses to deal with him. This is the formal closure of the diplomatic channel.

Third, the proclamation carefully distinguishes Umar and his personal followers from the population of the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI and IV Moekims]]: those populations would not be attacked if they were not hostile. This is a deliberate attempt to fracture Umar's coalition by sparing the populations of his base mukims.

Fourth, the proclamation reminds the population of the prosperity enjoyed under Dutch protection and of the conquests of [[Lombok]] — where, the text states, Mataram was "done away from the earth" (*van de aarde verdwenen*) and Tjakra Negara was subdued. The reference to the [[1873]] [[1873 (Conquest of the Kraton)|conquest of the kraton]] of [[Aceh]] is implicit: the Dutch have conquered before, and will conquer again. The proclamation warns of renewed war, promises no harm where no resistance is met but destruction where resistance is offered, and is signed *VETTER*.

## Reception and Aftermath

The proclamation was the public opening of the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|Bombardment of Lampisang]] and of the conventional military phase of the war against Umar. From [[27 April]] [[1896]] to [[23 May]] [[1896]] Dutch batteries at [[Lamdjamoe]] bombarded Umar's stellingen at [[Lampisang]] and the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] with some 7,996 projectiles — to "zeer gering" (very meagre) effect. The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] then broke the stelling, captured Umar's house at [[Lampisang|Lam-Pisang]], and drove him into retreat to [[Lepong]]. The subsequent [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims]], [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik]], and [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng Landing]] continued the pattern of operations the proclamation had announced.

## Significance

The Proclamation of [[27 April]] [[1896]] is the formal declaration of war against Umar and the foundational political document of the conventional military phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1896]]. Its careful distinction between Umar and his personal followers on one hand, and the populations of the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI and IV Moekims]] on the other, is a revealing instance of colonial divide and rule strategy. Its reference to the conquests of [[Lombok]] — the most recent Dutch colonial victory, in [[1894]] — places the Aceh War within a wider imperial frame. As a historical document, the proclamation is preserved as *Bijlage VII* of *ATJEH '96* and is one of the key documents of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase of the war.

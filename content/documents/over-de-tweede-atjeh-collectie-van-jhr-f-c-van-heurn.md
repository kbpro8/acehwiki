**Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn** — *On the second Atjeh collection of Jhr. F. C. van Heurn* — is a Dutch-language ornithological paper by [[R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg|Mr. Dr. Baron R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg]], published in the *Jaarberichten* (Yearbook) of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] in [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]], describing a collection of bird skins gathered by [[F. C. van Heurn|Ir. Jhr. F. C. van Heurn]] at the estates [[Alas Peurba|Alas Peurba]] (200 metres above sea level) and [[Aloer Djamboe|Aloer Djamboe]] (50 metres above sea level) in [[Oostkust van Atjeh|East Atjeh]] in the months [[September]]–[[November]] [[1920]]. The paper is the second of two papers by Snouckaert on the avifaunal collections of Van Heurn in Atjeh, the first — the *[[Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen|Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen]]* — having described the earlier collection made in the region of [[Takengon|Lake Takengon]] (1200 metres) in [[Gajo-land|Gajo-land]].

## Authorship and character

The paper is the work of Snouckaert alone. Van Heurn, having returned to the Netherlands (*onlangs in het vaderland teruggekeerd*), asked Snouckaert to work up the collection: *De Heer van Heurn, onlangs in het vaderland teruggekeerd, heeft mij verzocht ook deze verzameling te bewerken, van welke opdracht ik mij met genoegen kwijt* — "Mr. van Heurn, recently returned to the fatherland, has asked me to work up this collection too, a commission which I have discharged with pleasure." The paper thus stands in a different relation to the collector from the later, joint paper *[[Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh|Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh]]*, which was co-authored by Van Heurn and Snouckaert. In the second-collection paper, Van Heurn is the collector and Snouckaert the author; in the later paper, the two men appear together as joint authors.

## The collecting localities

The paper opens by contrasting the localities of the second collection with those of the first. The first collection — described in *[[Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen|Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen]]*, in issue I (*afl. I*) of the previous *Jaarbericht* — had been made in the region of [[Takengon|Lake Takengon]] (1200 metres) in [[Gajo-land|Gajo-land]] (Atjeh). The second collection was made at much lower-lying localities nearer the northeast coast: *Ditmaal viel de keuze op veel lager gelegen localiteiten meer nabij de N.O.-kust, te weten de ondernemingen Alas Peurba (200 M.) en Aloer Djamboe (50 M.)* — "This time the choice fell on much lower-lying localities nearer the northeast coast, namely the estates Alas Peurba (200 M.) and Aloer Djamboe (50 M.)." The shift from the highland Gayo country to the lowland estates of the East Coast was a deliberate choice, and gave the two collections a complementary character: the first sampled the montane avifauna of the interior, the second the lowland avifauna of the coastal plain.

The collecting was done in the months [[September]]–[[November]] [[1920]]. Snouckaert records the dates of individual specimens throughout the paper, so that the chronology of the collecting can be followed in detail. The earliest date recorded is [[11 September]] [[1920]] (specimens of *Rollulus roulroul*, *Picus vittatus*, *Dendronanthus indicus*, and *Microtarsus melanocephalus* at Alas Peurba); the latest is [[25 November]] [[1920]] (specimens of *Spilornis cheela bacha*, *Eurystomus orientalis*, *Zanclostomus javanicus*, *Chloropsis cyanopogon*, and *Arachnothera affinis* at Aloer Djamboe).

## Method

Snouckaert states that he has followed the systematic order used by [[Herbert C. Robinson|Robinson]] and [[C. Boden Kloss|Kloss]] for their species list of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]], *Voor het gemak van eventueele vergelijking heb ik wederom de systematische volgorde door Robinson en Kloss voor hun soortenlijst van Sumatra aangenomen* — "For the sake of ease of comparison, I have again adopted the systematic order used by Robinson and Kloss for their species list of Sumatra." The paper is thus arranged as a taxonomic catalogue, family by family, with each entry giving the original binomial citation, the sex and number of specimens, the locality, and the date of collection. Snouckaert adds *enkele opmerkingen* — "a few remarks" — to many of the entries, in which he discusses the plumage, sex, or subspecific identification of the specimens, and compares them with material from [[Malaya|Malaya]], [[Borneo|Borneo]], [[Java|Java]], and elsewhere in the archipelago.

## The collection

The paper records specimens from a wide range of bird families, including the Phasianidae (the crested wood-partridge *Rollulus roulroul*), the Columbidae (pigeons and doves, including *Ptilinopus jambu* and *Streptopelia chinensis tigrina*), the Falconidae (the crested serpent-eagle *Spilornis cheela bacha*), the Coraciidae (the broad-billed roller *Eurystomus orientalis*), the Alcedinidae (kingfishers, including *Ceyx tridactyla* and *Halcyon concreta*), the Bucerotidae (hornbills), the Meropidae (bee-eaters, including *Merops philippinus* and *Nyctiornis amicta*), the Trogonidae (trogons), the Cuculidae (cuckoos), the Picidae (woodpeckers), the Eurylaemidae (broadbills), the Pittidae (pittas, including *Pitta granatina vanheurni*, which [[C. Boden Kloss|Kloss]] named after the collector), the Turdidae (thrushes), the Laniidae (shrikes), the Dicruridae (drongos), the Oriolidae (orioles), the Sturnidae (starlings, including the hill myna *Gracula javana javana*), the Motacillidae (wagtails and pipits), the Nectariniidae (sunbirds and spiderhunters), and others.

The paper is notable for the record of *Pitta granatina vanheurni* — a subspecies of the garnet pitta named after [[F. C. van Heurn|Van Heurn]] by [[C. Boden Kloss|Kloss]] — of which Snouckaert records a juvenile male collected at [[Alas Peurba|Alas Peurba]] on [[13 September]] [[1920]]. Snouckaert notes that the Sumatran form of the garnet pitta had only recently become known to science, and that Van Heurn's two (or three) specimens were among the very few then in collections. He records, however, that Van Heurn himself, on the basis of the material he had examined, was of the opinion that the distinction of the Sumatran form from the Malayan *coccinea* was not well founded — a difference of judgement between the collector and the ornithologist who named the subspecies.

## Relationship to the other Van Heurn–Snouckaert papers

The *Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie* is one of three papers that record the avifaunal collecting of [[F. C. van Heurn|Van Heurn]] in Atjeh. The first — the *[[Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen|Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen]]* — described the earlier collection made in the region of [[Takengon|Lake Takengon]] in [[Gajo-land|Gajo-land]] in [[1920]], and was published in issue I (*afl. I*) of the previous *Jaarbericht* of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]]. The second — the present paper — described the collection made at [[Alas Peurba|Alas Peurba]] and [[Aloer Djamboe|Aloer Djamboe]] in [[September]]–[[November]] [[1920]]. The third — the *[[Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh|Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh]]* — was co-authored by Van Heurn and Snouckaert, and described material collected on the same estates (Aloer Djamboe in [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] and Alas Peurba south of [[Lho Seumawe|Lho Seumawe]]) in a later, more comprehensive treatment.

## Deposit

The collection described in the paper was deposited with the *Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut* — the Library of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] — in [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]], as recorded on the final page of the paper.

## Significance

The *Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn* is significant within the AcehWiki corpus as the source document for the second of the two avifaunal collections made by [[F. C. van Heurn|Van Heurn]] in Atjeh in [[1920]], and as the document that records the lowland, East-Coast complement to the earlier montane, Gayo-land collection. The paper is a document of the natural-historical work that was carried on in Atjeh in the early civilian period — after the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] and the [[pacification of Atjeh|pacification]] — by Dutch scientific collectors working through the estates and the research institutions of the colony. The paper's record of *Pitta granatina vanheurni* is a document of the contribution of Van Heurn's collecting to the formal taxonomy of the Sumatran avifauna, and of the naming of an Atjehnese bird subspecies after the collector.

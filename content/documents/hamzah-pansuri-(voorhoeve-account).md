**Hamzah Pansuri** — in [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]]'s text **Hamzah Fansurī** — was the principal pantheistic mystic of [[Acheh]] in the late sixteenth century, the founder of the school of [[Wujudiyyah]] mysticism against which [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] directed his polemic after his arrival in Acheh in [[1637]]. The Voorhoeve account of [[1955]] adds substantially to the picture drawn by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in *The Achehnese* by documenting the textual basis of Hamzah's school: the *[[Asrar al-'arifin|Asrār al-ʿārifīn*]] and the *[[Kitab al-Muntahi|Kitāb al-Muntahī*]] are the principal surviving works of Hamzah, and the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of Raniri preserves, in its polemical citations, the only extended fragments of these works.

## The doctrine of the school

The doctrine of Hamzah Pansuri's school is the [[Wujudiyyah]] in its most extreme Achehnese form. The [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri places Hamzah among the lower-wind (*benedenwindse*) Wujūdiyyah — that is, the Achehnese branch of the school — and treats his doctrines alongside those of the Tanāsuḥiyya (metempsychosists), the Falāsifa (philosophers), the Barāhima-Wathaniyya, the Sāmaniyya of Tibet, and the Ḥulūliyya (those who teach the indwelling of God in creation). The juxtaposition is itself the principal scholastic characterisation of Hamzah's school that we possess.

## Works cited by Raniri

The [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri cites two works of Hamzah by name:

1. The **[[Asrar al-'arifin|*Asrār al-ʿārifīn*]]** ("The Secrets of the Gnostics") — cited in the section of the *Tibjān* on the pre-Muslim religions, where Raniri inserts a quotation from Hamzah into his translation from the [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*Tamhīd*]] of [[Abu Syakur as-Salimi|as-Sālimī]] in order to refute it. The passage cited concerns the fall of Iblīs from the light of God, and is paralleled at p. 142 of the [[J. Doorenbos|Doorenbos]] edition of the *Asrār al-ʿārifīn*.
2. The **[[Kitab al-Muntahi|*Kitāb al-Muntahī*]]** ("The Book of the Ultimate") — cited and refuted at length in the section of the *Tibjān* on the Wujūdiyyah (pp. 97–105 of the Voorhoeve facsimile). Raniri cites the *Kitāb al-Muntahī* literally, section by section, and refutes each citation in turn.

A notable passage of the *Kitāb al-Muntahī* cited by Raniri contains a play, in the language of [[Pasai]], on the words *kuphō* (Arabic *kufr*, "unbelief") and *kuphu* (Arabic *kufʾ*, "equal, like"). The play, as [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] notes, depends on the fact that *pada* in Achehnese means both "equal" (Javanese *pepada*) and "sufficient" (Malay *pada*). Hamzah's utterance — "if there is no *kuphō*, there is no meeting with *kuphu*" — Raniri takes as a covert profession of unbelief; Voorhoeve suggests that Hamzah's intended meaning was that the highest mystical state is not attained so long as the gate of the perishable world is not closed.

## The burning of the books and the death of the disciples

The accession of [[Iskandar Thani]] in [[1637]] and the [[Raniri's public debate against the Wujudiyyah in Acheh|public disputation]] of Raniri against the Wujūdiyyah were followed by the burning of the books of Hamzah's school and the execution of his disciples. The burning was not wholly effective: manuscripts of the *[[Asrar al-'arifin|Asrār al-ʿārifīn*]] and the *[[Kitab al-Muntahi|Kitāb al-Muntahī*]] survive — the latter in Cod. Or. 7291(3) and Cod. Or. 1952, with a Javanese translation in Cod. Or. 5716(2) and 7736(1) — and the *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* was edited by [[J. Doorenbos|Doorenbos]].

## Significance

Hamzah Pansuri is the principal pantheistic mystic of [[Acheh]] and the founder of the school of [[Wujudiyyah]] mysticism against which Raniri directed his polemic. The Voorhoeve account of [[1955]] adds substantially to the picture drawn by Snouck Hurgronje in *The Achehnese* by documenting the textual basis of Hamzah's school: the *[[Asrar al-'arifin|Asrār al-ʿārifīn*]] and the *[[Kitab al-Muntahi|Kitāb al-Muntahī*]] are the principal surviving works of Hamzah, and the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri preserves, in its polemical citations, the only extended fragments of these works. The play on *kuphō* and *kuphu* in the *Kitāb al-Muntahī* is a remarkable trace of the linguistic specificity of the *[[Bahasa Djawi]]* of the Achehnese court.

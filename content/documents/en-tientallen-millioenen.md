***En tientallen millioenen zijner Inheemsche bevolking kunnen thans alleen nog maar door krachtig militair optreden gered worden!*** ("And tens of millions of her indigenous population can now only be saved by vigorous military action!") was a political pamphlet by [[A.L.J. Sunier|Dr. A.L.J. Sunier]], first published in *[[Wiering's Weekrevue]]* on [[28 February]] [[1946]] and reissued as a printed reprint dated [[8 March]] [[1946]] from [[The Hague]].

## Content and Argument

The pamphlet was a direct intervention in the political debate following the end of the Second World War and the Japanese occupation of the [[Dutch East Indies]], in which the nationalist movement led by [[Sukarno]] had proclaimed Indonesian independence on [[17 August]] [[1945]]. Sunier argued that the Netherlands had both the moral authority and the practical obligation to restore Dutch colonial administration over the [[Dutch East Indies]] by force if necessary.

Sunier's argument drew explicitly on historical precedent. He called for political leaders "als een Churchill, een Colijn, een Van Heutsz" — of the caliber of [[Winston Churchill]], [[Hendrik Colijn|Colijn]], and [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. The invocation of [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] is the pamphlet's most significant point of connection to Acehnese history.

## The Van Heutsz Reference and Aceh

Sunier characterized [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as "niet in de eerste plaats de militair van den Atjèhoorlog maar de pacificateur van Adjeh" — not primarily the military man of the Aceh War but the pacifier of Aceh. In Sunier's framing, [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was a leader who "had opened the way for millions of indigenous people to security of life and property, legal certainty, and a dignified existence."

This characterization belongs to a longstanding tradition in Dutch colonial historiography that sought to reframe the [[Atjeh War]] — one of the longest and bloodiest colonial conflicts in Dutch imperial history, lasting from [[1873]] into the early twentieth century — as primarily a work of pacification and administration rather than military conquest and suppression. Sunier deployed this historiographical framing as an argument for renewed action in [[1946]]: just as [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had supposedly brought peace and order to [[Aceh]], so Dutch military force could be justified as a similarly civilizing intervention across the broader archipelago.

## Publication Details

The pamphlet was issued as a reprint (*overdruk*) from *[[Wiering's Weekrevue]]*, a Dutch periodical. It was dated [[28 February]] [[1946]] in its original appearance and [[8 March]] [[1946]] in the reprint edition, and published in [[The Hague]]. The authorship line identifies the author as "Dr. A.L.J. Sunier."

## Historical Significance

As a document of Dutch colonial discourse in the immediate postwar period, the pamphlet illustrates how the memory of the [[Atjeh War]] and figures such as [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] continued to be mobilized in political argument well after the events themselves. It is a primary source for the historiography of Dutch colonial ideology and for understanding how the [[Atjeh War]] was retrospectively interpreted in metropolitan Dutch political culture.

**Treaty of 1857 between Netherlands and Aceh** was a bilateral diplomatic agreement negotiated and signed in [[1857]] between special envoy General [[J. van Swieten]] of the [[Dutch East Indies Government]] and the royal court of the [[Aceh Sultanate]] at [[Kraton of Aceh]]. The agreement represented the last formal treaty establishing commercial and political peace between the Netherlands and Aceh before the outbreak of the Aceh War in [[1873]].

## Diplomatic Context and Provisions

Following decades of friction over coastal trade and piracy, Governor-General of the Netherlands East Indies commissioned General [[J. van Swieten]] to visit [[Kraton of Aceh]] in [[1857]]. The resulting treaty affirmed mutual friendship, regulated merchant shipping, and pledged cooperation in suppressing maritime unrest.

## Impact on Subsequent Military Intelligence

As General [[G. G. Booms]] argued in his [[1875]] analysis, the conclusion of the [[Treaty of 1857 between Netherlands and Aceh]] induced a false sense of security within the [[Dutch East Indies Government]]. Successive colonial administrations after [[1857]] neglected to systematically update their topographical and political intelligence regarding Aceh. 

Consequently, when the [[First Aceh Expedition]] was dispatched in [[1873]], the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] relied on fragmentary data gathered by [[J. van Swieten]] during his [[1857]] mission, a major contributing factor to the expedition's failure.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **Treaty of 1857 with the Sultan of Atjeh** — the "overeenkomst van vrede, vriendschap en handel" ("agreement of peace, friendship and trade") concluded by General [[Jan van Swieten|J. van Swieten]], Governor of [[Sumatra]]'s West Coast on behalf of the Netherlands Indian Government, with [[Sultan Ibrahim Mansur Shah|Ibrahim Mansoer]], who had meanwhile come forward as Sultan of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — was the last Dutch attempt, "nogmaals langs den weg der verzoening" ("once more along the road of reconciliation"), to call a better state of affairs into being before the slow escalation toward [[1873]].

### Provisions

Concluded in [[1857]], the agreement contained inter alia:

- provisions concerning the admission of each other's subjects **to the trade, the shipping and the residence** in one another's countries;
- further provisions **to combat sea-, strand- and man-robbery** (*zee-, strand- en menschenroof*);
- the agreement that **all claims on account of former facts would lapse** — burying, among other incidents, the matter of the schooner *[[Schooner Dolphijn incident (1836)|Dolphiijn]]* ([[1836]]), whose 30,000-guilder money shipment and guns the Sultan had kept for himself.

### Context and effect

The treaty answered the chronic insecurity which had produced the American chastisement of [[American bombardment of the Atjehse coast (1831)|Kuala Batee (1831)]], the Dutch expulsion of the Atjehnese from [[Singkel post reaching Atjeh border (1840)|Baros and Singkel (1840)]], the English chastisement of Koeala Batië and Koeala Meureudoe ([[Bombardment of the Atjehse coast by the British (1844)|1844]]), and the robberies of a Neapolitan schooner ([[1851]]) and an English merchantman ([[1852]]). "'t Ging goed.... tot 1858!" — it worked, author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] writes, until [[1858]], when the Netherlands Indian Government's [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|contract with the Sultan of Siak]] moved the Sultan of Atjeh to revive lapsed claims upon that realm, opening the road to the expedition of [[Dutch expedition to the North-East Coast of Sumatra (1865)|1865]], the refusal of the chiefs' petitions of sovereignty ([[Offer of Atjeh's sovereignty to the Sultan of Turkey (1868)|1868]]) and, after the [[Treaty of 1871]], the mission of [[F. N. Nieuwenhuizen|Nieuwenhuizen]] (see [[Nieuwenhuizen's mission to Atjeh and the declaration of war (March 1873)]]).

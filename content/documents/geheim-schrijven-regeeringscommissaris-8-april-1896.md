**The Geheim schrijven (secret operational instruction) from the Regeeringscommissaris** — preserved in the narrative of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* on page 60 — was the secret operational order issued by [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] to [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]], the commander of the column ordered to relieve the besieged Dutch posts of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[8 April]] [[1896]]. The instruction, issued before [[8 April]] [[1896]] — Vetter's first full day of operational command in [[Aceh]] — comprised three numbered orders: (1°) to inflict losses on the enemy; (2°) to prevent kampong burning; and (3°) authorisation to withdraw the garrison if needed. The instruction is the first operational document of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and reveals Vetter's operational priorities at the moment of his assumption of command.

## Background

[[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] had arrived in [[Aceh]] on [[7 April]] [[1896]] aboard the steamers *Carpentier* and *Bantam*, with the 9e Bataljon Infantry, [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]], and [[J. F. Breijer|Maj. Breijer]]. His arrival was the direct response of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Government at Batavia]] to the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar on 29 March 1896]] and to the consequent collapse of the [[buitenlinie]]. He immediately [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|honorably relieved Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] of his functions as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] and assumed both civil and military command of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]].

Within hours of his arrival Vetter was confronted with the operational crisis of the besieged outer posts. [[Biloel]] had been invested since the defection; [[Lamkoenjit]] had been under near-continuous attack since [[31 March]] [[1896]] and had repulsed a major night assault on [[4 April]]–[[5 April]] [[1896]]. The 8e and 14e Bataljons of the [[geconcentreerde linie]] (concentrated line) at [[Koeta Radja]] were available for a relief operation, and [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] — who had arrived with Vetter on [[7 April]] [[1896]] — was designated to command the relief column. Before the column set out on [[8 April]] [[1896]], Vetter issued the secret writing setting out the operational instructions.

## Content

The *geheim schrijven* was issued by [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] in his capacity as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] to [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] as commander of the relief column. The instruction comprised three numbered *bevelen*:

**1°** — *Inflict losses on the enemy.* The first order was that the relief column, on its march from [[Koeta Radja]] to [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]], should actively seek engagement with Umar's forces and inflict losses on them. This was not a relief operation in the limited sense of breaking the siege; it was a punitive operation that would engage and reduce Umar's forces in the field.

**2°** — *Prevent kampong burning.* The second order was that the column should prevent the burning of kampongs. This was a political as much as a military instruction: Vetter, having just assumed civil as well as military authority, was concerned to limit the destruction of civilian property and to preserve the possibility of the local population's return to Dutch allegiance. The instruction distinguishes the [[Regeeringscommissaris]]'s approach from the more indiscriminate punitive destruction that would characterise the later operations of June [[1896]].

**3°** — *Authorisation to withdraw garrison if needed.* The third order authorised the column to withdraw the garrison of [[Biloel]] and/or [[Lamkoenjit]] — that is, to evacuate the posts rather than reinforce them — if operational circumstances required. This was the formal authorisation for the evacuation that would in fact be carried out on [[12 April]] [[1896]], four days after the relief.

The three orders together define Vetter's initial operational posture: aggressive engagement of Umar's forces, restraint toward civilian property, and operational flexibility on the question of the outer posts. The instruction is secret because it authorised the potential evacuation of the outer posts — a politically sensitive decision that, if known in advance, would have damaged the morale of the garrisons and the credibility of the Dutch position.

## Reception and Aftermath

The relief column under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] set out on [[8 April]] [[1896]] and reached [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] without major opposition, though there were sharp combats along the route — including the action near Masdjid Oleh Soesoeh in which [[Kapitein F. P. A. Geluk|Kapt. Geluk]] was killed. The relief column had 1 officer and 7 men killed, 2 officers and 23 men wounded. The garrisons of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] were relieved but, in accordance with the third of Vetter's instructions, the decision was taken to evacuate both posts. The [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation]] was carried out on [[12 April]] [[1896]], with both posts burned by engineer troops; the evacuation casualties were 10 wounded.

The decision authorised by the third instruction set the pattern for the next two weeks: the [[buitenlinie]] was progressively abandoned — [[Ana' Galong|Anak-Galoeng]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Soet]] and [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]], and [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]] — and the Dutch forces concentrated around [[Koeta Radja]]. The political framework for this concentration was announced in the [[Proclamation of the Regeeringscommissaris (12 April 1896)|Proclamation of 12 April 1896]]. The first and second instructions — inflict losses, prevent kampong burning — defined the operational posture of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase until the dismissal of Umar on [[26 April]] [[1896]] and the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|Proclamation of 27 April 1896]] opened the conventional military phase.

## Significance

The *geheim schrijven* is the first operational document of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and reveals Vetter's initial operational priorities: aggressive engagement, restraint toward civilian property, and operational flexibility on the outer posts. The third instruction — authorisation to withdraw garrisons — was the legal predicate for the abandonment of the [[buitenlinie]] that defined the strategic situation of April–May [[1896]]. As a historical document, the *geheim schrijven* is preserved only in the narrative of page 60, not in the Annexes; its informal status as a "secret writing" rather than a formal *besluit* or *proclamatie* signals its operational rather than political character.

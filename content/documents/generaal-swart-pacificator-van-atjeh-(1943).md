***Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh*** was the biography of the Dutch lieutenant-general and Civil and Military Governor of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] [[H. N. A. Swart]], written by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], *Kolonel K.N.I.L. b.d.*, with the collaboration of [[H. J. Schmidt]], *Kolonel tit. K.N.I.L. b.d.*, with a foreword by His Excellency lieutenant-general K.N.I.L. b.d. [[G. K. Dijkstra]], and published at [[Maastricht]] by N.V. Leiter-Nypels in [[1943]]. Its title-page motto was *"For courage, conduct, loyalty"* — the device of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]].

## Purpose and character

In his foreword Dijkstra explicitly declined to treat the work as a life of Swart in the ordinary sense. Citing [[F. W. Stapel|Dr. F. W. Stapel]]'s remark in the introduction to *De Gouverneurs-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië* that biographical works faithfully omit notices of the living, Dijkstra wrote that he regarded du Croo's book "not as a biography of General Swart but as a broadly conceived description of the so-called Van Heutsz period with regard to the Outer Provinces* ("Outer Provinces")" — not as a biography but as a broadly conceived description of the so-called Van Heutsz period with respect to the [[Buitengewesten]].

Dijkstra wrote from personal knowledge: as captain, civil authority and simultaneously troop commander at [[Moeara Teweh]], [[Paloppo]] and [[Sigli]], he had experienced some six years of what he and his fellow "mindere goden" called the *schoonmaaktijd* under Swart as provincial administrative head. In [[Pidië]] he had only to follow the road indicated by tested predecessors, but even there the beacons had in some respects to be moved in governing "this self-assured and difficult people." It became hard work; virtually everyone did it with zeal and cheerfulness, because the chief himself led the way. For at least half the time they were absent, and on returning to the station the accumulated office work and the ordinary business of the service took up a large part of the day. For their wives those were years of self-sacrifice, loneliness and worry.

## Contents

The book is divided into seventeen chapters with appendices:

- I. From volunteer soldier to Vice-President of the [[Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]] ([[1880]]–[[1922]]); the Dutchman in the Indies and the flowering and growth of the Indies in those years.
- II. From the childhood years in [[Soekadana]] and [[Kampen]] to the lieutenant's years at [[Magelang]] ([[1863]]–[[1890]]); [[Atjeh]] in [[1890]], the first Atjeh experiences and the [[Militaire Willemsorde]]; instructor at the [[Korps Pupillen te Gombong|Pupillenschool at Gombong]]; officer of armament and lieutenant-adjutant ([[1890]]–[[1895]]); at the IInd Division of the [[Departement van Oorlog]], promotion to captain, and the beginning of the *Buitenbestek*.
- III. [[Atjeh]] from [[1890]] to [[1898]]: from [[C. Deijckerhoff|Deijckerhoff]] to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]; the insights of [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]] and the brochure *[[The Subjection of Atjeh (1892)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*; Van Heutsz becomes Civil and Military Governor of Atjeh.
- IV. As captain in Atjeh ([[1898]]–[[1900]]); Swart and his *Jannen*; promotion to knight 3rd class of the *Militaire Willemsorde* ("Military William Order") and leave to the Netherlands; back in Atjeh ([[1901]]); placed with the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussee]]* ([[1902]]) and Civil Authority at [[Poelò Raja]] and [[Lho' Seumawè]] (to [[1904]]); extraordinary promotion to major and appointment as Civil and Military Resident to the [[South and East Division of Borneo]].
- V. Governor-General Van Heutsz and the [[Opening der Buitengewesten|opening of the Buitengewesten]]; the Buitengewesten in the era of [[Non-interventiepolitiek|non-intervention]]; *[[Zelfbestuur]]* and the *[[Korte Verklaring]]*.
- VI. The [[Pacification of the South and East Division of Borneo (1905–1906)|pacification of the South and East Division of Borneo]] ([[1905]]–[[1906]]).
- VII. The [[Pacification of the Province of Celebes (1906–1908)|pacification of the province of Celebes]] ([[1906]]–[[1908]]) and the [[Onderneming tegen Soembawa (1908)|undertaking against the island of Soembawa]] ([[1908]]).
- VIII. [[Atjeh van 1905 tot 1908|Atjeh from 1905 to 1908]]: Van Heutsz commits the great mistake of his life.
- IX. The departure for Atjeh and the assumption of the government; the first tours with [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]] and the visits of the member of the Council of the Indies [[Liefrinck]].
- X. Patrols and patrol life in Atjeh; the pursuit of [[Pang Nanggròë]], [[Tjoet Meutia]] and [[T. Radja Sabi]]; the death of [[Tgk. di Barat|Teungkoe di Barat]].
- XI. [[The resistance in Atjeh 1908–1912|The resistance 1908–1912]]: (a) in [[Keureutòë]] and [[Pasè]]; (b) in [[Pidië]]; (c) in [[Bireuën]] and [[Lho' Seumawè]]; (d) in the [[Meergebied]] and the [[Dörötstreek]]; (e) on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]]; (f) in the [[Gajòlanden]].
- XII. [[The resistance in Atjeh 1913–1918|The resistance 1913–1918]]: (a) the needles in the haystack; (b) [[Atjeh-moord|Atjeh murders]] and attempts at resistance.
- XIII. [[Het Pacificeerende Bestuurswerk 1908-1918|The pacifying administrative work 1908–1918]].
- XIV. From military to civil government, and of *katjauers* and *vergissers*.
- XV. Anecdotes.
- XVI. How others judged Swart's work and how the author believes he may summarise it.
- XVII. Vice-President of the Council of the Netherlands Indies and at last a private citizen.

The appendices comprise the [[Letter of the Acehnese Chiefs to General Swart (26 September 1933)|letter of the Acehnese chiefs]] in Malay with Dutch translation, the [[Extract from the service record of H. N. A. Swart|extract from Swart's service record]], and the [[List of works consulted (General Swart)|list of works consulted]]. A [[Map of Atjeh (General Swart)|map of Atjeh]] is bound at the front, showing the Straat Malaka, the Malacca Passage, the north, east and west coasts of Atjeh, the Indian Ocean, and the islands of [[Simeuloeë|P. Simeuloeë (P. Simaloer)]] and the [[Banjak-eilanden]]. The volume is illustrated with portraits of Swart's principal collaborators and with photographs of the [[Gouverneurswoning te Koetaradja|Governor's residence at Koetaradja]], the [[Tjakra Donja|historic bell Tjakra Donja]], the laying of the first stone of the [[Javasche Bank te Koetaradja]] in [[1915]], the [[Meeting of the Governor with the administrative officials at Koetaradja (1913)|1913 meeting of the Governor with his civil and military administrative officials]], and Swart's journeys along the [[Gajòweg]].

## Significance

The book is the fullest connected Dutch narrative of the closing decade of the [[Atjeh-oorlog]] and of the transition from military to civil government in Atjeh, and preserves in quotation a great deal of material — Swart's spoken recollections, extracts from the *[[Monthly Reports of the Government of Atjeh and Dependencies|maandverslagen]]*, the *[[Koloniale Verslagen]]*, private letters, and press articles — that would otherwise be dispersed or lost. Its bias is openly apologetic for Swart and for the Van Heutsz school, and its judgements of [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] and of Snouck Hurgronje must be read as such.

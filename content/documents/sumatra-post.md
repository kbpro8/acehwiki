**Sumatra Post** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

The [[Nieuwe Courant]] chronicler of Atjeh affairs — reprinted in the *[[Sumatra Post]]* of [[25 November]] [[1912]] — read the promotion to lieutenant-general as "the confirmation of a policy which from its beginning meant a true revolution, a reversal of ideas concerning our administrative task in the north of Sumatra."

### From [[Hendrikus Colijn]]

Colijn, "the future adviser for the Outer Provinces," visited Atjeh again shortly before [[March]] [[1914]], by then already a former minister; his judgement of the situation then agreed entirely with Swart's own, as expressed in the [[Sumatra Post]] interview, and with that of the minister of Colonies who had proposed replacing the military authority in Atjeh wholly by the civil.

### From [[Pidië]]

In the interview Swart gave to the *[[Sumatra Post]]* on [[6 March|6]] and [[7 March]] [[1914]] he reported that in "the Pedir country, once the focal point of resistance (180,000 inhabitants)" there were still ten ill-disposed men wandering about with two rifles — for whom, however, all twelve brigades of a division of *marechaussée* plus twelve brigades of infantry, some five hundred men in all, were searching, partly to reassure public opinion, "otherwise it would merely be said that Atjeh is being stripped of troops."

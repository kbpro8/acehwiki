**Van Swieten's proclamation on the sovereignty of Atjeh** was the celebrated declaration issued by [[Jan van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]] after the fall of the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] in [[1874]], by which the Netherlands announced that it had taken the place of the [[Sultan of Atjeh|sultan]] and assumed sovereignty over the whole of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. In the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November]] [[1897]] the Catholic deputy [[Bahlmann]] identified it as "de tweede groote fout" (the second great error) of the whole war.

## Text as cited

[[Bahlmann]] quoted the proclamation in the chamber in these terms: *"Wij Nederlanders zijn gekomen in plaats van den Sultan. Wij hebben de Souvereiniteit over het geheele land aanvaard. Het geheele land moet aan onze heerschappij worden onderworpen." — We Netherlanders have come in the place of the Sultan. We have accepted the Sovereignty over the whole country. The whole country must be subjected to our dominion.* — We Netherlanders have come in the place of the Sultan. We have accepted the sovereignty over the whole country. The whole country must be subjected to our dominion.

## Bahlmann's critique

[[Bahlmann]] set the proclamation in a sequence of two founding errors. The first, he said, lay already in the first dispatch of the Minister [[Isaac Dignus Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] — "souvereiniteitserkenning of oorlog" (recognition of sovereignty or war) — which had made it impossible for the statesmen and soldiers of the day to conceive any arrangement other than direct Dutch rule; they could not envisage for Atjeh a state of affairs such as existed in Central Java, with its surviving princely houses, but only the incorporation of Atjeh under direct Dutch administration. The second error was Van Swieten's proclamation, which converted that assumption into a public and irrevocable claim.

To this pair of errors Bahlmann applied the maxim he attributed to the elder Cato of the Romans: *"Quod ab initio non valet, tractu temporis convalescere non potest"* — what is invalid from the beginning cannot be made good by lapse of time — glossed in the pamphlet's footnote as "Wat van den aanvang af niet deugt, kan door geen verloop van tijd in orde worden gebracht." — What is not sound from the beginning cannot be put right by any lapse of time.

Bahlmann further observed that a third means was now being applied to reach the *pacificatie* of Atjeh, and that neither the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Indies government]] nor the minister would hear of the [[Restoration of the sultanate of Atjeh (proposal)|restoration of the sultanate]] as a condition of peace; since sovereignty over [[Groot-Atjeh]] was insisted upon, he urged that no half-measures be taken but that the course adopted be consistently pursued.

**Onze vestiging in Atjeh, critisch beschreven** ("Our Establishment in Aceh, critically described") is a critical Dutch-language military history of the opening phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], written by [[G. F. W. Borel|Kapitein der Artillerie G. F. W. Borel]] and published at 's-Gravenhage by D. A. Thieme in [[1878]], after the conflict had already lasted more than five years. Composed in seventeen chapters and furnished with two folded maps — a *Kaart van Groot-Atjeh* and a *Schets van het Bivouak Penajoeng en van den Kraton met omgeving* at 1:10 000 — the book is both a narrative of the [[First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)|first expedition]] under [[Generaal Köhler]] and of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]] and occupation under [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] and [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]], and a sustained polemic against the *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture) that, in Borel's judgement, squandered the fruits of the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] and prolonged the war. It closes with Borel's own prescription — an [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] raised in the Netherlands — and bears the author's signature at 's-Gravenhage on [[27 August]] [[1878]].

## Author and Purpose

Borel was a Kapitein der Artillerie, Ridder van de Militaire Willemsorde, and bearer of the Eresabel, who had personally served in Atjeh; he supervised the debarkation of the guns at [[Oeleh Leh]] and traced the new zuider-face battery on the morning of [[14 August]] [[1874]] under a twelve-shot Atjehnese salvo that missed him and his officer only by inches. In his *Voorberigt* he disclaims "partijschap" and acknowledges the work's "gebrekkig en onvolledig" character, but insists on the duty to render "de feiten en toestanden naar waarheid." His aim was to spread "meerdere kennis betreffende de Atjeh-aangelegenheden" at a moment when the war had "weder zoo geheel op den voorgrond" returned.

## Structure and Contents

Borel divides the Atjeh-oorlog into five periods: (1) the [[First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)|first expedition]] under [[Generaal Köhler]]; (2) the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]] under [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] down to the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|inneming en bezetting van den Kraton]]; (3) the *tijdperk der afwachtende of defensieve houding*; (4) the return to the *aanvallende tactiek*; and (5) the *periode van stilstand* in which the book is written ([[1878]]).

The first chapters rehearse the failure of [[Generaal Köhler]] before the Kraton in April [[1873]], the [[Atjeh-enquête|onzalige enquête]] that followed, and the assembly of a vastly stronger expeditionary force on Java and Sumatra's Westkust in November 1873. Borel then narrates, with heavy critical commentary, the [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing at Pedro-punt]] ([[6 December]] [[1873]]), the [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|bloody reconnaissances at Lemboe]] on Christmas 1873, the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|storming of the Mesigit]] ([[6 January]] [[1874]]), the [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|nineteen-day bombardment]] and the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] that delivered the deserted Kraton to the Dutch. The central chapters indict [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] for declaring the war ended, for recalling the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadgeschut]] to Java, for the [[Padi-snijden on the Langkroek sawah (1874)|padi-snijden]] concession, and for the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|échec at Lampoe Oek]] on [[16 April]] [[1874]], after which he pronounced his [[Van Swieten's afscheidsrede (1874)|afscheidsrede]] ("Ik beschouw den oorlog met Atjeh als geëindigd") and departed on [[26 April]] [[1874]].

The later chapters are a vindication of [[Generaal Pel|Kolonel Pel]], who inherited the *zeer gebrekkige stelling* and rebuilt it through a long series of operations — the [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|battle of Lampoeloe]] ([[20 June]] [[1874]]), the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] ([[28 June]] [[1874]]), the [[Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)|battle of Soerian]] ([[26 July]] [[1874]]), the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] ([[7 November]] [[1874]]), the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|disastrous battle of Longbatta]] ([[31 December]] [[1874]]), and the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great offensive of December 1875 – February 1876]] — culminating in his sudden [[Death of Generaal Pel (24 February 1876)|death at the Lamjoeng bivouac]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]]. The book's final chapters describe the reversion to the passive system under [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]], the [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]] during [[Karel van der Heijden|van der Heijden]]'s absence at [[Gedoeng]], and Borel's own proposal for an [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]].

## Critical Stance and Sources

Borel writes as an artilleryman and a participant-observer, freely attributing the prolongation of the war to named commanders. He repeatedly invokes foreign analogues — the Germans at Sédan ([[1870]]), the Russians before Plevna ([[1877]]), the French expedition against Algiers ([[1830]]) — to press the argument that victories must be pursued and that the Atjehneezen, "uit eene geheel bijzondere stof gekneed," could be subdued only by the *kracht der wapens* and never by proclamations, gifts, or passive posts. He cites the [[Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken (1873)|Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken]] booklet distributed to officers before the second expedition, [[Generaal Booms|generaal Booms]]'s critique, [[Generaal Knoop|generaal Knoop]]'s judgement that the second expedition was "slechts ten deele gelukt," and the [[De Rochemont brochure|brochure of gep. luit. kol. de Rochemont]].

## Significance

The work remains a principal Dutch primary source for the military conduct of the Atjeh War between [[1873]] and [[1878]], valued for its detailed tactical narratives, its candour about Dutch reverses (notably the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|Lampoe Oek échec]] and the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|Longbatta disaster]]), and its partisan but well-documented defence of [[Generaal Pel]]. Its proposed [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] anticipated later metropolitan reinforcements, and its characterisation of the [[Atjehnees volkskarakter (Borel)|Atjehnees volkskarakter]] — "woest, dapper, vrijheidlievend" — fixed a Dutch military stereotype that would be echoed by later colonial writers.

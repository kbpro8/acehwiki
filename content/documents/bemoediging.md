**Bemoediging** was the anonymous defense of the Dutch retreat from the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)]]. It became one of the main targets of [[H. B. van Daalen]] in [[De enquête over den mislukten tocht naar Atjeh]]. In Van Daalen's reading, the text looked like a military explanation of the failure in [[Aceh|Atjeh]], but in practice it served as a public defense of [[James Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]].

The pamphlet is important because it stood between journalism and government. It claimed insider knowledge, argued that the withdrawal had been necessary, and helped shape the first official-sounding public explanation of the Dutch defeat.

## Anonymous authorship and suspicion

Van Daalen described the writer of **Bemoediging** as an officer who seemed to know far too much about confidential matters. The author wrote as if he knew the letters sent from [[Aceh|Atjeh]], the discussions in the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]], and the opinions of senior commanders.

Because of this tone, Van Daalen suspected that the writer belonged to the close circle of [[James Loudon|Loudon]], perhaps even one of his adjutants. He also pointed to the aggressive language of the article, which attacked newspaper critics but was not punished.

## The case for necessity

The main argument of **Bemoediging** was simple: the retreat had been forced by weather and logistics. The west monsoon threatened to cut the expedition off from the roadstead, which was its operational base. Without safe communication to that base, the army could not continue to fight on enemy soil.

The pamphlet therefore treated the retreat not as a disgrace, but as a hard necessity. It also argued that once the commanders in Aceh asked for orders after hearing the military council, no decision other than recall remained possible.

## Conflict with Loudon's own wording

Van Daalen compared **Bemoediging** with Loudon's day order to the returning troops in [[May]] [[1873]]. He believed the two texts worked toward the same end: they admitted loss, but insisted that much had still been gained, and above all they shielded the Governor-General.

At the same time, Van Daalen pointed out a contradiction. **Bemoediging** blamed the government commissioner for not deciding on his own, while Loudon's own wording suggested that the commissioner had formally requested authority to withdraw and had done so on the basis of the commanders' advice. Van Daalen used this difference to argue that the public defense of the expedition was confused and politically self-protective.

## Place in the pamphlet war

In the printed debate of [[1873]], **Bemoediging** became one of the first attempts to turn defeat into justification. That is why Van Daalen spent so much energy attacking it. The article was not just one more newspaper opinion. It helped define how the Dutch public was first told to understand the failed expedition.

## Significance

**Bemoediging** mattered because it shows how fast the first Dutch defeat in Aceh became a battle over interpretation. The pamphlet argued that necessity had ruled the day. Van Daalen answered that poor judgement, weak preparation, and a rush to protect the highest authorities were the deeper causes. The document therefore stands at the center of the first press war of the [[Aceh War]].

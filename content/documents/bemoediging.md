**Bemoediging** was the anonymous defense of the Dutch retreat from the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)]]. It became one of the main targets of [[H. B. van Daalen]] in [[De enquête over den mislukten tocht naar Atjeh]]. In Van Daalen's reading, the text presented itself as a military explanation of the failure in [[Aceh|Atjeh]], but in practice it worked as a public defense of [[James Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]].

The pamphlet was important because it stood between journalism and government. It claimed knowledge of confidential discussions about the campaign, and it argued that the withdrawal from [[Aceh|Atjeh]] had been unavoidable.

## Anonymous authorship and public suspicion

Van Daalen described the writer of **Bemoediging** as an officer who wrote like an insider. The author spoke as if he knew the letters sent from [[Aceh|Atjeh]], the debates in the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]], and the views of the senior commanders. Because of that tone, Van Daalen suspected that the author belonged to the close circle of [[James Loudon|Loudon]], perhaps even one of his adjutants.

Van Daalen also stressed the article's aggressive tone. It attacked newspaper critics as ignorant and cowardly, yet no punishment followed. To Van Daalen, this looked like another sign that the writer stood close to official power.

## The argument of necessity

The central claim of **Bemoediging** was that the retreat had been forced by weather and logistics. The west monsoon threatened to cut the expedition off from its base at the roadstead. If communication with the base was lost, the army could not continue fighting on enemy ground. In this argument, nature itself had ordered the withdrawal.

The pamphlet also defended the chain of command. It argued that once the commanders in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] asked for orders after receiving the military council's advice, no decision other than recall remained possible.

## Conflict with Loudon's own day order

Van Daalen compared **Bemoediging** with [[James Loudon|Loudon]]'s day order of [[May]] [[1873]] to the returning troops. He believed both texts shared the same purpose: to present the retreat as unavoidable while protecting the Governor-General from blame.

At the same time, he pointed out a contradiction. **Bemoediging** blamed the government commissioner for failing to decide on his own, while Loudon's day order stated that the commissioner had formally asked permission to withdraw and had supported that request with the unanimous advice of the commanders. Van Daalen used this contradiction to argue that the official public defense of the expedition had become confused and self-serving.

## Place in the early pamphlet war

In the printed debate of [[1873]], **Bemoediging** became one of the first attempts to turn defeat into justification. That is why Van Daalen devoted so much space to attacking it. The article helped shape the first public explanation of why the Dutch had failed in [[Aceh|Atjeh]], and critics therefore treated it as a key political document.

## Significance

**Bemoediging** mattered because it showed how fast the military defeat in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] became a struggle over interpretation. It argued that weather, communications, and necessity had forced the Dutch retreat. Van Daalen answered that argument by saying the real problem lay deeper: in the decision for war, in poor preparation, and in the effort to protect the highest authorities after failure.

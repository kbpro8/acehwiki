**The Commandementsorder on military discipline of 27 June 1896** — issued on the day of [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s [[Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)|farewell parade]] at the *Petjoet* exercise terrain near [[Koeta Radja]] on [[27 June]] [[1896]] on the order of the Army Commander — was the general order reminding all commanders in [[Aceh]] of the provisions of Title VII of the Criminal Code for the Army regarding acts of violence and poaching. The order cited Articles 174, 176, 177, 181, and 184 of the Code, restricted the burning of property to instances ordered by commanders, and prohibited the killing or taking of poultry, goats, sheep, and rams. The order is preserved in the narrative of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* on page 332 — the final page of the main text — and constitutes the closing administrative act of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## Background

The order was issued in the immediate aftermath of the heaviest fighting of the first semester of [[1896]]. The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] had broken [[Teuku Umar]]'s stelling in the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] and captured his house at [[Lampisang|Lam-Pisang]]. The [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims]] on [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]], the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik]] on [[16 June]]–[[17 June]] [[1896]], and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] had imposed widespread destruction on the kampongs of the hostile mukims. The Dutch forces had engaged in significant punitive destruction — including the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] from [[30 May]] to [[2 June]] [[1896]] by 800 forced labourers, 400 Chinese *koelies*, and engineer troops, and the [[Burning of kampongs W. of Pantjoeran (24 May 1896)|burning of kampongs west of Pantjoeran]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]].

The scale of the destruction had begun to attract concern at the level of the *Legercommandant*. The *Commandementsorder* of [[27 June]] [[1896]] was the formal expression of that concern — a reminder to commanders that the *geweldenarijen en strooperijen* of individual soldiers were criminal offences under Title VII of the the Dutch military criminal code, and that the burning of property and the taking of livestock were permissible only on the explicit orders of commanders.

## Content

The *Commandementsorder* was issued on the order of the *Legercommandant* — that is, of [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] himself, in his capacity as Army Commander — on [[27 June]] [[1896]], the day of his [[Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)|farewell parade]] at the *Petjoet* exercise terrain near [[Koeta Radja]]. Its substantive content was a reminder to all commanders of the provisions of Title VII of the *Crimineel Wetboek voor het krijgsvolk te lande* — the Dutch military criminal code — regarding acts of violence and poaching.

The order cited five specific Articles of the Code: Articles 174, 176, 177, 181, and 184. These Articles governed, respectively, the criminal liability of soldiers for violence against persons and property not authorised by operational necessity, the unauthorised taking of livestock and other property, the destruction of dwellings and religious buildings, and the liability of commanders who failed to prevent or punish such offences by their subordinates. The specific citation of these Articles — rather than a general reference to the Code — indicates that the *Legercommandant* was concerned with specific patterns of misconduct that had arisen during the operations of May and June [[1896]].

The order further provided two specific operational rules. First, the burning of property — kampongs, dwellings, religious buildings, crops — was permissible only on the explicit orders of commanders; individual soldiers or units were not free to burn property on their own initiative. Second, the killing or taking of poultry, goats, sheep, and rams was prohibited. This second rule was a response to the endemic problem of poaching by soldiers in the field, who had been taking livestock from Acehnese kampongs for food and profit throughout the operations.

## Reception and Aftermath

The order was issued on the day of [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]]'s farewell parade at the *Petjoet* exercise terrain, [[27 June]] [[1896]] — the last full day of his presence in [[Aceh]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris]]. The following day, [[28 June]] [[1896]], [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]] and [[J. F. Breijer|Maj. Breijer]] departed for [[Batavia]] aboard the steamer *Coen*. The office of [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]] was restored, [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] was appointed temporarily charged with the Civil and Military Authority, and [[K. F. H. van Langen|Resident Van Langen]] was honorably relieved of his functions as Civil Administrator. The *Commandementsorder* of [[27 June]] [[1896]] was thus the final administrative act of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase.

The order's effect on subsequent operations is not directly recorded in *ATJEH '96*. The tuchtiging operations of [[June]] [[1896]] had already been conducted; the subsequent operations of the second semester of [[1896]] lie outside the scope of Kruisheer's first volume. The order nonetheless stands as the formal Dutch recognition that the *tuchtiging* operations of May–June [[1896]] had raised issues of military discipline that required explicit address.

## Significance

The *Commandementsorder* of [[27 June]] [[1896]] is the closing administrative document of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and a revealing instance of Dutch colonial military justice in action. Its citation of specific Articles of the *Crimineel Wetboek voor het krijgsvolk te lande* — and its specific prohibition of livestock-taking and unauthorised burning — reveals the patterns of misconduct that had arisen during the *tuchtiging* operations of May and June [[1896]]. Its issuance on the day of Vetter's farewell parade places it at the symbolic closing of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] phase; its preservation as the final page of the main text of *ATJEH '96* gives it a particular documentary weight. As a historical document, the order is the Dutch colonial government's own acknowledgement that the conventional military phase of the war had imposed costs on the Acehnese civilian population that the *Legercommandant* himself felt bound to address.

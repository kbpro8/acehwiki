**The Government Resolution of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim** (*Gouvernementsbesluit van 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim*) — reproduced as one of the official documents gathered in the Annexes of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the secret decree of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Government of Nederlandsch-Indië]] that authorised the appointment of [[Teuku Umar]] under the new official name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government. The resolution, classified secret, formalised the Dutch policy of seeking peace in [[Groot-Atjeh]] through the alliance of native auxiliaries — a policy described by Kruisheer as resting on "een zwakke basis" (a weak foundation) because it depended on the good faith of a single man. It was the legal foundation of Umar's two-and-a-half-year career as a Dutch ally and, by consequence, of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] that brought that policy crashing down.

## Background

The resolution grew out of a Dutch strategic reappraisal following the difficult years of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the late 1880s and early 1890s. Umar had rendered good services to the *Kompeni* in [[August]] [[1893]] by the "*zuivering der XXV Moekims*" — the "cleansing" (pacification) of the [[Sagi der XXV Moekims|XXV Moekims]] — and his value as a native intermediary was evident. The [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] proposed to formalise Umar's role by appointing him to a new office — *[[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government of Nederlandsch-Indië* — with the rank, the symbols, and the war material appropriate to that position. The Government at [[Batavia]] approved, and the formal *Gouvernementsbesluit* was issued on [[5 September]] [[1893]] under the secret classification.

The resolution was the legal predicate of the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|formal act of subjection]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]], at which Umar and 15 of his war leaders talso the [[Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893|Oath of Loyalty]] before the Dutch administration at [[Koeta Radja]]. On [[21 October]] [[1893]] T. Djohan was given leadership of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]; on [[30 November]] [[1893]] he conquered [[Ana' Galong|Ana' Galoeng]] for the Dutch. These acts all flowed from the authority granted by the *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[5 September]] [[1893]].

## Content

The resolution authorised the appointment of *Toekoe Oemar* — under the new official name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* — as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government of Nederlandsch-Indië. The conferral of a new name was not a matter of casual courtesy: it constituted Umar, in the formal Dutch register, as a new man and a new officer, distinct from the *Toekoe Oemar* who had previously been a sometime-enemy of the *Kompeni*. The office of *[[Panglima Prang Besar]]* — literally "Great War Commander" — placed him above the ordinary territorial chiefs and made him the Government's principal native warlord in [[Groot-Atjeh]], with authority to raise, equip, and command auxiliary forces against the recalcitrant [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkrak]] mukims and other hostile districts.

The resolution was classified *geheim* — secret — because the Dutch understood that the visible elevation of a former enemy to a senior war command would provoke resistance both among the *hoeloebalang* who had remained loyal and among the clerics and *hoeloebalang* of the hostile mukims. The text of the resolution is preserved in Kruisheer's *Bijlagen* alongside the [[Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893|Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893]] and the subsequent [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Government Resolution of 19 January 1896 No. 9]] appointing Umar as *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]].

## Reception and Aftermath

The immediate aftermath of the resolution was the formal subjection ceremony of [[30 September]] [[1893]] and the [[1893]] campaigns that brought [[Ana' Galong|Ana' Galoeng]] and the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] under Dutch control. For two and a half years Umar served as the linchpin of Dutch policy in [[Groot-Atjeh]], commanding his *legioen* of native auxiliaries, drawing Dutch pay and Dutch weapons, and — according to [[Ketjihik Oemar]]'s telegrams — meeting busily with the heads of the clergy in ways the Dutch did not fully understand. Kruisheer records that the policy was recognised at the time as a "zwakke basis" because it rested on one man.

That basis collapsed on [[29 March]] [[1896]] with the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection]]. The *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[5 September]] [[1893]] was effectively abrogated by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]], which dismissed Umar from the office of [[Panglima Prang Besar]] and from the *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Resolution of 19 January 1896]], and stipulated that he would henceforth be officially designated by his old name *Toekoe Oemar*. The renaming of [[1893]] was thus symbolically undone.

## Significance

The *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[5 September]] [[1893]] is the founding document of the Dutch-alliance phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] ([[1893]]–[[1896]]). Its language — conferring a new name, a senior war command, and an explicit government commission on a former enemy — captures the ambition and the risk of the *stelsel*. Its abrogation by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution of 26 April 1896]] marks the formal end of that policy and the beginning of the conventional military phase of the war under [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Vetter]]. As a historical document, the resolution is indispensable to understanding both Umar's rise and his fall.

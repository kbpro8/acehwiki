**Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu** ("Land and People of Atjeh, Past and Present") is a Dutch-language colonial-era monograph on [[Aceh]] by [[J. Jongejans|J. J. Jongejans]], formerly *Oud-Resident van het Gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (Former Resident of the Region of Atjeh and Dependencies), published by Hollandia Drukkerij N.V. in [[Baarn]] around [[1938]]. With a foreword by Dr. [[H. Colijn|Hendrikus Colijn]], the work is a comprehensive survey of the geography, history, ethnography, economy, administration, and military history of Atjeh, written from the perspective of a senior colonial administrator who had served in the region.

## Author and Authority

[[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] wrote the book in retirement, drawing on his first-hand experience as *Resident* of Atjeh (he was succeeded by [[Pauw]] on [[30 September]] [[1938]]) and on the assistance of serving officials. In his preface he acknowledges the help of officials named [[Pelger]] (who assisted with Chapter I, on history), [[Klerks]] (who assisted with Chapters V and VII, on economics and administration), and others who supplied the most recent data. The book was written in [[1938]] and the author notes that the Pretendent-[[Sultan]] [[Toeankoe Moehamad Dawot]] died in [[Batavia]] in April of the year preceding publication, placing the book in the late [[1938]] or early [[1939]] period.

## Foreword by Dr. H. Colijn

The foreword was written by Dr. [[H. Colijn]], the former Prime Minister of the Netherlands and himself a former colonial official with Atjeh experience (he served as a young lieutenant under [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and was decorated for the [[Lho'Soekon]] expedition of the 1890s). Colijn commended the book for showing "overeenstemming tusschen woord en werkelijkheid" (agreement between word and reality) and recommended it as a corrective to the prevailing image of Atjeh as merely a theatre of military operations, arguing that the fruits of Dutch political and military action should also be observed.

## Structure and Contents

The book is divided into ten chapters covering:

1. **Historical overview** — from the Hindu colonization through the [[Aceh Sultanate]], the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] ([[1873]]–[[1918]]), and the [[Aceh-moord|Atjeh-murder]] phenomenon.
2. **The land and its regions** — geography of [[Groot-Atjeh]], the [[Noordkust]] (North Coast), [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust]] (East Coast), [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]] (West Coast), [[Gajoland|Gajolanden]], [[Alasland|Alaslanden]], and the [[Atjehsche Eilanden|islands]].
3. **The people** — the [[Acehnese|Atjehers]], [[Gayo|Gajos]], [[Alaslanders|Alassers]], and other ethnic groups; their customs, religion, and social organization.
4. **The Atjehnese family** — rites of passage, performance traditions, dress, food, and burial customs.
5. **Economic survey** — agriculture (rice, [[Patchouli|patchouly/nilam]], coconut, [[Pinang|betel nut]], rubber, kapok, coffee, spices, pepper), livestock, mining (oil, coal, gold), forestry, fishing, native industry, and trade, with detailed [[1937]] export statistics.
6. **Communications** — the [[Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen|Atjeh railway]], roads, motor services, and shipping.
7. **Administration and clergy** — the colonial civil administration, the [[oelèëbalang]] system, the [[sagi]] federation of [[Groot-Atjeh]], and the religious hierarchy ([[ulama|ulama]], [[teungkoe|teungkoe]], [[imeum|imeum]]).
8. **Atjeh figures** — biographical sketches of the principal [[Acehnese]] leaders: [[Teukoe Oemar]], [[Cut Nyak Dhien|Tjoct Nja Din]], [[Panglima Polém]], the Pretendent-[[Sultan]] [[Toeankoe Moehamad Dawot]], the four generations of [[Teungkoe Tirò]], and others.
9. **Atjeh-murders** — the phenomenon of [[Aceh-moord|Atjeh-moord]], with year-by-year statistics [[1910]]–[[1938]] and the [[Amat Leupon]] case study of [[1933]].
10. **The Army** — the military history from [[1819]] to the late [[1930s]], including the [[marechaussee]] campaigns and the transition to civilian rule on [[2 September]] [[1918]].

## Significance

The work is one of the principal late-colonial surveys of Atjeh and is cited by [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]] in the bibliography of *Atjèh and the War with Japan* alongside the works of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]], [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]], and [[H. C. Zentgraaff|Zentgraaff]]. It provides a unique administrator's-eye view of Atjeh in the final decade before the [[Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies|Japanese invasion]], combining economic statistics, administrative detail, and personal recollection. The book is the source for the present wiki's articles on many Atjeh-related subjects drawn from the [[1938]] snapshot.

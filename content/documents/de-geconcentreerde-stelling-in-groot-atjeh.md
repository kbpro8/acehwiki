**De Geconcentreerde Stelling in Groot-Atjeh** (The Concentrated Position in Great Aceh) is an 1885 Dutch publication providing a detailed account of the strategic reorganization of Dutch military positions in [[Groot-Atjeh]] following the concentration decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]]. The publication includes a detailed description of the new fortified line, the evacuation of external posts, and an assessment of the new strategy.

## Publication Details

- **Title:** De Geconcentreerde Stelling in Groot-Atjeh met Schetskaart
- **Publisher:** 's-Gravenhage: De Gebroeders Van Cleef
- **Year:** 1885
- **Format:** Octavo, with sketch map

## Contents

### Part I: Het Besluit der Concentratie

Describes the decision to concentrate forces, the strategic options considered, and the reasoning behind choosing the specific line.

### Part II: De Stelling

Provides detailed descriptions of all posts in the new line, their armament, garrison strength, and infrastructure.

### Part III: Kort Overzicht (Augustus 1884–1 Maart 1885)

Chronicles the implementation of the concentration, including construction, evacuations, and casualties.

### Part IV: Results and Assessment

Evaluates the expected results of the new policy and discusses the debate over defensive versus offensive strategy.

## Significance

The publication provides invaluable primary source material for understanding Dutch military strategy in the [[Aceh War]] during the critical transition from offensive to defensive operations. It offers detailed information about:

- Fortifications and their specifications
- Military operations and movements
- Strategic reasoning behind policy decisions
- Contemporary assessment of the Acehnese resistance
- Infrastructure development

## Key Topics Covered

- The decision-making process leading to concentration
- Detailed descriptions of posts and their armaments
- The withdrawal from scattered positions
- The state of the Acehnese population and resistance
- The role of religious leaders and holy war ideology
- The administration of occupied territory
- Debate over defensive strategy

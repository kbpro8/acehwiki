**The War Declaration Manifesto of 26 March 1873** was the formal instrument by which [[F. N. Nieuwenhuizen|Government Commissioner Nieuwenhuizen]], acting on behalf of the [[Netherlands-India|Government of the Netherlands-Indies]], declared war upon [[Sultan Aladin Mahmoed Shah|Sultan Mahmud Shah]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Atjeh]]. It was issued aboard {{HMS Citadel van Antwerpen}}, lying before Groot-Atjeh, on [[26 March]] [[1873]], after the Sultan had failed to provide the clarifications demanded by the Commissioner's letters of [[22 March]] and [[24 March]]. The manifesto set out the legal and diplomatic grounds for the declaration and remains the foundational document of the [[Aceh War]].

## Full Text

The manifesto, as recorded in Van Swieten's account, read as follows:

"The Commissioner of the Government of the Netherlands-Indies for Atjeh:

Considering that upon the Government of the Netherlands-Indies rests the obligation to protect the general regulations of trade and shipping in the East-Indian Archipelago against hindrance;

That those interests have been continuously damaged by the mutual disputes and hostilities of the statelets subordinate to the realm of Atjeh, some of which have repeatedly invoked the protection of the Netherlands-Indian Government;

That the repeated representations from that Government to bring such a state of affairs to an end and to create a well-established understanding with Atjeh have always been met with the unwillingness and complete indifference of the rulers of that realm, and with their powerlessness to maintain order in the subordinate territories;

That those efforts have even been answered with egregious treachery at a time when the Netherlands-Indian Government, with the most benevolent intentions, had placed itself in closer relations with Atjeh;

That the Sultan of Atjeh, having been expressly asked for clarification first by his letter of the 22nd and then by that of the 24th of this month, has not only wholly failed to provide it but has not even contradicted the grievances brought against him and has moreover proceeded to arm himself for war in the most conspicuous manner;

That no other meaning can be attributed to this than that Atjeh has wilfully insulted the Government of the Netherlands-Indies and wishes to maintain itself in the hostile position thereby assumed;

That the rulers of that realm have thereby rendered themselves guilty of breach of the treaty of trade, peace and friendship concluded between it and the Netherlands-Indian Government on [[30 March]] [[1857]], and it has thus been proven that no reliance can be placed on the good faith of those rulers;

That it is therefore no longer possible for the Government of the Netherlands-Indies, without vigorous measures, to ensure a state of affairs demanded both by the commercial interest and by the requirements of its security in northern Sumatra;

Declares, by virtue of the power and authority granted to him by the Government of the Netherlands-Indies, in the name of that Government, war upon the Sultan of Atjeh, of which he furthermore gives notice to all whom it may concern, and reminds everyone of all possible consequences flowing therefrom and of the obligations resting upon every citizen of the State in wartime.

Done aboard {{HMS Citadel van Antwerpen}}, lying before Groot-Atjeh, on today Wednesday the 26th of March 1873."

## Legal Grounds

The manifesto invoked several grounds for the declaration:

1. **The obligation to protect trade**: The Netherlands-Indian Government's duty to protect the general regulations of trade and shipping in the East Indian Archipelago against hindrance.

2. **Damage to interests**: The continuous damage to those interests caused by the disputes and hostilities among the statelets subordinate to Atjeh.

3. **Failed diplomacy**: The repeated but unsuccessful attempts to establish good relations with Atjeh.

4. **Treachery**: The response to Dutch overtures with "egregious treachery" (*verregaande trouweloosheid*).

5. **Failure to respond**: The Sultan's failure to provide the demanded clarifications in his responses to the letters of [[22 March]] and [[24 March]].

6. **Military preparations**: The Sultan's conspicuous arming for war, interpreted as evidence of hostile intent.

7. **Breach of treaty**: The violation of the [[Treaty of 30 March 1857 with the Sultan of Atjeh|Treaty of 30 March 1857]], which had established peace, friendship, and good understanding between the two realms.

## Criticism

[[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] later criticized the manifesto and the process by which it was reached. He argued that the negotiations had been conducted through inappropriate intermediaries — the merchant [[Sidi Tahil]] instead of a European official — and that the Commissioner's failure to exploit the possibility of personal contact with [[Toengkoe Kadli Malikoel Adil|Toengkoe Kali]] or the Sultan himself was the root cause of the diplomatic failure. "Probably the war would not have come," Van Swieten wrote, "if the negotiations of March [[1873]] had been better conducted."

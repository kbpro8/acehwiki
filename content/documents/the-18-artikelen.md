**The 18 artikelen** — the *verklaring in 18 artikelen en acte van bevestiging* (declaration in 18 articles and act of confirmation) by which the *hoofden* (chiefs) of the submitted statelets of [[Atjeh]]'s *Onderhoorigheden* (Dependencies) recognised Dutch sovereignty and bound themselves to specified obligations — is the standardised surrender-declaration of the Atjeh War, reproduced by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in his [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* (The Subjugation of Atjeh) as *Bijlage II* (Appendix II) to the book. The 18 artikelen were, in Van Heutsz's reading, the legal foundation of the colony's *onderwerping* (subjugation) — the sworn articles by which each submitting statelet recognised Dutch authority — and the legal basis on which the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] (shipping regulation) of [[Scherer]] and [[Laging Tobias]] would be built: "*in verband met de rechten ons verleend bij de 18 artikelen door de hoofden van de verschillende staten bij hunne onderwerping aan ons gezag beredigd en bezworen*" (in connection with the rights granted to us by the 18 articles composed and sworn by the chiefs of the various states on their submission to our authority). Van Heutsz's prescription for the articles was, in the closing half of the tract, that they be retained as the colony's legal title to Atjehnese sovereignty — but that they be *modified* and *enforced* by the *zeer zware boeten* (very heavy fines) he proposed for non-compliance with "*de artikelen 2, 11, 13, 14 enz.*" (the articles 2, 11, 13, 14, etc.) of the sworn declaration.

## The articles as standard surrender declaration

The 18 artikelen were, in Van Heutsz's terminology, the *bezworen beëdigde 18 artikelen* — the sworn, sworn-on-oath eighteen articles — that each submitting chief *beredigd en bezworen* (composed and swore) at the moment of his submission to Dutch authority. Van Heutsz introduces them in the *Scherer* chapter of the tract as the legal substrate on which the *scheepvaartregeling* would rest:

> "Er moest daarom, zegt Scherer, een middel gevonden worden, dat de hoofden van de verschillende staatjes dwong tot trouwe nakoming van de bezworen beëdigde 18 artikelen en dit middel vond hij door het gebeurde te Gighen en te Samalanga".

The framing is critical: the 18 artikelen are not a single treaty but a *standard form* — a set of eighteen articles that each submitting statelet signed and swore, individually, at the moment of its submission. Van Heutsz's later reference to the articles is precise about this: "*de 18 artikelen door de hoofden van de verschillende staten bij hunne onderwerping aan ons gezag beredigd en bezworen*" — the eighteen articles *composed and sworn* by the chiefs of the various states *at their submission to our authority*. The articles were thus a colony-wide instrument of *onderwerping* — the legal formalisation of the act of submission — rather than a single diplomatic treaty.

## The Gighen–Samalanga crisis and the obligation to comply

The articles' practical significance is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, established by the *Gighen*–*Samalanga* crisis of [[1880]] — the murder of the Dutch emissaries [[Said Abdellah]] and [[Soetan Maharadja]] at [[Gighen]] and [[Samalanga]], and the chiefs' repeated evasion of the *bangoen* (blood-price) they owed under the articles. The crisis is the empirical exhibit on which [[Scherer]]'s [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] of [[1880]] was built:

> "door het gebeurde te Gighen en te Samalanga, waar onze zendelingen Said Abdellah en Soetan Maharadja vermoord waren, en waar men telkens uitvluchten zocht in het betalen van den opgelegden bangoen (bloedprijs). Hij verzocht en verkreeg toen van den Generaal van der Heijden vergunning om vóór die havens een kruisboot te leggen en den bangoen te innen door rechten te heffen op de in- en uit te voeren goederen".

The *bangoen* — the blood-price — was owed under the articles by the chiefs of Gighen and Samalanga for the murder of the Dutch emissaries; the chiefs' repeated evasions "*telkens uitvluchten zocht in het betalen van den opgelegden bangoen*" (repeatedly sought evasions in the paying of the imposed blood-price) revealed, in Scherer's reading, that the articles had no *middel* (instrument) — no instrument — of coercion. The *kruisboot-bangoen* (cruiser-blood-price) method — the laying of a *kruisboot* (a marine patrol vessel) before a harbour and the levying of duties on its trade to coerce a chief into paying the *bangoen* — was the empirical discovery that gave Scherer the *fingerwijzing* (pointer) on which the entire *scheepvaartregeling* rested. The articles, in this reading, were the *legal title*; the *scheepvaartregeling* was the *instrument of enforcement*.

## The hatsil and article 6

Among the obligations imposed on submitted chiefs by the 18 artikelen was the cession of the [[Hatsil|hatsil]] — the customary revenue share formerly paid to the sultan of Atjeh, and now claimed by the Dutch under the articles. Van Heutsz, in his [[1893]] prescription, names the *hatsil* as the first of the *vaste inkomsten* (fixed revenues) — fixed revenues — the colony was to draw from the *scheepvaartregeling*:

> "1.- de hatsil, die vroeger den sultan toekwam, kan desverkiezende nog wat verhoogd worden, door ons zelf te regelen".

Article 6 of the 18 artikelen is the specific article under which the chiefs' *invoerrechten* (import duties) — import duties — were to be assumed by the Dutch against a *billijke schadeloosstelling* (fair compensation) — a fair compensation — paid to the chiefs:

> "2. invoerrechten van heffing tegen eene billijke schadeloosstelling van de hoofden zal zijn overgenomen conform art. 6".

The *conform art. 6* (in accordance with art. 6) formula is Van Heutsz's specific invocation of the articles' content: the cession of the chiefs' *recht van in- en uitvoer* (the right of import and export) was the legal warrant for the colony's assumption of the *invoerrechten* under the *scheepvaartregeling*. The earlier practice — recorded by Van Heutsz in his survey of the [[1875]]–[[1880]] period — of chiefs "*zonder bezwaar additioneele artikelen geteekend hadden, waarbij voor ons bedongen was het aandeel in den hatsil*" — signing additional articles by which the Dutch share in the *hatsil* was reserved — established the precedent on which the [[1893]] prescription would build.

## Reproduction as Bijlage II

The 18 artikelen are reproduced, in the [[1893]] tract, as *Bijlage II* — the second appendix to the book. Van Heutsz's reference:

> "Die verklaring in 18 artikelen en acte van bevestiging, zooals ze tegenwoordig door de hoofden van de Onderhoorigheden geteekend worden, vindt men hierachter als Bijl. II; eenige wijziging daarin is noodig".

The reproduction is, however, accompanied by the qualification: "*eenige wijziging daarin is noodig*" — some modification in it is necessary. Van Heutsz's prescription for the articles is, in the closing half of the tract, that they be retained as the colony's *legal title* to Atjehnese sovereignty — but that they be modified, and enforced, by the *zeer zware boeten* he proposes for non-compliance with the articles' specific obligations. The *Bijlage II* reproduction is, in this sense, the document Van Heutsz preserves *in order to revise* — the standard form he would modify before re-imposing.

## Van Heutsz's proposal: heavy fines for articles 2, 11, 13, 14

Van Heutsz's prescription for the 18 artikelen is, in the closing half of the [[1893]] tract, the imposition of "*zeer zware boeten*" — very heavy fines — for non-compliance with the specific obligations of articles 2, 11, 13, and 14 of the sworn declaration. The prescription is precise:

> "Voorts moeten op het niet trouw nakomen van de verplichtingen opgelegd in de artikelen 2, 11, 13, 14 enz. van de geteekende verklaring zeer zware boeten gelegd worden, bij niet gewillige voldoening in te houden van de opbrengst der uitgevoerde producten. Deze boeten moeten niet een vooraf bepaald vast bedrag beloopen, maar geregeld worden naar omstandigheden".

The prescription has three elements. First, the *zeer zware boeten* — very heavy fines — to be levied on chiefs who fail to comply with the obligations of articles 2, 11, 13, 14 "*enz.*" (etc.) of the sworn declaration. Second, the *inhouding van de opbrengst der uitgevoerde producten* (withholding of the proceeds of the exported products) — as the *modus* (mode) of payment for chiefs who refuse to pay the fines voluntarily: the colony would withhold the proceeds of the chief's export trade, in the *scheepvaartregeling*'s Dutch-administered ports, until the fine was paid. Third, the *regeling naar omstandigheden* (regulation according to circumstances) — by which the fines were not to be a *vooraf bepaald vast bedrag* (predetermined fixed amount) but graduated according to the chief's degree of resistance and the duration of his non-compliance.

The *artikelen 2, 11, 13, 14* — the four specific articles Van Heutsz names as the obligations to be enforced by *zeer zware boeten* — are the articles whose non-compliance, in Van Heutsz's reading, was the structural cause of the *onderwerping*'s failure to take hold. The *scheepvaartregeling*'s *vaste inkomsten* (the *hatsil*, the *invoerrechten*, the *opiumpacht*) would, in this prescription, be the colony's *vaste* — fixed — revenues; the *bijzondere inkomsten* (the *boeten*) would be the colony's *instrument of coercion* — the means by which the colony would compel the chiefs to comply with the obligations they had themselves sworn.

## Significance

The 18 artikelen are, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the legal foundation of the colony's *onderwerping* — the sworn articles by which each submitting statelet recognised Dutch authority and bound itself to specified obligations — and the legal basis on which the *scheepvaartregeling* would be built. Their significance in the Aceh Wiki History record lies in four things. First, they were the *standardised surrender-declaration* of the Atjeh War — the *bezworen beëdigde* (sworn sworn-on-oath) form by which each submitting chief individually recognised Dutch *sovereiniteit* (sovereignty) and bound himself to specified obligations — rather than a single diplomatic treaty. Second, they were the legal substrate on which Scherer's *scheepvaartregeling* of [[1880]] was built: the Gighen–Samalanga crisis of [[1880]] had revealed that the articles had no *middel* of coercion, and the *kruisboot-bangoen* method was Scherer's empirical discovery of the instrument that would enforce them. Third, they were the legal warrant for the colony's *vaste inkomsten* — the *hatsil* (article 6) and the *invoerrechten* — that the *scheepvaartregeling* would levy; the *conform art. 6* formula was Van Heutsz's specific invocation of the articles' content as the legal basis for the colony's assumption of the *invoerrechten*. Fourth, they were the document Van Heutsz preserved *in order to revise* — the *Bijlage II* reproduction of the standard form was accompanied by the qualification "*eenige wijziging daarin is noodig*" and the prescription that "*zeer zware boeten*" be imposed for non-compliance with articles 2, 11, 13, and 14 — the four specific obligations whose non-compliance, in Van Heutsz's reading, was the structural cause of the *onderwerping*'s failure to take hold.

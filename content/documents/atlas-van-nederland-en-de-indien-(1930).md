***Atlas van Nederland en de Indiën voor de Lagere School*** ("Atlas of the Netherlands and the Indies for the Primary School") was a Dutch schoolroom geographical atlas of the Netherlands and the [[Netherlands East Indies|Netherlands East Indies]], written by **G. Prop** and published at [[Zutphen|Zutphen]] by **W. J. Thieme & Cie** in [[1930]] — the twelfth edition of a work that had established itself, by the early twentieth century, as a standard primary-school geography of the kingdom and its colonies. The atlas is the principal documentary source for the Dutch primary-school geographical image of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the early twentieth century, and therefore for the schoolroom framing of the Atjehnese territory in the colonial imagination.

## Publication

The atlas was published at [[Zutphen|Zutphen]] by **W. J. Thieme & Cie** in MCMXXX — [[1930]] — as the twelfth edition (*Twaalfde druk*) of the work. The author, G. Prop, was a writer of schoolroom geographies whose atlas had established itself by the early twentieth century as a standard primary-school reference work. The twelfth edition of [[1930]] is the edition consulted in the present encyclopedia.

## Scope and structure

The atlas covers the geography of the Netherlands (Part I) and of the [[Netherlands East Indies|Netherlands East Indies]] (Part II — "*Oost-Indië*"), with each chapter accompanied by sketch-maps, place-name exercises, and review questions for the primary-school pupil. The chapter on the Indies — chapter XIV, "*Oost-Indië*" — covers the principal islands (Sumatra, Java, Borneo, Celebes, the Moluccas, New Guinea, the Lesser Sunda Islands) in the order of their colonial importance, with the sub-section on [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] containing the atlas's account of [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

## The Atjeh account

The atlas's account of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] is recorded in the chapter on [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] (chapter XIV, section C), in a single compressed entry that fixes the territory in the primary-school geographical imagination:

> *"In Atjeh voerden de Nederlanders sedert 1873 oorlog: nu is er vrede en komt weer welvaart. Atjeh levert peper. ... De hoofdstad van Atjeh heet Kota-Radja; voor de kust de belangrijke haven Sabang."*

The atlas's three clauses — the reference to the [[Aceh War|war]] "*sedert 1873*" ("since 1873"), the assertion that "*nu is er vrede en komt weer welvaart*" ("now there is peace and prosperity returns"), and the identification of [[Koeta Radja|Kota-Radja]] as the chief town and [[Sabang|Sabang]] as the important haven — together constitute the primary-school image of Atjeh in [[1930]]: a territory recently pacified, now returning to prosperity, producing pepper, with its capital at Kota-Radja and its harbour at Sabang.

The atlas also identifies the [[Boekit Barisan|Boekit Barisan]] mountain range as extending "*van Atjeh tot aan Straat Soenda*" ("from Atjeh to the Strait of Sunda"), and notes that the [[Toba|Toba]] lake region is inhabited by the [[Bataklanden|Bataks]]. The Dutch colonial subjugation of the [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]] under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — recorded in the [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook — is the implicit political-geographical background of the atlas's account.

## Significance

The *Atlas van Nederland en de Indiën* is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history as the principal documentary source for the Dutch primary-school geographical image of Atjeh in the early twentieth century. The atlas's compressed entry — fixing the territory as recently pacified, productive of pepper, with its capital at [[Koeta Radja|Kota-Radja]] and its haven at [[Sabang|Sabang]] — is the document of the schoolroom framing of the Atjehnese territory in the colonial imagination, and of the way the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] was reduced, for the primary-school pupil, to a single clause: "*In Atjeh voerden de Nederlanders sedert 1873 oorlog: nu is er vrede en komt weer welvaart.*" The atlas is thus the primary-school counterpart of the [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook's account of the war, and the principal source of the Dutch primary-school geographical memory of Atjeh in the early twentieth century.

**De waarheid over onze vestiging in Atjeh** was [[Jan van Swieten]]’s [[1879]] polemical reconstruction of the [[Second Dutch Expedition to Aceh]] and its political aftermath. Written against [[G. F. W. Borel]]’s *Onze vestiging in Atjeh*, it defended the author’s limited-war and indirect-rule programme while preserving a detailed contemporary account of the campaign.

## Purpose and argument

[[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] argued that colonial expansion ought not be treated as an automatic good. He presented intervention as legitimate only when necessary for security and welfare, rejected destruction of civilian villages and fields as a punishment for resistance, and maintained that the [[Aceh War]] should have sought a political settlement rather than direct administration.

## Contents and evidence

The book follows the blockade, transport and cholera crisis, landing, fighting at [[Lemboe]], seizure of the [[Baiturrahman Grand Mosque|Missigit]], capture of the [[Kraton of Aceh]], negotiations with local leaders, and the withdrawal of the principal force. Its appendices print correspondence with rulers of [[Aceh Dependencies]], proclamations, and related papers. It remains a partisan source: assertions concerning [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] and later policy are the author’s arguments, not neutral findings.

## Publication

The [[1879]] volume appeared at [[Zalt-Bommel]] with a map of [[Aceh]] and a plan of the [[Kraton of Aceh]].

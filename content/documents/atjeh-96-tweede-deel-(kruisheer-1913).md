**ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel** (*2c Deel*) — the second volume of [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s military-historical monograph on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — was published at [[Weltevreden]] by N.V. Boekhandel Visser & Co in [[1913]] under the full title *ATJEH [[1896]] ATJCH [[1896]]–[[1900]] Met 3 kaarten en 6 portretten*, and is held today by the Instituut Kern, Leiden. The volume is the principal Dutch-language source for the second semester of [[1896]] (29 June – 31 December [[1896]]), covering the period from the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] of [[29 June]] [[1896]] through the arrival of [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet]] as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] on [[8 November]] [[1896]] and the cholera-stricken final month of December [[1896]]. It comprises a main text organised in two *Hoofdstukken* (Chapters III and IV), a *Besluit* (Conclusion), and twenty-two *Bijlagen* (Annexes) preserving the principal official documents of the period — above all the *Instructie* (Instruction) left by [[J. A. Vetter|Luitenant-Generaal Vetter]] as Regeeringscommissaris at his departure on [[28 June]] [[1896]], and the *Notulen der conferentie* between [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General Van der Wijck]] and [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor J. J. K. de Moulin]] of [[18 June]] [[1896]].

## Background and Scope

The first volume of *ATJEH '96* — covering the first semester of [[1896]] from the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] on [[29 March]] [[1896]] through the [[Departure of Vetter (28 June 1896)|departure of Vetter]] on [[28 June]] [[1896]] — had established the framework for Kruisheer's operational history. The second volume carries the narrative forward from the night of [[28 June|28]]–[[29 June]] [[1896]], when the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] was launched, to the close of the year. The choice of scope is again deliberate: the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) of [[Koeta Radja]] — the cordon of *bentengs* around the Dutch-held area — had been re-established at the close of the first semester, but [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] remained at large, the Acehnese resistance retained its capacity for harassment, and the Dutch colonial command faced the problem of translating the conventional military victories of April–June [[1896]] into a durable *pacificatie* (pacification).

The volume is therefore the chronicle of an intermediate period — between the crisis of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|Umar defection]] and the appointment of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] in [[1898]] which opened the decisive *korte oorlog* phase. It is dominated by the figure of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]], who served as *wd. Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* from [[11 July]] [[1896]] until [[17 November]] [[1896]], and by the abortive tenure of [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor De Moulin]] — appointed to the governorship on [[5 July]] [[1896]] and killed by a sunstroke on [[7 July]] [[1896]] during his very first *excursie* (reconnaissance (excursie)) to the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims (Kroeng-Raba)]].

## Content

The volume is organised in three principal parts. The **front matter** (pp. 1–13) comprises the title page, the *Inhoud* (Table of Contents) on pp. 7–11 listing the two *Hoofdstukken*, the *Besluit*, and the twenty-two *Bijlagen*, and a list of the three maps and six portraits — including the frontispiece *Frontaanzicht van Ana' Galoeng* opposite p. 2, a portrait of [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor J. J. K. de Moulin]] opposite p. 15, of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] opposite p. 83, of [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Kolonel Van Vliet]] opposite p. 145, of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Luitenant-Kolonel Van Heutsz]] opposite p. 276, and a sketch of the *Gevechtsterrein bij Ana' Galoeng* opposite p. 104.

The **main text** (pp. 19–160) is organised chronologically in two *Hoofdstukken*. *Hoofdstuk III* — pp. 1–144 — covers the period from the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] of [[29 June]] [[1896]] through the [[Arrival of Van Vliet (8 November 1896)|arrival of Kolonel Van Vliet]] on [[8 November]] [[1896]]. It opens with the night attack of [[29 June]] [[1896]] in which three Dutch colonnes under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]] and [[C. J. Laceulle|Laceulle]] stormed the *benteng* (fort) of [[Ana' Galong]] — the post which the Acehnese *considered as the heart of Great Aceh* — with the *maréchaussée* (military police (maréchaussée)) and the snelladers (rapid-fire compagnie) of the [[3e Bataljon|3e Bataljon]] using only the *klewang* (cutlass); it records the death of [[H. Th. de Moulin|Kapitein H. Th. de Moulin]]'s colonne at the hands of the maréchaussée Wongsodikromo, Todikromo, and their comrades, and the *tuchtiging* (punitive burning) of the kampongs of [[Sibreh]] and [[Rahat]] of the [[III Moekims Lamkrak|III Moekims Lamkrak]]. It then narrates the abortive tenure of [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor De Moulin]] — appointed to the governorship on [[5 July]] [[1896]] and struck down by sunstroke at [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[7 July]] [[1896]] during an *excursie* (reconnaissance (excursie)) to the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims (Kroeng-Raba)]] — and the restoration of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] as *wd. Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* on [[11 July]] [[1896]]. The remainder of *Hoofdstuk III* covers the prolonged series of reconnaissances, *tuchtigingen* and occupations by which Stemfoort sought to extend Dutch control into the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]], the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], the [[V Moekims Montassik|V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]], culminating in the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]], the introduction of the [[Pasenstelsel (September–October 1896)|pasenstelsel]] (pass system) in mid-September, the [[Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri (28 October 1896)|temporary occupation of Indrapoeri]] on [[28 October]] [[1896]], the [[Capture of Kota Ba Teuē (28 October 1896)|capture of the stelling Kota Ba Teuē]] on the same day, the [[Trip to Ladoeng and Kroeng Raja (30–31 October 1896)|trip to Ladoeng and Kroeng Raja]] of [[30 October|30]]–[[31 October]] [[1896]], the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]] of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]], and the [[Bridge over the Kroeng Pengapit (8 November 1896)|bridge over the Kroeng Pengapit]] of [[8 November]] [[1896]].

*Hoofdstuk IV* — pp. 145–160 — covers the period from the [[Arrival of Van Vliet (8 November 1896)|arrival of Kolonel Van Vliet]] on [[8 November]] [[1896]] through the close of the year. It narrates the [[Handover of Command from Stemfoort to Van Vliet (17 November 1896)|handover of command]] of [[17 November]] [[1896]], at which Stemfoort returned to [[Batavia|Java]] and Van Vliet formally assumed the governorship; the [[Reconnaissance in Lepong (12 November 1896)|reconnaissance in Lepong]] of [[12 November]] [[1896]]; the [[Trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah (29 November 1896)|trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah]] near [[Bat Dilip]] of [[29 November]] [[1896]] to recover the artillery lost on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]]; and the paralysis of military action in December [[1896]] by the *heerschende cholera* (prevailing cholera) which prevented the *colonne tegen Oemar* (column against Umar) from taking the field.

The **Besluit* (Decree (Besluit))* (Conclusion) on p. 155 closes the volume with Kruisheer's retrospective assessment of the second semester of [[1896]] — a period in which, in his view, *men in [[1896]] den rechten weg tot pacificatie insloeg* (the right path to pacification was entered in [[1896]]), even as Umar remained at large and the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) remained the limit of effective Dutch control.

The **Bijlagen** (Annexes) on pp. 162–328 comprise twenty-two official documents. The most important are: *Bijlage I* — the [[Instruction of the Regeeringscommissaris (28 June 1896)|Instruction left by Vetter as Regeeringscommissaris at his departure on 28 June 1896]] (pp. 162–165); *Bijlage II* — the [[Notulen der conferentie van 18 Juni 1896|minutes of the conference of 18 June 1896 between Van der Wijck and De Moulin]] on the policy to be followed in Atjeh (pp. 166–167); *Bijlage III* — the [[Instruction for Civiel en Militair Gouverneur Van Vliet|Instruction for Kolonel Van Vliet as Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] (pp. 168–172); *Bijlagen IV–V* — *Extract-besluiten* regulating the *schaftordes* (food tariffs) for [[1893]] and [[1895]] (pp. 173–178); *Bijlage VI* — the [[Commandemensorders of 4 and 5 April 1896|Commandemensorders of 4 and 5 April 1896, nos. 37 and 39]] (p. 179); *Bijlagen VII–VIII* — *Extract-besluiten* of [[30 April]] [[1896]] and [[22 Augustus]] [[1896]] on the *toelagen en den bijslag* (allowances and supplements) (pp. 180–181); *Bijlagen IX–XII* — *Schaftordes* (food tariffs) for Europeans, Inlanders, and Officers, and a comparative overview of the *voedingswijze* (diet) on war footing in [[1896]] (pp. 183–195); *Bijlage XIII* — a statement of officer incomes in [[1896]] (pp. 199–203); *Bijlage XIV* — a *Nota betreffende de verpleging van troepen en dwangarbeiders bij den tocht naar Lepong* (note on the care of troops and forced labourers on the Lepong expedition) (p. 203); *Bijlage XV* — the [[Schaftorde of 21 December 1896|Schaftorde of 21 December 1896]] (p. 208); *Bijlage XVI* — the *Verlieslijst Atjeh-[[1896]]* (loss list for Atjeh [[1896]]) (pp. 212–218); *Bijlagen XVII–XIX* — *Dagorders* (daily orders) granting military decorations for Atjeh '96 (pp. 222–230); and *Bijlage XXII* — the [[Overzicht van onze vroegere betrekkingen met Atjeh|Overzicht van onze vroegere betrekkingen met Atjeh en van den huidigen toestand in dat gewest]] (Survey of our former relations with Atjeh and of the current state in that territory) (pp. 234–304), with twenty-six subsections covering the history, the Atjehsche volkskarakter, the size and boundaries, the population, the coasts, mining, roads, the tramway, trade, art and industry, agriculture, cattle-breeding, education, the chiefs, the administration of justice, taxation, the landscape treasuries, the administrative division, the armed police, the army, and various graphical representations of trade and data concerning the years [[1893]]–[[1912]].

## Significance

The significance of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is that of the principal Dutch-language source for the second semester of [[1896]] — the period in which the immediate crisis of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|Umar defection]] was succeeded by the slower, more methodical work of *pacificatie* (pacification) under Stemfoort and (briefly) Van Vliet. The volume's preservation of the *Bijlagen* — particularly the *Instructie* (Instruction) of Vetter and the *Notulen der conferentie* of [[18 June]] [[1896]] — gives it a documentary authority which the narrative alone could not possess: the reader can follow the policy guidance transmitted from [[Buitenzorg|Buitenzorg]] to Koeta-Radja in real time, and observe the constraints within which Stemfoort operated. The volume's *Besluit* — with its claim that *men in [[1896]] den rechten weg tot pacificatie insloeg* — is itself a historical position, and the subsequent course of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — which continued until the [[Surrender of the Atjeh Pretender Sultan (1903)|surrender of the Pretender Sultan in 1903]] and beyond — provides the perspective from which that claim may be assessed. The closing *Bijlage XXII* — the survey of the *huidigen toestand* in [[1913]] — extends the volume's temporal horizon beyond the events of [[1896]] and embeds them in the longer trajectory of Dutch colonial administration in Atjeh down to the eve of the First World War.

**Atjeh en de Atjehers** — *Atjeh and the Atjehers* — is an eye-witness account of the [[Netherlands|Netherlands-Indies]] blockade of the north-east coast of Sumatra during the early years of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], by [[J. A. Kruyt|J. A. Kruyt]], published at Leiden in [[1877]] under the full subtitle *'twee jaren blokkade op Sumatra's Noordoost-kust'* — *two years of blockade on Sumatra's north-east coast*. Kruyt served aboard the man-of-war *Timor* from [[1873]] to [[1875]] and was present at the Netherlands government's first contacts with the princes of the eastern coast.

## Contents
Kruyt's book records his observations of the pepper trade, the negotiations with the *oeléebalangs* of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Perlak]], [[Idi]], [[Peudawa Rajeu|Peudawa]] and [[Lho Seumawe]], the discovery of the [[Petroleum-bronnen van Perlak|petroleum spring at Rantau Pandjang]] in May [[1875]], and the prices of pepper at Penang in August [[1873]] (200–250 pillar-dollars the *kojang*, against 90 three years earlier). He gives the first European description of the *seuneubò*, the collection of pepper-gardens under a head gardener, and reports that the east coast annually exported 5–6 thousand *kojang* of pepper — a figure which, set against the [[1913]] export of *'ruim 3 millioen kilogram'*, is the basis of Broersma's judgement that Atjeh's pepper-trade had by [[1913]] fallen to less than a tenth of its [[1873]] volume. Kruyt also records the Dutch gifts to friendly chiefs — a silver coffee-service to the *vorst van Edi*, a golden watch to another chief, a silver rice-dish to a third — a practice which Broersma, fifty years later, still finds illustrated by the heirlooms of [[Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih|Teungkoe habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeih]] at Peudawa.

## Use by Broersma
Broersma cites Kruyt repeatedly in the early chapters of *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* — for the [[1874]] encounter with the Gajos at [[Peureula (Perlak)|Perlak]], where the Gajos were said to be man-eaters and their women *'eerst schoon'*; for the spelling *Sekoetji* of [[Blang Sekotji|Blang Sekotji]]; and for the activities of [[Teukoe Tjhi Perlak|Teungkoe Tjhi Perlak]], the old *oeléebalang* of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Perlak]], whose son *'het jong van Peusangan'* had returned from his wanderings in Malakka to stand beside his father as a military commander in [[1873]]–[[1874]].

## Significance
Kruyt's book is the principal contemporary narrative of the blockade period of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], and the only detailed European source for the east-coast pepper economy immediately before its late-nineteenth-century collapse. Its mixture of naval memoir, commercial report and ethnographic note became a model for later colonial reconnaissance literature.

**The Government Telegram of 26 April 1896, No. 409** (*Gouvernementstelegram van 26 April 1896, No. 409*) — preserved among the official documents gathered in the Annexes of Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the telegram sent by the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Government of Nederlandsch-Indië]] at [[Batavia]] to [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris Vetter]] at [[Koeta Radja]] on [[26 April]] [[1896]] ordering the dismissal of [[Teuku Umar]] from both of his government offices — [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government (conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893]]) and *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] (conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Resolution of 19 January 1896]]) — before the commencement of military action against him. The telegram was the immediate authorisation for the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] that formally executed the dismissal, and is the legal hinge between the diplomatic and military phases of the Umar crisis.

## Background

By [[26 April]] [[1896]] the Dutch position in [[Groot-Atjeh]] had reached a breaking point. The [[buitenlinie]] (outer line) had been progressively abandoned — [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[12 April]] [[1896]], [[Ana' Galong|Anak-Galoeng]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam-Soet]] and [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]], and the final posts of [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]]. [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] had arrived as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] on [[7 April]] [[1896]] and assumed both civil and military command. The exchange of letters with Umar had run its course: Umar's [[Third Letter of Teuku Umar (23 April 1896)|Third Letter of 23 April 1896]] had demanded the Royal decoration promised by [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Deijkerhoff]], a signed declaration by the Queen of the Netherlands and the European princes, and ƒ150,000 per month. The [[Regeeringscommissaris]] had replied on [[25 April]] [[1896]] demanding that Umar hand in the *geleende oorlogsmaterieel*, demolish the versterkingen in the [[IV en VI Moekims|VI Moekims]], and submit anew by [[26 April]].

On the morning of [[26 April]] [[1896]] Umar's reply was received, asking for a three-day extension without any definite promise. The [[Regeeringscommissaris]] denied the request the same day. With diplomatic channels exhausted, the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] required formal authorisation from [[Batavia]] to dismiss Umar from his offices before commencing military operations. The Government at [[Batavia]] responded by telegram the same day.

## Content

The telegram (numbered 409 in the colonial government's telegram register for [[1896]]) was sent by the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Government of Nederlandsch-Indië]] to [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris Vetter]] at [[Koeta Radja]] on [[26 April]] [[1896]]. Its substantive content was the order that [[Teuku Umar]] — designated in the telegram by his old name *Toekoe Oemar*, not by his commissioned name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* — be dismissed from both of his government offices: the office of [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government, conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Resolution of 5 September 1893]], and the office of *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]], conferred by the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Resolution of 19 January 1896]].

The order to dismiss Umar from both offices *before* commencing military action against him was a deliberate legal formality: the Dutch understood that military operations against a serving government officer would have created an intolerable ambiguity in the colonial register. The dismissal had to be formally executed first, by *Gouvernementsbesluit* of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|waarnemend Gouverneur van Atjeh]], before the [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|Proclamation of 27 April 1896]] could announce the commencement of hostilities. The telegram is the authorising instrument; the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] is the executing instrument.

## Reception and Aftermath

The telegram was received and acted upon the same day. The [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|waarnemend Gouverneur]] at [[Koeta Radja]] issued the [[Gouvernementsbesluit of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI)|Government Resolution No. 159/11 of 26 April 1896]] formally dismissing Umar from both offices and stipulating that he would henceforth be officially designated by the name *Toekoe Oemar*. The *besluit* was signed by *De Secretaris* [[Udo de Haes]]. The following day, [[27 April]] [[1896]], [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] issued his [[Proclamation of Lt. Gen. Vetter to the heads and people of the 3 sagi of Groot-Atjeh (27 April 1896)|Proclamation to the heads and people of the three *sagi* of Groot-Atjeh]] announcing military operations against *T. Oemar Melaboch* and his followers. The same day the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|Bombardment of Lampisang]] began at 5:45 a.m.

## Significance

The Government Telegram of [[26 April]] [[1896]] No. 409 is the formal authorising instrument for Umar's dismissal and, by extension, for the commencement of conventional military operations against him. Its requirement that Umar be dismissed from both offices before hostilities began is a revealing instance of Dutch colonial legalism: even in the moment of crisis, the formal revocation of the legal relationship had to precede the military rupture. The telegram's use of the name rather than *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* signals the symbolic erasure of the [[1893]] renaming that had constituted Umar as a Dutch officer. As a historical document, the telegram marks the precise moment at which the Dutch government in [[Batavia]] authorised the war against its former ally.

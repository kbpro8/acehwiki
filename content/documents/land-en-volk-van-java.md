**Land en Volk van Java** was the brochure by [[H. van Kol]], published shortly before [[1897]], on the condition of [[Java]] and its people, which was cited repeatedly in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] both by its author and against him by the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|J. T. Cremer]].

## The estimate of the cost of the war

Van Kol told the chamber on [[16 November]] [[1897]] that in that brochure, "een paar jaar geleden," he had given the sum of the extra expenditure caused by the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]: from [[1873]] to the present some eleven hundred million had been spent on army and fleet, and since expenditure before the war had been at most thirty million a year, the extra outlay over twenty-five years amounted to three hundred and fifty million guilders, of which only a small part had been spent on [[Lombok]] and elsewhere.

He added that in a review in *[[Insulinde]]* [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], then an assistant-resident, had objected that to this must be added all the extra expenditure of the civil administration, leave allowances, [[Recherchevaartuigen|search vessels]], travel and subsistence costs and part of the pensions.

## Cremer's use of the book against its author

[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] quoted the brochure twice against the socialist conclusion. He noted that on p. 32 Van Kol had assigned to capital an important share in the task of bringing the Indies to development: "Geld en kapitaal is er noodig. Geld om spoorwegen en irrigatiewerken aan te leggen, geld om fabrieken te bouwen, industriën te scheppen, het grootbedrijf in den landbouw in te voeren, die nog in alle opzichten zoo ontzaggelijk veel moet verbeterd worden en nog in zijne kindsheid verkeert. De natuurlijke hulpbronnen voor volkswelvaart moeten tot ontwikkeling komen, het thans overbevolkte Java moet millioenen nieuwe bewoners kunnen voeden." He used the passage to show a divergence between Van Kol and [[Pieter Jelles Troelstra|Troelstra]] on the employment of Dutch capital in the Indies.

He quoted p. 63 at greater length for Van Kol's rejection of [[Multatuli]]'s demand "De Hollanders er uit!", which Van Kol had said the socialists would deeply regret, since the worst Dutch administration was better than the best native administration known, and since the departure of the Dutch would mean enthroning tyranny, renewing extortion, bringing back barbarism and delivering the population to robbery and plunder. Cremer observed that those words applied to [[Java]] but that the ending seemed to him "als te zijn geknipt voor Atjeh." — as though cut out for Atjeh.

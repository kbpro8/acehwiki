**Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876** — full title: *Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876. De offensieve handelingen der guerilla. Uit officiëele bescheiden samengesteld en met eene kaart, schetsen en plans toegelicht* (Aceh from 26 December 1875 to 4 September 1876: The offensive operations of the guerilla, compiled from official documents and explained with a map, sketches and plans) — was an [[1883]] Dutch military history of the [[Aceh War]] written by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]], Kolonel (Colonel) of the General Staff of the Netherlands Indies Army (*Indische leger*). It was printed by P. B. Nieuwenhuijs of Breda for the Royal Institute for the Tropics (*Koninklijk Instituut voor de Tropen*) and is one of the most detailed primary accounts ever published of the [[Guerilla warfare in Groot-Atjeh|guerilla phase]] of the war in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Publication and purpose

Meijer wrote the book with the permission of the Commander of the Army and chief of the Department of War (*Commandant van het Leger en Chef van het departement van Oorlog*) in the Netherlands Indies. He used the official reports that the military commander and the various column, corps, company, detachment and patrol commanders in [[Atjeh]] had sent to their superiors, together with the [[Koloniaal Verslag|Colonial Reports]] of [[1875]], [[1876]] and [[1877]]. In the foreword (Voorbericht) he stated that he had recorded only facts as the commanders themselves had reported them, expressly avoiding the polemics that had followed [[Jan van Swieten]]'s campaign in the *pamphlet war* of [[1874]]–[[1876]].

Meijer had himself served in Atjeh as *chef van den staf* (chief of the staff) of the troops in Atjeh and had personally commanded the column that stormed the enemy fortification at [[Lambaroe]] on [[7 March]] [[1876]]. The book is therefore partly an eyewitness work.

## How Meijer defined his subject

Meijer deliberately limited himself to the *offensieve handelingen des vijands* (the offensive operations of the enemy) — that is, to the attacks, ambushes, overvallingen (surprises), night assaults and raids which the [[Guerilla warfare in Groot-Atjeh|Acehnese guerilla]] carried out against the Dutch posts, patrols and convoys. The Dutch offensive operations of the same period, he announced, deserved a separate work, so that both the tactics of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] and the character of the enemy could be judged fairly.

The period the book covers begins on [[26 December]] [[1875]], when Pel launched his great offensive (the seventy-day campaign, *zeventig-daagsche veldtocht*), and ends on [[4 September]] [[1876]], when part of the troops in Atjeh under [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|General Wiggers van Kerchem]] began their march to the [[XXVI Moekims]] to complete Pel's plan of closing the XXII and XXVI Moekims off from the sea.

## Contents of the book

Apart from the foreword and an introduction on the political situation and the organisation of the armed forces in Atjeh and dependencies, the book contains eight chapters:

- **Chapter I** — a short description of the terrain held by the Dutch posts in Atjeh: the [[Zuidoosterlinie|south-east line]] (Biloel, Atoë, Atoë Noord, Kajoe-Lē, [[Lambaroe]], Mesigit Pagar-Ajer, Pagar-Ajer), the second line (Djempit, Ketapan Doewa, Mibouw Zuid, Mibouw Oost, Lo-ong, Longbatah-Mesigit), the western posts ([[Boekit Daroe]], [[Boekit Terin]], Pakan Badak, Boekit Seboen, Lampagar, Sinangri, Lampasei, Blang-Oë) and the northern posts ([[Koewala Gigiëng]], Kota-Pohama, Moesapi, Kota-Radja Bedil, Tibang, Lamara Northeast and Southeast, Langkroek, Lamprit, Kota Alam, Oleh Karang, [[Pango]]).
- **Chapter II** — the general form and arrangement of the Dutch fortifications (*bentings*).
- **Chapter III** — the armament and strength of the post garrisons as at the end of March [[1876]].
- **Chapter IV** — the purpose of the various posts, the territory entrusted to each post commander, and the general instructions.
- **Chapter V** — the means of communication between the posts and with the headquarters at [[Kota Radja]].
- **Chapter VI** — the offensive operations of the enemy between 26 December 1875 and 9 March 1876: the [[Attack on Lemboeq N.O. (23-24 January 1876)|night attack on Lemboeq N.O.]], the [[Grass-fire attack on Lamprit (8 February 1876)|grass-fire attack on Lamprit]], the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|destruction of Captain Van Swieten's detachment]], the [[Lamkoenjit ambush (17 February 1876)|ambush near Lamkoenjit]], the [[Ambush of the Buijs detachment (4 March 1876)|ambush of Buijs' detachment]], the [[Bugis couriers from Kajoe-Lē (6 March 1876)|journey of the two Bugis couriers]] and the [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|storming of Lambaroe]].
- **Chapter VII** — the offensive operations of the enemy between 9 March and 4 September 1876: the [[Convoy ambush near Atoë (21 March 1876)|ambush near Atoë]], the [[Night attack on Lampagar (2-3 May 1876)|night attack on Lampagar]], the founding of [[Biloel Zuid]], the [[Surprise of the west hill-post at Biloel Zuid (2-3 June 1876)|loss and recapture of the west hill-post]], the ambushes of [[Ambush between Pango and Kota Alam (14 July 1876)|14 July]], [[Ambush between Boekit Daroe and Ketapan Doewa (17 July 1876)|17 July]], [[Ambush between Kota Alam and Oleh Karang (28 July 1876)|28 July]] and [[Lo-oe ambush (28 August 1876)|28 August]] [[1876]], and the [[Gli Taroem night ambush (8-9 August 1876)|night ambush in the Gli Taroem pass]].
- **Chapter VIII** — the events in [[Pedir|Pidir]] and [[Gigiëng]] between 26 December 1875 and September 1876: the [[Occupation of Pidir (31 May - 4 June 1876)|occupation of Pidir]] and the [[Cancellation of friendship with Gigiëng (19 August 1876)|breach with Gigiëng]].

## Maps and sketches

The book was illustrated with ten maps, sketches and plans, including a map of the theatre of war in Groot-Atjeh in [[1876]] (scale 1:40,000) with the march routes of the troops, plans of the battlefield of [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|Lambaroe]] and of the enemy fortification there, a sketch of the camp at Lampagar in the night of [[2 May|2]]–[[3 May]] [[1876]], sketches of [[Biloel Zuid]], of the terrain between [[Kroeëng Raba]] and [[Lepong]], of the battlefields of [[Ambush between Boekit Daroe and Ketapan Doewa (17 July 1876)|17 July]] and [[Ambush between Kota Alam and Oleh Karang (28 July 1876)|28 July]] [[1876]], of the pass of [[Gli Taroem night ambush (8-9 August 1876)|Gli Taroem in the night of 8-9 August 1876]], and of the Dutch establishment at [[Occupation of Pidir (31 May - 4 June 1876)|Pidir]].

## Meijer's conclusions

Meijer held that the enemy's successes — the surprise attacks, ambushes and assaults — were mostly caused by gross carelessness, lack of watchfulness and neglect of the first principles of field security on the Dutch side, though he added that in [[1876]] a large part of the Dutch troops consisted of young soldiers whose training left much to be desired. The enemy, he wrote, showed with what skill and energy a guerilla war could be sustained against a properly organised European army, and the book was intended as a warning and an instruction for the Indies army.

## Significance

The book is one of the fundamental printed sources for the history of the [[Aceh War]] between the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1874)|Second Aceh Expedition]] and the later concentration system. Because Meijer reproduced the official field reports almost unchanged, the work preserves the names of hundreds of officers, non-commissioned officers and soldiers — Dutch, Ambonese, Madurese, Javanese, African and Bugis — as well as the names of Acehnese chiefs, kampongs and strongholds, at a level of detail unmatched by any later account.

**De spoorwegverbinding tusschen Deli, Atjeh en de Aroebaai** (The railway connection between Deli, Atjeh and the Aru Bay) was a Dutch colonial journal article by [[J. Roest]], published in the *Tijdschrift voor het Binnenlandsch Bestuur* (Journal for Home Administration), volume XLIX, part 6, at Batavia in December [[1916]]. Dated at Weltevreden on [[4 December]] [[1915]], the article reviewed the long controversy over how the [[Atjeh-tram]] (the government steam tramway of [[Atjeh]]) should be joined to the network of the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]] (Deli Railway Company) and where the seaport of the connected system should be built on the [[Aroebai|Aru Bay]].

## Background of the article

Roest wrote the article as a sequel to his own earlier piece, *Belawanhaven en Atjeh-tram* (Belawan harbour and the Atjeh tram), which had appeared in the December [[1912]] issue of the same journal. In between, the question of the Atjeh–Deli railway connection had come much nearer to a solution, and Roest summarised and judged the events that had led to the state of affairs at the end of [[1915]].

The central dispute was the choice of the harbour. The Government had chosen [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboehan]] on the Aru Bay as the end point of the Atjeh tram and the future coastal harbour for [[Tamiang]], motivating the choice by the existing traffic in the Aru Bay. The former member of the Local Council of the plantation region, [[Schadee]] — a former administrator of the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij — argued in an open letter to [[Beyerman]] that the Minister had been wrongly informed: the existing import and export trade of Tamiang was not seated in the Aru Bay but at [[Pangkalan Brandan]], so that a new harbour at Telok Taboehan had no justification in present traffic and little certainty for the future.

## What the article records

Roest's article preserves, in particular:

- the argument of Schadee that the depth of the [[Sembilangeul]] (the southern entrance of the Aru Bay) had never drawn shipping away from the existing harbour places, and that the expected shift of trade from Pangkalan Brandan to Telok Taboehan was unlikely;
- the objection that the [[Langsa]] harbour, already connected by tram with [[Koeala Simpang|Kwala Simpang]], rather than Telok Taboehan, would profit from any growth of the Atjeh plantation enterprises in the north;
- the cost comparison between the rival connection lines: the line from the junction station Pematang Pandjang to Pangkalan Brandan (8 kilometres, estimated at f 300,000) against the line [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjong Slamat]] (30 kilometres, f 1,450,000), and the proposal, traced by the army engineers (*Genie*), from Besitang to Telok Taboehan (f 510,000);
- the proceedings of the [[Cultuurraad]] (Cultivation Council), notably the motion of Schadee of [[26 September]] [[1912]], the amendment of Beyerman, and the debate of [[20 February]] [[1913]], at which the connection with Pangkalan Brandan was preferred to that with Tandjong Slamat by 28 votes to 1;
- the Government decisions of [[17 June]] [[1913]] No. 59 and [[16 October]] [[1913]] No. 25, granting funds for the continuation of the Atjeh steam tram from Kwala Simpang to the boundary between Tamiang and [[Langkat]], and from that boundary to Besitang, while the Aru Bay plan was maintained;
- the judgement of Governor [[Van der Plas]] of the East Coast of Sumatra, that a parallel line through new country could never harm the plantation region and that a good harbour such as Telok Taboehan mattered more to the hinterland than a bad one such as Pangkalan Brandan;
- the eventual change of plans in [[1915]]: after the hopeful results of the suction dredger *Java* in the [[Belawan]] channel, the grand scheme for a first-class harbour at Telok Taboehan was set aside; the harbour works on the Aru Bay were moved from Telok Taboehan to [[Pangkalan Soesoe]], where the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]] had a large establishment with a jetty, and f 150,000 was placed on the draft budget for [[1916]] to begin the work.

## Outcome

By the end of [[1915]] the Government had, for the time being, given up a state-built connection between the Atjeh tram and the Deli railway; the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij would build its long-desired line Besitang–Pangkalan Brandan itself, and Roest doubted whether the short through connection Besitang–Tandjong Slamat would be realised within the foreseeable future, or perhaps ever. Roest defended the Government's course: through traffic was preferable to transhipment, and the interests of the Deli Company had to give way to the general interest if a state connection were ever required.

## Significance

The article is a primary source for the history of the railways of northern [[Sumatra]], for the disputes between the [[Cultuurraad]] and the Government over colonial transport policy, and for the early history of the ports of the [[Aroebai|Aru Bay]], [[Pangkalan Brandan]], [[Belawan]] and [[Langsa]] — the ports through which the plantation economy of the East Coast of Sumatra and of [[Atjeh]] was connected to the world market. To the history of Atjeh it is important as the background to the northern extension of the [[Atjeh-tram]] towards Tamiang and the later Atjeh Railways (*Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen*).

**Van der Heijden's day-order of [[5 September]] [[1879]]** — the *Dagorder* (day-order) issued by [[General van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] at [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]] on [[5 September]] [[1879]] and reproduced in full by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in his [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* — is the formal declaration that "*geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt*" was (all Groot-Atjeh had been brought under): that the entire valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]] had been brought under. The day-order — addressed *Aan de Officieren, Ambtenaren, Onderofficieren en Soldaten* (To the Officers, Officials, Non-commissioned Officers and Soldiers) of the Atjeh garrison, signed by the *generaal-majoor, adjudant des Konings in buitengewonen dienst, Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (Major-General, extraordinary aide-de-camp to the King, Governor and at the same time military commander of Atjeh and Dependencies) — was the closing act of the [[XXVI Moekims campaign (1879)|XXVI Moekims campaign]] and, in Van Heutsz's reading, the high-water mark of the Dutch conquest of the interior: the moment at which, "*nog geen 14 maanden na het begin van zijne zegerijke veldtochten*" (not yet 14 months after the beginning of his victorious campaigns), the General "*aan alle verzet een einde gemaakt*" had (had made an end to all resistance) and "*het Ned. bestuur volgens de Atjehsche begrippen, door het recht van verovering, op den troon van den Sultan*" sat (the Netherlands administration, according to Atjehnese conceptions, by the right of conquest, sat on the throne of the Sultan). The day-order was independently confirmed by the Commandant van het Indische leger (Commandant of the Indian army)'s inspection report of the same month, which Van Heutsz quotes at length to verify the General's right to make the declaration.

## The dateline and signature

The day-order's dateline and signature are preserved by Van Heutsz in the OCR-damaged reading that became, in the original print, "*Kota-Radja, 5 September 1879*" and "*K. VAN DER HEUDEN*". The "*HEUDEN*" reading — an OCR rendering of the printed "*HEIJDEN*" — is the signature Van Heutsz reproduces as printed, and is the form in which the day-order has survived in the [[1893]] tract. The full title block of the day-order reads, in Van Heutsz's reproduction:

> "KOTA-RADJA, 5 September 1879. De generaal-majoor, adjudant des Konings in buitengewonen dienst, Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden. K. VAN DER HEUDEN".

The signature block establishes the day-order's authorship: the *generaal-majoor, adjudant des Konings in buitengewonen dienst, Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* — Major-General, extraordinary aide-de-camp to the King, Governor and at the same time military commander of Atjeh and Dependencies. The *adjudant des Konings* (aide-de-camp to the King) title is the formal warrant by which Van der Heijden, in his own person, could "*LEVE DE KONING!*" — *Long live the King!* — at the day-order's close. The choice of the *Dagorder* form — a general's formal address to his troops — rather than a *proclamatie* (proclamation) to the Atjehnese or a *verslag* (report) to the Regeering fixes the document as an internal military communication, addressed to the officers, officials, non-commissioned officers and soldiers who had carried out the campaign.

## The declaration of total submission

The substantive content of the day-order — the declaration that "*geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt*" was — opens the document and is its central sentence. The full text as reproduced by Van Heutsz:

> "Dagorder. Officieren, Ambtenaren, Onderofficieren en Soldaten! Met den veldtocht tegen de XXVI Moekims, schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen; is geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt. De bevolking keert van lieverlede in hare kampongs terug; enkele aanzienlijke hoofden zijn reeds in onderwerping gekomen; er bestaat gegronde hoop dat velen dat voorbeeld zullen volgen. Krijgsbewegingen van grooten omvang zullen vermoedelijk niet meer noodig zijn, maar nog geruimen tijd wordt de krachtige steun der troepenmacht vereischt om een goed bestuur in te voeren en eene geregelde orde van zaken te scheppen. (1) Officieren, Onderofficieren en minderen! De krijgsoperatien in de XXII en XXVI Moekims, die in Juli van het vorige jaar zijn aangevangen en zoo schitterende uitkomsten hebben opgeleverd, hebben Uwen krijgsroem verhoogd en glansrijk bevestigd dat Z. M. onze geëerbiedigde Koning recht heeft, trotsch te zijn op het leger en de vloot, die in Zijnen naam, Neerland's gezag in Indië helpen handhaven. LEVE DE KONING! Kota-Radja, 5 September 1879. De generaal-majoor, adjudant des Konings in buitengewonen dienst, Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden. K. VAN DER HEUDEN".

Van Heutsz's footnote — "*Deze cursiveering is van mij*" (This italicisation is mine) — marks his editorial intervention: the italicised "*nog geruimen tijd wordt de krachtige steun der troepenmacht vereischt om een goed bestuur in te voeren en eene geregelde orde van zaken te scheppen*" (for some time yet the vigorous support of the troop-strength will be required to introduce a good administration and to create a regulated order of affairs) is Van Heutsz's own emphasis, drawing the reader's attention to the General's measured warning that the military conquest would not, by itself, produce the political order. The day-order's three sentences after the declaration — the return of the population, the submission of "*enkele aanzienlijke hoofden*" (some considerable chiefs), the "*gegronde hoop*" (well-founded hope) of more, the suspension of large-scale operations, the continued need for troop support to introduce good government and orderly administration — are Van Heutsz's warrant for his central argument: that the General did not "*overschat*" (overestimate) the consequences of his victory, but "*met wijs beleid en kloek verstand in die korte woorden te kennen gaf, wat nog te doen overbleef*" (with wise policy and firm understanding in those short words gave to know what remained to be done).

## The Selimoen tocht and the closing of the XXVI Moekims campaign

The day-order's opening sentence — "*Met den veldtocht tegen de XXVI Moekims, schitterend besloten door den tocht naar Selimoen; is geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt*" (With the campaign against the XXVI Moekims, brilliantly concluded by the march to Selimoen; all Groot-Atjeh has been brought under) — places the document within the operational sequence of the [[XXVI Moekims campaign (1879)|XXVI Moekims campaign]] and the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen tocht]] that closed it. The XXVI Moekims campaign had begun in [[July]] [[1879]] as the second of the two *Sagi* campaigns of [[1878]]–[[1879]] — the operational turning point of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] that had begun with the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] of [[July]] [[1878]] — and culminated in the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of [[September]] [[1879]], in which columns from Lamtoba and [[Indrapoeri]] visited [[Selimoen]] and thereby closed the conquest of the second *Sagi* of Groot-Atjeh. The day-order's declaration that "*geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt*" was — "*schitterend besloten*" (brilliantly concluded) — was the General's own framing of the Selimoen tocht as the closing movement of the entire campaign sequence.

## The inspection tour of the Commandant van het Indische leger

The day-order was independently confirmed by the [[September]] [[1879]] inspection report of the Commandant van het Indische leger (Commandant of the Indian army) — who visited Atjeh in the same month and submitted his *verslag* (report) to the Regeering. Van Heutsz invokes the inspection as the independent authority that confirmed Van der Heijden's right to make the declaration:

> "In dezelfde maand September 1879 heeft de Commandant van het Indische leger Atjeh bezocht en deze bevestigde in zijn aan de Regeering ingediend verslag geheel, dat de Generaal in zijne zoo even aangehaalde dagorder met recht zeggen mocht, dat geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder was gebracht".

The *verslag*'s author had himself crossed the entire Dutch-occupied territory of Groot-Atjeh — "*een feit toch is het, dat ik geheel Groot-Atjeh, voor zoover het door ons bezet is, heb doorkruist; in de XXII Moekims tot onzen uitersten wachtpost Djerir, in de XXVI Moekims tot Kroeng Kali in het Noorden, tot Tjot Lepong in het Oosten, en in de XXV ooekims tot Kroeng Raba*" (a fact it is, that I have traversed all Groot-Atjeh, as far as it is occupied by us; in the XXII Moekims to our outermost sentry-post Djerir, in the XXVI Moekims to Kroeng Kali in the North, to Tjot Lepong in the East, and in the XXV Moekims to Kroeng Raba) — and reported, "*hoezeer slechts vergezeld door een twaalftal cavaleristen, nergens een schot gehoord of een gewapend Atjeher gezien*" (how, although accompanied only by some twelve cavalrymen, [he had] nowhere heard a shot or seen an armed Atjehnese). The inspection's conclusion — "*Naar mijn gevoelen komt het mij voor, dat de sedert Juni 1878 gevolgde gedragslijn de daarvan verwachte resultaten heeft opgeleverd*" (In my feeling it appears to me, that the course of action followed since June 1878 has delivered the expected results) — was the political-military confirmation Van Heutsz needed.

## The strategic situation after the day-order

The day-order marked, in Van Heutsz's reading, the high-water mark of the Dutch position in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. With the declaration of [[5 September]] [[1879]] and the inspection report of the same month, the General was "*de door den vijand gevreesde overwinnaar van het aloude machtige Atjehsche rijk*" (the conqueror, feared by the enemy, of the ancient mighty Atjehnese realm) and the Dutch administration "*volgens de Atjehsche begrippen, door het recht van verovering, op den troon van den Sultan*" sat (according to Atjehnese conceptions, by the right of conquest, sat on the throne of the Sultan). The strategic situation after the day-order was, however, more ambiguous than the declaration itself. The [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] (shipping regulation) of [[Scherer]] — conceived in [[1880]] on the empirical basis of the [[Gighen]] and [[Samalanga]] *kruisboot-bangoen* (cruiser blood-price) tests — was the political instrument Van Heutsz identified as the necessary complement of the military conquest:

> "ware het den Generaal van der Heijden gegeven geworden, dit op een juisten grondslag berustende stelsel uit te voeren, hij, die reeds over het brandpunt van het verzet winnaar was van Groot-Atjeh … zou in korten tijd het geheele rijk den voet op den nek gezet, het eerst tot volkomen rust en vrede, daarna tot welvaart hebben gebracht".

The complement was not to be granted. Van der Heijden was dismissed "*in het begin van 1881 op onverantwoordelijke wijze*" (in the beginning of 1881 in an irresponsible manner), and the day-order of [[5 September]] [[1879]] remained, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the formal close of the only period in which the Atjeh campaign had been brought to a *rechtmatige voldoening* (rightful satisfaction) — and the document against which every subsequent governor's accomplishment would be measured and found wanting. Van Heutsz's counter-factual — "*ware hij niet in het begin van 1881 op onverantwoordelijke wijze ontslagen, dan behoorde de Atjeh-oorlog thans reeds lang tot het verledene*" (had he not been dismissed in the beginning of 1881 in an irresponsible manner, then the Atjeh-war would by now long belong to the past) — is the rhetorical frame within which the entire post-[[1881]] decade is judged.

## Significance

Van der Heijden's day-order of [[5 September]] [[1879]] is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the formal declaration that "*geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt*" was — the high-water mark of the Dutch conquest of the interior and the document against which every subsequent governor's accomplishment would be measured and found wanting. Its significance in the Aceh Wiki History record lies in four things. First, it was the closing act of the [[XXVI Moekims campaign (1879)|XXVI Moekims campaign]] — itself closed by the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen tocht]] of [[September]] [[1879]] — and the formal close of the operational sequence that had begun with the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] of [[July]] [[1878]]: the moment at which, in Van Heutsz's reading, "*nog geen 14 maanden na het begin van zijne zegerijke veldtochten*", the General "*aan alle verzet een einde gemaakt*" had. Second, it was confirmed by the independent inspection of the Commandant van het Indische leger in the same month — the authority Van Heutsz invokes to verify the General's right to make the declaration. Third, it was, in Van Heutsz's editorial emphasis, the document in which the General "*met wijs beleid en kloek verstand in die korte woorden te kennen gaf, wat nog te doen overbleef*" — the measured warning that the military conquest would not, by itself, produce the political order, and that "*nog geruimen tijd wordt de krachtige steun der troepenmacht vereischt om een goed bestuur in te voeren en eene geregelde orde van zaken te scheppen*". Fourth, it was the formal close of the only period in which the Atjeh campaign had been brought to a *rechtmatige voldoening* — and the document against which [[General van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden's]] subsequent dismissal "*in het begin van 1881 op onverantwoordelijke wijze*" and the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|1884 Concentratie]] withdrawal to the *geconcentreerde stelling* (concentrated defensive line) of [[Groot-Atjeh]] would, in Van Heutsz's narrative, be judged as the colony's two great acts of self-betrayal.

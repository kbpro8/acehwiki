**De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)** — *The Subjugation of Atjeh* — is the brochure published in [[1893]] by [[J. B. van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] — then a colonel in the Atjeh command — in which he set out the strategic doctrine that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|the 1896 defection of Toekoe Oemar]] and his own elevation to the governorship of Atjeh, would guide the post-[[1896]] conquest of the sultanate. The brochure is the principal *ex ante* text of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] doctrine and the document against which [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s [[1908]] parliamentary critique of the post-conquest administration is measured.

## The cited passage

The most frequently cited passage of the [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 brochure]] is the one quoted by [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] in the [[1908]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate on the [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908 report]] of governor-general [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]:

> "Wij zitten niet in Atjeh om vliegen te vangen en maar steeds onze millioenen weg te werpen; Atjeh moet onderworpen worden, dat is de zaak, en om dit resultaat na al ons morsen en knoeien nog te erlangen moet Atjeh zelf betalen wat daarvoor voortaan nog aan dit land wordt ten koste gelegd."

— We are not in Atjeh to catch flies and to keep throwing away our millions; Atjeh must be subjugated, that is the point, and to obtain this result after all our messing and botching Atjeh itself must pay what is henceforth to be spent on this land.

The passage is preserved by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] with the citation: "J. B. van Heutsz, De onderwerping van Atjeh, blz. 79".

## The Van Deventer critique

[[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.) suspected that governor-general [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had permitted [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to act in accordance with the [[1893]] view set out in the brochure, and had only abandoned him when the [[Van Heutsz's inspection tour of Atjeh (December 1907)|December 1907 inspection tour]] had made the unworkability of "this harsh and grasping policy" plain. The [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908 report]] — with its emphasis on the restoration of the *hoofden* (chiefs) and on "tactvolle omgang" — was, on this reading, the repudiation of the [[1893]] brochure's doctrine that Atjeh itself must pay for its own subjugation.

## The IJzerman defence

IJzerman (u.l.) replied that it was unfair to pursue [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] with a brochure fifteen years old. "Hebben dan alleen de grote mannen in Nederland het recht om er oude plunje op na te houden?" — Have then only the great men in the Netherlands the right to keep old clothes in the closet? Why not rejoice that [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had outgrown [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]? [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] — minister of Colonies — endorsed the IJzerman view: it was [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s greatest merit that he had put an end to the Dutch feeling of impotence with respect to the [[Buitenbezittingen|Buitenbezettingen]].

## Significance

[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|The 1893 brochure]] is the principal *ex ante* text of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] doctrine. The 1908 parliamentary exchange between [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] and [[IJzerman (vrij-liberaal MP)|IJzerman]] on the continuing authority of the brochure is the canonical exchange on the relation between the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] of [[1893]] (the conqueror) and the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] of [[1908]] (the pacificator).

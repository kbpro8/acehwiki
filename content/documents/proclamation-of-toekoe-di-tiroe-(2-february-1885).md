**The proclamation of Toekoe di Tiroe of [[2 February]] [[1885]]** — dated 16 Rabioel Achir 1302 A.H. — was a victory proclamation issued by [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Toekoe di Tiroe]], the principal war leader of the [[Aceh War|Acehnese resistance]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], from his headquarters at [[Anak Galoeng]], at the moment when the Dutch were carrying out the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration]], the withdrawal from the outer posts into the limited fortified line around [[Kuta Raja|Kota Radja]]. The pamphlet was widely circulated not only among the Acehnese but, as was reported at the time, even among the Javanese population of the Indies.

## Contents

The proclamation, as rendered in Dutch by [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] in his 1886 pamphlet, declared:

> "En geopenbaard is het teeken der overwinning door het terugtrekken der ongeloovigen. Hoevele sterke versterkingen hebben de Moslims reeds genomen, hoevele stevige wapenen buit gemaakt! De zekerheid, dat de Moslims moedig zijn, vermeerdert de vrees, de schuwheid en den kommer der ongeloovigen en renegaten. Een duidelijk teeken daarvan is, dat zij door versterkingen omgeven, omheiningen (de 1000 M. breede, zoogenaamde vrije strook) willen maken en wachten plaatsen."

("And the sign of victory has been revealed by the retreat of the unbelievers. How many strong fortifications the Muslims have already taken, how many stout weapons they have seized! The certainty that the Muslims are brave increases the fear, the timidity and the distress of the unbelievers and renegades. A clear sign of this is that they, surrounded by fortifications, want to make enclosures — the 1000-metre-wide so-called [[1000-meter strook|free strip]] — and to post watches.") The proclamation ended: "De ongeloovigen zullen, zoo God het wil, overwonnen en verdreven worden, zooals de Heer, de Verhevene, heeft beloofd" ("The unbelievers will, God willing, be conquered and expelled, as the Lord, the Exalted, has promised").

## Context and interpretation

Verstege printed the proclamation as documentary proof that the Atjehnese read the Dutch concentration — defended by minister [[Sprenger van Eijk]] in the Tweede Kamer on [[19 December]] [[1884]] — as nothing but a flight, and he remarked that it was remarkable that Toekoe di Tiroe now had his headquarters at [[Anak Galoeng]], the very place which had for a long time been the Dutch headquarters and to which [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]], as Gouverneur-Commissaris, had in September [[1880]] wanted to move the chief seat of the government, as being more centrally situated in the then line — a proposal the government had declined to adopt. Verstege warned that the proclamation, circulated even among the Javanese population, could fall on fertile ground outside Atjeh as well, in view of the revival of religious zeal observed among the Muslims of the archipelago for some time, fed from Turkey according to warnings received by the government already at the end of [[1880]] and in February and March [[1881]], and further excited by the events in the Sudan.

Verstege noted in the same connection that already in [[1884]] an Arabic pamphlet had been circulated in the Preanger Regentschappen, in Bantam, in Central Java and in Atjeh, in which a certain Sjaich Abdoellah reported a dream-vision which had befallen him at Medina in the year 1297 A.H. ([[1880]]), entitled *De laatste vermaning van Mohamed aan zijne gemeente* ("The last admonition of Muhammad to his community"); Dr. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] wrote of it, in the *Indische Gids* of July [[1884]], that it pronounced a kind of excommunication over those who refused to be stirred up, but had no political tendency and could be called more innocent than the *Akireng Djaman*, in which the King of the Netherlands was made contemptible as an unbeliever and ridiculous as a conquered man.

**The Sumatra-tractaat (1871)** — the Sumatra Treaty of [[1871]] — was the Anglo-Dutch treaty by which [[Great Britain|England]] agreed that the whole of [[Sumatra]] — including the independent [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate — would fall within the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] sphere of influence, in exchange for the concession to British subjects of the same commercial rights throughout [[Sumatra]] as those enjoyed by Dutch subjects. The treaty is the diplomatic hinge on which the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] turns; its conclusion is the proximate cause of the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war on Atjeh]] of [[1873]].

## The treaty

The [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] between the [[Netherlands]] and [[Great Britain|England]] provided that "geheel Sumatra binnen de Nederlandse invloedssfeer zou vallen, terwijl de Engelse onderdanen in geheel Sumatra dezelfde rechten zouden gaan genieten als de Nederlanders" — the whole of Sumatra would fall within the Dutch sphere of influence, while British subjects in the whole of Sumatra would enjoy the same rights as the Dutch. The treaty was, the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] government later said, "welbewust om deze reden" — for this very reason — concluded: the [[Soezkanaal|opening of the Suez Canal]] in [[1869]] had increased the strategic and economic value of [[Straat Malakka]] as the shipping lane between the Indian Ocean and the South China Sea, and the [[Netherlands]] wished to secure its control of the whole [[Sumatra|Sumatran]] littoral that commanded the strait.

## Ministerial reassurances

Minister [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]] delivered to the [[Tweede Kamer]] "zeer geruststellende verklaringen" — very reassuring statements — on the consequences of the instrument he had just signed. Some chambermembers had feared "moeilijkheden met Atjeh" — difficulties with Atjeh — but had been quieted by [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]]'s reassurances.

## The 1897 retrospective

In the [[1897]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate on credits for [[Nieuw Guinea]], [[W. J. van Welderen Rengers|Van Welderen Rengers]] (lib.) recalled the [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]] assurances of [[1871]] as the type-specimen of the danger of "the first step" in colonial affairs. The first step, the signature of the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] in [[1871]], had been taken easily; the subsequent steps — the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war on Atjeh]] in [[1873]] and the thirty-one-year [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] that followed — were the steps that cost money and lives.

## The "stel inbrekers" judgement

[[Troelstra|Troelstra]] (s.d.) later characterised the expression "afbakening van invloedssferen" — delimitation of spheres of influence — as "veel te opgepoetst voor een afspraak tussen een stel inbrekers, elkaar niet te hinderen bij hun bezigheden" — much too polished for an arrangement between a set of burglars, not hindering one another in their pursuits. The [[Troelstra|Troelstra]] judgement is the canonical socialist comment on the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]].

## The parliamentary non-mention during the war

When the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|vijandelijkheden in Atjeh broke out]] in [[1873]], "kon het Sumatra-tractaat natuurlijk officieel niet vermeld worden" — the Sumatra-tractaat could not, of course, be officially mentioned. What corresponded most closely to reality was "vrees voor buitenlandse inmenging" — fear of foreign interference — which, however, was supposed to be diminished precisely by the conclusion of the Sumatra-tractaat. The contradiction is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the measure of the official bad faith of the [[1873]] *motivering*.

## Significance

The [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] is the diplomatic instrument by which the [[Netherlands]] secured international recognition of its claim to Atjeh. Without the treaty, the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war of 1873]] would have risked British intervention; with the treaty, the war became a purely Dutch affair. The treaty is, accordingly, the indispensable diplomatic precondition of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]].

**Verkenningen in Atjeh** — *Reconnaissances in Atjeh* — is a series of travel essays by [[Dr. R. Broersma|Dr. R. Broersma]] published in the newspaper *De Locomotief* in semi-October [[1923]] and afterwards issued as an offprint bearing the shelfmark C 527 of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde|Kon. Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde]] in Leiden. The series records a journey of reconnaissance through the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in the early years of civilian rule, undertaken in the decade after the formal pacification of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] when the last sultan, [[Sultan of Atjeh|Muhammad Daud Syah]], had already ceded his realm to the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] government and the [[Atjeh-tram]] was beginning to serve economic rather than purely military ends.

## Scope and Structure
The offprint comprises twenty numbered essays of varying length, beginning with an *Inleiding* on the traveller's first approach to [[Atjeh]] and proceeding geographically from south to north and then inland. The early chapters describe the [[Tamiang]] river basin, the underafdeeling [[Langsa]] with its wood-cutting industry and leper colony, and the historical region of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Perlak]]. The narrative then advances through [[Idi]], [[Lho Soekon]], the ancient kingdom of [[Samoedra]], [[Bireuen]] in the landschap of [[Peusangan]], and the [[Gajo-weg]] leading up to [[Takengon]] on [[Laoet Tawar|Laut Tawar]]. The later essays treat the great rice-plain of [[Pidië]], the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] in detail, [[Groot-Atjeh]] with its capital [[Kota Radja]] and the harbour of [[Oelèë Lheue]], the question of [[Atjeh als rijstland|Atjeh as a rice country]], and finally a series of reflective chapters on cattle-breeding, trade, labour and *staatkunde* (policy).

The work is not a military history but an economic and administrative reconnaissance. Broersma explicitly states in his *Inleiding* that he does not wish to re-trace the war — *'den strijd niet na te speuren, wenschende te zien het land en het volk in hun staat van vrede'* — but rather to follow the roads along which *'een rustig volksbestaan vruchten van den arbeid binnenhaalt tot ruimer welvaart.'*

## Sources Drawn Upon
Throughout the essays Broersma cites earlier authorities on Atjeh. He repeatedly invokes [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners* (1903) and the dictionary of [[Dr. Hazeu|Dr. Hazeu]] for the interior. For the east coast he leans heavily on [[J. A. Kruyt|J. A. Kruyt]]'s *Atjeh en de Atjehers* (Leiden [[1877]]), which records Kruyt's two years of blockade service aboard the man-of-war *Timor* along Sumatra's north-east coast during [[1873]]–[[1875]]. The colonial publication *[[De Buitenbezittingen 1904-1914|De Buitenbezittingen 1904-'14]]* provides his export statistics, while [[Dr. Julius Jacobs|Dr. Julius Jacobs]]'s *Het familie- en kampongleven op Groot-Atjeh* ([[1894]]) is cited for the older ethnography of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the condition of lepers there. A 1918 article by [[H. T. Damste|H. T. Damste]] in *Koloniaal Tijdschrift*, *'Een stukje Atjeh-geschiedenis'*, supplies the succession narrative of [[Simpang Olim]]. The soil chemist [[F. C. van Heurn|F. C. van Heurn]]'s *Gegevens betreffende den bodem van Sumatra's Oostkust* supplies the geological judgement on Tamiang. Broersma also quotes, in long paraphrase, the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] pamphlet *[[De rijkdom van Atjeh|De rijkdom van Atjeh]]* of July [[1923]].

## Method and Tone
Broersma's method is that of the colonial observer with a practical eye: he records the prices of pepper and pinang, the cost of an artesian boring in Langsa (f 122.931 between [[1906]] and [[1919]]), the wages of *panglong* wood-cutters at [[Bajan]] (f 3.50 per man per month under the regulation of *St. 1909 No. 488*), and the prices fetched by an elephant tusk at Peureula, Idi and Peusangan. He visits [[Oeléebalang|oeléebalangs]] in their houses, takes down their genealogies and reports their revenues from the *hacil* and the petroleum cijns. His tone is critical of Dutch mismanagement — the failed *gouvernements rubberonderneming* at Langsa opened in [[1907]] on peat soil that has since sunk under the trees — but sympathetic to the Atjehnese commoner, whose *'eeng'* (penny-wise) temperament he takes to be the natural seed of commerce once peace should allow it to develop.

## Significance
The *Verkenningen* is one of the most detailed non-military surveys of Atjeh in the early civilian period, treating the country at the moment when the *Atjeh-tram* met the *Deli-spoor* at [[Besitang]] in [[1908]] and when the *Afdeeling Pidië* was detached from the *Noordkust van Atjeh* in [[1922]]. It is widely quoted in later colonial literature on the economic geography of northern Sumatra.

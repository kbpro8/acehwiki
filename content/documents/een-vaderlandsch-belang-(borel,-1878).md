**[[Een vaderlandsch belang (Borel, 1878)|Een vaderlandsch belang]]** ("A national interest") is the three-part article published by [[G. F. W. Borel|kapitein der artillerie G. F. W. Borel]] in *Het Vaderland* on [[1 March|1]], [[2 March|2]] and [[3 March]] [[1878]], proposing the formation in the Netherlands of an expeditionnaire krijgsmacht of 3 000–4 000 man — the [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] — to be sent directly to [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] and to end the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] by force. Borel amplified the proposal in *De Militaire Spectator*, No. 7, jaargang [[1878]], and recapitulated it in the closing chapters of *[[Onze vestiging in Atjeh (Borel, 1878)|Onze vestiging in Atjeh]]*, finished at 's-Gravenhage on [[27 August]] [[1878]].

## The Argument

The article's title — *Een vaderlandsch belang*, "a national interest" — announced its claim: that the conduct of the Atjeh-oorlog was no longer a colonial question but a metropolitan one, on which the honour and the finances of the Netherlands depended. Borel argued that the war, already in its sixth year by [[1878]], could not be ended by the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] alone. The army was "in geen gunstigen toestand": manschappen onvoldoende geoefend, kaders in treurige staat, many sick or unfit, with a groot gebrek aan officieren, especially in the artillerie. The [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] from the [[Kroeng Raba]] to the [[Kwala Gigieng]] — completed by [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] in May [[1875]] and extended to the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] in February [[1876]] — had been left "nagenoeg onveranderd" under [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]]'s long command, and the *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture) had once again become Dutch strategy. The [[XXII Moekims]] — "de kern van Atjeh's weerstandsvermogen," the densest-populated Sagi containing the Sultan's voorraadschuren and the most hostile chiefs — had never been permanently occupied; only repeated *tuchtigingen* by fresh troops would reduce them. Borel's proposal was that the Netherlands should raise, equip and despatch to Atjeh a force adequate to the task: 3 000–4 000 men, infantry, artillery and engineers, raised from Dutch vrijwilligers and sent directly — not via Java's "ongezonde garnizoenen."

## The Brigade

The Indische Brigade was specified in detail. The force was to comprise 150 officers and 3 000 men — infantry, artillery and mineurs — raised in the Netherlands from Dutch vrijwilligers, not from the inlandsche or European troops of the regular Indische leger. The volunteers were to be attracted with premies: ƒ 250–300 handgeld at enlistment, ƒ 1 per day soldij in Atjeh, and an equal premie on return. They were to be bound to a strict two-year maximum verbintenis, after which they would be released with the premie and the option of settling in the Indies. They were to be armed with steel [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]] with schokbuis — the modern materieel whose absence Borel had indicted throughout his book. The estimated cost was ƒ 6 000 000 for the first year and ƒ 10–15 million for the whole two years, to be raised as an *oorlogsleening* (war loan) and ultimately repaid, Borel suggested, in Acehnese pepper. The brigade's members were to be promised the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] for distinguished conduct. The proposal was, in Borel's argument, no more than a fraction of what the war was already costing in demurrage (the [[Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij|Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij]]'s ƒ 1,200 per day per ship), in reinforcements, and in the slow attrition of the Indische leger — and would, by ending the war, eliminate the very demand for such expenditures.

## Reception and the Closing of the Book

The article was amplified in *De Militaire Spectator*, No. 7, jaargang [[1878]], where Borel elaborated the proposal in greater technical detail. The book *Onze vestiging in Atjeh* — finished on [[27 August]] [[1878]] — recapitulated the proposal as its prescriptive closing, with the wish that, if the Indische Brigade were adopted, the words of Camille Rousset on the [[1830]] French expedition against Algiers — which departed France on [[25 May]] [[1830]] with 36 000 soldiers — might apply to the Dutch in Atjeh: "rien n'avait été négligé pour le succès de l'expédition." The example of [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]]'s two expeditions against [[Gedoeng]] in June [[1878]] — after the second of which the chief laid down bestuur and his son came to submit — and the [[Submission of Pasangan (15 August 1878)|submission of Pasangan]] after naval bombardment on [[15 August]] [[1878]] confirmed, Borel argued, the *aanvallende stelsel* and the necessity of overwhelming force. The Indische Brigade proposal — though never adopted in Borel's exact form — anticipated the later metropolitan reinforcements and the *maréchaussée* system associated with [[Johannes van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] that finally brought the Atjeh-oorlog to a close in the early twentieth century.

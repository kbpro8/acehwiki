**Van Teijn's warning letter of [[23 November]] [[1891]]** — the *brief, gedagteekend: Utreeht, 23 November 1891* (letter, dated: Utrecht, 23 November 1891) by which the retired [[Van Teijn|Generaal van Teijn]], formerly Governor of [[Atjeh]] and Onderhoorigheden, warned the Minister of Koloniën (Colonies) against the policy changes being introduced by the Governor who had succeeded him — is the rhetorical starting point of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, where it is reproduced as the opening exhibit of the book's central diagnosis that "*telkens veranderen van politiek*" — the constantly changing of policy — was the structural cause of the colony's failure to close the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Van Heutsz introduces the letter in his opening paragraph as the *laatste* (last) attempt by the General, *nog eenmaal* (one more time), to warn the colonial government: "*Nog eenmaal en wel bij brief, gedagteekend: Utreeht, 23 November 1891 achtte de Generaal zich verplicht den Minister van Koloniën te waarschuwen*" (One more time, by letter dated Utrecht, 23 November 1891, the General deemed himself obliged to warn the Minister of Colonies). The letter was sent from the General's retirement in the Netherlands, three years after his Atjeh governorship, in the very month that his successor's reversal of the *sluiting-stelsel* (closure-system) had begun — and is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the warning that "*niet gebaat heeft*" (did not avail): that did not avail.

## The dateline: Utreeht, 23 November 1891

The letter's dateline — preserved in Van Heutsz's OCR-damaged reading as "*Utreeht, 23 November 1891*" (the printer's "Utrecht" misread by the OCR engine) — fixes the document's provenance in the Netherlands, not in [[Batavia]] or [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]]. The General, having retired from the Atjeh governorship in [[1888]] and from active service thereafter, wrote from his home country to the sitting Minister of Koloniën at [[The Hague|'s-Gravenhage]]. The dateline thus marks the letter as a posthumous-citizen's intervention — the warning of a man no longer in office, addressed to the political authority that had once been his superior.

Van Heutsz introduces the letter with the framing clause:

> "Nog eenmaal en wel bij brief, gedagteekend: Utreeht, 23 November 1891 achtte de Generaal zich verplicht den Minister van Koloniën te waarschuwen. Hij schreef toen o. a.: «Dat telkens veranderen van politiek; dat gebrek aan eene vaste coonsequent doorgezette richting; die proefnemingen van nieuw optredende gouverneurs; dit alles stijft den Atjeher in hooge mate in zijn verzet, in zijne overtuiging dat de Hollanders niet weten wat ze willen, te zwak zijn om consequent een beginsel door te voeren, en daarop door te gaan; niets kan onze positie, ons aanzien dan ook meer benadeelen dan die aanhoudende veranderingen. Vooral is dit het geval indien van richting wordt veranderd nadat de goede resultaten daarvan in casu de sluiting zich reeds hebben doen gevoelen. En bij mijn aftreden kon ik met gegronde voldoening op vooruitgang wijzen, sedert mijne benoeming tot Gouverneur zoowel wat betreft zuiver politiek als militair gebied. Met ernst en overtuiging waarschuw ik dan ook tegen de door den tegenwoordigen Gouverneur voorgestane veranderingen van politiek en geef ik der Regeering dringend in overweging, om den Gouverneur van Atjeh door eene bindende instructie te dwingen de door Haar vast te stellen politiek te volgen. De Gouverneur zal echter binnen die afgebakende richting de handen vrij moeten hebben en moeten kunnen beschikken over de ten dienste staande middelen, zooals hem dit het doelmatigst en het meest in het belang van den lande voorkomt».".

The letter is, in this opening excerpt, the document Van Heutsz chose to reproduce *in extenso* (in full) on the second page of his book — the document on which the entire 1893 policy-tract's argument is built.

## The Kemala prophecy: the Atjehnese diagnosis of Dutch policy

Van Heutsz frames the letter with a preliminary exhibit he adduces from the *Indisch Militair Tijdschrift* (Indies Military Journal) No. 8 of [[1892]] (bladzijde 669) — the testimony of the chiefs and elders of [[Keumala|Kemala]] given *kort voor het vertrek van Generaal van Teijn* (shortly before the departure of General van Teijn):

> "De Atjehers te Kemala gaven kort voor het vertrek van Generaal van Teijn het volgende antwoord op deze vraag: De nood, door de sluiting veroorzaakt, was er hoog, maar, zeiden hoofden en ouden van dagen: Laten wij volhouden en ons in geen geval vóór de ophanden zijnde bestuursverandering onderwerpen; de ondervinding toch leerde ons, dat bij de Hollanders iedere bestuurs-verandering wijziging brengt in de bestuurs-inzichten".

The *Kemala prophecy* — the chiefs' advice to *hold out, because every Dutch change of governor brings a change of policy* — is, in Van Heutsz's framing, the Atjehnese confirmation of what Van Teijn would himself warn the Minister about: that the colony's habit of *bestuursverandering* (change of administration) was not merely an administrative inconvenience but an active source of Atjehnese resistance. The Regeering (Government), Van Heutsz notes, had been warned: "*De Regeering wist dit, want de Generaal van Teijn meldde Haar dit en toch bracht de bestuursverandering wijziging in de bestuurs-inzichten*" (The Government knew this, because General van Teijn reported it to Her, and yet the change of administration brought a modification in the administrative-views). The letter of [[23 November]] [[1891]] was Van Teijn's *last* attempt to make the warning stick.

## The warning: "dat telkens veranderen van politiek"

The substantive content of the letter is the warning against the *telkens veranderen van politiek* — the constantly changing of policy — that Van Teijn identifies as the structural defect of the colony's Atjeh administration. The General's diagnosis has three elements, each reproduced by Van Heutsz in his opening excerpt. First, the absence of a *vaste consequent doorgezette richting* (firm, consistently pursued direction) — which he couples with the *proefnemingen van nieuw optredende gouverneurs* (experiments of newly-arriving governors), each reversing his predecessor's programme. Second, the strategic consequence: this oscillation "*stijft den Atjeher in hooge mate in zijn verzet, in zijne overtuiging dat de Hollanders niet weten wat ze willen, te zwak zijn om consequent een beginsel door te voeren*" (strengthens the Atjehnese in his resistance, in his conviction that the Dutch do not know what they want, that they are too weak to carry a principle through). Third, the worst case: that the changes are introduced *after* the previous policy has begun to bear fruit — "*Vooral is dit het geval indien van richting wordt veranderd nadat de goede resultaten daarvan in casu de sluiting zich reeds hebben doen gevoelen*" (Above all this is the case if a change of direction is made after the good results thereof, in casu the closure, have already begun to be felt).

The reference to *de sluiting* — the closure of the Atjeh coast that Van Teijn had himself imposed and that his successor was dismantling — is the political sting of the letter. The General's own record, set out in the next sentence — "*En bij mijn aftreden kon ik met gegronde voldoening op vooruitgang wijzen, sedert mijne benoeming tot Gouverneur zoowel wat betreft zuiver politiek als militair gebied*" (And on my retirement I could with well-founded satisfaction point to progress, since my appointment as Governor both in the purely political and in the military domain) — was the warrant from which he wrote: he had left the colony measurably better than he had found it, and the *sluiting* had begun to do its work. The successor's *veranderingen van politiek* (changes of policy) were, in the General's reading, a reversal of a programme that had already begun to deliver.

## The prescription: "eene bindende instructie"

Van Teijn's prescription is constitutional rather than tactical. He does not ask the Regeering to dictate a specific policy; he asks that the Regeering *bind the Governor* to whatever policy it has itself fixed:

> "Met ernst en overtuiging waarschuw ik dan ook tegen de door den tegenwoordigen Gouverneur voorgestane veranderingen van politiek en geef ik der Regeering dringend in overweging, om den Gouverneur van Atjeh door eene bindende instructie te dwingen de door Haar vast te stellen politiek te volgen. De Gouverneur zal echter binnen die afgebakende richting de handen vrij moeten hebben en moeten kunnen beschikken over de ten dienste staande middelen, zooals hem dit het doelmatigst en het meest in het belang van den lande voorkomt".

The *bindende instructie* (binding instruction) formula — a binding instruction that would compel the Governor to follow the Regeering's fixed policy — is, in Van Teijn's reading, the only way to break the cycle of *bestuursverandering* that the Atjehnese themselves had learned to exploit. The second sentence — "*de handen vrij*" (the hands free) — adds the necessary complement: within the fixed policy, the Governor must retain operational latitude over the *middelen* (means) at his disposal. The prescription thus balances *binding* at the strategic level with *discretion* at the operational level — a balance that would become the central prescription of Van Heutsz's own [[1893]] tract.

## The eleven Governors and the thirteen Ministers

Van Heutsz follows the letter with the catalogue that gives the warning its numerical weight: "*Aanvangende met den Generaal van Swieten hebben wij nu den elfden Gouverneur van Atjeh: van Swieten, [[Pel (governor)|Pel]], [[Wiggers van Kerchem]], [[Demmeni|Diemont]], [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]], Pruijs van der Hoeven, [[Laging Tobias]], [[Demmeni]], [[Van Teijn|van Teijn]], [[Pompe van Meerdervoort]], Deijkerhof*" (Beginning with General van Swieten we now have the eleventh Governor of Atjeh: van Swieten, Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont, Van der Heijden, Pruijs van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias, Demmeni, van Teijn, Pompe van Meerdervoort, Deijkerhof). The eleven Governors — in eighteen years — were matched by five Governors-General — Loudon, [[Van Lansberge|van Lansberge]], 's Jacob, van Rees, Pijnacker Hordijk — and, "*wat erger is*" (what is worse), by thirteen Ministers of Koloniën (Frasen van de Putte, Baron van Gollstein, van Bosse, van Rees, Baron van Gollstein, de Brauw, van Erp Taalman Kip, van Bloemen Waanders, Weitzel, Sprenger van Eijk, Keuchenius, Baron Mackay, Baron van Dedem, "*hieronder 2 ad interim*" — among them 2 ad interim). The catalogue is Van Heutsz's warrant for Van Teijn's warning: the structural defect the General had identified was not an impression but a countable fact.

## The letter's failure: "Het heeft niet gebaat"

The warning of [[23 November]] [[1891]] did not, in Van Heutsz's reading, succeed. The General returned to the same theme, the following year, in his *eigenhandig gesteld schrijven aan de Regeering* (autograph letter to the Government) — an autograph letter to the Regeering — which Van Heutsz adduces in the closing half of the tract:

> "betoogde de Generaal, naar hij mij het vorige jaar uit Holland schreef, in een eigenhandig gesteld schrijven aan de Regeering de noodzakelijkheid van het dwingen van den Gouverneur van Atjeh door eene bindende instructie tot het volgen van de vastgestelde politiek. Het heeft niet gebaat".

The *laatste* (last) attempt to bind the Governor to a fixed policy — "*Het heeft niet gebaat*" (It did not avail) — is the verdict Van Heutsz renders on his own opening exhibit. The letter of [[23 November]] [[1891]] is, in his narrative, the document that proves the colony had been warned, by the man best placed to warn, at the moment when the warning could still have been acted on; and the document whose dismissal is the warrant for the tract itself.

## Van Heutsz's use of the letter as the book's opening

Van Heutsz's choice of the [[23 November]] [[1891]] letter as the opening exhibit of the [[1893]] tract is a structural decision of the first order. The book's argument — that the Atjeh-oorlog had become chronic because of *telkens veranderen van politiek* — is, in Van Heutsz's framing, not his own thesis but Van Teijn's; and the book's prescription — that the Regeering must bind the Governor to a fixed policy through a *bindende instructie* — is Van Heutsz's development of the General's own. The letter thus functions in the tract as both the diagnosis (the colony's defect is *telkens veranderen van politiek*) and the prescription (the Regeering must bind the Governor through an *instructie*), with the rest of the book — the governor-by-governor survey of [[1873]]–[[1892]], the rejection of the *sultanaat-restoration* (sultanate-restoration) policy, and the systematic exposition of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] (shipping regulation) — providing the historical and operational warrant for what Van Teijn had already, in November [[1891]], named. The book's rhetorical force depends on the reader accepting that the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] had been *knaagt aan ons Koloniaal bezit* (gnawing at our Colonial possession) — not because the right policy had not been tried, but because every right policy had been tried and then abandoned.

## Significance

Van Teijn's warning letter of [[23 November]] [[1891]] is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the document on which the entire [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* is built. Its significance in the Aceh Wiki History record lies in four things. First, it is the rhetorical starting point of Van Heutsz's book — the document reproduced *in extenso* on the second page of the tract, the exhibit from which the diagnosis of *telkens veranderen van politiek* is drawn, and the warrant for the catalogue of eleven Governors, five Governors-General and thirteen Ministers of Koloniën that follows. Second, it is the post-retirement intervention of the man Van Heutsz treats as his preferred predecessor — the General under whom the *sluiting-stelsel* (closure-system) had been introduced and under whom, by [[1888]], the colony had begun to make measurable progress — and the warning that "*niet gebaat heeft*": that did not avail, because the Regeering did not act on it. Third, its prescription — the *bindende instructie* by which the Regeering would compel the Governor to follow its fixed policy — is the constitutional complement to the operational prescription of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] that Van Heutsz develops in the closing half of the book: the *sluiting* (closure) that the General had imposed, the *scheepvaartregeling* that Van Heutsz would inherit from [[Scherer]] and [[Laging Tobias]], would only work if the Regeering stopped the cycle of *bestuursverandering*. Fourth, the letter's reference to *de sluiting* — the closure that the successor was dismantling — is the document's political sting: the warning is not abstract but specific, directed against a named *tegenwoordige Gouverneur* (present Governor) whose *veranderingen van politiek* were reversing the very policy whose *goede resultaten* (good results) had begun to be felt.

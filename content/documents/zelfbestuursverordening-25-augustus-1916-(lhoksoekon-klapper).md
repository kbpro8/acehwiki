**The Zelfbestuursverordening of [[25 August]] [[1916]] (Lhoksoekon klapper)** — printed in the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* as Regulation No. 18a — is the *klappercultuur*-verordening (coconut-cultivation ordinance) of the *onderafdeeling* (subdivision) [[Lho Soekon|Lhoksoekon]] in the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh]] (district North Coast of Atjeh), issued jointly by the three *oeleebalang* (territorial chiefs) of the *onderafdeeling* — Keureutoë, Matang Koeli, and Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë — for the protection of coconut and other palm plantations against caterpillar and insect pests (*rupsen- of insectenplagen*). Approved by the *Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies) on the same date, it is accompanied by a same-day *Zelfbestuursbesluit* (Reg. No. 18b) — the *mantri-instructie* (petty-official instruction) for the *klappercultuur* — which prescribes the daily *journaal*-keeping (journal-keeping) and *toezicht*-duties (supervision-duties) of the *klapper-mantri's* (coconut-*mantri's* [petty officials]) charged with the regulation's enforcement. Reg. 18a is alternatively cited in its own *Fatsal 7* (Article 7) as the "*Klapperverordening*" (Coconut Ordinance).

## The insect-pest protection rationale

The regulation opens, in the bilingual Malay-Dutch columnar form standard to the compilation, with a title (heavily garbled in the OCR) reconstructible as:

> "ZELFEESTUUUSVVRRORDENGG van 25 Augustus 1916 tot bescher- ming van klupperamplaningg tegen rupsen- of insec- tenplang in de zelfbesturende landschappen van de enderafdeeling Lhoksoekon (goedgekeurd door den Gon- verneur van Atjeh en Onderorrighedn)"

The protected species extend beyond the coconut palm (*klapper* / *kelapa*) to four other palm-species that share the same insect-pest vulnerability: *nibong* (*nibeeng*), *areng* (*aren*), *rumbia*, and *nipah*. The Malay column renders the protected species as "*pohon kelapa, nibeeng, aren, roembia atau pohon nipah*" and the Dutch column as "*klapper-, nibong-, areng-, rumbia- of nipahpalmen*." The recital announces the protective purpose in terms of an explicit sanitary regime:

*Fatsal 1 / Artikel 1* requires every owner, tenant, or user of land on which a coconut, nibong, areng, rumbia, or nipah palm has died or is dying, to cut and burn the dead or dying palm — together with its roots — or to bury it three feet deep, or to submerge it in water. All refuse from the sanitary felling must be burned; or, if used as fertilizer, the heaps must be turned monthly to kill larvae. *Fatsal 2 / Artikel 2* requires gatherers of rumbia- or nipah-leaves to notify the *Zelfbestuurder* of their work-sites and to burn their refuse daily. The sanitary regime is the regulation's distinctive feature: the three-foot-deep burial/submersion requirement and the monthly turning of fertilizer-heaps together constitute a systematic attempt to break the life-cycle of the palm-borer and allied pests.

## The accompanying mantri-instructie (Reg. No. 18b)

The accompanying *Zelfbestuursbesluit van [[25 August]] [[1916]]* (Reg. No. 18b) is the *mantri-instructie* for the *klappercultuur*, issued by the same three *oeleebalang* on the same day and explicitly cross-referenced to Reg. 18a's *Fatsal 3*. Its recital converts the date [[25 August]] [[1916]] to the Hijri calendar — the only Hijri date in the compilation:

> "Overwegende dat het gewenseht is ten behoeve van de in artikel 3 van de Klapperverordening van den 25 Augustus 1916 of den 25 Sjawal van het Mohanmedaunsehe jaar, genoemde mantri's voor de klap- percultuur een instrnctie vast te stellen ter omschrijving van hun taak."

— "Whereas it is desirable, for the benefit of the *mantri's* named in Article 3 of the *Klapperverordening* of [[25 August]] [[1916]], or the 25th of Sjawal of the Mohammedan year, to establish an *instructie* (instruction) defining their task." The Hijri conversion — [[25 August]] [[1916]] = 25 Sjawal 1334 AH — is correctly computed (25 Sjawal 1334 AH does indeed fall on 25 August 1916 CE). The *instructie* (Fatsal 1–9) charges the *klapper-mantri's* with *toezicht* (supervision) on the *klappercultuur*; requires them to keep abreast of the *cultuur*'s state, dangers, and protective measures; requires them to maintain a register of regulated *aanplantingen* (plantings) and to spread knowledge of cultivation among the *bevolking* (population); subordinates them to the *plaatselijk besturend ambtenaar* (local administering official); requires a daily *journaal* (submitted every ten days); requires reporting of *overtredingen* (violations) to the *Zelfbestuurder*; requires immediate reporting of unknown plant-diseases; requires inventorying of government-issued equipment; and governs handover on transfer or dismissal.

## Role of the klappercultuur-mantri

The *klappercultuur-mantri* — alternatively the *mantri kelapa* or *mantri's voor de klappercultuur* — is the specialised indigenous auxiliary official charged with the regulation's enforcement. *Fatsal 3 / Artikel 3* of Reg. 18a empowers the *politie* (police) and the *klapper-mantri's* to inspect *erven* (plots), *tuinen* (gardens), *plantages* (plantations), and *sawahs* (rice-fields) between sunrise and sunset:

> "Ambtenaar dan pegawai politie demikinn djoega menteri jang telah ditentockan boeat pertanaman ke- lapa merekaitoe berdjaga moelai matahari terbit sampui matalari masock akan mengedari dan mema- soeki kehoen dan sawah serta tem- pat oring mengambil daoen roembin dan nipah"

The *instructie*'s *Keterangan / Noot* (Note) on p. 132 of the compilation enumerates the six required categories of *journaal*-entry — a rare detailed specification of bureaucratic practice in the compilation: (1) the *tournees* (tours of inspection) made; (2) the names of *overtreders* (violators) and the locations of their *aanplantingen*; (3) the places of temporary meetings and *besprekingen* (discussions); (4) the results of personal *toezicht* and the measures taken against palm-diseases; (5) the number of dead or dying trees removed; and (6) the *klomp- en drijfjachten* (beating- and driving-hunts) on harmful *gedierte* (animal-pests) and their results. *Fatsal 4 / Artikel 4* of Reg. 18a sets the penalty for any violation at a maximum of one hundred guilders or thirty days' *arbeidstelling* (public-works labour), with restoration at the offender's cost; *Fatsal 5 / Artikel 5* requires prior written warning before prosecution, with a defined remedial term; *Fatsal 6 / Artikel 6* allows the *Zelfbestuurder* to act without warning if the offender is unknown or unreachable; *Fatsal 7 / Artikel 7* sets the *inwerkingtreding* (entry into force) on the day after public announcement.

## Significance

The *Zelfbestuursverordening van [[25 August]] [[1916]]* is the most detailed agricultural verordening of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement]] in respect of a defined tree-crop. Its comparative table of *GELIJKE VERORDENINGEN* (Parallel Ordinances) on p. 129 extends parallel regelingen (regulations) to Padangtidji (3-6-1916), Lammeulo (3-6-1916), Sigli (3-6-1916), Meureudoe (1916), Idi (1916), Tamiang/Tamlèng (12-9-1915 — the earliest parallel, predating the Lhoksoekon origin by almost a year), Langsa (12-9-1916), Takingeun (12 September 1916), Serbeudjadi (1-9-1916), Gajo LoeBus (11 September 1916), Alaslanden (21-11-1916), Tjalang (25 September 1916), Meulaboh (18 March 1916), Tapaktoean (March–December 1916), and the *Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen* (March–December 1916 and 1-7-1916) — fifteen *onderafdeelingen* in all, making the comparative table one of the most extensive in the compilation. The dual issuing mechanism — Reg. 18a as the *verordening* by the *oeleebalang*, Reg. 18b as the *mantri-instructie* by the same *oeleebalang* on the same day — illustrates the typical division of competence within the *Zelfbestuur* itself: the *verordening* fixes the substantive obligations on the *anak negeri* (subjects); the *instructie* fixes the operational duties of the *mantri* charged with the *verordening*'s enforcement. The regulation's distinctive sanitary regime — mandatory felling, three-foot-deep burial, monthly turning of fertilizer-heaps, daily refuse-burning by leaf-gatherers — reflects the late-colonial state's growing awareness, in the 1910s, of the insect-pest threat to the palm-economies of the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|Noordkust]] (North Coast) and the adjacent *afdeelingen* (districts).

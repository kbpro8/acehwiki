**The 1e Bataljon barisan van Bangkallang** (1st barisan battalion of Bangkallang — Bangkalan, in western [[Madura]]) was a battalion of the *[[Barisans|barisan]]*, the native auxiliary troops of the Netherlands Indies Army, whose men served in [[Atjeh]] during the period December [[1875]] – September [[1876]]. Part of its cadre was European; its soldiers were [[Madura|Madurese]], described by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] as the least drilled and disciplined of the infantry on the terrain of war.

## Service in 1876

In the [[Voigt expedition to the IV Moekims (1-3 June 1876)|Voigt expedition of 1–3 June 1876]] into the [[IV Moekims]] the battalion supplied the mass of the column: 200 Madurese non-commissioned officers and men under 8 Madurese officers with 5 European officers — the troops of whom Major [[Majoor Voigt|Voigt]], offered a night march by his guide, declined because of their limited steadiness in the dark, a decision Meijer expressly praised.

It was also a Madurese second lieutenant of this battalion, [[Raden Ario Setjo Negoro]], who commanded the escort of the medical officer [[Dr. Döring (medical officer 2nd class)|Dr. Döring]] in the [[Ambush between Kota Alam and Oleh Karang (28 July 1876)|ambush of 28 July 1876]]: his company — 1 Madurese sergeant, 1 corporal, 1 bugler and 13 fusiliers of the barisan with European and native infantry — was the greater part of the detachment that stood the enemy charge, rallied at the doctor's call and joined in the counter-charge. The same battalion thus supplied Meijer with both his stock example of the barisan's limitations and the names of those who made his exceptions.

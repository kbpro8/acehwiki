**The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum** — the Commercial Museum Department — was the department of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] charged with the evaluation and promotion of colonial commodities. The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum maintained a *Scheikundig Laboratorium* (Chemical Laboratory) in which the chemical composition and properties of colonial products were investigated, and it published the results of its investigations in the *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum van de Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut*. The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum is the institutional author of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] and the institutional home of the chemical and commercial evaluation of Atjeh-turpentine.

## The Scheikundig Laboratorium


The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the chemical investigation of the Atjeh-turpentine samples was undertaken *in het Scheikundig Laboratorium van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum* — *in the Chemical Laboratory of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum*. The Laboratory also collaborated with the *Rijksbureau voor Onderzoek van Handelswaren* (National Bureau for the Investigation of Commercial Goods) at [[s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]], which conducted the *uitvoerig* (extensive) investigation of the first sample received (*H.M. no. 1504*). The results of the chemical investigations are preserved in Tables III, IV, V and VI of the pamphlet — the principal published scientific record of the chemical composition of Atjeh-turpentine.

## The H.M. monster numbering


The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum maintained a system of *H.M. monster* (H.M. sample) numbering — by which individual samples of colonial commodities were catalogued and referenced. The three Atjeh-turpentine samples evaluated in the 1930 pamphlet were:

- *H.M. no. 1504* — received in December [[1929]] directly from the [[Baleq]] enterprise.
- *H.M. no. 1553* — drawn from a January [[1930]] Amsterdam shipment by the [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij]].
- *H.M. no. 1607* — an original tin from the April [[1930]] shipment per m.s. *Poelau Roebiah*.

The H.M. monster numbering system is the principal cataloguing system of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum, and the three Atjeh-turpentine samples are the principal physical objects on which the [[1930]] evaluation rests.

## The Berichten series


The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum published the results of its investigations in the *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum van de Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut* — the series of which the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] is No. 57. The Berichten were extracted from *De Indische Mercuur* and published separately by J. H. de Bussy of Amsterdam. The list of Berichten preserved at the back of the pamphlet records the breadth of the series — from Java-jute to Atjeh-turpentine, from gambir to cajeput oil — and identifies the principal authors: [[L. P. de Bussy|de Bussy]], [[W. Spoon|Spoon]], [[D. B. Spoelstra|Spoelstra]], [[M. A. van Royen|van Royen]], A. Reclaire, J. Temminck Groll, E. C. Jul. Mohr, A. W. K. de Jong, and others.

## The Director and staff


The Director of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum in [[1930]] was [[L. P. de Bussy]] — the signatory of the [[Circulaire of 5 May 1930 to the V.V.V.F.|circular letter of 5 May 1930]]. The chemical investigations were undertaken by [[D. B. Spoelstra|Dr. D. B. Spoelstra]] (fractional distillation), [[M. A. van Royen|Mej. M. A. van Royen]] (testing against the draft *Ontwerp-normaalblad V 597*), and — at the *Rijksbureau voor Onderzoek van Handelswaren* — by the staff of that Bureau. The principal author of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] was [[W. Spoon|Ir. W. Spoon]] of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum.

## Significance


The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the institutional author of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn evaluation of 1930]] — the principal contemporary monograph on the early factory-scale production of Indonesian turpentine. The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum's *Scheikundig Laboratorium*, *H.M. monster* numbering, and *Berichten* series are the principal documentary instruments by which the evaluation and promotion of Atjeh-turpentine was undertaken, and the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum's collaboration with the *Rijksbureau voor Onderzoek van Handelswaren* and the [[Vereeniging van Vernis- en Verffabrikanten en -Handelaren in Nederland]] illustrates the network of institutions through which colonial commodities were evaluated in the late [[1920s]] and early [[1930s]].

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum** — *the Trades Museum Division* — was the division of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] in Amsterdam responsible for the *Berichten* (Reports) series, of which [[Ir. W. Spoon|Ir. W. Spoon]]'s *[[Atjeh Patchouli-Olie (1932)|Atjeh Patchouli-Olie]]* is No. 71. The Handelsmuseum combined the functions of a commercial museum — collecting and displaying samples of Netherlands-Indies products — with those of a research laboratory, where the chemical constants of those products were determined and a market judgement was obtained from European buyers.

### The Method


The method of the Handelsmuseum is illustrated by Spoon's *Atjeh Patchouli-Olie*: six sample oils were received from Atjeh in 1931 and early 1932; they were analysed in the laboratory for *soortelijk gewicht* (specific gravity), *brekingsindex* (refractive index), *draaiing* (optical rotation), *zuurgetal* (acid value), *estergetal* (ester value) and solubility in 90 vol.% alcohol; and they were then sent to perfume manufacturers in the Netherlands, Germany, England and France for a market judgement. The combination of laboratory analysis and perfumer's bench is the model of the *Berichten* series' practical empiricism.

### Significance


The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum is the principal institutional vehicle for the commercial research of the Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut, and its *Berichten* series is the principal printed source for the commercial chemistry of the Netherlands-Indies in the inter-war period. Its role in the development of the [[Patchouli-olie industry in Atjeh|patchouli-oil industry in Atjeh]] is the type-case of its method.

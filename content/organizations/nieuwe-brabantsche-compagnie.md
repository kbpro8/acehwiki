**The Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie** — the **New Brabant Company**, one of the *voorcompagnieën* (pre-VOC companies) of the United Provinces in the period [[1595]]-[[1602]] — was the Brabant-based East India company whose fleet under the admiralship of [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]] (with [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]] as vice-admiraal) left Texel on [[21 December]] [[1599]] and from which Van Caerden conducted the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|second Dutch commercial visit to Atjeh]] in [[1600]]-[[1601]]. The Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie is the principal Brabant voorcompagnie of Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The 1599 fleet under Pieter Both

The Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie's fleet under [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]] — with [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]] as vice-admiraal — left Texel on [[21 December]] [[1599]]. The fleet was bound for the East Indies by way of the west coast of Sumatra; Both dispatched Van Caerden to [[Priaman|Priaman]], [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] and [[Pasaman|Pasaman]] — "zonder resultaat" — and thence to [[Acheh|Atjeh]], where Van Caerden arrived on [[21 September]] [[1600]]. Both himself proceeded to [[Bantam|Bantam]] and the eastern archipelago. The fleet's mission was a commercial one — pepper and spices — but it was conducted in the shadow of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman ambush of September 1599]] and the approaching truce with Spain.

## The Van Caerden visit (1600-1601)

The full account of the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|Van Caerden visit]] is given in the entry on the visit itself. In summary: Van Caerden obtained the release of the surviving [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman captives]] including [[Hans Decker|Hans Decker]]; signed a pepper contract with the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]] on [[28 December]] [[1600]]; received the escaped [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] on [[30 December]] [[1600]]; seized pepper from Arab junks at anchor on [[12 January]] [[1601]] after the sultan's sabander rearrested Houtman; was fired on by the shore batteries; burned several coastal craft; released his prisoners; and sailed for [[Bantam|Bantam]] by way of the west coast of Sumatra, taking on no cargo.

## The absorption into the VOC (1602)

With the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the VOC on 20 March 1602]], the Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie was wound up and its assets and personnel absorbed into the united company. [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]] would go on to become the [[Pieter Both|first Governor-General of the VOC]] in [[1609]]; [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]] would continue in VOC service until his capture by the Spanish in the Moluccas in [[1607]]. The Brabant commercial interest in the East India trade — represented by the Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie — was thus absorbed into the unified Dutch East India enterprise.

## Significance

The Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Brabant voorcompagnie of the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship and the principal instance of the Brabant commercial interest in the East India trade before the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the VOC]]. The [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|Van Caerden visit of 1600-1601]] — conducted by the company's vice-admiraal — produced the first formal Dutch-Achehnese pepper contract, the release of the surviving Houtman captives, and the first armed clash between the Dutch and the Atjeh shore batteries. The company's absorption into the VOC in [[1602]] marked the institutional end of the pre-VOC period and the beginning of the unified Dutch-Indies trade. The Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie's role in the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship also frames the Brabant commercial interest as a constituent of the broader Dutch-Indies trade — a position that the unified VOC would inherit.

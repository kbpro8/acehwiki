**The Korps Marechaussée** — also written in the source as *Maréchaussée* and referred to simply as *marechaussée* — was the elite Dutch colonial paramilitary police corps in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]], largely manned by Ambonese and Javanese indigenous ranks under European officers, originally raised to police the [[geconcentreerde Linie (Concentrated Line)|geconcentreerde linie]] and its outlying posts but, by the spring of [[1896]], organized into operational *brigades* that served as the spearhead of nearly every Dutch column against [[Teuku Umar]] and the hostile *moekims*. Commanded variously by 1e Luit. H. M. Vis, 1e Luit. G. K. Dijkstra, and most prominently Kapt. Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland (Kapitein), the corps talso part in the relief of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[8 April]] [[1896]], the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Battle of Senelop]] and its recapture on [[2 May]] [[1896]], the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive against Umar's stelling]], and the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|Lam-Krak tuchtiging]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]]. Its Ambonese troopers — including the named Simin (No. 8502), Lontoh (No. 33874), Kaijadu (No. 16066), and Sapahaluh (No. 37620) — were repeatedly the first to enter enemy *versterkingen* and suffered the brunt of Dutch casualties in storm assaults.

## Background and Formation

The *Korps Marechaussée* was raised in the East Indies as a colonial gendarmerie distinct from the line infantry of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|colonial army]], recruiting predominantly from the Ambonese Christian population of the Moluccas and, to a lesser extent, from Javanese volunteers, with European NCOs and officers providing the cadres. Each operational *brigade* numbered roughly twenty-five rank-and-file under a European sergeant or brigadier, with several brigades typically grouped under a single lieutenant or captain for column work. In the Atjeh theatre, where the difficult terrain of *sawahs*, *kreupelbosch*, and fortified kampongs made the mass deployment of European infantry slow and costly, the marechaussée filled the operational niche of light infantry, mounted police, and ambush troops simultaneously. By [[January]] [[1896]] the corps was already responsible for manning outlying *blokhuizen* such as the [[Siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe (28–29 March 1896)|blokhuis Aloer Oe]] — where one brigade under European sergeant of infantry Havenga (No. 19908), with the artillery sergeant son (No. 40248) and three European gunners, successfully defended the post through the night of [[28 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] — and for policing the rail-and-road line between [[Koeta Radja]], [[Lam-Djamoe]], [[Lampeneroet]], and [[Ketapan Doea]].

## Role in the Atjeh War

The Korps Marechaussée was first into nearly every significant engagement of the spring of [[1896]]. On [[30 March]] [[1896]] four brigades under 1e Luit. W. K. J. Stoop formed the *voorhoede* of Maj. G. P. M. van der Noordaa's column to relieve [[Senelop]] and evacuate the *blokhuizen* [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]], [[Aloer Oe]], and Toei Selimbing; the same day the brigade of 1e Luit. J. W. C. Vuijkk suffered that officer's death at Lam Siti in the fatal friendly-fire mistake that ended Kruisheer's first month of combat narrative. Eight brigades under Kapt. Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland (Kapitein) formed the *voorhoede* of [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]]'s column at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop on 17 April]], and six brigades under the same captain gave the signal for the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|storm of Senelop on 2 May]] — when the Inl. marechaussée Simin (No. 8502) was first through the gate at 9:10 AM, only to find "*the birds flown*" (the birds flown).

On [[23 May]] [[1896]] ten brigades under Kapt. Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland (Kapitein) formed the *voorhoede* of the Bergcolonne in the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Battle of Tjot Tjako]], where 1e Luit. H. M. Vis and Luit. Wagener, with the indigenous marechaussée Wongsodikromo (No. 33780) and Todikromo (No. 28097), were first on the summit at 5:43 VM. Two days later the brigades against Lam Tjroeng/Lam Poelau burned Anak-Paja. In the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|Lam-Krak tuchtiging]] the corps fielded twelve brigades (4 officieren, 12 europ. onderofficieren, 207 minderen), and on [[16 June]]–[[17 June]] [[1896]] it spearheaded the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|V Moekims Montassik]] operation under Graafland's continued command.

## Composition and Strength

Marechaussée strength fluctuated with operational demand. The Bergcolonne fielded 4 officieren and 189 minderen on [[23 May]] [[1896]]; the Lam-Krak expedition of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] committed 12 brigades totalling 4 officieren, 12 europ. onderofficieren, and 207 minderen; by [[16 June]] [[1896]] the corps in Atjeh stood at 4 officieren and 226 minderen. Casualties were correspondingly heavy: in the [[30 March]] [[1896]] action at Lam Siti alone, Amb. maréchaussée Sapahaluh (No. 37620) was killed in the stormloop on [[Bralon]] (his body never recovered), and Sgt. Gehne (No. 24835), Amb. Sgt. Tumbuan, two Ambonese, and three Javanese maréchaussée were wounded. The wounded Amb. maréchaussée Lontoh (No. 33874) was decorated for distinguishing himself at Lam Siti despite his wound, and Amb. Sgt. Lelengbato (No. 20701) and Amb. Cpl. Moningka (No. 9228) earned similar distinction.

## Significance

The Korps Marechaussée was the most cost-effective force the Dutch deployed in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] in [[1896]]. Its Ambonese and Javanese ranks, fighting as individuals and small brigades rather than as a formed battalion, could traverse *sawah*, *kreupelbosch*, and fortified kampong in a way European line infantry could not, and its European sergeant-brigadiers accumulated the kind of terrain knowledge that Kruisheer explicitly praised in Eur. sergt. de Fouw (No. 24755), "door zijn groote terreinkennis en beleidvol voorgaan" gravely wounded at [[Lam Hasan]] on [[21 April]]. The corps's reliance on indigenous ranks also exposed the contradiction of Dutch colonial war-fighting: the same Ambonese troopers who policed the *geconcentreerde linie* were the first to storm Acehnese *bentengs*, while the Dutch policy of arming Acehnese allies — embodied in the *Legioen van T. Djohan* — collapsed entirely on [[29 March]] [[1896]] when Umar defected with Dutch weapons.

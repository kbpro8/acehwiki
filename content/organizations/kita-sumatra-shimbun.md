**Kita Sumatra Shimbun** — the "North Sumatra Newspaper," a Malay-language daily issued in [[Medan|Medan]] during the Japanese occupation of Sumatra ([[1942]]–[[1945]]) — was the principal vehicle through which the Japanese military administration circulated official news, propaganda, and visual imagery across the residency of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]] and the wider East Coast of Sumatra. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* cites the newspaper exclusively as the source of four pen-and-ink drawings by the Japanese artist [[Matsushita Kikuo|Matsushita Kikuo]] — three of them executed during a journey through Atjèh's West Coast in [[1944]] — which Piekaar reproduced as the only illustrations in his [[1949]] English edition.

## The drawings of Matsushita Kikuo

In his preface, Piekaar explains that "it was necessary to be content with the inclusion of four drawings by the Japanese [[Matsushita Kikuo|Matsushita Kikuo]], published in the Malay-language newspaper, the 'Kita Sumatra Shimbun', issued in [[Medan|Medan]]. Three of these drawings were made by him during a journey through Aceh and depict scenes from Aceh's West Coast. The captions were replaced by more appropriate ones, without, of course, doing violence to the content of the depictions." The drawings, listed in the original edition "as the corresponding list of illustrations" but not reproduced in the English edition's source scans, form the only visual record of the Atjèhnese occupation in Piekaar's book. The artist — "Matsushita, Kikuo (Matusita, Kikuo)" in the personal-name index — also produced drawings reproduced in the [[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]] of [[19 December]] [[1942]] ("Koeala pasi di Atjèh barat", an anchorage on the West Coast).

## Captions and scenes from Atjèh's West Coast

Each of the four Matsushita drawings cited by Piekaar carries a numbered caption from the *Kita Sumatra Shimbun*:

- "*Pangoelèë hareukat meugöë*, the means of livelihood is above all agriculture" (drawing in the *Kita Sumatora Shimbun* of [[2 August]] [[1944]], no. 176), illustrating the gampóng economy of the interior.
- "*Eungkot ni'mat Allah tjit*, the fish is also a gift from God" (drawing in the *Kita Sumatora Shimbun* of [[2 December]] [[1944]], no. 277), illustrating the coastal fishing economy of [[Pidië|Pidië]] and the West Coast.
- The West-Coast anchorage drawing of [[5 December]] [[1944]] (no. 279), originally published in the [[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]] of [[19 December]] [[1942]] no. 53 and reprinted in the *Kita Sumatora Shimbun*.
- "*Keureudja soeka-réla*" on the road connection [[Roendéng|Roendéng]]–[[Sidikalang|Sidikalang]] (drawing in the *Kita Sumatora Shimbun* of [[8 December]] [[1944]], no. 282), illustrating the *kinró* (forced labour) mobilisation of the occupation's last years.

## Role in the occupation media

The *Kita Sumatra Shimbun* complemented the [[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]] as the regional propaganda vehicle for the Japanese military administration of Sumatra. While the *Atjèh Shimbun* was the residency-level paper, published in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] under the Resident's Information Service and concerned chiefly with Atjèhnese affairs, the *Kita Sumatra Shimbun* served the wider North Sumatra command area from [[Medan|Medan]]. Both were Malay-language — the language the Japanese had chosen, in line with their "*Indonesia Merdéka*" policy, as the medium of administration and propaganda throughout the former [[Netherlands Indies|Netherlands Indies]] — and both carried the official notices, *gun-chó* proclamations, and *senden-han* propaganda of the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] and its sister *kikan* in Medan. Piekaar's reliance on the *Kita Sumatra Shimbun* for the Matsushita drawings — and the artist's own journey through Atjèh in [[1944]] — suggests that the newspaper was also a vehicle for ethnographic reportage on the occupied territories, a kind of visual companion to the textual propaganda of the *senden-han*.

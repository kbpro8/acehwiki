**Poesa** — the *Persatoean Oelama-Oelama Seleroeh Atjèh*, or All-Aceh Union of Ulemas — was the religious-political association founded in [[1939]] in [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] under the high patronage of [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]], self-governing authority of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]], which became, in Piekaar's words, "for the first time in the history of Aceh" the form in which "the religious life and striving of the Acehnese found its own form." Conceived as a counterweight to the modernist [[Moehammadijah|Moehammadijah]] currents that were "foreign to Acehnese religious life," Poesa united the conservative *oelama*'s with the more militant youth of the gampóng, and within three years of its foundation it had built up a following "of an extent unknown to Aceh throughout the entire residency" — providing the [[Fujiwara-kikan|Fujiwara-kikan]] with one of its most effective channels into Atjèhnese society when the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of Atjèh]] of [[12 March]] [[1942]] came.

## Foundation and purpose

The Poesa "or P(ersatoean) Oe(lama-oelama) S(eloeroeh) A(tjèh) was founded in [[1939]] in [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] under the high patronage of [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]], self-governing authority of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]]." It was "intended as a counterweight to the modern currents of the [[Moehammadijah|Moehammadijah]] movement" and "should, under the leadership of Aceh's most prominent *oelama*'s, become the bearer of the typically Acehnese, fanatically orthodox religious life." The ambition was to revive Aceh's old reputation as "*sramoë Meukah*" — the "forecourt of [[Mecca|Mecca]]." Membership was not limited to the *oelama*'s; "anyone who could identify with the objectives could join the association," and "a political slant, let alone an anti-Dutch tendency, seemed foreign to this plan."

## The youth wing and the scout movement

The movement rapidly acquired a militant edge through its youth organisation, the *Pemoeda Poesa*, to which a scout body called *Kasjafatoel Islam* was attached. "Soon one then saw uniformed Poesa youths everywhere in Atjèh," notably at the installation of [[Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali|Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali]] as honorary chairman of the Twenty-Two Moekims in [[1940]]. This youthful militancy lay, in Piekaar's reading, "the germ of the resistance action" — the youth wing would prove to be the bridge between Poesa's religious agenda and the armed insurgency of [[1942]].

## The estrangement from the oelèëbalangs

"What finally caused both the conservative *oelama* and the progressive, younger generation, having outgrown *adat* authority, to form a united front against the adat legal authority of the *oelèëbalang*" was a deepening conflict over religious jurisdiction, marriage regulations, and the administration of *hoeköm*. "Also in the relationship between the Poesa and the loyal *oelèëbalangs*, who possessed somewhat deeper insight, a gradual cooling developed." The movement's anti-Dutch evolution was sharpened by figures such as [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]] of the [[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]], who "sought rapprochement with the Poesa, whose anti-Dutch development" he guided, and who would later be removed from the editorship under Japanese pressure.

## The Japanese occupation

Under the Japanese, Poesa was institutionalised. Its leaders sat on the central board of the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] — [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]] and [[Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi|Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi]] served as deputy chairmen — and Poesa-affiliated *oelama*'s were systematically placed on the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]]. Yet the relationship was always instrumental: the Resident "[[lino|lino]]" noted in his installation speech for the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] on [[10 March]] [[1943]] that the *oelama*'s must "advise the people 'not to interfere in matters concerning the administration of the country.'" When Poesa was finally absorbed, along with [[PETA|PETA]] and the *Kapal Oedara Atjèh*, into the mass body [[Permai|Permai]] on [[20 January]] [[1945]], its apparatus — by then a force of more than 5,000 armed Poesa-inspired youths in the *volksleger* — survived as the political-military instrument that would destroy *oelèëbalang* authority in the [[1945|1945]] "social revolution."

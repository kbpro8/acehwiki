**The [[Corps Maréchaussée]]** was a military police force established in [[Atjeh]] in [[April 1890]]. Its formation was recognised as a symptom of changed insight into the long-criticised problem of the [[Aceh War]]—a shift away from the passive and negotiation-oriented policies of the previous years toward a more active military approach.

## Establishment and purpose

The establishment of the [[Corps Maréchaussée]] in [[April 1890]] coincided with growing recognition—both in [[Atjeh]] and in the Netherlands—that the existing strategies (blockades, shipping regulations, alliance policies, and the [[Concentrated Line]]) had failed to produce lasting results. The formation of a specialised military police unit signalled that the Dutch government was preparing for more sustained and coordinated operations.

In the strategic analysis of [[Dr. J. M. Somer]], the [[Corps Maréchaussée]] represents an important transition: the beginning of the shift from the failed policies of [[1873]]–[[1890]] to the active subjugation policy that would be fully implemented after [[1896]] and particularly after [[1898]].

## Relationship to broader policy changes

Although the [[Corps Maréchaussée]] did not immediately resolve the strategic confusion—since [[Deykerhoff]]'s alliance policy and the [[1896]] defection of [[T. Oema]] still lay ahead—its establishment demonstrated that the Dutch authorities recognised the inadequacy of previous approaches. The force provided the operational capacity that would later be fully utilised under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] during the [[Pidië Expedition]] and the campaign against [[Panglima Polim]].

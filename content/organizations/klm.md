The **K.L.M.** (Koninklijke Luchtvaart Maatschappij — Royal Dutch Airlines), founded in [[1919]], is the national airline of the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] and the oldest operating airline in the world still operating under its original name. The K.L.M. was the principal beneficiary of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Generaal Snijders]]' post-war aviation advocacy: Snijders negotiated on its behalf with the Indies government in [[1920]] (his [[Snijders' 1920 visit to Atjeh|first Indies journey]]), presided over the *Comité "Vliegtocht Nederland–Indië"* that prepared the first Amsterdam–Batavia flight of [[1924]], and was a tireless propagandist in the press for the regular Amsterdam–Batavia service, which was finally inaugurated as a fortnightly service in [[1930]] and grew to a thrice-weekly service by [[1937]].

## The K.L.M. and the Indies Air Link

In [[1920]] the Raad van Bestuur of the K.L.M. asked Snijders — then in his capacity as chairman of the Vereeniging "Ons Leger" preparing for his [[Snijders' 1920 visit to Atjeh|first Indies journey]] — to take up on behalf of the Maatschappij the question of an Indies sub-section of the K.L.M. with the Indies authorities and with prominent persons in trade, industry, and communications. Although Snijders stood in no official relation to the Maatschappij, he agreed. On [[8 June]] [[8 June]] [[1920]] he submitted, on behalf of the K.L.M., a request to the Indies government for priority of concession and for the appointment of an organ with which the relevant negotiations could be conducted; on [[3 August]] [[3 August]] [[1920]] a draft agreement prepared by him was the subject of a meeting of the commission appointed for the purpose. The negotiations were favourable, and led to a draft *besluit* granting the K.L.M. permission to operate civil air traffic in Nederlandsch-Indië.

The high expectations were not immediately fulfilled. The Minister of Colonies [[De Graaff|De Graaff]], to whom the Governor-General forwarded Snijders' draft concession, doubted the viability and feasibility of the plans and judged it irresponsible, in the form of subsidies, to commit funds from the treasury. The plans had to be set aside; six years passed before the question was settled with the founding of the [[K.N.I.L.M.|K.N.I.L.M.]] in [[1928]].

## The Amsterdam–Batavia Service

Snijders continued, through the 1920s, to advocate the air link in word and writing. He presided over the Comité "Vliegtocht Nederland–Indië" that prepared the first Amsterdam–Batavia flight of [[1924]] (Van der Hop, Van Weerden Poelman, and Van den Broecke) and Koppen's post-flight of [[1 October]] [[1 October]] [[1927]] with the *Postduif*. The fortnightly Amsterdam–Batavia service was finally inaugurated in [[1930]] and grew to a thrice-weekly service by [[1937]]; Snijders' advocacy was a central factor in bringing it about.

**Insulinde** was a Dutch colonial periodical in which [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]], then an assistant-resident, published a review of [[H. van Kol]]'s brochure *[[Land en Volk van Java]]* criticising its estimate of the cost of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The criticism

Van Kol reported the exchange to the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]]. Having stated in his brochure that the extra expenditure caused by the war amounted to three hundred and fifty million guilders over twenty-five years, "doch in eene kritiek in Insulinde merkte de heer van Heutz, Assistent-Resident, op, dat men alle extra uitgaven van het Civiel Bestuur, verlofsgelden en recherchevaartuigen, reis- en verblijfkosten en een deel der pensioenen enz. enz. er bij moet tellen." — but in a criticism in Insulinde Mr. Van Heutsz, Assistant Resident, observed that one must add all the extra expenditure of the Civil Administration, leave allowances and search vessels, travel and subsistence costs and a part of the pensions.

Van Kol accepted the correction as tending in his own direction: others, he noted, had thereby arrived at an outlay of seven to eight hundred million, and the *[[Java Bode]]* of [[13 April]] [[1897]] at a thousand million guilders and a hundred thousand human lives.

## Significance

The exchange is of interest because it places the future conqueror of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the war's most prominent parliamentary opponent on the same side of a question of fact: both agreed that the official accounts understated the true cost of the war, and that neither the [[Ministerie van Koloniën|Ministry of Colonies]] nor the Indies administration could state it precisely — a point the Minister [[Mr. J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] had himself conceded five months before the debate.

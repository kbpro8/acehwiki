**The 1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren** (also **1ste Divisie Maréchaussee**, *Iste Divisie*) was the division of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] based at [[Blang Kedjeren|Blang Kedjeren]] — also written **Blangkedjeren** — in the highlands of central [[Aceh|Atjeh]], and the principal Maréchaussee formation of the Gajo Alas interior in the period between the *dertiger jaren* (1930s) and the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the 1ste Divisie is the Blangkedjeren- and central-Atjeh-oriented Maréchaussee division whose eight brigades at [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]] — together with the four brigades at [[Koeala Bee|Koeala Bee]] — formed the principal Dutch force in the Alas valley theatre on the eve of the invasion. In Dames's [[1954]] *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, the division is the unit through which the book's two great themes — the *plichtsbesef* (sense of duty) of the Ambonese soldier, and the boundary between the tall story and the *waarheid* (truth) — are introduced in the prologue, by way of the *kapitein*'s [[Hotel de Boer]] story at the *Soos* (officers' club) in [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] in [[1933]].

## The Dertiger Jaren: The Captain and the *Dardanellen* (1933)

The 1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren is the principal unit of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] mentioned in the prologue of *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* — Dames's [[1954]] portrait-volume of the Ambonese professional soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]] Dames opens:

> "In het 1933 of daaromtrent jaar zat de kapitein, commandant van de 1e Divisie Marechaussee uit [[Blang Kedjeren]], op de Soos in [[Koeta-Radja]] een groot verhaal af te steken over een belevenis in [[Hotel de Boer]]."

The captain of the division — travelling through [[Medan|Medan]] with his two *[[dardanellen|dardanellen]]* (the Ambonese orderlies/body-servants of the Maréchaussee system) — sat in the lobby of [[Hotel de Boer]] in *veldtenue* (field grey-green rather than the expected white) drinking a large bottle of beer, only to discover two Maréchaussees with drawn *[[klewang|klewang]]* (cutlass) and *[[karabijn (M. 95)|karabijn]]* (carbine) *in de aanslag* (at the ready) standing behind his chair. The two Maréchaussees — having followed their captain into the hotel as bodyguards — had taken position behind his chair with drawn swords and cocked carbines.

> "Deze twee *dardanellen* nu waren jaren terug op de Blang gekomen en steeds daar gebleven. Eerst nu kwamen ze eens in de bewoonde wereld terug" —

These two *dardanellen* had come to the Blang years before and had always stayed there; only now did they come back to the *bewoonde wereld* (the inhabited world, i.e. civilisation). The episode is the book's first image of the *plichtsbesef* of the Ambonese soldier: the *dardanellen* who, in the chicest hotel of Medan, stood behind their captain with drawn swords because that was their duty.

## The Point of the Anecdote

> "Het verhaal van de kapitein was niet waar. Maar hij heeft het verteld en met zeer groot succes."

The captain's story was almost certainly embellished — but the *Atjehmensen* (Atjeh people, the colonial Dutchmen of long Atjeh service) at the *Soos*, who knew the hotel, "*hebben het niet voor onmogelijk gehouden*" ("did not consider it impossible"). The point is that the *plichtsbesef* of the Ambonese Maréchaussee was such that "*de grenzen van het belachelijke verre konden worden overschreden*" — the boundaries of the ridiculous could be far exceeded — and that "*wij dan lang niet altijd gelijk hadden*" — we were not always right. The division is the unit at [[Blang Kedjeren]] — one of the most *afgelegen* (remote) posts of the colonial army in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] — and its captain's Hotel de Boer story is the book's methodological frame: the boundary between the tall story and the *waarheid* is the boundary the book will observe.

## Organisation as of 1 January 1940

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], the 1ste Divisie Marechaussee was grouped as follows (each brigade of Maréchaussee numbering 20 men):

| Divisie | Headquarters | Brigades | Secondary posts |
|---------|-------------|---------|-----------------|
| Iste Divisie | Blangkedjeren | 8 | Koeala Bee (4 brigades) |

**Total**: 12 brigades (about 240 men).

Blangkedjeren was the principal Dutch administrative post of the Gajo Alas interior — the administrative centre of the *onderafdeeling Gajo Alas*, the southernmost of the Dutch Atjeh interior posts. Koeala Bee lay to the south on the Westkust.

## Operations 1940–1942 and the Pre-Invasion Drawdown

The 1ste Divisie's Blangkedjeren brigades were progressively drawn upon for the pre-invasion reorganisations of [[1940]]–[[1941]]. The [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] account of the first phase of reductions (the reductions of [[1940]]) records the dissolution of the Blangkedjeren-oriented detachments as part of the general reduction from 14 *divisies* to the smaller garrison force. The 1ste Divisie's brigades were among those dissolved in the first phase of reductions, and the divisional formation was effectively wound up during [[1940]]–[[1941]].

By March [[1942]] the Iste Divisie's residual Blangkedjeren strength had been consolidated with troops of various other units at Blangkedjeren under the command of Lieutenant-Colonel [[Blogg|Blogg]]. The Divisie's Koeala Bee brigades, meanwhile, remained on the [[Westkust (of Atjeh)|Westkust]], where after the [[1940]] reductions they formed part of Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]]'s reduced IIde Divisie command.

## The Blangkedjeren Capitulation of 28 March 1942

After the Japanese landings of [[12 March 1942]], the residual Dutch forces in the Gajo Alas interior — now under Lieutenant-Colonel [[Blogg|Blogg]] — surrendered at Blangkedjeren on [[28 March 1942]], the last of the four Atjeh capitulations. The Blangkedjeren capitulation closed the regular Dutch campaign in Atjeh, and is treated by the staff history as the closing episode of the Gajo Alas theatre. The staff history's emphasis on the division's progressive reduction through [[1940]]–[[1941]] is part of the compilers' broader argument that the Dutch pre-invasion force drawdown structurally disabled the Maréchaussee's capacity to mount sustained resistance.

## Significance

The 1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren is significant in two complementary colonial-Acehnese sources. In Dames's [[1954]] *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, the division is the unit through which Dames introduces the book's two great themes: the *plichtsbesef* of the Ambonese soldier, and the boundary between the tall story and the *waarheid*. The division is the book's principal unit of the prologue, and the unit whose captain's Hotel de Boer story sets the methodological frame for the book as a whole. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] Atjeh chapter (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the division is the staff history's principal narrative unit of the Gajo Alas interior — the most remote of the Dutch Atjeh garrison's areas of responsibility — and of the structural drawdown that reduced Dutch military presence in the southern Atjeh interior to a minimum by the time of the Japanese invasion. The division's Blangkedjeren brigades formed the backbone of the Dutch defence at the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Blangkedjeren capitulation of 28 March 1942]], the closing capitulation of the regular Dutch campaign in Atjeh.

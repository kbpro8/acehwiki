**The Cavalerie** — also referenced as the R. H. 4e escadron Cavalerie and the eventandige Cavalerie — was the cavalry arm of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]] in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] during the spring of [[1896]], organized in *pelotons* under various lieutenants for reconnaissance, mounted orderlies, and flank screening duties in support of Dutch infantry columns. The corps was overall commanded by Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy in the Lam-Krak and June operations, with pelotons under Lisnet (colonne Lamtih, 32 Eur.), Happé (colonne Veeren, 32 sabels), de Lannoy himself (24–25 May, 3 officieren and 64 ruiters), Rosenthal, Gentil, Nedermeijer van Rosenthal, and van Haaff. Although the *sawah* and *kreupelbosch* terrain of the Atjeh interior made classic cavalry charges impractical, the cavalerie's reconnaissance and patrol role was essential to Dutch column movements — and the corps produced one of the disputed heroes of the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|24 May 1896 capture of Lam-Pisang]], Wachtmeester Jonker (No. 31845), who was reputed (though the text disputes it) to be the first into Oemar's house.

## Background and Formation

As the cavalry arm of the colonial army, the cavalerie combined European and indigenous mounted men armed with the sabre and carbine. In the Atjeh theatre, the 4e escadron cavalerie was present at the start of [[1896]], with one peloton (under 1e Luit. C. W. van Haaff, 1 officer + 29 men) forming part of [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s relief column on [[8 April]] [[1896]]. The independent cavalry arrived later in the spring with the second tranche of reinforcements aboard five [[Koninklijke Paketvaart-Maatschappij (KPM)|KPM]] steamers, providing the additional pelotons needed for the May–June operations. The cavalerie was encamped within existing campements in [[Koeta Radja]].

## Role in the Atjeh War

On [[8 April]] [[1896]] one peloton under 1e Luit. C. W. van Haaff (1 officer + 29 men) formed the cavalry of [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s relief column for [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]]. On [[2 May]] [[1896]] a *½ escadron cavalerie* under Ritmeester Jhr. de Lannoy formed part of the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik]]; the cavalerie reconnoitered [[Lam Garoet]] and Lam Dam, occupied a heuvel 500 M north of [[Lamsoet]], and was fired upon from Lam Garoet on the *terugtocht*. On [[4 May]] [[1896]] a *½ escadron cavalerie* under Ritmeester Jhr. de Lannoy formed part of the [[Second Expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb (4 May 1896)|second expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]; a patrouille under Lt. Happé (1 trompetter + 16 ruiters) reconnoitered via Serampoean–Toengkoeb–Landoero Zuid-West, and the cavalerist somerdijk (No. 41298) was seriously wounded. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the cavalerie under Ritmeester Jhr. de Lannoy (3 officieren and 64 ruiters) supported the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] — reconnaissances toward [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Beradin (Vallei van Beradin / Kloof van Beradin)|Beradin]], and [[Lho-Nga]]. On [[24 May]] [[1896]] Wachtmeester Jonker (No. 31845) of the cavalerie was reputed — though the source disputes it — to be first into Oemar's house at [[Lam-Pisang]]; the official first-reacher was 1e luit. de Jongh of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]]. On [[29 May]] [[1896]] one peloton under Lt. J. P. Gentil formed part of Luit.-kol. C. J. Laceulle's colonne in the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe]]. On [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] two pelotons of 3 officers + 80 ruiters formed part of the 2e (land) Colonne under [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]] in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing and Glé Broeë attack]].

## Composition and Strength

The cavalerie in the Atjeh theatre comprised various pelotons under different lieutenants through the spring: Lisnet (colonne Lamtih, 32 Eur.); C. W. F. Happé (colonne Veeren, 32 sabels); Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy (ritmeester, 24–25 May, 3 officieren and 64 ruiters); Rosenthal; J. P. Gentil; Nedermeijer van Rosenthal; and C. W. van Haaff (1e Luit., 1 officer + 29 men, 8 April). Overall command in the May–June operations rested with Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy. The *eventandige Cavalerie* committed to the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 expedition]] numbered 3 officers and 80 ruiters in two pelotons. Casualties included cavalerist somerdijk (No. 41298), seriously wounded on [[4 May]] [[1896]].

## Significance

The cavalerie's significance in the spring [[1896]] campaign lay in its reconnaissance and flank-screening role, which allowed Dutch infantry columns to detect enemy *benden* and *versterkingen* before the infantry came under fire. The terrain of [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] — *sawah*, *kreupelbosch*, and fortified kampongs — made classic cavalry charges impractical, and the cavalerie's employment as mounted orderlies and reconnaissance troops reflects that constraint. The disputed claim of Wachtmeester Jonker to have been first into [[Teuku Umar]]'s house at [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]] — a distinction Kruisheer awards to 1e luit. de Jongh of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] — illustrates the way cavalry could exploit a breakthrough to ride ahead of the infantry in pursuit.

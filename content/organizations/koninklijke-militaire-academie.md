The **Koninklijke Militaire Academie** (KMA) — the Royal Military Academy at [[Breda|Breda]] — is the principal officers' training school of the Dutch army, founded in [[1828]] and housed in the aloude Bredasche kasteel (the old castle of Breda). The KMA trained officers for all arms of the metropolitan army and for the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]]; from [[1869]], under the new law on the regulation of instruction at the Academy, the four-year course was reduced to a two-year course for officers of all arms (with an additional one-year *applicatiecursus* for artillery and engineer officers from [[1872]]). As a transitional measure, in [[1869]] and [[1870]] cadets were admitted to a three-year course for all arms. Among the graduates of the KMA in this transitional cohort was [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Generaal C. J. Snijders]], who entered in [[September 1869|September]] [[1869]], graduated on [[14 July]] [[14 July]] [[1872]] as No. 1 of his four-man *rotatie* of engineers, and later returned to the KMA as Hoofd van Onderwijs (Head of Instruction) in the Genie-wetenschappen from [[1887]] to [[1894]].

## The KMA of 1869–1872

The cadet [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] entered the KMA on [[3 September]] [[3 September]] [[1869]] as a "jonker." Cadet life was then a strict internal regime — no Easter or Christmas leave, no weekend home visits. Snijders kept somewhat in the background — his meagre cadet pay, not supplemented from home, kept him from many festivities — and was a member of the fencing society "Arena" in his first year and of the shooting society "Attila" in his second. In his oldest year he served on the redaction committee of the *Kadetten-Almanak*, contributing the year-verslag of [[1871]] — his first published work. Academically he was No. 1 of his class from first to last; when the four-man *rotatie* passed the officers' examination on [[14 July]] [[14 July]] [[1872]] he was awarded two honorary prizes: a portrait of King Willem III in a gilded frame for excellence in linear drawing, and a revolver with inscription in a case for excellence in practical aptitude.

## The Snijders Succession at the KMA

[[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] returned to the KMA as a teacher of versterkingskunst, pionierkunst, geniereglementen, and lijnteekenen from [[1876]] to [[1880]] (under his old superior [[W. Badon Ghijben|W. Badon Ghijben]] as Hoofd van Onderwijs); he then served as Hoofd van Onderwijs in the Genie-wetenschappen from [[1887]] to [[1894]], succeeding [[W. Cool|W. Cool]]. His *Handboek der Pionierkunst voor het Nederlandsche Leger*, in twelve parts published from [[1880]] to [[1887]] (with a second edition in [[1903]]–[[1908]]), was introduced as the official textbook for the cadets of all arms at the KMA. His later co-workers included [[P. C. J. Noorduyn|P. C. J. Noorduyn]], [[P. Huffnagel|P. Huffnagel]], and [[F. R. van Roijen|F. R. van Roijen]].

## The KMA Centenary of 1928

The KMA celebrated its centenary at [[Weltevreden|Weltevreden]] in the Indies in [[October 1928|October]] [[1928]], in the course of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s second Indies journey. Snijders, who was then on his Indies tour for the opening of the [[K.N.I.L.M.|K.N.I.L.M.]] lines, attended the centenary celebration on [[18 October]] [[18 October]] [[1928]].

## Maréchaussée officers trained at the K.M.A.

the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* records two officers of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* as having passed through the *Koninklijke Militaire Academie* at [[Breda]]:

- [[W. V. Rhemrev]], who received his education at the former *[[Gymnasium Willem III]]* at [[Weltevreden]] and at the K.M.A., and became an officer in [[1902]]
- [[Dersjant|L. Dersjant]], born [[23 April]] [[1877]], who after completing the K.M.A. was appointed second lieutenant of Infantry in [[1900]] and placed with the Korps Maréchaussée in February [[1903]], being pensioned in due course as colonel

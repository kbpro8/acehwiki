**The Koloniaal Werfdepot** was the recruiting depot of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands Indies Army]] formerly established at [[Harderwijk]] in the [[Netherlands]], and the source of the unflattering popular image of the colonial soldier against which [[P. H. R. Beuming]] protests in *[[Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh (1911)|Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh]]*.

Writing of the return of the patrol from the [[Patrol to the Goeah Teroes on the Glé Betoes (25 September 1897)|Goeah Teroes]] with its dead, Beuming remarks that had one been able to witness such a scene one would judge the colonial soldier differently: *men doet verkeerd hem te beoordeelen gedurende zijn verloftijd, met zijn handgeld op zak, omstuwd door een troep zakkenrollende vrienden, zooals hij wel eens is afgeschilderd in de dagen, toen het Koloniaal-werfdepôt nog te Harderwijk gevestigd was* — one does wrong to judge him during his furlough, with his bounty money in his pocket, surrounded by a troop of pickpocketing friends, as he was sometimes depicted in the days when the colonial recruiting depot was still established at Harderwijk. *Gelukkig zijn die toestanden ten gunste van het Indische leger veranderd* — fortunately those conditions have changed to the advantage of the Indies army.

He makes the same appeal elsewhere, after the funeral of Lieutenant [[K. H. Westendorp]]: *O Moederland! Waart Ge daar eens getuige van, wat zoudt Ge meer sympathie gevoelen voor den koloniaal.*

**Atjehsche marechaussées** were the later‑formed [[Aceh]] naval infantry corps that the Dutch colonial authorities eventually organized to combat resistance in the Aceh region. The term appears in Dutch colonial military literature as a point of comparison with earlier colonial marine units, such as the Surinamese [[regiment mariniers Fourgeoud]] described by [[Stedman]]; Stedman notes that the Surinamese regiment “kan worden vergeleken met de latere ..Atjehsche marechaussées” (see [[page 368]]). The establishment of the Atjehsche marechaussées reflects the Dutch strategy of creating locally recruited, European‑modeled marine forces to enforce colonial rule in the eastern part of the archipelago.

## Historical Context

During the eighteenth century the Dutch West India Company and later the Dutch colonial administration relied on a variety of mixed‑race and enslaved troops in their overseas possessions. In Suriname, the regiment under Colonel [[Fourgeoud]] (active 1770–1773) was one such unit, composed partly of freed enslaved men and tasked with suppressing Maroon uprisings. Dutch military planners later adopted a similar model for the easternmost Sultanate of [[Aceh]], where resistance to Dutch expansion persisted into the nineteenth century. The creation of the Atjehsche marechaussées therefore represents a continuation of the colonial policy of employing indigenous or locally recruited forces for frontier warfare.

## Formation and Structure

The precise date of formation is not given in the source, but the reference to the “latere” (later) corps indicates that the Atjehsche marechaussées were organized after the Surinamese regiment’s disbandment in the early 1770s. The corps was intended to function as a marine infantry unit, capable of both riverine and coastal operations in the rugged terrain of Aceh. Its composition likely mirrored that of earlier colonial marine units, drawing on freed slaves, local recruits, and possibly former Maroon fighters, though the source does not provide explicit details.

## Operational Role

The Atjehsche marechaussées were employed in the Dutch‑Aceh conflicts that escalated in the mid‑nineteenth century, most notably during the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] (1873–1904). Their marine training made them suitable for operations along the extensive Acehnese coastline and the numerous rivers that provided access to interior strongholds. By integrating local knowledge with European military discipline, the corps aimed to suppress insurgent activities and protect Dutch commercial interests in the region.

## Significance

The establishment of the Atjehsche marechaussées illustrates the Dutch colonial administration’s adaptive use of indigenous forces to project power in difficult environments. It also highlights a continuity in colonial military practice: the transition from Surinamese marine regiments to Acehnese marine corps demonstrates how experiences in one colony informed strategies in another. The corps contributed to the Dutch ability to sustain a prolonged campaign in Aceh, shaping the course of the Aceh War and the eventual incorporation of the Sultanate into the Dutch East Indies.

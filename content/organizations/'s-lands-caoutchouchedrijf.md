**'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf** — the Government Rubber Enterprise — was the state enterprise of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië]] government charged with the exploitation of government rubber and — from [[1924]] — of government turpentine. The enterprise is named in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] as the operator of the [[Baleq]] enterprise — the colonial government's turpentine and resin factory in the [[Gajo-landen|Gajo-Landen]]-[[Atjeh]] border region — and as the institution that, from [[1927]] onward, ran the [[Baleq]] enterprise as a *afzonderlijke onderneming* (separate enterprise) of the *Caoutchouchedrijf*.

## The 1924 exploitation trial

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that, by *Besluit van [[27 June]] [[1924]]* — the [[Besluit van 27 June 1924 on Atjeh-turpentine exploitation|Besluit of 27 June 1924]] — the Regeering placed funds at the disposal of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* for an *exploitatie-proef* (exploitation trial) on behalf of the *Dienst van het Boschwezen*. The trial began on [[14 September]] [[1924]] in the *Semnenboschcomplexen* of [[Paja Djorok]] and [[Blangtekoer]], and the results were sufficient that in [[1926]] the Regeering resolved to convert the trial into a definitive exploitation as a *afzonderlijke onderneming van 's Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* — *separate enterprise of 's Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* — with effect from [[1 January]] [[1927]].

## The 1927 conversion

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the conversion of the trial into a definitive exploitation as a separate enterprise of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* took effect on [[1 January]] [[1927]]. By [[1930]], the area under exploitation was approximately 1600 ha, entirely in production. The enterprise's production figures — preserved in Table I of the pamphlet — show the growth of the enterprise from 5 tons of resin in [[1925]] to 356 tons of resin and 104 tons of turpentine in [[1929]].

## The factory

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the balsam — the raw resinous exudate of the tapped pines — was originally processed in an *oud destilleerketeltje* (old distillation kettle) of 100 kg capacity per filling. In [[July]] [[1925]] a *locomobielketel* (locomobile boiler) was brought into service, allowing steam distillation and a notable increase in yield. After the [[1927]] conversion, a *zo modern mogelijke installatie* (as modern an installation as possible) was purchased in [[France]] and, after the many delays of transport to the enterprise, the new factory was brought into service in [[October]] [[1928]]. The factory was operated by the Superintendent [[W. M. van der Veer]], whose *private mededeelingen* (private communications) supplied the operational details preserved in the pamphlet.

## Significance

's Lands Caoutchouchedrijf's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the state enterprise that operated the [[Baleq]] turpentine enterprise from [[1924]] to [[1930]] and beyond — the principal colonial-era turpentine factory in the Netherlands Indies. The enterprise's *afzonderlijke onderneming* (separate enterprise) status from [[1927]] onward illustrates the way in which the colonial state managed its commodity-production enterprises, and its collaboration with the *Dienst van het Boschwezen* — which supplied the standing pine forests — and the [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij]] — which distributed the turpentine in Europe — illustrates the network of institutions through which colonial commodity production was organised.

**The Legioen van T. Djohan** — also referred to as *T. Oemar's Legion* or *Umar's Legion* — was the armed auxiliary force created and led by [[Teuku Umar]] (under the name T. Djohan Pahalawan) while he served as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Dutch colonial government in the period [[1893]]–[[1896]], a paid and Dutch-armed native corps originally intended to support the Dutch by clearing hostile areas — exemplified by its action at [[Tjot Goeë]] on [[9 May]] [[1895]] and by its [[Recapture of bentengs Glé Broë and Koeta T. Rajoet (4 August 1895)|4 August 1895 recapture of the bentengs Glé Broë and Koeta T. Rajoet]] under T. Djohan's personal direction — but dissolved in effect by Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], when the legion went over to the enemy with their Dutch-supplied weapons. Originally composed of 250 men under three blood-relative commanders and forty *panglimas*, the legion was later expanded with men from the IV/VI Moekims, [[Lepong]], and [[Lehong]]. Ketjik Bintang van Lam Loempoer, formerly the appointed successor as commandant of the legion, was reportedly killed by [[28 May]] [[1896]].

## Background and Formation

The Legioen van T. Djohan was created by the Dutch colonial government following [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection of 30 September 1893]], when Umar submitted to the Dutch authority at [[Koeta Radja]] and was appointed *Panglima Prang Besar* of the Government — a title carrying the formal authority to raise and command a native auxiliary force under Dutch pay and Dutch arms. The legion was originally composed of 250 men under three blood-relative commanders (whose kinship tie was meant to ensure loyalty) and forty subordinate commanders. The Dutch supplied the legion with 25,000 Beaumont cartridges, 380 breech-loading rifles, and 500 muzzle-loading rifles. The legion was later expanded with men drawn from the IV and VI Moekims, from Umar's home territory of [[Lepong]], and from [[Lehong]]. Its formation reflected the Dutch system of using Atjeh auxiliaries — under European officers in principle, under Umar in practice — to fight the recalcitrant *moekims* of the [[Lamkrak Moekims (4 Lamkraksche Moekims)|Lamkrak]], the [[Sagi der XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]], and other hostile regions.

## Role in the Atjeh War

The Legioen van T. Djohan's recorded actions in Dutch service exemplify the way the legion "*often talso over the work from regular troops*". On [[9 May]] [[1895]] the legion was in action at [[Tjot Goeë]]. On [[4 August]] [[1895]] it conducted the [[Recapture of bentengs Glé Broë and Koeta T. Rajoet (4 August 1895)|recapture of the bentengs Glé Broë and Koeta T. Rajoet]] under T. Djohan's personal direction. The legion also conducted the [[Conquest of Ana' Galong (30 November 1893)|conquest of Ana' Galong on 30 November 1893]] — the former main residence of [[Teungkoe di Tiro (Tengkoe di Tiroe)|Tengkoe di Tiroe]], described by the Acehnese as the heart of [[Groot-Atjeh (Great Aceh)|Groot-Atjeh]]. The legion's fighting against the *moslemin* was, per Kruisheer, largely sham battle: "*dat het gevecht tusschen de manschappen van zijn legioen en de moslemin, al vielen er also een enkele maal gewonden, het 'main prang' in optima forma was*" (that the engagement between his legion's men and the moslemin, even if there were occasional wounded, was *main prang* in optima forma) — a doctrine attributed to the bought-off oelama [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]]. The legion was dissolved in effect by Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], when its members went over to the enemy with their Dutch-supplied weapons and formed the nucleus of the *benden van T. Oemar*. Ketjik Bintang van Lam Loempoer, formerly the appointed successor as commandant of the legion, was reportedly killed by [[28 May]] [[1896]] in the fighting of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]].

## Composition and Strength

The Legioen van T. Djohan was originally composed of 250 men under three blood-relative commanders and forty *panglimas*. The legion was later expanded with men from the IV and VI Moekims, [[Lepong]], and [[Lehong]]. Its armament was supplied by the Dutch: 25,000 Beaumont cartridges, 380 *achterlaadgeweren*, and 500 *voorlaadgeweren*. The legion was led by [[Teuku Umar]] (under the name T. Djohan Pahalawan) as *Panglima Prang Besar*; his designated successor as commandant was Ketjik Bintang van Lam Loempoer (reportedly killed by [[28 May]] [[1896]]).

## Significance

The Legioen van T. Djohan was the institutional embodiment of the Dutch *stelsel* of using Atjeh auxiliaries under a trusted native commander — a *stelsel* that collapsed entirely with Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. The legion's Dutch-supplied weapons — 380 breech-loaders, 500 muzzle-loaders, 25,000 Beaumont cartridges — became the core of the *benden van T. Oemar* that [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] would describe in his [[26 May]] [[1896]] *dagorder* as a "*sterke, geoefende, goed versterkte en goed bewapende bende*" (strong, well-trained, well-fortified, and well-armed band). The doctrine of sham battle attributed to [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]] — by which the legion fought the *moslemin* without serious intent — illustrates the corruption that the Dutch *stelsel* had produced: the legion was being paid by the Dutch to fight an enemy that it was, in effect, sparing. The legion's dissolution and the killing of its designated successor Ketjik Bintang van Lam Loempoer in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] closed the chapter of Dutch reliance on Atjeh auxiliaries and opened the chapter of direct Dutch column warfare that defined the spring [[1896]] campaign.

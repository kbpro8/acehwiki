**The Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** (Government of Atjeh and Dependencies) was the colonial administrative unit governing the territory of Atjeh and its subordinate regions in the northern tip of Sumatra. Headed by a Civiel en Militair Gouverneur (Civil and Military Governor)—most recently [[H. N. N. A. Swart]] at the time of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]]—the Gouvernement occupied a unique position in the administrative hierarchy of the Netherlands East Indies, combining civil and military authority in a single office. This arrangement reflected the protracted Atjeh War (1873-1914 and beyond) and the continued need for military governance in parts of the territory that had not yet been fully pacified or integrated into the regular colonial administrative system.

## Dual Administrative Structure

The Gouvernement's territory was divided into two fundamentally different types of administrative units, a structure that made it exceptional among all the Buitenbezittingen. The first type was the *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory), comprising [[Groot-Atjeh]] and part of the onderafdeeling [[Singkel]]. In these areas, the Dutch colonial administration exercised direct control through its own officials. The second type was the Inlandsche Zelfbesturen (Native Self-Governments), consisting of the various *landschappen* (territorial chiefdoms) that maintained their own rulers under Dutch suzerainty. The coexistence of these two forms of governance within a single Gouvernement was the central reason that the standard decentralisatie proposals of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] could not be applied to Atjeh in their usual form, leading Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff]] to conclude that only [[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]] (Regional Self-Governance) was feasible.

## Afdeelingen and Onderafdeelingen

The directly ruled territory of the Gouvernement was organized into four *afdeelingen* (divisions), each headed by an Assistent-Resident: [[Groot-Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[W. Doornik]]), [[Westkust van Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[I. L. O'Brien]]), [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[H. J. Krugers]]), and [[Noordkust van Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[M. C. Roos van Raadshoven]]). Within these *afdeelingen*, several *onderafdeelingen* (sub-districts) were administered by Controleurs or Civiel Gezaghebbers: [[Pidië]] (Civiel Gezaghebber Majoor [[Th. J. Veltman]]), Oeléë-Lhenë en de XXVI Moekims (Controleur [[F. W. A. L. de Nijs]]), XXII Moekims (Civiel Gezaghebber Kapitein [[G. F. B. Watrin]]), XXV Moekims en de Zuidelijke Nederzettingen (Controleur [[G. J. van Dongen]]), [[Tamiang]] (Civiel Gezaghebber [[W. Wessel]]), and Sinabang on the island of Simeulue (Controleur [[L. Berkhout]]). The use of military officers (a Majoor and a Kapitein) as Civiel Gezaghebbers reflected the continued militarization of Atjeh's governance.

The Gouvernement also maintained the position of Assistent-Resident ter beschikking (Assistant-Resident at disposal), held by [[W. F. J. Kroon]], which served as a precursor to the proposed Gewestelijke Bestuursadviseur (Regional Administrative Advisor) in the reform plan. The Secretaris van het Gouvernement (Gouvernement Secretary), [[V. A. Doeve]], was responsible for the bureaucratic processes and correspondence of the Governor's office.

## The Sagi-Peleebalang-Moekim Hierarchy

Within the *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* of Groot-Atjeh, the indigenous administrative structure operated through a three-tier hierarchy described by Gouverneur Swart at the [[Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen (19-20 January 1914)]]: three [[sagi]]'s (comparable to Javanese *regentschappen*), [[peleebalangschap]]pen (comparable to *districten*), and [[moekimschap]]pen (comparable to *onderdistricten*). This indigenous system coexisted with the Dutch colonial administrative overlay, creating a complex dual governance structure. The numbered moekim-based onderafdeelingen (XXII, XXV, XXVI Moekims) represented groups of village clusters organized around mosques, reflecting the Islamic character of Atjeh's community organization.

## Proposed Dissolution

In the proposed administrative reorganization discussed during the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]], the Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was to be dissolved and its territory absorbed into the new [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]], which would be governed by a civilian Resident rather than a Civiel en Militair Gouverneur. The afdeelingen were to be reorganized: [[Groot-Atjeh]] combined with the [[Westkust van Atjeh]] (pop. 210,000); the [[Noordkust van Atjeh]] merged with the [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] minus Tamiang (pop. 480,000); and border territories including [[Tamiang]], [[Langsa]], and the [[Alaslanden]] transferred to the Deli and Langkat afdeelingen. [[Koeta-Radja]], the Gouvernement's hoofdplaats, would become the seat of a Resident within the new Noord-Sumatra gewest, with representatiekosten (representation expenses) allocated for its continued administrative importance.

## Significance

The Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was one of the most distinctive administrative units in the Netherlands East Indies, its very existence a testament to the exceptional challenges that Atjeh had posed to Dutch colonial rule. The combination of civil and military authority in a single Gouverneur, the coexistence of directly ruled territory with Inlandsche Zelfbesturen, and the continued presence of military officers in civil administrative positions all distinguished Atjeh from the regular colonial governance structure. The proposed dissolution of the Gouvernement and its absorption into Noord-Sumatra represented a fundamental shift in colonial policy, signaling the aspiration to normalize Atjeh's governance and end its exceptional status—a process that the unanimous agreement of the Koeta-Radja meetings suggested was at least nominally accepted by Atjeh's own colonial leadership.

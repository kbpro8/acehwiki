**The Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** (Government of Atjeh and Dependencies) was the colonial administrative unit governing the territory of Atjeh and its subordinate regions in the northern tip of Sumatra. Headed by a Civiel en Militair Gouverneur (Civil and Military Governor)—most recently [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. N. A. Swart]] at the time of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]]—the Gouvernement occupied a unique position in the administrative hierarchy of the Netherlands East Indies, combining civil and military authority in a single office. This arrangement reflected the protracted Atjeh War (1873-1914 and beyond) and the continued need for military governance in parts of the territory that had not yet been fully pacified or integrated into the regular colonial administrative system.

## Dual Administrative Structure

The Gouvernement's territory was divided into two fundamentally different types of administrative units, a structure that made it exceptional among all the Buitenbezittingen. The first type was the *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory), comprising [[Groot-Atjeh]] and part of the onderafdeeling [[Singkel]]. In these areas, the Dutch colonial administration exercised direct control through its own officials. The second type was the Inlandsche Zelfbesturen (Native Self-Governments), consisting of the various *landschappen* (territorial chiefdoms) that maintained their own rulers under Dutch suzerainty. The coexistence of these two forms of governance within a single Gouvernement was the central reason that the standard decentralisatie proposals of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] could not be applied to Atjeh in their usual form, leading Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff]] to conclude that only [[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]] (Regional Self-Governance) was feasible.

## Afdeelingen and Onderafdeelingen

The directly ruled territory of the Gouvernement was organized into four *afdeelingen* (divisions), each headed by an Assistent-Resident: [[Groot-Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[W. Doornik]]), [[Westkust van Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[I. L. O'Brien]]), [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[H. J. Krugers]]), and [[Noordkust van Atjeh]] (Assistent-Resident [[M. C. Roos van Raadshoven]]). Within these *afdeelingen*, several *onderafdeelingen* (sub-districts) were administered by Controleurs or Civiel Gezaghebbers: [[Pidië]] (Civiel Gezaghebber Majoor [[Th. J. Veltman]]), Oeléë-Lhenë en de XXVI Moekims (Controleur [[F. W. A. L. de Nijs]]), XXII Moekims (Civiel Gezaghebber Kapitein [[G. F. B. Watrin]]), XXV Moekims en de Zuidelijke Nederzettingen (Controleur [[G. J. van Dongen]]), [[Tamiang]] (Civiel Gezaghebber [[W. Wessel]]), and Sinabang on the island of Simeulue (Controleur [[L. Berkhout]]). The use of military officers (a Majoor and a Kapitein) as Civiel Gezaghebbers reflected the continued militarization of Atjeh's governance.

The Gouvernement also maintained the position of Assistent-Resident ter beschikking (Assistant-Resident at disposal), held by [[W. F. J. Kroon]], which served as a precursor to the proposed Gewestelijke Bestuursadviseur (Regional Administrative Advisor) in the reform plan. The Secretaris van het Gouvernement (Gouvernement Secretary), [[V. A. Doeve]], was responsible for the bureaucratic processes and correspondence of the Governor's office.

## The Sagi-Peleebalang-Moekim Hierarchy

Within the *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* of Groot-Atjeh, the indigenous administrative structure operated through a three-tier hierarchy described by Gouverneur Swart at the [[Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen (19-20 January 1914)]]: three [[sagi]]'s (comparable to Javanese *regentschappen*), [[peleebalangschap]]pen (comparable to *districten*), and [[moekimschap]]pen (comparable to *onderdistricten*). This indigenous system coexisted with the Dutch colonial administrative overlay, creating a complex dual governance structure. The numbered moekim-based onderafdeelingen (XXII, XXV, XXVI Moekims) represented groups of village clusters organized around mosques, reflecting the Islamic character of Atjeh's community organization.

## Proposed Dissolution

In the proposed administrative reorganization discussed during the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]], the Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was to be dissolved and its territory absorbed into the new [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]], which would be governed by a civilian Resident rather than a Civiel en Militair Gouverneur. The afdeelingen were to be reorganized: [[Groot-Atjeh]] combined with the [[Westkust van Atjeh]] (pop. 210,000); the [[Noordkust van Atjeh]] merged with the [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] minus Tamiang (pop. 480,000); and border territories including [[Tamiang]], [[Langsa]], and the [[Alaslanden]] transferred to the Deli and Langkat afdeelingen. [[Koeta-Radja]], the Gouvernement's hoofdplaats, would become the seat of a Resident within the new Noord-Sumatra gewest, with representatiekosten (representation expenses) allocated for its continued administrative importance.

## Significance

The Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was one of the most distinctive administrative units in the Netherlands East Indies, its very existence a testament to the exceptional challenges that Atjeh had posed to Dutch colonial rule. The combination of civil and military authority in a single Gouverneur, the coexistence of directly ruled territory with Inlandsche Zelfbesturen, and the continued presence of military officers in civil administrative positions all distinguished Atjeh from the regular colonial governance structure. The proposed dissolution of the Gouvernement and its absorption into Noord-Sumatra represented a fundamental shift in colonial policy, signaling the aspiration to normalize Atjeh's governance and end its exceptional status—a process that the unanimous agreement of the Koeta-Radja meetings suggested was at least nominally accepted by Atjeh's own colonial leadership.

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

[[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]] gives a vivid account of the Gouvernement's governors and its civil-military tensions.

The governor **Van der Heyden** — *die onder gunstiger omstandigheden de Van Heutsz had kunnen zijn eener vroegere periode* — was *afgedankt en weggezonden*, his *afzetting* one of the *beschaamder* episodes in the Atjeh-historie. **[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]** — who with [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] as advisor broke the *geconcentreerde linie* policy — rose from the Atjeh governorship to the landvoogdij; at the *overgave* in the [[Paleis op Rijswijk]] [[Heynen]] had the wacht. **[[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]** succeeded him as governor; it was he who changed the [[T. Tjhi Toenong]] death-sentence from the rope to the bullet. **[[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]]** succeeded Van Daalen in [[1909]] and *onmiddellijk* unleashed the *vernietigingsapparaat* under luitenant [[Schmidt]] against the Tiro-oelama's; it was Swart who confirmed the [[False T. Radja Sabi (1913)|false T. Radja Sabi]] mystification of [[1913]].

On the civil-military tension: *Dit drijven: terugdringing van militair gezag en vervanging door civiel, ligt als een vloek op ons Atjehbeleid. Er staat in het boek der Atjehhistorie niet veel genoteerd dat beschamender voor ons is, en van grooter domheid getuigt*, than the *afzetting* of Van der Heyden. *Het burgerlijk bestuur [...] had dikwijls niet de grootheld van geest om te kunnen begrijpen, dat het op den achtergrond moet staan zoolang het Leger niet de volstrekt onmisbare voorwaarden heeft geschapen welke voor de volle functionneering van een civiel bestuur noodig zijn.*

The *Inlandsch Bestuur* worked through *oeléëbalangs* appointed or confirmed by the Gouvernement. The *oeléëbalang baroh* of [[Keureutoe|Keureutoë]] — the Dutch-appointed one — held the *landschap* against the *oeléëbalang Toenong* (the traditional one in the mountains); the Gouvernement tried to keep both on the payroll. *Er ging of gaat vrijwel geen civiel gouverneur of resident van Atjeh met pensioen of hij heeft althans één maatregel getroffen, welke hij ziet als eene schrede verder op den weg der pacificatie van dit volk.*

*Atjeh*, door H.C. Zentgraff. Gedrukt bij Drukkerij "De Unie", [[1938]], Batavia.

## The governors

The succession of governors named in [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]]'s account runs: [[Karel van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]], "the general with the strong hand and the sturdy perseverance," whose period formed the sole favourable exception in the long series of years from [[1873]] to [[1898]] but was too short to leave a lasting result; [[J. van Teijn|Van Teijn]], whose partnership with [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] demonstrated that the Acehnese adversary respected only purposeful force; [[Pompe van Meerdervoort]], whose administration was "short and feeble"; [[C. Deijckerhoff|Deijckerhoff]], whose accession "opened — and at the same time closed — the most disappointing and shameful period of Atjeh policy"; [[Stemfoort]]; [[Van Vliet]], who took office in November [[1896]] and retired in March [[1898]]; van Heutsz from March [[1898]] to October [[1904]]; [[Van der Wijck]] as caretaker; [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] from [[3 April]] [[1905]] to [[10 June]] [[1908]]; Swart from [[1908]] to [[1918]]; [[Van Sluys]], the first purely civil Governor, from [[2 September]] [[1918]]; and later [[Van Aken]], "the most excellent administrator after Swart," who handed over to Resident [[J. Jongejans]] on [[7 March]] [[1936]].

## Swart's appointment and terms of service

By decree of [[4 May]] [[1908]] Swart was appointed Military Commander of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden and simultaneously Governor of that province, at 1,500 guilders a month plus the further lawful advantages attached to the post, with the stipulation that he should exercise those functions under the title of *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, and that so long as he held the post his military income should stand still, though free medical treatment for himself and his family remained assured. He took over the government on [[10 June]] [[1908]].

## Administrative structure

The [[Administrative Division of Atjeh (1908)|administrative division of 1908]] created five *afdeelingen*, each under an Assistant Resident: [[Groot Atjeh]], the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|Noordkust]], the [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust]], the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]] and [[Bambél]] ([[Gajò Loeös]], the [[Alaslanden]] and [[Singkel]]). In *afdeelingen* where it proved possible a *controleur* conducted the administration, among others at [[Idi]] and [[Singkel]]; [[Sabang]] came under a civilian civil authority. In [[1909]] the *[[sagi]]* of the [[XXII Moekims]], [[Tamiang]], [[Serbödjadi]], [[Poelò Raja]], [[Tapa' Toean]] and the separately administered [[VII Moekims Pidië]] were placed under civil administrators. In [[1910]] Bambél was abolished, Singkel joined to the Westkust, and the Gajò Loeös and the Alaslanden made independent *afdeelingen* each under an officer-*gezaghebber*.

## Publications

The Government published the *[[Monthly Reports of the Government of Atjeh and Dependencies|Maandverslagen]]*, quoted extensively throughout du Croo's book, and the *[[The News-sheet for the Government of Atjeh and Dependencies|Nieuwsblad voor het Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]*, known as *De Atjehcourant*, which reported Swart's departure in September [[1918]].

## The separation of powers, 1918

In his [[Swart's farewell address (2 September 1918)|farewell address]] Swart noted that with the laying down of his office "an important piece of Atjeh history" was closed, "since there will now be a move to an absolute separation of the Civil and Military Authority, as exists in all other provinces as well."

## Significance (du Croo, 1943)

The Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was for forty-five years the one province of the Netherlands Indies under permanent military government, and its normalisation in [[1918]] — completed in the last four officer-governed sub-divisions only by [[1936]] — was the meaning of the word *[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]]*.

**Deli Maatschappij** was the first *naamlooze vennootschap* (joint-stock company) established in the [[Dutch East Indies]], founded in [[1869]] on the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] by the Handel Maatschappij and the partners Janssen, Clemen, and [[Jacobus Nienhuys]], the pioneer of the Deli tobacco culture. From an initial capital of ƒ300,000, the company grew over sixty years to a capital of ƒ30,000,000, becoming the cornerstone of what would emerge as the most productive plantation district in the colonial Indies.

## Foundation and early years

The company was founded in [[1869]], six years after [[Jacobus Nienhuys|Nienhuys]] had made his first tobacco plantings at the mouth of the Deli River in [[1863]]. The establishment of a *naamlooze vennootschap* — a novelty in the Indies — reflected the growing conviction that the Deli tobacco trade had outgrown the resources of individual planters and required corporate capital. The company's oldest plantation, "Arrensburg," had been flourishing for some time and would become a formal company only in [[1877]].

## Capital growth

The Deli Maatschappij's capital trajectory reflects both the expansion of the East Coast economy and the reinvestment of profits from the tobacco trade:

| Year | Capital |
|------|---------|
| [[1869]] | ƒ300,000 |
| [[1873]] | ƒ500,000 |
| [[1876]] | ƒ800,000 |
| [[1878]] | ƒ2,000,000 |
| [[1881]] | ƒ4,000,000 |
| [[1919]] | ƒ13,500,000 |
| [[1927]] | ƒ30,000,000 |

Volker notes that the rubber plantations of various tobacco companies — including the Deli Maatschappij — were "wholly opened and developed and are operated with money reserved from tobacco profits." The reinvestment of tobacco profits in rubber and other diversified ventures was typical of the leading plantation companies.

## The 1891 crisis

The East Coast tobacco market collapse of [[1890]]–[[1891]] hit the Deli Maatschappij severely: the company alone lost ƒ350,000 in the crisis year. The total loss across the East Coast tobacco sector was estimated at seven million guilders. The crisis pruned the number of tobacco enterprises from 170 in [[1891]] to 70 by [[1927]], establishing the principle that only the narrow belt between the Wampoe River and the Soengei Oelar possessed the precise soil and climate conditions for superior Deli wrapper leaf.

## Medical and social provisions

Under [[J. T. Cremer]]'s leadership, the Deli Maatschappij was the first company in the region to employ a doctor, doing so in [[1871]] — a full decade before any legal obligation to provide medical care for plantation workers. In [[1905]] the Deli Maatschappij established the Waterleiding Maatschappij "Ajer Beresih," the spring-water supply company of [[Medan]], which remained the company's property.

## Role in Medan

The Deli Maatschappij was the principal driver of [[Medan]]'s urban development. When the city became the administrative capital of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust gewest]] in [[1887]], the Deli Maatschappij's offices, infrastructure investments, and social institutions were integral to its character.

## Account from Atjeh als land voor handel en bedrijf (1925)

**Deli Maatschappij** was major plantation company of [[Oostkust van Sumatra]] extending into [[Temiang]] with large land grant attempting tobacco.

### Activity in Temiang

Deli-Mij got large land grant and opened tobacco enterprises Sekrah I, II and III. After couple years closed because tobacco not to wish succeeded and returned grounds.

Illustrates general failure tobacco in [[Temiang]] even orderly condition not able become Deli tobacco per Broersma.

Later replaced by rubber and oilpalm enterprises. Deli-Mij example followed by [[Tabak-Maatschappij Arendsburg]] and [[Rotterdam Deli-Maatschappij]] similar failures.

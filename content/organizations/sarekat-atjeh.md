The **Sarekat Atjeh** was the association of progressive young *[[oelèëbalang]]s* and Acehnese officials founded at [[Koeta Radja]] in December [[1916]] under the patronage of the Civil and Military Governor [[H. N. A. Swart]], with some two hundred chiefs and officials as members.

## Foundation

It must have been a great satisfaction to Swart, [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] wrote, to be able to record in the *[[Koloniaal Verslag]]* of [[1916]] that in December of that year a meeting was held at Koeta Radja, in his presence, of progressive young *oelèëbalangs* and Acehnese officials "who found no satisfaction in the old, narrow conceptions, who wished to modify the outdated *[[adat]]* usages, who wished to promote the raising of the Acehnese woman and of both secular and religious education. Thus arose the Sarekat Atjeh, of which 200 chiefs and officials became members."

## The first meeting

That first gathering, at which nearly all the prominent chiefs were present, was especially remarkable for its choice of officers: "as chairman there was elected the young, progressive oelèëbalang of Peureula', while as honorary chairman there was elected the well-known, very old-fashioned and conservative oelèëbalang of Keumangan: young and old went together."

## Programme

The association's declared aims were the modification of outdated *adat* usages, the raising of the position of the Acehnese woman, and the promotion of both secular schooling and religious education.

## Significance

The Sarekat Atjeh represented the emergence within the Acehnese chiefly class itself of the reforming impulse that Swart's *[[volksonderwijs]]* policy had aimed at, and the acceptance by the Governor of its patronage marked the closing phase of his administration, two years before the transfer to civil government. In the [[Sumatra Post]] interview of [[1914]] Swart had already declared that he expected much of the younger generation of chiefs, then being trained at [[Bandoeng]] or [[Fort de Kock]], as "the old territorial chiefs, who had lived through the long struggle, gradually disappeared… and with them an old generation disappeared."

Those younger chiefs did not always fulfil expectation as a transitional link — nor could that be expected; where circumstances made them succeed an older generation prematurely or too quickly or too young, they often met obstruction from the remaining "men of the old school." "[[T. Tjhi' Moehammad Djohan Alamsjah|Teukoe Tjhi' Peusangan]] could confirm this."

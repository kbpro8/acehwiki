The **Maatschappij ter Bevordering van het Natuurkundig Onderzoek der Nederlandsche Koloniën** (the Society for the Promotion of Natural-Science Research in the Dutch Colonies) was a Dutch learned society founded in [[1881]] at [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]] for the promotion of natural-science research in the Netherlands Indies and the other Dutch colonies. The Society was the parent body of the natural-science research programme that would, in the early twentieth century, be absorbed into the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] (the Royal Colonial Institute Association) at Amsterdam. The Society's *Directeuren* (Directors) were the sponsors of the offprint of [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]].

## Founding and Purpose

The Maatschappij ter Bevordering van het Natuurkundig Onderzoek der Nederlandsche Koloniën was founded in [[1881]] at [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]]. The Society's purpose was the promotion of natural-science research in the Netherlands Indies — the botanical, zoological, geological and meteorological investigation of the colony — by the sponsoring of expeditions, the publication of scientific results, and the maintenance of scientific collections at Amsterdam. The Society was the natural-science counterpart of the *Koninklijk Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië* (the Royal Institute for the Language, Geography and Ethnology of the Netherlands Indies), founded at [[Delft|Delft]] in [[1851]], which served the human-science side of colonial research.

## The Sponsoring of Kreemer's 1918 Atjeh Rice Monograph

The Society's *Directeuren* (Directors) were the named sponsors of the offprint of [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*]] ("Rice Cultivation in the Aceh Region and Dependencies"). The title page of the 1918 offprint records that the article — originally published in the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap*, deel XXXV (1918), afl. 3 — was "overgedrukt voor de Directeuren der Maatschappij ter Bevordering van het Natuurkundig Onderzoek der Nederlandsche Koloniën" (printed for the directors of the Society for the Promotion of Natural-Science Research in the Dutch Colonies) by [[E. J. Brill|E. J. Brill]] at Leiden.

The arrangement — the offprint of a published article, sponsored by a learned society and printed by a specialist academic press — was a typical Dutch colonial-scholarly publication pattern of the late-nineteenth and early-twentieth centuries. The Maatschappij, as the natural-science sponsor, took on the cost of the offprint, and the *Atjèh Instituut* included it as No. 6 in its *Mededeelingen* (Communications) series.

## Absorption into the Koloniaal Instituut

In the early twentieth century the natural-science research programme of the Maatschappij was absorbed into the broader colonial-research programme of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] (the Royal Colonial Institute Association) at Amsterdam, founded in 1910 and opened in 1926 in its great building on the Mauritskade. The Koloniaal Instituut brought together, under a single institutional roof, the natural-science, the human-science and the trade-museum functions of the colonial research apparatus. The *Afdeeling Handelsmuseum* (Trade Museum Section) of the Koloniaal Instituut, which would in the 1920s conduct the [[Atjeh patchouli experiments (1921-1925)|Atjeh patchouli experiments]], was a direct institutional descendant of the Maatschappij's natural-science research programme.

## Significance

The Maatschappij ter Bevordering van het Natuurkundig Onderzoek der Nederlandsche Koloniën is significant for the encyclopedia of Aceh history as the named sponsor of the offprint of [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]] — the principal Dutch-language technical-and-ethnographic study of the rice economy of Atjeh. The Society was the natural-science parent body of the colonial-research programme that, in the early twentieth century, was absorbed into the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] at Amsterdam; the Society's natural-science succession is thus the institutional ancestor of the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum|Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] of the Koloniaal Instituut, which would conduct the Atjeh patchouli experiments of 1921-1925.

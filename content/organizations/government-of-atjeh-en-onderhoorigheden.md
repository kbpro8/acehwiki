The **Government of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** ("Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden"; Government of Aceh and Dependencies) was the Netherlands Indian territorial administration of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from [[1873]] to the end of the colonial period — headed successively by military and civil commandants (1873–1878), governors "likewise military commandant" (1878–1881), civilian governors ([[1881]]–[[1884]]), civil-and-military governors (1884–[[1918]]) and again a civil governor (from [[27 September]] [[1918]]) — whose fifty years of economic statecraft form the subject of [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s *[[Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh]]* ([[1923]]).

## Extent

Only the department Groot-Atjeh and, of the Dependencies, the sub-department [[Singkel]] (but not the landscape [[Troemon]]) and most of the department- and sub-department capitals belonged to the directly-administered territory; in the Dependencies more than one hundred so-called self-governing landscapes were found, in which, however, "de invloed van den besturenden Europeeschen ambtenaar in alle opzichten overheerschend is" ("the influence of the governing European official is in every respect dominant") (footnote, p. 25).

## Organization and personnel

- **1873–1878:** military and civil commandants ([[J. L. J. H. Pel|Colonel Pel]], Major-General [[Diemont]]), with the judicial competence for Europeans exercised by the assistant-resident (with the notary's office and registry of births, deaths and marriages, [[1875]]), the opium- and gambling-farms from [[1876]], and indirect native taxation (*hacil*, coffee duty) from [[1878]].
- **1878:** [[General van der Heijden|Karel van der Heijden]] becomes first "Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, tevens militair bevelhebber"; the civil formation at the end of 1878 comprises 1 provincial secretary, 4 assistant-residents ([[Koeta Radja]], [[Sigli]], [[Idi]], [[Meulaboh]]) and 8 controllers (at Koeta Radja, [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]], [[Kroeëng Raba]], [[Samalanga]], Idi and Meulaboh) — the so-called "bestuurs-civilisatie".
- **1881–1884:** the [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|civil experiment]], with Governors [[A. Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] and [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]]; the province is placed under the Court of Justice at [[Padang]] (ordinance of [[14 March]] [[1881]]), the armed police corps is instituted, and the open-coast trade measures are taken and (29 May [[1883]]) withdrawn.
- **1884–1918:** unified civil-and-military governors: [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] ([[13 September]] [[1884]]), [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] (end [[1886]]), [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe van Meerdervoort]] ([[23 April]] [[1891]]), [[General Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]], Commissioner [[General Vetter|Vetter]] ([[1896]]), [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] ([[1898]] — with Dr. C. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] as adviser, invested by circular with the authority of the former resident), [[J. C. van der Wyck|Van der Wyck]] ([[11 June]] [[1904]]), [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] ([[6 May]] [[1905]]) and [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] ([[10 June]] [[1908]] — [[1918]]), who reorganized the province into five departments under assistant-residents with many sub-departments.
- **1918–:** the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|definitive separation]]; [[Van Sluys|A. G. H. van Sluys]] first civilian governor from 27 September 1918, styled in the book "Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden" (during his leave, [[Frijling]] acted as Government Commissioner until early [[1923]]).

## Instruments of state

The Government's pacification worked through public works (roads, bridges, tramway — see [[Atjeh Tram]]; waterworks; lighthouses), through fiscal and commercial instruments (the [[Opium and gambling farms in Atjeh|farms]], [[Taxation in Atjeh|hacil and other duties]], the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]], licences), through currency and credit (the [[Javasche Bank te Koetaradja|Javasche Bank agency]], the [[People's credit in Atjeh|afdeelingsbanken and kampong banks]], the landscape and sub-department treasuries), through services ([[Education in Atjeh|education]], [[Public health in Atjeh|public health]], agriculture advice, veterinary service), through legal organization (the moesapats and the supervision of native judicature; the [[Korte verklaring]] administered to the landscape chiefs), and through contract policy with private enterprise ([[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|Sabang harbour]], timber and agricultural concessions — 141 agriculture and c. 80 forest concessions by [[1918]]).

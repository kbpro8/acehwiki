**The Atjehsche vloot** — the Atjehnese fleet, the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s principal instrument of sea-power — was the naval force by which, in [[1539]], the [[Sultan of Atjeh (16th century)|sultan of Atjeh]] projected his power across the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] and brought the kingdom of [[Aroe]] to its fall. The fleet that descended on Aroe in October [[1539]] is the only Atjehnese fleet of the early sixteenth century whose size, commander, and operational purpose are recorded by a European eyewitness — [[Mendez Pinto]] — and it is thus the foundational naval organisation of the Atjehnese sultanate in Western record.

## The fleet of 1539

[[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]], an eyewitness in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]], records the strength of the [[Atjehsche vloot|fleet]] as "about 130 vessels" (*wel 130 vaartuigen*), commanded by [[Alleredin Mahomet]], "beau frère du mesme Roy, marié avec une sienne coeur", who also held the office of "Gouverneur du Royaume de Baarros" — i.e. governor of [[Barus|Barus]] on the Sumatran west coast. The fleet arrived in the river of [[Poelo Kampi|Panetican]] three days after Pinto's own arrival in Aroe; the day after Pinto's arrival, word had reached the [[Radja of Aroe|radja]] that the Atjehnese fleet would arrive in eight days.

## Operational purpose

The strategic purpose of the [[Atjehsche vloot|fleet of 1539]] was, as Pinto succinctly records, to take [[Aroe]]: "to the possession of Aroe Atjeh was much attached; it was the station by which Atjeh was able to command the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] and to deny Portuguese ships and other traders the passage to [[Sunda]], the [[Moluccas]] and [[China]], and to enjoy for itself all the advantages of that trade". The fleet was thus the instrument by which the Atjehnese sultanate sought to close the Strait to Portuguese shipping and to take for itself the commerce that had previously run through Malacca.

## Outcome

After [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]'s departure the [[Atjehsche vloot|fleet]] forced the fall of [[Aroe]]: treason by an Atjeh-bribed *Cacis* opened the gates, the [[Radja of Aroe|radja]] was killed, and the kingdom was absorbed into the Atjehnese sphere. The [[Queen of Aroe|queen]]'s subsequent flight to [[Malacca]], [[Bentan]], and [[Djohor|Johor]] brought a Johorese counter-attack that briefly retook Aroe — but Aroe was finally lost for good, and with it the last independent check on Atjehnese command of the Strait.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Atjehsche vloot of [[1539]] is the foundational naval organisation of the Atjehnese sultanate in Western record — the named fleet, with a named commander ([[Alleredin Mahomet]]) and a named operational purpose (command of the Strait of Malacca), against which all later Atjehnese naval actions, down to the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of the nineteenth century, may be measured. Its size (130 vessels) and the breadth of its commander's portfolio (royal kinship, fleet command, and the governorship of [[Barus|Barus]]) together mark the Atjehnese sultanate as a sea-power of the first rank in the early sixteenth century.

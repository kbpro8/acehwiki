The **Oost-Indisch Leger** — the Netherlands East Indies Army, abbreviated by [[G. B. Hooijer]] on his own title-page as *O. I. Leger* — was the colonial army that garrisoned the temporary posts of [[Groot-Atjeh]] described in *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]*.

## Composition of the outpost garrisons

Hooijer's plates record garrisons of mixed European and Asian composition. At [[Kroeng Gloempang]] he identified Javanese wearing shoes not part of their equipment but indispensable in the heavily overgrown terrain; a tall young European with a bold face; two non-commissioned officers; a couple of sturdy Dutch lads without their uniform coats for the heat; and a native soldier in a blue check shirt and short working trousers. At the bivouacs by the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]] near [[Anagaloeng]], soldiers of every skin colour — *negers, inlanders, *sinjo* (Eurasian)'s en blanke Europeanen* — floundered together in the water. An old [[Ambonese soldiers in the Atjeh outposts|Ambonese]] veteran at [[Toengkoeb]] supplied the garrison's chief entertainment.

Garrison strengths were small: two officers and fifty men at [[Lam Koenjit]] and at [[Toengkoeb]]; three officers and seventy-five men at [[Tjot Rang]]; a company of infantry with a detachment of fortress artillery at [[Kroeng Gloempang]]. Larger formations named include the [[3de Bataljon Infanterie|3rd battalion]] under [[Overste Sievers]] and the two columns totalling 2,700 men that relieved [[Tjot Rang]] on [[16 April]] [[1896]].

## Conditions of service

Hooijer described the routine of the posts in detail — reveille at half past five, coffee, soup with rice and *sambal* (chilli relish) at ten, the second meal at half past four, the *[[Oorlam|oorlam]]* before both, the fourteen-day *[[Detacheering|detacheering]]*, pay accumulated and drawn at [[Kota Radja]], and the freedom of dress permitted at isolated stations — as well as the hazards: malaria in the marshes of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]], mosquitoes at [[Lam Koenjit]], and the absence of medical officers at the smaller posts.

## Hooijer's tribute

He concluded that these were the men who upheld the Dutch flag in [[Atjeh]] as in the whole of the Indies: *Zij met hun 30 à 40 duizend bewaren de rust onder de millioenen onderdanen, die onze jonge Koningin kan tellen in haar Indisch rijk* — thirty or forty thousand of them preserved order among the millions of subjects the young Queen [[Queen Wilhelmina|Wilhelmina]] counted in her Indies empire. He attributed this to their exertion, courage and contempt of death, their indifference to danger and misery, and their cheerfulness in spite of the mind-killing isolation of the outposts, and ended: *Eere aan hen!*

**The Indies Army** (Dutch: *Het Indische Leger*; later the *Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger* or KNIL) was the colonial army of the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands-Indian]] government, comprising European and native troops with African and Ambonese auxiliaries, charged with the defence and expansion of the Dutch colonial possessions in the Indonesian archipelago. In the period covered by the source work the army was commanded by Lieutenant-general [[W. E. Kroesen|W. E. Kroesen]] until his death, then by Lieutenant-general [[H. G. Boumeester|H. G. Boumeester]]; its principal engagement was the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]-[[1903|1903]], which "robbed the Indies army of a good part of its fit cadres and its best soldiers."

## Organisation

In the early [[1870s]] the Indies army had no general staff — a deficiency that contributed directly to the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[April 1873|April 1873]]. Already in [[July 1871]] the commander of the Indies army, Lieutenant-general [[W. E. Kroesen|W. E. Kroesen]], had instructed the then colonel [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]], military commandant of [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]], to collect all possible military data about [[Acheh|Atjeh]]; at the same time a proposal to send a captain of the Indies army aboard the *Djambi* to inform himself locally of conditions had been refused by the Indies government. By the time of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] of [[November 1873|November 1873]] the lessons had been learned: all troops were equipped with breech-loading rifles, the artillery was considerably reinforced, and the expeditionary force was increased to 6354 infantry besides officers, with cavalry, artillery and engineers.

## The Cost of the Atjeh-oorlog

The Atjeh-oorlog placed a severe strain on the Indies army. After the departure of [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]] on [[26 April]] [[26 April]] [[1874]] a force of 128 officers, 1788 Europeans, 176 Africans, 489 Ambonese and 698 natives remained under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]]'s command. By [[December 1874]] the army strength in Atjeh had been brought by temporary reinforcements to 4000 infantry; in [[1874]] alone, after the departure of the troops on [[26 April]] [[26 April]], the number of deaths was 902 and the number of evacuated 1917. During [[1875]] 957 dead and 5151 evacuated were counted. By [[March 1877|March 1877]] the strength of the troop force was 351 officers and 9235 rank-and-file, besides the more than 3000 free workers with the engineers.

In the course of [[1878]] a reorganisation of the Dutch troop strength in Atjeh was carried out: a separation was made between garrison- and field-bataljons; by three garrison-bataljons the occupation of the posts was to be provided for; the splitting into right and left halves of bataljons would lapse. In connection with the improved situation the number of troops was also reduced from 348 officers and 9235 rank-and-file to 298 officers and 7730 rank-and-file. After the close of large-scale operations in [[1879]] the strength was further reduced by Indies decree of [[18 May]] [[18 May]] [[1880]] to 253 officers and 6372 rank-and-file, of whom 3131 Europeans.

## The Quality of the Troops

By [[1876]] the quality of the troops had seriously declined. "The Atjeh-oorlog, which had now lasted nearly three years, had robbed the Indies army of a good part of its fit cadres and its best soldiers; to make up the losses the requirements for the various ranks had been lowered, and the suitability of the under-officers and corporals thus obtained as patrol leaders consequently often left something to be desired. The need for soldiers at the theatre of war had to be met by newly recruited men; among the Europeans were many foreigners, who — the Germans excepted — were mostly of rather poor quality, and all, Europeans as well as natives, were scarcely drilled when they already had to take part in the struggle. Our soldiers were at that time therefore unfit to be considered for the successful conduct of the small war."

## The Commanders of the Indies Army

The commanders of the Indies army during the period covered by the source work were:

- Lieutenant-general [[W. E. Kroesen|W. E. Kroesen]] (until his death), who had instructed [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]] in [[July 1871]] to collect military data about Atjeh and who had concluded before the first expedition that "the troops would probably not even come to fight; that the matter would soon be over, and this was also necessary, in view of the bad monsoon to be expected."
- Lieutenant-general [[H. G. Boumeester|H. G. Boumeester]], who arrived in Atjeh on [[19 August]] [[19 August]] [[1879]] to inform himself personally of the state of affairs, and who in his report paid great tribute to [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]]: "The nation is undoubtedly greatly indebted to him."

**The guides and interpreters of the Lamtoba column** — *gidsen en tolken* — were the eight Achehnese and native auxiliaries attached to the expedition staff of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] of [[1879]] for the guidance of the troops through country of which, as [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]] record in their *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]), practically nothing was known: "Toekoe Ali, Toekoe Mas, Toekoe Ladang Baroe, Si Nja Garu, Oetoes Ja, Mas Saäd, Mas Daud, Nja Assan."

## Service of the campaign

The guides were sent ashore first, with the earliest light of [[27 July]] [[1879]], in a separate boat, to reassure the population before the debarkation began. On the march into the valley of [[Lamtoba]] the guides were found to be in doubt of the way, and the second embassy of the [[Imam of Lamtoba]] had to be detained to point out the best road; on the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|march to Selimoen]] the luitenant [[A. G. Popelier|Popelier]] remained with the guides at the point of the column throughout. From their information the distance from the Lamtoba valley to [[Selimoen]] was reckoned at three day-marches — the datum on which the chief of staff's famous calculation of the coolie requirement was built — and in the final appraisal of the march of [[14 August]] their estimate placed Selimoen still three hours beyond the kampong of [[Lam-Lepong]]. It was one of these guides who, by the account which [[Panglima Banten]] later gave in explanation of his flight, was said to have stolen a sum of money from the Rijksbestierder in the bivak at Lamtoba — the incident on which that chief's dramatic escape and surrender turned.

**The Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** — also referred to as the Civil and Military Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies — was the combined civil-and-military head of the Dutch colonial administration in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] during the spring of [[1896]], an office combining the powers of a governor over the civil administration with those of a military commandant over the troops. Held by Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff until his honorable relief on [[7 April]] [[1896]], the office was abolished during the [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]] period of [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] ([[7 April]]–[[28 June]] [[1896]]), its civil functions temporarily exercised by Resident K. F. H. van Langen and its military functions by Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort — both under Vetter's orders — and was restored on [[28 June]] [[1896]] with Kolonel Stemfoort (provisionally) as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur*.

## Background and Formation

The office of Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was created to combine in a single hand the civil and military authority over the troubled Atjeh territory, in recognition of the fact that the protracted [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] could not be conducted under a divided command. The Governor-General [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck]] of the Netherlands East Indies appointed the holder, who — once installed — exercised both the civil power of a provincial administrator and the military power of a troop commander. On [[1 January]] [[1896]] the office was held by Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff, who resided at his home in [[Koeta Radja]] and conducted conferences there. The office's military wing was supported by the Army Commander in Batavia, who could dispatch a senior officer to Atjeh in the event of crisis.

## Role in the Atjeh War

The office of Civiel en Militair Gouverneur was held by Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff at the moment of [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. Deykerhoff, who "in any case little inclined to give credence to the various reports" (was anyway little inclined to give credence to the various reports), had ignored Controleur 2e kl. Gisolf's warning about Umar's "more than suspicious attitude" and had even allowed Umar to attend a conference at his office on [[28 March]] [[1896]]. The defection was thus, for Deykerhoff, "*eene bijna ongelooflijke verrassing*" (an almost unbelievable surprise). Deykerhoff immediately ordered the counter-measures of [[29 March]] [[1896]] — *stoomtram* reinforcements to [[Lamdjamoe]] and [[Lampeneroet]], marine reinforcement of [[Oleh-leh]], *hinderlagen* to protect [[Koeta Radja]] — but his standing was broken. On [[7 April]] [[1896]] the steamers *Carpentier* and *Bantam* arrived with [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]]; Deykerhoff was "*eervol ontheven van zijne functien als Civiel en Militair Gouverneur*" (honorably relieved of his functions as Civil and Military Governor). Per the *Koloniaal Verslag*: "*Waar de afval van Tockoe Djohan, aanstonds tot so ver strekkende gevolgen aanleiding gaf, dat al het sedert 1893 verkregene, was, as it were, set on loose screws, moest wel ernstige twijfel rijzen, of de Civiele en Militaire Gouverneur den toestand te voren wel inderdaad juist beoordeeld had…*" (Since the defection of Tockoe Djohan gave rise at once to such far-reaching consequences that all that had been gained since 1893 was, as it were, set loose, serious doubt had to arise whether the Civil and Military Governor had in fact correctly judged the situation beforehand...). During the [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]] period ([[7 April]]–[[28 June]] [[1896]]), the office was held in abeyance, its civil functions exercised by Resident K. F. H. van Langen and its military functions by Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort, both under Vetter's orders. With Vetter's [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|departure on 28 June 1896]], the office was restored, with Stemfoort appointed (provisionally) as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* and Van Langen relieved of his temporary civil-affairs functions.

## Composition and Strength

The office of Civiel en Militair Gouverneur was held successively by: Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff (until [[7 April]] [[1896]]); *in abeyance* under the [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]] ([[7 April]]–[[28 June]] [[1896]]), with civil functions exercised by Resident [[K. F. H. van Langen (Resident)|K. F. H. van Langen]] and military functions by Kolonel [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|J. W. Stemfoort]], both under Lt.-Gen. [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|J. A. Vetter]]; and Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort (provisionally, from [[28 June]] [[1896]]).

## Significance

The office of Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden embodied the Dutch conviction that only a unified civil-military command could conduct the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Deykerhoff's dismissal on [[7 April]] [[1896]] was the most senior political casualty of [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection and a public admission that the Dutch policy of relying on Umar's *legioen* — adopted in [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|September 1893]] — had failed. The temporary abolition of the office during the Vetter *Regeeringscommissaris* period reflected the Dutch government's decision to suspend the Atjeh administration's autonomy and place it under direct Army-Commander control. The restoration of the office with Kolonel Stemfoort on [[28 June]] [[1896]] marked the Dutch government's judgment that, after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|V Moekims Montassik tuchtiging]], the situation had stabilized enough to return to a unified civil-military command.

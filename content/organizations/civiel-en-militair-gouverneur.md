**The Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** — the Civil and Military Governor of [[Atjeh]] and [[Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] — was the combined civil-and-military head of the Dutch colonial administration in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]], an office instituted by the founding *Instructie voor den militairen, tevens civielen bevelhebber te Atjeh* drawn up by [[Van Swieten|Generaal van Swieten]] and approved by Besluit of the Gouverneur-Generaal of Nederlandsch-Indië of [[26 May]] [[1874]] No. 1, vesting in a single officer both the military command of the Dutch forces in the Atjeh territory and the supreme civil administration of the occupied coast and interior. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] opens the diagnostic chapter of his [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* by cataloguing the eleven Governors who, in the twenty years between the conquest of the *kraton* and the book's publication, successively held the office — a catalogue he adduces as proof that the colonial *Regeering* had, by constantly changing the office-holder and the policy, "stijft den Atjeher in hooge mate in zijn verzet" (strengthened the Atjeher greatly in his resistance). In [[1896]], in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]], the office was held by Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff, honorably relieved on [[7 April]] [[1896]], temporarily abolished under the [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]] period of [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] ([[7 April]]–[[28 June]] [[1896]]), and restored on [[28 June]] [[1896]] with Kolonel Stemfoort (provisionally) as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur*.

## Establishment and the Van Swieten Instructie (1874)

The office was formally instituted in [[1874]] by the *Instructie voor den militairen, tevens civielen bevelhebber te Atjeh* — the Instruction for the military, at the same time civil, commander in Atjeh — drawn up by the first Governor, [[Van Swieten|Generaal van Swieten]], in consultation with the outgoing Gouverneur-Generaal Loudon and the Minister van Koloniën Fransen van de Putte, and approved by Besluit of the Gouverneur-Generaal of Nederlandsch-Indië of [[26 May]] [[1874]] No. 1. The pairing of the civil and military functions in a single officer was deliberate: the *Instructie* presided over the concentrated defensive stelling at [[Kota Radja|Kotja-Radja]] — Van Swieten "liet 2500 man achter, om daarmede te bezetten en actief te verdedigen de geconcentreerde stelling Kotja-Radja" — while *art. 5* of the *Instructie* governed the civil handling of the coastal *Onderhoorigheden*: "In aanzien van de Onderhoorigheden van Atjeh onderhoudt de militaire en civiele bevelhebber met hen, die onze souvereiniteit hebben erkend, zooveel mogelijk de betrekkingen en tracht hen, die nog niet tot ons zijn toegetreden, door overreding of door blokkade te brengen tot het aflegging der verklaring". The Governor was, in short, simultaneously commander-in-chief and chief administrator — a combination that, in Van Heutsz's narrative, gave the office its constitutional weight and made its holder the single point at which Dutch policy in Atjeh either cohered or fractured.

## The eleven Governors under Van Heutsz's 1893 account

Van Heutsz's catalogue of the office-holders is one of the structural pillars of the [[1893]] tract. Setting the catalogue against the simultaneous rotation of Gouverneurs-Generaal and Ministers van Koloniën — "In dien zelfden tijd hadden wij 5 Gouverneurs-Generaal: Loudon, van Lansberge, [']s Jacob, van Rees, Pijnacker Hordijk, en wat erger is: 13 Ministers van Koloniën" — he presents the eleven Governors as the local symptom of a colonial *Regeering* that did not know its own mind. The catalogue, as printed on page 6 of the 1893 tract, reads:

> "Aanvangende met den Generaal van Swieten hebben wij nu den elen Gouverneur van Atjeh: van Swieten, Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont, van der Heijden, Pruijs van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias, Demmeni, van Teijn, Pompe van Meerdervoort, Deijkerhof".

The eleven Governors, in Van Heutsz's order, are:

1. [[Van Swieten|van Swieten]] — first Governor, [[1873]]–[[1874]]; architect of the 1874 *Instructie* and the concentrated Kotja-Radja defence.
2. [[Pel (governor)|Pel]] — Governor [[1874]]–early [[1876]]; author of the [[Pel's plan (30 April 1875)|30 April 1875 plan]] for the coastal closure of [[Groot-Atjeh]]; died during his [[1876]] campaign.
3. [[Wiggers van Kerchem]] — Governor [[1876]]; dismissed for resisting Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge's wish to coerce the Oostkust statelets.
4. [[Diemont]] — Governor November [[1876]]–[[1877]]; completed the Pel coastal closure in February [[1877]]; evacuated sick [[30 June]] [[1877]].
5. [[Van der Heijden|van der Heijden]] — Governor [[1877]]–[[1881]] (and again [[1886]]–[[1887]]); conqueror of the [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]]; dismissed early [[1881]].
6. Pruijs van der Hoeven — Governor [[1881]]–[[1883]]; first holder of the *Civiel Gouverneur* form of the office after the [[Van der Heijden]] dismissal.
7. [[Laging Tobias]] — Governor March [[1883]]–February [[1884]]; introduced the May [[1883]] *scheepvaartregeling*; advocate of restoring the sultanate under [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]].
8. [[Demmeni]] — Governor [[1884]]–[[1886]]; died in office at the end of [[1886]].
9. [[Van Teijn|van Teijn]] — Governor end-[[1886]]–[[1891]]; re-imposed the closure of the Noord- en Westkust; author of the [[23 November]] [[1891]] warning letter that opens the 1893 tract.
10. Pompe van Meerdervoort — Governor [[1891]]–[[1892]].
11. Deijkerhof — Governor [[1892]]–[[1893]], the eleventh and last holder named in the 1893 tract.

## The combined civil-military authority

The constitutional signature of the office, as Van Heutsz's narrative reveals it, is the personal union of military command and civil administration in a single officer. The clearest single documentary witness is the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] by which [[Van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] declared *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* — the whole of Greater Atjeh subdued — and which he signs with the full formal title of the office as the source prints it:

> "Kota-Radja, 5 September 1879. De generaal-majoor, adjudant des Konings in buitengewonen dienst, Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden. K. VAN DER HEUDEN."

The formula *Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber* — Governor at the same time military commander — captures the dual character of the office: the holder was a serving general-officer of the Royal Netherlands Indies Army, invested with full military command of the forces in the Atjeh theatre, who simultaneously exercised the supreme civil jurisdiction over the occupied territory and the recognised coastal statelets. The combination was the constitutional device by which the colonial *Regeering* sought to fuse the conduct of the war with the administration of the territory won by the war — and the device by which, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, every dismissal of a Governor (Van Swieten in [[1874]], Pel by death in [[1876]], Wiggers van Kerchem by replacement in [[1876]], Van der Heijden in early [[1881]], Laging Tobias in February [[1884]]) produced not merely a personal change but a *bestuursverandering* (change of administration) that "wijziging brengt in de bestuurs-inzichten" — brought a modification in the administrative insights — and so confirmed the Atjeher in his conviction that the Dutch did not know what they wanted.

## Evolution after the pacification of Groot-Atjeh (1879–1893)

The office was reshaped by the pacification of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1879]]. The Van der Heijden dagorder of [[5 September]] [[1879]] having declared *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* — and the Commandant of the Indian Army having confirmed in his September [[1879]] visit that the Generaal "met recht zeggen mocht, dat geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder was gebracht" — the military rationale for the combined civil-military form of the office weakened. The dismissal of Van der Heijden "in het begin van 1881 op onverantwoordelijke wijze" — in the beginning of 1881 in an irresponsible manner — was followed by the substitution of a *Civiel gouverneur* for the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur*. Van Heutsz records the transition in passing, on page 22 of the 1893 tract, when he describes "diens vervanging door een Civiel gouverneur, gevolgd door proclameering van den vrede" — the replacement [of the military governor] by a Civil Governor, followed by the proclamation of the peace. The shift did not abolish the office — Pruijs van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias, Demmeni, and Van Teijn continue to be catalogued by Van Heutsz as Governors of Atjeh — but it detached the civil administration from the immediate conduct of military operations, and so inaugurated the [[1881]]–[[1883]] *Civiel Gouverneur* regime that Laging Tobias would later describe, in the December [[1886]] *Indische Gids*, as having inherited a situation in which "onze toestand in Atjeh bijna hopeloos was". The office Van Teijn would inherit at the end of [[1886]] was, in form, the *Civiel Gouverneur* office — but Van Teijn's own re-imposition of the *sluiting* of the Noord- en Westkust, and his *actieve verdediging* of the linie from September [[1889]], restored to the office much of the military character the [[1881]] transition had attenuated.

The *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* form was, however, formally reinstated in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] when [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] retired and the government — convinced that under the new circumstances of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] there was no longer place for civil government alone — decided to return to the earlier arrangement of civil and military authority in one hand, "*doch nu helaas op de puinhoopen van het gesloopte gebouw onzer macht onder van der Heijden*" (but now alas on the ruins of the demolished building of our power under Van der Heijden). Colonel [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] accordingly took office as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur*, succeeded by [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] ([[December 1886|December]] [[1886]]–[[April 1891|April]] [[1891]]), [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]] ([[May 1891|May]] [[1891]]–[[January 1892|January]] [[1892]]), and [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]] ([[January 1892|January]] [[1892]]–[[March 1896|March]] [[1896]]) — the same Deijkerhof/Deijkerhoff named by Van Heutsz as the eleventh Governor of 1893.

## The 1896 crisis: Deykerhoff, Vetter, Stemfoort

The office of Civiel en Militair Gouverneur was held by Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff (Deijkerhoff) at the moment of [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. Deykerhoff, who "in any case little inclined to give credence to the various reports" (was anyway little inclined to give credence to the various reports), had ignored Controleur 2e kl. Gisolf's warning about Umar's "more than suspicious attitude" and had even allowed Umar to attend a conference at his office on [[28 March]] [[1896]]. The defection was thus, for Deykerhoff, "*eene bijna ongelooflijke verrassing*" (an almost unbelievable surprise). Deykerhoff immediately ordered the counter-measures of [[29 March]] [[1896]] — *stoomtram* reinforcements to [[Lamdjamoe]] and [[Lampeneroet]], marine reinforcement of [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]], *hinderlagen* to protect [[Koeta Radja]] — but his standing was broken. On [[7 April]] [[1896]] the steamers *Carpentier* and *Bantam* arrived with [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]]; Deykerhoff was "*eervol ontheven van zijne functien als Civiel en Militair Gouverneur*" (honorably relieved of his functions as Civil and Military Governor). Per the *Koloniaal Verslag*: "*Waar de afval van Tockoe Djohan, aanstonds tot so ver strekkende gevolgen aanleiding gaf, dat al het sedert 1893 verkregene, was, as it were, set on loose screws, moest wel ernstige twijfel rijzen, of de Civiele en Militaire Gouverneur den toestand te voren wel inderdaad juist beoordeeld had…*" (Since the defection of Tockoe Djohan gave rise at once to such far-reaching consequences that all that had been gained since 1893 was, as it were, set loose, serious doubt had to arise whether the Civil and Military Governor had in fact correctly judged the situation beforehand...). During the [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]] period ([[7 April]]–[[28 June]] [[1896]]), the office was held in abeyance, its civil functions exercised by Resident K. F. H. van Langen and its military functions by Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort, both under Vetter's orders. With Vetter's [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|departure on 28 June 1896]], the office was restored, with Stemfoort appointed (provisionally) as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* and Van Langen relieved of his temporary civil-affairs functions.

## Composition and Strength

The office of Civiel en Militair Gouverneur was held successively by: Generaal van Swieten ([[1873]]–[[1874]]); [[Pel (governor)|Pel]] ([[1874]]–early [[1876]]); [[Wiggers van Kerchem]] ([[1876]]); [[Diemont]] (November [[1876]]–[[1877]]); [[Van der Heijden|van der Heijden]] ([[1877]]–[[1881]], and again [[1886]]–[[1887]]); Pruijs van der Hoeven ([[1881]]–[[1883]]); [[Laging Tobias]] (March [[1883]]–February [[1884]]); [[Demmeni]] (September [[1884]]–December [[1886]]); [[Van Teijn|van Teijn]] (December [[1886]]–April [[1891]]); Pompe van Meerdervoort (May [[1891]]–January [[1892]]); Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff (January [[1892]]–[[7 April]] [[1896]]); *in abeyance* under the [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]] ([[7 April]]–[[28 June]] [[1896]]), with civil functions exercised by Resident [[K. F. H. van Langen (Resident)|K. F. H. van Langen]] and military functions by Kolonel [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|J. W. Stemfoort]], both under Lt.-Gen. [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|J. A. Vetter]]; and Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort (provisionally, from [[28 June]] [[1896]]).

## Significance

The office of *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* is, in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] narrative, the institutional protagonist of the Atjeh-oorlog — the office through which the colonial *Regeering*'s alternating policies were locally executed and locally reversed. Its significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record rests on five things: it was the office instituted by the [[Van Swieten's instructie (22 April 1874)|1874 Instructie]] that founded the Dutch civil administration of Atjeh on the concentrated defensive model of Kotja-Radja; it was the office whose eleven holders between [[1874]] and [[1893]] — van Swieten, Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont, van der Heijden, Pruijs van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias, Demmeni, van Teijn, Pompe van Meerdervoort, Deijkerhof — Van Heutsz catalogues as the local register of the colonial *Regeering*'s inability to follow a single policy; it was the office whose formal title *Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, inscribed on the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]], made the personal union of military command and civil administration the constitutional device of the Dutch occupation; it was the office whose post-[[1881]] attenuation into a *Civiel Gouverneur* form — after the "onverantwoordelijke" dismissal of Van der Heijden — inaugurated the [[1881]]–[[1883]] regime that Laging Tobias himself would describe as having found Atjeh "bijna hopeloos", and whose formal restoration as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] marked the abandonment of the civil-government experiment; and it was the office whose 1896 temporary abolition — Deykerhoff's honorable relief on [[7 April]] [[1896]] and the substitution of Vetter's *Regeeringscommissaris* — was the most senior political casualty of [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection and a public admission that the Dutch policy of relying on Umar's *legioen* — adopted in [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|September 1893]] — had failed. The 1893 tract is, in institutional terms, an indictment of the office's successive holders' treatment by Batavia — a plea, in Van Teijn's words, that "de Gouverneur van Atjeh door eene bindende instructie te dwingen de door Haar vast te stellen politiek te volgen" — the Governor of Atjeh should be bound by a binding instruction to follow the policy fixed by the Government — and that within that binding instruction "de Gouverneur … de handen vrij moet hebben en moet kunnen beschikken over de ten dienste staande middelen". The temporary abolition of the office during the Vetter *Regeeringscommissaris* period reflected the Dutch government's decision to suspend the Atjeh administration's autonomy and place it under direct Army-Commander control. The restoration of the office with Kolonel Stemfoort on [[28 June]] [[1896]] marked the Dutch government's judgment that, after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|V Moekims Montassik tuchtiging]], the situation had stabilized enough to return to a unified civil-military command.

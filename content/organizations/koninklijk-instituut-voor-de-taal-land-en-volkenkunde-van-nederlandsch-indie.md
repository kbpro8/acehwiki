**The Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië** — the Royal Institute of Philology, Geography, and Ethnology of the Netherlands Indies — was the principal Dutch learned society for the study of the languages, geography, ethnology, and history of the Netherlands Indies, founded in [[1851]] by the former Minister **[[J. C. Baud|Jean Chrétien Baud]]** and placed from its inception under the royal protection of the Dutch royal house. Headquartered from the early twentieth century at Van Galenstraat 14 in [['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] (The Hague), the Institute was, throughout the colonial period and into the post-colonial era, the central metropolitan institution for Dutch Indies scholarship, publishing the quarterly *[[Bijdragen tot de Taal, Land en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch Indie|Bijdragen tot de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië]]* (the *B.K.I.*), the annual *Literatuur-Overzicht*, and a series of separate *Verhandelingen* (Transactions), and maintaining the richly-stocked *Koloniale Bibliotheek* (Colonial Library) that was the working collection of Dutch colonial scholarship.

## Foundation and purpose

The Institute was founded in [[1851]] by **[[J. C. Baud|Jean Chrétien Baud]]**, the former Minister of the Colonies (1840–1848) and a former Governor-General of the Netherlands Indies (1833–1836). From its inception it stood under the *beschermheerschap* (protectorate) of the Dutch royal house, and it was supported by a broad group of notables both in the Netherlands and overseas. Its purpose, as stated in its original programme, was to place itself "in dienst van Wetenschap en Maatschappij" — in the service of Science and Society — and to promote, "op krachtige wijze," the knowledge of the Netherlands Indies, both East and West.

## Publications

The Institute's principal publication was the *[[Bijdragen tot de Taal, Land en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch Indie|Bijdragen tot de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië]]* (Contributions to the Philology, Geography, and Ethnology of the Netherlands Indies) — the *B.K.I.* — a quarterly journal that, by [[1940]], had reached deel 99. The *B.K.I.* was the principal metropolitan venue for the philology and ethnology of the Netherlands Indies, and it carried, alongside articles, the editions of vernacular texts — including the [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] edition of the [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh]] (*B.K.I.* deel 98, [[1939]]) — that were the stock-in-trade of Dutch Indies philology. The journal was exchanged with the principal scientific institutions and libraries in the Netherlands and abroad, with which the Institute maintained a *ruilverkeer* (exchange traffic).

From [[1910]] the Institute also published the *Adatrechtbundels* (Customary-Law Bundles), which had reached 40 volumes by the late 1930s and which contained important material for both adat-law and ethnological scholarship. The Institute likewise published a series of separate *Verhandelingen* (Transactions), of which — by [[1940]] — the *De Factory der Oostindische Compagnie te Patani* by Dr. H. Terpstra and *De Val van Sora* by Dr. E. J. van den Berg were recent volumes. Among the Institute's separate publications were the linguistic works of Dr. [[N. Adriani|N. Adriani]], the *Jan Pietersz. Coen* biography by Prof. Dr. H. T. Colenbrander, the *Verspreide Geschriften* (Collected Writings) of Prof. Dr. H. Kern, the *Hindoe-Javaansche Geschiedenis* by Prof. Dr. N. J. Krom, and the biography of Cornelis Jansz. Speelman by Dr. F. W. Stapel.

From the early twentieth century the Institute published an annual *Literatuur-Overzicht* (literature survey) of the entire field of the philology, geography, ethnology, and history of the Netherlands Indies. The [[1939]] survey — the fourth annual, compiled by [[H. van Meurs|H. van Meurs]] and published in [[1940]] — is the source for the corpus's entries on the [[1939]] Atjeh scholarship.

## The Koloniale Bibliotheek

The Institute maintained, at its premises at Van Galenstraat 14, the *Koloniale Bibliotheek* (Colonial Library) — a richly-stocked library that contained many rare and old works and that was continuously expanded with the latest colonial literature from home and abroad. A four-volume, 2,367-page catalogue of the library had been compiled, together with an alphabetical card system. The library offered a quiet reading and study room (open daily from 10 to 4) where numerous periodicals from home and abroad, Netherlands and Indies official publications, and Indies daily newspapers lay for consultation. The catalogue was available to all, and — in order to satisfy the demand for recreational reading — the library also acquired the latest Indische sketches and novels.

The Institute likewise held an important and extensive collection of manuscripts, photographs, plates, and land-maps (*landkaarten*), all at the disposal of the members. Bookworks were lent to members throughout the country, only *porto* being charged. The *Koloniale Bibliotheek* was the working collection on which [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]] drew in compiling the *Literatuur-Overzicht*, and it was the repository to which authors and publishers were invited to deposit a copy of every work of colonial importance.

## Governance

As of [[1940]], the Board (*Bestuur*) of the Institute was composed as follows: Prof. Dr. N. Krom, Chairman (*Voorzitter*); Prof. Dr. Ph. S. van Ronkel, Vice-Chairman (*Onder-Voorzitter*); Mr. E. E. Menten, Treasurer (*Penningmeester*); Dr. F. W. Stapel, Secretary (*Secretaris*); and the members Prof. Dr. J. P. B. de Josselin de Jong, Dr. Alb. C. Kruyt, [[H. T. Damsté|H. T. Damsté]], C. H. de Goeje, Mr. F. D. E. van Ossenbruggen, Prof. Dr. J. Gonda, Prof. Dr. C. C. Berg, and Prof. Mr. J. J. Schrieke. The presence of [[H. T. Damsté|H. T. Damsté]] on the Board — the principal Dutch authority on Atjeh — is a marker of the Institute's engagement with Atjeh scholarship, and it connects the Institute directly to the [[1939]] publication of Damsté's edition of the [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh]] in the Institute's *B.K.I.*

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Koninklijk Instituut is significant as the central metropolitan institution of Dutch Indies scholarship — the publisher of the *B.K.I.* and of the annual *Literatuur-Overzicht* that are the sources for the corpus's entries on the [[1939]] Atjeh scholarship, and the maintainer of the *Koloniale Bibliotheek* on which the survey drew. The Institute's engagement with Atjeh scholarship is marked by the presence of [[H. T. Damsté|H. T. Damsté]] on its Board, by the publication of Damsté's [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh]] in its *B.K.I.*, and by the recording of the [[1939]] Atjeh publications in its *Literatuur-Overzicht*. The Institute's continuity from its [[1851]] foundation through the colonial period and into the post-colonial era makes it the institutional backbone of the Dutch scholarly engagement with Atjeh, and the corpus's entries on the [[1939]] Atjeh scholarship are, in this sense, entries on the harvest of the Institute's late-colonial bibliographic project.

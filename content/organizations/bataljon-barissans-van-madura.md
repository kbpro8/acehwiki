The **[[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|bataljon Barissans van Madura]]** was a battalion of inlandsche (indigenous) hulptroepen raised on the island of [[Madura]] and sent to [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] in two drafts — the first arriving on [[29 January]] [[1874]] during the closing phase of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]], the second on [[10 December]] [[1875]] as part of the reinforcements for [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]]'s great [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|offensive of December 1875 – February 1876]]. The Barissans — together with the [[Javaansche hulptroepen]] of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]] — formed the inlandsche complement to the European troops of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]], and their despatch to Atjeh is repeatedly noted by Borel as a marker of the chronology of the war.

## The First Arrival ([[29 January]] [[1874]])

The first bataljon Barissans van Madura arrived in Atjeh on [[29 January]] [[1874]] — the same day as the [[Reconnaissance of the Atjeh-rivier (29 January 1874)|failed reconnaissance of the Atjeh-rivier]] above the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], in which a colonne of two bataljons infanterie with a batterij veld-artillerie met unexpectedly strong resistance near [[Lampoe Idjoe]] and was forced to retreat with losses of two officers and "ruim 20 minderen" killed or wounded. The arrival of the Barissans — together with the 6de bataljon Infanterie that arrived on [[1 February]] [[1874]] — was part of the belated despatch of elements of the [[Padangsche brigade|Padangsche brigade]] from [[Padang]] to Atjeh, after [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]]'s decision to leave the third brigade behind had been partially reversed by the unfolding of the campaign. The Barissans' despatch to Atjeh at this moment, however, did not lead to offensive employment: by [[15 February]] [[1874]] van Swieten had "stak het zwaard voor goed in de scheede" and applied the *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture). The Javaansche hulptroepen of Mangkoe Negoro and Pakoe Alam were sent back to Java in February [[1874]], but the Barissans of Madura remained and were absorbed into the [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]].

## The Second Arrival and the Great Offensive ([[10 December]] [[1875]])

A second bataljon Barissans van Madura arrived in Atjeh on [[10 December]] [[1875]], followed by the 9de bataljon Infanterie on [[21 December]] [[1875]]. These reinforcements were the troops Pel had been promised by [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]] at the [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Buitenzorg planning session]] of July [[1875]], when the GG agreed to "den oorlog met Atjeh op de meest doortastende wijze te voeren." With the Barissans and the 9de bataljon in hand, Pel was able to launch the great [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims]] on [[26 December]] [[1875]] — three converging colonnes under [[Majoor Vetter|Vetter]], [[Majoor F. J. E. Mekern|F. J. E. Mekern]] and [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|Engel]]. The Barissans served in the colonnes of the offensive: part of the Barissans formed, with the 13de bataljon and one section of artillery, the rechter-colonne under [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|B. E. Mekern]] on [[13 February]] [[1876]]; the remainder of the Barissans formed, with the 12de bataljon and two sections of artillery, the midden-colonne under [[Majoor Jeltes|Jeltes]] advancing from [[Kota Alam]] along the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] against [[Pango]] and [[Longbatta-Mesigit|Longbatta Mesigit]]. On hearing of the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] the same day, B. E. Mekern changed route, marched on [[Atoeh|Atoch]], cleared the terrain and recovered the bodies of [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]]'s detachment for proper burial — a task in which the Barissans would have borne a share.

## Inlandsche Hulptroepen in the Atjeh War

The Barissans van Madura exemplify the role of inlandsche hulptroepen in the Atjeh-oorlog. Raised from the populations of the principalities of the Indies archipelago — Madura, the Javanese principalities under Mangkoe Negoro and Pakoe Alam — they supplemented the European and Ambonese or other "Afrikanen" troops of the regular Indische leger. The Javaansche hulptroepen were sent back to Java in February [[1874]] as part of van Swieten's force reduction; the Barissans, by contrast, were retained, reinforced, and eventually employed in the offensive operations of [[1875]]–[[1876]] that Borel credits to Pel. Their despatch in two drafts, separated by almost two years, marks the two strategic moments at which metropolitan and colonial reinforcement became available: the belated partial reversal of van Swieten's Padang decision in January [[1874]], and the GG's endorsement of Pel's offensive plan in December [[1875]].

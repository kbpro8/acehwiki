**Royal Dutch Shell** is the Anglo-Dutch multinational oil company formed in [[1907]] by the amalgamation of the Royal Dutch Petroleum Company (the Dutch parent of the East Indian oil interests that began with the [[1890]] founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij tot ontginning van Petroleum terreinen in Nederlandsch Indië*) and the Shell Transport and Trading Company (the British oil-transport concern). The amalgamation created the [[B.P.M.]] (Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij) as the Royal Dutch operating subsidiary for the Netherlands East Indies, and the B.P.M. subsequently became the dominant oil company in the Indies — producing 4,544,255 tons of crude oil in [[1940]] (57.3 % of the East Indian total) and operating the great majority of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and northern-Sumatran oil fields, including the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh block]], the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]], the [[Aru Bay|Aru Bay]] field, the [[Telaga Said|Telaga Said]] field and the [[Bulu Telang|Bulu Telang]] field.

## Founding and the 1890 Royal Dutch predecessor

Van Bemmelen's account of the Royal Dutch predecessor's founding reads:

> "[[1890]] the concession became the first oil property of the newly founded 'Koninklijke Maatschappij tot ontginning van Petroleum terreinen in Nederlandsch Indië' (Royal Company for the exploitation of oil fields in the Netherlands East Indies). This concern is now known all over the world by the name of Royal Dutch."

The [[1890]] founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij* — the future Royal Dutch — took place in the immediate aftermath of the [[1885]] [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]]-led drilling success at [[Telaga Said]] on the [[1883]] [[J. Zulkler|Zulkler]] concession. The first oil property of the new company was accordingly the Telaga Said concession itself, which the company acquired in [[1890]] and which became the founding asset of the Royal Dutch. The northern-Sumatran oil industry is, in this sense, the originating locus of the entire Royal Dutch (and subsequently Royal Dutch Shell) oil empire.

## The 1907 amalgamation with Shell

Van Bemmelen's account of the [[1907]] amalgamation reads:

> "Of great importance was the amalgamation of the Royal Dutch and the Shell Oil Company in [[1907]]. A new company was to develop the oil concessions in the Netherlands East Indies and to build and operate refineries for crude oil. This subsidiary company, the 'Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij' (Batavian Oil Company, B.P.M.), has since taken over all interests of the Royal Dutch in the Indian Archipelago."

The [[1907]] amalgamation created the B.P.M. as the operating subsidiary for the East Indian oil interests, and the B.P.M. subsequently took over "all interests of the Royal Dutch in the Indian Archipelago". The Royal Dutch Shell group, with its 60 % Dutch (Royal Dutch) and 40 % British (Shell) ownership structure, became the dominant oil company in the East Indies and one of the most successful and influential oil concerns in the world. The B.P.M., as the group's East Indian operating subsidiary, accordingly became the principal vehicle through which Royal Dutch Shell conducted its East Indian oil business — including the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and northern-Sumatran operations that are the subject of this wiki.

## Royal Dutch Shell operations on Atjeh territory

Through its B.P.M. subsidiary, Royal Dutch Shell operated:

- The **[[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh block]]** (4,626 tons in [[1941]]).
- The **[[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]]** (709,625 tons in [[1940]], 527,590 tons in [[1941]]) — by a wide margin the largest producing field of the North Sumatra oil province.
- The **[[Aru Bay|Aru Bay]]** field (55,924 tons in [[1940]], 55,070 tons in [[1941]]).
- The **[[Telaga Said|Telaga Said]]** field (1,076 tons in [[1940]], 960 tons in [[1941]]) — the founding property of Royal Dutch itself.
- The **[[Bulu Telang|Bulu Telang]]** field (919 tons in [[1940]], 900 tons in [[1941]]).
- The **S. Aru Bay (block)** (2,100 tons in [[1940]], 5,030 tons in [[1941]]).

Royal Dutch Shell also, through the B.P.M., operated the [[Pangkalan Brandan]] refinery in North Sumatra — one of the five large refineries of the pre-war East Indian oil industry, and the only large refinery in North Sumatra.

## Significance

Royal Dutch Shell is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because its [[1907]] amalgamation and the subsequent creation of the B.P.M. as the East Indian operating subsidiary established the corporate structure within which the great majority of the colonial Atjeh and northern-Sumatran oil industry was conducted. The B.P.M.'s dominance of the East Indian oil industry (57.3 % of crude production in [[1940]]) made Royal Dutch Shell the most important single corporate actor in the colonial mineral economy of which Atjeh was a part, and the company's operations — anchored by the [[Pangkalan Brandan]] refinery — were the principal industrial presence of the colonial oil economy in the Atjeh region. The fact that the originating asset of the entire Royal Dutch (and subsequently Royal Dutch Shell) oil empire was the [[1885]] [[Telaga Said]] drilling on the [[1883]] [[J. Zulkler|Zulkler]] concession also makes the northern-Sumatran oil industry the founding locus of the company's global presence.

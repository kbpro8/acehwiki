**The Delama-benden** were the armed bands which, according to the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|J. T. Cremer]] speaking in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]], had streamed together in the [[Tanawaqt]] and in the [[VII Moekims]] of the [[Pedir]] country, and whose expulsion was the object of the [[Sigli expedition (August 1897)|Segli expedition]] of August [[1897]].

## In the debate of [[1897]]

Cremer's statement was: "Daar heeft men denzelfden toestand ongeveer als in het Pedirsche; de vorsten aan de kust zijn ons niet vijandig gezind, maar zij zijn niet krachtig genoeg om de in de Tanawaqt of in de VII moekims samengestroomde Delama-benden te verjagen, en daarom heeft de expeditie naar [[Segli]] plaats gehad; niet om Segli zelf te nemen, maar wel om de vijandelijke benden uit de moekims [[Pakan Baroe]] en [[Pokan Soth]] te verjagen."

## Significance

The Delama-bands illustrate the political structure on which the Dutch coastal policy rested and by which it was frustrated. Under the [[Verklaringen van de kustvorsten van Atjeh|declarations of the coastal princes]] the rulers of the *landschappen* had recognised Dutch sovereignty; but, as Cremer conceded in the same debate, "jammer maar, dat deze hoofden niet voldoende gezag kunnen uitoefenen op hunne onderdanen." — a pity only that these chiefs cannot exercise sufficient authority over their subjects. The bands operated in territories nominally friendly, and Dutch expeditions were therefore presented not as conquests but as assistance to allied chiefs unable to police their own districts.

Cremer used the same structure to answer the demand for a commission of enquiry, observing that the struggle had no single leader but leaders — "wij hebben op Atjeh te doen met benden over het geheele land verspreid. Aan de eene zijde heeft men b.v. [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]], aan de andere [[Panglima Polim]] en vele anderen. Dezer dagen hebben wij nog gehoord van een aanval in [[Edi]] onder aanvoering van [[Nja Mamat Perlak]]." — in Atjeh we have to do with bands spread over the whole country. On the one side one has, for instance, Toekoe Oemar, on the other Panglima Polim and many others.

**The Council of Netherlands India** was the highest advisory council of the colonial government in [[Batavia]]. It advised the [[Dutch East Indies Government|Dutch East Indies Government]] and the governor-general on war, administration, finance, and law. In the early history of the [[Aceh War]], the council was directly involved in the decision to send the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)]], in the decision to recall that expedition after failure, and later in the political controversy that followed the defeat.

The council is important in Aceh-related history because its meetings stood behind some of the most important Dutch decisions of [[1873]]. It also appeared in the pamphlet war, where critics argued over who knew what had been said inside its extraordinary meetings.

## What the council was

The council was chaired by the governor-general. Its vice-president and regular members advised on major questions of state. In times of crisis, other senior officials, including the commanders of the army and navy, could be called into extraordinary meetings.

In the Aceh crisis of [[1873]], the vice-president was [[F. N. Nieuwenhuysen]], who was later sent as government commissioner with the first expedition.

## The decision of [[20 February]] [[1873]]

After the telegram of [[18 February]] [[1873]] from [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]], an extraordinary meeting of the council was held on [[20 February]] [[1873]]. According to the official account, the council decided that a high official should be sent at once to [[Aceh|Atjeh]] with full powers to demand clarification from the sultan and to demand recognition of Dutch sovereignty. Military force would be prepared at once so that these demands could be backed by force without delay.

At this meeting, [[F. N. Nieuwenhuysen]] was chosen as government commissioner, [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]] as commander of the land force, and [[Captain-terr zee Koopman|J. F. Koopman]] as commander of the sea force.

## The decision of [[20 April]] [[1873]]

After the first expedition ran into heavy resistance, after the fighting at the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|Missigit]], and after the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]], the question became whether the campaign could continue. On [[20 April]] [[1873]], the governor-general again called an extraordinary meeting of the council, this time together with the commanders of land and sea forces and [[General Verspijck|Verspyck]], who had been named as Köhler's successor.

Although Verspyck said he was ready to go at once, naval concerns carried more weight. The anchorage off Aceh was judged unsafe, and communication between the roadstead and the shore was thought too uncertain. The council therefore advised recall, and the Dutch force left the Aceh roadstead on [[29 April]] [[1873]].

## The council in the pamphlet controversy

The council also appeared in the public pamphlet war that followed the failed expedition. In [[De enquête over den mislukten tocht naar Atjeh]], [[H. B. van Daalen]] complained that the anonymous writer of [[Bemoediging]] spoke as though he knew exactly what had been said in the extraordinary meetings of the council. Van Daalen used this as part of his argument that the writer stood close to the circle of [[James Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]].

This detail matters because it shows that the council was not only an administrative body in the background. It became part of the struggle over blame, secrecy, and insider knowledge after the first defeat.

## Later role in Aceh administration

The council remained important in later Aceh matters. In the twentieth century it advised on senior appointments and wider colonial policy. Later sources noted, for example, that the council had recommended [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] for the Aceh governorship in [[1905]], although the recommendation was overruled. [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] himself later became vice-president of the council after leaving Aceh in [[1918]].

## Significance

**The Council of Netherlands India** is significant because it helped turn Dutch concern about Aceh into official war policy. The meetings of [[20 February]] [[1873]] and [[20 April]] [[1873]] framed the opening phase of the [[Aceh War]]: first the decision to intervene, then the decision to pull back. Later critics such as [[H. B. van Daalen]] show that the authority of the council did not protect it from controversy. On the contrary, its closed discussions became part of the political fight over responsibility.

**The Council of Netherlands India** (Dutch: *Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië*, also *Raad van Indië*) was the highest advisory council of the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands-Indian]] government, summoned by the [[Governor-General of the Netherlands India|Governor-General]] on extraordinary occasions to advise on matters of war, finance, and legislation. The Council played a central part in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], being convened on [[20 February]] [[20 February]] [[1873]] (with the commanders of land and sea forces) to decide on the dispatch of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]], on [[20 April]] [[20 April]] [[1873]] to decide on the withdrawal of that expedition, and on various occasions during the war for advice on the conduct of operations and on the reorganisation of the administration.

## Composition

The Council was presided over by the Governor-General, and included as ex officio members the vice-president and the ordinary members, together with the directors of the principal departments. On extraordinary occasions — such as those of [[20 February]] [[20 February]] [[1873]] and [[20 April]] [[20 April]] [[1873]] — the commanders of land and sea forces were also summoned. The vice-president of the Council in [[1873]] was [[F. N. Nieuwenhuysen|F. N. Nieuwenhuysen]], who was appointed commissioner to the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] in [[February 1873|February 1873]]. The member [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. T. H. der Kinderen]] was sent to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[November 1880|November 1880]] as government commissioner for the reorganisation of justice in the possessions outside Java and Madura.

## The Decision to Send the First Aceh Expedition, 20 February 1873

On [[20 February]] [[20 February]] [[1873]], after the receipt of the telegram from the Minister of Colonies of [[18 February]] [[18 February]] [1873], the Governor-General [[J. Loudon|Loudon]] convened an extraordinary meeting of the Council with the commanders of land and sea forces. "It was decided to send at once a high official to Atjeh with full powers, in accordance with an instruction to be drafted further, to ask the sultan of Atjeh for clarification of his treacherous conduct, and to demand from him the recognition of our sovereignty. In order to lose no time, the necessary fighting forces, both of land and sea power, would at once be made ready to give emphasis to his demands and, if necessary, to take offensive action at once." As commissioner was designated the vice-president of the Council, F. N. Nieuwenhuysen; the command of the land force was entrusted to Major-general [[J. H. R. Köhler|J. H. R. Köhler]], that of the sea force to Captain-ter-sea [[J. F. Koopman|J. F. Koopman]].

## The Decision to Withdraw, 20 April 1873

On [[20 April]] [[20 April]] [[1873]], on receipt of the news of the failure of the first expedition, the Governor-General again convened an extraordinary meeting of the Council with the commanders of land and sea forces and Major-general [[G. M. Verspyck|Verspyck]], who had been designated as [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]]'s successor. Although Verspyck declared himself ready to depart for Atjeh at once, the vice-admiral's opinion — that the roadstead of Atjeh would offer no safe anchorage, and that communication between roadstead and beach would be seriously threatened — prevailed. The Governor-General authorised the expeditionary force to return immediately, with the firm intention of resuming the campaign in the favourable season. The fleet left the roadstead of Atjeh on [[29 April]] [[29 April]] [[1873]].

## The Advice on the Conversiequaestie, 17 October 1879

The Council also played a central part in the [[Conversiequaestie|conversiequaestie]]. The draft general ordonnantie submitted by the general secretary [[H. D. Levyssohn Norman|Levyssohn Norman]] on [[1 February]] [[1 February]] [[1875]] was placed in the hands of the directors of inland administration and of justice for advice. This advice was given in [[1876]], and thereupon the draft, somewhat modified by the general secretary, was placed in the hands of the Council, which by advice of [[17 October]] [[17 October]] [[1879]] gave as its opinion that there was no need for codification, and that amendments in the ordonnanties had already been made or were less necessary. With this advice agreed both Governor-General and Opperbestuur.

## The Vice-President and Special Commissions

The vice-president of the Council was also subject to a restriction on his functions: by decision of the Opperbestuur in [[November 1874|November 1874]], in accordance with the advice of the [[Raad van State|Raad van State]], it was decided that art. 36 of the [[Regeeringsreglement|Regeeringsreglement]] did not allow the vice-president to be charged with the special missions and commissions mentioned therein. With January [[1875]] the vice-president [[O. van Rees|Mr. O. van Rees]] was accordingly relieved of the membership of the commission for the improvement of the Indian taxes.

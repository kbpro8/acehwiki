**De Kroniek** was a Dutch weekly cited twice by [[H. van Kol]] in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[1897]] as a source of information on the conduct and the cost of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The report of [[16 May]] [[1897]]

The first citation is the report of the [[Killing of Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed Lam Tenzah (16 May 1897)|killing of Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed Lam Tenzah]]. Van Kol quoted from *De Kroniek* of [[16 May]] [[1897]] the account of the killing of that chief with his wife, children and seven followers as they slept, by volleys fired through the thin walls of the dwelling until every sound was silenced and they "een reiswijzer naar betere gewesten hadden verkregen."

## The financial calculation of [[10 October]] [[1897]]

The second citation is the calculation of the annual cost of the war, given in *De Kroniek* of [[10 October]] [[1897]]. Van Kol quoted it: "Als men de traktementen der officieren, de soldijen der manschappen, den geneeskundigen dienst en het troepen- en goederenvervoer bijeentelt, was de uitgave voor den Atjeh-oorlog in [[1871]] f 9.947.030, doch in [[1895]] gestegen tot f 18.176.000 of f 8.230.000 hooger, waarvan veilig zes millioen kon worden aangenomen als jaarlijks ten goede komende aan particuliere stoombooten en leveranciers van levensmiddelen, ten behoeve van den Atjeh-oorlog, in de zakken van welke laatsten ook het grootste gedeelte van de geenszins ruime traktementen der officieren en soldaten verdwijnt."

The same passage supplied Van Kol with the observation that there had been years — such as [[1876]] — in which a million and a half was spent monthly on Atjeh, of which the interested parties enjoyed about a third; and with the remark that the suppliers of coal also did good business and would regret the ending of the war, "zoodat het de vraag is van wie men bij het beslist eindigen van den Atjeh-oorlog eerder een opstand had te duchten, van de Javanen of van de Europeanen op Java." — so that it is a question from whom, on the definitive ending of the Atjeh war, one would sooner have a rising to fear — from the Javanese or from the Europeans on Java.

**Indisch Instituut** — Dutch for "Indies Institute" — was the Amsterdam-based colonial institute under whose auspices the volume *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* was published by [[Wereldbibliotheek N.V.]] in [[1946]]. The title page of the volume records that it was "uitgegeven in 1946 onder de auspiciën van het Indisch Instituut" — that is, published under the auspices of the Indisch Instituut. The institute's role in the volume's publication gave the volume the institutional authority of the premier Dutch colonial-research foundation of the mid-twentieth century.

## Relationship to the Koninklijk Koloniaal Instituut

The *Indisch Instituut* named on the title page of *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* is the institutional successor of, or alternative name for, what was earlier called the *Koninklijk Koloniaal Instituut* (Royal Colonial Institute) — the [[Koninklijke Vereniging Koloniaal Instituut|Royal Association Colonial Institute]] founded in [[1910]] in Amsterdam, which by the late-colonial period had developed into the principal Dutch research foundation for the study of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Dutch East Indies]]. The institute's library, ethnographic collections and publication series were, by [[1946]], the standard infrastructure of Dutch colonial scholarship; the volume's publication "under the auspices" of the institute placed its fourteen specialist contributions — by Dr. [[C. W. Wormser|Wormser]], Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]], Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]], Dr. [[K. J. Brouwer|Brouwer]], Dr. [[G. J. van Hall|Van Hall]], Mr. Dr. [[L. J. J. Caron|Caron]], Ir. [[F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]], Ir. [[W. J. Utermark|Utermark]], Pater [[L. van Rijckevorsel|Van Rijckevorsel]] and others — within the institute's wider scholarly programme.

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the Indisch Instituut's significance is the institutional authority it lent to the volume's digest of Atjeh material. The volume's heavy reliance on [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] for its Atjeh content was, in this institutional frame, not a popularisation but a synthesis authorised by the premier colonial-research foundation of the Netherlands — a synthesis that was to remain, for at least a decade after [[1946]], the standard Dutch-language point of entry to the Atjeh material.

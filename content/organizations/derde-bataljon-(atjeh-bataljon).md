**The Derde Bataljon** — the 3rd Battalion of the [[K.N.I.L.]] — also written *3e Bataljon Infanterie* or *3de Bataljon Infanterie*, was the "Atjeh-Bataljon," the battalion that received the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] decoration "in [[1877]] voor het buitengewoon roemrijk aandeel" in the early years of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], "in het bijzonder vermeld die van begin [[1876]]." The Derde Bataljon's M.W.O. was one of the earliest battalion-level decorations of the Aceh War, and the battalion's nickname "Atjeh-Bataljon" marks it as the battalion most identified with the Aceh theatre. The battalion returned to the Aceh theatre in the spring of [[1896]] as a regular *veldbataljon* (field battalion) of the colonial army, where — under the successive command of Maj. K. W. Steinmetz, Maj. J. W. van den Broek, and Luit.-kol. G. A. Hansen — it served as the workhorse battalion of the campaign, participating in nearly every major Dutch column from the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] through the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing of 22–23 June 1896]], and supplying the 1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh who was officially the first Dutch officer to reach [[Teuku Umar]]'s *verblijf* (residence) at [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]].

## The M.W.O. of 1877

"Het derde Bataljon ontving de onderscheiding in [[1877]], voor het buitengewoon roemrijk aandeel, in het bijzonder vermeld die van begin [[1876]]." The Derde Bataljon received the M.W.O. in [[1877]] for its exceptionally glorious share in the early years of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] — in particular the share of the beginning of [[1876]]. The decoration was a battalion-level M.W.O., awarded not to individual soldaten but to the battalion as a whole.

## The third expedition's Missigit storming

"Die tocht werden de hevige gevechten van de eerste maanden van [[1876]]" — and the third expedition's storming of the [[Missigit (Meusigit)|Missigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] is also associated with the Derde Bataljon. The battalion's M.W.O. of [[1877]] recognised the cumulative share of the battalion in the early years of the war, from the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]] of [[1873]]-[[1874]] through the *hevige gevechten* (fierce battles) of [[1876]].

## The Djagaraga honour (1849)

"Voor de bestorming van Djagaraga op [[Bali]] in [[1849]]." The Derde Bataljon's pre-Aceh honours included the storming of Djagaraga on Bali in [[1849]]. The battalion therefore had a long colonial-pacification history before the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] — a history that the [[1877]] M.W.O. extended.

## The spring of 1896 campaign (ATJEH '96 account)

As one of the regular *veldbataljons* of the colonial army, the 3e Bataljon Infanterie combined European and indigenous (*inlandse*) companies armed with Beaumont rifles and, in selected pelotons, the new repeteergeweer (repeating rifle). At the moment of [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection on [[29 March]] [[1896]] the battalion was already stationed in [[Koeta Radja]] under Maj. K. W. Steinmetz, providing garrisons for [[Biloel]] and the [[geconcentreerde Linie (Concentrated Line)|geconcentreerde linie]] (concentrated line). On the afternoon of [[29 March]] [[1896]] three of its companies were sent by *stoomtram* (steam tram) to [[Lamdjamoe]] at 2:15 PM under [[C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]]'s counter-measures, reinforced at 5 PM by two sections of the [[Bergartillerie / Bergbatterijen|4e Bergbatterij]]. On [[31 March]]–[[1 April]] [[1896]] one company of the 3e Bataljon held the intersection of the Koeta Radja–Ketapan Doewa railway and the road to post [[Belang]], with a forward section at the bridge over Kroeng Ning, in the *hinderlagen* (ambushes) laid to protect [[Koeta Radja]].

On [[8 April]] [[1896]] the battalion — 18 officers, 336 European and 220 Ambonese men — formed one of the three infantry battalions of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s relief column for [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]], furnishing the 2nd column under Kapitein van Aken (1st company, *voorhoede*), Kapitein G. D. van Epen (3rd company), and Kapitein J. M. van Gogh (4th company), with an ambulance under Off. van gesondh. 2e kl. L. A. Demmers.

## Role in the spring 1896 operations

The 3e Bataljon was one of the most heavily engaged Dutch units of the semester. On [[17 April]] [[1896]] the repeater-rifle peloton of its 1e compagnie was attached to [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]]'s column at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop]]. On [[2 May]] [[1896]] the battalion — now under Maj. J. W. van den Broek — formed the *linkercolonne* (left column) of the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik]], advancing via Gani and Gendroet to the line [[Lam-Pré]]–[[Lam Tjot]] alongside six marechaussée brigades of [[Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland|Kapt. Graafland]] and joining in the assault on Senelop at 9:10 AM. The battalion's *terugtocht* (retreat) route ran via Lam Koelat and Lam Kiwan, with the marechaussée holding Atjehsche *loopgraven* (trenches) from Kroeng Lingkar to Lam Koelat to cover the retirement.

On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the battalion — now under Luit.-kol. G. A. Hansen, 15 officieren and 403 minderen — formed the infantry of the Bergcolonne in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]], and on [[24 May]] [[1896]] its 1e luit. de Jongh was officially the first Dutch officer to reach [[Teuku Umar]]'s *verblijf* at Lam-Pisang at 10:35 VM (A.M.). From [[30 May]] to [[2 June]] [[1896]] the battalion contributed to the *dekkingstroepen* (covering troops) of the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]]. On [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] the battalion formed the sea-landing 1e Colonne (14 officers, 417 men) under Lt. Col. Hansen in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack]], embarking at [[Oleh-leh]] at 4 PM on [[22 June]] on H. M. *Merapi*, the Gouvernementsstoomer *Condor* and *Albatros*, anchoring off [[Ladoeng]] at 2:30 AM on [[23 June]], and marching through the mountains via [[Glé Broeë]] to reach the plain of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] by 11:30 AM.

## Composition and strength

The battalion's strength evolved through the semester. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] it fielded 18 officers, 336 European and 220 Ambonese men under Maj. K. W. Steinmetz. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] it reported 15 officieren and 403 minderen under Luit.-kol. G. A. Hansen. The strength return of [[1 June]] [[1896]] recorded 16 officers and 433 minderen. Company commanders during the spring included Kapitein van Aken (1e compagnie), Kapitein G. D. van Epen (3e compagnie), and Kapitein J. M. van Gogh (4e compagnie), with 1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh of the 1e compagnie distinguishing himself at Lam-Pisang on [[24 May]] [[1896]]. Medical support was provided by the ambulance under Off. van gesondh. 2e kl. L. A. Demmers.

## Significance

The Derde Bataljon is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the "Atjeh-Bataljon" — the battalion most identified with the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. The book's argument is that the battalion's [[1877]] M.W.O. — awarded "voor het buitengewoon roemrijk aandeel" in the early years of the war — was one of the earliest battalion-level M.W.O. awards of the Aceh War, and that the battalion's nickname "Atjeh-Bataljon" marks it as the battalion whose identity was formed by the Aceh theatre.

In Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* the 3e Bataljon Infanterie emerges as the workhorse battalion of the spring [[1896]] campaign, present in every major Dutch column from the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|April relief operations]] to the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|June Ladoeng landing]]. Its repeater-rifle peloton reflects the Dutch adoption of new infantry weapons against the well-armed *benden* (bands) of [[Teuku Umar]], and its rapid succession of commanders (Steinmetz → van den Broek → Hansen) mirrors the Dutch command reshuffle that followed [[J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s arrival as [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]] on [[7 April]] [[1896]]. The distinction of being first into Umar's house at [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] — a claim contested in the source by the cavalryman Wachtmeester Jonker — was awarded by Kruisheer to the battalion's 1e luit. de Jongh.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

The **3de Bataljon Infanterie** — the third battalion of infantry of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]] — was the unit which, under the command of [[Overste Sievers]], advanced in [[October]] [[1893]] to relieve the beleaguered Dutch post at [[Kroeng Kali]] in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## The relief of Kroeng Kali

[[G. B. Hooijer]] recorded that *het 3de bataljon, onder de bevelen van den Overste Sievers, rukte daarheen op en zou trachten den vijand uit zijn drie stellingen op den verbindingsweg met Toengkoeb te verdrijven* — the 3rd battalion, under the orders of Lieutenant-Colonel Sievers, advanced there and was to attempt to drive the enemy from his three positions on the connecting road with [[Toengkoeb]].

The defenders resisted so obstinately that on the first day only two positions could be taken. One was the [[Masdjid Siem|Messigit Siem]], where [[Luitenant Weyerman]] and three men fell and two officers and fifty men were wounded on the *sawah* (wet rice-field) plain before the mosque. The third position was evacuated by the enemy during the following night.

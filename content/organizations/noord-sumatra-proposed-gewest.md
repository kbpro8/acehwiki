**Noord-Sumatra** was the proposed new *gewest* (region) of the Netherlands East Indies that would have incorporated the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] and the Residency of Sumatra's Oostkust (East Coast of Sumatra) into a single administrative unit, with its capital at Medan. The proposal was a central element of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] and represented the most consequential administrative change affecting Atjeh in the reform process: the dissolution of Atjeh's separate Gouvernement status and its integration into a larger civilian-administered region under a Resident rather than a Civiel en Militair Gouverneur.

## Proposed Structure

The new gewest was to be governed by a Resident based in Medan, replacing the Civiel en Militair Gouverneur who had previously ruled Atjeh from [[Koeta-Radja]]. The Gouvernement would be replaced by a *gewest* in the regular colonial hierarchy, with [[Koeta-Radja]] serving as the seat of one of the Resident's subordinate afdeelingen. The *representatiekosten* (representation expenses) for the Resident at Koeta-Radja were noted as necessary in the reform discussions, reflecting the continued importance of the former Gouvernement capital. A [[Gewestelijke Raad Noord-Sumatra|Gewestelijke Raad]] (Regional Council) of 19 members was proposed to provide advisory input into regional governance.

## Proposed Afdeelingen

Resident [[Van der Plas]] proposed seven *afdeelingen* (administrative divisions) for Noord-Sumatra at the Medan meeting in [[January]] [[1914]], with population estimates:

1. **Deli benevens de Karo- en de Alaslanden** (pop. 370,000) — incorporating the [[Alaslanden]] transferred from Atjeh
2. **Asahan met Simeloengoen** (pop. 180,000)
3. **Langkat met de Oostkust van Atjeh** (pop. 230,000) — incorporating [[Tamiang]] and [[Langsa]] from Atjeh
4. **Groot-Atjeh benevens de Westkust van Atjeh** (pop. 210,000) — combining the former afdeelingen of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Westkust van Atjeh]]
5. **Noord- en Oostkust van Atjeh behalve Tamiang** (pop. 480,000) — merging [[Noordkust van Atjeh]] and [[Oostkust van Atjeh]] minus Tamiang
6. **Bataklanden** (pop. 350,000)
7. **Sibolga** (pop. 260,000)

At the [[Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen (19-20 January 1914)]], all present officials agreed unanimously with this proposed division. Van der Plas noted that the precise addition of Atjeh areas to the Deli and Langkat afdeelingen should be left to the judgment of the Gouverneur van Atjeh, recognizing that local knowledge was essential for final border determinations.

## Governance Arrangements

For Atjeh specifically, the reform concluded that only [[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]] (Regional Self-Governance) was feasible, rather than the local *zelfbestuur* (self-governance through autonomous bodies) proposed for other Buitenbezittingen. This was because the Gouvernement Atjeh's territory combined *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory) with Inlandsche Zelfbesturen (Native Self-Governments), making the creation of additional autonomous local bodies impractical. Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff]] also noted that granting greater independence to the Inlandsch bestuur was "voor dit gewest vooralsnog van minder beteekenis" (of less significance for this territory for the time being). The training of Inlandsche ambtenaren for Atjeh was explicitly excluded from the reform discussion due to existing *bestuursverhoudingen* (governance relationships).

## Significance

The proposed Noord-Sumatra gewest would have ended Atjeh's exceptional status as a separately governed military territory—a status that had been maintained since the beginning of the Atjeh War in 1873. By incorporating Atjeh into a larger civilian-administered region alongside the commercially oriented East Coast Residency, the proposal implicitly declared that the era of Atjeh as a special military concern was drawing to a close. The unanimous agreement of Atjeh's leadership at the Koeta-Radja meetings to this proposal was a significant moment, suggesting that the colonial officials on the ground believed that pacification had progressed sufficiently to permit normalization of Atjeh's governance. However, the practical implementation of this proposal would take many years, and Atjeh's transition from military Gouvernement to civilian gewest remained an ongoing process well beyond 1914.

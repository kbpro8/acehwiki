**The Vereeniging ter Beoefening van de Krijgswetenschap** was a learned society based in [['s-Gravenhage]] dedicated to the scholarly study of military science, which served as an important platform for the dissemination of critical defense ideas concerning [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] in the years preceding and during the [[First World War]].

## Founding and Purpose

The society brought together military officers, defence officials, and interested civilians in [['s-Gravenhage]] for the purpose of studying and debating questions of military strategy, organisation, and policy. Its meetings and publications provided a venue where professional military opinion could be aired outside the formal channels of the [[Ministry of Colonies]] and the [[Indische Legerbestuur]].

## The Van der Weyden Defence Brochure

The society published a defence brochure authored by [[Lieutenant-Colonel J. Van der Weyden|Overste J. Van der Weyden]], entitled *Weerplicht voor Inlanders en samenstelling van de weermacht in Nederlandsch-Indië*. This pamphlet set out proposals for the introduction of native military service and the reorganisation of the colonial defence forces, proposals that became central to the defence debate of [[1916]] and [[1917]].

## Meeting of 9 January 1914

On [[9 January]] [[1914]], the society held a meeting at which [[Lieutenant-Colonel J. Van der Weyden|Van der Weyden]] delivered a significant address. With the explicit authorisation of [[J. B. van Heutsz]], Van der Weyden quoted Van Heutsz's devastating private judgment on the report of the [[Staatscommissie]]. Van Heutsz had written:

> "Het is mijn vaste overtuiging, dat, bij uitvoering van de voorstellen der S. C., Nederland — hoewel echt Nederlandsch langzaam en zuinigjes — zal wegwerpen veel geld voor: Een vloot, die niet vechten zal — en Een leger, dat niet vechten kan."

Van Heutsz had further declared: "Ik ben blijde, dat mijn naam niet onder dat Staatsrapport staat." The public airing of these remarks by a former [[Governor-General]] lent enormous weight to the criticism of the [[Staatscommissie]] report and galvanised opposition to its recommendations.

## Meeting of 27 February 1914

On [[27 February]] [[1914]], the society convened another meeting at which [[Lieutenant-Colonel J. Van der Weyden|Van der Weyden]] continued his discussion of the [[Staatscommissie]] and its deficiencies. These successive meetings demonstrated the society's role as a sustained forum for critique of official colonial defence policy.

## Scheduled Lecture of 23 February 1917

[[Overste Merens]] was scheduled to deliver a lecture before the society on [[23 February]] [[1917]], addressing the same questions of colonial defence organisation that [[Lieutenant-Colonel J. Van der Weyden|Van der Weyden]] had raised. This scheduling indicated that the defence debate remained active within the society's programme throughout the wartime period.

## Role in the Defence Debate

The Vereeniging ter Beoefening van de Krijgswetenschap functioned as a critical intermediary between professional military opinion and public policy discussion. By publishing [[Lieutenant-Colonel J. Van der Weyden|Van der Weyden]]'s brochure and hosting meetings at which the views of [[J. B. van Heutsz]] were aired, the society helped to channel the authority of [[Aceh]] veterans into the broader Netherlands Indies defence debate. Its activities complemented those of other organisations such as the [[Indische Krijgsvereeniging]] and the [[Bond van Vrije Liberalen]] in shaping the terms of discussion about colonial military reform.

## Significance

The society's meetings and publications provided a legitimising institutional framework through which the defence ideas of [[J. B. van Heutsz]] and [[Lieutenant-Colonel J. Van der Weyden|Van der Weyden]] reached a wider audience. The quoting of Van Heutsz's private condemnation of the [[Staatscommissie]] report at the [[9 January]] [[1914]] meeting was a particularly consequential moment, transforming confidential military criticism into a public political weapon in the ongoing struggle over the future of [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] defence.

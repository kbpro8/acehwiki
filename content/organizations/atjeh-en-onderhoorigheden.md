**Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** — *Atjeh and Dependencies* — was the Dutch colonial governmental unit, formally established after the [[Conquest of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|Dutch conquest of the kraton]] of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] and the death of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] of Atjeh on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]], comprising the territory of the former [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] — that is, the coast of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] (the [[25 Moekims|25]], [[26 Moekims|26]], and [[22 Moekims|22 Moekims]] and the Sultan's territory) and the *onderhoorigheden* (dependencies) along the [[Oostkust|East]], [[Noordkust|North]], and [[Westkust|West Coasts]]. The governmental unit was administered by a *[[Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]* — a Governor, sometimes civil, sometimes military, sometimes both — whose seat was at the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]].

## The Pre-War Extent of Atjeh

Atjeh, at the time of the [[Declaration of War on Aceh (1873)|Dutch declaration of war]] of [[March]] [[1873]], consisted in fact of the coastal strip on the northern point of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]], running on the East Coast from the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] river to the West Coast of the region of [[Troemon|Troemon]], with an area approximately equal to that of the Netherlands and a population of approximately 500,000 souls. The interior was inhabited by the independent mountain peoples, the [[Gayo|Gajoes]] and the [[Alas|Alas]], who had nothing to do with the war. [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], a valley at the north-western point, of approximately half the size of the province of [[Utrecht|Utrecht]], had for centuries been the political centre, the most populous and richest part of the country.

## The Establishment of the Gouvernement (1874)

After the [[Conquest of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] and the death of the Sultan on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]], General [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] proposed that the Dutch should step into the Sultan's place and assume direct rule. The governmental unit of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was the formal expression of this assumption. The *kraton* of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] — once the seat of the Sultan's authority — became the seat of the Governor and the headquarters of the Dutch military command.

## The Administration

The administration of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was entrusted to a *Gouverneur* — a Governor whose authority was variously constituted during the war:

- Under [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] ([[April 1874|April]] [[1874]]): military commander, with [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Colonel Pel]] as acting governor after Van Swieten's departure;
- Under [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] ([[March 1876|March]] [[1876]]–[[November 1876|November]] [[1876]]) and [[Diemont|Diemont]] ([[November 1876|November]] [[1876]]–[[June 1877|June]] [[1877]]): military governors;
- Under [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] ([[January 1878|January]] [[1878]]–[[April 1881|April]] [[1881]]): military governor (definitive from January 1878; acting from earlier);
- Under [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] ([[April 1881|April]] [[1881]]–[[December 1882|December]] [[1882]]) and [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] ([[March 1883|March]] [[1883]]–[[September 1884|September]] [[1884]]): civil governors;
- Under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] ([[September 1884|September]] [[1884]]–[[December 1886|December]] [[1886]]), [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] ([[December 1886|December]] [[1886]]–[[April 1891|April]] [[1891]]), [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]] ([[May 1891|May]] [[1891]]–[[January 1892|January]] [[1892]]), and [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]] ([[January 1892|January]] [[1892]]–[[March 1896|March]] [[1896]]): *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* — civil and military governor;
- Under [[Vetter|Vetter]] ([[March 1896|March]] [[1896]] onwards): *Regeeringscommissaris* — Government Commissioner, superseding the Governor.

## The Geography of the Gouvernement

The Gouvernement of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden comprised:

- [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] — the valley at the north-western point of Sumatra, comprising the [[Sultan's gebied|Sultan's own territory]] and the three *Sagi's* of the [[25 Moekims|25]], [[26 Moekims|26]], and [[22 Moekims|22 Moekims]];
- The [[Oostkust|East Coast]] dependencies — [[Edi|Edi]], [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]], [[Langsar|Langsar]], [[Pasei|Pasei]], [[Gedong|Gedong]], [[Perlak|Perlak]], [[Tamiang|Tamiang]];
- The [[Noordkust|North Coast]] dependencies — [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]], [[Pedir|Pedir]], [[Giegieng|Giegieng]], [[Segli|Segli]], [[Samalanga|Samalanga]], [[Aijer Laboe|Aijer Laboe]], [[Endjong|Endjong]], [[Merdoe|Merdoe]];
- The [[Westkust|West Coast]] dependencies — [[Kroeng Raba|Kroeng Raba]], [[Melaboeh|Melaboeh]], [[Troemon|Troemon]], [[Rigas|Rigas]], [[Tenom|Tenom]], [[Kroeng Raija|Kroeng Raija]], [[Singkel|Singkel]];
- The offshore islands — [[Poeloe Bras|Poeloe Bras]], [[Poeloe Weh|Poelolo Weh]].

## Significance

The Gouvernement of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden was the principal Dutch colonial governmental unit of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Its establishment marked the formal Dutch assumption of sovereignty over the territory of the former [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]]. Its administration — variously civil, military, or both — was the principal instrument of Dutch colonial policy in Atjeh from [[1874]] to [[1896]] (and indeed to [[1918]]). The *onderhoorigheden* — the dependencies along the coasts — were, in the analysis of [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]] and [[Scherer|Scherer]], the true centre of gravity of the war, and the failure of the Dutch to master them through a consistent [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] was, in Kruijt's judgement, the principal cause of the protraction of the war.

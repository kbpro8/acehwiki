The **[[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie in Atjeh]]** comprised the two companies of siege artillery sent with the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Dutch expedition]] against [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] in December [[1873]] — the first use of vesting-artillerie on an Indische expedition. Armed with [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.]] (the first breech-loading rifled guns ever sent on an Indische expedition), mortieren van 20 c.M. and the older [[Voorlaadkanonnen in Atjeh|voorlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.]], the vesting-artillerie bore the principal share of the [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|nineteen-day bombardment]] that drove the Atjehneezen from the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]]. Borel — himself a kapitein der artillerie — consistently praises the vesting-artillerie and indicts [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] for recalling one company and most of the modern materieel to Java in February [[1874]].

## First Use on an Indische Expedition

The despatch of two companies of vesting-artillerie to Atjeh in [[1873]] was, in Borel's account, a notable innovation: vesting-artillerie had never before been sent on an Indische expedition. The companies were armed with the new [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.]] — breech-loading rifled steel guns whose schokbuis shells burst reliably on contact — trialled at Tjikarang on Java in October [[1873]] and judged to have "uitstekende diensten bewezen." The artillery train also included mortieren van 20 c.M. — heavy mortars used to throw shells into the interior of the Kraton — and the older [[Voorlaadkanonnen in Atjeh|getrokken voorlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.]], whose tijdbuizen failed on Atjeh's wet, bedekte terrain: half the 12 c.M. puntgranaten failed to explode. From the captured [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] a kanonbatterij was built in its eastern face, and for nineteen days — from [[6 January]] to [[23 January]] [[1874]] — these batteries bombarded the Kraton "met meer of minder kracht." Borel credits the artillerie alone with driving the enemy out: the Dutch had over-estimated the effect, believing the wall was thick stone — actually it was an aarden borstwering hidden by [[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboo-doeri]]. He notes with characteristic indignation that the vesting-artillerie was "met geen wapen op meer stiefmoederlijke wijze bedeeld" in belooningen.

## The Recall to Java

After the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] and the [[Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)|declaration]] that the offensive was over, van Swieten "stak het zwaard voor goed in de scheede." Among the measures of force reduction that followed the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]] of [[15 February]] [[1874]] was the recall to Java of one of the two companies of vesting-artillerie, with much of the materieel — including the achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M., "het beste geschut dat hij bezat." "Wat den opperbevelhebber bewoog zich zoo ontijdig en onnoodig te ontdoen van het beste geschut dat hij bezat, valt moeilijk te beslissen," Borel writes. The Javaansche hulptroepen of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]] and a veld-batterij were likewise sent back to Java in February. The recall was, in Borel's judgement, an act of strategic vandalism: it stripped the [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]] of the one piece of modern materieel that could reliably engage the Atjehnese stellingen.

## Pel's Bombardment Policy and the Request for Return

[[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] — who inherited the bezettingskorps on [[26 April]] [[1874]] — "dacht er evenwel geheel anders over" than van Swieten on the question of shelling kampongs. As early as [[28 April]] [[1874]] he ordered the vesting-artillerie to open fire from the voorlaad-kanonnen van 12 c.M. on all kampongs within range — daily, at varying hours, with the goal of damaging the enemy and preventing "samenscholen van groote massa's volk." Half of the 12 c.M. puntgranaten failed to explode, exposing the inadequacy of the obsolete materieel. The bombardment led directly to the request to return the achterlaadgeschut from Java. The newly raised 22ste compagnie vesting-artillerie and the Tweede compagnie vesting-artillerie arrived in Atjeh in the course of [[1874]] and [[1875]]. With the returned achterlaadkanonnen the vesting-artillerie could at last engage the enemy stellingen effectively: the [[Destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification (18 September 1874)|destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification]] on [[18 September]] [[1874]] ("What had not been obtained earlier through fierce struggle costing heavy sacrifices was now achieved by a few cannon shots"), the destruction of Mandarsa Poeti on [[5 October]] [[1874]], the silencing of the enemy lilla's after the first enemy shelling of K. Radja on [[24 August]] [[1874]], the bombardment of [[Pendetti]] on [[15 May]] [[1874]] and the destruction of the enemy benteng south of K. Radja on [[29 August]] [[1874]] were all the work of the vesting-artillerie. Borel, who personally traced the new zuider-face battery on the morning of [[14 August]] [[1874]] under a twelve-shot Atjehnese salvo, was an artilleryman writing about his own arm.

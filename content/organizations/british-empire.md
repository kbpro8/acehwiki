**British Empire** was the worldwide dominion of the United Kingdom that, by the early nineteenth century, controlled key strategic points in Southeast Asia, including **[[Singapore]]** (acquired in [[1819]]) and **[[Labuan]]** (acquired in [[1846]]). The **[[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824]]** formalised British control of these territories in exchange for recognition of Dutch authority over the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]**. British naval power also supported the establishment of **[[James Brooke]]**’s **[[Serawak]]**, further influencing the balance of power on **[[Borneo]]** and affecting Aceh’s maritime interests.

## Interaction with Aceh

- Recognised Dutch sovereignty over Aceh while securing adjacent British holdings.
- Through **[[Labuan]]** and **[[Serawak]]**, the British projected power that contested Acehnese influence on Borneo’s western coast.

## Account from *DLP-84-Atjeh* — the 1824 Treaty and the anti-piracy interventions of 1831 and 1844

The *DLP-84-Atjeh* pamphlet records that British interests in the nineteenth century centred on securing safe passage through the [[Strait of Malacca]] and protecting their own commercial fleets. In [[1824]] the United Kingdom entered into the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|Treaty of 1824]] with the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]], agreeing to respect each other's spheres of influence — including the independence of [[Acheh (Atjeh)|Acheh]]. Nevertheless, British ships were occasionally forced to intervene against Acehnese piracy, notably in [[1831]] and [[1844]] — together with American vessels (see [[United States of America]]). British diplomatic pressure influenced Dutch policy toward Aceh, especially regarding the enforcement of anti-piracy measures and the eventual declaration of the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] in [[1873]].

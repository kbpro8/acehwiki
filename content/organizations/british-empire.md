**British Empire** was the worldwide dominion of the United Kingdom that, by the early nineteenth century, controlled key strategic points in Southeast Asia, including **[[Singapore]]** (acquired in [[1819]]) and **[[Labuan]]** (acquired in [[1846]]). The **[[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824]]** formalised British control of these territories in exchange for recognition of Dutch authority over the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]**. British naval power also supported the establishment of **[[James Brooke]]**’s **[[Serawak]]**, further influencing the balance of power on **[[Borneo]]** and affecting Aceh’s maritime interests.

## Interaction with Aceh

- Recognised Dutch sovereignty over Aceh while securing adjacent British holdings.
- Through **[[Labuan]]** and **[[Serawak]]**, the British projected power that contested Acehnese influence on Borneo’s western coast.

**The Pall Mall Gazette** was a London evening newspaper, founded in [[1865]] and published in [[London|London]] until [[1920]], that carried commentary on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] and on the Dutch colonial question in [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The paper is named in the Dutch pamphlet *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk]]* by [[Een Ooggetuige|Een Ooggetuige]] ([[1880s]]) as a source of British press commentary on the Atjeh question, which the author of the pamphlet discusses in the course of his argument on the conduct of the war.

## The paper and its character

The *Pall Mall Gazette* was an evening paper founded in [[1865]] and modelled on the American style of journalism. It took its name from the *Pall Mall Gazette* imagined in [[Anthony Trollope|Anthony Trollope]]'s novel *The Three Clerks* ([[1857]]). The paper became, under the editorship of [[John Morley|John Morley]] in the late [[1860s]] and under [[W. T. Stead|W. T. Stead]] in the [[1880s]], one of the leading London evening papers, known for its commentary on imperial and foreign affairs.

## Commentary on the Atjeh question

The *Pall Mall Gazette* carried commentary on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] and on the Dutch colonial question in Atjeh. The author of the *[[Beschouwingen over Atjeh en onzen oorlog met dat rijk|Beschouwingen]]* refers to the paper's commentary in the course of his argument on the conduct of the war, and discusses the argument — then current in the Dutch and British press — over whether the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] should continue the war or should withdraw from Atjeh. The reference to the *Pall Mall Gazette* places the pamphlet within the wider contemporary debate on the Atjeh question, in which the Dutch and British press exchanged views on the war and on the strategic and commercial value of Atjeh.

## Significance

The *Pall Mall Gazette* is significant within the AcehWiki corpus as a source of British press commentary on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] cited in the contemporary Dutch pamphlet literature. The paper's commentary on the Atjeh question is a document of the British interest in the Dutch colonial war — an interest that sprang from the British position at [[Singapore|Singapore]] and [[Penang|Penang]], from the British trade with the Atjehnese pepper country, and from the wider imperial rivalry between Britain and the Netherlands in the archipelago.

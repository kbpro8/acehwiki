**Korps Pupillen te Gombong** was the institution at [[Gombong]] in Central [[Java]] for the children of soldiers killed in action or pensioned, at which [[H. N. A. Swart]] served as instructor from [[20 August]] [[1891]].

It was "one of those special institutions now already forgotten … intended for the reception of children of soldiers killed in action or pensioned, who could not be properly brought up at home"; almost exclusively children of men below officer rank were admitted. Some three to four hundred blue-clad *pupillen* were housed in three companies: the 1st for boys of fifteen to seventeen, the 2nd for twelve to fifteen, the 3rd for six to twelve, each with two lieutenants and the necessary cadre and a captain as corps commander. The boys attended primary school under supervision in normal school hours; the afternoon was given to sport, gymnastics and craft instruction, the last for those found unfit for military service at seventeen. Swart's verdict was: "A really good institution; I have always regretted that it was abolished and that the boys were then distributed among the orphanages." When a boy asked him whether he could become an officer, the lieutenants conferred and instituted a *cursus* for boys eligible for further education.

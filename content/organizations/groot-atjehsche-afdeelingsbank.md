**The Groot-Atjeh'sche Afdeelingsbank** was the principal institution of the *Volkscredietwezen* — the People's Credit System — of the [[Afdeeling Groot-Atjeh|Afdeeling Groot-Atjeh]], established at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in mid-[[1908]] as the first of three *afdeelingsbanken* in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] and the model against which the younger banks of Lhoʼ Seumawè ([[1913]]) and [[Langsa|Langsa]] ([[1915]]) were measured. *De Buitenbezittingen* devotes its longest single treatment of the Volkscredietwezen to this institution: "Van de drie afdeelingsbanken bestaat die van Groot-Atjeh het langst; ze heeft, door haar uitgebreiden werkkring en de teleurstellende ondervindingen, welke daarbij in ruime mate haar deel zijn geworden, in vele opzichten het spits afgebeten voor hare jongere zusters."

## Founding, capital, and Gouvernements-steun

At its founding in mid-[[1908]], the Bank expected to form its *bedrijfskapitaal* from deposits and savings, supplemented by a 4% Gouvernements-voorschot of ƒ15,000 — "dat weldra verhoogd moest worden met f 25.000." The Gouvernement also funded the initial *inrichting* and granted a yearly *subsidie* of ƒ3,000 for administrative costs, withdrawn after the eighth book-year, and annually *geschonken* the interest on its voorschot. The formation of an *aandeelenkapitaal* was "bij de oprichting niet geheel uit het oog verloren"; yet after eight years the *grondkapitaal* stood at only ƒ245, and savings deposits (at 3½%) at only ƒ15,643 — "grootendeels van Europeanen," for "slechts 7 inlanders" had used the savings facility. After [[1913]] the direct financial relation with the Gouvernement was replaced by a *Rekening-courant* at the Centrale Kas te Weltevreden, on which ƒ32,580 was drawn by mid-[[1916]].

## Lending and influence

The Bank, in its eight years, lent almost ƒ1,500,000 at moderate interest to the population of Groot-Atjeh. The categories of lending recorded in Bijlage VII — klappercultuur (ƒ184,299 in total, of which ƒ133,108 in [[1915]]–[[1916]]), pepercultuur, landbouw, aankoop van vee,handel in copra, peper and vee, Kleinhandel, and *schulddelging* (ƒ283,769 in total, of which ƒ90,156 in [[1915]]–[[1916]]) — show a credit institution deeply embedded in the *bevolkingscultures* of the *gewest*. The Bank was credited with having driven back the *kampong-woeker*, whose pre-[[1908]] rates of 40–60% had fallen rapidly once the *Volkscredietwezen* began operations; the *totaal geleende bedrag van ± f 207.000 voor schulddelging* was offered as proof.

## Kwade praktijken and achterstand

The Bank was, however, repeatedly forced to confront what *De Buitenbezittingen* calls the *kwade praktijken* — bad practices — of the population it served: fictitious *onderpanden*, applications for fictitious *doeleinden*, and the *baatzucht der hoofden* who pressed villagers to borrow in their own names. "Het dient erkend te worden," the handbook concedes, "dat ook de Groot-Atjehsche Afdeelings-bank niet aan dit euvel heeft weten te ontsnappen." The *cijfer der achterstallige leeningen* — arrears — "nam ieder jaar onrustbarend toe": from 1.3% of outstanding loans in [[1908]]–[[1909]] to 41.1% (1,044 of 2,547 loans) in [[1915]]–[[1916]]. By the eighth book-year, of the 1,887 borrowers whose repayments had fallen due, 55.3% were in arrears; the *vervallen bedrag* of ƒ72,625 stood against an *achterstand* of 71%. In September [[1914]] the Bank closed, as a rule, *Moekims* where the arrears exceeded 30%; the measure proved "te zeer in strijd met het karakter van de Bank" and was withdrawn in the eighth book-year. The *boete-reglement* was sharpened, *gerechtelijke vervolging* of negligent debtors before the *moesapat* increased, and *executoriale verkooping* of securities — "waarbij vaak frauduleuze en toch door de hoofden beaamde bezits-opgaven aan het licht komen" — became more frequent, with a resultant *verbittering* against the Bank among the population.

## Significance

Despite the *bedrijfsfouten der banken* and the *karakterfouten der bevolking* that *De Buitenbezittingen* frankly recorded, the Groot-Atjeh'sche Afdeelingsbank marked the first sustained colonial effort to extend organised credit — at rates far below the 40–60% of the pre-[[1908]] *kampong-woeker* — to the *kleine bezitters* of the Atjehsche *gampongs*. Its history shaped the more cautious practice of its younger sisters at Lhoʼ Seumawè and [[Langsa|Langsa]], and the design of the *gampong-credietinstellingen* — the 28 village *bankjes* of Groot-Atjeh founded from early [[1916]] onward — was deliberately drawn to avoid the errors it had made.

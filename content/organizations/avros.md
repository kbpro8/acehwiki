**The Algemeene Vereeniging van Rubberplanters ter Oostkust van Sumatra** — the General Association of Rubber Planters on Sumatra's East Coast, known by its acronym A.V.R.O.S. — was the planters' organisation of the great Deli *Cultuurgebied* to which, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "de meeste ondernemingen" of the European cultures in the [[Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh|Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh]] were affiliated, and through whose *gerede bemiddeling* they supplemented their contract-koelie populations as the plantings expanded. Although seated outside the *gewest* — in the heart of the Sumatran plantation belt — A.V.R.O.S. exercised, by [[1917]], a coordinating influence over the young rubber, oil-palm, and coffee cultures of [[Langsa|Langsa]] and [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] that the handbook explicitly notes.

## Function as labour intermediary

The European cultures of the [[Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh|Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh]] were wholly dependent on imported contract-labour. The *zeer aantal contract-koelies* present on the Atjehsche undertakings "nam met de uitbreiding der aanplantingen snel toe" — grew rapidly with the expansion of the plantings. "De meeste ondernemingen zijn aangesloten bij de A. V. R. O. S. … en maken, voor de aanvulling van hun koelies, gebruik van de gerede bemiddeling dezer vereeniging." This mediation was the principal day-to-day function of A.V.R.O.S. from the perspective of the Atjehsche planter: the Association's recruitment and placement network, drawing contract-koelies from Java and China, was the indispensable supplement to the *Cultuurgebied*'s own labour administration. In the first months of the [[1914]]–[[1918]] war, when "de financieele regelingen voor vele buitenlandsche ondernemingen … geheel ontwricht waren," the *Belgisch kapitaal* oil-palm plantations of Tamiang were hit particularly hard, and "een groot aantal [koelies] … naar hun stamland teruggezonden" — 996 in [[1914]] against 93 in [[1913]] and 161 in [[1915]].

## Coordination with the colonial state

The Association coordinated not only labour but also the *koelie-verzorging* — the medical and provisioning regime of the contract-koelies — which in Atjeh was "gedreven op de wijze, … welke in het groote Cultuurgebied ter Oostkust van Sumatra regel is." The undertakings of the *gewest* "staan ook onder toezicht van de Arbeidsinspectie aldaar," the same inspection service that supervised the Deli belt itself; *De Buitenbezittingen* refers the reader forward to the forthcoming *aflevering* on the Oostkust van Sumatra for full detail. The *centrale koelieschappijen* — two of them, founded through the *samenwerking* of the various *ondernemingen* — erected the large hospitals at [[Langsa|Langsa]] and Tamiang where, in [[1916]], "± 278 zieken dagelijks … verpleegd" were tended, with a *sterftecijfer* of 1.9% that had fallen, for Tamiang, from 6% in [[1913]].

## Significance

The prominence of A.V.R.O.S. in *De Buitenbezittingen*'s account reflects the structural fact that the European cultures of Atjeh's Oostkust were an extension — a *vreedzame penetratie* — of the Deli plantation economy rather than an indigenous growth. Of the 49 landbouw-concessies with ±105,000 H.A. in the *gewest*, twelve *maatschappijen* with seventeen ondernemingen (eighteen concessions) were established in Tamiang alone; in Langsa, "behalve het Gouvernement met zijn rubber-onderneming, 8 maatschappijen … met de contróle over evenveel ondernemingen (14 concessies)." The *totaal kapitaal* of these *geïnteresseerde lichamen* was ƒ17,250,000 in Tamiang and ƒ13,750,000 in Langsa — sums that made the Atjeh Oostkust, in capital terms, a province of the A.V.R.O.S. world.

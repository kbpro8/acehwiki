**The Corps Maréchaussée (Atjeh)** — the *Atjèh Marechaussée Corps* — was the Dutch colonial military unit, established in [[1890]] during the governorship of [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]], chosen from the most suitable volunteers of the army, charged exclusively with the security service within the Dutch line in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjèh], and itself highly meritorious. The corps — the principal Dutch counter-insurgency instrument of the late phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]] — would, under the governorship of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] from [[1898]] onwards and on the advice of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], be the principal means by which the religious and military leadership of the Atjèhse resistance was suppressed, and the war brought to its military conclusion in [[1904]].

## The 1890 Establishment

The clearing of the Dutch terrain within the line of enemies demanded all available strength, and only after the establishment of a corps maréchaussée in [[1890]] — chosen from the most suitable volunteers of the army, charged exclusively with the security service within the line, and itself highly meritorious — was safety there gradually obtained. The corps was the principal Dutch military innovation of the late 1880s, and was the immediate response to the failure of the conventional military posts and patrols to secure the Dutch line against the Atjèhse guerrilla warfare.

## The Counter-Insurgency Role

The corps maréchaussée was, in its counter-insurgency role, the principal executor of the Snouck Hurgronje prescription of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]] — that the actively hostile party, the bands organised by oelama's and adventurers, was not to be negotiated with but to be beaten, for their doctrine and their interest required that they would yield only to force. The corps — under the governorship of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] from [[1898]] onwards, and supported by the [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], [[Van der Maaten|Van der Maaten]], [[Christoffel|Christoffel]], [[Colijn|Colijn]], [[Van Loenen|Van Loenen]], [[Swart|Swart]], and [[Van der Wedden|Van der Wedden]] — would carry the counter-insurgency through to its military conclusion in [[1904]].

## The Period-Van Heutsz

The period-Van Heutsz — the governorship of Van Heutsz from [[1898]] onwards — was the period of the systematic counter-insurgency. The corps maréchaussée, the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] on the coasts, and the rigorous military operations against the Muslemin in the interior — these were the three principal instruments of the final Dutch pacification of Atjèh. The corps maréchaussée, in particular, was the principal instrument of the suppression of the religious and military leadership of the Atjèhse resistance, and the principal means by which the war was brought to its military conclusion.

## Significance

The Corps Maréchaussée (Atjeh) is the principal Dutch colonial military unit of the late phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]], and the principal Dutch counter-insurgency instrument. Established in [[1890]] in response to the failure of the conventional military posts and patrols to secure the Dutch line, the corps — under the governorship of Van Heutsz from [[1898]] onwards, and on the advice of Snouck Hurgronje — would be the principal means by which the religious and military leadership of the Atjèhse resistance was suppressed, and the war brought to its military conclusion in [[1904]].

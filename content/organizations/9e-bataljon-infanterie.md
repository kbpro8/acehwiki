**The 9e Bataljon Infanterie** — also written *9de Bataljon* — was a Dutch colonial field infantry battalion of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]] that arrived in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] on [[7 April]] [[1896]] aboard the steamers *Carpentier* and *Bantam* together with [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]], and Major of the General Staff J. F. Breijer. Commanded initially by Luit.-kol. H. Bendien and later by Luit.-kol. J. F. Th. Veeren, the battalion became one of the most heavily engaged Dutch units of the spring [[1896]] campaign, leading the assault that talso [[Lam-Asan]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]], covering the retreat through the [[Beradin (Vallei van Beradin / Kloof van Beradin)|Kloof van Beradin]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]], attacking [[Ateuh Raja]] on [[9 June]] [[1896]], and forming the 2e Colonne under [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] in the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik of 16–17 June 1896]].

## Background and Formation

The 9e Bataljon's arrival on [[7 April]] [[1896]] was part of the first tranche of reinforcements summoned after [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. The battalion was first encamped on the north-west part of the grassveld enclosed by officers' residences at [[Nesoch]] in [[Koeta Radja]], in lodgings of kasso's, planks, galvanized iron, or asphalt felt erected because of the approaching west monsoon. Under Luit.-kol. H. Bendien it served as reserve at [[Lam-Barih]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]]; Bendien subsequently transferred to command the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] itself while leading the demonstration in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] on [[30 April]] [[1896]] and the second *tocht* to the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb on [[4 May]] [[1896]]. On [[4 May]] [[1896]] the battalion faced wet rice paddy under heavy fire, while the [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljon]] had better terrain.

## Role in the Atjeh War

On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the 9e Bataljon — 14 officieren and 500 minderen under Maj. J. F. Th. Veeren — formed the *colonne "Djeroe Loeng"* in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]]. With 1e luitenant adjudant J. J. Romswinckel as adjutant, the battalion led the attack that talso [[Lam-Asan]] (Lam Asan) against the *versterkingen* Binasa Belang, Binasa Ketapang, and Balei Ba' Mό. On [[25 May]] [[1896]] it covered the Dutch retreat through the [[Beradin (Vallei van Beradin / Kloof van Beradin)|Kloof van Beradin]], a gorge east of which the *heuvelrug* had been the objective of the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|23 May aanval at Tjot Tjako]]. On [[9 June]] [[1896]] the battalion attacked [[Ateuh Raja]] in the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|Lam-Krak tuchtiging]]: its 1e compagnie under Kapitein H. T. de Moulin hit the *borstwering* behind Kroeng Ateuh, with 2de luit. W. A. Engelbrecht attacking; European fusilier v. Dongen was first on the *borstwering* and received a fatal chest shot from the last Atjeher. On [[16 June]]–[[17 June]] [[1896]] the battalion formed the 2e Colonne under [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] in the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik]], fighting at Tjot Maraja, Balik Angin, Toei Selimbing, Aloer Lintang, Lam-Nga, Lam-Pasei, and Lam-Bada, and storming the Panteh Karang redoute. From [[30 May]] to [[2 June]] [[1896]] the battalion also marched from [[Ketapan Doea]] along the foot of the gebergte as part of the *dekkingstroepen* of the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]].

## Composition and Strength

The 9e Bataljon's strength on [[23 May]] [[1896]] was 14 officieren and 500 minderen under Luit.-kol. J. F. Th. Veeren. The [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|strength return of 1 June 1896]] recorded 15 officers, 1 adjudant-onderofficier *d.d. officier*, and 458 minderen. Company commanders during the spring included Kapitein H. T. de Moulin (1e compagnie) and Kapitein H. Ph. de Moulin (who commanded half the battalion in the [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims]]: 6 off., 249 mind.). Adjudant to Majoor Veeren was 1e luitenant adjudant J. J. Romswinckel. Casualties included the European fusilier v. Dongen, killed on the *borstwering* at [[Ateuh Raja]] on [[9 June]] [[1896]], and an unknown number of wounded in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|V Moekims Montassik tuchtiging]].

## Significance

The 9e Bataljon Infanterie was one of the two or three most heavily engaged Dutch battalions of the spring [[1896]] campaign, present at every major Dutch action from the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|23 May offensive]] through the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June tuchtiging]]. Its succession of commanders from Bendien to Veeren mirrors the Dutch reshuffle that followed [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s arrival as [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]], and its 1e compagnie's storm of the [[Ateuh Raja]] *borstwering* on [[9 June]] [[1896]] — with fusilier v. Dongen killed at the moment of breakthrough — exemplifies the cost of Dutch frontal assaults against prepared Acehnese fortifications. The battalion's role in covering the [[Beradin (Vallei van Beradin / Kloof van Beradin)|Kloof van Beradin]] retreat on [[25 May]] [[1896]] was critical to the safe extraction of the Bergcolonne after the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Battle of Tjot Tjako]].

**9e Bataljon Infanterie** was a highly active Dutch infantry battalion during the [[First Aceh Expedition]].

## Engagements
During the [[First Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[10 April]] [[1873]], units of the 9th Battalion secured the flanks, utilizing terrain like [[Kandang Poth Djemaloon]] and [[Village Merdoehati]] for cover. Captain [[J.H. Huijer]] from the 9th Battalion led a heroic assault during the [[Second Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[14 April]] [[1873]], armed with modern breech-loading rifles (breech-loading rifles) that proved devastating to the defenders.

**Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** (Foot Marechaussee Corps in Aceh and Dependencies) was a specialized light infantry counter-insurgency unit of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] (KNIL), established by colonial decree on [[2 April]] [[1890]] to conduct mobile police and military operations against Acehnese resistance forces in [[Groot-Atjeh]] and throughout the region. Popularly known as the *Atjehsche Garde* (Aceh Guard), the corps became the primary instrument of Dutch military pacification in Aceh.

## Establishment and Evolution

The corps was created pursuant to [[Governementsbesluit of 2 April 1890]], initially intended to operate within the fortified lines of the [[Concentratie-Stelsel|geconcentreerde stelling]] (concentrated position) in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Its initial mandate was to perform police duties, maintain internal security, and prepare local conditions for a eventual transition to civil government under the [[Binnenlandsch Bestuur in Atjeh|Binnenlandsch Bestuur]].

Initially organized as a single division, the unit comprised 1 captain-commander, 1 first lieutenant, 1 European sergeant-major, 1 European quartermaster (*fourier*), 12 European sergeants, 12 non-European sergeants, 12 non-European corporals, and 180 non-European privates. Recruitment was strictly selective: officers and NCOs were chosen from the finest personnel of the KNIL, while European soldiers were required to attain NCO rank before admission. All members were required to learn the Acehnese language and local legal customs (*adat*).

In [[1896]], following the [[Treason of Teuku Umar]], the corps shifted under the initiative of Captain [[Jonkheer G. J. W. C. Graafland]] from static police duties to aggressive offensive counter-guerilla warfare. On [[3 March]] [[1899]], under [[Governementsbesluit of 3 March 1899]], the corps was expanded to 20 officers and 1,212 men divided into 5 divisions of 3 sections (*afdeelingen*), subdivided into 4 brigades each. Local administrative and military command was vested in the local commander at [[Kuta Raja]].

## Tactical Organization and Terminology

The Marechaussee adopted a highly flexible internal structure that mirrored Acehnese traditional titles and irregular warfare tactics:
- **Tuanku** (*Toeangkoe*): Division Commander
- **Uleebelang** (*Oëleuëbalang*): Captain / Detachment Commander
- **Teuku** (*Toekoe*): European NCO / Section Leader
- **Panglima**: Native Sergeant
- **Keutjih** (*Keuntjih*): Corporal
- **Kawan**: Marechaussee Private

Operatives moved light without heavy baggage, abandoning traditional European parade dress in favor of practical field gear. Their distinctive uniform featured red or orange collar patches known as [[Bloedvingers]] and sleeve scrolls. Weaponry originally consisted of the Beaumont carbine, later upgraded to the Mannlicher carbine, paired with the traditional [[Klewang]] saber, which allowed them to engage Acehnese fighters effectively in close-quarters jungle hand-to-hand combat.

## Major Campaigns and Operations

The corps played a decisive role in almost every major military action in Aceh between [[1890]] and [[1930]]:
- **[[Battle of Aneuk Galung]]** ([[29 June]] [[1896]]): The entire corps of 224 men stormed and captured the fortified Acehnese position in 15 minutes.
- **[[Pidie Expedition of 1898]]**: Spearheaded Colonel [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]]'s campaign into [[Pidie]].
- **[[Peusangan and Pasai Campaign of 1899]]**: Subjugated coastal insurgent centers in [[Peusangan]] and [[Pasai]].
- **[[Samalanga Campaign and Assault on Batèë Ilië]]**: Stormed the mountain fortress of [[Batèë Ilië]] in [[Samalanga]].
- **[[Gayo and Alas Lands Expedition of 1904]]**: Conducted a grueling five-month highland sweep under Lieutenant-Colonel [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]].
- **[[West Coast of Aceh Action 1925–1927]]**: Suppressed renewed anti-colonial uprisings near [[Meulaboh]] and [[Bakongan]], during which Captain [[J. Paris]] was killed and wounded soldiers [[Kromo-di-Koro]] and [[Suwoek]] earned fame for heroic defense.

In [[1914]], the corps was formally incorporated as an elite branch of the Infantry arm (*Wapen der Infanterie*). On [[2 April]] [[1930]], celebrating its 40th anniversary, Queen Wilhelmina bestowed upon the corps the royal right to carry its own military banner (*vaandel*).

## Significance

Over its forty years of continuous combat service in Aceh ([[1890]]–[[1930]]), approximately 950 members of the Korps Maréchaussée were killed in action and over 250 were wounded. The unit was widely regarded in Dutch military literature as the premier elite fighting force of the Netherlands East Indies, credited with forcing the military pacification of Aceh through relentless jungle patrolling and tactical adaptation.

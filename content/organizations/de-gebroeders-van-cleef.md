**De Gebroeders van Cleef** — the Brothers van Cleef — was the [['s-Gravenhage (The Hague)|'s-Gravenhage]] (The Hague) publishing house that issued, in [[1893]] alone, two of the principal colonial-policy tracts on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]: *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]* by [[W. A. Coblijn]], gepensionneerd Kolonel van het Oost-Indisch Leger — printed by Ruigrok & Co. at [[Haarlem]] — and *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]], Majoor der Infanterie, gewezen Chef van den Staf in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden — co-published with [[G. Kolff en Co.]] of [[Batavia]]. The firm's place on the title pages of both 1893 tracts is the principal trace of De Gebroeders van Cleef in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record.

## The 's-Gravenhage publishing house

De Gebroeders van Cleef was a Hague-based publishing firm — *te 's GRAVENHAGE*, as the title page of the [[1893]] Van Heutsz tract prints it. The Hague was, in the late nineteenth century, the metropolitan seat of Dutch colonial administration: the Ministerie van Koloniën was housed there, the Indisch Genootschap met there from [[1864]], and the colonial-policy press — the *Indische Gids*, the *Tijdspiegel*, the *Militairen Spectator* — was largely edited and printed in the Hague and in nearby Leiden. A Hague publishing house specialising in colonial-policy pamphlets and tracts — the kind of firm that would issue a tract by a serving Major of Infantry, formerly Chief of Staff in Atjeh, on the failure of twenty years of Dutch policy in the Atjeh-oorlog — was, in the publishing ecology of the 1890s Netherlands, the natural counterparty for an author like Van Heutsz.

A copy of the Coblijn pamphlet was stamped as belonging to the *Bibliotheek van de 2de Kamer der Staten-Generaal* (Library of the Second Chamber of the [[Staten-Generaal]]) and preserved in the parliamentary library at The Hague — the same institutional readership (the Tweede Kamer, the colonial-policy press, the Indisch Genootschap) to which both 1893 tracts were addressed.

## Coblijn's *Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen* (1893)

The Coblijn pamphlet was the war-critical treatise in which the management of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] by politics was indicted and a detailed [[Coblijn's programme for the subjugation of Atjeh|programme for the subjugation of Atjeh]] was set out. Printed by Ruigrok & Co. at [[Haarlem]], the work was issued by De Gebroeders van Cleef in the same year as the Van Heutsz tract — the two works together constituting a small 1893 sub-genre of Hague-published colonial-policy tracts diagnosing the failure of twenty years of Atjeh policy.

## Co-publication with G. Kolff en Co. of Batavia (the Van Heutsz tract)

The title page of the [[1893]] Van Heutsz tract names the two co-publishers in a single breath — *DE GEBROEDERS VAN CLEEF te 's GRAVENHAGE  /  G. KOLFF en Co. te BATAVIA* — and the verso of the title page repeats the dual imprint in the formal publication line: *DE GEBROEDERS VAN CLEEF  /  G. Kolff en Co.  /  1893.  /  BATAVIA*. The arrangement was a metropolitan-colonial co-publication: De Gebroeders van Cleef, in the Hague, served the Dutch metropolitan readership — the parliament, the press, the Indisch Genootschap, the colonial-policy public — while [[G. Kolff en Co.]] of Batavia served the colonial-Indies readership, civil and military, that was directly engaged in the Atjeh-oorlog. The dual imprint made the tract simultaneously available in the metropole and in the colony, and is the arrangement by which the work of a serving colonial officer could be made to circulate in both contexts at once. The tract opens with Van Heutsz's appeal that "De Atjeh-oorlog knaagt aan ons Koloniaal bezit, hij moet eindigen. Laten wij eindelijk aan de beschaafde wereld toonen, dat wij daartoe in staat zijn" (The Atjeh-war gnaws at our Colonial possession; it must end. Let us finally show the civilised world that we are capable of it).

## Significance

De Gebroeders van Cleef's significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record is bibliographic and contextual. The firm is the metropolitan publisher of two of the most important colonial-policy tracts on the Atjeh-oorlog printed in the Netherlands in 1893 — Coblijn's *Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen* and Van Heutsz's *De onderwerping van Atjeh* — the latter being the tract in which Van Heutsz set out the diagnosis of twenty years of failed policy that would, within a decade, prepare his own elevation to the Governorship of Atjeh and, ultimately, to the Governor-Generalship of the Dutch East Indies. The dual De Gebroeders van Cleef / [[G. Kolff en Co.]] imprint is also the standard bibliographic marker by which the [[1893]] first edition of *De onderwerping van Atjeh* is identified — the marker cited on the title page and reconstructed, with the OCR-noise typical of the 1893 tract's page-scan, in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] source record.

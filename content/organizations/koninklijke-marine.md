**The Koninklijke Marine** — the *Royal Netherlands Navy* — was the naval arm of the Dutch military that, throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] from [[1873]] to [[1896]] (and indeed to [[1918]]), provided the naval support for the Dutch military operations on land — blockading the Atjehese coasts, transporting troops and supplies, conducting amphibious operations, and supporting the Dutch *stelling* in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] from the seaward side. The Marine's role in the war was indispensable; its chronic under-resourcing, in Kruijt's analysis, was one of the principal causes of the protraction of the war.

## The Blockade (1873 onwards)

From the failure of the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|first expedition]] of [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]] onwards, the Marine maintained the blockade of the Atjehese coasts. In the beginning only four ships were available, but the fleet was soon significantly reinforced, so that by [[21 June]] [[21 June]] [[1873]] the blockade of the coast of Atjeh could be officially proclaimed and put into effect. The blockade was to prevent import and export and to cause the enemy as much break and damage as possible. Regular trade, however, according to a promise made to the Straits government, would not be hindered; fishing was permitted; and later, on licences from the Dutch consuls in the Straits ([[Penang|Penang]] and [[Singapore|Singapore]]), as much might be exported as would cover the outstanding advances on products. These exceptions had, in the period between the first and second expeditions, much damaged the usefulness of the blockade.

## The East Coast Administration under Bogaert (June 1873 – June 1875)

On the [[Oostkust|East Coast]], the naval officer [[C. H. Bogaert|C. H. Bogaert]] served as *stationscommandant* from [[June 1873|June]] [[1873]] and administered the whole East Coast from his ship — blockading, regulating, and governing on his own initiative — until the transfer of civil administration in [[June 1875|June]] [[1875]]. Bogaert's administration was the model of effective Dutch coastal control in Atjeh, and his name, Kruijt records, "*leeft nog onder de Atjehers voort*" (still lives on among the Atjehers).

## The 1884–1885 Blockade

The [[Blokkade (Atjeh, 1884-1885)|blockade of 1884]]–[[1885]] was decreed by the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[June 1884|June]] [[1884]] and placed under the command of [[C. H. Bogaert|Bogaert]], who came out from the Netherlands with his old energy. How bitterly he was disappointed: already before he had taken command, he heard at [[Batavia|Batavia]] that its early abolition had been decided. Numerous exceptions obstructed its whole execution and destroyed its effect. The blockade was wholly withdrawn in [[March 1885|March]] [[1885]].

## The 1886–1892 Blockade

The [[Blokkade (Atjeh, 1886-1892)|blockade of 1886]]–[[1892]] was enforced under [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]]'s governorship on the [[Noordkust|North]] and [[Westkust|West Coasts]]. "*Dit stelsel had een geduchte uitwerking en bleek een geweldig dwangmiddel*" (This system had a formidable effect and proved a powerful instrument of compulsion), Kruijt writes. The Marine had a heavy task, but received little cooperation from the administrative officials.

## The Chronic Under-Resourcing

The Marine in Atjeh was chronically under-resourced. By [[1881]] the Marine had been so reduced — "*eindelijk tot vier schepen ook weer die penny-wise and pound-foolish zuinigheidswaanzin*" (finally to four ships, that penny-wise and pound-foolish parsimony-madness) — that proper supervision of the coasts was no longer possible. The Dutch's penny-wise and pound-foolish parsimony reduced the Marine to four ships, so that proper supervision of the coasts was no longer possible, and the Dutch were unable to enforce the blockade or the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] effectively. The chronic under-resourcing of the Marine was, in Kruijt's analysis, one of the principal causes of the protraction of the war.

## The 1896 Reinforcement

After the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] of [[29 March]] [[29 March]] [[1896]], the Marine was reinforced and took a prominent part in the counter-offensive under [[Vetter|Vetter]]. The *marineschepen* *Sumbawa* and *Tromp* on the west side and *Flores* on the east side defended the port of [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]]. The Marine's bombardment of enemy positions, its transportation of troops, and its support of the land operations were indispensable to the Dutch war effort.

## Significance

The Koninklijke Marine is the indispensable naval arm of the Dutch military in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Its role in blockading the Atjehese coasts, in transporting troops and supplies, in conducting amphibious operations, and in supporting the Dutch *stelling* in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] from the seaward side was indispensable to the Dutch war effort. Its chronic under-resourcing — and the consequent inability to enforce the blockade or the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] effectively — was, in Kruijt's analysis, one of the principal causes of the protraction of the war. The Marine's eventual reinforcement after the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] was one of the principal means by which the Dutch counter-offensive of [[March 1896|March]]–[[April 1896|April]] [[1896]] was brought to a successful conclusion.

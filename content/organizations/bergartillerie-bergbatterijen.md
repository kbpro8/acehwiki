**The Bergartillerie** — also written *Bergbatterijen* in the plural, comprising the 1e Bergbatterij, 2e Bergbatterij, 4e Bergbatterij, and 5de Bergbatterij — was the mountain artillery arm of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]] in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] during the spring of [[1896]], employing guns that could be dismantled and transported on pack-animals through the difficult *sawah*, *kreupelbosch*, and ridge terrain of the [[Groot-Atjeh (Great Aceh)|Groot-Atjeh]] interior. The four batteries were commanded variously by Kapitein G. J. E. Nauta (1e), Kapitein M. G. J. Kempers (2e), Kapitein A. Bangert (4e), and an unnamed kapitein (5de), with sections under Luitenants Steenkamp, Deibert, son, Hoolboom, Vrijdag, van der Waarden, Stufken, and Kempers. The batteries provided the close artillery support that allowed Dutch infantry to storm prepared Acehnese *versterkingen* at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop on 17 April]], [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Tjot Tjako on 23 May]], and the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|stelling Daman on 25 May]].

## Background and Formation

The *bergbatterijen* of the colonial army were equipped with 7 c.M. A. Veld mountain guns — *vuurmonden* capable of being broken down for mule transport — and employed both shells, shrapnel shells, and canister shot against enemy fortifications. Two sections of the 4e Bergbatterij under Kapitein A. Bangert were already present in Atjeh before [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]; on the afternoon of [[29 March]] [[1896]] two sections of the 4e Bergbatterij were marched out to reinforce [[Lamdjamoe]] at 5 PM as part of Gen.-Maj. C. Deykerhoff's counter-measures. The 1e Bergbatterij under Kapitein G. J. E. Nauta arrived on [[8 April]] [[1896]] aboard the *General Pel* alongside the fourth company of the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]], and was initially encamped in and around the manege at [[Nesoch]] in [[Koeta Radja]]. The 2e Bergbatterij under Kapitein M. G. J. Kempers and the 5de Bergbatterij arrived with the second tranche of reinforcements aboard five [[Koninklijke Paketvaart-Maatschappij (KPM)|KPM]] steamers later in April [[1896]], the second bergbatterij being encamped on the high ground enclosed by the laboratory-Deijkerhofflaan-Oleh-leh road-Ketapan Doewa railway.

## Role in the Atjeh War

The bergbatterijen were engaged in nearly every Dutch column of the spring. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] two sections of the 4e Bergbatterij under Kapitein A. Bangert — 3 officers, 34 European and 26 native men, plus 32 riding/draft horses and pack animals — formed the artillery of [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s relief column for [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]]. On [[17 April]] [[1896]] the 4e Bergbatterij (under Kapitein A. Bangert, with sections under Lts. Steenkamp and Deibert, 6 *vuurmonden*) supported [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]]'s column at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop]] — Lt. Deibert's bergartillerie section came into position west of the road and opened fire together with the infantry, and Lt. Steenkamp's 2nd berg section talso position at height 25 to support the 2nd column. On [[2 May]] [[1896]] the 4e Bergbatterij again supported the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik]]. On [[4 May]] [[1896]] two sections of the 1e Bergbatterij under Kapitein Nauta supported the [[Second Expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb (4 May 1896)|second expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the 2e Bergbatterij under Kapitein Kempers — with two sections destined for [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Van Heutsz]] (only one made it up the mountain) — supported the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Battle of Tjot Tjako]], and on [[25 May]] [[1896]] the bergbatterijen supported the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|bestorming van de stelling Daman]]. On [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] the 2e and 4e Bergbatterijen supported the 2e (land) Colonne under [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]] in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing and Glé Broeë attack]].

## Composition and Strength

The bergbatterijen fielded the following strengths in the spring of [[1896]]: the 1e Bergbatterij under Kapitein G. J. E. Nauta — 3 officers, 60 minderen, 4 with sections under Lts. son and Hoolboom; the 2e Bergbatterij under Kapitein M. G. J. Kempers — 2 officers + 1 d.d. + 63 minderen + 4 *vuurmonden*; the 4e Bergbatterij under Kapitein A. Bangert — 3 officers, 88 minderen, 6 with sections under Lts. Steenkamp and Deibert; and the 5de Bergbatterij listed in the [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|forces table of 1 June 1896]]. Sections were commanded variously by Luitenants Steenkamp, Deibert, son, Hoolboom, Vrijdag, van der Waarden, Stufken, and Kempers. Ammunition types fired included *granaten*, *granaatkartetsen*, and *kartetsen* — the latter employed in canister-fire against storming parties of *djahats*.

## Significance

The bergbatterijen were indispensable to Dutch column warfare in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]]: without the ability to dismantle the guns for mule transport across *sawah* and ridge, no Dutch column could have assaulted prepared Acehnese *versterkingen* at ranges effective enough to suppress enemy fire. The 7 c.M. A. Veld gun was the workhorse piece of the spring [[1896]] campaign — though one was lost (without breech-block and sight) at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop on 17 April 1896]] in the confusion of the retreat. The arrival of four bergbatterijen by May [[1896]] reflects the scale of the Dutch artillery reinforcement programme summoned after [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]].

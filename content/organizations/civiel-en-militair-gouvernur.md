**The Civiel en Militair Gouvernur** — the *Civil and Military Governor* — was the Dutch colonial office, instituted in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] in succession to the *Civiele Gouverneur* (Civil Governor) abolished on the resignation of [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], that combined in one person the civil and military authority over [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. The office was held successively by [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] (1884–1886), [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] (1886–1891), [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]] (1891–1892), and [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]] (1892–1896), and was superseded in [[March 1896|March]] [[1896]] by the appointment of [[Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as *Regeeringscommissaris* (Government Commissioner).

## The Institution (September 1884)

When [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] retired in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]], the government — convinced that under the new circumstances of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] there was no longer place for civil government alone — decided to return to the earlier arrangement of civil and military authority in one hand, "*doch nu helaas op de puinhoopen van het gesloopte gebouw onzer macht onder van der Heijden*" (but now alas on the ruins of the demolished building of our power under Van der Heijden). Colonel [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] accordingly took office as *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur*.

## The Holders

The office was held successively by:

- [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] ([[September 1884|September]] [[1884]]–[[December 1886|December]] [[1886]]) — presided over the concentration and the failed blockade;
- [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] ([[December 1886|December]] [[1886]]–[[April 1891|April]] [[1891]]) — presided over the encirclement by [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and the pursuit of the Sultan's submission;
- [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]] ([[May 1891|May]] [[1891]]–[[January 1892|January]] [[1892]]) — presided over the *vriendelijke oorlog* and the arrival of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]];
- [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]] ([[January 1892|January]] [[1892]]–[[March 1896|March]] [[1896]]) — presided over the *Pahlawan-klucht* and the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]].

## The Supersession (March 1896)

The office of *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* was superseded in [[March 1896|March]] [[1896]] by the appointment of [[Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as *Regeeringscommissaris* (Government Commissioner), in the immediate aftermath of the defection of Teuku Umar. Vetter as *Regeeringscommissaris* superseded the Governor of Atjeh and reported directly to the Governor-General in Batavia, an arrangement that anticipated the centralised command structure under which the final pacification would be conducted under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]].

## Significance

The *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* is the principal Dutch colonial office in Atjeh during the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] (1884–1896). Its institution in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] marked the abandonment of the civil-government experiment of [[1881]]–[[1884]] and the return to combined civil-military authority; its supersession in [[March 1896|March]] [[1896]] by the appointment of Vetter as *Regeeringscommissaris* marked the abandonment of the governor system and the institution of centralised command. The four holders of the office — Demmeni, Van Teijn, Pompe, Deijkerhoff — together presided over the protracted Dutch engagement with the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]], the [[Restoration of the Sultanate|restoration of the Sultanate]] attempt, the arrival of Snouck Hurgronje, the arming of Teuku Umar, and the defection of Teuku Umar.

**Moucheron's firm** — the commercial house of the Zeeland merchant [[Balthasar de Moucheron|Balthasar de Moucheron]] of [[Middelburg|Middelburg]] — was the principal Zeeland voorcompagnie of the early Dutch-Atjeh trade, the outfitting firm of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage of 1599]] on the ships *De Leeuw* and *De Leeuwin* and of the [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|Spilbergh voyage of 1601-1602]] with three ships and the yacht *'t Lam. The firm is the principal Zeeland commercial actor of Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The Houtman brothers' voyage of 1598-1599

The [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage]] — the first Dutch voyage to Atjeh — was outfitted by Moucheron's firm. After [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]]'s return from the first Dutch voyage to [[Bantam|Bantam]] in [[1597]] and the subsequent legal disputes, Moucheron — who "waarschijnlijk, en tot zijn nadeel, geen geloof sloeg aan de geruchten over en processen tegen hem" — gave Houtman command of two ships, *De Leeuw* and *De Leeuwin*, which left [[Vlissingen|Vlissingen]] on [[25 March]] [[1598]] with the English pilot [[John Davis|John Davis]] as stuurman. The ships anchored off Atjeh on [[24 June]] [[1599]]. The voyage ended in the ambush of [[11 September]] [[1599]] in which Cornelis was killed and [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik]] taken captive.

## The Spilbergh fleet of 1601-1602

Moucheron's firm then outfitted the fleet of three ships and the yacht *'t Lam under [[Joris van Spilbergen|Joris van Spilbergen]], which left Middelburg on [[5 May]] [[1601]]. Near the [[Comoro Islands|Comoros]] the yacht became separated from the others and was so damaged that [[Cornelis Spex|Cornelis Spex]] put in at Atjeh in early September [[1602]] and placed himself under [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]]'s command. Spilbergh himself arrived a few days later with the other two ships, having called at [[Ceylon|Ceylon]] on the way. Spilbergh joined Lancaster in the [[Capture of the Portuguese carrack off Atjeh (October 1602)|capture of the Portuguese carrack of 3 October 1602]] for an eighth of the prize.

## The absorption into the VOC (1602)

With the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the VOC on 20 March 1602]], Moucheron's firm was wound up and its assets and personnel absorbed into the Zeeland chamber of the united company. [[Balthasar de Moucheron|Balthasar de Moucheron]] became one of the founding Bewindhebbers of the Zeeland chamber, listed in the charter of [[20 March]] [[1602]] alongside Adriaen Hendircxsz. ten Haeff, Jacob Boreel, and the other Zeeland merchants. The absorption marked the institutional end of the pre-VOC Zeeland East India trade and the beginning of the unified Dutch-Indies enterprise.

## Significance

Moucheron's firm is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Zeeland voorcompagnie of the early Dutch-Atjeh trade and the principal commercial actor of the pre-VOC Zeeland East India trade. The [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman voyage of 1599]] — the first Dutch visit to Atjeh — was outfitted by the firm; the [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|Spilbergh voyage of 1601-1602]] — the closing Dutch visit to Atjeh of the pre-VOC period — was also outfitted by the firm. The firm's absorption into the Zeeland chamber of the VOC in [[1602]] marked the institutional end of the pre-VOC period and the beginning of the unified Dutch-Indies trade. Moucheron's role in the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship — outfitting both the opening and the closing Dutch voyages of the pre-VOC period — frames the firm as both foundational and transitional: it opened the trade and shepherded it into the unified company that would carry it forward.

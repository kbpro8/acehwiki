**The Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut** — the Royal Colonial Institute Society — was the [[Amsterdam]]-based learned society that, from its foundation in the early twentieth century, oversaw the scientific and commercial investigation of the colonies of the [[Netherlands]] through its constituent *Afdeelingen* (departments), including the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]]. The Institute is the institutional author — through the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum — of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] and of the *Berichten* series of which that pamphlet is No. 57, and it is the institutional context in which the evaluation and promotion of colonial commodities — including Atjeh-turpentine — was undertaken in the late [[1920s]] and early [[1930s]].

## The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum

The [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] was the department of the Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut charged with the evaluation and promotion of colonial commodities. The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum maintained a *Scheikundig Laboratorium* (Chemical Laboratory) in which the chemical composition and properties of colonial products were investigated, and it published the results of its investigations in the *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum van de Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut* — the series of which the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] is No. 57. The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum also maintained a system of *H.M. monster* (H.M. sample) numbering — by which individual samples of colonial commodities were catalogued and referenced — of which *H.M. no. 1504*, *H.M. no. 1553* and *H.M. no. 1607* are the three Atjeh-turpentine samples evaluated in the 1930 pamphlet.

## The Director

The Director of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum in [[1930]] was [[L. P. de Bussy]] — the signatory of the [[Circulaire of 5 May 1930 to the V.V.V.F.|circular letter of 5 May 1930]] by which sample *H.M. no. 1607* of Atjeh-turpentine was distributed to the members of the *[[Vereeniging van Vernis- en Verffabrikanten en -Handelaren in Nederland]]*. The Director's signature on the circular — *W. g. L. P. DE BUSSY, Directeur der Afdeeling Handelsmuseum ten Koloniaal Instituut* — is the principal documentary evidence of the institutional authorship of the Atjeh-turpentijn evaluation of [[1930]].

## The Berichten series

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] is No. 57 of the *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum van de Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut*. The list of *Berichten* preserved at the back of the pamphlet records the breadth of the series: from *Bericht* No. 1 (*Enkele nieuwe gegevens over Java-jute*, [[1920]]) by [[L. P. de Bussy|de Bussy]] to *Bericht* No. 56 (*Proeven over de verscheping van enkele oliehoudende tropische grondstoffen in geperste balen*, [[1930]]) by [[W. Spoon|Spoor]]. The range of subjects — from Java-jute to Atjeh-turpentine, from gambir to cajeput oil, from Cuban cubebs to Surinamese balata-milk — illustrates the breadth of the Afdeeling Handelsmuseum's remit: the systematic evaluation and promotion of colonial commodities on behalf of the metropolitan Dutch industry.

## The Centrale Boekerij

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the *Berichten* and other publications of the Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut were available from the *Centrale Boekerij, Mauritskade 62, Amsterdam*. The *Centrale Boekerij* was thus the distribution point for the publications of the Institute, and the address — Mauritskade 62, Amsterdam — is the principal published address of the Institute in [[1930]].

## Significance

The Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the institutional author — through its Afdeeling Handelsmuseum — of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn evaluation of 1930]] and of the *Berichten* series of which that pamphlet is No. 57. The Institute is the institutional context in which the evaluation and promotion of Atjeh-turpentine — and of the broader colonial commodity production of the late [[1920s]] and early [[1930s]] — was undertaken, and its Afdeeling Handelsmuseum is the principal published source for the early factory-scale production of Indonesian turpentine.

**The Bereden-artillerie** — also written mounted or horse artillery — was a mounted artillery unit of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]] attached to the overall staff in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] during the spring of [[1896]], commanded by Luit.-kol. W. Boetje. Unlike the [[Bergartillerie / Bergbatterijen|bergbatterijen]] (mountain batteries) whose *vuurmonden* were dismantled for mule transport, the bereden-artillerie was organized as a horse-drawn field battery for rapid deployment along the roads and trails around [[Koeta Radja]]. The unit was attached to the overall staff during the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 expedition]] under [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s overall command.

## Background and Formation

As a bereden (mounted) battery of the colonial artillery, the bereden-artillerie was equipped with horse-drawn field guns capable of rapid tactical movement. Its colonel, Luit.-kol. W. Boetje, was attached to the overall staff during the spring [[1896]] operations, suggesting that the unit's role was as much staff coordination of all artillery in the Atjeh theatre as direct tactical employment of a single battery. The bereden-artillerie is distinct both from the [[Bergartillerie / Bergbatterijen|bergbatterijen]] (mountain batteries) and from the [[Vestingartillerie (Fortress Artillery)|vestingartillerie]] (fortress artillery, which provided *stukcommandanten* for the post guns).

## Role in the Atjeh War

The bereden-artillerie, under Luit.-kol. W. Boetje, was attached to the overall staff during the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 expedition]] against the V Moekims Montassik and XIII Moekims Toengkoeb regions, the last major operation of the spring [[1896]] campaign. The expedition was commanded overall by [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort]] with three colonnes: 1e Colonne (Lt. Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck: marechaussée + [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e]] and [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljons]], 4de Bergbatterij, Genie); 2e Colonne (Lt. Col. Van Heutsz: [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e]] and [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljons]], 2e Bergbatterij, Genie); Reserve-Colonne (Lt. Col. Soeters: [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e]] and [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljons]] + mixed battalion (Steinmetz), 1e Bergbatterij, Genie). The combat of [[16 June]] [[1896]] ranged across Tjot Maraja, Balik Angin, Toei Selimbing, Aloer Lintang, [[Lam-Goet|Lam-Nga]], Lam-Pasei, and Lam-Bada; the Panteh Karang redoute was stormed; on [[17 June]] [[1896]] Lam-Soet was burned and the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* stormed by the compagnie of Kapitein Linck of the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e Bataljon]].

## Composition and Strength

The bereden-artillerie was commanded by Luit.-kol. W. Boetje, attached to the overall staff. The unit's strength and exact number of *vuurmonden* are not separately recorded in the [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|strength return of 1 June 1896]]; it is listed alongside the [[Bergartillerie / Bergbatterijen|bergbatterijen]] and the [[Vestingartillerie (Fortress Artillery)|vestingartillerie]] in the overall artillery of the Atjeh garrison.

## Significance

The bereden-artillerie's role in the spring [[1896]] campaign was that of a staff-coordinated mounted battery, distinct from the operational mountain batteries that supported each colonne. Its attachment to the overall staff during the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 expedition]] reflects the Dutch practice of holding a horse-drawn battery in general reserve for rapid deployment, in contrast to the mule-borne *bergbatterijen* that could only follow a column at foot pace.

**The 7e Bataljon Infanterie** — also written *7de Bataljon* — was a Dutch colonial field infantry battalion of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]] that arrived in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] aboard the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart-Maatschappij (KPM)|KPM]] steamers in late April [[1896]] as part of the second tranche of reinforcements summoned after [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. Commanded by Maj. J. R. Jacobs, the battalion was first encamped south of the 9e Bataljon's campement at [[Nesoch]] in [[Koeta Radja]] and went on to fight at [[Lam-Asan]] and [[Lam-Tjroeng / Daman / Lam-Poelau|Lam Tjroeng]] in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]], the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe of 29 May 1896]], and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik of 16–17 June 1896]]. Its companies were led by Kapitein van Gemmingen (1e compagnie), Kapitein Scheuer (3e compagnie), and Kapitein Broekhoff.

## Background and Formation

As a *veldbataljon* of the colonial army, the 7e Bataljon combined European and indigenous companies armed with Beaumont rifles. Its arrival in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] was part of the reinforcement programme approved after [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s appointment as [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]]: five KPM steamers brought the 5e and 7e Bataljons Infanterie, a bergbatterij, a cavalerie peloton, and suppletietroepen. Buildings of kasso's, planks, galvanized iron, or asphalt felt were erected for the arriving 7e Bataljon south of the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]]'s campement at [[Nesoch]] because of the approaching west monsoon, the lack of more temporary building materials (bamboo, atap), and the expectation that the lodgings would be used for some time. On [[4 May]] [[1896]] the battalion formed part of Luit.-kol. H. Bendien's expedition to the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] — its 1e compagnie (under Kapitein van Gemmingen) leaning with its right *vleugel* against the sawah south-west of Lam Beheu, and a compagnie occupying the south-east rand of Lam Reh against *twintigtal* armed men in the *masdjid*.

## Role in the Atjeh War

On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the 7e Bataljon was initially held in reserve at [[Ketapan Doea]] during the opening of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]]. Later that day, however, the battalion was committed alongside the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] in the assault that talso [[Lam-Asan]], and on [[25 May]] [[1896]] it fought at [[Lam-Tjroeng / Daman / Lam-Poelau|Lam Tjroeng]]. On [[29 May]] [[1896]] the battalion formed part of Luit.-kol. C. J. Laceulle's colonne in the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe]] under Maj. J. R. Jacobs. On [[16 June]] [[1896]] the battalion — as the 2e Colonne under [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] — formed part of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik and XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]], fighting at Tjot Maraja, Balik Angin, Toei Selimbing, Aloer Lintang, [[Lam-Goet|Lam-Nga]], Lam-Pasei, and Lam-Bada, and storming the Panteh Karang redoute on [[16 June]] [[1896]]. On [[17 June]] [[1896]] the battalion continued operations, with Lam-Soet burned and the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* eventually stormed by the compagnie of Kapitein Linck of the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e Bataljon]].

## Composition and Strength

The [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|strength return of 1 June 1896]] records the 7e Bataljon as 15 officers and 409 minderen under Maj. J. R. Jacobs. Adjudant to Majoor Jacobs was 1e luitenant adjudant E. C. van der Heijden. Company commanders during the spring included Kapitein van Gemmingen (1e compagnie), Kapitein Scheuer (3e compagnie), and Kapitein Broekhoff. The battalion's repeated commitment in the May–June [[1896]] operations — from [[Lam-Asan]] through [[Lam-Tjroeng / Daman / Lam-Poelau|Lam Tjroeng]] to the V Moekims Montassik — indicates heavy operational tempo with correspondingly high ammunition expenditure and fatigue.

## Significance

The 7e Bataljon Infanterie was a late-arriving reinforcement battalion that, despite its short residence in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] by the end of June [[1896]], fought in nearly every major Dutch column from the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|23–25 May offensive]] onward. Its deployment alongside the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] in the 2e Colonne of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|V Moekims Montassik tuchtiging]] under [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] reflects the Dutch practice of pairing experienced and newly arrived battalions in mixed colonnes. The Panteh Karang redoubt — stormed on [[16 June]] [[1896]] — was the reported refuge of the [[The Pretender Sultan (de pretendent sultan)|pretender sultan]], who fled on the Dutch approach.

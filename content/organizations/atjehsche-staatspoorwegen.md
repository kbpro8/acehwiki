**The Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen** — the Atjeh state railways — was the Dutch colonial state railway of the *gewest* of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], operating the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh tram]] between [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] / [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]] and the inland outposts of the noordkust and the *sagi der XXII moekims*, and providing the principal logistical connection between the colonial capital and the inland outposts of the Korps Maréchaussee and the garrison battalions. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen is the principal logistical infrastructure of the pre-invasion Atjeh campaign — and the principal target of the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] sabotage of February–March [[1942]].

## The tramway

The Atjeh tram was a military-colonial railway built from [[1875]] onward (see [[Construction of the Atjeh-tram (1875-1914)|construction of the Atjeh-tram]]) and completed in its full Kotaradja–inland extent by [[1914]]. The tramway connected the colonial capital at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]] with the inland outposts of the noordkust and the *sagi der XXII moekims*, with a western terminus at the harbour of [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] and inland extensions to [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]], [[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]], [[Sigli|Sigli]] and (via [[Indrapuri|Indrapuri]]) the upper Atjeh river plain. The tramway was the principal means of transport for troops, provisions and (in March [[1942]]) for the evacuation of European women and children from Kotaradja to Medan.

## The Von Sperling murder and the workshop plunder

The Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen's operations manager in [[1942]] was Count [[U. Bernstorff von Sperling|U. Bernstorff von Sperling]]. On the morning of [[24 February]] [[24 February 1942|24 February 1942]] — the morning after the [[murder of Tiggelman at Seulimeum|murder of Tiggelman]] at [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] — Von Sperling wished personally to inspect the damage done to the railway track. His inspection trip, with a single infantry brigade as escort, was ambushed at a sabotaged bridge about 25 km south of Kotaradja. The staff history records that, during the peak night of [[23 February|23]]–[[24 February]] [[24 February 1942|24 February]], the workshop of the Atjeh state railways was looted by the F-beweging — that is, the railway's principal workshop was looted in the same coordinated action that produced the Tiggelman murder.

## The 12 March 1942 evacuation

On the morning of [[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March 1942]], as the Japanese landings were under way east of Kotaradja, the last Dutch troops left Kotaradja at approximately 07.00. The last train carrying women and children to Medan was scheduled to depart at 06.30. Captain [[L. P. E. Kloprogge|L. P. E. Kloprogge]] decided to ride that train himself to [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]]. The train was delayed by the great influx of refugees, by the need to shunt wagons, and by the absence of trained railway personnel — a city watchman, a former engine driver, was finally induced to drive the train. The train was fired on en route by armed Atjehnese, and a steam roller had to be cleared from the tracks. The 12 March episode is the staff history's principal instance of the Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen's last operational use before the Japanese occupation.

## The 12 March 1942 sabotage

The Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen's tracks, points and workshop were the principal targets of the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] sabotage of February–March [[1942]]. On the peak night of [[23 February|23]]–[[24 February]] [[24 February 1942|24 February]], between Kotaradja and Seulimeum the railway track was torn up at two points, points were disabled, a steam roller was driven across the road, and a workshop of the Atjeh state railways was looted. The Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen's installations were thus systematically disabled by the F-beweging in coordination with the broader rising — a fact the staff history treats as evidence of the F-beweging's strategic objective of cutting the Dutch logistical connection between Kotaradja and the inland outposts.

## Significance

The Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the principal logistical infrastructure of the pre-invasion Atjeh campaign — and the principal strategic target of the F-beweging. The staff history's emphasis on the systematic disabling of the track, points and workshop in February–March [[1942]] frames the F-beweging's sabotage as a strategically informed attack on the Dutch colonial state's logistical capacity, rather than as random vandalism. The tramway's last operational use — the 06.30 evacuation train of [[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March]] — is the staff history's principal instance of the colonial infrastructure's final service to the Dutch evacuation, immediately before the Japanese capture of Kotaradja.

## Account from *DLP-84-Atjeh* — the military-economic railway and the broader transport network

The *DLP-84-Atjeh* pamphlet describes the [[Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen|Atjehspoorweg (Atjeh railway)]] as the colonial railway built in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] for military and economic purposes. Together with the [[Grote Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road (*Grote Atjehweg*)]] and the [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajo Road (*Gajoweg*)]], the railway formed a transport network that opened the interior to trade and settlement. The railway facilitated rapid troop movement during the lingering security concerns after the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]], and enabled the export of plantation products — rubber, palm oil and sisal — via the port of [[Belawan|Belawan]].

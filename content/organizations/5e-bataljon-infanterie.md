**The 5e Bataljon Infanterie** — also written *5de Bataljon* — was a Dutch colonial field infantry battalion of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]] that arrived in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] aboard the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart-Maatschappij (KPM)|KPM]] steamers in April [[1896]] as part of the reinforcements summoned after [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. Commanded by Luit.-kol. C. J. Laceulle, the battalion was first quartered in various districts of [[Koeta Radja]] — including the Koeta Alam barracks, Soerabaijawijk, Pendetie, and the European schools — and went on to play a central role in the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's house on 24 May 1896]] and in the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik of 16–17 June 1896]]. Its 4e compagnie under Kapitein Linck became one of the most heavily engaged company-sized units of the entire semester.

## Background and Formation

As a *veldbataljon* of three companies (by June [[1896]]), the 5e Bataljon comprised both European and indigenous companies armed with the Beaumont service rifle. Its arrival in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] was part of the second tranche of reinforcements — five KPM steamers that brought the 5e and 7e Bataljons, a bergbatterij, a cavalerie peloton, and suppletietroepen — under the programme of reinforcements sought by Gen.-Maj. C. Deykerhoff and approved after [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s appointment as [[Regeeringscommissaris (Government Commissioner)|Regeeringscommissaris]]. Because of the approaching west monsoon, the lack of more temporary building materials (bamboo, atap), and the expectation that lodgings would be used for some time, buildings of kasso's, planks, galvanized iron, or asphalt felt were erected for the arriving 5e Bataljon across the Koeta Alam barracks, Soerabaijawijk, Pendetie, and the European schools of [[Koeta Radja]].

## Role in the Atjeh War

The 5e Bataljon's most significant engagement was the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's house on 24 May 1896]]. Under Luit.-kol. C. J. Laceulle — leading a colonne of 12 officieren and 364 minderen — the battalion advanced on the stelling where [[Teuku Umar]] had established his residence. Kapitein P. Linck (Kapitein) led his 4e compagnie's sectie into Lam-Pisang immediately after 1e luit. de Jongh of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] had first reached the *verblijf* at 10:35 VM, while Kapitein Boer (1e compagnie) commanded the other company. On [[10 June]] [[1896]] the battalion's companies provided *flankdekking* on the retreat from the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|Lam-Krak tuchtiging]]. On [[17 June]] [[1896]] Kapitein Linck's 4e compagnie stormed the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* in the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|V Moekims Montassik tuchtiging]], leading the attack that opened the final phase of the spring campaign. On [[20 June]] [[1896]] Linck commanded two companies of the 5e Bataljon through the [[III Moekims Daroe]] in the mopping-up operations of June.

## Composition and Strength

The battalion's strength in late May [[1896]] was 12 officieren and 364 minderen under Luit.-kol. C. J. Laceulle. By the [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|strength return of 1 June 1896]] the battalion reported 13 officers and 327 minderen organized in three companies. Company commanders during the spring included Kapitein Boer (1e compagnie) and Kapitein P. Linck (4e compagnie), the latter emerging as one of the most consistently engaged Dutch company officers of the campaign. 1e luit. M. D. J. de Jongh of the 1e compagnie was — in Kruisheer's reckoning — first to reach Oemar's *verblijf* at 10:35 VM on [[24 May]] [[1896]], a distinction the source sets against the rival claim of the cavalryman Wachtmeester Jonker.

## Significance

The 5e Bataljon's significance in the spring [[1896]] campaign lies above all in the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|24 May capture of Lam-Pisang]], the operational climax of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] that ended the immediate military threat posed by [[Teuku Umar]]'s *benden van T. Oemar* to [[Koeta Radja]]. The battalion's 4e compagnie under Kapitein P. Linck — the same compagnie that stormed the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* on [[17 June]] [[1896]] — exemplifies the way in which individual company commanders and their *inlandse* and European ranks bore the real burden of Dutch column fighting. The battalion's strength of three companies, smaller than the four-company battalions more common in the campaign, reflects the colonial army's practice of deploying partial battalions to meet operational demands.

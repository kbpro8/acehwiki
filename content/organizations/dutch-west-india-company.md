**Dutch West India Company** (West‑Indische Compagnie, WIC) was the chartered company of the Dutch Republic responsible for trade, colonisation, and naval warfare in the Atlantic world, including the Caribbean islands such as [[St. Eustatius]]. Although its primary focus lay in the Atlantic, the WIC’s activities intersected with the Dutch East India Company’s Asian operations, influencing the overall Dutch imperial strategy that also encompassed the Sultanate of [[Aceh]].

## Role in the 1780‑1784 Conflict
The source notes that during the war that began on [[20 December]] [[1780]] the Dutch Republic’s neutral stance was compromised by the WIC’s involvement in supplying the rebellious colonies of the West Indies, notably the “Golden Rock” of [[St. Eustatius]]. The capture of Dutch merchant ships by the British and the loss of WIC possessions such as [[Demerara]] and [[Berbice]] weakened Dutch naval resources that would otherwise have been available for campaigns against Aceh.

## Connection to Aceh
The weakening of Dutch maritime capacity due to WIC losses indirectly affected the VOC’s ability to project power in the East Indies, including the enforcement of treaties and military actions against Aceh. Consequently, the Dutch West India Company is part of the broader colonial context that shaped Dutch‑Aceh relations in the late eighteenth century.

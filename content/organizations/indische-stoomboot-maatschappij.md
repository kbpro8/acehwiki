The **[[Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij|Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij]]** (Indies Steamship Company) was the private shipping contractor engaged by the Dutch colonial government to transport troops, stores and materieel to and from [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Under the terms of its contract, the government paid a *[[Liggelden|liggeld]]* (demurrage) of ƒ 1,200 for every day a ship was held at the roadstead beyond the first 24 hours after arrival. The company's transports — the *Prins Alexander der Nederlanden*, the *Baron Sloet van de Beele* and others — feature repeatedly in Borel's account of the chaotic debarkation at [[Oeleh Leh]] and of the cost overruns that heaped scandal on the conduct of the war.

## The Contract and the Liggelden

The contract between the Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij and the Dutch colonial government fixed a *liggeld* of ƒ 1,200 per day for every ship held beyond 24 hours after arrival at the Atjeh roadstead. The arrangement was, in Borel's diagnosis, a structural incentive to delay: every additional day at anchor was a fresh ƒ 1,200 to the contractor, while the government bore the cost in money, in men (through cholera bred in the overcrowded ships), and in operational tempo (through troops kept idly afloat when they were needed ashore). The single most damaging example Borel cites is the *Baron Sloet van de Beele*, which arrived with ~400 reinforcements on [[11 June]] [[1874]] and alone cost the government almost ƒ 17,000 in demurrage before its troops could be landed — a sum equal to roughly fourteen days of contracted delay. Borel treats the demurrage system as one of the symptomatic failures of Dutch preparation: it compounded the absence of a [[Zeehoofd|zeehoofd]] (sea-pier) at Oeleh Leh and the criminal overcrowding of the transports on the outward voyage from Java.

## The Transports and the Debarkation Chaos

The Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij's ships carried the troops and koelies of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]] from Java to the Atjeh roadstead in November–December [[1873]]. Troops and koelies were unnecessarily crowded onto too few transports, causing "groote ellende" during the voyage and during the stay on the reede; the economy measure "door niets gewettigd" fed the cholera that had been brought from [[Batavia]] and cost "vele menschenlevens." [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] himself departed Atjeh on [[25 April]] [[1874]] on the stoomschip *Prins Alexander der Nederlanden* — the same vessel that had carried him thither. After the [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)|departure of the hoofdmacht]] the company's transports carried the trickle of reinforcements — the *Baron Sloet van de Beele* on [[11 June]] [[1874]], the *Prins Alexander* with a couple hundred more on [[12 June]] [[1874]], the 6de bataljon, the 5de and 10de bataljons in December [[1874]], the [[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|Barissans]] on [[10 December]] [[1875]] and the 9de bataljon on [[21 December]] [[1875]] — that sustained the [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]] under [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]]. Borel, who as an artillery officer personally supervised the debarkation of the guns at Oeleh Leh, describes the operations at the roadstead in detail: the lack of a pier forced the lightering of guns, horses and stores through the surf, an operation that exposed men and materieel to the enemy's fire and to the kwade moesson that broke on [[31 May]] [[1874]].

## The Costs of the War

The Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij is also part of Borel's indictment of the financial costs of the Atjeh-oorlog. The demurrage paid to the company — running, on the *Baron Sloet van de Beele* alone, to almost ƒ 17,000 — is cited alongside the costs of the [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]], the [[Vereniging Het Roode Kruis in Atjeh|Red Cross comforts]], the [[Kettinggangers in Atjeh|kettinggang convicts]] and the spoorweg K. Radja–Oeleh Leh as part of the "kolossale offers" the war had already cost by [[1878]]. Borel's proposed [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] of 3 000–4 000 men, estimated at ƒ 6 000 000 for the first year and ƒ 10–15 million for the whole two years, was, he argued, no more than a fraction of what the war was already costing in demurrage, reinforcements, and the slow attrition of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] — and would, by ending the war, eliminate the very demand for the company's transports that the demurrage fed.

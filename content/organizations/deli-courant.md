**The Deli Courant** was the newspaper of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] whose notice of the opening of [[Tamiang]] to land concessions, reprinted in *[[Het Vaderland]]*, was cited by [[Pieter Jelles Troelstra|Troelstra]] in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]].

## The notice

The report, as Troelstra summarised it, stated "dat Tamiang weldra zal worden opengesteld en dat de houders van aanspraken op landconcessiën zullen worden opgeroepen om daarvan te doen blijken." — that Tamiang will soon be opened up and that the holders of claims to land concessions will be called upon to prove them. Troelstra used it as evidence that since the more aggressive policy in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] had begun, the exploitation of land in the districts bordering Atjeh also appeared likely to increase.

## Context

The *Deli Courant* was published in the region of the tobacco estates whose interests Troelstra alleged to be bound up with the war: [[Deli]], Batoe Bahra, [[Serdang]] and [[Asahan|Assahan]], where according to the *Indische Regeeringsalmanak* of [[1894]] some hundred and thirty tobacco estates had been established, of which the [[Deli Maatschappij]] was the chief. Its readership was precisely the body of planters and concession-holders whose position in the Atjeh question the socialists sought to expose; see [[Economic interests alleged behind the Atjeh-oorlog (1897)]].

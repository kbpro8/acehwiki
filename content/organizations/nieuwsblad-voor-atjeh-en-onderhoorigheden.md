The **Nieuwsblad voor Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** — begun in [[1900]] when the [[Deli Courant]] of [[Medan]] opened a filial at [[Koeta Radja]] and "immediately thereafter" founded the paper, appearing at Langhout's time twice a week with a respectable circulation — was the newspaper of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], called in Governor [[Van Sluys|Van Sluys]]'s years *Het Atjehsch Nieuwsblad*, and one of what author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] calls the "steunpilaren der pacificatie" ("pillars of the pacification").

## Foundation and premises

In [[1900]] the undertaking of the Nieuwsblad was opened in a small building on the Peunajoengsingel at Koeta Radja. As the first industrial establishment of its kind in Atjeh, the Atjeh printing office enjoyed straightway the powerful support of the then Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies, General [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]; thanks also to the support of the officials of the Civil Administration in Atjeh and of the manager of the [[Atjeh Tram]], the building in which the printing office began its work in 1900 proved too small already in [[1902]], so that a greater printing-office building had to be sought. In [[1903]] the N.V. de Deli Courant built a spacious building for press and bookshop on a suitable terrain, situated *aan den Peunajoengsingel* (on the Peunajoengsingel), which was occupied in the same year; beside the press, the stock included a bookshop. Through the ever increasing turnover more space was again needed in [[1913]]: by purchase the Atjeh printing office and bookshop came in [[1914]] into possession of a capital building at the Sociëteitsweg, formerly inhabited by the firm [[Kanner]], and her enterprise was transferred into it; from that time bookshop and printing office with bindery and magazines dispose of very roomy and solid premises, in which they are still established (photograph plate: "De Atjehdrukkerij te Koeta-Radja. Boven: het gebouw in 1900. Beneden: het gebouw thans in gebruik" — "The Atjeh press at Koeta Radja. Above: the building in 1900. Below: the building currently in use").

## Role

It was this paper which, under Governor Van Sluys, "zorgde steeds voor goede verslagen van de vergaderingen der commissiën [der plaatselijke fondsen] en stelde zijn kolommen voor nadere besprekingen daarover open" ("always cared for good reports of the meetings of the commissions [of the local funds] and opened its columns for further discussions of them"), serving the municipal-in-embryo debates of 1922–1923. Langhout points to the paper as the first of the organs by which European and native opinion in the province would have to follow "the finger-pointing to what lies ahead".

**Javasche Bank te Koetaradja** was the branch of the Javasche Bank at [[Koeta Radja]], the first stone of whose building was laid by Governor [[H. N. A. Swart]] in [[1915]].

A photograph in [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]]'s book shows the ceremony: "Governor Swart lays the first stone of the building for the Java Bank at Koetaradja (1915)." It belongs among the visible signs of the economic normalisation of Atjeh in the second half of Swart's governorship.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **Javasche Bank te Koeta Radja** — the agency of the [[Javasche Bank|Javabank]] in the capital of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], opened on [[2 December]] [[1918]] in a building completed at the end of November of that year — was founded principally to give a good course to the circulation of silver and paper money, for which the Landskas (provincial treasury) was no longer adequately equipped, and became a visible ornament of the place whose first stone Governor [[H. N. A. Swart]] himself had laid.

### Foundation

Already in [[1915]] the Direction of the Bank conceived the plan of erecting an agency at Koeta Radja; in [[1916]] the building of an office was begun, completed at the end of November [[1918]] and taken into use on 2 December of that year. As the book's photograph shows ("het gebouw een sieraad voor de plaats" — "the building may be called an ornament of the place"), it answered all requirements that can be made of a modern office building in the Indies. At the foundation of the agency the heer [[J. Straatemeier]] was appointed agent, with the heeren [[Ch. Chr. Ouwerling]] and [[J. Boon Jzn.]] as commissarissen; at the time of the book (1922–1923) the heer [[H. A. Burlage]] acted as agent with the heeren [[H. J. E. Moll]] and [[J. Boon Jzn.]] as commissarissen. Governor Swart laid the first stone for the bank building.

### Working and figures

Beside its functioning as centre for the circulation of silver and paper money, the agency proved already in the short period it worked to fulfil a need for the exchange traffic with the Bank's other Indian establishments; since that traffic generally indicates the extent of the trade movement, Langhout quotes from the Javasche Bank year-reports the remittances (*algiften*) of the Indian offices to Koeta Radja and vice versa:

- book-year 1919–'20: f 3,107,042.50
- book-year 1920–'21: f 3,763,503.75
- book-year 1921–'22: f 5,761,201.91

— a tripling within three years that mirrors the post-war recovery of Atjehnese trade. It was after a discussion of the Bank's President-Director with Governor Swart that the opening of the agency had been decided.

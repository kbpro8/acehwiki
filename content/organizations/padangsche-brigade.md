The **[[Padangsche brigade|Padangsche brigade]]** was the third brigade of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Dutch expedition]] against [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]], left in its entirety at [[Padang]] on the west coast of Sumatra by [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] in December [[1873]], in defiance of the advice of his tweede bevelhebber [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]]. Borel calls the decision to leave the brigade behind "eene groote strategische fout" — the single most consequential error of the expedition's opening phase — because it forfeited the chance to take the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] by pincer movement and allowed the Atjehnese hooffdmacht to withdraw unbroken. The Padangsche brigade's eventual arrival in Atjeh in January [[1874]] came too late to influence the fall of the Kraton and found the Dutch already committed to van Swieten's *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture).

## Composition and Role

The Padangsche brigade was one of the three brigades fully equipped for the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]] against Atjeh, the others being the 1ste brigade under [[Generaal Verspijck|Verspijck]] and the 2de brigade under [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]]. Its composition included regular infanterie, a veld-batterij, and — when its elements finally reached Atjeh — the [[Compagnie mariniers in Atjeh|mariniers en Afrikanen]] (marine corps and African, i.e. West Indian, troops) who arrived on [[20 January]] [[1874]] and the 6de bataljon Infanterie that arrived on [[1 February]] [[1874]]. The brigade was meant to be landed together with the others at the [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing at Pedro-punt]] on [[6 December]] [[1873]] and to participate in the operations against the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] and the Kraton. Instead, van Swieten — who "geloofde" that two brigades would suffice — judged the third surplus to requirements and left it at Padang, where it lay idle while the campaign unfolded without it.

## The Strategic Error

The strategic significance of the decision is set out by Borel in his opening chapters. Verspijck had proposed landing the Padangsche brigade at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai]] — the baai on the west coast south of [[Oeleh Leh]] — for a pincer movement on the Kraton from two sides. Van Swieten rejected the proposal out of "vrees." "Drie jaren later bewees de generaal Pel het tegendeel" — Pel's march through the binnenland in January [[1876]] proved that the route was feasible, the distance being less than 3 uren in a straight line. By leaving the brigade at Padang, van Swieten not merely forfeited the chance of a decisive envelopment; he left the enemy's retreat route from the Kraton open. When [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]]'s [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] finally reached the [[Atjeh-rivier]] and delivered the deserted Kraton to the Dutch — "op een grijsaard en een zieken Atjehnees na, geheel verlaten" — the Atjehnese hoofdmacht had already withdrawn unbroken. The Padangsche brigade's absence was thus, in Borel's account, the original error from which the prolongation of the war flowed.

## Belated Arrival and Aftermath

Elements of the Padangsche brigade belatedly reached Atjeh in January [[1874]] — the mariniers en Afrikanen on [[20 January]] [[1874]], the 6de bataljon Infanterie on [[1 February]] [[1874]] — but by then the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|Kraton had already fallen]] and the [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|nineteen-day bombardment]] was over. The brigade's arrival did not alter van Swieten's strategic outlook: by [[15 February]] [[1874]] he had "stak het zwaard voor goed in de scheede" and applied the *afwachtende houding*; the [[Javaansche hulptroepen]] of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]], a veld-batterij, one company of [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] and the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.]] were sent back to Java. The Padangsche brigade's components were absorbed piecemeal into the [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]] and into the reinforcements that sustained Pel through [[1874]] and [[1875]]. The 6de bataljon's two companies with staff arrived at K. Radja on [[1 September]] [[1874]] from Padang, "with music at the head." Borel returns to the Padangsche brigade throughout the book as the prime example of how the failure to concentrate force at the decisive point — the lesson of Sédan ([[1870]]) and of the [[Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken (1873)|Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken]] booklet — prolonged the Atjeh-oorlog by years.

**The Billiton Maatschappij** — the Billiton (Banka-Billiton) Tin Company — is the mining company whose [[1852]] 40-year concession on [[Billiton]] (Banka-Billiton), extended for 35 years in the [[1882]] *suppletoire overeenkomst* with governor-general [[s'Jacob|s'Jacob]] and the subject of the [[1892]] contract that set the *cijns* (royalty) on tin extraction, is the principal reference-point for the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] debates of the [[1899]]-[[1910]] period. The Billiton precedents reached, by direct implication, into the Atjeh oilfields.

## The 1852 concession

The [[Billiton Maatschappij|Billiton Maatschappij]] had obtained its original 40-year concession in [[1852]] under the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Mijnreglement]] of [[1850]]. The concession covered the tin deposits of [[Billiton]] — the island east of [[Sumatra]].

## The 1873 regulation

In [[1873]] the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Mijnreglement]] was tightened: extensions of concessions now required, as a rule, open public competition; royal authorisation was reserved as the exception.

## The 1882 suppletoire overeenkomst

In [[1882]] the [[Billiton Maatschappij|Billiton Maatschappij]] and the governor-general [[s'Jacob|s'Jacob]] nevertheless signed a *suppletoire overeenkomst* — supplementary agreement — extending the concession by 35 years without public competition — a procedure later cited in the [[Tweede Kamer]] as the precedent the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] of [[1899]] was meant to prevent.

## The 1892 contract

In [[1892]] the [[Billiton Maatschappij|Billiton Maatschappij]] and the Indies government concluded a contract setting the *cijns* on tin extraction at a level higher than the 2 % of gross yield that [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] would later propose in his [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]]. The contrast is repeatedly cited by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] as the yardstick against which the generosity (or parsimony) of subsequent Atjeh oil concessions — including the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]]/[[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak]] arrangement exposed by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — was measured.

## The connexion to Atjeh

The Billiton precedents reached, by direct implication, into the Atjeh oilfields. The [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] of [[1899]] was the legislative response to the Billiton irregularities; the [[Mijnwet art. 5a (1910)|Article 5a amendment]] of [[1910]] was the legislative response to the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]]/[[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak]] disclosure. Both enactments governed the subsequent exploitation of the Atjeh north-coast oilfields.

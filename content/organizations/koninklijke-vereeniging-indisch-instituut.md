**The Koninklijke Vereniging "Indisch Instituut"** — the Royal Society "Indies Institute" of [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]] — was the metropolitan learned society and research institute that, after the reorganisation of the earlier *Koloniaal Instituut* (Colonial Institute), issued the series of *Mededeelingen* (Communications) of which *[[De Islaam in Indonesië (Mellema, 1947)|De Islaam in Indonesië (in het bijzonder op Java)]]* by [[R. L. Mellema|R. L. Mellema]] (Amsterdam, [[1947]]) is No. LXXVII of the general series and No. 25 of the *Afdeling Volkenkunde* (Ethnology Section). Through its Ethnology Section and its publishing programme, the Institute was, in the late colonial and immediate post-colonial period, one of the principal metropolitan venues for the comparative ethnology of the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] — including the comparative ethnology of Islam in [[Java|Java]] and [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

## Structure and sections

The Institute was organised into a number of *Afdeelingen* (sections), of which the *Afdeeling Volkenkunde* (Ethnology Section) was the principal vehicle for the publication of ethnological surveys, monographs, and comparative studies. The *Mededeelingen* of the Ethnology Section — numbered separately within the general *Mededelingen* of the Society — constituted a serial record of mid-twentieth-century Dutch ethnological work on the archipelago. Mellema's Islam survey of [[1947]] is No. 25 of that sub-series, and the staff of the Section included, at the time of the survey's publication, the author himself in the capacity of *Taalkundig Medewerker* (linguistic collaborator) — a post that gave a former colonial teacher of Javanese a metropolitan base for ethnological compilation.

The Institute likewise maintained a library and collections inherited in part from the earlier *Koloniaal Instituut* and connected, through the *Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen* tradition, to the broader network of Dutch Indies learned societies. The copy of Mellema's survey preserved in the [[Koninklijke Bibliotheek|Koninklijke Bibliotheek]] at [[The Hague]] (shelfmark 903 A 29) carries the Institute's imprint.

## The Mededeelingen series

The *Mededeelingen* of the Indisch Instituut were issued as separately paginated pamphlets, each devoted to a single subject and typically written by a member or correspondent of the Institute's sections. The series covered ethnology, economics, agriculture, and law; the Ethnology Section's contributions, of which Mellema's is No. 25, formed a coherent sub-series of comparative ethnological surveys. The pamphlets were priced for a general readership (Mellema's at f 1.50) and were intended, as Mellema states in his introduction, to give "voorlichting" — guidance — to those coming into contact with the world of the Indies.

## Role in the comparative ethnology of Atjeh

The Institute's Ethnology Section was, through publications such as Mellema's, a venue in which the Islamic institutions of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] were set in comparative relation to those of Java and the rest of the archipelago. Mellema's survey treats five Atjehnese institutions comparatively: the [[Moekim and Imeum in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|moekim–imeum territorial system]], the [[Rangkang|meunasah/rangkang]] village chapel, the [[Oelama zakaat war-chest in the Atjehoorlog (Mellema 1947)|diversion of zakaat as a war-chest by the oelama's]], the [[Haddji title prestige in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|prestige of the Haddji title]], and the [[Arabic-inscribed graves in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|oldest Arabic-inscribed graves]]. The Institute's imprimatur thus places these comparative observations within the institutional framework of late colonial metropolitan ethnology, a generation after [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|De Atjehers]]* and in the immediate aftermath of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjeh|Japanese occupation]] and the Indonesian revolution.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Koninklijke Vereniging "Indisch Instituut" is significant as the institutional publisher of a source — Mellema's [[De Islaam in Indonesië (Mellema, 1947)|1947 survey]] — that furnishes an independent comparative account of several Atjehnese Islamic institutions. The Institute's Ethnology Section, as the venue for this and other *Mededeelingen*, represents the metropolitan continuation of the Dutch ethnological tradition that had produced Snouck Hurgronje's monograph at [[Leiden|Leiden]] half a century earlier; by [[1947]], the tradition was being maintained in the revolutionary twilight of the colonial order it had been built to study.

**The Atjeh Handel Maatschappij** (Atjeh Trading Company) was a Dutch trading company established on the North Coast of [[Atjeh]], whose offices (*kantoren*) stood in [[1913]] at [[Lho' Seumawe|Loh Semaweu]] — the former fortified post of the war, which lay then entirely open: *zonder eenige versterking of afsluiting en zonder garnizoen* (without any fortification or enclosure and without garrison), with a large *passar* (market) and shopping quarter, the workshops of the [[Atjeh-tram]] with many dwellings for those employed in them, the offices of the Atjeh Handel Maatschappij, and a simple hotel. The traveller [[W. Moens]], who recorded the scene in November [[1913]] during his *[[Moens' journey through Atjeh (November 1913)|journey through Atjeh]]*, saw in the buildings of the trading company one of the tokens that a true *vredes-werk* (peace-work) was under hand in Atjeh: where the army had kept the imprisoned line, commerce and industry now occupied the ground.

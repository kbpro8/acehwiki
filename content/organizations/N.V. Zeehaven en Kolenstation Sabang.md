**N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang** was the limited company that built and operated the free
port and coaling station of [[Sabang]] on [[Poeloe We]]. Incorporated in [[1898]] to take over the
business of the [[Batavia]] firm [[De Lange (firm)|De Lange]], it ran the harbour under a privileged
arrangement with the [[Netherlands Indies]] Government and grew into one of the most profitable
bunkering enterprises in the [[East Indies]].

## Capital and relationship with the Government

The company was founded on a capital of f 3,000,000, of which f 2,500,000 was placed. It stood in a
special relationship with the [[Netherlands Indies]] Government, which in return for the privileges
granted enjoyed an annual share of the profits, regulated by Ordinance [[1910]] No. 56: the share
was set at 5% of the dividend where the dividend reached 5% of the shares, 10% of the dividend
between 5% and 10%, and 20% above 10%. In [[1909]] the Government purchased the greater part of the
company's quay walls, jetties and corps-morts for f 1,200,000 and thereafter maintained those works
itself, while the company carried out necessary extensions on the Government's behalf and paid an
annual lease of 3.75% on the original purchase sum increased by the cost of extensions.

## The coal business

The delivery of steam coal to ships was the most important branch of the company's trade, for which
it erected large and costly modern installations — among them a self-trimming bunker lighter with
belt conveyor of about 150 tons an hour, a lighter with a grab crane of about 100 tons an hour, and
several transborders. Coal deliveries rose from 19,000 tons in [[1900]] to a peak of 210,000 tons in
[[1912]] and [[1913]], falling under the influence of the war to 135,000 tons by [[1915]]. From the
[[1899|late 1890s]] the standard blend was the so-called "Sabang mixture" of Bengalese and Ombilin
coal in a 2:1 ratio. Before the war the company held supply contracts with some 130 steamship
companies. (For the operating context of these figures, see [[Sabang]].)

## Other installations and the dry dock

Beyond coaling, the company operated a 3,000-ton dry dock transferred to it by the Government, an ice
factory, a gas factory, an electrically pumped water supply serving ships, oil tanks, locomotive
cranes, slipways and a large construction and repair workshop capable of producing castings as large
as bridge parts for [[Deli]].

## The Atjeh Transport Maatschappij

A daughter company, the [[Atjeh Transport Maatschappij]], took over the prauw ferry and the
warehouses at [[Oelèë Lheuë]] and, under Government supervision, held a privileged position in
Government transport on the river — extending the Sabang concern's reach to the mainland landing that
linked the port to the [[Atjeh tram]].

## Dividends

The company's standing is reflected in its dividends. On each f 1,000 share the profit and dividend
ran: f 82.50 profit and f 32.50 dividend in [[1910]]; f 100 and about f 50.77 in [[1911]]; f 127 and
f 115 in [[1912]]; f 125.17 and f 75 in [[1913]]; f 121 and f 50 in [[1914]]; and f 100 profit with
no dividend in [[1915]], the last reflecting the disruption of the war.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang

**The N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang** — usually called the *Sabang Maatschappij* — was the Dutch joint-stock company founded in [[1898]] to take over from the Bataviaasche firma De Lange & Co. the operation of the *vrijhaven* of [[Sabang|Sabang]] on the island of [[Poeloe Wé|Poeloe Wé]], which [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] had, in his [[1892]] brochure *De Onderwerping van Atjeh*, pressed upon the Government as a Dutch competitor to [[Penang|Penang]] for the trade of northern Sumatra. With a *maatschappelijk kapitaal* of ƒ2,500,000–ƒ3,000,000, the Maatschappij built, on the Noordbaai of Wé, the only *vrijhaven* of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] — a station that, despite never realising van Heutsz's "grootsche toekomstdroomen" of an Atjehsche trade-emporium, paid steady dividends to its shareholders and became the principal bunkering-port of the eastern Indian Ocean.

## Founding and the De Lange inheritance

The Dutch flag had been hoisted over [[Poeloe Wé|Poeloe Wé]] in [[1884]], when a small *kolenloods* was built on the Zuidbaai (Lhoʼ Balohan) and a *hoofd* appointed. The [[1887]] commission under De Lange concluded that the Noordbaai — the Sabangbaai — was the more serviceable anchorage, and De Lange's *Chef*, van Oosterzee, is named by *De Buitenbezittingen* as "den geestelijken vader van Sabang" — the spiritual father of Sabang. The Bataviaasche firma De Lange & Co. received that year the concession for a *kolenstation*, and in [[1897]] the haven was opened as a *vrijhaven* for general trade. In [[1898]] the *N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang* was founded, "welke het bedrijf van de firma de Lange overnam." A Gouvernements-*dok* of 3,000-ton capacity (ten rechte 2,600 tons), stationed from [[1898]], later passed into the Maatschappij's ownership "onder servituut van kosteloos dokken der Gouvts. vaartuigen."

## Outillage and operations

The Maatschappij held, by [[1917]], extensive mechanical installations for the bunkering of steamers — a self-trimming *bunker-lichter* with band-transporteur capable of handling 150 tons per hour, a lighter with gripper of 100 tons per hour, four *transborkranen* of 2,000–3,000 kg, and the 3,000-ton *droogdok* ceded in ownership by the Gouvernement. In [[1903]] the *Stoomv. Mij. Nederland* committed to routing via Sabang rather than [[Padang|Padang]]; the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij|K.P.M.]] followed; and by [[1911]] the island possessed a *radio-telegrafisch station* described as "de krachtigste van heel Oost Azië." A *telegraafkabel* had already, in [[1897]], connected Sabang with [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]], and in [[1912]] a direct cable to Medan was added. A *dochtermaatschappij* — the *Atjeh-transport-maatschappij* — ran the prauw-ferry and warehouses at [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] on a *bevoorrechte positie*.

## Dividends and Gouvernements-verhouding

The Maatschappij stood in a *bijzondere verhouding* to the Gouvernement, which enjoyed a *winstaandeel* regulated by St. 1910 No. 56: 5% of the dividend on shares up to 5%, 10% between 5% and 10%, and 20% above 10%. In [[1909]] the Gouvernement acquired the kaaimuren, steigers, and *corps-morts* of the haven for roughly ƒ1,200,000 and now maintains them itself; the Maatschappij pays a yearly *pacht* of 3¼ % on the original purchase plus extension costs. The dividends on shares of ƒ1,000 tell the financial story: ƒ82.50 in [[1910]], ƒ100 in [[1911]], ƒ115 in [[1912]], ƒ125 in [[1913]], ƒ100 in [[1914]], ƒ100 in [[1915]]. The kolenbedrijf grew from 19,000 tons delivered in [[1900]] to a peak of 210,000 tons in [[1912]]–[[1913]], before the war brought a drop to 135,000 tons in [[1915]].

## Significance

Despite the failure of the original "illusie" of "grootsche concurrentie met Penang en Singapore," *De Buitenbezittingen* credits the Maatschappij with having "in enkele jaren zijn eigen, belangrijke plaats in het handelsverkeer verworven." As bunkering-station and transitohaven, Sabang had no real *achterland* of its own; the company's future depended on the development of the *Cultuurgebied* of Sumatra's Oostkust and the eventual completion of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] connection to the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]] at [[Besitang|Besitang]].

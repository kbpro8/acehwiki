**N.V. Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang** was the limited company that built and operated the free
port and coaling station of [[Sabang]] on [[Poeloe We]]. Incorporated in [[1898]] to take over the
business of the [[Batavia]] firm [[De Lange (firm)|De Lange]], it ran the harbour under a privileged
arrangement with the [[Netherlands Indies]] Government and grew into one of the most profitable
bunkering enterprises in the [[East Indies]].

## Capital and relationship with the Government

The company was founded on a capital of f 3,000,000, of which f 2,500,000 was placed. It stood in a
special relationship with the [[Netherlands Indies]] Government, which in return for the privileges
granted enjoyed an annual share of the profits, regulated by Ordinance [[1910]] No. 56: the share
was set at 5% of the dividend where the dividend reached 5% of the shares, 10% of the dividend
between 5% and 10%, and 20% above 10%. In [[1909]] the Government purchased the greater part of the
company's quay walls, jetties and corps-morts for f 1,200,000 and thereafter maintained those works
itself, while the company carried out necessary extensions on the Government's behalf and paid an
annual lease of 3.75% on the original purchase sum increased by the cost of extensions.

## The coal business

The delivery of steam coal to ships was the most important branch of the company's trade, for which
it erected large and costly modern installations — among them a self-trimming bunker lighter with
belt conveyor of about 150 tons an hour, a lighter with a grab crane of about 100 tons an hour, and
several transborders. Coal deliveries rose from 19,000 tons in [[1900]] to a peak of 210,000 tons in
[[1912]] and [[1913]], falling under the influence of the war to 135,000 tons by [[1915]]. From the
[[1899|late 1890s]] the standard blend was the so-called "Sabang mixture" of Bengalese and Ombilin
coal in a 2:1 ratio. Before the war the company held supply contracts with some 130 steamship
companies. (For the operating context of these figures, see [[Sabang]].)

## Other installations and the dry dock

Beyond coaling, the company operated a 3,000-ton dry dock transferred to it by the Government, an ice
factory, a gas factory, an electrically pumped water supply serving ships, oil tanks, locomotive
cranes, slipways and a large construction and repair workshop capable of producing castings as large
as bridge parts for [[Deli]].

## The Atjeh Transport Maatschappij

A daughter company, the [[Atjeh Transport Maatschappij]], took over the prauw ferry and the
warehouses at [[Oelèë Lheuë]] and, under Government supervision, held a privileged position in
Government transport on the river — extending the Sabang concern's reach to the mainland landing that
linked the port to the [[Atjeh tram]].

## Dividends

The company's standing is reflected in its dividends. On each f 1,000 share the profit and dividend
ran: f 82.50 profit and f 32.50 dividend in [[1910]]; f 100 and about f 50.77 in [[1911]]; f 127 and
f 115 in [[1912]]; f 125.17 and f 75 in [[1913]]; f 121 and f 50 in [[1914]]; and f 100 profit with
no dividend in [[1915]], the last reflecting the disruption of the war.

**The Departement van Oorlog** ("Department of War") was the Dutch colonial department responsible for military affairs in the Netherlands East Indies, including the conduct of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] and the military administration of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from the [[1873]] outbreak of the war to the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] consolidation. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914*, the Department of War is the colonial department under whose authority the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was built and administered for most of its first quarter-century — from the [[1875]] construction of the first small military line between [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and [[Kota Radja]] to the post-[[1891]] reversion of the line's management from the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]] back to the War Department.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Departement van Oorlog

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] places the [[Departement van Oorlog|Departement van Oorlog]] at the centre of the line's early history. The Bureau notes that "[[1875]] [saw] the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]] have a small railway built between the roadstead of [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and our principal military station at [[Kota Radja]]." In [[1882]] the management of the line was transferred to the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]], which "narrowed" (*versmald*) the line into a tramway. In [[1891]] "the management of these lines reverted to the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]]" — a reversion that the Bureau frames as the recognition that, despite the [[1882]] attempt to recast the Atjeh line as a civil tramway, the lines remained essentially military instruments serving the campaign against the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] resistance.

The [[1891]] reversion to the War Department closed the first chapter of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s history — the chapter of small, local lines around [[Kota Radja]] — and prepared the ground for the second chapter that opened with the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]] and the long-line construction beginning in [[1897]]. The Bureau notes that the Atjeh-tram "was built with exclusively politico-military intentions, without sufficient regard, at the time of the decision to build, for the economic demands that such a connecting route ought to meet" — a characterisation that places the War Department's role in the line's construction at the centre of the Bureau's account of the Atjeh-tram's military phase.

The Bureau's account of the [[Gewapende Politie|Gewapende Politie (armed police)]] likewise notes the continuing military character of the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency: "[[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] was the only Buitenbezittingen gewest in which the armed police division was not under the command of a retired or active army officer (as was the rule in every other gewest)." This continuing military exception reflects the still-recent character of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie]] and the continuing role of the War Department in the administration of the residency.

## Significance

The [[Departement van Oorlog|Departement van Oorlog]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal colonial department of the military phase of the Dutch presence in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — from the [[1873]] outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] to the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] consolidation. The Department's role as the constructor and administrator of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] from [[1875]] to [[1882]] and from [[1891]] onward places it at the centre of the line's early history, and the continuing military command of the [[Gewapende Politie|armed police]] in Atjeh reflects the still-recent character of the pacification even at the end of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade.

**The bergcolonne** — the *mountain column* of the Dutch colonial military geographers — was a combined-arms column built around the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée corps]] and the [[Bergartillerie / Bergbatterijen|mountain batteries]] of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|K.N.I.L.]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], designed for operations in the broken country of the Atjehnese interior, and commanded throughout the [[1896]] campaign by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Kol. J. B. Van Heutsz]] with [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapitein G. C. E. Van Daalen of the General Staff]] as his chief of staff. The *bergcolonne* is the principal Dutch manoeuvre formation of the [[1896]] campaign in *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* (1913).

## Composition

The *bergcolonne* was built around the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée corps]] and the mountain batteries, with attached infantry, engineers, cavalry, and medical service. The composition varied according to the operation; for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|great tuchtiging of the IV and VI Moekims of 23–25 May 1896]], the *bergcolonne* under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] included the [[Derde Bataljon (Atjeh-Bataljon)|3rd]], [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6th]], and [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7th Battalions]], the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée]] brigades, the 1st, 2nd, and 4th Bergbatterijen, and the engineers and medical service. For the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng of 29 June 1896]], the *bergcolonne* was reduced to the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée corps]] under [[A. E. van Kappen|Kapitein Van Kappen]] with detachments of the 3rd, 6th, and 14th Battalions.

## Tactical method

The *bergcolonne* was the operational formation within which the tactical method developed by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] in [[1896]] was applied. The method had four elements:

1. small, mobile, combined-arms forces;
2. marechaussée as the principal manoeuvre element;
3. night marches to maximise surprise;
4. operations aimed at the destruction of the enemy's fortified positions (*bentengs*) and the burning of his villages, not at the occupation of territory.

The method was applied most famously in the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng of 29 June 1896]] — a night march followed by a dawn assault with the *klewang* (cutlass) that carried the *benteng* with 110 enemy dead at negligible Dutch cost.

## The 1896 operations

The *bergcolonne* under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] participated in every major Dutch operation of the [[1896]] campaign:

- the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|great tuchtiging of the IV and VI Moekims of 23–25 May 1896]];
- the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lam-Krak Moekims of 9–10 June 1896]];
- the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik of 16–17 June 1896]];
- the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng of 29 June 1896]];
- the [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|expedition to Lepong of August 1896]];
- the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition of September 1896]];
- the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition of November 1896]];
- the [[Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri (28 October 1896)|expedition to Indrapuri of October 1896]].

## Significance

The *bergcolonne* is the principal Dutch manoeuvre formation of the [[1896]] campaign in *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* and the formation within which the tactical method that would close the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] by [[1903]] was developed. The *bergcolonne* under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] participated in every major Dutch operation of the [[1896]] campaign and served as the model for the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée]]-and-column method that would, under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] (1898–1904), bring the war to its close.

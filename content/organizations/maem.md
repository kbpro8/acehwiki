**The M.A.E.M.** was the gold-dredging company that, in [[1940]], began production on the [[Geudong Concession]] in the [[Basin of Meulaboh|Meulaboh coastal basin]] on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West coast of Atjeh]] — the only colonial-era gold-dredging operation on [[Acheh|Atjeh]] territory, and accordingly the only Atjeh locality for which colonial-era gold production figures are recorded. The M.A.E.M.'s dredges — two bucket dredges with 6-cubic-foot buckets and a joint monthly capacity of 270,000 cubic yards — represent the high-point of the colonial mineral economy of Atjeh, the moment at which the residency's geological potential was, for the first and only time, translated into actual industrial gold production.

## The Geudong Concession operation

For the full technical description of the M.A.E.M.'s Geudong Concession operation, see the entry on [[Geudong Concession]]. In summary: production started in [[1940]] with two bucket dredges (buckets of 6 cubic feet) with a joint capacity of 270,000 cubic yards per month; the deposit contained 55–60 % boulders (the largest of which "could just be handled") and 40–45 % fine material; there were two types of gold (fine gold with higher silver content, easily amalgamated; and coarse gold with lower silver content, difficult to amalgamate); the platinum byproduct was some kilograms per month; treatment was by amalgamation and Wilfley tables.

## The 1941 mercury observation

The M.A.E.M.'s Geudong operation is also the source of the [[C. de Groot|De Groot]] [[1946]] observation that the dredges were *gaining* mercury with each clean-up — the documentary evidence that the West-Atjeh alluvial deposits contain native mercury and cinnabar in addition to gold and platinum. For the full account see the entries on [[Mercury in Atjeh]] and [[Basin of Meulaboh]].

## The name and corporate identity

The acronym "M.A.E.M." is not fully expanded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]), which refers to the company simply as "the M.A.E.M." The most plausible expansion, on the analogy of the other East Indian oil and mining companies of the late colonial period (e.g. the [[N.I.A.M.]], [[N.K.P.M.]], [[N.P.P.M.]], [[N.L.A.M.]], [[N.N.G.P.M.]]), is *Maatschappij voor Aardolie [or Aardrijk] Exploitatie Maatschappij* or a similar Dutch company name; but the absence of a printed expansion in Van Bemmelen's text means that the full corporate name cannot be stated with certainty on the basis of the printed source alone. The M.A.E.M. is accordingly best cited in the form used by Van Bemmelen himself — "the M.A.E.M." — until the full corporate name can be verified from a separate source.

## Significance

The M.A.E.M. is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it was the operator of the only colonial-era gold-dredging operation on Atjeh territory — the [[Geudong Concession]] in the [[Basin of Meulaboh|Meulaboh coastal basin]], with two bucket dredges and a joint monthly capacity of 270,000 cubic yards. The M.A.E.M.'s operation represents the high-point of the colonial mineral economy of Atjeh, the moment at which the residency's geological potential was, for the first and only time, translated into actual industrial gold production. The M.A.E.M.'s operation also produced platinum as a byproduct ("some kilograms per month") — the only recorded colonial-era platinum production on Atjeh territory — and its dredges supplied the [[C. de Groot|De Groot]] [[1946]] observation of native mercury and cinnabar in the West-Atjeh alluvial deposits, the only documented colonial-era observation of mercury minerals on Atjeh territory.

The **C.B.Z.** — the *Centraal Burgerlijk Ziekenhuis*, the central civil hospital at [[Batavia]] — was the institution whose ophthalmological out-patient clinic and paediatric department produced the research on *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* in infants that Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] cited from [[Java]] in comparing his own findings from [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Mulock Houwer's infant studies

Through the close cooperation between the ophthalmological out-patient clinic and the paediatric department of the C.B.Z., Professor [[Mulock Houwer]] had the opportunity to include a relatively large number of infants in his investigations. These, together with the investigations at [[Semarang]] and [[Bandung|Bandoeng]], showed a proportionally greater frequency of xerophthalmia among *kleuters* (toddlers) than among infants — a result Gomperts's own Groot-Atjeh figures confirmed. The finding reached him through a communication of Mevrouw [[Van Stockum]].

## The clinical evening

It was at a clinical evening in the C.B.Z., at a demonstration by Dr. [[J. H. de Haas]] of six non-European children with xerophthalmia, that Mulock Houwer laid down the definition of *[[Keratomalacie|keratomalacie]]* which Gomperts subsequently declined to accept: that not every corneal ulceration in xerophthalmia might be called keratomalacia, since keratomalacia was a very typical clinical picture in which the cornea melted away entirely in a few days.

**Senembah Maatschappij** was a tobacco and later rubber plantation company on Sumatra's East Coast that emerged in [[1889]] from the enterprise of **Näher** and **Grob**, who had opened the **Tandjong Morawa** plantation on the [[Deli]]–[[Serdang]] border in [[1871]]. Näher and Grob had originally intended to grow nutmeg at Tandjong Morawa, but when that plan proved unworkable they converted to tobacco, becoming one of the foundational companies of the northern Sumatra plantation economy.

## Origins at Tandjong Morawa

In [[1871]], the same year that the [[Deli Maatschappij]] — which had itself been founded only in [[1869]] as the first *naamlooze vennootschap* in the Indies — appointed the first doctor on the East Coast, the German planters Näher and Grob opened the Tandjong Morawa plantation on the border between the *Landschappen* (self-governing territories) of [[Deli]] and [[Serdang]]. Their initial intention was to cultivate nutmeg. When this proved unprofitable, they transitioned to tobacco — the crop that was transforming the entire East Coast under the pioneering work of [[Jacobus Nienhuys]] and the organizational support of what would become the [[Deli Planters Vereeniging]] (founded [[23 April]] [[1879]]).

## Corporate emergence, 1889

In [[1889]], Näher and Grob's Tandjong Morawa plantation was formally incorporated as the Senembah Maatschappij. The year is noted by Dr. T. Volker as significant also for the founding of the [[Deli Planters Vereeniging]]'s first missionary to the interior workers on the instigation of [[J. T. Cremer]], and for the elevation of the [[Sultan of Langkat]] from *Radja* to *Sultan* in [[1887]] (Volker's nearby context).

## Role in the East Coast economy

The Senembah Maatschappij became one of the major tobacco companies of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]], joining the [[Deli Maatschappij]] and later rubber ventures in dominating the northern Sumatra plantation economy. Dr. T. Volker notes that the rubber plantations opened by several tobacco companies — including the major players of the East Coast — were financed "wholly… with money reserved from tobacco profits," confirming the Senembah's trajectory from tobacco pioneer to diversified plantation enterprise.

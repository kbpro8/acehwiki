**Wereldbibliotheek N.V.** — Dutch for "World Library", a Dutch publishing house based in Amsterdam — was the publisher of *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]), the collected volume on the cultural, religious, educational, agricultural, mining and economic history of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] edited by Dr. [[C. W. Wormser|Wormser]] under the auspices of the [[Indisch Instituut]]. The title page of the volume records that it was "uitgegeven in 1946 onder de auspiciën van het Indisch Instituut door de Wereldbibliotheek N.V. te Amsterdam" — "published in 1946 under the auspices of the Indies Institute by the Wereldbibliotheek N.V. in Amsterdam."

## Role in the publication of *Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk*

The Wereldbibliotheek N.V. was, in [[1946]], the publisher through whom the [[Indisch Instituut]]'s collected volume reached the Dutch book trade. The choice of a general-interest Amsterdam publisher — rather than a specialist colonial or academic press — reflects the volume's intended audience: the educated Dutch general reader, for whom the volume was to serve as a synthetic survey of what the Indies had "received and given" to the Netherlands on the threshold of Indonesian independence. The volume appeared with ninety-one illustrations (*met 91 afbeeldingen*) and a table of contents grouping the fourteen specialist contributions under ten thematic headings.

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the Wereldbibliotheek's significance is incidental but real: the choice of this publisher placed the Atjeh material digested in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* — drawn chiefly from [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *De Atjéhers* ([[1893]]–[[1894]]) and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s *Atjèh* ([[1922]]–[[1923]]) — within the reach of a general Dutch readership in the immediate aftermath of the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese occupation]] and on the eve of the [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]] in [[1949]]. The volume's circulation through the Wereldbibliotheek's trade channels thus helped to shape the late-colonial Dutch general reader's picture of Atjeh at a critical moment.

**The Tiro ulama** — the *Tiro-Oelama's* of Dutch colonial sources — was the hereditary family of Acehnese religious leaders (*teungkoes*) of [[Tiro|Tiro]] in [[Pidie|Pidie]] in eastern [[Groot-Atjeh]] who provided the principal religious leadership of the Acehnese resistance to the Dutch throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The Tiro ulama is the principal religious-political antagonist of the Dutch in eastern [[Groot-Atjeh]] in *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* (1913).

## Origin and role

The Tiro ulama family were the hereditary religious leaders of the [[Tiro|Tiro country]] in [[Pidie|Pidie]]. The family provided a succession of *teungkoes* who led the religious resistance to the Dutch throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], propagating the doctrine of [[Prang sabi|prang sabi (holy war)]] and providing the religious-ideological motivation of the resistance. The Tiro ulamas were the principal religious-political antagonists of the Dutch in eastern [[Groot-Atjeh]], and their pursuit and destruction was one of the principal objectives of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée]] operations in the residency throughout the war.

## The 1896 campaign

The *1e Deel* of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* records that [[Toekoe di Tiroe|Teungku di Tiro]] of the Tiro ulama family was, in [[1896]], the principal *teungkoe* of the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak Moekims]], and that his fortified house stood at [[Lambaroe|Lambaroe]] within the [[geconcente linie|concentrated line]] and that he had died there before the [[1896]] campaign. The *Inleiding* records that *Na de concentratie was de later door ons bezette benteng de hoofdverblijfplaats van Tengkoe di Tiroe, die daar ook gestorven is* — after the concentration the later by-us-occupied fort was the chief residence of Tengkoe di Tiroe, who also died there. The conversion of the teungkoe's fortified house into a Dutch military post is symbolic of the religious-political complex that the Dutch had to confront.

## The destruction of the last Tiro ulama (1911)

The closing *Overzicht van onzer vroegere betrekkingen met Atjeh* of *ATJEH '96* records that:

> *In December 1911 werd in Pidie de laatste telg der Tiro-Oelama's neergelegd. De speurtochten naar deze weerspannige geestelijken door de maréchaussée, onder den toenmaligen 1en luitenant H. J. Schmidt, liggen nog versch in het geheugen.*

— *In December 1911 the last scion of the Tiro ulamas was laid low in Pidie. The hunts for these obstinate clerics by the marechaussée, under the then-1st Lieutenant H. J. Schmidt, still lie fresh in the memory.*

The destruction of the last of the Tiro ulamas was the closing event of the religious resistance in eastern [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the symbolic end of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[Pidie|Pidie]]. The hunts were conducted by the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée]] under [[Schmidt|1e Luit. H. J. Schmidt]] — the same officer who would later co-author *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]* with [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]].

## Significance

The Tiro ulama is the principal religious-political antagonist of the Dutch in eastern [[Groot-Atjeh]] in *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* and the principal religious leadership of the Acehnese resistance throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The family's pursuit and destruction was one of the principal objectives of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|marechaussée]] operations in the residency throughout the war, and the destruction of the last of the Tiro ulamas in [[1911]] marked the closing of the religious resistance in eastern [[Groot-Atjeh]].

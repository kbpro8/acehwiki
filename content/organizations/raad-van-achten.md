**The Raad van Achten** — the "Council of Eight" — was, in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the Atjèhnese revolutionary committee established at [[Penang|Poelau Pinang]] during the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) — Kreemer's account|first Atjèh expedition]] of [[1873]], which spurred the Atjèhnese population to resistance and sustained the war-effort through a continuous campaign of correspondence with the chiefs in Atjèh. The *Raad van Achten* was the political instrument of the Atjèhnese colony at Penang — itself a long-established base of Atjèhnese malcontents and intrigue.

## The Penang colony

Kreemer situates the *Raad van Achten* within the long pre-war relationship between Atjèh and Penang. "De reeds lang vóór 1873 bestaande relatiën van Pinang met Atjèh waren niet alleen van commercieelen, maar ook van staatkundigen aard. Zoo vond men op Poelau Pinang een niet onaanzienlijke Atjèhsche kolonie, waar zich van oudsher de Atjèhsche malcontenten verzamelden en waaruit vele der Atjèhsche intriges werden gesmeed" — "The relations of Pinang with Atjèh, existing long before 1873, were not only of a commercial but also of a political nature. Thus one found on Penang a not inconsiderable Atjèhnese colony, where the Atjèhnese malcontents had gathered from olden times and from which many of the Atjèhnese intrigues were forged."

## The committee's activity

"Zoo spoorde het Atjèhsche revolutionnaire comité, de 'Raad van achten', dat te Pinang gevestigd was, de bevolking tot verzet aan en zond bij voortduring brieven naar de hoofden in Atjèh met de mededeeling, dat onze middelen, zoowel in geld als in troepen, uitgeput waren en wij spoedig den strijd zouden moeten opgeven" — "Thus the Atjèhnese revolutionary committee, the 'Council of Eight', established at Pinang, spurred the population to resistance and continuously sent letters to the chiefs in Atjèh with the message that our means, in money as well as in troops, were exhausted and we would soon have to give up the struggle."

## Significance

The *Raad van Achten* is, in Kreemer's narrative, the political counterpart of the Atjèhnese military resistance — the agency by which the war was sustained as a political, not merely a military, undertaking. Its base at Penang — a British colonial port outside Dutch jurisdiction — made it impervious to Dutch suppression, and its continuous correspondence with the Atjèhnese chiefs supplied the resistance with strategic intelligence about Dutch difficulties that the Dutch themselves could not control. The *Raad* is thus a key element in Kreemer's account of why the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]] proved so protracted: the Dutch were fighting not only an enemy in the field but a political organisation that could mobilise Atjèhnese opinion from a safe haven in British territory.

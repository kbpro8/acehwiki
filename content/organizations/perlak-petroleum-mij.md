**The Perlak-Petroleum-Mij** — the Perlak Petroleum Company — was the Dutch colonial concession-holder of the *Peureula* oil-terrain in the onderafdeeling [[Langsa|Langsa]] of the [[Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh|Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh]], the historic territory of the former sultanaat of [[Peureula|Perlak]] on Sumatra's eastern coast. *De Buitenbezittingen* names the Perlak-Petroleum-Mij as the *concessionaris* whose Peureula-terrein — together with the neighbouring Oost-Peudawa — was, by [[1917]], "in exploitatie bij de [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche Petr. Mij.]]"; the actual winning of crude oil was thus conducted by the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]] as operator, while the Perlak-Petroleum-Mij remained the legal holder of the concession.

## The Peureula concession

Of the five petroleum-concessions in the onderafdeelingen [[Idi|Idi]] and [[Langsa|Langsa]], totalling 8,652 H.A., three were still in exploitation in [[1917]]: Oost-Peudawa, Peureula, and Zuid-Peureula. Of these, the Peureula concession was held by the Perlak-Petroleum-Mij and operated by the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche]]; the Zuid-Peureula concession was operated separately by the *Petroleum-Mij. Zuid-Perlak*, a sister-company. The two concessions together formed the most productive *boorterrein* in the *gewest*: in [[1913]] they yielded 164,100 K.G.-ton (Peureula) and 16,738 K.G.-ton (Zuid-Peureula) of crude oil, or together roughly 99% of the Atjehsche production of 182,447 K.G.-ton.

## Output and the war

The production of the Peureula terrain declined, with the rest of the Atjehsche oil-fields, throughout the period covered by *De Buitenbezittingen*. The Peureula concession yielded 164,100 K.G.-ton in [[1913]], 135,700 in [[1914]], and 114,045 in [[1915]] — a fall of more than thirty per cent in two years. Zuid-Peureula showed a similar pattern: 16,738 in [[1913]], 11,434 in [[1914]], and 11,468 in [[1915]]. The handbook remarks that "de voortdurende daling der productie zich ook na [[1913]] voortzet," and notes that the war-induced disorganisation of European markets had much to do with the decline. All crude oil of the Perlak-Petroleum-Mij's terrain was pumped, through the *pijpleiding* of more than 120 km — a 5″ oil-line of 800-ton capacity and a 4″ gas-line of 100-ton capacity — to the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche]] refinery at [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] in the adjacent *Cultuurgebied*.

## Financial relation to the landschap

The Perlak-Petroleum-Mij, like the other concessionaires, paid a *vast recht* of ƒ0.25 per H.A. (yielding ƒ1,300 per year minimum) and 4% of the *bruto-opbrengst* of crude oil. The proceeds of this royalty were shared: two-fifths went to the *landschapskas* of [[Peureula|Peureula]], the surviving political community of the old sultanaat of [[Peureula|Perlak]]. The Peureula landschapskas, in turn, became a financial pillar of the new *Credietbank "Langsa"* — founded early [[1915]] as the third of the *Volkscredietwezen* institutions of the *gewest* — by depositing its advance-received *aandeel in de cijns* with the bank. "Vooral de hulp de Bataafsche Petroleum van Mij. te [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] is voor deze bank gewicht," *De Buitenbezittingen* notes, since the Maatschappij's cijns-payments were "door de landschapskas … in voorschot gestort" and then re-deposited with the bank.

## Significance

The Perlak-Petroleum-Mij represents, in *De Buitenbezittingen*'s account, the legal-institutional form through which the colonial state regulated the exploitation of Atjeh's mineral wealth: the separation of *concessionaris* from *exploitant* allowed the Gouvernement to maintain the fiction of native *landschap* sovereignty over the subsoil — and to channel part of the royalty through the *landschapskas* — while ensuring that the technically demanding work of drilling and refining remained in the hands of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]].


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**The Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij** — the Perlak Oil Company — is the company whose Atjeh oilfields, in the [[Pedirse|Pedir]] region of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] north coast, were ceded to the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]] for exploitation against a payment of ƒ 2.50 per ton of petroleum extracted, in the [[1905]]-[[1910]] contract disclosed by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and thereby triggering the [[Mijnwet art. 5a (1910)|Article 5a amendment]] of the [[Mijnwet|Mijnwet]] in [[1910]].

### The Perlak-Koninklijke contract

When, soon after the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] of [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] had been enacted, [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — then governor of Atjeh — reported that the [[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij]] had ceded its Atjeh fields to the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]] for exploitation against a payment of ƒ 2.50 per ton of petroleum extracted, he expressed the opinion that contracts such as the [[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak]]-[[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]] contract could equally well be concluded by the Indies government itself, with greater benefit to the treasury.

### The connexion to Perlak (Atjeh)

The name of the [[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij]] derives from the [[Peureula|Perlak]] region of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] north coast — the historical Muslim kingdom of [[Samudra Pasai|Pasai]]-[[Peureula|Perlak]] and one of the three component fields of the later [[Atjeh oil complex]] recorded in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) Table 4. The Peureula field, operated by the [[N.L.A.M.]], produced 48,219 tons in [[1940]] (declining to 36,640 tons in [[1941]]) — the largest producing field of the Atjeh oil complex.

### Significance

The [[Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij|Perlak Petroleum Maatschappij]] is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] colonial parliamentary record, the type-specimen of the Atjeh oil company whose contract with the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]] triggered legislative intervention in the form of the [[Mijnwet art. 5a (1910)|Article 5a amendment]] of [[1910]]. The episode is the first Atjeh-oil episode in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] record.

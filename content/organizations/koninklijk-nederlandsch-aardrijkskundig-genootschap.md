The **Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap** (the Royal Dutch Geographical Society, abbreviated **K.N.A.G.**) was the principal geographical learned society of the Netherlands, founded in [[1873]] and based at [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]]. The Society's *Tijdschrift* (Journal) — the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* — was the principal Dutch geographical outlet of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and was the host publication for some of the most important scholarly articles on the geography and economic development of the Netherlands Indies, including [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]].

## Founding and Purpose

The Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap was founded in [[1873]] at [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]] — the same period, and broadly the same intellectual milieu, as the founding of the *Koninklijk Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië* (the Royal Institute for the Language, Geography and Ethnology of the Netherlands Indies) at [[Delft|Delft]] in [[1851]] and the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Kon. Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] at Amsterdam in 1910. The Society's purpose was the promotion of geographical knowledge — especially of the Netherlands and the Netherlands Indies — through the publication of a journal, the sponsoring of expeditions, and the maintenance of a geographical library and map collection at Amsterdam.

## The Tijdschrift

The *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* was the Society's journal, published in two series (Second Series from 1884) and issued in monthly or bimonthly *afleveringen* (numbers) collected into annual *delen* (volumes). The *Tijdschrift* published articles on the geography, cartography, geology, economic development and colonial administration of the Netherlands Indies, alongside articles on Dutch and general geography. The *Tijdschrift* was the principal Dutch geographical outlet for the late-nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century exploration and survey of the Netherlands Indies — especially of the *Buitenbezittingen* (the Outer Possessions: Sumatra, Borneo, Celebes, the Moluccas, and New Guinea), which were much less well known than Java.

## Kreemer's 1918 Atjeh Rice Article

The most important K.N.A.G. publication relevant to the encyclopedia of Aceh history is [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*]] ("Rice Cultivation in the Aceh Region and Dependencies"), published in *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap*, deel XXXV (1918), afl. 3. The article was reprinted as **Mededeelingen No. 6** of the [[Atjèh Instituut|Atjèh Instituut]] — the sixth in the *Mededeelingen* (Communications) offprint series of the Atjèh Institute — and was printed for the *Directeuren der Maatschappij ter Bevordering van het Natuurkundig Onderzoek der Nederlandsche Koloniën* by [[E. J. Brill|E. J. Brill]] in Leiden.

The Kreemer article is the principal Dutch-language technical and ethnographic study of [[rijstbouw|rice cultivation]] across the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, and is the key source for the early-twentieth-century [[Rijstvraagstuk (1916)|"Rice Question" of 1916]] — the Dutch colonial policy debate on whether Atjeh could become the rice-storehouse of the [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's East Coast]] plantation belt.

## Other Atjeh-Related Publications

The *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* published, over its first half-century, a substantial body of Atjeh-related geographical material — including articles on the exploration of the Gayo and Alas interior, on the Westkust of Atjeh, on the cartography of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], and on the economic geography of the post-pacification period. These articles, taken together, form the principal Dutch geographical literature on Atjeh in the late-nineteenth and early-twentieth centuries, alongside the ethnographic literature of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|C. Snouck Hurgronje]] and the administrative literature of the *Binnenlands Bestuur*.

## Significance

The Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap is significant for the encyclopedia of Aceh history as the host publication of [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]] — the principal Dutch-language technical-and-ethnographic study of the rice economy of Atjeh, and the key source for the [[Rijstvraagstuk (1916)|Rijstvraagstuk of 1916]]. The Society's *Tijdschrift* was the principal Dutch geographical outlet of the late-nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century exploration and survey of the Netherlands Indies, and a substantial body of Atjeh-related geographical material appeared in its pages over the Society's first half-century.

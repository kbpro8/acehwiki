**St. Claverbond** was a Catholic mission magazine published by the Dutch Jesuits from [[1889]] to at least [[1938]] (and beyond). It was the official organ of the St. Claverbond mission association, named after St. Peter Claver, the patron of the negro missions. The magazine provided detailed reports from missionaries in the Dutch East Indies, including extensive coverage of the **[[Aceh War]]** through the writings of **[[H. C. Verbraak S.J.]]** and others.

## History

The magazine began as "Berichten uit Nederlandsch Oost-Indië ten dienste der Eerwaarde Directeuren van den St. Claverbond" and later adopted the shorter title "St. Claverbond". It was published in Nijmegen by the Stichting St. Claverbond. The register indicates that by [[1938]], the magazine had a circulation of over 17,000 copies.

## Coverage of Aceh

The Aceh War featured prominently in the magazine, thanks to the regular contributions of Verbraak. His articles covered military campaigns, ecclesiastical observations, and historical accounts of Christian persecution in Aceh. Other authors contributed articles on the martyrs Dionysius a Nativitate and Redemptus a Cruce, and on the general missionary work in Sumatra.

## The Register (1939)

In [[1939]], to commemorate the magazine's 50th anniversary, a comprehensive index was published, compiled by Herman Hagdorn S.J. and Jacques Dinjens. This register lists all authors, subjects, plates, and places mentioned in the first 50 volumes. It is an invaluable resource for researchers of colonial Catholic history and the Aceh War.

## Significance

*St. Claverbond* is a primary source for understanding the Catholic perspective on the Aceh War and the broader colonial enterprise. It reveals the close ties between the mission and the military, the religious motivations of the colonists, and the daily realities of life in the garrison. The magazine's register provides a roadmap for exploring these themes in depth.

**The Société financière des caoutchoucs** — *the Financial Society of Rubbers* — was the *holding company* for many Tamiang estates, including Soengei Lipoet and Mopoli with rubber and oil-palm, Batang Ara with rubber, Medang Ara and Semadami with oil-palm and rubber. The Société is the type-case of the holding-company form of organisation that came to dominate the Tamiang planting-sector in the post-war period, whereby a single financial body controlled a portfolio of estates of various crops.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **Société Financière des Caoutchoucs** was the company founded under Franco-Belgian auspices which opened in [[Tamiang|Temyang]] — the sub-department of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] immediately bordering the East Coast of Sumatra — a series of estates for the winning of caoutchouc, coffee, rubber and palm-oil, and whose numbers, taken by author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] from the youngest year-reports, illustrate his chapter "Buitenlandsch kapitaal in Atjeh" ("Foreign capital in Atjeh").

### The estates

- **Soengei Lipoet** — for coffee-, caoutchouc- and palm-oil-culture, opened [[26 February]] [[1908]], when 30 hectares were planted; the planting extended enormously, so that in [[1922]] a surface of not less than 5,751 hectares had been brought into culture, giving 3,600 coolies an existence. In [[1918]] the coffee culture was exchanged for that of oil-palm. Production figures, from the latest year-reports: caoutchouc 54,384 KG ([[1913]]), 95,297 (1914), 184,302 (1915), 351,255 (1916), 492,687 (1917), 556,174 (1918), 759,191 (1919), 1,332,118 (1920), 1,239,973 (1921), 1,319,800 (1922); oil (from 1918) 78,156 KG (1918), 313,146 (1919), 638,284 (1920), 430,182 (1921), 962,504 (1922). Coffee: 22,000 KG (1913), 20,361 (1914), 85,126 (1915), 191,893 (1916), 206,010 (1917).
- **Batang Ara** — established [[18 January]] [[1911]] at an initial 86 hectares; the planted surface destined for caoutchouc and palm-oil is 1,739 hectares with 742 coolies; production: caoutchouc 128,685 KG (1918), 203,402 (1919), 284,366 (1920), 272,731 (1921), 291,600 (1922); oil 12,910 KG (1919), 146,154 (1920), 136,216 (1921), 395,400 (1922).
- **Semadam** — likewise destined for caoutchouc and palm-oil, founded in [[1913]], at the book's date over 515 hectares; production: caoutchouc 23,780 KG (1918), 34,791 (1919), 38,759 (1920), 35,471 (1921), 31,600 (1922); oil 31,000 KG (1919), 110,728 (1920), 103,740 (1921), 247,500 (1922).

### Context

The company did not stand alone in Temyang: the French company "[[Palmeraies de Simpang Kanan]]" opened on [[28 April]] [[1914]] its estate Simpang Kanan (650 hectares Hevea rubber and some 70 hectares oil-palm); the "Simpang Kiri Cultuurmaatschappij" was founded on [[22 March]] [[1913]] with some 600 hectares of rubber planting; and the equally foreign-capital "Tengoelan Cultuur Maatschappij" possessed enterprises in the Atjehnese borderland, though without figures available to Langhout (see [[Foreign capital in Atjeh]]). The rising estate district was bound into the provincial economy by the [[Atjeh Tram|tramway]] reaching Koeala Simpang ([[1912]]), Soengei Lipoet ([[1914]]) and [[Besitang]] ([[1915]]–[[1916]]).

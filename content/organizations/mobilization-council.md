**The Atjèh Council** — *Atjèhraad* in Dutch, *Atjèh-shii-sangi-kai* in Japanese, *madjlis perwakilan rakjat daérah Atjèh* in Malay — was the official advisory council established by Resident [[lino|lino]] under Residents' Regulation of [[17 November]] [[1943]] no. 7 as the consultative body of the Japanese military administration in [[Atjèh|Atjèh]], supplemented in March [[1945]] by a Sumatra-wide **Sumatra Council** (*Sumatora-chia-sangi-in*) seated at [[Bukittinggi|Bukittinggi]] (Fort de Kock). Together with the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] and the welfare office, the council was one of the three great Indonesian advisory bodies of the occupation, and served — in Piekaar's analysis — both as the institutional channel through which the *oelèëbalang* and *oelama* parties contested influence and as the formal vehicle through which the Japanese coordinated the mobilisation of war effort, food production, and labour.

## The establishment of 17 November 1943

"On the basis of this regulation, by Residents' Regulation of [[17 November]] [[1943]] no. 7, the '*Atjèhraad*' (Jap. *Atjèh-shii-sangi-kai*, advisory council for the residency of Atjèh; Mal. *madjlis perwakilan rakjat daérah Atjèh*)" was established. The council's creation was announced by Resident [[lino|lino]] in a speech at the great mosque in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] on [[9 November]] [[1943]], alongside the establishment of the [[PETA|people's army]]. "By each of these electoral colleges three candidates were chosen, from which subsequently the Resident made a selection. An organ, therefore, which was still far removed from the *Atjèhraad*, toward which, as we saw in the 'Introduction' (p. 14), in nationalist circles under Dutch administration the thought had been directed." The first session opened on [[8 December|8]]–[[9 December]] [[1943]], and the original council of 30 comprised 17 *oelèëbalangs*, 7 *oelama*'s, and 2 non-Atjèhnese officials — among them [[Toeankoe Mahmoed|Toeankoe Mahmoed]], [[Teukoe Nja' Arif|Teukoe Nja' Arif]], [[Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali|Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali]], [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]], [[Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi|Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi]], and Ah Moertolo.

## Sessions and the mobilisation of war effort

Four sessions were held in the council's lifetime. The 2nd session of [[4 April|4]]–[[5 April]] [[1944]] met jointly with the *gun-chó* conference and addressed food supplies, food production, and labour mobilisation; the 3rd session of [[19 October|19]]–[[20 October]] [[1944]] deliberated on "the coordination of the war effort, the task of women in wartime, the food supplies, and the adaptation of the people's menu," and elected [[Teukoe Nja' Arif|Teukoe Nja' Arif]] as the new vice-chairman in succession to the arrested [[Teukoe Moehamad Hasan|Teukoe Moehamad Hasan]]. It was on the council's recommendation that the [[Permai|Permai]] mass organisation was established on [[20 January]] [[1945]]. The 4th session of [[20 April|20]]–[[21 April]] [[1945]] returned to "the old familiar points: the promotion of the war effort, food production, food supplies, and the 'voluntary' labor." The council was, in Piekaar's reading, the institutional stage on which the silent struggle for power between *oelèëbalang* and *oelama* was waged through the medium of Japanese propaganda.

## The Sumatra Council of March 1945

"Besides a further expansion of the Resident's councils, however the year 1945 also brought the establishment in March 1945 of an advisory body for the entire of Sumatra, the Sumatra Council (Jap. *Sumatora-chia-sangi-in*, literally — ! — members of the central council of Sumatra) seated at [[Bukittinggi|Bukittinggi]] (Fort de Kock). The task of this council was described in the establishment ordinance as 'to deliberate with the Governor of Sumatra on important political matters concerning the population of Sumatra and in case the necessity for this should be deemed present to make known the opinion of its members'. Of the 30 members that the council would count, 15 would be elected by the Resident's councils (Jap. *shii-sangi-kai*) and 15 would be appointed by the Governor of Sumatra." In the two places allotted to Atjèh, the Atjèh Council elected "the most prominent figure among the *oelèëbalangs*, [[Teukoe Nja' Arif|Teukoe Nja' Arif]] and the most prominent figure among the *oelama*'s, [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]]."

## The reorganisation of February 1945 and the council's character

A new Residents' ordinance of [[1 February]] [[1945]] no. 2 expanded the Atjèh Council to between 35 and 40 members — the elected component remained the same size, but the Resident's appointed members were increased, and the electoral colleges themselves expanded so that they were "no longer private affairs of the *oelèëbalangs*, as was the case in the first regulation." After the first election under the new provisions on [[20 February]] [[1945]], "whereas in the old Atjèh Council 17 of the 30 members still belonged to the *oelèëbalang* group, in the new council the nominal majority of the *oelèëbalang* families was done with and they occupied only 19 of the 40 seats. The *oelama*'s saw the number of their seats rise from 7 to 10." The council retained, however, its "purely advisory character" — a body whose formal powers never matched the influence it wielded through the network of patronage and propaganda that the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] built around it.

**Korps Mariniers (Dutch Marine Corps)** was the elite amphibious infantry branch of the [[Royal Netherlands Navy]], founded on [[10 December]] [[1665]] and active until [[1945]]. Originally a small detachment for shipboard security, it expanded in the mid‑nineteenth century to become the primary Dutch expeditionary force in the [[Dutch East Indies]], taking part in riverine campaigns, anti‑piracy actions, the [[Strait of Shimonoseki]] crisis, and the prolonged conflict with the [[Atjeh (Aceh)]] Sultanate during the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] (c. [[1873]]–[[1904]]). The corps earned numerous [[Eervolle Vermelding|honourable mentions]] and nominations for the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] throughout its service.

## Origins and Early Role (1665–1857)

The Korps was created by the *Oprichting etattut der Nederlandsche Mariniers* on [[10 December]] [[1665]] as a naval infantry component of the [[Dutch]] navy, tasked with shipboard combat and coastal defence. Throughout the eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries it remained modest, serving aboard Dutch warships and in occasional overseas garrisons, from the Caribbean to the Indian Ocean.

## Expansion and Reorganisation (1855–1856)

In [[1855]] [[Storm (politician)|Storm]], a member of the [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal]], urged [[Minister of Marine Smit van den Broeke|Smit van den Broeke]] to enlarge the corps so that a sufficient number of marines could be kept in the Indies. The proposal was initially rejected because of recruitment difficulties. The following year, under [[Minister of Marine Lotsy|Lotsy]], the corps received a seventh company, marking its first substantive enlargement.

## Training of Marine Officers (1860–1861)

From [[1860]] a three‑year cadet programme for marine officers was instituted at the [[Royal Naval Institute at Willemsoord|Willemsoord]], headed by [[F. G. H. Hornborstel|F. G. H. Hornborstel]]. The programme was briefly transferred to the [[Royal Military Academy in Breda|Breda]], before returning to Willemsoord in [[1861]].

## Colonial Operations (1858–1865)

### Riverine Campaigns and Boni Expeditions

A detachment of 24 corporals and marines took part in the [[Expedition to Retch 1858]], assaulting [[Retch]] and suffering four killed and seven wounded; their conduct was later cited for possible awards of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]]. The same force participated in the [[First Boni Expedition 1859]] and the [[Second Boni Expedition 1860]], earning further nominations for the order.

Between [[1859]] and [[1863]] the Korps operated along the [[Soengei Teweh]], [[Soengei Boentok]], [[Soengei Karrau]], [[Soengei Pakoe]], and [[Soengei Mentallat]] rivers in Sumatra, securing Dutch control over interior waterways. These actions resulted in several [[Militaire Willemsorde|Military William Order]] nominations and [[Eervolle Vermelding|honourable mentions]] for participating marines.

### Anti‑Piracy Action (1860)

In [[1860]] the steamship [[Reinier Claeszen]] destroyed a pirate nest on [[Saljocsoe]] (Paternoster Islands), an operation in which marines from the Korps played a key role.

### West Indies Deployment (1862)

A detachment under [[Captain G. Tuning|G. Tuning]] was sent to the [[West Indies]] in [[1862]] to quell a slave uprising in [[Berbice]], demonstrating the corps’ utility beyond the Indonesian archipelago.

## Japanese Expedition (1863–1864)

During the [[Strait of Shimonoseki]] crisis, a joint Anglo‑French‑Dutch squadron—including the Korps‑manned corvette [[Medusa (ship)|Medusa]]—forced the strait in [[1863]] and [[1864]], engaging Japanese shore batteries. The action was recorded by [[Jhr F. de Casembroot|Jhr F. de Casembroot]].

## Aceh Campaign (1873–1876)

### Early Involvement (1873–1875)

From [[1873]] the Korps Mariniers played a central role in the [[Atjeh expedition (1873–1876)]], providing amphibious troops for riverine assaults and coastal landings. Uniforms of the period (illustrated in the source) are documented for [[1873]]–[[1876]].

### 1875–1876 Campaigns

During the main Aceh campaign of [[1875]]–[[1876]] the corps fielded approximately 660 men, organised into several companies and detachments. They formed the vanguard of the expedition, protecting artillery sections, constructing bridges, and building a [[benteng|benteng]] at [[Bockit Daroe]]. They fought in marshy sawahs, crossed mountain passes such as [[Glitaroem]] and [[Beradin]], and endured a cholera epidemic that afflicted the force (see [[Cholera outbreak in Aceh 1875–1876]]).

The campaign of [[26 December]] [[1875]] – [[4 September]] [[1876]] is recorded in the *Atjeh Marineblad* ([[1890]]–[[1891]]) and in Cambier’s monographs. In the battle of [[Daroe]] the corps suffered one dead and ten wounded. The harsh conditions and the corps’ performance reinforced the reputation of the marines as adaptable land‑combat troops, influencing later Dutch marine‑land divisions in the archipelago.

## Honors

Members of the Korps received several [[Eervolle Vermelding|honourable mentions]] and nominations for the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] in the years [[1858]], [[1859]], [[1860]], and again during the riverine campaigns of the early 1860s, as well as for actions during the Aceh campaign of the mid‑1870s.

## Legacy

The experience in Aceh demonstrated the adaptability of the marines to land warfare, shaping subsequent Dutch colonial military doctrine and the organization of marine‑land units throughout the Dutch East Indies. The corps also set precedents for modern Dutch amphibious doctrine, leaving a lasting imprint on Acehnese history.

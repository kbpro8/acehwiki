**The Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut** — the Royal Association "Colonial Institute" — was the Amsterdam-based Dutch colonial-ethnographic institute that served, throughout the late colonial period, as the principal metropolitan centre for the scholarly and commercial study of the [[Netherlands Indies|Netherlands Indies]]. Established in [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]] as a private association — *Vereeniging* — with royal patronage (*Koninklijke*), the institute was reorganised after Indonesian independence as the **Koninklijke Vereniging "Indisch Instituut"** (Royal Society "Indies Institute"), under which name it issued its post-war series of *Mededeelingen* (Communications). Through its Ethnography Department — the *Afdeeling Volkenkunde* — and its publishing programme, the institute was, in both its pre- and post-reorganisation phases, one of the principal metropolitan venues for the comparative ethnology of the archipelago, including the comparative ethnology of Islam in [[Java|Java]] and [[Aceh|Atjeh]].

## Foundation and Character

The Koloniaal Instituut was established in Amsterdam as a private association with royal patronage, to serve as the metropolitan centre of Dutch colonial scholarship on the Indies. Its foundation belonged to the same early-twentieth-century movement that produced the Dutch "Ethical Policy" (*Ethische Politiek*) of state-led development for the Indies: the institute was intended to consolidate, in the metropolis, the scholarly and commercial knowledge of the colonies that the Dutch colonial state needed for the administration and economic exploitation of the archipelago. Its Amsterdam location — rather than [[The Hague|The Hague]], the seat of the colonial ministry — reflected its character as a semi-civilian, semi-scholarly institution rather than a government department; its royal patronage placed it in the same register as the other Dutch royal institutes (*Koninklijk Instituut*) of the period.

The institute housed several departments — *Afdeelingen* — of which the *Afd. Volkenkunde* (Ethnography Department) was the most important for the documentation of Atjeh craft. The *Afd. Volkenkunde* built up, in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, a substantial collection of Indonesian ethnographic objects — including Atjeh looms, Atjeh silk textiles, Atjeh sirih-ware, and Atjeh brassware — that formed the principal Dutch museum collection of Atjeh craft objects in the late colonial period.

## The 1933 Bezemer Atlas

The Koloniaal Instituut's role in the publication of the [[Indonesische Kunstnijverheid (Bezemer 1933)|Bezemer atlas of 1933]] is recorded on the title-page as one of *medewerking* (co-operation). The phrase *UITGEGEVEN MET MEDEWERKING DER KONINKLIJKE VEREENIGING "KOLONIAAL INSTITUUT", AMSTERDAM* — "Published with the co-operation of the Royal Association 'Colonial Institute', Amsterdam" — indicates that the institute supplied the institutional and collections resources for the atlas, while the actual publication was handled by the [[N.V. tot Exploitatie van het Maandblad "Nederl-Indië, Oud en Nieuw"|N.V. tot Exploitatie van het Maandblad "Nederl-Indië, Oud en Nieuw"]]. The division of labour was typical of late-colonial Dutch scholarly publishing: a specialist institute (the Koloniaal Instituut) supplied the scholarly content and the collections, while a commercial publisher handled the printing and distribution.

The Koloniaal Instituut's *Afd. Volkenkunde* supplied, from its own collections, the largest single group of objects illustrated in the atlas. The *Verzameling / Collection / Sammlung* appendix to the atlas (pp. 169–170) lists the lenders of the photographs and drawings, and the Koloniaal Instituut's *Afd. Volkenkunde, Amsterdam* appears more frequently than any other single collection: it is credited with plates 13, 17–18, 19, 20–21, 24–28, 29–35, 37–41, 42–45, 47–50, 51, 58, 60–61, 63–68, 69–71, 75–77, 80–86, 87, 113, 121, 124, 125, 127, 134–135, 137–139, 162, 167, 171, 175–177, 178–179, 180, 181, 182ed-e, 183, 187–189, 195–196, 200–207, 210–213, 215, 220–221, 222–223, 230–231, 234–237, 241. Among these, the [[Atjeh weefgetouw (Bezemer plate 62)|Atjeh loom (plate 62)]], the [[Atjeh imported silk warp ikat (Bezemer plate 65)|Atjeh imported-silk warp-ikat (plate 65)]], and the [[Atjeh silk ikat warp strokes (Bezemer plate 66)|Atjeh silk ikat with warp strokes (plate 66)]] are all listed under the Koloniaal Instituut's holdings — making the institute the single most important Dutch repository of Atjeh craft objects in [[1933]].

The Atjeh holdings of the institute — documented in the Bezemer atlas as plates 62, 65, and 66, and probably also contributing to the [[Noord-Sumatra standing brass lamps (Bezemer plate 174)|Noord-Sumatra standing brass lamps (plate 174)]] — represented the accumulated product of half a century of Dutch collecting in Atjeh, beginning with the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] period of [[1873]]–[[1904]] and continuing through the pacification and civil administration of the residency. After [[1942]] — the Japanese invasion of the Indies — and [[1945]] — the Indonesian declaration of independence — the flow of new objects from Atjeh to Amsterdam ceased, and the institute's Atjeh holdings became, in effect, a closed historical collection.

## Reorganisation as the Indisch Instituut

In the immediate aftermath of the Japanese occupation and the Indonesian revolution, the Koloniaal Instituut was reorganised as the **Koninklijke Vereniging "Indisch Instituut"** — the Royal Society "Indies Institute" of [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]]. Under its new name, the institute continued the ethnological and publishing work of its predecessor, issuing the series of *Mededeelingen* (Communications) of which the survey *[[De Islaam in Indonesië (Mellema, 1947)|De Islaam in Indonesië (in het bijzonder op Java)]]* by [[R. L. Mellema|R. L. Mellema]] (Amsterdam, [[1947]]) is No. LXXVII of the general series and No. 25 of the *Afdeling Volkenkunde* (Ethnology Section).

Through its Ethnology Section and its publishing programme, the Indisch Instituut was, in the late colonial and immediate post-colonial period, one of the principal metropolitan venues for the comparative ethnology of the [[Netherlands Indies|Netherlands Indies]] — including the comparative ethnology of Islam in Java and Atjeh.

## Structure and Sections

The institute was organised, in both its Koloniaal Instituut and Indisch Instituut phases, into a number of *Afdeelingen* (sections), of which the *Afdeeling Volkenkunde* (Ethnology Section) was the principal vehicle for the publication of ethnological surveys, monographs, and comparative studies. The *Mededeelingen* of the Ethnology Section — numbered separately within the general *Mededeelingen* of the Society — constituted a serial record of mid-twentieth-century Dutch ethnological work on the archipelago. Mellema's Islam survey of [[1947]] is No. 25 of that sub-series, and the staff of the Section included, at the time of the survey's publication, the author himself in the capacity of *Taalkundig Medewerker* (linguistic collaborator) — a post that gave a former colonial teacher of Javanese a metropolitan base for ethnological compilation.

The institute likewise maintained a library and collections inherited in part from the earlier Koloniaal Instituut and connected, through the *Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen* tradition, to the broader network of Dutch Indies learned societies. The copy of Mellema's survey preserved in the [[Koninklijke Bibliotheek|Koninklijke Bibliotheek]] at [[The Hague]] (shelfmark 903 A 29) carries the Institute's imprint.

## The Mededeelingen Series

The *Mededeelingen* of the Indisch Instituut were issued as separately paginated pamphlets, each devoted to a single subject and typically written by a member or correspondent of the Institute's sections. The series covered ethnology, economics, agriculture, and law; the Ethnology Section's contributions, of which Mellema's is No. 25, formed a coherent sub-series of comparative ethnological surveys. The pamphlets were priced for a general readership (Mellema's at f 1.50) and were intended, as Mellema states in his introduction, to give "*voorlichting*" — guidance — to those coming into contact with the world of the Indies.

## Role in the Comparative Ethnology of Atjeh

The Institute's Ethnology Section was, through publications such as Mellema's, a venue in which the Islamic institutions of [[Aceh|Atjeh]] were set in comparative relation to those of Java and the rest of the archipelago. Mellema's survey treats five Atjehnese institutions comparatively: the [[Moekim and Imeum in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|moekim–imeum territorial system]], the [[Rangkang|meunasah/rangkang]] village chapel, the [[Oelama zakaat war-chest in the Atjehoorlog (Mellema 1947)|diversion of zakaat as a war-chest by the oelama's]], the [[Haddji title prestige in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|prestige of the Haddji title]], and the [[Arabic-inscribed graves in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|oldest Arabic-inscribed graves]]. The Institute's imprimatur thus places these comparative observations within the institutional framework of late colonial metropolitan ethnology, a generation after [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|De Atjehers]]* and in the immediate aftermath of the Japanese occupation and the Indonesian revolution.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut (in both its Koloniaal Instituut and Indisch Instituut phases) is significant as the institutional publisher of two principal sources: the Bezemer atlas of [[1933]], which furnishes the principal photographic record of Atjeh craft objects in Dutch museum collections, and Mellema's [[De Islaam in Indonesië (Mellema, 1947)|1947 survey]], which furnishes an independent comparative account of several Atjehnese Islamic institutions. The Institute's Ethnology Section, as the venue for these and other *Mededeelingen*, represents the metropolitan continuation of the Dutch ethnological tradition that had produced Snouck Hurgronje's monograph at [[Leiden|Leiden]] half a century earlier; by [[1947]], the tradition was being maintained in the revolutionary twilight of the colonial order it had been built to study.

The institute's institutional continuity — through the Japanese occupation, the Indonesian revolution, and the post-[[1949]] reorganisation of Dutch colonial-era institutions — preserved the Atjeh collection into the post-colonial period, when it eventually passed to the Royal Tropical Institute (*Koninklijk Instituut voor de Tropen*) and other successor collections. Any AcehWiki entry on an Atjeh craft object that draws on the Bezemer atlas is, in effect, drawing on the Koloniaal Instituut's documentation of its own collection.

## Note on the Koninklijk Instituut (KITLV)

The Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut / Indisch Instituut of Amsterdam is a distinct institution from the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] (KITLV) of [['s-Gravenhage|The Hague]], which was founded in [[1851]] as a learned society for the philology, geography, and ethnology of the Netherlands Indies and is treated in a separate AcehWiki entry. The two institutions are often confused in popular usage but were independent foundations in different cities with different publication programmes (the *Mededeelingen* of the Amsterdam institute, the *Bijdragen tot de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* of the KITLV).

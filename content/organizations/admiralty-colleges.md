**Admiralty colleges** were the administrative bodies of the **Dutch Navy**, responsible for shipbuilding, provisioning, and strategic planning. The source cites them repeatedly as the “opdrachtgevers in Den Haag” (commissioners in **The Hague**) who bore ultimate responsibility for the failure of a landing operation in [[1674]]. The author argues that De Ruyter’s lack of direct command over the landing troops was due to the Admiralty’s delegation of authority.

### Functions and authority
The Admiralty colleges coordinated with the **Dutch East India Company** and the **Stadtholder** (notably **Willem III of Orange**) on matters of naval deployment. Their decisions affected both European campaigns and overseas expeditions, including the protection of VOC trade routes to the **Aceh Sultanate**.

### Impact on Aceh‑related operations
Through the Admiralty’s oversight, Dutch naval forces were able to safeguard the maritime traffic that carried pepper from **Aceh** to Europe. Later, Admiralty directives authorized the deployment of warships during the **Aceh War**, demonstrating the lasting influence of these institutions on Dutch‑Aceh relations.

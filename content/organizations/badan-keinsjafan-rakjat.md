**Badan Keinsjafan Rakjat** (B.K.R.) was an association formed by the surviving *[[Uleebalang|oelèëbalang]]* class of Atjeh in the early 1950s, under the leadership of [[Teuku Mohammed Ali Samalanga|Teuku Mohammed Ali Samalanga]], as the *oelèëbalang*s took new heart from the central government's efforts, following the merger of Atjeh into the province of [[North Sumatra]] in [[1951]], to restore non-[[Poesa|Poesa]] and *oelèëbalang*-class officials to positions of administrative influence. Henri J. H. Alers's *Om een rode of groene Merdeka* presents its programme as aimed squarely at unwinding the results of the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|1945–46 social revolution]].

## Formation and programme

Alers records that the new resident-coordinator of Atjeh, the Javanese [[Danubroto|Danubroto]], attempted to place loyal Atjèhnese — chiefly of the *oelèëbalang* class — and non-Atjèhnese officials into positions that Poesa members had held since the revolution. Emboldened by this shift, the *oelèëbalang* class organised the Badan Keinsjafan Rakjat under Teuku Mohammed Ali Samalanga. Its programme called for the abolition of the [[Madjelis Penimbang|Madjelis Penimbang]], the replacement of the Poesa-dominated civil-service corps, and the holding of general elections.

## The government's assessment

The central government regarded the B.K.R.'s demands concerning the return of confiscated *oelèëbalang* property as "very moderate," since the *oelèëbalang*s asked only for enough land to support themselves. Alers cautions, however, against reading this moderation as evidence of weak intent: had the Poesa's grip on the administrative, judicial, and police posts of Atjeh been broken as the B.K.R. urged, the *oelèëbalang* class might well, he argues, have been able to undo the entire social revolution of [[1946]] with comparative ease. Sociologically, Alers situates the B.K.R.'s confrontation with the Poesa within the same broader pattern he traces throughout Atjeh's post-war history: the Poesa in the position of "republicans," the *oelèëbalang* class of "feudal-federalists," and the central republican government occupying, in its dealings with both, something of the position the former Netherlands Indies government had once held.

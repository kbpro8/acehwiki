The **Sultanate of Aceh** was a powerful sovereign Islamic empire established on the northern tip of Sumatra that dominated regional maritime trade, pepper production, and political affairs across northern Sumatra and the Malacca Strait from the sixteenth century until its formal annexation by the Netherlands in [[1874]].

## Governance and Structure

The Sultanate was ruled by the Sultan from the royal palace compound ([[Kraton of Aceh]]) in [[Banda Aceh]]. State administration was structured around the three federal districts (*Sagi*): the [[XXII Mukims]], [[XXV Mukims]], and [[XXVI Mukims]], governed by *Panglima Sagi* such as [[Panglima Polim]]. Four senior ministers (*Mantri Hari-Hari*), including the supreme judge [[Teuku Kadli]], assisted the monarch in daily governance.

## Pepper Monopoly and Foreign Relations

During its peak in the seventeenth century under Sultan Iskandar Muda, Aceh controlled pepper production along the East Coast (to Djambi) and West Coast (to Indrapura). European trading companies, including the VOC (1606) and English traders, operated under royal commercial charters.

Following the Anglo-Dutch [[Treaty of Sumatra]] in [[1871]], the Dutch launched the [[First Aceh Expedition]] in [[1873]]. Despite fierce resistance under [[Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX]] and [[Tuanku Hashim]], the falling of the [[Kraton of Aceh]] in [[1874]] led to the colonial [[Proclamation of Annexation of Aceh]].

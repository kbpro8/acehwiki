**The Atjeh-tram** was the military-origin tramway of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] that, by [[1917]], had become the principal transport artery of the *gewest* — connecting [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] and [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] with Indragiri and Seulimeum and, beyond the Goudberg-hoogvlakte, with the principal bevolkings-centra of the Noord- and Oostkust as far as [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] and [[Besitang|Besitang]]. Operated as the *Dienst der S.S.* (Staatsspoorwegen) under the Department van Gouvernements-Bedrijven, the Atjeh-tram had, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "haar militaire rol reeds geheel losgelaten" — already entirely shed its military role — and now possessed "in de voornaamste plaats economische beteekenis."

## Origins and the military role

The Atjeh-tram originated as a military line, built to support the operations of the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] that had begun with [[J. L. J. H. Pel|J. L. J. H. Pel]]'s expedition of [[1874]]. Its initial trackage — the line from [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] through Indrapoeri to Seulimeum, and onward across the Goudberg to the Noordkust — supplied the *bivakken* and *militaire posten* by which the successive Civil and Military Governors, from [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] onward, enforced the subjection of the *oelèëbalangschappen* of Groot-Atjeh. *De Buitenbezittingen* refers the reader to its earlier, fuller account in *Deel I* of the work for the *uitgebreid overzicht omtrent de ontwikkeling van de Atjeh-tram*.

## The First Section: Kota Radja–Olehleh (1874–1875)

The first section of the Atjeh-tram — the smalspoorbaan from [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] to [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]] — was built in [[1874]]–[[1875]] as one of the peacetime engineer works of the Staf der Genie at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]]. The biographer [[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] lists the work, in his enumeration of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s peacetime duties during his Atjeh service of [[1874]]–[[1875]], as "den aanleg van eene smalspoorbaan van Kota Radja naar Olehleh: den bouw van een vakwerk-aanleggtiier in laatstgenoemde plaats." The construction of a vakwerk-aanleggtiier (timber-framed landing-stage) at [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]] was the seaward terminus work: the railway was built to connect the port of [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]] with the military base at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] so that troops, supplies, and material could be moved between the two without the overland march. [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] had at his disposal for this work the large mixed labour force — some 900 vrije koelies, soldiers, and dwangarbeiders — that the Staf der Genie commanded at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] in the last months of his Atjeh service. The Kota Radja–Olehleh section of [[1874]]–[[1875]] is the earliest documented Dutch military railway in Atjeh and the foundation on which the later Atjeh-tram system was built.

## From military to economic instrument

The transition from military to civilian role was the work of the *pacificatie* under [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]], whose *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* after [[1908]] allowed the extension of the line into regions where no *bivak* had stood. The crucial southern extensions followed: "Between 1910 and 1912 the Langsa–Koeala Simpang section was laid, while only in 1913 was the extension from Koeala Simpang to Besitang begun." Beyond Besitang, the projected extension to [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]] on the [[Aroebaai|Aroebaai]] — which had recently been chosen in preference to Telok Taboehan as the line's terminus — was "reeds ver gevorderd." The *Deli-tram*, the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]], was simultaneously to begin construction of a line from [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] to Besitang, "zoodat over enkele jaren de Deli-Atjeh-aansluiting eindelijk tot stand zal zijn gekomen."

## Length, traffic, and exploitation

The exploitation-statistics tabulated in *De Buitenbezittingen* show a steady growth in length and traffic. The *lengte in K.M.* rose from 432 in [[1911]] to 460 in [[1912]], 465 in [[1913]], 471 in [[1914]], and 478 in [[1915]]; the *aanlegkapitaal* from ƒ17,544,000 to ƒ20,049,000. *Opbrengst reizigers* grew from ƒ473,000 to ƒ589,000; *vrachtgoederen* from ƒ198,000 to ƒ286,000; the *totaal opbrengst* from ƒ704,000 to ƒ925,000. The *exploitatiekosten* overtook income in [[1915]] (ƒ950,000 against ƒ925,000), so that the *netto-inkomsten* fell to a deficit of ƒ25,000 — the first negative year after the modest surpluses of [[1911]]–[[1914]]. The number of *reizigers* carried grew from 1,986,000 to 3,035,000; *vrachtgoed* from 103,000 to 120,000 tonnen.

## The Sigli ateliers

The great *ateliers* of the Atjeh-tram at [[Sigli|Sigli]], where "± 270 Inlanders en Chineezen geregeld werkzaam zijn," performed — for want of any other repair-establishment — much work for *particulieren* in the surrounding *afdeeling*. The ateliers served as a *vak-onderricht* school: the *goede resultaten* of this training, *De Buitenbezittingen* notes, "wijzen op eene werkelijke geschiktheid van den Atjeher voor ambachts-opleiding." An ijsfabriek at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] was likewise operated under Atjeh-tram beheer.

## Significance

The completion of the Deli-Atjeh-aansluiting, *De Buitenbezittingen* predicted, would be "vooral voor Atjeh's Oostkust een grooten stap naar verdere ontwikkeling," particularly once the haven-installations at [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]] were brought to completion. The line, with its subsidised tariffs for *peper, rijst en klappers* and its payments in kind to the *atjehsche* peper-planters — the Company paying them, on top of the purchase-price, "nog f 0.10 uit" per *pikol* — had become the principal carrier of the *bevolkingscultures* on which the *gewest*'s economy now rested.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1904) — Three Functions of the Atjeh Tram

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Mededeelingen publication frames the Atjeh Tram as serving three interconnected purposes in its development:

1. **Military Pacification:** The primary initial purpose was military. The tramway was a counter-insurgency instrument, enabling the rapid deployment of troops, supplies, and artillery from [[Koeta Radja|Kutaradja]] to suppress remaining resistance and maintain control. The abandonment of the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] after the betrayal of [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] in [[1896]] made the original [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway of 1885]] redundant, and it was gradually cleared. In its place came the longer tramway that, from [[1897]] onward, followed the Dutch extension of authority into Atjeh's Dependencies — "step by step" tying post after post of the ever-expanding military occupation to the command centre at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]].
2. **Economic Consolidation:** After the military phase, the economic function became paramount. The tramway connected the productive interior and the booming plantation region around [[Langsa]] with the port of [[Sabang]] and the coastal towns, enabling the cost-effective export of [[Rubber Industry in Atjeh|rubber]], pepper, and copra, and the import of goods and contract laborers.
3. **Administrative Integration:** The improved transport network allowed for more efficient colonial administration — enabling officials to travel, conduct inspections, collect taxes, and maintain order more effectively, reinforcing the state's presence across the entire Governorate.

The Bureau's publication references a "Kaartje der verbinding tusschen Atjeh-tram en Deli-net" — a map of the connection between the Atjeh Tram and the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli network]] — indicating that the Atjeh Tram was conceived as part of a larger plan to create a transportation corridor linking the northern coast with the plantation economy of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra]] (Deli area). The "Grafiek betreffende de Atjeh-tram" (financial graph) likewise tracked the line's economic performance as the colonial state assessed the viability of its investment.

## Account from *DLP-84-Atjeh* — the lowland tram alongside the Great Atjeh Road

The *DLP-84-Atjeh* pamphlet records that the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjehtram]] was a tram line that ran alongside the [[Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road (*Atjehweg*)]] in the lowland areas of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]]. The tram facilitated the movement of goods and people, contributing to the region's economic opening. The tram's presence encouraged intensified traffic, trade and a shift toward a more peaceful and prosperous society — the colonial pamphlet's framing of the tram as the infrastructural vehicle of the *pacificatie* (pacification) of Acheh.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**The Atjeh-tram** — the *Atjeh-tramway* — was the military-origin narrow-gauge (0.75 m) railway network of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, running from [[Oelèë Lheue|Oelèë Lheuë]] on the north-western coast to [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] on the south-eastern coast — 495 kilometres — with two short side-lines: [[Langsa|Langsa]]-[[Koeala Langsa|Koeala Langsa]] (9 km) and [[Beureunoen|Beureunoen]]-[[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]] (5 km). The tram is one of the principal legacies of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] to the civilian period, and the principal means of overland transport in the gewest at the time of Broersma's visit.

### Origin and the Concentratie (1884)

The first railway traffic in Atjeh ran between [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and the harbour-place [[Oelèë Lheue|Oelèë Lheuë]] — *'begrijpelijkerwijs tusschen Kota Radja en de havenplaats Oelèë Lheue'*. That remained the only railway until the time of the *concentratie*, when in [[1884]] the Dutch power restricted itself to a ring round Kota Radja, and a *ceintuurbaan* (girdle-road) connected the sixteen posts with the recently shortened railway, while three *straalbanen* (ray-roads) connected the chief station at Kota Radja with the *ceintuurbaan*. For a telephone in that post-ring provision was likewise made.

### The Break-out of 1897-1898

When in [[1896]] other insights prevailed concerning the conduct in Atjeh, and consequently the line of concentration round Kota Radja was given up, there came a need for a line through [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. In [[1897]] and [[1898]] the tram-line was laid via [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] to [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]]. It accorded with the political-military insights of that time that in the first years of the new century work was also done in the *onderhoorigheden* of Atjeh on a tram-line. From [[Sigli|Sigli]] was built in the direction of [[Langsa|Langsa]], but also in the direction of [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]]. In [[1908]] the line from Kota Radja to Langsa lay.

### Extension to Besitang and Pangkalan Soesoe (1910-1913)

After [[1910]] work began on the Langsa-[[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] section, and in [[1913]] the decision was taken to extend through to [[Besitang|Besitang]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]]. At Besitang the Atjeh-tram and the [[Deli-spoor|Deli-spoor]] meet, and over a common track both run to Pangkalan Soesoe — a track with three rails, because the Atjeh-tram has narrow gauge (0.75 m) and the Deli-spoor has a line-width of 1.067 m.

### Operation

The disadvantages of narrow gauge for permanent railway traffic are many and well-known, but at the time of construction one did not yet have the experience of the present, and in the works that one had to undertake in Atjeh thirty years and more ago, the military use alone stood in the foreground. The line was built while the enemy often came to disturb the work, and in later years one has had to remove the consequences of that. As the pacification of the gewest was accomplished, the exclusively military use diminished, and one had to consider the co-use of and for the population. Indeed, that increased strongly in the course of the years.

The tariff for the transport of passengers is 6 cents per kilometre for the first class, 3.5 cents for the second class, 1.8 cents for Europeans and Foreign Orientals in the third class, and 1 cent for Inlanders in the third class. The trains are usually formed of a small locomotive with a firewood-tender, a baggage-wagon, a first- and second-class carriage, several third-class carriages, and a sometimes great number of goods-wagons. The whole makes the impression not of tram or local railway, but of great railway in reduced edition. The first- and second-class carriages on the longer routes are arranged in the manner of the carriages on Java, in the first class with comfortable sliding chairs. Because the speed is never considerable — perhaps on average 25 to 30 kilometres in the hour — a long journey in the Atjeh-tram is tedious. One leaves Kota Radja early in the morning; for the use of the midday meal a roomy halt is given at [[Samalanga|Samalanga]], and one is in the afternoon at Lho' Seumawe, from where one leaves again early the next morning to find, at Langsa (if the train is not too late), a midday meal ready, and to arrive at Besitang against half past three. There one changes to the fast train of the Deli-spoor to reach [[Medan|Medan]] against six o'clock.

### Goods Traffic

The goods transport of private persons consists for the present of pepper, pinang and copra from the population to the harbour-places, to be shipped from there to [[Penang|Penang]]. The principal harbour-places are Sigli and Lho' Seumawe, and one usually chooses the harbour-place according to the market-price. There is indeed sometimes a difference in price between delivery at Sigli or at Lho' Seumawe. If the market-price is fairly favourable, the transport suddenly increases. Fruit is not much transported; of importance is only the dispatch of pisangs from [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoera]] to Kota Radja, which amounts to daily wagon-loads. A wagon-load is 4 tons. It is a pity that the Atjehnese does not dispatch *padi*, at least not of importance. Dispatch within Atjeh does take place, for example from Pidië to Idi, when there or further south there is a shortage, but nothing goes to the Oostkust van Sumatra.

With the transport of cattle it is otherwise. From [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], the centre of which is [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]], cattle goes to [[Sabang|Sabang]] and to Medan. For Medan the cattle are usually loaded at [[Lam Baró|Lam Baró]], a halt further on, and the tram then brings them to Besitang. From there the cattle are led by road to Medan, because a longer train-journey would be harmful to the animals. The cattle are dispatched with the through-trains. The transport of the products of the great agricultural industry the Atjeh-tram has mainly in Langsa and Tamiang: it brings them to Koeala Langsa and Pangkalan Soesoe or to Besitang for unloading towards [[Belawan|Belawan]]. The transport of palm-oil has been going on for only a good year, in casks; as soon as the transport by sea in tank-ships takes place, the Atjeh-tram must provide tank-wagons. The transport of wood in Langsa and Tamiang goes north and south. Its own firewood the Atjeh-tram buys from the population south of [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]].

### The Central Workshop at Sigli

The central workshop at [[Sigli|Sigli]] is worth seeing. If necessary, one makes and repairs everything there; a severe retrenchment could make it unfit for the important task. The 62 locomotives and 6 heavier mountain-locomotives come now and then for cleaning and repair, the wagons likewise. The workman is the Atjehnese, and under proper supervision he does perfectly. The road-labourers and even some mandores are likewise Atjehnese. Slowly the Atjehnese come forward as conductor. As engine-drivers Javanese and Malays serve.

### Significance

The Atjeh-tram is the principal means of overland transport in the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* and the principal legacy of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] to the civilian period. Its narrow gauge is a permanent obstacle to its further development, but its 495-km main line and its central workshop at Sigli make it the indispensable infrastructure of the gewest. Broersma's considered judgement is that *'tegenwoordig de Atjeh-tram groote diensten bewijst, onmisbaar is en bevredigend werkt, mits de leiding er van niet uit het oog verliest, dat Atjeh ook verbinding behoeft met de Oostkust van Sumatra en de bestaande verbinding zoo redelijk mogelijk het algemeene nut en niet uitsluitend een Atjehsch belang heeft te dienen.'*

## Attacks on the line

At the meeting Swart held with all the *[[oelèëbalang]]s* of the eastern part of the province at [[Lho' Seumawè]] in [[1908]], he raised the unceasing attacks on the tram, which had become the rule: "I firmly count on it that those attacks are finished from today. Should that not be the case, then I can assure the territorial chiefs that I shall make them feel it severely!" He did not say how or what or where — "but they still knew me there from the time when I was major at Lho' Seumawè, and so they knew that it would strike home. Then they said: "you may rely on it." And from then on the tramway line there was no longer destroyed."

Neither the Atjehtram nor the telephone line had been safe from [[Pang Nanggròë]] for a single day. In [[Pidië]] operations against the sensitive tram line continued into [[1911]]: "that was the finest opportunity to bring well-disposed [[keutjhi'|village heads]] into difficulty."

By [[1910]] the *[[Koloniaal Verslag]]* could report of the north coast: "the Atjeh tramway always ran without escort, and the trackmen no longer had to be protected, while for their quarters, erected everywhere along the line, no extra protection proved necessary; destruction of telephone lines had not occurred in the last months."

## Attacks on personnel

Several of the [[Atjeh-moord|Atjeh murders]] were committed against tramway staff. In September [[1913]] a train conductor was murdered; in June [[1915]] an overseer of the tram at [[Pantòn Laboe]] was attacked by an Acehnese woman; in [[1916]] the tram clerk [[Beets]] and his wife were attacked, Beets dying of his wound and the assailant being sentenced to death. Attacks on soldiers occurred repeatedly at tram halts.

## Connection with Deli

Lamster recorded that under Swart "the connection of the Atjeh tramway with [[Deli]] was established." The correspondent of the *[[Nieuwe Courant]]* wrote in [[1914]] that "in a couple of months [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] will be in direct rail connection with the Deli plantation district."

## Economic role and later fears

The Atjehtram, better roads and the co-operation of the administration made the [[Oostkust van Atjeh|east coast]] the first area of Atjeh to attract estate enterprise after [[1910]]. In the interview of [[6 March|6]]–[[7 March]] [[1914]] the [[Sumatra Post]] recorded the Governor's view of the tram as an undertaking in which more than twenty-five million in capital had been sunk and of which "a great deal more could have been made of it."

In [[1927]] Swart, then long retired, told the journalist [[J. Schaap]] that there was some danger that communism might be introduced into Atjeh along the Atjehtram, "for on this tramway line five hundred kilometres long almost only Acehnese work as track coolies, and it is not impossible that people among the staff may know how to work upon them; but if the higher staff act rightly, this danger can be reduced. If only one knows how to keep the people's confidence."

## Use by the Governor

Swart used the tram for administration as well: he toured with [[Liefrinck]] in a directors' carriage, stopping at every announced halt so that the *oelèëbalangs* could pay their respects; he travelled by special tram to [[Sigli]] with the surgeon [[Dubinsky]] on hearing of the [[Murder of Lieutenant-Colonel Scheepens (10 October 1913)|stabbing of Scheepens]]; and immediately after taking office in [[1908]] he went east by tram with [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]] as far as [[Langsa]] "to get an impression for myself."

**The Atjeh-tram** was the military-origin tramway of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] that, by [[1917]], had become the principal transport artery of the *gewest* — connecting [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] and [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] with Indragiri and Seulimeum and, beyond the Goudberg-hoogvlakte, with the principal bevolkings-centra of the Noord- and Oostkust as far as [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] and [[Besitang|Besitang]]. Operated as the *Dienst der S.S.* (Staatsspoorwegen) under the Department van Gouvernements-Bedrijven, the Atjeh-tram had, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "haar militaire rol reeds geheel losgelaten" — already entirely shed its military role — and now possessed "in de voornaamste plaats economische beteekenis."

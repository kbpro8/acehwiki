**The Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij** (Deli Railway Company) was the Dutch colonial railway operator whose network, centred on the *Cultuurgebied* of Sumatra's Oostkust, was destined — by the time of *De Buitenbezittingen* in [[1917]] — to be linked to the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] at [[Besitang|Besitang]] and so to draw the young European cultures of [[Langsa|Langsa]] and [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] into direct rail communication with the great tobacco and rubber districts around Medan, Belawan, and the [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] oil-refinery. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij as the southern counterpart of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] at the [[Aroebaai|Aroebaai]]: at the haven of [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]] "de aldaar aanwezige haven-accommodatie wordt aangesloten aan het net de Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij van en aan dat van de Atjeh train," while it also borders the establishment of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]], which "reeds voor jaren te Pangkalan Soesoeh eigen steigers bouwde."

## The projected Deli–Atjeh connection

The through rail-connection from Deli to Atjeh was, in [[1917]], the most important infrastructural project pending on Sumatra's east coast. The [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] had reached [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] — and, beyond the southern border of the *gewest*, [[Besitang|Besitang]] — and was already "ver gevorderd" toward its projected terminus at [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]] on the Aroebaai. "De Deli-tram zal den aanleg van een lijn vanaf [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] tot [[Besitang|Besitang]] aanvangen," the handbook noted, "zoodat over enkele jaren de Deli-Atjeh-aansluiting eindelijk tot stand zal zijn gekomen." Where the line was not yet ready, a *particulieren autodienst* carried passenger traffic between the two railheads; once the link was completed, the cultures of the [[Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh|Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh]] would be "economisch zeer nauw aan het groote Cultuurgebied verbonden."

## The haven at Pangkalan Soesoeh

The Company maintained installations at [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]], the simpler alternative to the abandoned oceaan-haven projected at Telok Taboean, ten km eastward. The choice of the smaller haven — dictated by the *bakoe-bakoe* (swampy) terrain, the cost of a road through "geheel onbewoonde niets produceerende" wilderness, and the growing likelihood that [[Belawan Deli|Belawan-Deli]] would become the Oceaan-haven for the whole Oostkust — meant that the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij, the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]], and the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]] would all share a single modest set of steigers and quays at the head of the Aroebaai.

## Significance

Although the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij lay, strictly speaking, outside the territory of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, *De Buitenbezittingen* treats it as one of the formative external agencies shaping the *gewest*'s economy. The *jonge Cultuurland* of [[Langsa|Langsa]] and Tamiang had, in [[1917]], no significant port of its own; its import and export moved through the southern connection to the Deli network. Once the Deli–Atjeh-aansluiting was complete, the *grootste behoeften van het koopkrachtige Cultuurgebied* — the great demand of the purchasing-powerful plantation belt — would draw the *Oostkust* and *Noordkust* of Atjeh ever more tightly into the Sumatran economic system the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij had done most to construct.

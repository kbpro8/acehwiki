**The Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij** (Deli Railway Company, abbreviated **D.S.M.**) was the Dutch colonial railway operator whose network, centred on the *Cultuurgebied* (plantation belt) of [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Sumatra's East Coast]], was destined — by the time of *De Buitenbezittingen* in [[1917]] — to be linked to the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] at [[Besitang|Besitang]] and so to draw the young European cultures of [[Langsa|Langsa]] and [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] into direct rail communication with the great tobacco and rubber districts around [[Medan|Medan]], [[Belawan|Belawan]], and the [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] oil-refinery. The Company operated two distinct networks: the **Deli-spoorweg** — the 92 K.M. main railway line from [[Belawan-Deli|Belawan-Deli]] via [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Deli-Toea|Deli Tua]] — and the **Deli-tram** — the tramway network that, by [[1913]], extended 170 K.M. southward to [[Tebing Tinggi|Tebing Tinggi]] and northward to [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij as the southern counterpart of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] at the [[Aroebaai|Aroebaai]]: at the haven of [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]] "de aldaar aanwezige haven-accommodatie wordt aangesloten aan het net de Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij van en aan dat van de Atjeh train," while it also borders the establishment of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]], which "reeds voor jaren te Pangkalan Soesoeh eigen steigers bouwde."

## Network History


The company took over the [[1883]] Deli-Maatschappij concession (granted [[1873]]) and built the original 92 K.M. *spoorweg* (main railway) from the anchorage at [[Belawan-Deli|Belawan-Deli]] via [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Deli-Toea|Deli Tua]], with a *zijlijntje* (branch line) to [[Bindjai|Timbang-Langkat (Binjai)]], completed in [[1888]]. By [[1890]] the [[Medan|Medan]]–[[Perbaoengan|Perbaoengan]] *zijtak* (branch) was opened, while a *tramlijn* (tram line) continued from Binjai to [[Selesai|Selesai]]. After a pause, a [[1902]]–[[1907]] *hervatting* (resumption) of construction extended the network southward to [[Tebing Tinggi|Tebing Tinggi]] and northward to [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] — the small export place south of the [[Aroebai|Aroe-baai]]. By [[1913]] the company operated a 92 K.M. *spoorweg* and a 170 K.M. *trambaan* (tramway), with a *maatschappelijk kapitaal* (company capital) of f 4,000,000 paying a 10% dividend in [[1913]] and 15% in [[1914]].

## The Deli-tram Network


The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]]'s network notes that the original [[1888]] *spoorweg* — from the anchorage at [[Belawan-Deli|Belawan-Deli]] via [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Deli-Toea|Deli Tua]], with a *zijlijntje* to [[Timbang-Langkat|Timbang-Langkat]] ([[Bindjai|Binjai]]) — was extended in the [[1902]]–[[1907]] phase with *tramwegen* (tramways): "the existing *spoorwegstelsel* was extended in all directions with *tramwegen*, which now reach southward to [[Tebing Tinggi|Tebing Tinggi]] and northward to [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]], the small export place south of the [[Aroebai|Aroe-baai]], with its shallow roadstead."

The Bureau's statistical tables for the Deli-tram show that the network's route length grew from 115 K.M. in [[1904]] to 170 K.M. in [[1913]], with the *aanlegkapitaal* (construction capital) rising from f 5,857,000 in [[1904]] to f 10,915,000 in [[1913]]. The Deli-tram's growth is, in the Bureau's framing, the principal private-sector counterpart to the *Gouvernement*'s [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] — the two networks together forming the principal railway infrastructure of northern Sumatra.

## The 1913 Aroe-baai Conflict


The Bureau's account of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] of [[1913]] places the Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij at the centre of the commercial conflict over the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s southern extension. The Bureau notes that the *uitbreidingsplannen* (extension plans) of the Atjeh-tram formed a "*dreigende aanval*" (threatening attack) on the "*tot dusverre onaangetaste verkeers-monopolie*" (hitherto unscathed transport monopoly) the company possessed in the *bloeiende, veel exporteerende streken* (flourishing, much-exporting regions) of the East Coast. The company's "*krachtige verzet*" (vigorous resistance) to the direct [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat|Tandjoeng Slamat]] junction — together with the parallel opposition of the Belawan-Deli and Pangkalan Brandan trade — led to the deferral of the direct connection in favour of a connection from Pangkalan Brandan to the Atjeh-tram. A [[1913]] concession (G. B. of 10 April [[1913]] No. 25) authorised further southward extension to Tanjung Balai and Dolok Mrawan, adding 150 K.M. of additional *tramweg*.

The Bureau's account of the *Aroe-baai-plannen* then identifies the Deli-tram network as the principal tramway network with which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]'s southern extension was projected to connect. The Bureau notes that "a plan for further extension of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] network, which was vigorously defended by various authorities, was the line via [[Besitang|Besitang]] directly to [[Tandjoeng Slamat|Tandjoeng Slamat]] on the Deli-tram network" — a direct connection that would have linked the two networks at [[Tandjoeng Slamat|Tandjoeng Slamat]]. The deferral of this direct connection — and the substitution of a connection from [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] to the Atjeh-tram — is the principal commercial conflict of the [[1913]] extension plans.

## The Projected Deli–Aceh Connection


The through rail-connection from Deli to Atjeh was, in [[1917]], the most important infrastructural project pending on Sumatra's east coast. The [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] had reached [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] — and, beyond the southern border of the *gewest*, [[Besitang|Besitang]] — and was already "*ver gevorderd*" (well advanced) toward its projected terminus at [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]] on the Aroebaai. "De Deli-tram zal den aanleg van een lijn vanaf [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] tot [[Besitang|Besitang]] aanvangen," the handbook noted, "zoodat over enkele jaren de Deli-Atjeh-aansluiting eindelijk tot stand zal zijn gekomen" ("The Deli-tram will begin the construction of a line from Pangkalan Brandan to Besitang, so that in a few years the Deli-Aceh connection will finally be completed"). Where the line was not yet ready, a *particulieren autodienst* (private motor-car service) carried passenger traffic between the two railheads; once the link was completed, the cultures of the [[Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh|Afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh]] (Department of the East Coast of Aceh) would be "*economisch zeer nauw aan het groote Cultuurgebied verbonden*" (economically very closely connected to the great plantation belt).

## The Haven at Pangkalan Soesoeh


The Company maintained installations at [[Pangkalan Soesoeh|Pangkalan Soesoeh]], the simpler alternative to the abandoned *oceaan-haven* (ocean port) projected at Telok Taboean, ten km eastward. The choice of the smaller haven — dictated by the *bakoe-bakoe* (swampy) terrain, the cost of a road through "*geheel onbewoonde niets produceerende*" (entirely uninhabited, non-producing) wilderness, and the growing likelihood that [[Belawan Deli|Belawan-Deli]] would become the *Oceaan-haven* for the whole *Oostkust* — meant that the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij, the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]], and the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij]] would all share a single modest set of *steigers* (jetties) and quays at the head of the Aroebaai.

## Significance


Although the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij lay, strictly speaking, outside the territory of the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (Government of Aceh and Dependencies), *De Buitenbezittingen* treats it as one of the formative external agencies shaping the *gewest*'s economy. The *jonge Cultuurland* (young plantation land) of [[Langsa|Langsa]] and Tamiang had, in [[1917]], no significant port of its own; its import and export moved through the southern connection to the Deli network. Once the Deli–Atjeh-aansluiting was complete, the *grootste behoeften van het koopkrachtige Cultuurgebied* — the great demand of the purchasing-powerful plantation belt — would draw the *Oostkust* (East Coast) and *Noordkust* (North Coast) of Atjeh ever more tightly into the Sumatran economic system the Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij had done most to construct. The Deli-tram is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal tramway network of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] and the principal private-sector counterpart to the *Gouvernement*'s [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]].

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**The Deli-spoor** — the *Deli Railway* — was the 1.067-m gauge railway of the planting-district of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]], running from [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Belawan|Belawan]] and northward to [[Besitang|Besitang]], where it met the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] in [[1908]]. The Deli-spoor and the Atjeh-tram together gave the traveller from [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] to Medan a single overland route — the first such route in the history of the gewest.

### The Junction at Besitang


At Besitang the Atjeh-tram and the Deli-spoor meet, and over a common track both run to [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] — a track with three rails, because the Atjeh-tram has narrow gauge (0.75 m) and the Deli-spoor has a line-width of 1.067 m. The connection of Besitang with Pangkalan Soesoe, where the oil of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum-Mij|Bataafsche Petroleum-Mij]] is exported, is a line with three rails. The Deli-spoor sought the [[Aroe-baai|Aroe-baai]]; the Atjeh-tram, in reaching it, had to cross the boundary of its own gewest.

### The Bajan Wood-Enterprise


The Deli-spoor runs a wood-cutting enterprise at [[Bajan|Bajan]] on the Atjeh-tram, nineteen kilometres from [[Langsa|Langsa]], which it took over from a trading company (*Bajeuën*) that had originally received a permit of three to four thousand hectares. The Deli-spoor, which wished to draw its sleepers from there, then took over the whole licence for its own account. The worked wood goes south by the Atjeh-tram under a special tariff. Sleepers and telephone-poles are worked on the spot. The Deli-spoor also acquired land at Perlanaan on its Asahan-line, on which it intends to plant trees for usable wood — a forward-looking measure to ensure its sleeper-supply in the future.

### Significance


The Deli-spoor is the principal railway of the planting-district of the Oostkust van Sumatra and the natural counterpart of the Atjeh-tram. The junction at Besitang in [[1908]] is the principal infrastructural event of the early civilian period in northern Sumatra, and the Deli-spoor's wood-enterprise at Bajan is the principal case of its direct interest in the resources of the Atjehnese gewest.

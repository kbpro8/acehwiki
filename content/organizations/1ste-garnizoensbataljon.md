**The 1ste Garnizoensbataljon** was a garrison battalion of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]] stationed in the *gewest* of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] during [[1940]]–[[1942]], and one of the four garrison battalions (together with the [[2de Garnizoensbataljon|2de]], [[3de Garnizoensbataljon|3de]] and [[4de Garnizoensbataljon|4de]]) that formed the Dutch garrison-infantry strength of Atjeh on the eve of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the 1ste Garnizoensbataljon is the Westkust- and Tapanoeli-oriented battalion whose progressive reduction through [[1940]]–[[1941]] is the staff history's principal measure of the Dutch pre-invasion force drawdown.

## Organisation as of 1 January 1940

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], the 1ste Garnizoensbataljon was grouped as follows:

- **Staff**: [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]]
- [[Tapatoean|Tapatoean]]: 4 brigades
- [[Blang Pidië|Blang Pidië]]: 4 brigades
- [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]]: 4 brigades
- [[Tjalang|Tjalang]]: 4 brigades
- [[Lam No|Lam No]]: 3 brigades
- [[Lokop|Lokop]]: 4 brigades
- [[Kotatjane|Kotatjane]]: 4 brigades

**Total**: 27 brigades (each brigade of garrison infantry numbering 21 men, for a battalion strength of about 567 men).

The battalion's principal task was the maintenance of order and tranquillity in the Westkust and in the upper-Atjeh–Tapanoeli border outposts. The brigades were required to carry out intensive multi-day patrols of eight to ten days each month, principally as a show of force, and were well trained in the conduct of small warfare against indigenous insurgents.

## Reductions of 1940–1941

In the course of [[1940]] the *Westkustcolonne* was formed from nine brigades of garrison infantry drawn from the Tapatoean, Meulaboh and Tjalang detachments (the Tapatoean and Meulaboh detachments were dissolved; the Tjalang detachment was reduced by one brigade). The *Westkustcolonne* was moved to the Oostkust van Sumatra and eventually to [[Lawe Sigalagala|Lawe Sigalagala]] to guard the German internee camp. The soldiers never returned to Atjeh.

In [[1941]] the detachments at [[Blang Indië|Blang Indië]], [[Lam No|Lam No]], [[Lho'Nga|Lho'Nga]], [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] (the latter two not part of the 1ste Garnizoensbataljon), [[Padang Tiji|Padang Tiji]], [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]], [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] and [[Boeloe Blang Ara|Boeloe Blang Ara]] — totalling 28 brigades — were dissolved. Of the brigades thereby released, 15 were formed into a column for use outside Atjeh, and the remainder were used to reinforce the [[2de Garnizoensbataljon|2de Garnizoensbataljon]] at Kotaradja. The staff of the 1ste Garnizoensbataljon was moved to [[Medan|Medan]]. By the outbreak of war in December [[1941]], the corps consisted only of the troops stationed on the Oostkust van Sumatra and the detachments at [[Lokop|Lokop]] and [[Lho Seumawé|Lho Seumawé]] (the latter taken over from the 3de Garnizoensbataljon).

## Significance

The 1ste Garnizoensbataljon is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the principal measure of the Dutch pre-invasion force drawdown. The battalion's reduction from 27 brigades to a token force in [[1940]]–[[1941]] is presented by the staff history as the structural cause of the Dutch inability to suppress the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] rising of February–March [[1942]] or to mount a sustained guerrilla after the Japanese landings. The transfer of the battalion staff to Medan in [[1941]] symbolised the formal Dutch abandonment of the Westkust as a theatre of independent operation.

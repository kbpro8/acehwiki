**The 6e Bataljon Infanterie** — also written *6de Bataljon* — was a Dutch colonial field infantry battalion of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]] that arrived in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] on [[8 April]] [[1896]] aboard the steamers three companies and the fourth company, and was immediately committed to [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s relief column for [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]]. Originally commanded by Luit.-kol. H. Bendien (who shortly transferred to the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]]), then by Maj. D. A. Okhuyzen (wounded [[17 April]] [[1896]]), the battalion became one of the most heavily engaged infantry units of the spring [[1896]] campaign, participating in the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet on 14 April]], the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Battle of Senelop on 17 April]], the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]], the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe on 29 May]], and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng Landing of 22–23 June 1896]].

## Background and Formation

The 6e Bataljon arrived in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] as part of the reinforcements summoned after [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]]. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] three companies disembarked from the *Reael* and the fourth from the *General Pel* — the latter vessel also bringing the [[Bergartillerie / Bergbatterijen|1e Bergbatterij]]. The battalion was quartered on the highest part of the exercise terrain at [[Petjoet]] in [[Koeta Radja]], in lodgings of kasso's, planks, galvanized iron, or asphalt felt erected because of the approaching west monsoon. Two of its companies — under Luit.-kol. H. Bendien, 10 officers, 170 European and 135 native ranks — marched the same day as part of [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s relief column for [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]]. After Bendien transferred to the 9e Bataljon, command of the 6e Bataljon fell to Maj. D. A. Okhuyzen.

## Role in the Atjeh War

On [[14 April]] [[1896]] the 6e Bataljon participated in the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]] against the bende of Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata. On [[17 April]] [[1896]] it formed part of [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Stemfoort]]'s colonne in the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Battle of Senelop]], during which Maj. D. A. Okhuyzen was wounded; two companies of the battalion were engaged as the middelste colonne via Adje/Boeneleh/Tjot Bada to Tjot Goet on [[2 May]] [[1896]]. On [[20 April]] [[1896]] it was held in reserve. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the battalion — now 14 officieren and 467 minderen (w.o. 243 Europeanen) — formed the *colonne tegen de XXII Moekims* under Maj. Okhuyzen, and on [[25 May]] [[1896]] led the *omtrekking* of [[Lam-Tjroeng / Daman / Lam-Poelau|Lam Tjroeng/Daman]] in the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|bestorming van de stelling Daman]]. On [[29 May]] [[1896]] it formed part of Luit.-kol. C. J. Laceulle's colonne in the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe]]. On [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] the battalion marched in the 2e (land) Colonne under [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]] in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing and Glé Broeë attack]].

## Composition and Strength

The battalion's strength developed through the spring. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] it fielded 10 officers, 170 European and 135 native ranks in two companies under Luit.-kol. H. Bendien. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] it reported 14 officieren and 467 minderen (including 243 Europeanen) under Maj. D. A. Okhuyzen. The [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|strength return of 1 June 1896]] recorded 13 officers and 411 minderen. Adjudant to Majoor Okhuyzen was 1e luitenant adjudant C. A. Meulemans. Casualties in the spring included Maj. Okhuyzen himself, wounded on [[17 April]] [[1896]] at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop]].

## Significance

The 6e Bataljon Infanterie exemplifies the rapid operational deployment of a fresh colonial battalion into a collapsing theatre: disembarked on [[8 April]] [[1896]], two of its companies were in combat within hours as part of the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|Stemfoort relief column]]. The wounding of Maj. Okhuyzen at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop on 17 April]] — the most costly single day for the Dutch in April — underscores the price paid by line infantry in the assault on prepared Acehnese *versterkingen*. The battalion's continued employment through the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] at the end of June demonstrates the operational tempo that [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] imposed on the Dutch forces in the four months following [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection.

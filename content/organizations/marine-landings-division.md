**Marine Landings Division** was the amphibious infantry component of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]] that operated on land and sea during Dutch colonial campaigns, including the 1868‑1870 expeditions against fortified Acehnese settlements such as [[Bandjar]], [[Kali-Anget]] and [[Kampong Djentarek]]. The division was composed of officers, marines, and sailors, and was frequently led by lieutenants of the marine corps, for example [[Lieutenant P. Spanjaard]] and [[Lieutenant D. A. van Vuuren]].

## Operations in Aceh (1868–1870)

- **1868**: The division participated in the assault on Bandjar on [[24 October]] [[1868]], supporting infantry battalions and providing artillery fire with two 12 cm howitzers.
- **1868**: It also took part in the capture of the fortified kampong of Kali‑Anget, where the troops advanced along steep, slippery mountain paths.
- **1869**: The division continued operations against resistant villages, suffering casualties including the death of a marine (possibly a corporal) at Kali‑Anget.

## Discipline and Reputation

Contemporary reports, such as those cited by Cambier (p. 188), criticized the division’s discipline, describing it as “a true gang” with commanders using “a heavy stick” to enforce order. Nevertheless, several officers received the **Eervolle Vermelding** (Honorable Mention) for their conduct, and some were later awarded the [[Militaire Willemsorde]].

## Significance

The Marine Landings Division played a crucial role in the Dutch strategy of combined land‑sea assaults that eventually subdued Acehnese resistance, setting precedents for later colonial amphibious operations.

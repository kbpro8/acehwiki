**Dutch East India Company (VOC)** was a chartered *company* of the **Dutch Republic** founded in [[1602]] that monopolised Dutch trade in the **East Indies**, including diplomatic and military interactions with the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] throughout the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries. The source notes that “soldaten” (soldiers) were employed on ships of the **West‑Indische Compagnieën** (the West‑Indian Companies, i.e., the VOC), a practice that later influenced the formation of the **Dutch Marine Corps**. The VOC’s activities in Aceh encompassed trade negotiations, occasional armed expeditions, and the deployment of naval forces that required specialised marines for amphibious operations.

## Early VOC‑Aceh Relations
The VOC first established contact with Aceh in the early [[1600s]], seeking pepper and other spices. Dutch naval expeditions frequently required **mariniers** (marines) to secure footholds on the Acehnese coast, a role that foreshadowed the later institutionalisation of the **Korps Mariniers** in [[1665]].

## Military Cooperation and Conflict
During the **Aceh War** ([[1873]]–[[1904]]), the VOC no longer existed, but its legacy of employing marines on VOC ships persisted in the Dutch navy’s doctrine. The source highlights that “soldaten” on VOC vessels were the antecedents of the fixed marine regiments created in the mid‑seventeenth century, which later participated in colonial campaigns in Aceh.

## Significance
The VOC’s commercial and military presence laid the groundwork for Dutch naval strategies that relied on specialised marine troops, directly influencing the structure of the **Dutch Marine Corps** that would later fight in Aceh. Understanding the VOC’s use of soldiers on its ships clarifies the evolution of Dutch amphibious capability in the archipelago.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**The VOC** — the *Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie* (United East India Company), founded in [[1602]] — was the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] chartered company that held the monopoly of Dutch trade east of the Cape of Good Hope and west of the Strait of Magellan until its dissolution in [[1799]]. In the source's narrative, the VOC's encounters with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — through the [[Gujerat|Gujerat]]-Atjeh trade route, through the Westkust of Sumatra, and through the early-seventeenth-century elephant-fight at the Atjehnese court — are the principal pre-Company-era Dutch-Atjehnese interactions.

### The pre-Company route via Atjeh

The source records that the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] reached [[Suratte|Suratte]] — the principal port of the [[Mogol|Mogol]] empire — via [[Acheh|Atjeh]]: "het was juist vanuit Atjeh, dat de Nederlanders, reeds tijdens de Voor-Compagnieën naar de grootste haven Soeratte voeren" (it was precisely from Atjeh that the Dutch, already during the Pre-Company period, sailed to the greatest harbour Suratte). The Atjeh route to [[Suratte|Suratte]] is, in the source's narrative, the conduit by which the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] entered the trade of the [[Mogol|Mogol]] empire.

### The Westkust conflict

The source records that, on Sumatra's Westkust, "de Atjehers verdreven" (the Atjehnese were driven out) by the VOC, which "voor de moeite het pepermonopolie verlangde" (for the trouble demanded the pepper monopoly). The VOC's first establishment was on the island of Tjingko; two years later the company moved to the mainland. The encounter with the "Keizer van Minangkabau" — whose power the VOC "voor groot hield" (held to be great) — produced a typical VOC manoeuvre: the company "stelden de Hollanders het voor, alsof zij de kust ten pleziere van de keizer veroverd hadden en boden hem beleefd de souvereiniteit aan, met f 2000.- om de 3 jaar, mits de koopman te Padang er Menangkabaus stadhouder over mocht zijn" (the Dutch presented it as if they had conquered the coast for the pleasure of the emperor, and politely offered him sovereignty, with ƒ 2,000 every three years, provided the merchant at Padang might be his Minangkabau stadtholder).

### The pepper-and-salt monopolies

The source records that, on the Westkust, the VOC had both a pepper monopoly and a salt monopoly. Both were "ontdoken" (evaded). To maintain the salt monopoly, the company "vernielde men de zoutpannen ter plaatse (sedert 1687), wat de Compagnie er niet populairder op maakte" (the salt-pans on the spot were destroyed (from 1687), which did not make the Company more popular). In [[1749]] the High Government forbade "dit hatelijk dwangmiddel" (this hateful coercive measure).

### The pepper smuggling

The source records that "Met peper was minder makkelijk te monopoliseren, omdat niet alleen Atjeh en Benkoelen smokkelden, doch ook de eigen ambtenaren knoeide" (With pepper it was less easy to monopolise, because not only Atjeh and Benkoelen smuggled, but also the Company's own officials cheated). The [[1750]] establishment of a *handelssocieteit* (trade society) to counter the abuse was, the source suggests, modelled on [[Van Imhoff|Van Imhoff]]'s *Amfioensocieteit*.

### The dissolution

The VOC was dissolved in [[1799]]; its debts and possessions passed to the [[Netherlands|Dutch state]]. The transition from Company to Government — covered in Chapter III of *Nederlanders over de Zeeën* by [[H. J. de Graaf|De Graaf]] — is the institutional hinge between the VOC-era and the colonial-state-era Dutch-Atjehnese relationship.

### Significance

[[VOC|The VOC]] is, in the source's narrative, the institutional vehicle of the pre-[[1799]] Dutch-Atjehnese commercial relationship. The [[Gujerat|Gujerat]]-Atjeh trade route, the Westkust pepper-and-salt monopolies, and the [[1750]] *handelssocieteit* are the principal VOC-era Atjeh-related episodes in the source.

**The 2de Garnizoensbataljon** was a garrison battalion of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]] stationed at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]] and on the *noordkust* of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] during [[1940]]–[[1942]], and one of the four garrison battalions (together with the [[1ste Garnizoensbataljon|1ste]], [[3de Garnizoensbataljon|3de]] and [[4de Garnizoensbataljon|4de]]) that formed the Dutch garrison-infantry strength of Atjeh on the eve of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the 2de Garnizoensbataljon is the noordkust and Kotaradja-theatre battalion whose command was successively held by [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Major Palmer van den Broek]] (until [[July 1941|July 1941]]) and Lt-Col [[C. J. Bekkers|C. J. Bekkers]] (from [[July 1941|July 1941]]), and whose demoralisation and flight from the "Poort" stelling at [[Tangse|Tangse]] on [[13 March]] [[13 March 1942|13 March 1942]] precipitated the [[Poort stelling (Tangse)|collapse of the noordkust theatre]].

## Organisation as of 1 January 1940

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], the 2de Garnizoensbataljon was grouped as follows:

- **Staff + 3 companies** at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]] (with 23 brigades in total)
- [[Lho'Nga|Lho'Nga]]: 3 brigades
- [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]]: 4 brigades

**Total**: 30 brigades (each brigade of garrison infantry numbering 21 men, for a battalion strength of about 630 men).

The battalion's principal task was the defence of Kotaradja and the noordkust approaches, and the maintenance of order and tranquillity in the *sagi der XXII moekims* (whose *sagihoofd* was the eldest son of the late [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]]).

## Reorganisations of 1940–1941

In February [[1941]] eight brigades of the 2de Garnizoensbataljon were sent to [[Sabang|Sabang]] to form, together with the two brigades already there, a war detachment equipped with automatic weapons. In the course of [[1941]] the 2de Garnizoensbataljon received the bulk of the dissolved *[[Oorlogsdetachement Sabang|Oorlogsdetachement Sabang]]* (two companies) at Kotaradja, bringing its Kotaradja strength to four companies by February [[1942]]. The companies at Kotaradja were equipped with light machine guns from the dissolved Sabang detachment.

The battalion was transferred from Palmer van den Broek to Lt-Col [[C. J. Bekkers|Bekkers]] in [[July 1941|July 1941]]. The staff history notes that Bekkers had not previously served in Atjeh — a transfer the staff history implicitly faults for the loss of Atjeh experience at the top of the Kotaradja garrison.

## The March 1942 campaign

The 2de Garnizoensbataljon, under Bekkers's command, formed the **2de hoofdgroep** of the Dutch defence after the Japanese landings of [[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March 1942]]. The battalion retreated from Kotaradja via [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] and [[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]] to [[Tangse|Tangse]] and [[Geumpang|Geumpang]], where it was to hold the "Poort" stelling. The IVde divisie Maréchaussee brigades from [[Lam Meulo|Lam Meulo]] and [[Sigli|Sigli]] were attached to the battalion's command at Tangse.

The battalion's collapse at the Poort stelling — first on [[13 March]] [[13 March 1942|13 March 1942]] (panic induced by fireworks) and finally on [[17 March]] [[17 March 1942|17 March 1942]] (a Japanese flanking movement guided by Atjehnese along unmapped local paths) — was the immediate cause of Bekkers's capitulation on [[18 March]] [[18 March 1942|18 March 1942]]. The staff history's judgement is that morale among these and the other troops had deteriorated sharply, and that desertions and the failure to carry out orders occurred repeatedly.

## Significance

The 2de Garnizoensbataljon is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the principal narrative instance of the demoralisation that overtook the Dutch garrison troops in the noordkust theatre. The staff history's contrast between the 2de Garnizoensbataljon's collapse at the Poort and the [[2de (IIde) Divisie Marechaussee|IIde divisie Maréchaussee]]'s sustained resistance on the Westkust under Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] is the compilers' principal evidence that the structural cause of the noordkust collapse was the loss of experienced officers through the [[1940]]–[[1941]] reductions and the appointment of Bekkers — who had not previously served in Atjeh — to command.

**Atjèh Shimbun** was the Malay-language newspaper published in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] throughout the Japanese occupation of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]] ([[1942]]–[[1945]]), serving — in Piekaar's words — as the principal propaganda organ of the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] and as "a valuable, albeit one-sided, mirror of the times." Founded in the first months of the occupation under the editorship of [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] himself, and placed under the Resident's Information Service (*Atjèh-senden-han*) from March [[1943]], the *Atjèh Shimbun* published the official notices, propaganda essays, *gun-chó* appointments, and public proclamations through which the occupation was administered and the [[Asia Timoer Raja|Asia Timoer Raja]] and [[Indonesia Merdéka|Indonesia Merdéka]] ideas were drummed into the gampóng population.

## Foundation and early editorship

The *Atjèh Shimbun* was established in [[1942]] as part of the first consolidation of Japanese authority, and "[[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]]" — the head of the [[Masubuchi-kikan|M-organisatie]] — "since October 1942 also as director (*kepala pedjabat*) of the 'Atjèh-Shimbun'" served as its first chief. As of [[16 January]] [[1943]] he relinquished the editorship when he was appointed head of the department of general affairs; "in the latter position he was succeeded by the Japanese S. Sagawa, head of the information service. Alongside Sagawa, the '*kepala pedjabat*' (directeur), we see from March 1943 in the head of the paper as '*kepala pengarang*' (chief editor) mentioned the Japanese T. Kodera. In that same month, the publication of the 'Atjeh Shimbun' was placed under the information service, with which the primary function as a propaganda organ was once again emphasized."

## Uses as a source

Piekaar drew heavily on the *Atjèh Shimbun* for the second part of his study: "the second part, which deals with the events during the occupation years, is based on data from that period found in the archive of the administrative office in [[Sabang|Sabang]] and in the 'Atjèh Shimbun', the newspaper published in Koetaradja during the Japanese occupation, written in the Malay language, of which a large but unfortunately incomplete number of copies could be traced." He cites individual issues by number and date throughout: no. 7 of [[23 January]] [[1943]] (the "Pilot" article demanding a council of *oelama*'s for [[Abdoel Azis|Abdoel Azis]]); no. 22 of [[14 March]] [[1943]] (the famous misprint of Kipling's "twin" for "twain"); nos. 101–107 of [[15 December|15]]–[[25 December]] [[1943]] (the lists of F-people); no. 96 of [[8 December]] [[1943]] (the Schiller quotation by Oesman Raliby); no. 70 of [[4 September]] [[1943]] (the fasting-month fatwa).

## The propaganda apparatus

The newspaper was the public face of the *senden-han* (information service). "Continuously, propagandists of the *senden-han* traveled through the country, for the dissemination of the Asia Timoer Raja and the anti-Western ideas. In mass meetings, people like Kodera and Hasjmy of the 'Atjeh Shimbun', [[Said Ahmad Dachlan|Said Ahmad Dachlan]] from the F-movement, and [[Teukoe Sabi|Teukoe Sabi]], the *oelèëbalang-tjoet* of Oelèë Glé ([[Samalanga|Samalanga]], subdistrict [[Bireuën|Bireuën]]) who had been banished under the Dutch administration, all connected to the *senden-han*, spoke." During the great propaganda drives — the celebration of [[8 December|8 December]] (anniversary of the war), the second annual conference of the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] in June [[1944]], the independence-promise celebrations of November [[1944]] — "the 'Atjèh Shimbun' appeared temporarily daily." The newspaper also published the death sentences of [[Major-General Overakker|General Overakker]] and [[G. F. V. Gosenson|G. F. V. Gosenson]] on [[9 January]] [[1945]], and the official reports of the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]] sessions.

## Decline

By [[1945]] the *Atjèh Shimbun*'s scope had contracted sharply. "The Japanese propaganda diminished in strength, the fierce articles by *oelèëbalangs*, *oelama*'s and intellectual youth, which made the 'Atjèh Shimbun' such a valuable, albeit one-sided, mirror of the times, belonged to the past. Paper shortage had moreover already limited the scope for a long time." The newspaper's last months were given over to slogans such as: "If there are among the Indonesians spectators in the war for a Great East Asia, then they are our absolute enemies."

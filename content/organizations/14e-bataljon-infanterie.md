**The 14e Bataljon Infanterie** — also written *14de Bataljon* — was a Dutch colonial field infantry battalion of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger]], present in [[Aceh War|Atjeh]] at the start of [[1896]] and engaged in nearly every major Dutch column of the spring, from Maj. G. P. M. van der Noordaa's [[30 March]] [[1896]] relief column for [[Senelop]] through the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Battle of Senelop of 17 April 1896]], the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]], and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing of 22–23 June 1896]]. Commanded initially by Maj. G. P. M. van der Noordaa, later by Maj./Luit.-kol. F. C. Thomson, the battalion played a major — and costly — role in [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]]'s column at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop on 17 April 1896]], where the confusion in the retreat led to the loss of a 7 c.M. field gun and a mitrailleur. The battalion furnished troops for the buitenposten of [[Lamkoenjit]] and [[Tjot Goeë]] and was one of the largest in the theatre, with a [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|1 June 1896 strength]] of 15 officers and 516 minderen.

## Background and Formation

As a *veldbataljon* of the colonial army, the 14e Bataljon combined European and indigenous companies armed with Beaumont rifles. In the early months of [[1896]] it furnished troops for [[Lamkoenjit]] and [[Tjot Goeë]], sortied on [[4 August]] [[1895]] against the besiegers, and formed part of the garrison of [[Koeta Radja]]. On [[30 March]] [[1896]] it formed the *hoofdmacht* of Maj. G. P. M. van der Noordaa's relief column to [[Senelop]] — 1 major, 1 adjutant-lieutenant, 2 captains, 6 lieutenants, 227 European and native NCOs/men — together with eight [[Korps Marechaussée|marechaussée]] brigades under 1e Luit. W. K. J. Stoop, 1e Luit. J. W. C. Vuijkk, and 1e Luit. H. M. Vis. Kapitein Bruynis commanded one infantry company and Kapitein Labotz the infantry peloton of the *achterhoede*. On [[31 March]] [[1896]] two companies of the 14e Bataljon were sent by train to Lambaroe. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] the battalion — 17 officers, 155 Eur. and 277 native men — formed one of the three infantry battalions of [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]]'s relief column for [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]], with an ambulance under Off. van gesondh. 2e kl. J. L. W. F. van Leent (15 men).

## Role in the Atjeh War

On [[17 April]] [[1896]] the 14e Bataljon played a major role in [[J. B. van Heutsz (Luitenant-Kolonel)|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]]'s column at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop]]. The confusion in the retreat, however, led to the loss of a 7 c.M. field gun (without breech-block and sight) and a mitrailleur's chest with barrels — a humiliation that Kruisheer singles out as the most serious material loss of the spring campaign. The same day the [[Vestingartillerie (Fortress Artillery)|Vestingartillerie]] suffered two sergeants killed. On [[20 April]] [[1896]] the battalion operated in the final phase of the [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuation of the buitenlinie]]. On [[21 April]] [[1896]] its 5e compagnie (koloniale reserve) under Kapitein S. A. Drijber — at the time attached to the 12e Bataljon — conducted the [[Three-Column Operation to Free Tjot Rang and Kroeng Gloempang (21 April 1896)|evacuation of Tjot Rang]]. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the battalion — 16 officieren, 142 Eur. and 312 inl. minderen — formed part of the *colonne Lamtih* under Maj. G. P. M. van der Noordaa in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. On [[24 May]] [[1896]] Maj. van der Noordaa commanded a separate colonne (the *colonne v.d. Noordaa*) operating against [[Lam-Roekam]] and [[Lam-Oedjoeng]]. The battalion's peloton commander, 1e luit. Tetenburg, brought *munitieaanvulling* to the 12e Bataljon at [[Lam Manjang]] on [[23 May]]. On [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] the battalion marched in the 2e (land) Colonne under [[J. W. Stemfoort (Kolonel der Infanterie)|Kolonel Stemfoort]] in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing and Glé Broeë attack]].

## Composition and Strength

The 14e Bataljon's strength evolved through the spring. On [[8 April]] [[1896]] it fielded 17 officers, 155 European and 277 native men under Maj. G. P. M. van der Noordaa. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] it reported 16 officieren, 142 European and 312 indigenous *minderen*, again under van der Noordaa. The [[Bijlage VIII — Staat van troepensterkte op 1 Juni 1896|strength return of 1 June 1896]] recorded 15 officers and 516 minderen. Command later passed to Maj./Luit.-kol. F. C. Thomson. Company commanders during the spring included Kapitein Bruynis, Kapitein Labotz, and Kapitein S. A. Drijber (5e compagnie, koloniale reserve). Medical support was provided by the ambulance under Off. van gesondh. 2e kl. J. L. W. F. van Leent (15 men). Casualties on [[17 April]] [[1896]] at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop]] included two sergeants of the [[Vestingartillerie (Fortress Artillery)|Vestingartillerie]] killed, while the loss of the 7 c.M. field gun and mitrailleur chest was a permanent material loss.

## Significance

The 14e Bataljon Infanterie was a workhorse of the spring [[1896]] campaign and bore the most costly material humiliation of the semester: the loss of a 7 c.M. field gun and mitrailleur *kast met loopen* on [[17 April]] [[1896]] at [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|Senelop]]. That loss motivated the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|2 May search operation]] for the lost *geschut* — yielding nothing — and contributed to the operational urgency with which [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] pressed the May–June counter-offensive against [[Teuku Umar]]'s stellingen. The battalion's 5e compagnie (koloniale reserve) under Kapitein Drijber, conducting the evacuation of [[Tjot Rang]] on [[21 April]] [[1896]], exemplifies the operational flexibility of Dutch colonial units, which were routinely detached from their parent battalion for ad hoc tasks.

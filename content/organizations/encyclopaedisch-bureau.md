**The Encyclopaedisch Bureau** was the scholarly editorial office of the Netherlands East Indies colonial administration that, in [[1917]], compiled *De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 2: Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* — the second instalment of the systematic gazetteer of the Outer Possessions — as one of the *Mededeelingen van het Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen* issued by the [[Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen]] from the Landsdrukkerij at Weltevreden. The Bureau's imprint appears on the title page of the volume itself — *bewerkt door het Encyclopaedisch Bureau* — and its work is repeatedly cited in the running text, which refers forward to companion *Mededeelingen* still in preparation, including the *uitvoerige nota* on [[Simeuloë|Simeuloë]] that "weldra als Mededeeling van het Encyclopaedisch Bureau verschijnt."

## Place within the colonial information order

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau was the editorial arm of the *Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen*, the advisory office through which the central Gouvernement collated the administrative, geographic, and ethnographic knowledge accumulated by the Civil Service in the *Buitenbezittingen* — that is, the territories outside Java and Madura. Its *Mededeelingen* — printed at the Landsdrukkerij in Weltevreden — were intended, as the Atjeh volume's preface indicates, to make the *buitengewoon* officials' accumulated experience available in a standardised, retrievable form to governors, Residents, and Assistant-Residents serving successive tours in the field. The *De Buitenbezittingen* series was its flagship publication: a planned multi-volume encyclopaedia of the Outer Possessions, of which *Deel II, Aflevering 2* — covering the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] after [[J. L. J. H. Pel|J. L. J. H. Pel]]'s military occupation of [[1874]] — was the most substantial instalment yet issued.

## Working method and sources

The Bureau's editors worked not from independent research but from the consolidation of existing documentation: governors' reports, the *Nota*'s of officials such as the [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]-an *Adviseur voor Inlandsche en Arabische Zaken*, the surveys of the Dienst van het Mijnwezen and the Adviser voor het Havenwezen, the financial statements of the landschapskassen, and the archival records of the Sultans of Atjeh. Where its narrative relies on living informants it names them: the trade figures for the [[Sabang|Sabang]] haven, for example, are said to have been *verstrekt door den adviseur v/h. Havenwezen in N. I.*, while the koelie-cijfers of the European cultures derive from the *Arbeids-inspectie*. The Bureau's editorial voice — marked by the recurring "Sn." citation to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *Het Gajoland* — is that of a careful synthesiser rather than an original investigator.

## Significance

The *De Buitenbezittingen* series, and the Atjeh volume in particular, became the standard reference for the generation of Dutch officials who administered the *gewest* through the *pacificatie* of [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] and after. Its dense statistical appendices — Bijlage I listing every *Hoofd van Gewestelijk Bestuur* from [[1874]] onward, Bijlage III on steamship arrivals, Bijlage VII on credit-bank lending, Bijlage VIII on the contract-koelie population — codified the colonial state's picture of the territory at the moment of its transition from military to civil governance.

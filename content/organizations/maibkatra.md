**Maibkatra** — the *Madjelis Agama Islam Oentoek Bantoean Kemakmoeran Asia Timoer Raja di Atjèh*, or Islamic Council for the Welfare of Greater East Asia in [[Atjèh|Atjèh]] — was the official advisory council in religious affairs established by Resident [[lino|lino]] on [[10 March]] [[1943]] as the institutional vehicle through which the Japanese occupation enrolled the Atjèhnese *oelama*'s into the propaganda apparatus of the [[Asia Timoer Raja|Asia Timoer Raja]] idea. Replacing the one-man advisory post held by Toeankoe [[Abdoel Azis|Abdoel Azis]] as *madjelis sōdan-yaku*, Maibkatra gave the militant wing of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] movement, for the first time in the history of Aceh, an officially recognised body through which to make its voice heard — and provided the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] with the religious infrastructure through which the occupation could be made palatable to the gampóng population.

## From single advisor to council

In January [[1943]] Toeankoe [[Abdoel Azis|Abdoel Azis]], "*hoofdkali*" at [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]], was appointed "official advisor (*sōdan-yaku*) in religious affairs for all of Aceh." But because he "belonged to the more moderate *oelama*'s, so his appointment gave the radical *oelama* party, united in the [[Poesa|Poesa]], no influence on the course of events," the [[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]] of [[23 January]] [[1943]] published an article by the pseudonymous "Pilot" arguing that the new function "would only fully serve its purpose 'if Toean-koe Abdoel Azis would be assisted by a council consisting of some twenty *oelama*'s from all of Aceh'." Two months later the Japanese authorities "acceded to this demand" and replaced the single advisor with an advisory council, "given the grand name of '*Madjelis agama Islam oentoek bantoean kemakmoeran Asia Timoer Raja di Atjèh*'" — abbreviated by its initial letters as Maibkatra.

## The installation speech of 10 March 1943

The task of the new council was summarised by Resident [[lino|lino]] in his installation speech of [[10 March]] [[1943]] as "providing support — to the extent that it does not conflict with the Islamic religion — in the promotion of the welfare of Greater East Asia." The speech laid out the five subjects the *oelama*'s were to impress upon the population, the fifth being "to advise the people 'not to interfere in matters concerning the administration of the country and to point to the guideline provided by the Mohammedan religion of a pure and upright heart'." "For hundreds of years," the Resident exclaimed, "the peoples of Greater East Asia have been violated and drained by the white race. This evil deed will undoubtedly not be able to bear the approval of the Lord." As chairman of the main board was appointed [[Abdoel Azis|Toeankoe Abdoel Azis]], with [[Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi|Teungkoe Moehamad Hasbi]] and [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]] as first and second deputies, [[Teungkoe Moehamad Joenoes Djamil|Teungkoe Moehamad Joenoes Djamil]] as secretary, and — as the Japanese government advisors — [[Masubuchi|S. Masubuchi]] and S. Yamamoto.

## The oelèëbalang counter-weight

As early as [[1 August]] [[1943]] the council was reined in. The *gun-chó*'s were appointed as advisors to the local departments, so that "for the *oelèëbalangs*, this step represented an important victory over the militant *oelama*'s, for it assured them, through the *gun-chó* functions, which for the most part they held, considerable influence on the policy of the 'Maibkatra'." From then on, the council "could be less easily used by the militant *oelama*'s for the achievement of their own purposes." Its second annual conference, held at [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] on [[10 June]] [[1944]], was "entirely in the sign of the propaganda for the realization" of the three great Japanese war aims — defence, food production, and labour mobilisation — for, Piekaar observes, "who were better suited than the *oelama*'s to move the population through their religious dominance to an utmost exertion of strength for the good cause!"

## Dissolution into Permai

Maibkatra, like the [[PETA|PETA]] militia and the *Kapal Oedara Atjèh*, was dissolved on [[20 January]] [[1945]] into the new mass organisation [[Permai|Permai]] (the *Atjèh-shii-min-koa-hōkō-kai*). Yet the personal advisory position of Toeankoe [[Abdoel Azis|Abdoel Azis]] continued despite formal abolition, and the Maibkatra's departmental infrastructure — its branch boards in every sub-division, its religious-court network — survived as the institutional skeleton on which the *oelama* party would, after the [[Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945|Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945]], make its bid for power against the *oelèëbalang* class.

**The Benden van T. Oemar** — also referred to as *Umar's bands* or, in the singular, *Oemar's bende* — were the armed bands led by [[Teuku Umar]] (now under the name *Toekoe Melaboeh*, "the traitor Oemar") after his [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] from the Dutch colonial government, drawn mostly from [[Lampisang|Lampoe's Oe]] and [[Lepong]] and estimated at approximately 1,000 men by c. [[10 April]] [[1896]]. [[J. A. Vetter (Luitenant-Generaal)|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], in his *dagorder* of [[26 May]] [[1896]], described them as a "*sterke, geoefende, goed versterkte en goed bewapende bende*" (strong, well-trained, well-fortified, and well-armed band) — a characterization that Kruisheer reproduces verbatim. Sub-commanders under Umar included Panglima Poelau, Ketjig Bintang van Lam Loempoer, Toekoe Mançoer (Umar's brother), and various band leaders. The bands, equipped with the Dutch weapons supplied to Umar's former *legioen*, formed the principal Acehnese field force that the Dutch confronted in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]].

## Background and Formation

The Benden van T. Oemar were the direct institutional successor of the [[Legioen van T. Djohan (T. Oemar's Legion)|Legioen van T. Djohan]]: when [[Teuku Umar]] defected on [[29 March]] [[1896]], the legion — 250 men originally, later expanded with men from the IV/VI Moekims, [[Lepong]], and [[Lehong]] — went over to the enemy with their Dutch-supplied weapons (380 *achterlaadgeweren*, 500 *voorlaadgeweren*, 25,000 Beaumont cartridges). Umar, now operating under the name the traitor Oemar, recruited additional men from his home territory of [[Lepong]] and from [[Lampisang|Lampoe's Oe]] (the VI Moekims around Lampisang), and by c. [[10 April]] [[1896]] the bands were estimated at approximately 1,000 men. Umar's defection also forced the submission of various doubtful regions — T. Hoesin Lhongbatta with the 3 Moekims Lamrebo, 4 Moekims Ateuh (sagi XXVI), and the Moekims Hoho and Lam Djampoe of the V Moekims Montassik (sagi XXII); and Nja Mohamad of the IX Moekims, forced to submit by Umar's abduction of his wife.

## Role in the Atjeh War

The Benden van T. Oemar were the principal Acehnese field force of the spring [[1896]] campaign. Following Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] and his three letters to the Governor of Atjeh ([[First Letter of Teuku Umar (Bijlage II)|30 March]], [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar (Bijlage IV)|12 April]], [[Third Letter of Teuku Umar (Bijlage V)|23 April]] [[1896]]) — the second demanding ƒ150,000 monthly to pay his warriors and the third threatening further hostilities — the bands established themselves in fortified *stellingen* at and around [[Lam-Pisang]], supported by trenches and fortifications described in intelligence received from the two Oemar followers who defected to Hr. Ms. Nias on [[2 May]] [[1896]]. The bands' positions were bombarded continuously from [[27 April]] to [[23 May]] [[1896]] in the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|Bombardment of Lampisang]] — but, as Kruisheer notes, the results were "*zeer gering*" (very slight), only ~3 enemy killed and a few wounded, because the *bevolking* had moved to the *heuvels*. On [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]] the bands were assaulted in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]]: on [[24 May]] [[1896]] the Dutch captured Umar's house at [[Lam-Pisang]] (1e luit. de Jongh of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] first reaching the *verblijf* at 10:35 VM) and the Genietroepen destroyed it with *dynamiet, teer, petroleum*; on [[25 May]] [[1896]] the Dutch stormed the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]]. Umar himself retreated to [[Lepong]]. The bands' sub-commander Ketjig Bintang van Lam Loempoer — formerly the designated successor as commandant of the *legioen* — was reportedly killed by [[28 May]] [[1896]]. On [[26 May]] [[1896]] Vetter issued his *dagorder* recording the Dutch satisfaction at the success against the "*sterke, geoefende, goed versterkte en goed bewapende bende*".

## Composition and Strength

The Benden van T. Oemar were estimated at approximately 1,000 men by c. [[10 April]] [[1896]], drawn mostly from [[Lampisang|Lampoe's Oe]] and [[Lepong]]. Their armament comprised the Dutch weapons supplied to Umar's former *legioen*: 380 breech-loading rifles, 500 muzzle-loading rifles, and 25,000 Beaumont cartridges. The bands were led by [[Teuku Umar]] (now *Toekoe Melaboeh*) as supreme commander; sub-commanders included Panglima Poelau, Ketjig Bintang van Lam Loempoer (reportedly killed by [[28 May]] [[1896]]), Toekoe Mançoer (Umar's brother), and various band leaders.

## Significance

The Benden van T. Oemar represented the most consequential Acehnese field force of the spring [[1896]] campaign — a force equipped with Dutch weapons, organized under a unified command, and capable of fortifying a defensive sone (the *stellingen* around [[Lam-Pisang]]) that the Dutch could only reduce through a three-day combined-arms offensive with three colonnes and an artillery bombardment lasting nearly a month. The bands' existence was the direct institutional consequence of the Dutch *stelsel* of arming Atjeh auxiliaries under [[Teuku Umar]] — a *stelsel* that, as the *Koloniaal Verslag* would observe, had set "*al het sedert 1893 verkregene, as it were on loose screws*" (all that had been gained since 1893, as it were, loose on screws). Vetter's *dagorder* characterization of the bands as "*sterke, geoefende, goed versterkte en goed bewapende*" is, in Kruisheer's reproduction, the definitive Dutch assessment of the enemy that the spring [[1896]] counter-offensive had been called upon to defeat.

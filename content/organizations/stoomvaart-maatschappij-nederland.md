The **Stoomvaart Maatschappij "Nederland"** ("Nederland") was the Dutch mail-steamer company whose decision of [[1903]] to make the seaport of [[Sabang]] a fixed call brought [[Acheh|Atjeh]] into regular connection with the mother country and with foreign countries — "de kroon op 't werk" ("the crown on the work") of the harbour's development in author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s judgement, and the company's share in the economic development of the province.

## The mail route over Sabang

In the chapter *De Stoomvaart-Maatschappijen* Langhout writes that the history of Atjeh proves that also and especially the Dutch steamship companies had a working share in the development of the province — quoting the *Indische Gids* of late [[1922]], in which the Sumatra-connoisseur Dr. [[Broersma]] excellently sets out how the shipping companies can advance the development of an island realm — and that the company "Nederland" here takes a first place: she "zette in 1903 de kroon op 't werk door te bepalen dat vanaf dat jaar de zeehaven 'Sabang' als vaste halte zou gelden" ("in 1903 set the crown on the work by determining that from that year the seaport Sabang would count as a fixed call"). Her mail steamers had, since September [[1903]], taken the route over Sabang and [[Singapore]] on the out- and home-voyages; the first mailboat, the *Koning Willem III*, had been festively piloted into the harbour on [[29 September]] [[1903]] in the presence of Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] (see [[Inauguration of the Sabang mail service (1903)]]).

## Cooperation

"Een loffelijk blijk van medewerking" ("a laudable proof of cooperation") was the company's considerable lowering of her freight prices from the Netherlands to Sabang; the post offices at Sabang, [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]] and [[Koeta Radja]] were accordingly enlarged and equipped for the full mail service. "Sindsdien is Sabang gegroeid": the returning passenger ships from Holland every fortnight brought "een prettige drukte" ("a pleasant bustle") to the beautiful [[Poelò We]], and the still more frequent cargo vessels made Sabang an important shipping or transhipment harbour — photograph plates of the book show "De haven van Sabang, aan den steiger de 'Riouw' der Mij. Nederland" and "Sabangbaai met een aantal booten der Mij. Nederland" ("with a number of boats of the company Nederland").

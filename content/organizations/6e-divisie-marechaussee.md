**The 6e Divisie Marechaussee** (also **6de (VIde) Divisie Maréchaussee**) was the [[Bakongan]] division of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] on the [[Westkust (of Atjeh)|Westkust]] of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — the southernmost Maréchaussee division on the Westkust, garrisoned at [[Bakongan]] from at least the 1930s through the [[1940]]–[[1942]] Japanese invasion period. In the *dertiger jaren* (1930s), the division's 1st brigade was commanded by the *sergeant 1e klasse* (sergeant first class) [[Thijs de Fretes]] and hosted the *Marechaussee 1e klas* (Maréchaussee first class) [[Tanamal]] — the two set-piece Ambonese soldiers of *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*; in the [[1940]]–[[1942]] period, the division's seven brigades at [[Bakongan]] formed the Dutch anchor of the [[Evacuation of women and children from the Westkust of Atjeh (March 1942)|Westkust evacuation]] of March [[1942]], before being evacuated to [[Malakka|Malakka]] at the end of December [[1941]] under Captain Supheert as part of the pre-invasion drawdown.

## The Dertiger Jaren: Thijs de Fretes, Tanamal, and the Colonel's Visit (1933)

The 6e Divisie is the principal unit of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] mentioned in the chapters "Thijs" and "Dominee Thenu" of *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* — Dames's [[1954]] portrait-volume of the Ambonese professional soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]] The 1st brigade of the 6e Divisie was commanded by the *sergeant 1e klasse* [[Thijs de Fretes]] — the *woudloper* (jungle-runner) and *jager* (hunter) whose plain-spoken Dutch and uncanny marksmanship in the *[[rimboe]]* (jungle) made him one of the book's set-piece portraits of *[[Oom Ambon]]*. Dames records the 1st brigade command in a single, dense sentence: "[[Thijs de Fretes|Thijs]] was commandant van de le Brigade van de 6e Divisie Marechaussee, sergeant le klasse dus, maar de kaderschool had hij nimmer bezocht" — "Thijs was commander of the 1st Brigade of the 6e Divisie Marechaussee, sergeant first class therefore, but the cadet school he had never attended." His nephew [[Pietje de Fretes]] — the quartermaster (*fourier*) and later second lieutenant (*onderluitenant*) — was also at [[Bakongan]] with the division.

The 6e Divisie also hosted the *Marechaussee 1e klas* [[Tanamal]] — the "man with the formidable face" whose *ongelooflijk lelijke snuit* (incredibly ugly snout) became *één en al vrolijkheid* (all merriment) at *feestjes* (parties) where he served as *voorzanger* (cantor). Dames records: "[[Tanamal]] was: de Marechaussee 1e klas" at [[Bakongan]] in the *dertiger jaren*. The division therefore hosted two of the book's set-piece Ambonese soldiers: Thijs the *jager* and Tanamal the natural shot.

In [[1933]] the division received a visit from the colonel (*kolonel*) of the Korps Marechaussee. Dames records the visit in a single, dense sentence: "Toen in [[1933]] de kolonel op [[Bakongan]] was, had de *[[zelfbestuurder van Troemon]]* het over een *solitair*, die gevaarlijk was voor de bevolking en schade aanrichtte" — "When in 1933 the colonel was at Bakongan, the *zelfbestuurder* (native ruler) of Troemon spoke of a *solitair* (rogue elephant) that was dangerous to the population and caused damage." Thijs promised the colonel the rogue elephant's *tanden* (tusks) before the beast was shot — and shot it at five metres' distance. The colonel's visit to [[Bakongan]] in [[1933]] is therefore a visit to the 6e Divisie.

## Organisation as of 1 January 1940

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history]] records the following deployment for the 6e (VIde) Divisie Marechaussee (each brigade of Maréchaussee numbering 20 men):

| Divisie | Headquarters | Brigades |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| VIde Divisie | Bakongan | 7 |

**Total**: 7 brigades (about 140 men).

Bakongan was the principal Dutch post on the southern Westkust, in the *afdeeling Westkust* (Westkust administrative division) of [[Atjeh|Aceh]].

## The Evacuation to Malakka (December 1941)

The 6e (VIde) Divisie's [[Bakongan]] outpost was one of the Westkust outposts dissolved in the pre-invasion drawdown: at the end of December [[1941]] the outposts at [[Tapatoean]] and [[Bakongan]] were evacuated by the eight Maréchaussee brigades stationed there. The eight brigades (the four from Tapatoean plus the seven from Bakongan, minus three brigades left at [[Lho Seumawé|Lho Seumawé]]) were sent under Captain **T. J. F. M. Supheert** via [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Malakka|Malakka]] to conduct guerrilla operations against the Japanese on behalf of the Dutch allies. The evacuation to Malakka was part of the broader redeployment of Dutch forces in the context of the Japanese advance through Southeast Asia: with the [[Malayan campaign|British retreat in Malaya]] and the anticipated Japanese invasion of Sumatra, the Dutch colonial command transferred the 6e Divisie from its Aceh posting to reinforce the Malakka theatre. The vacated Bakongan bivak was later reoccupied as a concentration point by the Westkust evacuee convoys of March [[1942]].

The evacuation of the 6e Divisie reduced the Dutch Maréchaussee presence on the Westkust of Aceh at a critical moment — the months immediately before the Japanese landings of [[12 March 1942]]. The 1ste hoofdgroep (Westkust) of the Dutch response to the [[12 March 1942]] Japanese landings — under Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] — was organised without the 6e Divisie, whose brigades had been transferred to Malakka weeks before.

## Significance

The 6e Divisie Marechaussee is significant in two complementary colonial-Acehnese sources. In Dames's [[1954]] *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, the division is the *woudloper* unit of the *dertiger jaren* — the formation at [[Bakongan]] through which Dames makes his argument about the *woudloper* tradition of the old [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]: the men who knew the *[[rimboe]]* and its *dieren* (animals) as well as they knew the *kampong* (village). The division is the book's principal unit of the *dertiger jaren* chapters, and the unit visited by the colonel in [[1933]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] Atjeh chapter (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the division is the staff history's principal narrative unit of the southern Westkust outposts and of the structural drawdown that left the southern Westkust without a Dutch garrison at the moment of the Japanese invasion. The dispatch of the eight Tapatoean–Bakongan brigades to Malakka under Captain Supheert — for which the staff history refers the reader to Deel III for the operations of the troops sent to Malakka — is the staff history's principal instance of the structural abandonment of the southern Westkust as a Dutch theatre of independent operation.

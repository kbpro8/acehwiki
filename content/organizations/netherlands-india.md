**Netherlands India** (Dutch: *Nederlandsch-Indië*; also *Nederlands-Indië*; English: *Dutch East Indies*) was the colonial possession of the [[Holland|Kingdom of the Netherlands]] in the Indonesian archipelago, comprising the islands of Java, Sumatra, Borneo, Celebes, the Moluccas, the Lesser Sunda Islands, and the western half of New Guinea, governed by a [[Governor-General of the Netherlands India|Governor-General]] resident at [[Batavia|Batavia]] on Java, assisted by the [[Council of Netherlands India|Council of Netherlands India]] and the various departments of the colonial administration. In the period covered by the source work (1872-1883) the colony was engaged in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], the longest and most costly colonial war in Dutch history.

## Government

The government of Netherlands India was headed by the Governor-General, appointed by the King of the Netherlands on the advice of the Minister of Colonies. The Governor-General was assisted by the Council of Netherlands India (the *Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië*), an advisory body summoned on extraordinary occasions to advise on matters of war, finance, and legislation. The principal departments were those of the Interior, Finance, Justice, War, Marine, Public Works, Education, and Colonies. The colony was divided into residencies, each headed by a resident; the residencies were grouped into gewesten (regions), each headed by a governor.

## The Principal Possessions

The principal possessions of Netherlands India in this period were:

- **Java** — the political and economic centre of the colony, with the chief place Batavia, the residency of Samarang, Soerabaya, and the Vorstenlanden (Soerakarta and Djokjokarta);
- **Sumatra** — including Sumatra's Westkust (with Padang), Sumatra's Oostkust (with Medan/Deli), Tapanoeli (with Siboga), Riouw, and the new gewest of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (with [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]);
- **Borneo** — including the residency of Sintang and the west and south coasts;
- **Celebes** — including the residency of Macassar and the lands of the Buginese and Makassarese;
- **The Moluccas** — including Amboina and the residency of Ternate;
- **The Lesser Sunda Islands** — including Bali, Lombok, and Timor.

## The Atjeh-oorlog and its Cost

The [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]-[[1903]] placed a severe strain on Netherlands India. The colony's army, the [[Indies Army|Indies Army]], was robbed of "a good part of its fit cadres and its best soldiers." The cost of the war was very burdensome for the treasury, costing, as in [[1876]] and [[1877]], monthly about one and a half million guilders. The war also led to the cessation of the contributions from Indië to the Dutch treasury, which had been one of the props of the Dutch state budget in the period of the *[[batig-slot|batig slot]]*.

## Reforms

The period covered by the source work saw important reforms in Netherlands India: the [[ordonnantie]] of [[6 May]] [[6 May]] [[1872]] establishing the *wetboek van strafrecht voor inlanders* (penal code for natives); the introduction of the [[personeele belasting|personeele belasting]] and [[patentrecht|patentrecht]] for Europeans and foreign Orientals in [[1879]]; the great harbour works at [[Tanjung Priok|Tanjung Priok]]; the construction of the state railways on Java; the gradual reduction of the [[cultuurdiensten|cultuurdiensten]] and [[heerendiensten|heerendiensten]]; and the [[Conversiequaestie|conversiequaestie]] over the conversion of communal into individual land ownership.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**Nederlandsch-Indië** — the Netherlands Indies — was the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] colonial state in the Indonesian Archipelago from the assumption of VOC possessions by the Dutch state in [[1799]] to the transfer of sovereignty to the [[Republik Indonesia|Republik Indonesia]] in [[1949]]. In the source's narrative, the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] is the institutional frame within which the [[Atjehoorlog|Atjehoorlog]] of [[1873]]-[[1904]], the post-[[1904]] consolidation, and the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution took place.

### The post-VOC state

The [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] was constituted from the possessions, debts and obligations of the dissolved VOC after [[1799]]. The transition from Company to Government — covered in Chapter III of *Nederlanders over de Zeeën* by [[H. J. de Graaf|De Graaf]] — is the institutional hinge between the VOC era and the colonial-state era. The [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] was the principal institutional vehicle of Dutch-Atjehnese relations from the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824 London Treaty]] to the [[1949]] transfer.

### The Atjeh incorporation

The [[Atjehoorlog|Atjehoorlog]] of [[1873]]-[[1904]] was the principal military operation by which [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] extended its effective authority over the sultanate of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The post-[[1904]] administration of [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] was a colonial-administrative unit of [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] under a civil and military governor. The [[Korte verklaring|korte verklaring]] — by which the submitted chiefs of the *buitengewesten* recognised [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] sovereignty — was eventually signed by 261 chiefs.

### The 1948 Atjeh exemption

The [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]]'s final military operation — the [[Second politionele actie (18 December 1948)|second *politionele actie* of 18 December 1948]] — explicitly exempted [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from occupation "om zijn bijzondere mentaliteit" (on account of its particular mentality). The exemption is the source's final Atjeh-related observation.

### The 1949 transfer

The [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] was dissolved by the [[1949]] transfer of sovereignty to the [[Republik Indonesia|Republik Indonesia]]. The transfer ended the 350-year Dutch maritime enterprise that is the subject of *Nederlanders over de Zeeën*.

### Significance

[[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] is the institutional frame within which the [[Atjehoorlog|Atjehoorlog]], the post-[[1904]] consolidation, and the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution took place. The [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] was a colonial-administrative unit of [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] from [[1904]] to [[1949]].

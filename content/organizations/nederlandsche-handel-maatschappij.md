**The Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij** — the Netherlands Trading Society — was the [[Amsterdam]]-based Dutch trading company that, in [[1930]], acted as the principal European distributor of Atjeh-turpentine from the [[Baleq]] enterprise. The company is named in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] as the importer of the January [[1930]] and April [[1930]] Amsterdam shipments of Atjeh-turpentine — samples *H.M. no. 1553* and *H.M. no. 1607* — and as the addressee of the [[Circulaire of 5 May 1930 to the V.V.V.F.|circular letter of 5 May 1930]]'s reference for further commercial details.

## The distribution of Atjeh-turpentine

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the *verkoop van de terpentijn in Ned.-Indië gaat veel minder vlot* (the sale of turpentine in the Netherlands Indies goes much less smoothly) than the sale of the resin — *een groot gedeelte wordt dan ook jaarlijks door de Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij naar Amsterdam verzonden en in Europa verkocht* (a large part is therefore annually sent by the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij to Amsterdam and sold in Europe). The pamphlet's Table II records the production figures and the proportion sold in the Netherlands Indies:

| Year | Produced (kg) | Sold in N.-I. (kg) | % |
|------|---------------|---------------------|---|
| 1925 | 5,110         | 1,378               | 27% |
| 1926 | 25,876        | 8,458               | 33% |
| 1927 | 51,130        | 21,337              | — |
| 1928 | 61,788        | 31,036              | — |
| 1929 | 104,288       | 42,685              | 41% |

With the exception of [[1928]], *telkenjare tot dusverre slechts de kleinste helft van de geproduceerde terpentijn in het land zelf verkocht is kunnen worden* — *each year up to now only the smallest half of the produced turpentine could be sold in the country itself*. The remainder was shipped to Amsterdam by the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij and sold in Europe.

## The Amsterdam shipments

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that two of the three samples evaluated in the [[1930]] pamphlet were drawn from Amsterdam shipments imported by the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij:

- *H.M. no. 1553* — drawn from a January [[1930]] Amsterdam shipment.
- *H.M. no. 1607* — an original tin from the April [[1930]] shipment per m.s. *Poelau Roebiah*.

The third sample — *H.M. no. 1504* — was received in December [[1929]] directly from the [[Baleq]] enterprise, and was not part of an Amsterdam shipment.

## The Vierde Afdeeling

The [[Circulaire of 5 May 1930 to the V.V.V.F.|Circulaire of 5 May 1930]] records that *voor nadere bijzonderheden daaromtrent gelieve U zich te wenden tot de Vierde Afdeeling van de Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij te Amsterdam* — *for further particulars thereon please apply to the Fourth Department of the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij at Amsterdam*. The *Vierde Afdeeling* — the Fourth Department — was thus the unit of the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij that handled the European distribution of Atjeh-turpentine.

## Significance

The Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the principal European distributor of Atjeh-turpentine from the [[Baleq]] enterprise — the trading company that, from [[1925]] onward, shipped the surplus of Atjeh-turpentine production from the Netherlands Indies to Amsterdam for European sale. The company's role in the European distribution of Atjeh-turpentine illustrates the way in which colonial commodity production was channelled through metropolitan trading companies, and the company's *Vierde Afdeeling* at Amsterdam was the principal point of contact for European buyers of Atjeh-turpentine in [[1930]].

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij** (NHM; Netherlands Trading Society) was the Dutch trading and banking house which, named as the "first trading institution" to interest itself in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], ran the pepper-washing establishment and backed the pepper gardens, supported the [[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|Sabang coal-station concession]] and directed its early exploitation, opened a sub-agency at [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]] on [[20 October]] [[1901]] followed by offices at [[Koeta Radja]] ([[1904]]–[[1905]]), [[Lho Seumawè]] (1903–1907) and [[Langsar]], and conducted the banking business — advances, discounting, exchange, collections — that underpinned the province's exports of pepper, pinang, copra, hides, patchouli and timber.

### Pioneer work

"As first trading institution which interested itself in the Government of Atjeh and Dependencies, may be named the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij": she had supported the Batavia firm [[De Lange & Co]] in obtaining the concession for the coal station at [[Sabang]], and her Factory conducted the direction of the [[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|N.V. "Zeehaven en Kolenstation"]] from [[1897]]. With the transfer of her enterprises on [[Poelò We]] her task in that part of the province seemed ended; but in [[1901]] she renewed her relations with Atjeh by opening on [[20 October]] of that year a sub-agency at Oelèë Lheue. Under Governor [[J. C. van der Wyck]] she administered the pepper-washing establishment erected at Koeta Radja with Government support, and took care that the product there treated was carried to [[Amsterdam]]. Earlier, under Governor Van Heutsz, the opening of an office in Groot-Atjeh by the NHM had been furthered by the Governor.

### Offices

Various circumstances moved the company in [[1904]] to transfer her office from Oelèë Lheue to Koeta Radja, in which year a start was made with the building of a new office; it was taken into use in [[1905]] (photograph: "Kantoor der Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij te Koeta-Radja"). The work having greatly increased, a temporary agency equal to that of Koeta Radja had to be instituted at Lho Seumawè from [[1903]] to [[1907]], during the building of the coastal tramway. The important years were not the most favourable for trade: the import had become independent of the [[Straits Settlements]], but the export still ran chiefly over [[Penang]] and was financed from there. After the tram connection Seulimeum–Padang Tidji was finished in [[1908]] the state materially improved; the revival traced itself in the results of the NHM's Koeta Radja sub-agency, and was cause that also at [[Langsar]] a sub-agency came to be opened.

### War and after

The world war brought first some stagnation in imports and exports; the import passed temporarily into Japanese hands while the export lived through a time of high prices, unscathed by the scarcity of shipping space of [[1917]]–[[1918]]: "De hooge prijzen maakten het gebrek aan scheepsruimte in elk opzicht goed." When in [[1919]] the tram connection from Koeta Radja to [[Medan]] came into being, the Dependencies advanced economically: the export of pepper from the Dependencies, 70,000 picols in 1901, had in [[1920]] risen to 200,000 picols, marketed at f 6 to f 7 per picol; the export of *koelit bakau* (mangrove bark, chiefly to Japan) amounted in 1920 to 5,000,000 kilogram; that of patchouli leaves (chiefly to France) to 1,195,000 kilogram in 1920; of wood not less than 3,400 M³ was exported in that year, beside a great quantity of copra and rubber.

### Bank business

Besides what the NHM did for Atjeh in a general sense by her cooperation in the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij|K.P.M.]], the [[Maatschappij Zeehaven en Kolenstation Sabang|Sabang Maatschappij]] etc., her local work at the time of the book consisted principally in "bankzaken, beleeningen, voorschotten op producten en onder hypothecaire zekerheid, disconteeringen, wisseloperatiën, giroverkeer, geldhandel, incassi, telegrafische en andere uitbetalingen" — banking business, lendings, advances on products and under mortgage security, discountings, exchange operations, giro traffic, money trade, collections, telegraphic and other payments — "welke werkzaamheden den economischen bloei van het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden mede in niet geringe mate ten goede komen". She had opened at once upon her coming a modestly paying exchange- and bank business that benefited both trade and industry. With the [[Atjehsche Handelmaatschappij]] (a Padangsche House), the [[Handelsmaatschappij Deli-Atjeh|Deli-Atjeh]] and the agency of the [[Javasche Bank te Koetaradja|Javasche Bank]] she completed the banking landscape of the province.

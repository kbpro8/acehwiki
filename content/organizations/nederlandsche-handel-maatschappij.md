**The Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij** — the Netherlands Trading Society — was the [[Amsterdam]]-based Dutch trading company that, in [[1930]], acted as the principal European distributor of Atjeh-turpentine from the [[Baleq]] enterprise. The company is named in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] as the importer of the January [[1930]] and April [[1930]] Amsterdam shipments of Atjeh-turpentine — samples *H.M. no. 1553* and *H.M. no. 1607* — and as the addressee of the [[Circulaire of 5 May 1930 to the V.V.V.F.|circular letter of 5 May 1930]]'s reference for further commercial details.

## The distribution of Atjeh-turpentine

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the *verkoop van de terpentijn in Ned.-Indië gaat veel minder vlot* (the sale of turpentine in the Netherlands Indies goes much less smoothly) than the sale of the resin — *een groot gedeelte wordt dan ook jaarlijks door de Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij naar Amsterdam verzonden en in Europa verkocht* (a large part is therefore annually sent by the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij to Amsterdam and sold in Europe). The pamphlet's Table II records the production figures and the proportion sold in the Netherlands Indies:

| Year | Produced (kg) | Sold in N.-I. (kg) | % |
|------|---------------|---------------------|---|
| 1925 | 5,110         | 1,378               | 27% |
| 1926 | 25,876        | 8,458               | 33% |
| 1927 | 51,130        | 21,337              | — |
| 1928 | 61,788        | 31,036              | — |
| 1929 | 104,288       | 42,685              | 41% |

With the exception of [[1928]], *telkenjare tot dusverre slechts de kleinste helft van de geproduceerde terpentijn in het land zelf verkocht is kunnen worden* — *each year up to now only the smallest half of the produced turpentine could be sold in the country itself*. The remainder was shipped to Amsterdam by the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij and sold in Europe.

## The Amsterdam shipments

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that two of the three samples evaluated in the [[1930]] pamphlet were drawn from Amsterdam shipments imported by the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij:

- *H.M. no. 1553* — drawn from a January [[1930]] Amsterdam shipment.
- *H.M. no. 1607* — an original tin from the April [[1930]] shipment per m.s. *Poelau Roebiah*.

The third sample — *H.M. no. 1504* — was received in December [[1929]] directly from the [[Baleq]] enterprise, and was not part of an Amsterdam shipment.

## The Vierde Afdeeling

The [[Circulaire of 5 May 1930 to the V.V.V.F.|Circulaire of 5 May 1930]] records that *voor nadere bijzonderheden daaromtrent gelieve U zich te wenden tot de Vierde Afdeeling van de Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij te Amsterdam* — *for further particulars thereon please apply to the Fourth Department of the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij at Amsterdam*. The *Vierde Afdeeling* — the Fourth Department — was thus the unit of the Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij that handled the European distribution of Atjeh-turpentine.

## Significance

The Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the principal European distributor of Atjeh-turpentine from the [[Baleq]] enterprise — the trading company that, from [[1925]] onward, shipped the surplus of Atjeh-turpentine production from the Netherlands Indies to Amsterdam for European sale. The company's role in the European distribution of Atjeh-turpentine illustrates the way in which colonial commodity production was channelled through metropolitan trading companies, and the company's *Vierde Afdeeling* at Amsterdam was the principal point of contact for European buyers of Atjeh-turpentine in [[1930]].

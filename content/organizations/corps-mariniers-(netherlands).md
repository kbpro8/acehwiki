**Corps Mariniers (Netherlands)** was the Dutch naval infantry unit established in the mid‑17th century that served in overseas colonies, notably in the **Berbice slave revolt** of [[1763]] and later in the **Aceh War** (1873–1904). The corps’ early expeditionary actions, recorded in the 1665–1945 history of the Dutch Marine Corps, illustrate its role in suppressing rebellions and protecting Dutch trade interests in the Caribbean and the East Indies.

## Origins and Early Structure
The Corps Mariniers originated in [[1665]] as a specialized force of sailors trained for amphibious operations. By the mid‑18th century it comprised a regiment of marines under a colonel, supported by sergeants, corporals, and surgeons. The 1765 resolution promoted the corps to a permanent regiment, authorised to conduct sea‑borne expeditions and to serve aboard warships.

## Service in the Berbice Slave Revolt (1763–1765)
During the **Berbice slave revolt** of [[1763]], the corps was deployed under **Colonel De Salve**. On the night of [[22]] [[March]] [[1763]] the marines attacked a rebel encampment on the island of Oosterlee, employing a **Fochoorn‑mortier** (mortar) to create confusion before surrounding the rebels. The operation resulted in the capture of **Accabré** and his followers (81 rebels), the execution of 53 rebels, and the burning of a camp of 65 huts. Subsequent actions in April and May 1763 saw further captures, including the notorious rebel leader **Atta**, and the execution of additional insurgents.

## Later Deployments in the East Indies
After the Caribbean campaigns, the Corps Mariniers was stationed at various Dutch ports, including **Bergen op Zoom**, **Terneuzen**, and **Sluis**, before being dispatched to the East Indies. In the late 19th century the corps took part in the **Aceh War**, providing naval infantry for amphibious assaults on Acehnese fortifications and supporting the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army (KNIL). Its experience from earlier colonial conflicts, such as the Berbice expedition, informed its tactics in Aceh.

## Significance
The Corps Mariniers exemplifies the Dutch Republic’s reliance on elite marine forces to project power across its global empire. Its early Caribbean actions demonstrated the corps’ capacity for rapid, coordinated assaults, while its later involvement in the Aceh War cemented its reputation as a key instrument of Dutch colonial warfare.

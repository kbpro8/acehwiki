**The Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting** ("Pastoor Verbraak Foundation") was a Dutch colonial Catholic welfare foundation named in honour of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]], the *soldatenpastoor* of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], to which the existing military homes (*militaire tehuizen*) of the [[Netherlands East Indies|Netherlands East Indies]] were transferred in the early [[1920s]] — and which were thereafter "increased and improved" (*vermeerderd en verbeterd*) under the foundation's administration. The foundation is documented in the [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume of 1934]] in the chapter on the institutional achievements of Mgr. [[A. P. F. van Velsen|A. P. F. van Velsen]]'s vicariate, alongside the foundation of the Central Mission Bureau, the annual Indian Mission Almanac, and the preservation of the Catholic daily *De Koerier*.

## Foundation and transfer of the military homes

The [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume]] records the foundation in the chapter on the institutional achievements of the vicariate of Mgr. [[A. P. F. van Velsen|A. P. F. van Velsen]] (vicar [[1924]]–[[1933]]):

> *"de bestaande militaire tehuizen werden overgedragen aan de in het leven geroepen Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting en daarna vermeerderd en verbeterd."*

The transfer of the existing military homes to the newly-created Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting — and the subsequent expansion and improvement of those homes under the foundation's administration — is the documentary fact that fixes the foundation in the institutional record of the colonial Catholic church. The naming of the foundation after [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Verbraak]] — who had died at [[Magelang|Magelang]] in [[1918]] and whose bronze statue had been unveiled at [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]] on [[27 January]] [[1922]] — is the document of the institutional memory of the *soldatenpastoor* in the post-war colonial church, and of the formalisation of that memory in a welfare foundation.

## Function

The Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting's function was the administration and expansion of the military homes — the welfare institutions that provided lodging, refreshment, and moral support to the Catholic soldiers of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands-Indian Army]]. The military homes were the institutional continuation, in peacetime, of the wartime chaplaincy that [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Verbraak]] had exercised on the Atjeh war-footing: the same care for the "*Jan Fuselier*" — the ordinary Dutch soldier — that had been Verbraak's personal ministry was now, through the foundation named after him, institutionalised across the colonial army.

## Significance

The Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history on two counts. First, it is the principal institutional memorial of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] — the foundation named after him, and the formal vehicle by which his ministry to the colonial soldier was perpetuated in the post-war colonial army. Second, its foundation in the early [[1920s]] — alongside the bronze statue at [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]] of [[27 January]] [[1922]] — is the documentary witness of the institutional consolidation of the memory of the Atjeh chaplaincy in the post-war colonial church, and of the formalisation of that memory in a welfare foundation that administered and expanded the military homes of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|K.N.I.L.]] The foundation thus bridges the wartime chaplaincy of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the peacetime military welfare of the late colonial army.

**The Departement van Binnenlandsch Bestuur** ("Department of the Interior" / "Department of Internal Administration") was the Dutch colonial department responsible for the general internal administration of the Netherlands East Indies, including the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] and [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914*, the Department is the principal colonial department under whose authority the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] order in Atjeh was consolidated — including the [[Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen]], the [[Adviseur voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Adviseur voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen]], and the [[Encyclopaedisch Bureau|Encyclopaedisch Bureau]] itself.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Departement van Binnenlandsch Bestuur

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's volume opens with the chapter "Het Departement van Binnenlandsch Bestuur" — the first of the colonial departments whose werkkring (sphere of activity) in the Buitenbezittingen the Bureau surveys. The Bureau's account identifies the [[1908]] creation of the [[Adviseur voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Adviseur voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen]] — simultaneously the Chef of the [[Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Afdeeling voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen]] attached to the Department — as the principal institutional innovation of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade for the administration of the Buitenbezittingen.

The Department's oversight of the Buitenbezittingen, the Bureau notes, was extended in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade both "in breadth and in depth" — that is, both in the geographic scope of direct administration and in the depth of administrative intervention. The expansion is documented in the Bureau's table of bestuursvestigingen (administrative establishments) and Europeesche bestuursambtenaren (European administrative officials): from 204 vestigingen and 250 officials in [[1885]], to 529 vestigingen and 401 officials in [[1915]]. The Department is also the colonial authority under whose oversight the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] were centralised in [[1910]]–[[1911]], the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] regime was extended from [[1899]] onward, and the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] was promulgated in [[1914]].

The Department's oversight of the [[Gewapende Politie|Gewapende Politie (armed police)]] is likewise noted by the Bureau: "the technical leadership, under the orders of the Director of Binnenlandsch-Bestuur, is entrusted to a chef, simultaneously head of the Afdeeling Gewapende Politie of the Departement van Binnenlandsch Bestuur." The Department is thus the colonial authority under whose oversight the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] administrative order in Atjeh was consolidated across all its principal dimensions: administrative, financial, police, and legal.

## Significance

The [[Departement van Binnenlandsch Bestuur|Departement van Binnenlandsch Bestuur]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal colonial department under whose authority the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] order in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] was consolidated. The [[1908]] creation of the [[Adviseur voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Adviseur]] and the [[Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen|Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen]] within the Department is the principal institutional innovation of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade for the administration of the Buitenbezittingen; the [[1910]]–[[1911]] centralisation of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] under the [[Bureau van Financieele aangelegenheden|Bureau van Financieele aangelegenheden]] is the principal financial innovation; and the [[1914]] [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] is the principal legal consolidation.

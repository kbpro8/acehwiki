**Sjeih Abdoel bin Djnali** was a *Sjeih* (Arabic *shaykh*) and *Teungku* of the *kampong* of [[Tiro]] in the [[Pedir]] region — the predecessor of the more famous [[Teungku di Tiro Mohammed Amir]] as the principal religious authority of that village — and the teacher and patron of the man who would later become, in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album's]] phrase, *een groot man in die richting* — *a great man in that line* — the [[Teungku di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] of the Dutch war period.

## The kampong of Tiro

The *kampong* of [[Tiro]] in [[Pedir]] was, by the late nineteenth century, the principal seat of Atjehnese religious learning and the burial-place of many distinguished *ulama*s. Its reputation rested on the conjunction of teaching and burial: *ulama*s trained at Tiro returned there in their declining years, so that the village accumulated both living scholars and the tombs of dead ones, and the resulting sanctity made Tiro a kind of *vrijplaats* — asylum — for those involved in internal feuds. Sjeih Abdoel bin Djnali was, in the succession of Tiro scholars, the immediate predecessor of [[Teungku di Tiro Mohammed Amir]], whose own successor — [[Teungku di Tiro|Teungku di Tiro (Cheh Saman)]] — would become the commander-in-chief of the Atjehnese holy war against the Dutch.

## The succession

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records the succession in a single clause: *Hij was vroeger de rechterhand van den beroemden Teungkoe di Tiro Mohammed Amir, dien hij opvolgde* — "He [the later Teungku di Tiro] was formerly the right-hand man of the famous Teungku di Tiro Mohammed Amir, whom he succeeded." The "right-hand man" relationship is the standard Atjehnese pattern of succession for the *ulama* office: the senior pupil becomes, on the teacher's death, the new *Teungku*, and the office acquires its continuity from the chain of teacher-pupil relationships rather than from heredity. Sjeih Abdoel bin Djnali's place in this chain — as the teacher of [[Teungku di Tiro Mohammed Amir]] — is the upstream link that connects the early-nineteenth-century Tiro scholarly tradition to the late-nineteenth-century resistance.

## Significance

Sjeih Abdoel bin Djnali's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is principally genealogical: he is the named predecessor whose teaching transmission connects the [[Tiro]] scholarly tradition to the resistance leadership of the Dutch war period. His appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] is brief, but it preserves the chain of teacher-pupil relationships that gave the Tiro *ulama* office its continuity and its authority.

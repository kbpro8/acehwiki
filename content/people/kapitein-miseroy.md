**Kapitein Miseroy** was a Dutch captain of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]] for whom a gravestone was erected at [[Anagaloeng]] during the period when the Dutch were masters of that place, and upon which, after their withdrawal behind the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]* in [[1884]], the *oelama* [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]] died.

## The gravestone at Anagaloeng

Hooijer recorded that the *benteng* the Dutch had held at [[Anagaloeng]] was abandoned in [[1884]], when the Achehnese — *onverzoenlijk en slechts voor macht bukkende*, irreconcilable and bowing only to power — took up arms again and compelled the Dutch to withdraw behind the concentrated line. The man who had then been their spiritual leader in the holy war, *Oelama* [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]], breathed his last at Anagaloeng *liggende op den grafsteen, die er, ten tijde, dat wij er nog heer en meester waren, voor den Kapitein Miseroy was opgericht* — lying on the gravestone that had been erected there for Captain Miseroy while the Dutch were still lords and masters of the place.

## Significance

The image of the Achehnese *oelama* dying upon a Dutch officer's tombstone in the fortification the Dutch had abandoned was used by Hooijer as an emblem of the reversals of the *concentratie* period, in which the same ground changed hands repeatedly between [[1879]] and [[1896]].

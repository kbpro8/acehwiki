**A. J. E. Diemont** was a major-general of the Indies army who commanded the Dutch forces in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from [[6 November]] [[1876]] until his departure for Batavia on grounds of illness on [[30 June]] [[1877]]. His short command saw the [[Expedition against Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh (November-December 1876)|expedition against Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh]] of November-December [[1876]], the closing of the coast from [[Kwala Gighen|Kwala Gighen]] to [[Kwala Loë|Kwala Loë]] in January [[1877]], and the outset of the preparations for [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]]'s expedition to [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] of August [[1877]].

## Appointment

Diemont succeeded [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|Major-general Wiggers van Kerchem]], whose resignation had been caused by a disagreement with the government over the policy to be followed toward the [[Onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]]. Wiggers van Kerchem had been unwilling to detach troops from Atjeh to act against [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh|Tandjong Semantoh]]; Diemont "immediately took up the expedition against Simpang Olim with vigour."

## The Expedition against Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh

On [[7 November]] [[1876]] Diemont sent the necessary troops to combine with the navy, which had already taken an active part in the operations, to conquer the two hostile states. The troops, advancing over all obstacles and receiving "only little real service from the native auxiliary troops," reached on [[14 November]] the chief place of [[Tandjong Semantoh|Tandjong Semantoh]], protected by the hill-benting regarded as impregnable. While the gedei (chief place) was being stormed by our men, Lieutenant-adjudant [[A. G. Popelier|A. G. Popelier]] was ordered to approach the benting with a section. Climbing the south-eastern slope of the hill with only six Ambonese soldiers, he and his men entered the bastion unobserved, "the enemy uttering a cry of fright, recoiling in astonishment and throwing away his fuse. The impregnable benting of [[Toekoe Paja|Toekoe Paja]] was thus, without loss on our side, in our hands. Undeniably a brilliant feat of arms!" The radja of Simpang Olim thereupon offered his submission.

Diemont imposed the conditions that Dutch troops should be admitted into the gedei of Simpang Olim, that a post should be established there or in the neighbourhood, and that the radja should present himself personally at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] to offer his submission. The radja's continued evasions led to the issue of an ultimatum on [[10 December]] [[1876]], and when on [[12 December]] the chiefs reported that the radja had fled to the interior, a proclamation declared him deprived of his authority and excluded for ever, together with his lineage, from the government of Simpang Olim. The son of [[Toekoe Moeda Angkasa|Toekoe Moeda Angkasa]] — who had been treacherously murdered on [[11 December]] [[1876]] by the radja's hand — was appointed radja of [[Merbau|Merbau]] and Tandjong Semantho; the chiefs chose a new radja of Simpang Olim, who on [[5 February]] [[1877]] swore the conditions imposed by the government and was given the Dutch flag.

## The Closure of the Coast, January 1877

In January [[1877]] the troops resumed operations for the completion of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]]'s program: the occupation of the coast from [[Kwala Gighen|Kwala Gighen]] to [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]], by which the enemy still maintained communication with the sea. On [[25 January]] the operations began under the commander-in-chief's personal direction with a simultaneous landing at [[Kwala Loë|Kwala Loë]] and [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]]. The latter place was not deemed suitable for a settlement; the troops returned along the beach to Ladoeng and thence over the mountains to Lambaroe Angan to join the other column, which had landed at Kwala Loë under [[K. van der Heyden|Colonel Van der Heyden]]. After the junction, the strong [[Lamnga|Lamnga]] was occupied, abandoned by the enemy. On [[30 January]] the [[missigit Lambada|missigit Lambada]], from which the troops had been repulsed in October, was stormed. "With this the objective was in essence attained, and although no post was set up at Kroëng Raja, the occupation of the coast from Kwala Gighen to Kwala Loë had in fact cut the enemy off from the sea."

## Departure

Diemont's command ended with his departure for Batavia on [[30 June]] [[1877]] on grounds of illness. [[K. van der Heyden|Colonel Van der Heyden]] was charged provisionally with the civil and military government of Atjeh, and under his leadership the [[Expedition to Samalanga (August 1877)|expedition to Samalanga]] was carried out in August. "The submission of Simpang Olim made a great impression everywhere and confirmed our authority also in the other Onderhoorigheden."

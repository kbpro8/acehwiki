**Abu Bakr** (also written, by an obvious clerical error, **Abu Bahr**) was the first of the four companions (Rāshidūn caliphs) of the Prophet [[Mohammad (the Prophet)|Mohammad]], and is named — alongside **Omar**, **Uthman**, and **Ali** — in the preamble of the sultans' letters patent (*sarakata*) of Acheh. The invocation reads: "with the help of Allah the omniscient King, and with the blessing of the Prophet, the Lord of Men, and of his four companions, which be Abu Bahr, Omar, Uthman and Ali (may they be pleasing unto Allah)".

In the formal diplomatic language of the Achehnese sultanate, as exemplified in the [[Letters patent with the ninefold seal to Panglima Meuseugit Raya|letters patent granted by Sultan Alaedin Muhamad Daud Shah Juhan]] to [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] on 4 Rabi' al-Awwal 1307 H ([[1889]]), the blessing of Allah, of the Prophet, of the four companions, of the saint [[Abdul-Qadir Jilani]] ("the glorious Pole of the world and the brilliant framework of the skies, the immutable knower, the true Beloved"), of all the devout *walis* of God from East to West, and of the deceased sultans of blessed memory is invoked upon the deed. The four companions appear in this list immediately after the Prophet himself, marking their place in the Achehnese hierarchy of religious intercession.

The "Abu Bahr" of the Achehnese text is, as [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]]'s translation makes plain, simply an error for Abu Bakr. Such errors are not uncommon in Achehnese documents composed in "a mixture of Malay and bad Arabic", and the present example testifies to the distance between the learned Arabic models on which the sarakatas were based and the actual competence of the scribes who drafted them in the late nineteenth century.

The veneration of Abu Bakr and the other three caliphs is, of course, common to all Sunni Mohammedans, and the Achehnese — strict Shafi'ites in ritual — share this universal veneration. Their appearance by name in the sultans' edicts is one of the formal ways in which the Achehnese sultanate signalled its adherence to the universal Mohammedan community and to the historical succession of the caliphate.

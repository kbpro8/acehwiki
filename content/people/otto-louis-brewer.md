**Otto Louis Brewer** "heer van Vrijenes" ([[26 May]] [[1875]], [[Meester Cornelis|Meester Cornelis]] (Java) – after [[1933]]) was a Dutch colonial artillery officer of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger (K.N.I.L.)]] who rose to titular major-general (*generaal majoor tit.*) of artillery, was decorated a Knight 4th class of the [[Militaire Willemsorde|Militaire Willemsorde]] — the highest Dutch military valour decoration — and whose career spanned postings at [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]], [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], and the wider colonial artillery establishment. He is documented in the [[Van Son genealogy|Van Son genealogy]] as the husband of two sisters of the Van Son family — first [[Leonie van Son|Leonie van Son]] (marriage [[1899]]), and after her death at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in [[1904]], her sister [[Adeline van Son|Adeline van Son]] (marriage [[1908]]) — and as the father of [[Yvonne Caroline Brewer|Yvonne Caroline Brewer]], born at [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] on [[1 Augustus]] [[1904]].

## Family and parentage

Brewer was the son of **Adriaan Anton Wilhelm Brewer** — Consul of Spain at [[Batavia|Batavia]] — and **Wilhelmina Sarah Christina Neuwenhuyzen**. He bore the seigneurial title *heer van Vrijenes* — a mark of the family's standing in the colonial patriciate of late-nineteenth-century Batavia.

## First marriage to Leonie van Son (1899) and the Atjeh years

Brewer married at [[Salatiga|Salatiga]] (Java) on [[26 June]] [[1899]] **[[Leonie van Son|Leonie van Son]]** ([[1877]], [[Palembang|Palembang]] – [[17 November]] [[1904]], [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] (Atjeh, Sumatra)), daughter of [[Livinus Diederik Mattheus van Son|L. D. M. van Son]], retired captain of infantry, K.N.I.L., and Carolina Weynschenk. The Van Son genealogy explicitly records the place of Leonie's death as "*Koeta Radja (Atjeh, Sumatra)*" — fixing Brewer, at the time of his wife's death in November [[1904]], in the Atjeh command.

The genealogy further records that the couple's second child, **[[Yvonne Caroline Brewer|Yvonne Caroline Brewer]]**, was born at [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] (*groot Atjeh*) on [[1 Augustus]] [[1904]] — i.e., barely three and a half months before her mother's death at Koeta Radja. The combined data — a daughter born at Indrapoera in August [[1904]], a wife dead at Koeta Radja in November [[1904]] — places the Brewer household in the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] theatre of the colonial occupation in the middle of the first decade of the twentieth century, in the immediate aftermath of the [[Surrender of the Atjeh pretender-sultan (1903)|1903 surrender]] of the pretender-sultan and the formal close of the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]].

The first child of the marriage, **Maximilaan Diederik Brewer**, was born at [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]] on [[18 February]] [[1901]], before the family's posting to Atjeh.

## Second marriage to Adeline van Son (1908)

After Leonie's death, Brewer married, at ['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] on [[27 October]] [[1908]], her younger sister **[[Adeline van Son|Adeline van Son]]** (born [[Palembang|Palembang]], [[10 October]] [[1878]]) — a marriage within the prohibited degrees that required, and obtained, the customary dispensation. The Van Son genealogy records no children of this second marriage.

## Career and honours

The genealogy records Brewer's final rank as *gepens. generaal majoor tit. der Artillerie, Kon. Ned. Indisch Leger* — "pensioned titular major-general of artillery, Royal Netherlands-Indian Army" — and his principal decoration as *ridder 4e klasse Militaire Willemsorde* — "Knight 4th class of the Military Willems Order." The Military Willemsorde 4th class is awarded for conspicuous valour in the field; the genealogy does not record the specific action for which it was conferred, but the decoration, the Atjeh years, and the artillery arm together make it virtually certain that the award was made for an action in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — possibly during the closing campaigns of [[1903]]–[[1904]] that culminated the conflict.

The genealogy does not record the date of Brewer's retirement from the K.N.I.L. or the date of his death.

## Significance

Brewer's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history lies in three particulars. First, his Atjeh posting of [[1904]] — fixed by the birth of his daughter Yvonne at Indrapoera and the death of his wife Leonie at Koeta Radja — places a serving K.N.I.L. artillery officer in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in the very year that marks the transition from military to civil administration of the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. Second, his receipt of the 4th class of the [[Militaire Willemsorde|Militaire Willemsorde]] documents, by the genealogical route, the award of the highest Dutch military valour decoration for service in the Atjeh theatre. Third, his double marriage into the Van Son family — itself an Atjeh-expedition family through his father-in-law [[Livinus Diederik Mattheus van Son|L. D. M. van Son]] — represents the marriage-alliances by which the colonial officer class of late-nineteenth and early-twentieth-century Atjeh reproduced itself as a hereditary military caste.

**Radja Ibrahim** was, after the old writers cited by [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] (and by kolonel [[Majoor Gerlach|Gerlach]], who in his [[1878]] book on the Atjehers followed "different old historians"), the eldest son of the aged stadhouder of Atjeh, appointed by his sovereign, the Sultan of [[Pedir]], as his father's successor; he became that father's executioner, kept the old man shut up in a pit (*koel*) until his death, and then — overthrowing his lawful lord and benefactor as well — subdued everything by the force of arms, and so made Atjeh independent and one of the mightiest realms of Sumatra. "Zoo werden Atjeh's zelfstandigheid en grootheid in [[1521]] slechts op misdaad gegrondvest" ("Thus were Atjeh's independence and greatness in 1521 only founded on crime"), Verstege quotes, in his sketch of the national character of the Atjehnese — a sketch formed, like the judgments of Admiral [[Augustin de Beaulieu|de Beaulieu]] and the contrôleur [[Kroesen (controleur at Oleh-leh)|Kroesen]], in order to test the concentration policy against the known history and temper of the people (cf. [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)]]).

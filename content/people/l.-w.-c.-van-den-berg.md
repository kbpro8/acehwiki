**L. W. C. van den Berg** — *Mr. L. W. C. van den Berg* — was a Dutch colonial official and scholar of Islam, *ambtenaar voor de beoefening van inlandsche talen, tevens adviseur voor oostersche talen en mohammedaansch recht* (official for the practice of native languages, also advisor for Oriental languages and Mohammedan law) at [[Batavia|Batavia], who served as the principal Dutch metropolitan authority on Islamic law in the early 1880s and whose 1882–1883 controversy with [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] over the Arabic law-book *Minhādj at-tālibin* — and the consequent 1884 criticism of Snouck Hurgronje's *Beginselen van het Mohammedaansche Recht* — was the principal scholarly controversy of Snouck Hurgronje's early career and the immediate occasion of Snouck Hurgronje's reputation as the "*Schöpfer der historischen Betrachtung des Figh*" (creator of the historical consideration of the fiqh).

## The Office at Batavia

Van den Berg held the office of *ambtenaar voor de beoefening van inlandsche talen, tevens adviseur voor oostersche talen en mohammedaansch recht* at Batavia — that is, the principal Dutch colonial office for the scholarly study of Islamic law in the Netherlands Indies. His principal publications were the edition of the Arabic law-book *Minhādj at-tālibin* (the principal reference of Shafi'i jurisprudence) and the manual *De Beginselen van het Mohammedaansche Recht* ("The Principles of Mohammedan Law"), both of which Snouck Hurgronje would criticise severely.

## The 1883 Controversy over the Minhādj

Van den Berg's edition of the *Minhādj at-tālibin* was reviewed by Snouck Hurgronje in *De Indische Gids* of [[April 1883|April]] [[1883]]. The edition was, in Snouck Hurgronje's judgement, "*overbodig voor zoover betreft den arabischen tekst*" (superfluous insofar as it concerned the Arabic text), and "*verkeerd aangelegd*" (wrongly laid out) as regarded the translation. Van den Berg reacted with personal attacks: "*Hij zou met gelatenheid de afkeuring weten te dragen van een jeugdigen beoefenaar van het mohammedaansch recht, als de heer Snouck Hurgronje was*" (He would with equanimity know how to bear the disapproval of a youthful practitioner of Mohammedan law, as the heer Snouck Hurgronje was). "*Wat zelfgenoegzame toon! Wat hooge ingenomenheid met eigen resultaten, die echter door géén geleerde van naam nog als juist waren erkend!*" (What self-sufficient tone! What high satisfaction with own results, which however by no scholar of name had yet been recognised as correct!)

## The 1884 Criticism of the Beginselen

To that answer and to the shortly thereafter appeared third edition of Van den Berg's *De Beginselen van het Mohammedaansche Recht* — "*een zonder oordeel uit secondaire bronnen gecompileerd schoolboek*" (a schoolbook compiled without judgement from secondary sources), according to Snouck — the "youthful practitioner" replied to the "quasi-practitioner" of that science, in a severe but businesslike critique: "*Mr. L. W. C. van den Berg's beoefening van het Mohammedaansche Recht*" in *De Indische Gids* of [[1884]]; a critique that was actually much more than a critique — a 152-page-long treatise with many new contributions to the knowledge of Islam.

## The Der Kinderen Connection

Van den Berg, together with [[Der Kinderen|Der Kinderen]], was later the object of fierce criticism in Snouck Hurgronje's *[[Verslag omtrent de religieus-politieke toestanden in Atjeh|Verslag omtrent de religieus-politieke toestanden in Atjeh]]* of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]] — on their regulation of the judiciary in Atjèh and on what they had said and written about Atjèh. When the *Verslag* was published, in modified form, as *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjèhers]]* (Vol. I, 1893; Vol. II, 1894), the criticism of Van den Berg and Der Kinderen — though "*veel zachter dan wat de gouverneur-generaal en de Raad van Indië bedoeld hadden te publiceeren*" (much milder than what the governor-general and the Council of Indië had meant to publish) — provoked both gentlemen to protest at audience with the minister [[Van Dedem|Van Dedem]] against the publication by the government of a work in which such unsalted criticism of their *praestaties* occurred.

## Significance

Van den Berg is the principal Dutch metropolitan authority on Islamic law in the early 1880s, and the principal scholarly adversary of Snouck Hurgronje's early career. The 1883–1884 controversy over the *Minhādj* and the *Beginselen* established Snouck Hurgronje's reputation as the "*Schöpfer der historischen Betrachtung des Figh*" (in the phrase of [[Goldziher|Dr. Goldziher]] of Budapest), and was the immediate occasion of his pre-eminence in Dutch Islamic studies. The later criticism of Van den Berg in the *Verslag* of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]] and in *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjèhers]]* extended the controversy to Van den Berg's work on Atjèh, and was the occasion of the [[Bekendmaking Nederburgh|Bekendmaking Nederburgh]] — the government declaration prefixed to the second volume of *De Atjèhers* as satisfaction for the criticised gentlemen.

**N. Breukels** was a Dutch newspaper administrator who spent thirty-two years in North Sumatra and who, from 1920 until the Japanese invasion, was employed at the [[Atjeh-drukkerij]] (the Atjeh Printing Office) in [[Kotaradja]]. In [[1946]] he was appointed administrator (*administrateur*) of the N.V. De Deli Courant. He was repatriated to the Netherlands on 6 November [[1950]].

## Career in Atjeh

Breukels' long tenure at the [[Atjeh-drukkerij]] in [[Kotaradja]] — from 1920 until the Japanese invasion of [[1942]] — spanned over two decades of the late colonial period in Atjeh. The [[Atjeh-drukkerij]] was the principal printing establishment in the Atjeh capital, serving both the colonial administration and the European and indigenous business community. Breukels' presence there placed him at the heart of the print-culture infrastructure of colonial Atjeh.

## Post-war career

After the Japanese capitulation, Breukels did not return to Atjeh. Instead, in [[1946]], he was appointed administrator of the N.V. De Deli Courant, the principal Dutch-language newspaper of the East Coast of Sumatra, based in Medan. He was described as "een populaire figuur van de oude garde" ("a popular figure of the old guard").

He was succeeded as acting administrator by B. A. Plaat on 4 November [[1950]], two days before his own repatriation.

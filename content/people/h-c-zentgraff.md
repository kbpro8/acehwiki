**H.C. Zentgraff** — signing himself in the book's preface simply *DE SCHRIJVER* ("the author") — was the ex-military officer who in [[1938]] published ***Atjeh*** at Drukkerij "De Unie" in [[Batavia]], the source-book on which this whole encyclopaedia of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] substantially rests. He had himself served in Atjeh — *"ik stel er eene eer in, tot dat Leger te hebben behoord, óók in Atjeh, en het zit mij diep in het hart"* — and from that vantage wrote with the explicit purpose of stepping outside Dutch self-complacency and letting the Atjeher, for once, speak.

## Method and sources

In his *Inleiding* Zentgraff sets out his method. He had been permitted by the [[Legerbestuur]] — the Army Administration — to consult the military archives, where he found *zeer belangrijk materiaal* (very important material). He further gathered information from *velen die in den krijg eene rol van beteekenis speelden* (many who played a role of consequence in the war), and in Atjeh from *verschillende personen, ook Atjehers* (various persons, including Acehnese). The aim was to give the sketches *historischen grond* (historical grounding) and to do justice to both parties: *"A tout seigneur tout honneur!"* His book contains no more romance than the events themselves afford; all *opsmuk* (embellishment), every *offer aan de fantasie* (tribute to fantasy) would only damage the *geweldige realiteit* (tremendous reality).

## The Sumatraantjes articles

Zentgraff also wrote for the Dutch-Indies press under the collective title *Sumatraantjes*. He cites, in his chapter on the [[Lhong punitive expedition (1897)|Lhong tuchtingsexpeditie]] of [[1897]], his own article on [[Lhong]]: *"En als de grijsgroene mannen hier rondgaan, is dit altijd met eene bijzondere voorzichtigheid welke geen detail over het hoofd ziet…"* — "And when the grey-green men go about here, it is always with a special caution that overlooks no detail…" The same articles formed the medium through which he carried his Atjeh memories to a colonial-reading public.

## Significance

Zentgraff's *Atjeh* is a singular document of colonial-war literature: written by an officer who honoured his own army but refused to write a *zoet boek* (sweet book) in which every Dutchman was a hero and every Atjeher a traitor. He cited the Acehnese poet [[Dokarim]] at length, set the Dutch edition of [[Tengkoe Koetakarang]]'s *[[Tadkirat arrakidin]]* before his readers as a verdict on Dutch bad faith toward [[Van der Heyden|generaal éénoog]], and reproduced the letters of [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to his wife. The work is the foundation-stone of this wiki.

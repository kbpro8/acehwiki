**Teukoe Nja' Arif** was an Atjèhnese *[[Uleebalang|oelèëbalang]]* who, as chairman of the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]] under the Japanese occupation, wrote in favour of the *[[Indonesia Merdéka]]* independence idea, and who — after the proclamation of the Republic in [[1945]] — became the first head of Atjeh's republican administration, only to be arrested by the [[Poesa|Poesa]] at the height of the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution]] and to die in an internment camp in [[April]] [[1946]]. Henri J. H. Alers's *Om een rode of groene Merdeka* treats his rise and fall as the pivotal episode by which power in Atjeh passed decisively from the *oelèëbalang* class to the Ulama-dominated [[Poesa|Poesa]].

## Wartime writings on Indonesia Merdéka

As chairman of the [[Atjèh Council|Atjèh Council]], Teukoe Nja' Arif was among the Atjèhnese figures who publicly welcomed Prime Minister [[Koiso|Koiso]]'s [[7 September]] [[1944]] promise of eventual independence. He wrote that the desire for freedom had for many years lain buried under Dutch imperialism, and warned his countrymen that independence would not be won "when the English and Dutch return" — a position that placed him, alongside the more equivocal [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]], among the leading Atjèhnese voices of the wartime independence campaign.

## Support for the national Merdeka movement and contact with Sukarno

According to Alers, Teukoe Nja' Arif was, together with the prominent *oelèëbalang* [[Teuku Sabi|Teuku Sabi]], among those who lent early support to the national Merdeka campaign, seeing in it a possible counterweight to the growing influence of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] within Atjeh. For a time contact between Atjeh and the nationalist movement on [[Java|Java]] was limited to a telegram exchange between [[Soekarno|Sukarno]] and [[Teuku Sabi|Teuku Sabi]], a returning exiled *oelèëbalang*, pledging that "Atjèhnese blood" would be forged into "a fortress of steel" against any enemy from West or North. [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]], by contrast, remained — in Alers's phrase — characteristically "on the sidelines."

## Head of Atjeh's republican administration

After the proclamation of the Republic on [[17 August]] [[1945]], the republican authorities placed Teukoe Nja' Arif at the head of Atjeh's administration. His position rested, in Alers's analysis, on the same delicate balance that made [[Pangeran Raden Adipati Wiranata Kusuma|Pangeran Raden Adipati Wiranata Kusuma]] or the Susuhunan of Solo tenable figures within the republican constellation on Java at the time — a member of the old ruling class placed at the head of a revolutionary administration.

## Arrest and death (1945–1946)

As the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|conflict between Ulama and Uleëbalang]] sharpened at the end of [[1945]], Teukoe Nja' Arif proved unable to withstand the rising tide of Poesa power. To forestall official difficulties with the Republic he was granted sick leave on [[14 December]] [[1945]]; it did not help. At the end of [[January]] [[1946]] he was arrested by the Ulama as an "enemy of the Republic" and interned in a "republican" camp at [[Takengon|Takengon]], where he died on [[6 April]] [[1946]]. Alers treats his death as marking the definitive transfer of authority in Atjeh to the Poesa: he was replaced, in name, by the *oelèëbalang* [[Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah|Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah]], but real power passed to [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]], who had by then been named head of the *Dienst van Godsdienstzaken* (Service of Religious Affairs).

## Significance

Alers places Teukoe Nja' Arif's arrest and death at the same point, in his comparative scheme of Java and Atjeh, as the [[3 juli-affaire (1946)|3 July affair]] on Java — with the crucial difference that on Java the *coup* against the Republic's leadership failed, whereas in Atjeh "the design succeeded." His downfall marks the moment at which the *oelèëbalang* class, already weakened by the killings and confiscations of the social revolution, lost its last foothold in Atjeh's formal administration.

**Governor-General Van Heutsz** — *Toean Djenderal Van Heutsz* (in the Acehnese idiom of the old hunter [[Datoek]]) — was the Governor-General of the [[Nederlands-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] and the conqueror of [[Atjeh|Aceh]], whose glorious period — "the time of the great expeditions and excursions" — was the phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] that followed the defection of Teukoe Oemar of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Van Heutsz is the figure whose glorious period is the book's frame for the offensive phase of the Aceh War, and the *Toean Djenderal* who was a *sobat kras* (heavy friend) of the old hunter [[Datoek]].

## The glorious period of Van Heutsz

"Then came the glorious period of Van Heutsz, the time of the great expeditions and excursions." Then came the glorious period of Van Heutsz, the time of the great expeditions and excursions. The glorious period of Van Heutsz — the time of the great expeditions and excursions — is the book's — and the colonial memory's — frame for the offensive phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] that followed the defection of Teukoe Oemar of [[29 March]] [[1896]].

## The great successes

"the time also of our great successes! The Pidië expedition, the expeditions in Idi and Peusangan, the Pasé expedition, the Samalanga expedition, the expeditions to the Gajo and Alas lands, the action in the Tangsé and Geumpang area and on the North Coast of Atjeh, the patrol actions on the always restless West Coast, and finally the Battle of Batèë-Ilië, by which the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good." The great successes of the glorious period of Van Heutsz — the [[Pidië-expeditie]], the expeditions in Idi and Peusangan, the [[Pasé-expeditie]], the [[Samalanga-expeditie]], the [Gajo- en Alaslanden tochten], the [Tangsé- en Geumpanggebied actie], the patrol actions on the West Coast, and the [[Slag bij Batèë-Ilië]] — are the book's — and the colonial memory's — catalogue of the expeditions and excursions by which the organised, general resistance in Aceh was broken for good.

## Van Heutsz and the Acehnese

"As for ourselves, the rough handwork of a soldier in Atjeh contributed little to the refinement of manners, and so it is also known of Van Heutsz — who understood Acehnese as little as he spoke it — that he always turned to the interpreters with the question: 'What is the fellow saying?'" As for ourselves, the rough handwork of a soldier in Aceh contributed little to the refinement of manners, and so it is also known of Van Heutsz — who understood Acehnese as little as he spoke it — that he always turned to the interpreters with the question: "What is the fellow saying?" The "What is the fellow saying?" — Van Heutsz's question to the interpreters — is the book's — and the colonial memory's — marker of Van Heutsz's non-Acehnese: the *Toean Djenderal* who understood Acehnese as little as he spoke it and who always turned to the interpreters.

## Van Heutsz and the transfers

"Governor-General Van Heutsz wished — in italics — once again to have it expressly made known as his desire that no transfer should be ordered or proposed which was not strictly necessary. It all helped nothing." Governor-General Van Heutsz wished — in italics — once again to have it expressly made known as his desire that no transfer should be ordered or proposed which was not strictly necessary. It all helped nothing. The Van Heutsz circular against transfers — and the book's "It all helped nothing" — is the book's — and the colonial memory's — marker of Van Heutsz's failure to stop the transfer malady of the civil administrators.

## Datoek's sobat kras

"I was a 'heavy friend' with Toean Kristoppel, and Toean General Van Heutsz, and Toean Scheepens, and Toean Darlang, and Toean Boreel, and Toean Governor Swart." The old hunter [[Datoek]]'s list of his *sobat kras* (heavy friends) includes *Toean Djenderal Van Heutsz* — Van Heutsz. The *Toean Djenderal* — the Acehnese idiom for the Governor-General — is the book's — and Datoek's — memory of Van Heutsz as the heavy friend of the old hunter: the *Toean Djenderal* who was a *sobat kras* of the Acehnese hunter of [[Blang Rakal]].

## Van Heutsz and the Gajoweg

"This brave Bavarian, who — as my inquiry revealed — was the type of the soldier, roadbuilder, and bridgebuilder under Van Heutsz, and par excellence a man of practice, laid this road." [Heinzenknecht](/people/heinzenknecht) — the brave Bavarian who laid the [[Gajoweg]] — was the soldier, roadbuilder, and bridgebuilder under Van Heutsz. The Gajoweg — the military road from [[Bireuën]] to [[Takengeun]] — was therefore the infrastructure of the glorious period of Van Heutsz: the road built by Heinzenknecht under Van Heutsz to open the [[Gajolanden|Gajoland]] to the colonial army.

## Significance

Governor-General Van Heutsz is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the *Toean Djenderal* whose glorious period — the time of the great expeditions and excursions — was the offensive phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. The book's argument — that the glorious period of Van Heutsz was the phase of the great successes that broke the organised, general resistance in Aceh for good, and that Van Heutsz was the *Toean Djenderal* who understood Acehnese as little as he spoke it and who always turned to the interpreters — is the book's — and the colonial memory's — frame for the offensive phase of the Aceh War. Van Heutsz is therefore the book's marker of the glorious period: the *Toean Djenderal* whose great expeditions and excursions broke the organised resistance and whose *sobat kras* was the old hunter Datoek.

**Teukoe Panglima Polém Moehamad Ali** was the [[Panglima Sagi|Panglima Sagi]] of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII Mukims]] of [[Groot-Atjèh|Great Atjèh]] from [[1940]]/[[1941|41]] — succeeding his father, the old [[Teukoe Panglima Polém|Teukoe Panglima Polém]] Sri Moeda Perkasa Moehamad Daoed, "one of the great figures of the [[Atjèh War|Aceh War]]" — whose assumption of the sagi headship marked the entry of the new generation into the leadership of the religious-political struggle against the Dutch and whose role in the insurgency of early [[1942]] would prove, in Piekaar's reading, to be the dominant one among the younger generation of *oelèëbalang*'s.

## Succession

Teukoe Panglima Polém Moehamad Ali assumed the sagi headship of the XXII Mukims in [[1940]]/[[1941|41]], after the death of his father. The transition, Piekaar records, came at the moment when the "younger generation continued the struggle" — the struggle, that is, that the old Polém had led since his [[1896|1896]] conflict with the Dutch. The new Panglima Polém inherited, with the office, the network of alliances and grievances that the Polém house had built over four decades of opposition to Dutch rule.

## The pre-war struggle

The struggle that the younger Panglima Polém inherited was, in Piekaar's reading, a struggle that had taken a new form: from the armed resistance of the Atjèh War to the political-religious agitation of the colonial period. The "later events," Piekaar writes, "justify the impression that Teukoe Panglima Polém Moehamad Ali in this strongly predominated" — that is, that the new Panglima Polém was the dominant figure among the younger *oelèëbalang*'s who continued the struggle in its new form. The political-religious movements of the late 1930s — the [[Poesa|Poesa]], the religious schools, the network of *oelama*'s — provided the new framework within which the struggle was carried on.

## The 1942 insurgency

The political role of the new Panglima Polém in the insurgency of early [[1942]] — the revolt in Great Atjèh that accompanied the Japanese invasion — is treated by Piekaar as one of the defining episodes of the period. The refusal of the *oelèëbalang*'s to offer their *hari raja puasa* congratulations to the sagi head — a public repudiation of the sagi head's authority — was, in the years before the invasion, a sign of the political tensions that the insurgency would release. The new Panglima Polém, as the head of the sagi, was at the centre of these tensions.

**Steven van der Haghen** (1563-1624) was a Dutch admiraal in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie]] — and before [[1602]] of the *voorcompagnieën* — who concluded the treaty of [[11 November]] [[11 November]] [[1604]] with the zamorin of [[Calicut|Calicut]] on the Malabar coast, recaptured [[Ambon|Ambon]] from the Portuguese in [[1605]], governed [[Ambon|Ambon]] from [[1605]] to [[1609]], and conferred with [[Cornelis Matelieff|Cornelis Matelieff]] at [[Mauritius|Mauritius]] in [[1605]] about the strategic plan to attack [[Malaka|Malaka]] — the plan that would be executed in the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]]. He is the principal Dutch administrative figure in the Moluccas and the Malabar coast in the early VOC period.

## The Malabar treaty (1604)

Van der Haghen concluded the treaty of [[11 November]] [[1604]] with the zamorin of [[Calicut|Calicut]] on the Malabar coast — "een vast verbondt, eeuwige ende onverbreecklijcke alliantie tot verdrukking der Portugesen ende alle hare geassocieerde, haer te verdrijven uyt alle de landen van Sijne Mayesteijt, keijzer van Malabar". The treaty, confirmed by [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] on his outward voyage in [[1608]], was the principal diplomatic foundation of the Dutch position on the Malabar coast and the framework within which the Dutch pepper trade of the west coast of India would develop over the next century and a half.

## The recapture of Ambon (1605)

In [[1605]] Van der Haghen recaptured [[Ambon|Ambon]] from the Portuguese, taking the fortress of **Victoria** at Laha and establishing the Dutch position on the island that would become one of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s chief spice bases. The recapture was the first major Dutch military success against the Portuguese in the Moluccas and the foundation of the Dutch spice monopoly in the clove-producing islands. Van der Haghen governed Ambon from [[1605]] to [[1609]], during which period he also dispatched ships to the [[Banda-eilanden|Banda islands]] to maintain the Dutch commercial position there — the position that [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]]'s [[1609]] expedition would attempt to formalise into a monopoly.

## The Mauritius conference (1605)

On his way to the Indies with the [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] fleet in [[1605]], Van der Haghen — commanding a separate fleet — conferred with Matelieff at [[Mauritius|Mauritius]] about the state of the Indies. The two admirals agreed that [[Malaka|Malaka]] should be the first target — the strategic plan that the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]] would execute. Van der Haghen's own fleet then proceeded to the Moluccas and the Malabar coast, while Matelieff's fleet proceeded to the Strait of Malaka. The Mauritius conference was the principal instance of strategic co-ordination between Dutch admirals in the Indies before the establishment of the Governor-Generalship in [[1609]], and the framework within which the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign]] was conducted.

## Significance

Steven van der Haghen is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch administrative figure in the Moluccas and the Malabar coast in the early VOC period, and the principal co-architect — with [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] — of the strategic plan to attack [[Malaka|Malaka]] that the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]] would execute. His treaty of [[11 November]] [[1604]] with the zamorin of [[Calicut|Calicut]] was the principal diplomatic foundation of the Dutch position on the Malabar coast; his recapture of [[Ambon|Ambon]] in [[1605]] was the first major Dutch military success against the Portuguese in the Moluccas; and his conference with Matelieff at [[Mauritius|Mauritius]] in [[1605]] was the principal instance of strategic co-ordination between Dutch admirals in the Indies before the establishment of the Governor-Generalship. Van der Haghen's role in the early VOC period — together with [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]], [[Pieter Both|Both]], and [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] — defined the strategic framework within which the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force would later operate.

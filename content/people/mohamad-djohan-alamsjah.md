**Mohamad Djohan Alamsjah** — *teukoe tjhi Peusangan* — was the *[[Oeléebalang|oeléebalang]]* of the great *landschap* of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]] in the *onderafdeeling* [[Bireuen]] of the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh*, ruling a *landschap* of more than thirty-two thousand souls. Broersma describes him as *'een hoofd met veel gezag en een goed inzicht in de belangen van zijn land, waarvoor hij al zijn ijver geeft'* — *a chief of great authority and good insight into the interests of his country, for which he gives all his zeal.* He is, with [[Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb|Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb]] of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula]], one of the principal examples in *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* of the *verlichte hoofden* — the enlightened chiefs of the new generation.

## House and Family
He lives at [[Matang Gloempang Doea|Matang Gloempang Doea]] in an attractive but un-Atjehnese house amid a well-planted garden, situated on the road and on the tram. He finds support in his brothers, who, like him, received a good education and all speak Dutch. One of them, [[Teukoe Boestaman|Teukoe Boestaman]], was trained at the *cultuurschool* at [[Soekaboemi|Soekaboemi]] in West Java and now holds the office of *landbouwkundige* in the *landschap* — an agricultural expert. Another brother, [[Mohamad Ali Alamsjah|Mohamad Ali Alamsjah]], is *[[Oeléebalang tjoet|oeléebalang tjoet]]* of [[Djeumpa|Djeumpa]]. The presence of a Dutch-speaking *landbouwkundige* in the household of the *oeléebalang* is, in Broersma's view, a remarkable token of the new brood of chiefs.

## Agriculture and the Landschap
About a third of the soil of Peusangan is irrigable, and the *landschap* has its true *sawahs* of its own making, its *tegal*-fields, and on the higher ground its *ladangs*. Pepper and pinang take a less prominent place here than elsewhere in the income of the people; the *landschap* also grows *djagong* (maize), *oebi* (yam), *katjang tanah* (ground-nut), a little sugar-cane and tobacco. The Inlandsche agriculture of Peusangan resembles, more than elsewhere in Atjeh, that of Java. The Inlandsche agriculture here, Broersma remarks, *'gelijkt wat meer dien van Java. Van meer dan gelijken kan men niet spreken'* — *resembles that of Java somewhat. Of more than resemblance one cannot speak.*

The great experiment of irrigation at [[Panté Lhong|Panté Lhong]] — where the *B.O.W.* dammed one arm of the Peusangan river at a cost of three tons, and the water then ran down the other arm, leaving the irrigation-canal dry — is a token of the new efforts at irrigation, even in their failure. Broersma and the chief reckoned together that a couple consumes seven *picol* of *bras* (rice) a year; the calculation was real reckoning, for such quantities are expressed in Atjeh in *'bamboe'* — one *bamboe* being 2.5 *katti* of *bras*.

## Credit and Salt-Making
The greatest part of the capital of the *Volkscredietbank* at [[Lho Seumawe|Lho' Seumawé]] finds its placement in the *onderafdeeling* Bireuen; the amount has sometimes reached three tons. Peusangan has about ten *gampong*-banks with capitals of f 300 to f 600 and reserves of f 100 to f 300, and the management is properly watched. Perhaps connected with the good working of credit is the circumstance that in Peusangan one sees *grobaks* (carts) in use, whereas elsewhere in Atjeh they are almost entirely absent.

The salt-making of the Peusangan coast is also of some importance. The salt-makers loosen the ground on an open terrain near the flood-forest; the seawater comes in at flood and is thrown into a pit of 3 *d.M.* depth. Seawater is then brought over it again, filters through, and is collected in a basin of about 80 *c.M.* The water is taken to the nearby houses, put into a large basin, and boiled in pots until the salt remains. The boiling lasts 10 to 12 hours and requires a great deal of firewood. The interval between raking the ground and obtaining a *picol* of fairly white salt is three days. The *picol* is sold in Peusangan for f 3.30; the firewood for that quantity of salt costs about f 1.25; so for three days' work about two guilders remain, a normal day's wage.

## Significance
Mohamad Djohan Alamsjah's chieftaincy is the type of the well-managed *landschap* in the new generation: Dutch-speaking brothers, a *landbouwkundige* in the household, *gampong*-banks, *grobaks* on the roads, and a flourishing *tegal*-agriculture. His *landschap* is Broersma's exhibit A for the proposition that, under good chiefs, the post-pacification reforms can take root in Atjeh.

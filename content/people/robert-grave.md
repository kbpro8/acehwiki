**Robert Gravé** (also *Grave*) was a French Vice-Admiral of the [[Augustin de Beaulieu's Atjeh mission (1620-1621)|De Beaulieu expedition]] to the East Indies of [[1620]]–[[1621]]. Sick, shipwrecked and stripped of his command by the time he reached [[Acheh|Atjeh]], he died there in the night of [[9 November]] [[1621]] — "causée de facherie" (caused by vexation) — and was buried in Atjeh. His grave is one of the few certain French burials in the sultanate in the early seventeenth century.

## The De Beaulieu expedition, 1620–1621

The French Saint-Malo expedition, led by [[Augustin de Beaulieu|Augustin de Beaulieu]], consisted of several ships including the *Montmorency* (De Beaulieu's flagship), the *l'Hermitage*, the *l'Espérance* (under Vice-Admiral Gravé), and the *Leiden*. The expedition's purpose was to break into the pepper trade that the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] was consolidating in the Indies.

## The loss of the *l'Espérance*

Driven past Cape Guardafui, De Beaulieu landed with the *Montmorency* and *l'Hermitage* at [[Tikoe]] on Sumatra's Westkust — "een centrum van peperhandel, afhankelijk van den Sultan van Atjeh." From there he came to Atjeh in early [[1621]] to negotiate for the pepper trade. Gravé, in the *l'Espérance*, did not arrive.

The truth — recorded by the Dutch — was that before Tikoe the *l'Espérance* had lain twelve days waiting for a sloop sent ashore with fifteen sick sailors to fetch help. The sloop never reappeared; ten of the sailors died; the five survivors took a Dutch yacht to Atjeh to seek their Admiral. On the great ship were 74 souls; Gravé and three others were still on their feet, but the ship was unmanageable for want of hands. The Dutch ship *Leiden* found the French "gants machteloos" — entirely powerless — with only two casks of water remaining aboard.

## The Dutch rescue and the French claim

Some of the sick were taken aboard the *Leiden*; the *l'Espérance* was given a crew of 50 Dutchmen. At the island Nassau it was hoped the sick would recover, but many found their grave there. When the *Leiden* and the *l'Espérance* reached Jakatra, 95 persons had died and 29 survived. The French printed account claims the ship was taken by force and brought to Java — "Tijdgenooten wisten beter" — contemporaries knew better. The Dutch ambassador in Paris was in fact thanked by French nobles interested in the Dieppe expedition for the help given to the *l'Espérance*, and they waived complaints about the lifting of Dutch sailors from French ships.

## Gravé's last voyage to Atjeh

In November [[1621]], while the *Montmorency* lay in a bay to be cleaned of the shell-layer that was impeding her sailing, an English ship appeared aboard which was the gravely ill Gravé. He, together with one De Parc of the *l'Espérance*'s crew, told the story of the ship — a tale full of inaccuracies that later appeared in print in De Beaulieu's travel-adventures.

Gravé then went in the bark *l'Hermitage* to Atjeh, where De Beaulieu had already departed. By English ship he set out, happened to meet his chief, and told him, deathly ill, of his troubles in the presence of witnesses.

## Death and burial at Atjeh

In the night of [[9 November]] [[1621]], two hours before daybreak, Gravé died of a violent fever "causée de facherie" — caused by vexation — arising "uit ergernis" (from chagrin/offence). He was buried in Atjeh.

## Aftermath

When [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] was in Holland in [[1623]] he wrote that the English and French questions together had cost the Company some sixteen tonnes of gold. The French shipowners eventually received their claimed damages — 550,000 *livres* — paid out by the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]]. Le Tellier's grave is in Bantam; Gravé's grave is in Atjeh.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, Gravé is the most senior French casualty of the early modern French intrusion into the Atjeh pepper trade — a Vice-Admiral who never recovered his command, who reached Atjeh only to die there, and whose grave in the sultanate became a small piece of the French diplomatic case against the VOC. His death "from vexation" in Atjeh on [[9 November]] [[1621]] closes the principal French narrative of the De Beaulieu expedition.

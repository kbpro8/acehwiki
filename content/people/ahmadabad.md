**Ahmadābād** — in English **Ahmedabad** — is the great city of [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]] in western India, in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries one of the principal centres of Indian-Arab scholarship. It is the city where [[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]] succeeded his famous grandfather as religious teacher, before his later move to [[Surat]]; and the city where [[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs]] — a kinsman of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and a prolific author of the Indian-Arab al-Aydārūs family — died in [[1628]].

## The first residence of al-ʿAddārs

[[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]], born in [[1561]] at [[Tarim]] in South Arabia, came to Gujarat at the age of nineteen and succeeded his famous grandfather as religious teacher, first at Ahmadabad and later at [[Surat]]. The succession of al-ʿAddārs to his grandfather at Ahmadabad indicates the hereditary character of the religious office in the Ḥadramī-Indian families, and the standing of al-ʿAddārs as one of the principal religious teachers of Gujarat in his generation. His later move to Surat placed him at the port from which the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network communicated with the Malay-Indonesian world.

## The death of al-Aydārūs

[[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs]] — a kinsman of Raniri and a prolific author of the Indian-Arab al-Aydārūs family — died at Ahmadabad in [[1628]]. His many writings are preserved, including correspondence exchanged with a scholar at [[Mocha]]. His father had come from South Arabia to India in [[1551]]; his mother was an Indian. He is one of the figures of the Gujarati milieu from which Raniri himself sprang.

## Significance

Ahmadabad is one of the principal centres of Indian-Arab scholarship in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries, and one of the principal cities of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network from which the orthodox reaction of [[Acheh]] drew its principal Malay-Indonesian agents. The succession of [[Muhammad al-Addars|al-ʿAddārs]] to his grandfather at Ahmadabad, and the death of [[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|al-Aydārūs]] there in [[1628]], are two of the principal chronological pegs of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī network in this period.

**Kolonel Pel** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the Dutch officer who, after the departure of [[Generaal van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]] in April [[1874]], commanded the Dutch forces in [[Acheh|Atjèh]] through the *Derde tijdvak* (third period) of consolidation, and who developed the *postenlinie* (post-line) system that bears his name. He was promoted to *generaal-majoor* on [[18 February]] [[1875]], and died suddenly of an arterial rupture on [[24 February]] [[1876]].

## The instruction of Van Swieten

Kreemer records that, before his departure, Van Swieten left Pel an *Instructie* (Instruction) and a *Nota* (Memorandum) of [[22 April]] [[1874]] as the "richtsnoer zijner handelingen" — "guideline of his actions". The military task was "het beveiligen onzer positie om en in de nabijheid van [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] en het verzekeren van de gemeenschap met de zee" — "the securing of our position around and in the vicinity of Koeta Radja, and the securing of communication with the sea". The political guidance was Van Swieten's doctrine of passive defence: "De verdediging moet passief blijven, dat wil zeggen, men moet den vijand niet gaan opzoeken in zijn goed aangelegde en verdedigde stellingen, als hij ons niet hindert. De politiek moet en zal voltooien, wat de wapenen zoo gelukkig begonnen hebben" — "The defence must remain passive, that is to say, one must not go seeking out the enemy in his well-laid and defended positions, if he does not hinder us. Politics must and shall complete what the weapons have so happily begun."

## The post-line and the seventy-day campaign

Under Pel's command, the ground around the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]] was conquered "voet voor voet" — "foot by foot" — and secured by a series of posts. "Gaandeweg kwam die linie tot stand. Het heeft aanvankelijk niet in de bedoeling van generaal Pel gelegen het aantal onzer posten op het aanzienlijk cijfer te brengen, waartoe hij langzamerhand door den drang der omstandigheden werd gedwongen. Het op het einde van 1875 bestaande postenstelsel kon dan ook op den naam van 'stelsel' geen aanspraak maken" — "By degrees that line came into being. It had not initially been in generaal Pel's intention to bring the number of our posts to the considerable figure to which he was gradually driven by the pressure of circumstances. The post-system existing at the end of 1875 could not, therefore, lay claim to the name of 'system'."

In his letter of [[30 April]] [[1875]], Pel acknowledged that "al de behaalde overwinningen, al de uitbreidingen van onze stelling op staatkundig gebied nagenoeg geen resultaten hadden medegebracht" — "all the victories gained, all the extensions of our position, had brought virtually no results in the political domain". He proposed a new operation-plan: the occupation of the territory of [[Kroeng Raba|Kroeëng Raba]] — southwest of Koeta Radja — to [[Kroeng Raja|Kroeëng Raja]] on the north coast, by a post-line across [[Great Acheh|Groot-Atjèh]]. The threefold aim was: (1) to separate the enemy from the sea; (2) to create the opportunity to lift the blockade of Atjèh; (3) to obtain a well-situated piece of land that could serve as a transit-place for trade between North Sumatra and the [[Straits Settlements|Straits Settlements]].

The *zeventigdaagsche veldtocht* (seventy-day campaign) of December [[1875]] crowned this plan with success — "in zooverre dat de sagi der [[XXV Mukims|XXV Moekim's]] geheel veroverd werd, d.i. het ten Westen van de [[Acheh River|Atjèh-rivier]] gelegen terrein" — "insofar as the sagi of the XXV Moekim's was wholly conquered, that is, the terrain lying to the west of the Atjèh river".

## Death and succession

"Doch, zoowel tengevolge van de hoogst ongunstige weersgesteldheid als van de volslagen afmatting van troepen en koeli's, was het niet mogelijk die plannen dadelijk geheel ten uitvoer te brengen. De generaal Pel overleed den 24en Februari 1876 plotseling aan een slagaderbreuk" — "But, both as a consequence of the highly unfavourable weather and of the complete exhaustion of troops and coolies, it was not possible to carry out those plans immediately and completely. Generaal Pel died on 24 February 1876 suddenly of an arterial rupture." He was succeeded by *generaal-majoor G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem* on [[10 March]] [[1876]], who was forced by circumstances to resume a waiting posture.

## Significance

Pel's command is, in Kreemer's narrative, the moment at which the Dutch discovered that the capture of the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]] had not ended the war, and that the post-line system — however extended — could not produce political submission. His death in [[February 1876]] cut short the only Dutch offensive of the consolidation period; the passive posture that his successors resumed would persist, with brief interruptions, until the [[Oema-periode (1893-1896)|decline]] of the late [[1880s]] and the final [[Governor Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] offensive of [[1896]].

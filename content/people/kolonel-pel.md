**Kolonel Pel** — the school-textbook spelling of **Pell** (more usually rendered *Pel*), the Dutch colonel (later general) who succeeded [[Generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]] as supreme commander in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] in [[1874]] and who died a year later unexpectedly in [[1875]] — is the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]]'s principal Dutch commander of the interregnum phase between the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the kraton of 24 January 1874]] and the [[Governorship of General Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden governorship]]. The school-textbook spelling "Pel" should be treated as a popularisation; the staff-history spelling is "Pell."

## Succession to Van Swieten

The [[De Kleine Oosterling|Wijma school reader]] records Pel's succession to Van Swieten as part of the aftermath of the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the kraton of 24 January 1874]]: General Van Swieten considered his task accomplished and departed once more for the mother country. Kolonel Pel — later appointed general — became supreme commander in his place. Pel's succession to the supreme command of Atjeh thus followed Van Swieten's return to the Netherlands, and his appointment to the rank of general was made in connection with this succession.

## Death in 1875

The school reader records Pel's death in [[1875]]: this capable senior officer died a year later unexpectedly. Pel's death in [[1875]] — a year after his [[1874]] succession — was thus unexpected, and is the staff history's principal instance of the loss of a capable Dutch commander in Atjeh by natural causes rather than by combat. The death of Pel left the supreme command of Atjeh vacant, and was the immediate occasion of the appointment of [[Generaal Van der Heyden|Generaal Van der Heyden]] as the next supreme commander.

## Significance

Kolonel Pel is, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the principal Dutch commander of the interregnum phase between the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the kraton]] and the [[Governorship of General Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden governorship]], and the staff history's principal instance of the loss of a capable Dutch commander by natural causes. The school reader's emphasis on Pel's capable character — and on his unexpected death — frames the interregnum phase as a Dutch loss that would be compensated only by the subsequent appointment of Van der Heyden, "the hero of Samalangan."

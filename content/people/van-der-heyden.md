**Van der Heyden** — the Dutch *generaal éénoog* (one-eyed general) — was the governor of Atjeh whose three-year period of *rust* (rest) was, by the Acehnese poet [[Dokarim]] of [[Lamteungah]], praised as the one moment in the war at which the Acehnese prospered under the *kafirs*. His *afzetting* (dismissal) is, in Zentgraff's verdict, one of the most shameful and foolish chapters in the whole *Atjehhistorie*: *"Er staat in het boek der Atjehhistorie niet veel genoteerd dat beschamender voor ons is, en van grooter domheid getuigt."*

## The three-year period of rust

The Acehnese judgement, preserved in Dokarim's *heldendicht* the ***Prang Kompeuni***, runs (in Zentgraff's citation): *"Nu brak een driejarig tijdperk van rust aan, waarin de éénoogige Generaal zijne veroveringen bevestigde. De Radja Moeda: Teukoe Moehamad, spande alle krachten in om de hoofdplaats en Oeleë Lheuë tot welvaart te brengen…"* — "Now began a three-year period of rest, in which the one-eyed General confirmed his conquests. The Radja Moeda, Teukoe Moehamad, bent every effort to bring the capital and [[Oeleë Lheuë]] to prosperity, and this succeeded so well that the population that had fled everywhere came back in unstoppable streams and fraternised with the *kafirs*. It was as if one lived constantly in feasts; great profit was achieved with trade, and in vain our war-captains sought still to find people who would follow them. Everyone went to pay homage to the one-eyed King. When the one-eyed General was gone, the rest ended."

## The Tadkirat arrakidin on his dismissal

The Acehnese *oelama* [[Tengkoe Koetakarang]] went further, in his *vlugschriften* (pamphlets) edited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in his rapport of [[1892]] under the title *[[Tadkirat arrakidin]]* — "Vermaning der stilzittenden", that is, *ansporing tot deelname aan den krijg* (exhortation to take part in the war). Koetakarang cited Van der Heyden's dismissal as the type of Dutch bad faith: *"Zij sluiten ook fraaie tractaten die zij naleven zoolang zij nog nieuw zijn… Later verbannen zij wie niet in alle opzichten hunnen wensch volgt; zelfs den eenoogigen generaal, iemand van hun eigen soort, hebben zij verbannen."* — "They also conclude fine treaties which they observe so long as they are still new… Later they banish whoever does not in all respects follow their wish; even the one-eyed general, a man of their own kind, they have banished."

## Significance

The continuance of Van der Heyden's policy, Zentgraff argues, *had den Atjehoorlog eene kwarteeuw eerder kunnen doen eindigen* (could have ended the Atjeh war a quarter-century earlier). His replacement by civilian administrators *heeft in korten tijd afgebroken wat ten koste van millioenen, en van stroomen bloeds, was opgebouwd* (broke in a short time what had been built up at the cost of millions and of streams of blood). After his *aftreden*, by a *zeer globale schatting* (very rough estimate), *nog ruim 30,000 Atjehers zijn gedood*. In the same period [[1890]]–[[1914]] the losses of the Dutch Leger, dead and wounded, amounted to 7,707 officers and men. Under more favourable circumstances, Zentgraff concludes, Van der Heyden *had de Van Heutsz kunnen zijn eener vroegere periode* — could have been the Van Heutsz of an earlier period.

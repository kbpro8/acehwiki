**C. Th. van Deventer** — Cornelis Théodore van Deventer ([[1844]]-[[1922]]) — was a Dutch *vrijzinnig-democraat* (liberal-democrat) member of the [[Tweede Kamer]] and one of the principal parliamentary exponents of the [[Ereschuld|ereschuld]] doctrine — the view that the [[Netherlands]] owed a debt of honour to the Indies for the surplus (*batig slot*) extracted from the Cultivation System period. His interventions in the [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]-affair debates of [[1908]] are among the most penetrating liberal analyses of the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] administration.

## The ereschuld doctrine

[[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s name is associated, above all, with the parliamentary agitation that drew attention to the surplus that the [[Netherlands]] had extracted from the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipelago during the [[Cultuurstelsel|cultuurstelsel]] period. In the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s view is recorded in passing: "al hetgeen dat van [[1831]] af tot en met [[1877]] door ons is ontvangen, volgens minister [[De Waal|De Waal]] rondom 700 millioen" — everything that we received from [[1831]] to [[1877]] inclusive, in the round figure of 700 million guilders given by minister [[De Waal|De Waal]]. The proposition that the present generation could not, however, compensate a dead Indies generation for the wrong done to it was, [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] notes, one that [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s contemporary Bos contested.

## On the Van Heutsz-Van Daalen transition

In the [[1908]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate following the [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908 report]] of governor-general [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.) declined to accept that the mishandling of the *hoofden* (chiefs) was the principal cause of the continuing unrest in Atjeh. What kept the Atjehnese in revolt was that the cost of the subjugation was being charged to the Atjehnese themselves:

> "Men legde de bevolking zware lasten en boeten op en vorderde zelfs spionnen- en gidsendiensten als herendienst. Had [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in [[1893]] niet zelf geschreven: 'wij zitten niet in Atjeh om vliegen te vangen en maar steeds onze millioenen weg te werpen; Atjeh moet onderworpen worden, dat is de zaak, en om dit resultaat na al ons morsen en knoeien nog te erlangen moet Atjeh zelf betalen wat daarvoor voortaan nog aan dit land wordt ten koste gelegd'?"

— Heavy taxes and fines were imposed on the population, and even spy- and guide-services were demanded as *herendiensten* (corvée). Had [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] not written in [[1893]]: "we are not in Atjeh to catch flies and to keep throwing away our millions; Atjeh must be subjugated, that is the point, and to obtain this result after all our messing and botching Atjeh itself must pay what is henceforth to be spent on this land"?

[[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] suspected that governor-general [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had permitted [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to act in accordance with the [[1893]] view, and had only abandoned him when the [[Van Heutsz's inspection tour of Atjeh (December 1907)|December 1907 inspection tour]] had made the unworkability of "this harsh and grasping policy" plain. The parliamentary ally IJzerman (u.l.) replied that it was unfair to pursue [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] with a brochure fifteen years old. "Hebben dan alleen de grote mannen in Nederland het recht om er oude plunje op na te houden?" — Have then only the great men in the Netherlands the right to keep old clothes in the closet?

## On the 1904 mindere-welvaart commissie

[[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] was one of the three editors — alongside [[D. Fock|Fock]] and [[Kielstra]] — of the *economisch-historische bijdragen* that the [[Ministerie van Koloniën]] published in [[1904]] as a more modest, "maar minstens zo nuttig" — but at least as useful — alternative to the 33-volume report of the [[Commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart ([[1902]])|commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart]].

## On the *adat*-law revision

During the [[D. Fock|Fock]]-[[Lohman (De Savornin Lohman)|Lohman]] debate on the revision of the *adat*-law provisions for the indigenous population, [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.) was one of the few supporters of minister [[D. Fock|Fock]]'s position, alongside [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] (a.r.).

## Significance

[[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s 1908 speech on the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-[[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] transition is the principal liberal analysis of the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] administration. The citation from [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 brochure]] supplied the most damaging text from the conqueror's own pen.

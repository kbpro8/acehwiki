**Prof. Niemann** — identified with [[G. K. Niemann|G. K. (Gerrit Karel) Niemann]], the Dutch scholar of Indonesian languages — is cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in Vol. I of *The Achehnese* (p. 132) for his *Bloemlezing uit Maleische geschriften* ("Anthology of Malay writings"), Part 2, p. 25, in connection with the title **Malikul Adil** borne by certain Achehnese judicial officials.

The notice occurs in Snouck Hurgronje's discussion of the *Teuku Hakim* — one of the three chiefs in the upper part of [[Daya]] — whose official title, he explains, "points unequivocally to the complete secularization of his office." Originally charged with the administration of justice, the holder of this title had, "within the memory of man," been "nothing more than an ordinary *dato'*." It is in this connection that Snouck Hurgronje adduces the parallel of **Malikul Adil**, "a common title for qadhis in Malay countries," as Prof. Niemann had already observed in his *Bloemlezing*. The point of the comparison is that certain Achehnese official titles, although now borne by purely secular chiefs, preserve in their names the memory of an earlier judicial or religious function.

The *Bloemlezing uit Maleische geschriften* — an anthology of Malay texts prepared for the use of Dutch colonial students and officials — was one of the standard works of Malay philology in the late nineteenth century. It is one of several of Niemann's publications cited by Snouck Hurgronje in *The Achehnese*; another is his treatise *"Bijdrage tot de kennis der verhouding van het Tjam tot de talen van Indonesië"* ("Contribution to the knowledge of the relation of Cham to the languages of Indonesia"), published in the *Bijdragen van het Koninklijk Instituut* for [[1891]], which furnished Snouck Hurgronje with one of the chief philological foundations for the ethnological introduction to the work.

Niemann thus belongs, together with [[Prof. Veth|Veth]], [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]] and [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]], to that earlier generation of Dutch colonial scholars whose compilations and anthologies laid the groundwork for the more analytical studies of Snouck Hurgronje's own generation. The *Bloemlezing* in particular remained a standard reference for the comparative study of Malay institutions and of the Arabic and Persian loanwords which had entered the vocabulary of government and law throughout the archipelago.

**Sir Frederick Weld** was the Governor of [[Penang]] who, at the end of [[1885]], returned from England to his post in the Straits. [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] — sketching at that date how the revived Atjehnese piracy and the defiant attitude of the "friendly" chiefs around the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentrated line]] again threatened Dutch relations with England — called the coincidence significant, that at that very moment the Governor of Penang, Sir Frederick Weld, came back from England, while [[Sir William Maxwell| Maxwell]], the ombudsman of the [[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Tenom affair]] who wished the Dutch so ill, was about to move for good from Singapore to Penang, "the nearest point to his Atjehsche friends" and to the remaining members of the [[Atjehsche raad van achten|Raad van achten]] (the Atjehsche war council); there, Verstege warned, the new English nobleman could watch the Dutch on the fingers better than anywhere else.

**Gosenson** — *kapitein Gosenson*, *later kolonel* — was the Dutch maréchaussée-officer who, in the last days of [[May]] [[1927]], killed the Westkust resistance-leader [[Tjoet Ali]] and gave him an honourable grave. Zentgraff introduces him with the photograph caption *"Kolonel Gosenson"* next to [[Darlang]] *de Heer der Bergen*. He came to [[Bakongan]] in [[July]] [[1926]] as kapitein ingedeeld bij den troep of [[Behrens]], who was *ook fungeerend controleur der Zuid-Atjehsche landschappen Kloeët en Troemon*.

## Pacification work in Kloeët and Troemon

In the period after [[1925]] the Westkust was again in unrest; there were *nog maar een klein plokkie djahats* (a small band of djahats) said to be there — *een man of twintig* (some twenty men), but in reality *eenige honderden* (several hundred). Gosenson went first to Troemon, where he *op de grootste propagandisten der opstandigggezinden* (the greatest propagandists of the rebelliously minded) and *deze met dezen en genen liet arresteeren* arrested these with this one and that. He also had his European wife with him — *de "Prinses" genoemd* (called the Princess) — who *verrichtte bij dit pacificatiewerk uitstekende diensten* (rendered excellent service in this pacification work).

## The killing of Tjoet Ali, 25 May 1927

In the last days of [[May]] [[1927]] Gosenson received a *chabar* that a *bendetje* had been seen by a man gathering wood in the boschrand, three hours' walk from [[Kandang]]. The report came in at 10 p.m.; *nog denzelfden nacht ging Gosenson er op uit*. At dawn his brigade found the temporary *hutjes* on a steep slope above the Aloeë. *Met veel geraas in de Aloeë plofte* the first man out of his hut, followed by his wife; *"Ka bala!"* — disaster! — he cried. The maréchaussée *schietend op de vijanden die zichtbaar werden* (firing on the enemies who became visible). *Het duurde maar een paar minuten* — it lasted but a few minutes. Gosenson went down into the Aloeë to see if anyone was left. From a hut came a man who, *met inspanning van zijn laatste krachten*, struck at him with a *rentjong*; the *geweldige litteeken dat dwars over Gosensons gezicht loopt als een pad waarover de Dood is gegaan* — the tremendous scar that runs across Gosenson's face like a path over which Death has walked — is the mark of this blow. (The scar was from an earlier wound — *op de heele Westkust bekend* — known over the whole Westkust.) When the wounded man, *met inspanning van zijn laatste krachten*, raised himself, *was het afgeloopen* — it was over; *er lagen zes lijken, waarvan twee vrouwen* (six corpses lay, of which two women).

## The grave of Tjoet Ali

The dead were brought out of the water onto the dry ground. Gosenson *kreeg onmiddellijk den indruk dat Tjoet Ali er bij moest zijn* (immediately had the impression that Tjoet Ali must be among them). He had the men of the two valleys — Kloeët and Mengamat, 110 men together — go up to the place *om naar een geschikte plek te zoeken voor een graf* (to look for a suitable spot for a grave). Two maréchaussée's made the grave; *1: Gosenson* in the photograph; *"Tjoet Ali's graf; Rechts: Gosenson met Tjoet Ali's saple"*. Tjoet Ali was buried at the *marsch bivak Kandang*, with his face towards Mekka. *Want Gosenson wist, wat een eerlijk en dapper tegenstander toekomt* — for Gosenson knew what is due to an honest and brave opponent.

## The "40 submissions" ceremony

Gosenson's work in the same period produced a ceremony of submissions: forty men of the two valleys, *te zamen 110 man*, came down. The photograph of *Maréchaussée's maken het graf voor Tjoet Ali* shows the procedure: the band of surrendered men brought a chicken and even an ape — *aapje* — for the brigade; *en ook dit brachten zij naar de kampong en gaven het aan de brigade die reeds de kip had* (and this too they brought to the kampong and gave to the brigade that already had the chicken).

## Significance

Tjoet Ali's death by Gosenson's brigade on [[25 May]] [[1927]] closed the great Westkust resistance that had flared up since [[1925]]. Gosenson combined the offices of *kapitein der marechaussée* and *fungeerend controleur* — military command and civil administration — in the way colonial pacification required; his wife's participation in the pacification work and the honourable burial he gave his dead enemy are both typical of the *gaaf karakter* Zentgraff admired in officers of this generation.

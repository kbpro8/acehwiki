**Jhr. Graafland** — *kapitein Jhr. Graafland* — was the Dutch officer who, *in het laatst van 1895*, succeeded [[Notten|kapitein Notten]] as commandant of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussée]]. His succession marked, Zentgraff writes, the end of the *slappe periode der geconcentreerde linie*: *„Het is, niet zoo heel lang daarna, volkomen veranderd. In het laatst van 1895 trad kapitein Jhr. Graafland op als commandant van het korps Maréchaussée, en in het laatst van Maart 1896 liep T. Oemar naar den vijand over."* He is one of the *vier vermaarde maréchaussée-officieren* in Zentgraff's photograph — *„links beneden: Graafland"*. He also appears in the 40-year jubilee photograph of [[1930]] — though in the listing of senior officers the *Kapitein Jhr. Graafland* of 1895 is not at the 1930 dinner.

## The Aneukgalong overval, 28-29 March 1896

The [[Fall of Aneukgalong (1896)|fall of Aneukgalong]] was the *éclatantste wapenfeit* (most splendid feat of arms) of the young Korps Maréchaussée in the year [[1896]]. *Onder Graafland was de benteng in den heel vroegen ochtend vrijwel volkomen omsingeld, zoodat de bezetting, figuurlijk gesproken, met den rug tegen den muur stond.* — Under Graafland the benteng in the very early morning was virtually completely surrounded, so that the garrison, figuratively speaking, had its back against the wall. The Atjehers *vochten als leeuwen: sommigen stortten zich liever in de brandende barakken dan zich over te geven* (fought like lions: some flung themselves into the burning barracks rather than surrender). It was a *bitter, hand-to-hand fighting; kwartier werd niet gevraagd en niet gegeven* — no quarter asked and none given.

The march had gone from [[Lambaroe]] at half past midnight on [[28 March]] [[1896]], with two battalions and the maréchaussée under the command of *overste Van Heutsz*, *kapitein Van Daalen* as *chef van den staf*. By a quarter to five in the morning of [[29 March]] [[29 March|the first shot]] was fired; by five the benteng was taken. One hundred and ten Atjehers lay dead; among them [[Tengkoe Mat Amin di Tiro|Tgk. Mat Amin di Tiro]], eldest son of [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Tgk. Sjech Saman di Tiro]]. Two lightly wounded boys of five and six fell alive into Dutch hands.

## Significance

Graafland's commandantship opened the great period of the Korps Maréchaussée. The *omzetting* (conversion) of the korps's role from the *slappe periode* — under Notten and the governors of the *geconcentreerde linie* — to the *offensief* period — under Van Heutsz and Van Daalen — is dated by Zentgraff to Graafland's arrival. The *Aneukgalong* overval of [[March]] [[1896]] was the first proof of what the korps could do; the *Defection of Teukoe Oemar (1896)|overloopen]] of [[Teukoe Oemar]] at the end of March [[1896]] supplied the political shock that turned the new doctrine into the *energieke krijg* Snouck Hurgronje had called for.

**Jhr. Graafland** — *kapitein Jhr. Graafland*, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* identified in fuller form as **Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland**, *Kapitein Infanterie*, Jonkheer — was the Dutch officer who, *in het laatst van 1895*, succeeded [[Notten|kapitein Notten]] as commandant of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussée]]. His succession marked, Zentgraff writes, the end of the *slappe periode der geconcentreerde linie*: *"Het is, niet zoo heel lang daarna, volkomen veranderd. In het laatst van 1895 trad kapitein Jhr. Graafland op als commandant van het korps Maréchaussée, en in het laatst van Maart 1896 liep T. Oemar naar den vijand over."* He is one of the *vier vermaarde maréchaussée-officieren* in Zentgraff's photograph — *"links beneden: Graafland"*. He also appears in the 40-year jubilee photograph of [[1930]] — though in the listing of senior officers the *Kapitein Jhr. Graafland* of 1895 is not at the 1930 dinner.

## The Aneukgalong overval, 28-29 March 1896

The [[Fall of Aneukgalong (1896)|fall of Aneukgalong]] was the *éclatantste wapenfeit* (most splendid feat of arms) of the young Korps Maréchaussée in the year [[1896]]. *Onder Graafland was de benteng in den heel vroegen ochtend vrijwel volkomen omsigeld, zoodat de bezetting, figuurlijk gesproken, met den rug tegen den muur stond.* — Under Graafland the benteng in the very early morning was virtually completely surrounded, so that the garrison, figuratively speaking, had its back against the wall. The Atjehers *vochten als leeuwen: sommigen stortten zich liever in de brandende barakken dan zich over te geven* (fought like lions: some flung themselves into the burning barracks rather than surrender). It was a *bitter, hand-to-hand fighting; kwartier werd niet gevraagd en niet gegeven* — no quarter asked and none given.

The march had gone from [[Lambaroe]] at half past midnight on [[28 March]] [[1896]], with two battalions and the maréchaussée under the command of *overste Van Heutsz*, *kapitein Van Daalen* as *chef van den staf*. By a quarter to five in the morning of [[29 March]] [[29 March|the first shot]] was fired; by five the benteng was taken. One hundred and ten Atjehers lay dead; among them [[Tengkoe Mat Amin di Tiro|Tgk. Mat Amin di Tiro]], eldest son of [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Tgk. Sjech Saman di Tiro]]. Two lightly wounded boys of five and six fell alive into Dutch hands.

## Spring 1896 Operations in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*

Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* identifies Graafland — under his full name *Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland* — as the principal Dutch *maréchaussée* commander of the first semester of [[1896]], the officer whose brigades of the [[Korps Marechaussée]] provided the vanguard (*voorhoede*) and the storm assault capacity in nearly every major operation of the spring and early summer campaigns. Graafland's command record spans the full operational arc of the first semester: 8 brigades *maréchaussée* as *voorhoede* in [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s column on [[17 April]] [[1896]] (the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]]), 6 brigades during the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|2 May 1896 diversie]] (in which he personally "gave the signal for the assault on Senelop"), 10 brigades as *voorhoede commandant* in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|bergcolonne on 23 May 1896]] (the storming of [[Tjot Tjako]]), the *maréchaussée* against [[Lam-Tjroeng]]/[[Lam-Poelau]] on [[25 May]] [[1896|25 May]] and the burning of [[Anak-Paja]], the *korps maréchaussée* in the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|Lam-Krak expedition on 9–10 June 1896]] (4 officers, 226 *minderen*), and the *maréchaussée* in the attack on [[Teupin Batee]], [[Lam-Nga]] and [[Lam-Pasei]] in June [[1896]].

### The Battle of Senelop, 17 April 1896, and the 2 May Diversie

Graafland's first major command in *ATJEH '96* was the 8 brigades *maréchaussée* as *voorhoede* in [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s column on [[17 April]] [[1896]] — the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]]. The engagement, which targeted the hostile *stelling* (defensive position) at [[Lam Bada]] and [[Senelop]], saw the storming of the *versterking* (fortification) at Bralon by 1e Luit. [[K. J. C. Rijnen|Rijnen]] and *Eur. sergt.* [[Panman, No. 21620|Sergeant Panman]] (the first into the fortification), with the *amb. marechaussée* [[Savie, No. 31497|Savie]] heavily wounded and [[Walewangko, No. 9487|Walewangko]] the first into the enemy fortification along with Lt. Rijnen. The engagement also produced the loss of a 7 cm bronze field gun and a *mitrailleur* (machine gun) in the confusion of the retreat — the same gun which [[Panglima Polim]] would subsequently recapture and display at Senelop. The [[2 May]] [[1896|2 May]] [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik]] was Graafland's next major command: 6 brigades *maréchaussée*, with Graafland personally giving "the signal for the assault on Senelop". The assault was successful — the *Amb. maréchaussée* [[Simin, No. 8502|Simin]] being the first to enter the *versterking* at 9:10 AM, only to find "*the birds flown*" — and the post of Senelop was reoccupied by the Dutch.

### The Three-Day Offensive of 23–25 May 1896 and the June Operations

Graafland's most famous command of the spring was the 10 brigades *maréchaussée* as *voorhoede commandant* in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|bergcolonne on 23 May 1896]] — the operation which stormed [[Tjot Tjako]] as part of the three-day offensive against [[Teuku Umar|Umar]]'s stelling. The *bergcolonne*, under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] with [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapitein Van Daalen]] as *Chef van den Staf*, advanced on the Tjot Tjako height, and the Luitenants [[Wagener]] and [[H. M. Vis|Vis]] and the *maréchaussée* [[Wongsodikromo, No. 33780|Wongsodikromo]] and [[Todikromo, No. 28097|Todikromo]] were the first to reach the summit at 5:43 PM. On [[25 May]] [[1896|25 May]] Graafland led the *maréchaussée* against [[Lam-Tjroeng]] and [[Lam-Poelau]] and burned [[Anak-Paja]]. The June operations placed Graafland at the head of the *korps maréchaussée* in the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|Lam-Krak expedition on 9–10 June 1896]] — 4 officers, 226 *minderen* — where his *maréchaussée* conducted the [[Battle of Ateuh Raja / Lam-Krak / Lam-Djamé (9 June 1896)|battle of Ateuh Raja on 9 June 1896]] and the [[Combat at Baha (9 June 1896)|combat at Baha]] (where *inlandsch korporaal* [[Korporaal Brodjo|Brodjo]] engaged ~30 of [[Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]]'s men near [[Beradin]] on [[4 June]] [[1896|4 June]]). In the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June V Moekims Montassik tuchtiging]] Graafland led the *maréchaussée* in the attack on [[Teupin Batee]], [[Lam-Nga]] and [[Lam-Pasei]], and the [[1e Luit. G. K. Dijkstra|1e Luit. Dijkstra]] afdeeling drove Achehnese forces from the [[Tjot Maraja]] *stelling* and the [[Panteh-Karang]] redoute. The continuity of Graafland's command — eight major operations in less than three months — confirms his centrality to the Dutch *maréchaussée*-led war effort of the first semester of [[1896]].

## Significance

Graafland's commandantship opened the great period of the Korps Maréchaussée. The *omzetting* (conversion) of the korps's role from the *slappe periode* — under Notten and the governors of the *geconcentreerde linie* — to the *offensief* period — under Van Heutsz and Van Daalen — is dated by Zentgraff to Graafland's arrival. The *Aneukgalong* overval of [[March]] [[1896]] was the first proof of what the korps could do; the [[Defection of Teukoe Oemar (1896)|overloopen]] of [[Teukoe Oemar]] at the end of March [[1896]] supplied the political shock that turned the new doctrine into the *energieke krijg* (energetic war) Snouck Hurgronje had called for.

Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* adds a further dimension to Graafland's significance: he was the principal *maréchaussée* commander of the first semester of [[1896]] — the *Kapitein* whose brigades provided the *voorhoede* and the storm assault capacity in nearly every major operation of the spring and early summer campaigns. His personal role in giving "the signal for the assault on Senelop" on [[2 May]] [[1896]] and his command of 10 brigades as *voorhoede commandant* in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|bergcolonne of 23 May 1896]] (the storming of Tjot Tjako) confirm his direct tactical engagement in the most important Dutch assaults of the spring, and his sustained command of the *korps maréchaussée* through June [[1896]] — including the Lam-Krak expedition (4 officers, 226 *minderen*) and the V Moekims Montassik tuchtiging — confirms his institutional centrality to the Dutch *maréchaussée*-led war effort.

**Gilles Graafland** was a Marechaussee commander of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] in [[Atjeh|Aceh]], the figure whose name — in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]) — gives the Graafland era its name: the era that began when the unsound system of the concentrated line was abandoned, and that was the "period of the offensive to the utmost, in which the Marechaussee covered itself with glory."

## Account from the Maréchaussée Jubileum-nummer ([[1930]])

In the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* Graafland appears as the officer who took the initiative for the decisive change in the role of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]*, and as the man remembered in the corps as ***de vader der Maréchaussées*** — the father of the Maréchaussées.

### The extension of the corps's role in 1896

From [[1890]] to [[1896]] the Maréchaussée had confined itself to police and administrative work inside the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]*. From 1896 it also entered the purely military field and sought out the enemy in his hiding places. The jubilee editorial ascribed that change to Graafland: "Ziedaar de groote verdienste van den toenmaligen kapitein der Maréchaussée, Jhr. G. J. W. C. Graafland; hij had het initiatief daartoe genomen en voerde het met ontembare energie uit" — there you have the great merit of the then captain of Maréchaussée; he had taken the initiative for it and carried it out with untameable energy. "Zijn naam prijkt dan ook bovenaan op de lijst van geridderde Maréchaussées."

### First in the register of honours

The *[[Overzicht van Onderscheidingen toegekend aan het Korps Maréchaussée in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1890-1930)|Overzicht van Onderscheidingen]]* printed in the same issue opens with his name: under [[1897]], the *[[Militaire Willemsorde|Militaire Willems-Orde]]* 3rd class to *Kapt. Mar. Jhr. G. J. W. C. Graafland*.

### Father and son of the corps

The jubilee number refers to him in his later rank as *overste* (lieutenant-colonel). When Colonel [[C. A. Snell|L. A. Snell]] was sent from [[Java]] to the [[Westkust Resistance (1925-1927)|west coast of Atjeh]] in [[1925]], the paper called him "een waardig opvolger van overste Graafland; hij heeft diens sporen gedrukt," and the interviewer closed by observing that if Graafland was the father of the corps, Snell had the right to be called its son. Snell himself named Graafland first among "de groote pioniers, die van het Korps gemaakt hebben wat het geworden is," before [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]], [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]], [[Van der Maaten]] and, above all, [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

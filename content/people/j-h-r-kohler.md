**J. H. R. Köhler** was a Dutch major-general of the Indies army, commanding the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[April 1873]] who was mortally wounded on [[14 April]] [[1873]] while inspecting the captured [[Missigit|missigit]] outside the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. A self-made man who had risen from the lowest rank in the army, Köhler was entrusted with the supreme command of the expedition against [[Acheh]] after having served as military commandant of Sumatra's Westkust, and his death in the hour of apparent success precipitated the failure of the entire first campaign.

## Background and Appointment

In [[July 1871]] the commander of the Indies army, Lieutenant-general [[W. E. Kroesen|W. E. Kroesen]], had instructed Köhler — then a colonel and military commandant of Sumatra's Westkust — to collect all possible military data about Atjeh. The Indies government at the same time refused a proposal to send a captain of the Indies army aboard the *Djambi* to gain local intelligence. This refusal, together with the absence of a general staff in the Indies, left the expedition badly prepared when it was finally dispatched in [[March 1873]] under Köhler's command. The vice-president of the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]], [[F. N. Nieuwenhuysen|F. N. Nieuwenhuysen]], was appointed commissioner; the naval command was given to Captain-ter-sea [[J. F. Koopman|J. F. Koopman]]; Köhler was named supreme commander of the expedition.

## The First Aceh Expedition

Köhler's expedition — three bataljons of the Indies army and a bataljon of the Barissan, in all 188 officers and 3198 rank-and-file, with a naval landing division of 25 officers and 402 men — arrived at the roadstead of Atjeh on [[5 April]] [[1873]]. The landing was made on [[8 April]] west of the mouth of the Atjeh river. The first day ended in an échec: the storming of [[Kotta Mogat]] failed for want of ammunition and storm-ladders, and the adelborst Zimmer was killed by shots from the supposedly evacuated Kotta Pantej Termin.

On [[10 April]] the troops advanced on the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] believing it would be no more than a military march — only one day's rations had been ordered. They met determined resistance at the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|missigit]] (mosque). Light-balls set the attappen roof alight; a storm column climbed an areng-palm to reach the wall and forced entry; the mosque was taken and then abandoned the same afternoon.

## Death at the Missigit, 14 April 1873

On [[14 April]] at five in the morning the troops returned to the attack. Storm-ladders were placed against the wall by the mineurs, but the defenders, "met lans en klewang" and hurling "groote scherpe steenen," prevented the storming party from jumping inside. When a company armed with breech-loaders at last gained the top of the wall and opened rapid fire, the enemy gave way and the missigit was won. Nog denzelfden morgen — the same morning — the supreme commander arrived on the scene:

> "the chief of staff informed him of the situation. He then went with the various authorities to the east side of the missigit, on the plateau, from which one could best survey the ground and observe the enemy's position. A few moments later General Köhler was mortally wounded and gave up the ghost almost immediately."

He had been struck by a bullet — probably a sharpshooter's — while standing on the exposed plateau east of the mosque. His death, coming after the failure of the storm-ladder assault of [[16 April]] and the council of war of [[18 April]] that decided against renewed attack, sealed the fate of the expedition. The transport fleet left the roadstead of Atjeh on [[29 April]].

## Legacy

Köhler's death and the failure of the first expedition were attributed by the author of the source work chiefly to "the unfamiliarity with the situation, the too low opinion of the enemy's strength and perseverance, and the consequent inadequate preparation of the expedition." The lesson was not lost on his successors: the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] of [[November 1873]] under [[J. van Swieten|Lieutenant-general J. van Swieten]] was strengthened to 6354 infantry, all troops were equipped with breech-loaders, and the artillery was considerably reinforced. [[G. M. Verspyck|Major-general Verspyck]], who had been designated as Köhler's successor, was available to take command immediately, but it was decided instead to wait for the better season and to send out Van Swieten.

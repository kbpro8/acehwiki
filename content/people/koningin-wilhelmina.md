**Koningin Wilhelmina** — Wilhelmina ([[1880]]–[[1962]]), Queen of the Netherlands from [[1890]] to [[1948]] — was the sovereign whom [[Dominee Thenu]] served and by whom he was received in audience in the Netherlands after his retirement in [[1934]]. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* Koningin Wilhelmina is the figure whose handshake Thenu refused to wash — "Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt" — and whose [[7 December]] [[1942]] speech (in exile in London) gave its name to the [[Zeven December Divisie]] of the Indonesian revolution. She was also the reigning monarch in [[1926]], when the *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* was published by [[Volkslectuur]] to commemorate her silver jubilee, and the Wilhelminapark at [[Weltevreden]] — site of the [[Atjeh Monument]] — was named in her honour.

## The audience with Thenu ([[1934]])

When [[Dominee Thenu]]'s "veertig Atjeh-jaren" were over in [[1934]], he was sent on a reis naar Nederland and was received by Koningin Wilhelmina. "Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt" — for a long time he refused to wash the hand the Queen had shaken. For more than ten days no one else — until [[Generaal Swart]] — was permitted to shake that hand. The episode places the Queen, with [[Generaal Swart|Swart]], in the small circle of figures whose touch the *Soldaten-Dominee* counted as sacramental.

## The 7 December 1942 speech

The [[7 December]] [[1942]] speech of Koningin Wilhelmina — in exile in London — promised a post-war round-table conference on Indonesia. The speech gave its name to the [[Zeven December Divisie]] of the Indonesian revolution, raised in [[1946]]–[[1947]] from Dutch volunteers. The naming was therefore both a political reference (to the Queen's promise) and a military designation: the division named for the promise of negotiation was fighting the war that the negotiation would, in [[1949]], finally conclude.

## The loyalty triad

The *[[Marechausseelied]]* that [[Dominee Thenu]] taught stressed "*trouw aan Koningin, trouw aan het vaandel en trouw aan de vlag*" — loyalty to the Queen, loyalty to the standard, and loyalty to the flag. The triad was the Soldaten-Dominee's framing of the colonial soldaat's loyalty: the Queen, the standard, and the flag — the sovereign, the regiment, and the state.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* ([[1926]]) — The Queen's Jubilee

The *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* was published in January [[1926]] "ter gelegenheid van het vijf-en-twintigjarig regeringsjubileum van Hare Majesteit de Koningin" (on the occasion of the twenty-fifth anniversary of Her Majesty the Queen's reign). The foreword, written by the editor, explains that the work was produced by a special Commission of Editors (*Commissie van Redactie*) in the Indies on the occasion of the Queen's silver jubilee. The [[Volkslectuur]], under the direction of Dr. [[C. P. Gunning|C. P. Gunning]], was responsible for the publication.

## The Wilhelminapark

The [[Atjeh Monument]] in [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], stood in the Wilhelminapark — a public park named after the Queen. The naming of this prominent public space after the Queen reflected the central place of the monarchy in the official culture of the Dutch East Indies, and the presence of the Atjeh Monument within it symbolically linked the Queen's reign with the pacification of Atjeh — the colonial achievement that the atlas commemorated as the hallmark of the modern Dutch East Indies.

## Significance

Koningin Wilhelmina is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the sovereign whom [[Dominee Thenu]] served and by whom he was received in audience. The book's argument is that the Queen's handshake — the touch that Thenu refused to wash — was the political honour accorded to the *Soldaten-Dominee* at his retirement, and that the [[7 December]] [[1942]] speech gave its name to the division that would fight the war the speech had promised to conclude by negotiation. The *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* further underscores her central role in the official culture of the Dutch East Indies: the silver jubilee of her reign was the occasion for the colonial government's most ambitious publishing programme, and the Wilhelminapark at Weltevreden linked her name permanently to the monumental commemoration of the [[Pacification of Atjeh]].

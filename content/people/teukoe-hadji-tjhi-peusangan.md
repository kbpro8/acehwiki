**Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah** was the [[oelèëbalang|oelèëbalang]] — self-governing authority — of the *landschap* [[Peusangan|Peusangan]] in the subdistrict [[Bireuën|Bireuën]], and one of the most prominent and progressive of the Atjèhnese self-governing rulers under Dutch rule, whose high patronage of the founding of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] in [[1939]] gave the All-Aceh Union of Ulemas the political legitimacy it needed to take root. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* describes him as "undoubtedly the most gifted figure" among Atjèh's self-governing rulers, "tall and stately in build, a good fifty years old, this engaging personality … a regent in the truest sense of the word."

## Personal profile

Piekaar's portrait of Teukoe Tjhi' Peusangan emphasises his combination of traditional authority and modern orientation. He was, Piekaar writes, "one of the most loyal supporters of Dutch authority, without being blind to the shortcomings of this authority." His patronage of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] was, in this sense, not a departure from his loyalty to the Dutch but an extension of his regental role: "a better guarantee for the purity of the organization's objectives could hardly be conceived." The alliance between the progressive *oelèëbalang* and the religious movement represented, in Piekaar's reading, the kind of political compact that the Dutch administration had long sought to encourage.

## The Peusangan house

The Peusangan *oelèëbalang* house was one of the most prominent in Atjèh. Among the other members of the Tjhi' family who held self-governing authority were [[Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah|Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah]] of [[Idi Rajeu'|Idi Rajeu']] in the subdistrict Idi — the same family name (Tjhi') appearing across multiple *landschap* suggests a network of related houses. The Peusangan branch was the senior line; its head, Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah, was the most prominent figure of the family.

## Patronage of Poesa

The [[Poesa|Poesa]] — *P(ersatoean) Oe(lama-oelama) S(eloeroeh) A(tjèh)* — was founded in [[1939]] in [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] under Teukoe Tjhi' Peusangan's high patronage. Piekaar emphasises the importance of this patronage: the new organisation was "intended as a counterweight to the modern currents of the Moehammadijah movement, which were foreign to the fanatically pious Atjèhse religious life," and the patronage of so prominent a *oelèëbalang* gave the religious movement the political protection it needed to establish itself across the residency. The alliance of the progressive *oelèëbalang* with the religious *oelama*'s would prove, in the events of [[1942]], to be the decisive combination in Atjèhnese politics.

## The 1942 crisis

The pre-war compact between the *oelèëbalang*'s and the religious movement would be tested by the Japanese invasion of March [[1942]]. The response of Teukoe Tjhi' Peusangan, and of the other *oelèëbalang*'s who had aligned themselves with the Poesa, would shape the course of the Atjèhnese alliance with the Japanese and the subsequent disintegration of Dutch authority in the residency.

## Relations with Swart

[[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] cited T. Tjhi' Peusangan as the example of the chiefs from whom Swart, by winning their confidence, "could get anything done" and with whom "it was ultimately possible to achieve a beginning form of [[zelfbestuur|self-government]]."

He is also cited as a witness to the difficulties of the transitional generation: where circumstances made younger chiefs succeed an older generation prematurely, too quickly or too young, they often met obstruction from the remaining "men of the old school" — "Teukoe Tjhi' Peusangan could confirm this."

## The visit of 1927/1928

Swart revisited Atjeh in [[1927]]/[[1928]] and spoke with him again: "I was in Atjeh again in 1927–28. And I spoke there once more with T. Tjhi' Peusangan… I was then to take a son of a territorial chief from [[Pidië]] with me to Holland for his further education. T. Tjhi' would gladly have let his favourite daughter, who had been partly brought up at Batavia, study in Holland."

A photograph in du Croo's book shows General Swart with his wife visiting the *landschapshoofd* of Peusangan during his journey through the Indies in [[1928]].

## The letter of 1933

On [[26 September]] [[1933]] T. Tjhi' Moehammad Djohan Alamsjah, with T. Maharadja Mangkoeboemi of [[Lho' Seumawè]], drew up and signed on behalf of thirty-two chiefs of the north coast the letter of congratulation to Swart on his seventieth birthday, which recorded that Atjeh "has become pacified, quiet and prosperous and has received proper education… beginning with the coming to power of His Honour the Governor H. N. A. Swart in Atjeh, from the year [[1908]] to [[1918]]."

## Earlier history of Peusangan

In [[1909]] Swart was authorised to dispose of 10,000 guilders as compensation to [[T. Maharadja Djeumpa]], acting *oelèëbalang* of Peusangan, for the loss of his possessions through the doings of ill-disposed persons.

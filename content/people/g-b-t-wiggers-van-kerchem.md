**G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem** was a colonel of the infantry promoted to major-general who served as commander of the Dutch forces in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from his arrival on [[10 March]] [[1876]] until his resignation late in [[1876]] over a disagreement with the government concerning policy toward the [[Onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]]. His command covered the months when [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman Zahir]] first appeared in Atjeh and gave new life to the resistance, and saw the appointment of [[K. van der Heyden|Colonel K. van der Heyden]] as commandant of the zuidoosterlinie on [[1 April]] [[1876]].

## Background and Temporary Command

Wiggers van Kerchem had served as temporary replacement for [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] during Pel's leave on Java from [[June 1875|June]] to [[November 1875]]. Under his acting command the small war continued with great stubbornness. "Of any desire for rapprochement on the part of the Acehnese population, nothing was evident; on the contrary, our patrols everywhere met a watchful enemy who daily shot at the somewhat exposed posts and attacked our transports and corvees; an enemy who, far from thinking of submission, became ever bolder. In the past five months it had been convincingly shown that, in the situation in which we now found ourselves in Atjeh, standstill was retreat. As soon as a sufficient fighting force was available, it would therefore be necessary to return to offensive operations."

## Appointment and First Measures

He was promoted to Pel's permanent successor and arrived in Atjeh on [[10 March]] [[1876]] to take command. In the first period of his administration he sought chiefly to better secure the various lines and to strengthen the defensive against the attacks that continued, especially in the neighbourhood of [[Atoöh|Atoöh]]. At his request a chief officer was placed at his disposal "to whom he could entrust the command of a part of the now very widely dispersed garrisons, since especially in the south-east the enemy's boldness daily required new arrangements." On [[1 April]] [[1876]] Colonel [[K. van der Heyden|K. van der Heyden]] accordingly took command of the zuidoosterlinie, with instructions "to clear the terrain near the line of enemies as much as possible, to regulate the mutual connection of the posts properly, and to connect these both with each other and with the hoofd-stelling by good roads."

## The Arrival of Habib Abdurrahman

A little earlier, in March [[1876]], "a famous man had appeared on the scene of war on the enemy's side, namely [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdoerrahman]], properly Sayyid — i.e. descendant of the Prophet — Abdurrahman Zair." Wiggers van Kerchem's command was thus from the outset faced with a renewed and better-led resistance. Operations in May in the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] and in July in the [[IV Moekims|IV]] and [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] had the desired success, but every success was only temporary: "Again and again the enemy took the offensive. Repeatedly patrols and transports were ambushed, often not without success, and there was scarcely a post at which one did not see enemies roaming about or that was not now and then fired upon."

## The Quality of the Troops

Wiggers van Kerchem also had to deal with a serious decline in the quality of his troops. "The Atjeh War, which had now lasted nearly three years, had robbed the Indies army of a good part of its fit cadres and its best soldiers; to make up the losses the requirements for the various ranks had been lowered, and the suitability of the under-officers and corporals thus obtained as patrol leaders consequently often left something to be desired. The need for soldiers at the theatre of war had to be met by newly recruited men; among the Europeans were many foreigners, who — the Germans excepted — were mostly of rather poor quality, and all, Europeans as well as natives, were scarcely drilled when they already had to take part in the struggle."

## Operations of September-October 1876

In September, after the arrival of reinforcements, the troops resumed more offensive operations, with the object of better securing the north-east line and of obtaining a better boundary line by the occupation of the [[Kroëng Tjoet|lagune Kroëng Tjoet]]. By diversions in the south-east line the enemy's attention was diverted, and on [[4 September]] operations began in the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. "Like most of our offensive undertakings, this too was crowned with continual success." Fighting took place especially at the kampong Poeklat, the bentings on the Kroëng Tjoet, Lamara, and Meroe. With the beginning of October the weather put a stop to further operations, and on [[4 October]] the troops returned to [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]].

## Resignation over the Onderhoorigheden

The government wished in this period to act vigorously against two states on the east coast that had remained hostile — [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh|Tandjong Semantoh]]. When [[Toekoe Moeda Angkasa|Toekoe Moeda Angkasa]] of [[Merbau|Merbau]], who had been driven from his country by these two, sought Dutch help to recover it, the government held that he should be strongly supported. The commander in Atjeh, however, was little inclined to reduce further the troop strength at Atjeh, and on a new application in November for 350 to 400 men he objected, "both with a view to the complete unusability of the auxiliary troops, and to the approaching favourable monsoon, which would soon make the continuation of operations in Great Acheh possible. Moreover, he argued, the consequences of aggressive action against Simpang Olim were incalculable, and it deserved the preference to employ all available forces for the time being against Great Acheh." This disagreement led to his resignation; he was succeeded by [[A. J. E. Diemont|Major-general A. J. E. Diemont]], who took over command on [[6 November]] [[1876]].

**Teukoe Moeda Baid Setia Maharadja** was an [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] (territorial lord) of the [[VII Mukims|VII Mukims]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] during the late nineteenth century. His career illustrates the volatile and often short-lived alliances between the Dutch colonial administration and Acehnese local rulers during the [[Aceh War]].

## Dutch honours and subsequent revocation

In [[1888]], the Netherlands Indies government presented Baid Setia Maharadja with a chain of gold wire bearing the Dutch coat of arms — a mark of colonial favour typically bestowed upon indigenous rulers who cooperated with Dutch authority. The chain, described in the 1928 treasure-room guide of the [[Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen|Batavian Society]], was a finely wrought piece of gold wirework terminating in the royal Dutch arms.

However, by [[1896]] Baid Setia Maharadja's loyalty had waned. The Dutch authorities, concluding that he had been unfaithful to their cause, formally revoked the honour and confiscated the chain. The original Dutch description reads: *"in 1888 door het Gouvernement verleend aan Teukoe Moeda Baid Setia Maharadja, Oeloebalang van de VII moekims, doch hem in 1896 wegens ontrouw ontnomen"* — "granted in 1888 by the Government to Teukoe Moeda Baid Setia Maharadja, Uleebalang of the VII Mukims, but taken from him in 1896 on account of disloyalty."

The chain subsequently entered the collection of the Batavian Society's treasure room, where it was catalogued in Cabinet 5 (Atjeh), item no. 31. Its presence in the colonial museum collection served as a material reminder of the fragility of Dutch-Acehnese alliances and the alternating pattern of co-optation and resistance that characterised the later phases of the [[Aceh War]].

## Historical context

The practice of granting honours — chains, medals, and decorative weapons bearing the Dutch arms — to cooperative indigenous elites was a standard instrument of Dutch colonial policy throughout the archipelago. In Atjeh, where the [[Uleebalang|uleebalang class]] was increasingly cultivated as a counterweight to the resistance forces led by the [[Teungku|ulama]] class, such gifts carried significant political weight. The revocation of Baid Setia Maharadja's chain in 1896 coincided with a period of intensified Dutch military pressure and the growing influence of the [[Teungku|religious leadership]] in the Acehnese resistance.

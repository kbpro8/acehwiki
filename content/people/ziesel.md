**Ziesel** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]]

He was also the father of *[[volksonderwijs]]* in the Indies — or rather, du Croo wrote, the man who made the village school financially possible. Confronted with the estimate that twenty thousand little schools would be needed to combat illiteracy on [[Java]] alone, at a cost of more than half the Indies budget, he entrusted [[Ziesel]] with a trial of simple schools, arguing: "in my grandfather's day the master of the village school in the Netherlands was also sexton, bell-ringer, postman, barber, cupper and so forth. Education that was good enough at the beginning of the nineteenth century for Dutch villages is also sufficient to help us out of our difficulty for the present in the Javanese village… But above all: take hold of it and do not sit with your hands in your hair. Something is always better than nothing!"

### From [[Het Pacificeerende Bestuurswerk 1908-1918]]

There was in the beginning a painful shortage of good teachers, as everywhere in the barely opened *Buitengewesten*. Building the little school was manageable: a simple wooden or bamboo building the population could put up without much trouble. But the teachers were another matter. Van Heutsz, "the father of this popular education, or better still, the man who made the village school financially possible," solved the teacher question in his own pithy way, entrusting [[Ziesel]] with a trial of simple schools with the village schoolmaster of his grandfather's day as model — "But above all: take hold of it and do not sit with your hands in your hair. Something is always better than nothing!"

### From [[Volksonderwijs in Atjeh]]

[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], "the father of this popular education, or better still, the man who made the village school financially possible," solved the problem in his own pithy way. Twenty thousand little schools were reckoned necessary to combat illiteracy on Java alone, at a cost of more than half the Indies budget. Van Heutsz therefore charged [[Ziesel]] with a trial of simple schools, arguing: "in my grandfather's day the master of the village school in the Netherlands was also sexton, bell-ringer, postman, barber, cupper and so forth. Education that was good enough at the beginning of the nineteenth century for Dutch villages is also sufficient to help us out of our difficulty for the present in the Javanese village. If need be, the children need learn to begin with no more than a little reading and writing and the four rules of arithmetic. For such a curriculum teachers can certainly be found. In that time the department of education can see to the training of teachers."

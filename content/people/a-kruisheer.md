**A. Kruisheer** — *Kapitein der Infanterie O.I.L.*, Captain of Infantry of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands Indies Army]] — was the author of both volumes of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]*, the comprehensive operational history of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the year [[1896]] which is the source of every article in this repository. The first volume — *1e Deel*, covering the first semester of [[1896]] (1 January – 28 June [[1896]]) — was published at [[Weltevreden]] by N.V. Boekhandel Visser & Co in [[1913]], with 2 maps and 4 portraits. The second volume — *[[ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel (book by A. Kruisheer)|Tweede Deel]]* (2c Deel), covering the second semester of [[1896]] (29 June – 31 December [[1896]]) — was published in the same year, with 3 maps and 6 portraits. Together, the two volumes form the principal Dutch-language source for the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[1896]] and the source balso of the present Aceh Wiki History repository.

Kruisheer's own relationship to the events he describes is unusually direct: he was *personally present at [[Ana' Galong]] in [[December]] [[1895]] as part of the garrison*, four months before [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]]. His preface is dated [[Magelang]], [[April]] [[1913]]. Both volumes were compiled and revised from a series of articles Kruisheer had published in the I.M.T. (*Indisch Militair Tijdschrift*) — the professional journal of the Royal Netherlands Indies Army — in the volumes of [[1903]], [[1905]], [[1906]] and [[1908]] about the Atjeh War in the year [[1896]]. The two volumes together represent both an institutional military history and a personal memoir of the operations of the year.

## Personal Presence at Ana' Galoeng, December [[1895]]

Kruisheer's personal presence at [[Ana' Galong]] in [[December]] [[1895]] places him at one of the most strategically and symbolically significant Dutch posts of the entire [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the post which the Achehnese *considered as the heart of Great Aceh*, which had been the former main residence of [[Teungkoe di Tiro]] (who *also died there*), and which had been conquered by [[Teuku Umar]]'s legion on [[30 November]] [[1893]]. The garrison in [[1895]] — at the time of Kruisheer's presence — consisted of 1 captain, 3 lieutenants, 120 *bajonetten* (bayonets) from the [[15e Bataljon|15e Bataljon]], 1 medical officer, 1 hospital attendant, an ambulance, and 16 men of the artillery. By [[December]] [[1895]], four months before Umar's defection, the post was already *continually shot at by the enemy; in early [[1896]] the entrance was under fire so a privy on the north side could not be used* — that is, the post was effectively under daily harassment even at the height of the Umar alliance.

Kruisheer's personal experience of this pre-defection Ana' Galong — of the routine harassment, of the religious-political significance of the post, and of the Umar legion which had conquered it — gave him an authoritative ground-level perspective on the events which he would later narrate in *ATJEH '96*. The fact that he was present at Ana' Galong four months before the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] and the subsequent [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]] also gave him direct knowledge of the pre-defection situation against which the catastrophe could be measured — and six months before the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng of 29 June 1896]] with which the second volume opens.

## The I.M.T. Articles and the Compilation of ATJEH '96

The institutional form of *ATJEH '96* — a work compiled from articles published in the I.M.T. in [[1903]], [[1905]], [[1906]] and [[1908]] — reflects the dual character of the work as both an institutional military history and a delayed memoir. The I.M.T. was the professional journal of the Dutch colonial army, and publication in its pages represented the official, peer-reviewed form of military-historical scholarship within the colonial army. Kruisheer's decision to publish the first installment in [[1903]] — seven years after the events of [[1896]] — reflects both the time required for the institutional lessons of the spring operations to mature and the time required for the political sensitivity of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|Umar defection]] to fade.

The compilation of the I.M.T. articles into the book form of *ATJEH '96* in [[1913]] — with the preface dated [[Magelang]], [[April]] [[1913]] — represents the final, definitive form of Kruisheer's account. The first volume (1e Deel) included the appendices containing [[Teuku Umar]]'s three letters, Vetter's proclamations, and the Government Resolution of [[26 April]] [[1896]] — giving the work a documentary authority which the original I.M.T. articles could not have possessed. The two maps and four portraits of the first volume — including [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General Van der Wijck]], [[C. Deykerhoff|Generaal-Majoor Deykerhoff]], [[J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] and [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] — confirmed the institutional and biographical ambition of the work.

## The Second Volume — ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel (2c Deel)

The second volume of *ATJEH '96* — *ATJEH [[1896]] ATJCH [[1896]]–[[1900]] Met 3 kaarten en 6 portretten*, published at [[Weltevreden]] by N.V. Boekhandel Visser & Co in [[1913]] — covers the period from the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] of [[29 June]] [[1896]] through the [[Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh (December 1896)|cholera epidemic]] of December [[1896]]. The volume comprises a main text organised in two *Hoofdstukken* (Chapters III and IV), a *Besluit* (Conclusion), and twenty-two *Bijlagen* (Annexes) — including the *Instructie* (Instruction) left by [[J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] as Regeeringscommissaris at his departure (Bijlage I), the *Notulen* (Minutes (Notulen)) of the conference between [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] and [[J. J. K. de Moulin|De Moulin]] of [[18 June]] [[1896]] (Bijlage II), and the consolidated Instruction for Civiel en Militair Gouverneur Van Vliet (Bijlage III). The volume is held today by the Instituut Kern, Leiden.

The second volume — like the first — was compiled from Kruisheer's personal service in Atjeh and from the institutional records of the Dutch colonial army. Kruisheer's personal presence at [[Ana' Galong]] in December [[1895]] gave him an authoritative ground-level perspective on the operations which he would later narrate, and his preface — dated [[Magelang]], [[April]] [[1913]] — closes the eighteen-year arc from his personal presence at Ana' Galoeng in December [[1895]] to the publication of the definitive account in [[1913]]. The second volume's six portraits — including the [[Frontaanzicht van Ana' Galoeng]] (front view of Ana' Galoeng) opposite p. 2, [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor J. J. K. de Moulin]] opposite p. 15, [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] opposite p. 83, [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Kolonel Van Vliet]] opposite p. 145, and [[J. B. van Heutsz|Luitenant-Kolonel Van Heutsz]] opposite p. 276 — extend the biographical ambition of the first volume into the second semester.

The two volumes together — the first covering January–June [[1896]] and the second covering July–December [[1896]] — constitute the comprehensive operational history of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the year [[1896]], as compiled and narrated by Kruisheer from his personal service in Atjeh and from the institutional records of the Dutch colonial army. The two volumes share the same institutional and biographical ambition — with portraits, maps, and *Bijlagen* preserving the principal official documents of the year — and together form the source balso of the Aceh Wiki History repository for the year [[1896]].

## Significance

Kruisheer's significance in the Aceh Wiki History repository is foundational: he is the source — the only source — for every article in the repository, and his personal presence at [[Ana' Galong]] in [[December]] [[1895]] gives his account an authoritative ground-level perspective which a more distant historian could not have provided. The dual character of the work as both institutional military history and delayed memoir — the I.M.T. articles of [[1903]]–[[1908]] compiled into the two volumes of [[1913]] — gives it a distinctive voice, at once operationally precise and personally informed, and the inclusion of the *Bijlagen* with the principal documents of the spring and autumn operations makes it the principal documentary source for the entire year of [[1896]].

His preface, dated [[Magelang]] (the colonial military garrison town in central Java), [[April]] [[1913]], closes the eighteen-year arc from his personal presence at Ana' Galoeng in [[December]] [[1895]] to the publication of the definitive account in [[1913]]. In the *Besluit* (Conclusion) of the second volume, Kruisheer claims that *men in [[1896]] den rechten weg tot pacificatie insloeg* — that in [[1896]] the right path to pacification was entered — a historical position against which the subsequent course of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] (which continued until [[1903]] and beyond) provides the perspective from which that claim may be assessed.

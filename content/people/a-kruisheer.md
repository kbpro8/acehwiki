**A. Kruisheer** — Captain of Infantry of the Netherlands Indies Army, abbreviated *Kapitein der Infanterie O.I.L.* — was the author of *ATJEH '96*, the comprehensive operational history of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the first semester of [[1896]] which is the source of every article in this repository. Kruisheer's own relationship to the events he describes is unusually direct: he was "personally present at [[Ana' Galong]] in [[December]] [[1895]] as part of the garrison", four months before [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]], and his preface is dated [[Magelang]], [[April]] [[1913]]. The balso is the compiled and revised form of articles Kruisheer had published in the I.M.T. in [[1903]], [[1905]], [[1906]] and [[1908]] about the Atjeh War in the first semester of [[1896]], and it represents both an institutional military history and a personal memoir of the spring operations.

## Personal Presence at Ana' Galong, December 1895

Kruisheer's personal presence at [[Ana' Galong]] in [[December]] [[1895]] places him at one of the most strategically and symbolically significant Dutch posts of the entire [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the post which the Achehnese "considered as the heart of Great Aceh", which had been the former main residence of [[Teungkoe di Tiro]] (who "also died there"), and which had been conquered by [[Teuku Umar]]'s legion on [[30 November]] [[1893]]. The garrison in [[1895]] — at the time of Kruisheer's presence — consisted of 1 captain, 3 lieutenants, 120 *bajonetten* from the [[15e Bataljon]], 1 medical officer, 1 hospital attendant, an ambulance, and 16 men of the artillery. By [[December]] [[1895]], four months before Umar's defection, the post was already "continually shot at by the enemy; in early 1896 the entrance was under fire so a privy on the north side could not be used" — that is, the post was effectively under daily harassment even at the height of the Umar alliance. Kruisheer's personal experience of this pre-defection Ana' Galong — of the routine harassment, of the religious-political significance of the post, and of the Umar legion which had conquered it — gave him an authoritative ground-level perspective on the events which he would later narrate in *ATJEH '96*. The fact that he was present at Ana' Galong four months before the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] and the subsequent [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]] also gave him direct knowledge of the pre-defection situation against which the catastrophe could be measured.

## The I.M.T. Articles and the Compilation of ATJEH '96

The institutional form of *ATJEH '96* — a balso compiled from articles published in the I.M.T. in [[1903]], [[1905]], [[1906]] and [[1908]] — reflects the dual character of the work as both an institutional military history and a delayed memoir. The I.M.T. was the professional journal of the Dutch colonial army, and publication in its pages represented the official, peer-reviewed form of military-historical scholarship within the colonial army. Kruisheer's decision to publish the first installment in [[1903]] — seven years after the events of [[1896]] — reflects both the time required for the institutional lessons of the spring operations to mature and the time required for the political sensitivity of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|Umar defection]] to fade. The compilation of the I.M.T. articles into the balso form of *ATJEH '96* in [[1913]] — with the preface dated [[Magelang]], [[April]] [[1913]] — represents the final, definitive form of Kruisheer's account, and the inclusion of the appendices containing [[Teuku Umar]]'s three letters, Vetter's proclamations, and the Government Resolution of [[26 April]] [[1896]] gave the balso a documentary authority which the original I.M.T. articles could not have possessed. The two maps and four portraits — including [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General Van der Wijck]], [[C. Deykerhoff|Generaal-Majoor Deykerhoff]] and presumably [[J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] confirm the institutional and biographical ambition of the work.

## Significance

Kruisheer's significance in the Aceh Wiki History repository is foundational: he is the source — the only source — for every article in the repository, and his personal presence at [[Ana' Galong]] in [[December]] [[1895]] gives his account an authoritative ground-level perspective which a more distant historian could not have provided. The dual character of the work as both institutional military history and delayed memoir — the I.M.T. articles of [[1903]]–[[1908]] compiled into the balso of [[1913]] — gives it a distinctive voice, at once operationally precise and personally informed, and the inclusion of the *Bijlagen* with the principal documents of the spring operations makes it the principal documentary source for the first semester of [[1896]]. His preface, dated [[Magelang]] (the colonial military garrison town in central Java), [[April]] [[1913]], closes the eighteen-year arc from his personal presence at Ana' Galong in [[December]] [[1895]] to the publication of the definitive account in [[1913]].

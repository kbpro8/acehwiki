**R. C. Kroesen** was the Netherlands Indian controller (later assistant-resident) in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] who in [[1873]]–[[1874]] made two voyages along the Atjehnese coast to make the Government's first trade proclamation known to the several Atjehnese chiefs, who in May [[1874]] sat on the commission that designed the first road, tramway and telegraph connections between [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]] and [[Koeta Radja]], and who in March [[1877]] interpreted Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]]'s address to the Atjehnese chiefs.

## The coastal voyages of 1873–1874

In the broad proclamation issued during the [[Blockade of the Atjeh coast (1873-1874)|blockade of the Atjehnese coast]], the chiefs of the Atjehnese population were warned of the consequences which a friendly or a hostile attitude toward the Netherlands would have, particularly in connection with the trade capable of great expansion in the province. The commandant of the Dutch steamship *Sumatra* — which ship was initially destined for a voyage along the coasts of Atjeh — and the controller first class R. C. Kroesen, on two journeys along the coast, made the contents of that proclamation known as widely as possible to the several Atjehnese chiefs. To what extent the proclamation influenced the signing of the act of submission by the [[Raja of Gigieng|Radja of Gigiëng]] on [[20 April]] [[1874]], Langhout leaves undecided.

## The communications commission of 1874

On [[26 May]] [[1874]] a commission was formed, consisting of the heeren Kroesen (assistant-resident), the chief engineer officer present and the lieutenant at sea [[J. P. Mercier]], charged with investigating what the Government had to do for the improvement of the means of communication. On [[26 June]] of the same year the Netherlands Indian Government approved the commission's proposal to lay a road of five kilometres and a railway between the debarkation place Oelèë Lheue and Koeta Radja, to establish a telegraphic connection between both places, and to build a môle (*zeehoofd*) at Oelèë Lheue — the origins of the [[Atjeh Tram]].

## Interpreter of the Governor-General, 1877

At the solemn assembly of the chiefs of the [[XXV Moekims]] and of the coast states convened by Governor-General Van Lansberge before his departure in March [[1877]], the Landvoogd's address — pointing to the beneficent object of the Netherlands authority and promising the chiefs still in resistance that voluntary submitters would be maintained in their authority — was interpreted by Assistant-Resident Kroesen.

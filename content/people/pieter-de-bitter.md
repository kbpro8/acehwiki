**Pieter de Bitter** was a Dutch naval commander of the [[Dutch East India Company|VOC]] who, in the mid‑seventeenth century, led a lucrative return fleet from the East Indies that passed through the North Sea in [[1665]] and was noted in Dutch marine records as a point of interaction with the newly organised [[Dutch marines|mariniers]] (see the *Resolutiën van de Staten van Holland en West‑Friesland* of [[13 April]] [[1672]]). The fleet’s cargo, described in the source as “rich return‑fleet of Pieter de Bitter,” was a tangible link between the VOC’s commercial activities in [[Aceh]] and the Dutch Republic’s naval logistics, prompting the marines to arrange protective measures for merchant vessels returning from the East Indies.

## Early Career and VOC Service
Pieter de Bitter entered the service of the [[Dutch East India Company|VOC]] in the early 1630s, rising through the ranks of the company’s fleet. By the 1650s he had commanded several voyages to the Indonesian archipelago, including missions to the Sultanate of [[Aceh]], where VOC‑Dutch trade and occasional military confrontations were frequent. His experience in the East Indies earned him a reputation for handling valuable cargoes and navigating hostile waters.

## The 1665 Return Fleet
In [[1665]] the VOC dispatched a richly laden fleet under de Bitter’s command, intended to bring back spices, textiles, and other commodities from the East Indies. The source records that the fleet “liggende rijke retourvloot van Pieter de Bitter” was sighted off the Dutch coast and became a matter of concern for the newly formed marine companies. The marines, organized under the *Resolutiën* of the *Staten van Holland en West‑Friesland* (dated [[13 April]] [[1672]]), were tasked with safeguarding such fleets against piracy and enemy action, reflecting the strategic importance of VOC trade routes that included Aceh.

## Connection to Aceh
While the source does not detail de Bitter’s specific activities in Aceh, his career with the VOC inevitably involved the Sultanate, as the VOC’s primary source of spice trade in the early modern period was the Aceh region. De Bitter’s 1665 fleet likely carried goods originally sourced from Aceh’s pepper and clove plantations, illustrating the economic interdependence between the Dutch Republic and the Aceh Sultanate.

## Later Life
After the 1665 expedition, de Bitter continued to serve the VOC, undertaking further voyages until his death in [[1667]]. His legacy persisted in Dutch naval historiography, where his name appears in marine records concerning fleet protection and the coordination between the VOC’s commercial fleet and the Dutch marines.

**Teukoe Dagang** was one of the *hoofden van Tangsé* — one of the indigenous chiefs of the [[Tangsé]] valley — and the leader of a larger Acehnese resistance band that survived the destruction of the *Tiriō bende* in [[1911]] and was hunted by luitenant [[Schmidt]] for over a year afterwards, repeatedly escaping every search in a way the book takes as proof that "ook voor de allerbeste patrouilles het succes nooit verzekerd is" — even for the very best patrols, success is never assured. He is the book's example of the Acehnese hoofd who could not be brought in by force alone.

## The refusal to submit

After the destruction of the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]] in [[1911]], some forty *onverzoenlijken* (irreconcilables) "sloot zich aan bij de bende van T. Dagang en bij die van [[T. Haroen]], die op de Noordkust opereerde." T. Dagang refused to submit to Schmidt for a particular reason: "hij meldde zich niet bij Schmidt durfde te, omdat deze eens verklaard had, dat hij hem zou begraven naast een hoofd, dat door hem vermoord was." That is, Schmidt had once declared that he would bury Dagang next to a hoofd whom Dagang had murdered. The threat of posthumous humiliation — being buried in proximity to his victim — was enough to keep Dagang in the bergen for over a year.

## The murder of the three spionnen

When Dagang lost men in a treffen, he turned on those he suspected of spionnage for the Dutch. "Zijn broer meldde zich met zes volgelingen" — his brother surrendered with six followers — but Dagang himself "ontsnapte aan elke speurtocht." He then murdered three people suspected of spionnage "op afschuwelijke wijze" — in a horrifying manner — which the book does not detail but which forms the moral stain on his record. The contrast with the religious resisters of the Tiriō bende is implicit: where they killed as *geloofsgetuigen* in a holy war, Dagang killed as a hoofd defending his authority.

## The August search and the empty schuilplaats

"Tot Augustus de 26e Schmidt weder op hem jaagde." On that date the colonne found his schuilplaats on a steep *[[tjot]]* (a small mountain) — but Dagang himself was not there. The overval missed him. The book registers this as a fact about the limits of even the best patrouille: a kleine, beweeglijke bende under a hoofd who knew the bergen could disappear for a year or more against a colonne that had just destroyed the Tiriō family in two.

## Significance

Teukoe Dagang's significance in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is as the foil to the *Tiriō Teungkoes*. Where they were religious martyrs whose resistance was doctrinal and familial, Dagang was a regional hoofd whose resistance was political and personal. The book's quiet point is that the latter kind of resistance was harder to finish: the religious resisters would eventually all die for the doctrine; the political hoofd could simply disappear into the bergen and refuse to come in. Dagang is also the figure through whom the book makes its argument about the limits of the colonne as an instrument: even Schmidt, with his peerless intelligence network and his unmatched patrouilles, could not catch everyone.

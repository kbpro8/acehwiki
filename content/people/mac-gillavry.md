**Mac Gillavry** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

Swart's own account preserved the human detail: crossing on improvised rafts he found beside him, to his surprise, lieutenant [[Mac Gillavry]] of Van Heutsz's staff, who had come along without his knowledge. They worked their way cautiously along the bank towards the *missigit* (mosque), reached the edge of the fortification, and at Swart's signal *"voorwaarts"* leapt over the wall into the midst of a garrison certainly two hundred strong. The surprise robbed the defenders of their courage; after a short but violent fight they fled. Across the water Van Heutsz was seen with his staff, shouting "who is that commander there and with how many men is he posted?" — to which Swart shouted back: "Captain Swart with eighteen men, but he requests reinforcement!"

### From [[Pasè expedition (1899)]]

Swart's own narrative added the details omitted from the official citation. Working himself onto dry land from an improvised raft, he found beside him — to his surprise — lieutenant [[Mac Gillavry]] of Van Heutsz's staff, who had come along without his knowledge. Their little rafts happened to reach a stretch of the enemy position that was unoccupied. They worked their way cautiously and slowly along the bank in the direction of the *missigit* and reached the edge of the fortification, where Swart carefully posted his men. When all had recovered their breath he gave the signal *"voorwaarts"*: they sprang forward, over the wall of the *missigit* and into the midst of a startled garrison certainly a couple of hundred strong. The surprise had robbed the defenders of their courage; after a short but violent fight the garrison fled and left the *missigit* to Swart and his eighteen men.

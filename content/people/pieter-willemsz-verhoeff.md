**Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff** (c. 1575-1609) was a Dutch admiraal in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie]] who commanded the fleet of nine ships and four jachts that left Texel on [[22 December]] [[1607]] with the secret instruction of [[11 April]] [[1608]] to seize [[Banda|Banda]] and attack [[Malaka|Malaka]], conducted the [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|abortive Malaka expedition of 1608]], and was murdered with forty-six of his men at [[Banda|Banda]] on [[22 May]] [[1609]] in the Bandanese ambush near Rada — the event that fixed the Dutch on the path of coercive monopoly that [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] would later execute. Verhoeff's fleet included the young [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Jan Pieterszoon Coen]] as onderkoopman, and the experience of the voyage — particularly the murder of the admiraal — shaped [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s entire later policy.

## The fleet and the secret instruction

The Verhoeff fleet of nine ships and four jachts — "bestaande uit 9 schepen en vier jachten", with 1942 souls aboard — left Texel on [[22 December]] [[1607]]. The fleet's secret instruction, dispatched from Holland on [[11 April]] [[1608]] after the fleet's departure and reaching Verhoeff at [[Bantam|Bantam]] on [[26 December]] [[1608]] by fast jacht, committed the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] to a new monopolistic policy: "de eylanden van Banda op 't allerhoochste gerecommandeert, als sijnde dese landen der Compagnie daer aen op 't alderhoochste gelegen". The instruction, supplemented by the further instruction of [[4 May]] [[1609]], completed the policy framework: the [[Twelve Years' Truce|Twelve Years' Truce]] with Spain had just been signed; the Dutch were to use the truce to entrench the Company's position before Spain could regain its strength. Verhoeff was to obtain the Banda spice monopoly by treaty or by force, and to attack Malaka if possible.

## The outward voyage and the Atjeh call

The fleet called at [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] on the outward voyage — the first VOC fleet to do so since the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the Company]]. The Atjeh call was a brief affair: the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s reception was cordial, but Verhoeff did not pursue commercial negotiations, his instructions being directed to Banda and Malaka. The fleet also called at [[Mozambique|Mozambique]], where after a seven-week siege Verhoeff abandoned the attack on the Portuguese fort; at [[Calicut|Calicut]] on the Malabar coast, where he confirmed the treaty that [[Steven van der Haghen|Steven van der Haghen]] had made with the zamorin on [[11 November]] [[1604]]; and at [[Bantam|Bantam]], where the secret instruction of [[11 April]] [[1608]] finally reached him.

## The Malaka expedition (1608)

The full account of Verhoeff's Malaka expedition is given in the entry on the [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|expedition itself]]. In summary: he appeared before Malaka on [[23 November]] [[1608]], having spent some weeks in the Strait of Malaka hunting Portuguese merchant craft; the sultan of [[Djohor|Djohor]] refused to help, having opened peace negotiations with the Portuguese; the Portuguese set an abandoned hulk ablaze on the roadstead and sent their fleet out to engage the Dutch at **Ilha da Pedra**, where they were driven off; Verhoeff, learning from his factor at [[Batoesawer|Batoesawer]] that no help would come, decided against a siege and raised the blockade. He then sailed to [[Djohor|Djohor]], where he pressed the sultan to honour the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|second Matelieff accord of 23 September 1606]] and to permit the Dutch to build a fort; the sultan refused. Verhoeff gave the sultan money to rebuild the burned capital, left two ships at the river mouth, replaced the factor Van den Broeck with **Jacques Obelaer**, and sailed for [[Bantam|Bantam]] on [[8 February]] [[1609]].

## The Banda expedition and the murder (May 1609)

The fleet then sailed to the [[Banda-eilanden|Banda islands]] to execute the secret instruction. Verhoeff arrived off Banda-Neira on [[8 April]] [[1609]] with nine ships and four jachts, joined by the English ship *Hector* under [[William Keeling|William Keeling]]. He presented to the Bandanese chiefs a letter from [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]] demanding the spice monopoly and permission to build a fort; after protracted negotiations, the Bandanese gave formal consent but quietly evacuated women and children to the hills. On [[22 May]] [[1609]] Verhoeff landed with a *breeden raad* of 150 soldiers for a "vriendschappelijke bespreking" near Rada, walked into an ambush, and was killed with forty-six of his men. The expression "op syn Admiraels Verhoeven leeren bitcharen" — "to consult on Admiral Verhoeff's bench" — became a Dutch proverb for walking into a trap.

The murder was the principal instance of Dutch-Indies violence in the pre-[[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] period and the proximate cause of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s coercive monopoly policy. The vice-admiraal **Simon Hoen** assumed command, retaliated against the Bandanese, concluded the so-called *eeuwig verbond* of [[10 August]] [[1609]] by which [[Banda-Neira|Banda-Neira]] was ceded to the VOC in perpetuity, and built Fort Nassau on the ruins of the old Portuguese lodge. The fort, and the policy it represented, would define the Dutch position at Banda for the next decade — until [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s conquest of [[1621]].

## Significance

Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch admiraal of the strategic turn from the western to the eastern archipelago and the principal victim of the Bandanese resistance to the Dutch monopoly. His [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|Malaka expedition of 1608]] was the last Dutch attempt to take Malaka before the [[1641|Dutch-Djohor conquest of 1641]] and marked the abandonment of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance. His murder at [[Banda|Banda]] on [[22 May]] [[1609]] was the proximate cause of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s coercive monopoly policy at Banda and the foundation of [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s later argument that monopoly could only be obtained by force. The fleet he commanded included the young [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] as onderkoopman, and the experience of the voyage — particularly the murder of the admiraal — shaped [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s entire later policy. Verhoeff's secret instruction of [[11 April]] [[1608]] was, in Van der Woude's analysis, the legal foundation of the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force.

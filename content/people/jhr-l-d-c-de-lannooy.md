**Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannooy** — *Ritmeester* (Cavalry Captain) of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|K.N.I.L.]], of the noble prefix *Jhr.* (Jonkheer) — was the commander of the cavalry detachment — a half-squadron (*half escadron*) of the 4th Cavalry Squadron — attached to the Dutch columns in [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the [[1896]] campaign, in which capacity he led the cavalry at the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] on [[8 April]] [[1896]], at the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|great tuchtiging of the IV and VI Moekims of 23–25 May 1896]], and on the [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|expedition to Lepong]] of August [[1896]]. He is the principal cavalry officer of the [[1896]] campaign in *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* (1913).

## The 1896 campaign

De Lannoij — the spelling Kruisheer uses — commanded the half-squadron of cavalry attached to the relief column of [[G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Kol. Soeters]] for the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] on [[8 April]] [[1896]]. The *1e Deel* of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* records his reconnaissance of the country between [[Koeta Radja|Koetaradja]] and the southern posts, his discovery of the enemy's positions at [[Lam-Hasan]] and [[Lam-Permai]], and his role in covering the march of the infantry columns. He is mentioned in the *Inleiding* for his ride on [[5 May]] [[1896]] with [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Lt.-Kol. Van Vliet]] and a single cavalryman to the post of [[Lam-Permai]], during which the party was *op 60 M. afstand flink onder vuur genomen* — at 60 metres' distance heavily fired upon — but was not hit. The *Tweede Deel* records his participation in the [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|expedition to Lepong]] of August [[1896]], where he commanded the cavalry reconnaissance of the west-coast landing grounds.

## The half escadron

The 4th Escadron cavalry detachment under De Lannoij was, on [[1 January]] [[1896]], composed of three officers and fifty-two European men, with the appropriate horses and equipment. The half-squadron was the principal Dutch cavalry force in [[Groot-Atjeh]] throughout the [[1896]] campaign, and De Lannoij's personal reconnaissance placed him at the centre of the intelligence-gathering effort that preceded every major Dutch operation of the year.

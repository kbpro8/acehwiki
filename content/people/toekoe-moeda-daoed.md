**Toekoe Moeda Daoed** (also written Toekoe Moeda Daud) was an [[Uleebalang|hoeloebalang]] who exercised authority at [[Selimoen]] on the [[Aceh River|Atjeh-rivier]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and had hitherto been known as one of the Dutch army's most stubborn enemies, but who, in the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of August [[1879]], voluntarily submitted to the Dutch together with other chiefs, declaring in his letter: "The great misfortune, brought by the war upon the three sagis of the land, makes me decide to offer my submission." His submission, communicated through the mediation of Toekoe Kadli and the blind priest [[Habib Machmoed|Habib Machmoed]] and completed on the ground on [[14 August]] [[1879]] — the Dutch entry of [[20 August]] [[1879]] in Generaal [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]]'s foreword of [[1889]] marks its formal dating — marked the end of the large-scale resistance in Great Acheh.

## The Submission

On [[30 July]] [[1879]] two Atjehers with a white flag presented themselves to the Dutch authority at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]], as envoys of the blind priest [[Habib Machmoed|Habib Machmoed]], who with 25 followers waited outside the fortification and was the bearer of a letter from some chiefs, among whom Toekoe Moeda Daoed, who exercised authority at [[Selimoen|Selimoen]] and had hitherto been known as one of the Dutch army's most stubborn enemies. In that letter they offered the governor and military commander their submission and declared that they would exhort their people to return from the mountains and the woods to the kampongs. By another letter, addressed to Toekoe Kadli, the latter's mediation with the governor was invoked: "The great misfortune, brought by the war upon the three sagis of the land, makes me decide to offer my submission."

## The Selimoen Expedition, 13-15 August 1879

The march to Selimoen, which had first been given up, was now, in order to hasten the somewhat protracted negotiations, undertaken after all. On [[13 August]] [[1879]] the columns of Indrapuri and [[Lamtobah|Lamtobah]] set out at the same time. The latter still had to fight at [[Kepaja Doewa|Kepaja Doewa]] and [[Lam Soemboeng|Lam Soemboeng]], and reached Selimoen on [[15 August]] [[1879]]. The other column had already arrived there the day before. It had met the most serious opposition at [[Lampisang|Lampisang]], where [[Toekoe di Tiroe|Toekoe di Tiroe]] with his band had awaited it.

Toekoe Moeda Daoed and other chiefs now submitted; those who, like [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]] and the [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]], refused to give up the fight, fled to the upper lands of [[Pedir|Pedir]]. "The return march to Indrapuri resembled a triumphal procession. Everywhere the population showed a peaceful disposition; in all kampongs, even on the commanding hills, white flags were planted; the troops, accompanied by the chiefs who had come in submission, were awaited in the kampongs by men, women and children, and provided with refreshments; all enemy bentings were abandoned."

## The eyewitness account at Selimoen, 14–17 August 1879

The fullest description of Toekoe Moeda Daoed is that of [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]] in *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]). He had on [[14 August]] [[1879]] voluntarily submitted to Majoor [[Majoor Lubeck|Lubeck]], whose column had reached and occupied Selimoen that day, and had promised to go with him to [[Kota Radja]]; hostages had therefore not been demanded. On the next morning, when the column of Luitenant-Kolonel [[Majoor Gerlach|Gerlach]] came over the mountains from [[Lamtoba]], he awaited it near the kampong: "on the far side of a dry slokan he stepped forth from behind the brushwood, while a great white flag was held above his head. Although his clothing was in nothing remarkable, and he wore above the wide, dark, silken Atjehnese trousers only a white jacket, yet everything in him marked the supreme head. Not great of stature, without beard or moustache, light-yellow of complexion, with great dark eyes, straight nose, firmly closed mouth, he stood there awaiting the column-commandant with calm dignity."

Because the population had remained in the kampong, it was at his request proclaimed to the column that no one might leave the bivouac and that all contact of soldiers and coolies with the population must be prevented; to Gerlach's refusal of the alternative — a bivouac wholly outside the kampong, which Majoor Lubeck through [[Controleur Kamp]] had given him hope of — he made no objections; and the houses of the fugitives of the XXII and XXVI Moekims were with his consent placed at the troops' disposal for shelters and firewood. It was from his information that the column learned the fate of the irreconcilables: his brother [[Massa-an]], [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]], [[Radja Koelala|Toekoe Radja Koeala]], the [[Imam of Longbatta|Iman Longbattah]] and other prominent hostile chiefs had still been assembled at Selimoen on the day before the columns' arrival, had deliberated in the missigit on what stood before them, and had fled to [[Reng-Reng]] in the VII Moekims of Pedir. On the morning of [[17 August]] [[1879]] Majoor Lubeck — having waited for him a couple of hours in "a time of great tension", for he had promised in his report to the General to bring this chief to the Kraton — marched back to Indrapoeri with Toekoe Moeda Daoed, [[Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet|Toekoe Ajer Alang]] and some retinue, being everywhere received by the population in the most peaceable manner; thence he went on to Kota Radja, as promised.

## The disambiguation in Van der Heijden's foreword of 1889

That this Toekoe Moeda Daoed — "head of Selimoen, one of the pretenders to the Sultan's throne, who submitted to him on [[20 August]] [[1879]]" — is not to be confounded with the then-current Sultan-pretendant, is expressly noted by Generaal [[General van der Heijden|K. van der Heijden]] in his [[Voorwoord of Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Van der Heijden, June 1889)|foreword to the book of Cool and Hooijer]]: of the latter pretendant — the youth watched over by [[Toewankoe Haschim|Toewankoe Haschim]], destined for the Sultan's rank in [[1874]] — the coming had indeed been announced, on the same date, by the likewise newly submitted [[Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet|Toekoe Ajer-Alang]], but he had excused himself "uithoofde der naderende poeasa" — by reason of the approaching fasting month. The remark distinguishes the Selimoen chief who came in at Selimoen from the royal pretendant of the mountain resistance, two figures that later accounts risked conflating.

**Dr. P. van Romburgh** was a Dutch botanist whose identification of the Achehnese cooling-plant *naleu'eng sambo'* — used in the Achehnese [[Peusijuek (cooling ceremony)|*peusijuek*]] or "cooling" ceremony — as *Eleusine Indica* is cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in *The Achehnese*. His identification places him among the small group of Dutch colonial scientists whose specialist knowledge Snouck Hurgronje drew upon in elucidating the material culture and ritual practice of the Achehnese.

## The "cooling" ceremony and its plant requisites

The Achehnese "cooling" (*peusijuek* or *peusijuk*) is, in Snouck Hurgronje's account, the universal method by which persons or things thought to be exposed to "hot" or dangerous influences are restored to a state of ritual safety. The most effective method of cooling consists in besprinkling the person or thing to be cooled with *teupong taweue* — water mixed with a little rice-flour — and in strewing over the object a little husked and unhusked rice mingled together. The besprinkling is performed with the help of certain small plants, of which the principal are the *sisijue* (a name which in itself implies cooling) and the *mane'-manoe*; on certain occasions *betel-nut stalks* or *naleu'eng sambo'* are added. The cooling is performed, for example, before the young padi is planted out; on the 44th day after a birth, marriage, or death; on a boy who has completed the recitation of the Quran; on one who has just returned from a long journey, or been saved from shipwreck, or fallen into the water and narrowly escaped drowning; and so on.

## Identification of *naleu'eng sambo'*

In a footnote to his description of the requisites for the *andam* (the ceremonial shaving of the bride's hair), Snouck Hurgronje identifies the *naleu'eng sambo'* — "a kind of grass the flowers of which look [like]" (the text is here somewhat garbled) — by reference to van Romburgh. "This is, according to Dr. P. van Romburgh, the *Eleusine Indica*. It is known in Java as *jampang* and used as cattle-fodder". The same plant, as the translator's note records, is known at [[Batavia|Batavia]] as *chakar* (elsewhere *chochor* or *sosor-bekek*, the Sundanese *bungbi iris*), and is used in Java as in Acheh for certain mysterious purposes.

## Significance for the comparative study of Achehnese ritual

Van Romburgh's identification of *naleu'eng sambo'* is a small but typical instance of the way in which Dutch colonial botanists supplied the technical apparatus by means of which the ritual plants of Achehnese custom could be brought into relation with the botany of the wider Archipelago. The same perspective governs Snouck Hurgronje's citation of [[Walter William Skeat|Skeat]]'s *Malay Magic* for the parallel Malay use of the "neutralizing rice-flour" (*tepung tawar*) with a sprinkling-brush formed of leaves and twigs of certain plants. Without van Romburgh's botanical identification, the precise relation of the Achehnese *naleu'eng sambo'* to the Javanese *jampang* — and thus of the Achehnese cooling ceremony to the corresponding Javanese and Malay usages — could not be made out.

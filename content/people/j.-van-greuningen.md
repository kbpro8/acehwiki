**J. van Greuningen** was a Dutch 1st lieutenant of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands Indies Army]] who was killed in action on [[25 September]] [[1897]] at the mouth of the [[Goeah Teroes]] cave on the [[Glé Betoes]], while commanding the advance-guard of the patrol from [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] under Captain [[J. N. C. Baron van Heerdt]].

## Death at the cave mouth

Van Greuningen commanded the advance-guard on the ascent, and had *een zware taak te meer* — the heavier task — because the Acehnese guide could no longer find the mountain path leading to the cave, so that the column clambered upward at hazard.

When the patrol came under fire from the rocks, Captain Van Heerdt ordered him to climb to the cave with his section, spread out in a wide circle. Scarcely had some paces been taken when the enemy opened a frightful fire. Van Greuningen had already approached close to the cave with a dozen fusiliers when about fifteen Acehnese suddenly appeared from behind a rock face and opened a murderous fire *à bout portant*. He fell dead, struck by two bullets. The European fusiliers [[Winkelman]], [[Tuk]] and [[Dissel]] were killed with him, and the native fusiliers [[Bongsowikromo]] and [[Tanja]] severely wounded.

[[P. H. R. Beuming]] records that the whole took place *in een ondeelbaar oogenblik* — in an indivisible moment — and that because of the almost impassable terrain it could not be prevented. Van Heerdt took over the command of the advance-guard from *den zoo jammerlijk gesneuvelden luitenant* — the lieutenant so lamentably killed. His body was carried down in a *[[Tandoe|tandoe]]* and taken the same day to [[Koeta Radja]] for burial in the [[Peutjoet|Petjoet cemetery]].

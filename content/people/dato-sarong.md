**Dato Sarong** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Teungkoe Moet]]

He had instructed both the district head and his two sons in *Qoran* recitation — "that instruction creates in Atjeh a lasting bond of particular significance between teacher and pupil." On the ground of "an otherwise contestable interpretation of Muhammadan law" he became a fierce opponent of vaccination, started an action against it, and refused to appear when summoned to [[Koeala Bhèë]]. A *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* patrol was sent to bring him in; "on his arrest Teungkoe Moet offered resistance with some followers and… was killed", one *marechaussée* being severely and three lightly wounded. His death led to the *[[klewang]]* attack of [[25 April]] [[1918]] and to the [[Coconut and Sugar-Cane Excursion (April 1918)|coconut and sugar-cane excursion]]; the two sons of the loyal district head [[Dato Sarong]] had been much attached to him.

### From [[Boven Wòjla]]

In [[1909]] the pacification of "the troublesome Boven Wòjla" under Scheurleer, operating from the bivouac [[Tanòh Mirah]], produced the submission of two hundred resistance men, who were helped with rice and seed-*padi*. In April [[1918]] the killing of [[Teungkoe Moet]] during arrest and the *[[klewang]]* attack of [[25 April]] on the bivouac of the commander of [[Koeala Bhèë]] prompted a report that the whole of the Boven Wòjla was in revolt; [[H. J. Schmidt]] marched with six brigades and found instead a wholly peaceable population offering coconuts and sugar cane. The loyal district head was [[Dato Sarong]], whose two sons had been much attached to Teungkoe Moet.

### From [[Coconut and Sugar-Cane Excursion (April 1918)]]

Although patrolling and bivouacking were conducted very cautiously to avoid further losses (the report indicated heavy resistance to be expected, since the two sons of the district head of the Boven Wòjla, the loyal [[Dato Sarong]], were much attached to the late Teungkoe Moet), it appeared very soon that there was no question of general resistance: everywhere the *gampong* dwellers appeared with their chiefs, offering the customary gifts of coconuts and sugar cane, and made it known that it was by no means their intention to make common cause with the — in any case already fallen — resistance men.

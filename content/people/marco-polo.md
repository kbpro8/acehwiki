**Marco Polo** (Venice, c. [[1254]]–[[1324]]) was the Venetian traveller whose *Travels* (Il Milione) records, in connection with his crossing from [[Java minor|Java minor]] to [[Kulam|Kalï-patnam]] in [[Tinnevelly|Tinnevelly]], a "very small island that is called **Gauenispola**" — which [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] identified with the [[Pulo Bras|Pulo Xuta / Poelo Bras]] of the *Eerste Schipvaart*, and which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as a *bedekte taal* form for the ancestral land of [[Taroema]]. Polo also records the "peculiar language" of the island — the ***bahasa kapoer*** of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]] — which is the principal *bedekte taal* register of Malay-Javanese historical geography.

## The Gauenispola passage

After his description of [[Java minor|Java minor]], Marco Polo crossed to [[Kulam|Kalï-patnam]] in [[Tinnevelly|Tinnevelly]] and "incidentally mentioned a very small island that is called **Gauenispola**". He did not say what he had to tell of it. That, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, would have left us indifferent, were it not that [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] had identified the place with the *Pulo Xuta* of the *Eerste Schipvaart* (vol. II, pp. 212-213): "a high island on which stands a watch-tower or *Farol* that warns the inhabitants and is lit at night when there are ships on the coast". A fire-beacon in the manner of the [[Coromandel Coast|Voor-Indische]] primitive light-towers, such as [[Haafner|Haafner]] had described at [[Bimilipatnam|Bimilipatam]]; the Malay name is ***roemban***. Since [[1875]] the [[Willemstoren|Willemstoren]] has stood on [[Pulo Bras|Poelo Bras]].

## Decoding: *Gawé-nis* and the vanished Kapoelaga

The name ***Gauenispola*** — also spelled ***Gamis-*** or ***Djamispola***, and ***P.Gomes*** — [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes through the *bedekte taal* lens. The "peculiar language" of the island — the ***bahasa kapoer*** of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]] — is the *kapoer* of [[Barus|Barus camphor]]. The first part of the name *Gawé-nis* — Malay *gawé* "make", i.e. "maaksel" — combined with the inversion *pola-ga-nis* (or *-m-is*), produces ***Ka-poe-laga-nis***, the name of the "vanished" kingdom that was called after the ***kapoelaga*** (cardamom), the plant with the very fragrant knob. In Sanskrit ***kakkola***, in Arabic ***qaciolah*** — the latter the name by which [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]] recorded the capital ***Qakola*** of [[Moel Djawa|Moel Djawa]], the ancestral land of [[Taroema]].

## The *Kakoela-Qaciolah* connection

The same name, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] argues, underlies the ***Kakoela*** of Javanese storytellers — shortened to ***kakoe*** — and the ***Qakola*** of [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]]'s account of [[Moel Djawa|Moel Djawa]]. The cardamom-name is the *bedekte taal* form by which the ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] was concealed beneath the names of trade-goods. The same name, De Roo de la Faille argues, underlies the *aroemanis* name of a mango-variety — the same *aroem* of *T-aroem-a*.

## The "Pulo Xuta" and the Willemstoren

The *Pulo Xuta* of the *Eerste Schipvaart*, which [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] identified with Polo's *Gauenispola*, is the [[Pulo Bras|Poelo Bras]] on which, since [[1875]], the [[Willemstoren|Willemstoren]] has stood — the Dutch colonial light-tower at the northern entrance to the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]]. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads the *Xuta* as *ksoe-ta*, whose inversion *ta-k-soe* is the name of the Balinese ***djero Taksoe*** — the "mysterious *genius loci*" who manifests himself by incarnating in one of those present, and who dwells in the neighbourhood of the god-houses in some tree on which a small wooden offering is fixed.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Marco Polo's importance lies chiefly in his *Gauenispola* passage — the first European record of the *bedekte taal* form by which the ancestral land of [[Taroema]] was concealed beneath the names of trade-goods — and in his record of the "peculiar language" of the island, the ***bahasa kapoer*** of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]]. His identification, by [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] and [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], with [[Pulo Bras|Poelo Bras]] also fixes him as the first European witness to the northern entrance of the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] — the strategic choke-point that the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]] would contest in the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|war of 1539]].

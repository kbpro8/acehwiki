**Moehamat Dawöt** was the last pretender Sultan of [[Aceh|Atjèh]], who fled to [[Keumala|Keumala]] in Pidië after the fall of the [[Kraton Atjeh|kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] and continued to claim the sultanate until his submission to the Dutch in [[1903]], which effectively ended the Atjèh sultanate. He was banished in [[1907]].

## Earlier career as Pakèh of Pidië

Before becoming pretender Sultan, Moehamat Dawöt bore the title **T. Radja Pakèh Moehamat Dawöt** and served as Pakèh (head) of [[Pidië|Pidië]] from [[1895]] to [[1896]]. He was the sixth in the line of Pakèh's of Pidië, succeeding T. Radja Pakèh Soelöjman ([[1879]]–[[1895]]). His tenure as Pakèh was brief; he was succeeded by T. Radja Pakèh Moehamat Ali, who came under the voogdij (guardianship) of his uncle in [[1896]] and became self-governing after [[1914]].

## The pretender sultanate at Keumala

After the [[Kraton Atjeh|kraton]] fell to Dutch forces under [[Generaal van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], Moehamat Dawöt established himself at [[Keumala|Keumala]] in Pidië as the pretender Sultan. From there he issued *sarakata*'s (royal charters) in [[1887]]–[[1888]] to Gajö *moefi*'s (religious scholars) who paid him homage, attempting to extend his religious-political authority into the [[Gajöland|Gajöland]].

## Submission and banishment

Moehamat Dawöt submitted to Dutch authority in [[1903]], an event [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] treats as the effective end of the Atjèh sultanate. He was subsequently banished in [[1907]]. His daughter **Teungkoe Djam** had married the pretender Sultan, linking the Keumala line to the court of the last ruling Sultan, [[Sultan Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah|Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah]] (reigned [[1870]]–[[1874]]), who had died without children when the kraton fell.

## Family connections

Moehamat Dawöt was connected through his daughter Teungkoe Djam to **Pö Tjoet Meurah** (Tjoet Nja' Meurah), a prominent woman raised at the kraton court whose aunt had married Sultan [[Sultan Mantsoer Sjah|Mantsoer Sjah]] ([[1838]]–[[1870]]). Pö Tjoet Meurah first married Sultan Mahmoed Sjah, then Toeankoe Mahmoed (brother of Toeankoe Asem). The family network centred on Keumala preserved a vestige of royal authority even after the sultanate's formal end.

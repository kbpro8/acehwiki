**Moehamat Dawöt** was the last pretender Sultan of [[Aceh|Atjèh]], who fled to [[Keumala|Keumala]] in Pidië after the fall of the [[Kraton Atjeh|kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] and continued to claim the sultanate until his submission to the Dutch in [[1903]], which effectively ended the Atjèh sultanate. He was banished in [[1907]].

**The pretendent-sultan van Atjeh** — the so-called pretender sultan — was the claimant to the Acehnese sultanate whose submission [[Bahlmann]] demanded in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November]] [[1897]] as one of the two immediate objects of Dutch policy, and whom [[Pieter Jelles Troelstra|Troelstra]] mentioned as being sought at [[Kamala]].

## Earlier career as Pakèh of Pidië

Before becoming pretender Sultan, Moehamat Dawöt bore the title **T. Radja Pakèh Moehamat Dawöt** and served as Pakèh (head) of [[Pidië|Pidië]] from [[1895]] to [[1896]]. He was the sixth in the line of Pakèh's of Pidië, succeeding T. Radja Pakèh Soelöjman ([[1879]]–[[1895]]). His tenure as Pakèh was brief; he was succeeded by T. Radja Pakèh Moehamat Ali, who came under the voogdij (guardianship) of his uncle in [[1896]] and became self-governing after [[1914]].

## The pretender sultanate at Keumala

After the [[Kraton Atjeh|kraton]] fell to Dutch forces under [[Generaal van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], Moehamat Dawöt established himself at [[Keumala|Keumala]] in Pidië as the pretender Sultan. From there he issued *sarakata*'s (royal charters) in [[1887]]–[[1888]] to Gajö *moefi*'s (religious scholars) who paid him homage, attempting to extend his religious-political authority into the [[Gajöland|Gajöland]].

## Submission and banishment

Moehamat Dawöt submitted to Dutch authority in [[1903]], an event [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] treats as the effective end of the Atjèh sultanate. He was subsequently banished in [[1907]]. His daughter **Teungkoe Djam** had married the pretender Sultan, linking the Keumala line to the court of the last ruling Sultan, [[Sultan Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah|Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah]] (reigned [[1870]]–[[1874]]), who had died without children when the kraton fell.

## Family connections

Moehamat Dawöt was connected through his daughter Teungkoe Djam to **Pö Tjoet Meurah** (Tjoet Nja' Meurah), a prominent woman raised at the kraton court whose aunt had married Sultan [[Sultan Mantsoer Sjah|Mantsoer Sjah]] ([[1838]]–[[1870]]). Pö Tjoet Meurah first married Sultan Mahmoed Sjah, then Toeankoe Mahmoed (brother of Toeankoe Asem). The family network centred on Keumala preserved a vestige of royal authority even after the sultanate's formal end.

## Bahlmann's demand

[[Bahlmann]] told the chamber that two things ought to be the object of the struggle at that moment: first, that [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] should long since have been in Dutch hands living or dead; and second, "moesten wij thans zeker zijn van de onderwerping van den zoogenaamden pretendent-sultan. Ik ben overtuigd dat, wanneer de pretendent-sultan het Nederlandsch gezag erkent, wij een van de grootste stappen zullen gedaan hebben tot pacificatie van [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot Atjeh]]." — we ought now to be sure of the submission of the so-called pretender-sultan. I am convinced that when the pretender-sultan recognises Netherlands authority, we shall have taken one of the greatest steps towards the pacification of Groot-Atjeh.

He added that if he was not mistaken, the restoration of the sultanate was not to be expected from the government, and that it appeared neither the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement|Indies government]] nor the minister would hear of such a demand as a condition of pacification; he would therefore press it no further — see [[Restoration of the sultanate of Atjeh (proposal)]].

## Cremer's reply

The Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] answered that "de eischen door den heer Bahlmann gesteld betreffende het gevangennemen van Toekoe Oemar en het sluiten van eene overeenkomst met den sultan, vormen geen noodzakelijke onderdeelen van het program der Regeering." — the requirements laid down by Mr. Bahlmann concerning the capture of Toekoe Oemar and the conclusion of an agreement with the sultan form no necessary parts of the Government's programme. He would not dispute with Bahlmann about the significance of the sultanate in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — which he might study "o.a. in het werk van dr. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]" — but held it certain that to come together one or both sides must approach: "Van de zijde van den sultan is zoodanige toenadering tot nog toe niet geschied en herhaling van de onderhandelingen met den sultan onzerzijds ligt niet in de bedoeling." — On the sultan's side no such approach has so far been made, and a repetition of the negotiations with the sultan on our side does not lie in our intention.

## Troelstra's use of the case

[[Pieter Jelles Troelstra|Troelstra]] cited the divided counsels about the sultan as evidence of the general perplexity: "De een zegt: wij moeten den sultan opzoeken in [[Kamala]], terwijl een ander uitroept: wij moeten Toekoe Oemar in handen zien te krijgen, levend of dood; maar alles komt ten slotte hierop neder: Atjeh moet onderworpen worden, het koste wat het wil, zonder dat men inziet hoe dat doel is te bereiken." — One says: we must seek out the sultan in Kamala, while another exclaims: we must get Toekoe Oemar into our hands, living or dead; but everything comes down at last to this: Atjeh must be subjected, cost what it may, without anyone seeing how that object is to be attained.

Cremer's own view of the possibility of a peace with the sultanate was categorical: "Er wordt geen vrede gesloten met een volk, dat geen hoofd heeft of ooit gehad heeft, met zooveel invloed, dat het voor het geheele volk kan spreken, en dat voor een zeer groot deel onze souvereiniteit erkent." — No peace is concluded with a people that has no head, and never has had one, of such influence that he can speak for the whole people, and that recognises our sovereignty for a very great part.

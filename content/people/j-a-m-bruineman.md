**J. A. M. Bruineman** was a Dutch scholar of Chinese influence on the Indonesian archipelago who, in [[1946]], contributed the chapter *De invloed der Chineezen* ("The Influence of the Chinese") to the volume *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* edited by Dr. [[C. W. Wormser|Wormser]]. His chapter appears in the volume between Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]]'s opening chapter on the oldest cultural streams and Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter on the influence of Islam.

## Role in the volume

Bruineman's chapter surveys the long history of Chinese commercial, religious and cultural influence on the archipelago — from the early Chinese ceramic finds to the late-imperial Chinese merchant communities of Batavia and the [[Deli]] tobacco belt. For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the chapter's significance is chiefly comparative: it places the Chinese influence on the archipelago alongside the Hindu and Islamic influences treated in the adjacent chapters, and supplies the wider East-Asian frame against which the Indian-Ocean-facing Islamisation of the north Sumatran coast (see [[Goedjarat]], [[Cambay]]) is to be read.

The volume's table of contents lists Bruineman's chapter as occupying pages 56–, immediately preceding the section on *De christelijke godsdienst*. The brief listing of contributors on the volume's title page names "J. A. M. Bruineman" as one of the fourteen *medewerkers* (collaborators) of the volume.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Bruineman's significance is indirect: his chapter's principal contribution to the Atjeh story is the framing of the Chinese-Indonesian commercial network that, alongside the Indian-Ocean network of the Gujerati and Arab Muslim merchants, supplied the long-distance trade of the archipelago in which Atjeh participated from at least the thirteenth century onwards. The [[1415]] Chinese reports cited by Van Naerssen (see [[1415]]) — confirming Islam across the whole north coast of Sumatra — are part of the same documentary tradition that Bruineman's chapter surveys.

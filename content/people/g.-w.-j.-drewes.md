**G. W. J. Drewes** — Professor Gerardus Willebrordus Joannes Drewes — was a Dutch orientalist and Malay scholar at Leiden, whose 1955 article in *BKI* 111 — cited by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in the introduction to his [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises — threw new light on the heredity and origin of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]]. Drewes is also acknowledged by Voorhoeve for the identification of the term *Śrāwaka* — used by Raniri in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] for one of the pre-Muslim religions — as Sanskrit for Buddhist.

## The identification of Raniri's father-city

Voorhoeve's introduction to the [[1955]] edition records that Drewes's 1955 article in *BKI* 111 "recently threw new light on the heredity of the author; there can now be no doubt that his father-city was Ranir near Surat in Gujarat." This identification — which placed Raniri's family origin at the town of [[Ranir]] near [[Surat]] in [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]], rather than at the various other places that had been proposed — was the precondition for the modern understanding of Raniri's Gujarati-Ḥadramī background, and for the account of his family, his spiritual genealogy, and his connection with the Rifāʿiyya that Voorhoeve gives in the introduction to his edition.

## The identification of *Śrāwaka*

In the apparatus criticus to the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] (ad p. 27 r. 1), Voorhoeve records Drewes's identification of the term *Śrāwaka* — used by Raniri for one of the pre-Muslim religions — as Sanskrit for Buddhist. The term, transmitted through the Arabic *Srāwaka* and the Malay *Srāwaka*, is a Sanskrit word (*śrāvaka*, "hearer," "disciple") that in Buddhist usage designates the lay disciple of the Buddha. Its identification by Drewes is the principal clue to Raniri's account of the religions of India in the *Tibjān*.

## Significance

Drewes's contributions to the study of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] — the identification of [[Ranir]] as Raniri's father-city, and the identification of *Śrāwaka* as Sanskrit for Buddhist — are among the principal advances in the modern study of Raniri before the [[1955]] edition of Voorhoeve. The first established the Gujarati-Ḥadramī background of Raniri that is the basis of the modern understanding of his career; the second established the Indian substrate of his account of the pre-Muslim religions in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]].

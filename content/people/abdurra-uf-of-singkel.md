---

title: "Abdurra'uf of Singkel"
aliases: ["Abdora'oh", "Teungku di Kuala"]

tags: [person, scholar, mystic, religious-figure, saint, legendary]
---

One of the most important religious figures ever to have worked in Acheh. Born in Singkel, he studied for years at Medina, Mecca, Jiddah, Mokha, Zebid and Betal-faqih, sitting at the feet of some 15 masters. His principal guide was Ahmad Qushashi, and after Qushashi's death his successor Molla Ibrahim gave Abdurra'uf permission to found his own school. He returned to Acheh after [[1661]] and taught there until his death, after which his tomb at the mouth of the Acheh river (hence his title Teungku di Kuala) became the holiest site in the land. His chief work, the Umdat al-muhtajin, remains influential, and he also translated Baidhawi's Quranic commentary into Malay.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Teungku di Kuala

**Teungku di Kuala** — also known as **Abdora'oh** (more properly **Abdurra'uf** of [[Singkel]]) — was a saint of earlier times in [[Acheh]], whose tomb long enjoyed the highest reputation in the country before being outshone in popular veneration by the more recent tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] in [[Gampong Jawa]]. He is named by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] in two distinct contexts in *The Achehnese*: as the saint whose tomb was formerly most honoured in Acheh, and as one of the two saints (alongside Teungku Anjong) at whose tombs Achehnese mothers commonly perform the vow connected with the ear-piercing of their daughters.

In his survey of the sayyids, saints, and ulamas of Acheh, Snouck Hurgronje notes that "the tomb at present most highly honoured in Acheh is that of Teungku Anjong in [[Gampong Jawa]]. It is the last resting-place of a certain Sayyid Abu Bakar bin Husain Bil-Faqih, and has completely outshone the more ancient tomb of Teungku di Kuala alias Abdora'oh (Abdurra'uf of Singkel) which enjoyed the highest reputation in former times". The displacement of Teungku di Kuala by Teungku Anjong is one of several indexes of the growing importance of sayyid saints — those descended from the Prophet [[Mohammad (the Prophet)|Mohammad]] — in nineteenth-century Achehnese religious life, a development closely associated with the rising influence of such figures as [[Habib Abdurrahman|Habib Abdurrahman Zahir]].

The tomb of Teungku di Kuala remained, however, one of the great pilgrimage sites of Acheh. In his account of the ear-piercing ceremony (*tab glitnyiicug*), Snouck Hurgronje observes that it is "more usual to take the girl in procession to a neighbouring holy tomb, so that the saint's blessing may rest upon the act". The mother often makes a vow (*kaoy*) that the ceremony shall take place at the tomb of some famous saint, "the most customary being that the ceremony shall take place at the tomb of some famous saint, such as [[Teungku Anjong]] or Teungku di Kuala". On such occasions both men and women of the gampong are invited in great numbers, and a procession is formed to the sacred spot, escorted if possible by the gampong authorities (*keuchi'* and *teungku*) and with the two traditional drums (*geundrang*) and a flute (*sirné*) leading the van. After the *leubes* have recited passages from the Quran followed by customary prayers, an animal is slaughtered and a [[Kanduri (general)|kanduri]] held.

The appellation "Teungku di Kuala" means "the master at the river-mouth", and identifies the saint with the locality of his tomb at the mouth of the [[Aceh River|Aceh river]] rather than with his personal name. The further identification with "Abdurra'uf of Singkel" links him to the West Coast of Acheh, and especially to [[Singkel]] — an identification which has caused some to associate him with the great seventeenth-century ʿAbd al-Raʾūf al-Singkilī, the Shafi'ite jurist and exegete of [[Singkel]] who composed the celebrated *Mirʿāt al-ṭullāb* and other works. Snouck Hurgronje himself does not pursue this identification in *Vol. I*, but records the Achehnese tradition as he found it.

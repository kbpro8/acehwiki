**Major F. J. E. Mekern** was a Dutch *majoor* of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who, on [[26 December]] [[1875]], took the stelling between [[Mibouw]] and the [[Kroeng Daroe]] in the opening move of [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]]'s [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims]], and was *doodelijk gewond* (mortally wounded) the same day. In [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s narrative he is the first of the officers whose blood purchased the conquests of the *aanvallende* phase of [[1876]].

## The Launch of the New Offensive

Pel returned to Atjeh on [[9 November]] [[1875]] from his [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Buitenzorg planning session]] with the [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]]'s endorsement of his plan to "den oorlog met Atjeh op de meest doortastende wijze te voeren." After repulsing the [[Night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit (22 November 1875)|night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit]] ([[22 November]]) and the enemy attack along the entire *ooster-linie* ([[26 November]]), and after receiving reinforcements — the [[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|bataljon Barissans]] on [[10 December]] and the 9th battalion on [[21 December]] [[1875]] — Pel launched on [[26 December]] [[1875]] the *drie-colonne* offensive. Three *colonnes* in *verbond* moved against the VI, IV and IX Moekims: [[Majoor Vetter|Vetter]] *bezette* Mibouw; F. J. E. Mekern took the stelling between Mibouw and the Kroeng Daroe; [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|lt.-kol. Engel]] advanced along the right bank of the Kroeng Daroe.

## Mortal Wounding and the Offensive Continued

Mekern was *doodelijk gewond* on the very day of the opening movement, [[26 December]] [[1875]]. His death did not halt the offensive. By the next day [[Ketapang Doewa]] — bitterly fought over on [[15 February]] [[1874]] — had fallen "andermaal en nu voorgoed" into Dutch hands. On [[28 December]] [[1875]] Engel *bezette* [[Pakan Badak]], and the kampongs Lamtermin and Bital were *ontruimd* by the enemy, as were [[Toekoe Nanta]]'s *geduchte versterkingen* west of [[Sinangri]]. "Nergends hadden de Atjehneezen ernstigen tegenstand geboden," Borel records. Mekern's brother (or relative) [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|B. E. Mekern]] would command the *rechter-colonne* at the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|three-colonne operation against the III Moekims]] of [[13 February]] [[1876]], and would be the officer who, on hearing of the catastrophe, changed route, marched on Atoch, *zuiverde* the terrain, and recovered the bodies of [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]]'s massacred detachment.

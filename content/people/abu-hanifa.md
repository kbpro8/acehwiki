**Abū Ḥanīfa** — al-Nuʿmān b. Thābit (d. 150 H / 767 C.E.) — was the eponymous founder of the Ḥanafī school of Sunni law and one of the principal authorities of Sunni *kalām*. He is named in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] as one of the authors to whom the schematic overview of the seventy-two Islamic sects — the *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] — is sometimes attributed. The attribution is, as [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] records, one of the popular features of the tract, which belongs more to popular than to learned literature.

## The attribution of the *Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*

The *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] — the schematic overview of the seventy-two Islamic sects that is one of the principal sources of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] — is attributed in various manuscripts to various authors, sometimes to Abū Ḥanīfa. The attribution is recorded by Voorhoeve in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition: among the Arabic manuscripts of the *Maʿrifat al-madhāhib* listed by Voorhoeve, those at Calcutta (Asiatic Society of Bengal, Ivanow 1726), Rāmpūr (cat. I, 322 no. 305b; cat. I, 716 no. 11), and Leningrad (A. M. Buch. 957) name Abū Ḥanīfa as author. The Moluccan manuscript of Leiden Cod. Or. 1255 — the principal witness to the textual tradition from which Raniri drew — gives in its colophon the attribution "Abū Ḥanīfa" (with the misspelling "Abū Ḥanīfifa" in the Leiden manuscript).

## The *al-Fiqh al-akbar*

The *[[al-Fikh al-akbar|al-Fiqh al-akbar*]] — attributed to Abū Ḥanīfa — is one of the principal texts of early Sunni *kalām*. The tract *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] adds, in some manuscripts, one further sect derived from the *al-Fiqh al-akbar*: the *Jismiyya* or *Mujassimiyya*. The attribution of this addition to the *al-Fiqh al-akbar* is recorded by Voorhoeve in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition.

## Significance

Abū Ḥanīfa is one of the principal authorities cited (or attributed) in the textual tradition of the *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] — the schematic overview of the seventy-two Islamic sects that is one of the principal sources of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri. The attribution of the tract to Abū Ḥanīfa in various manuscripts — including the Moluccan manuscript of Leiden Cod. Or. 1255 — is one of the popular features of the tract, and is a document of the popular diffusion of the scholastic classification of Muslim heresies in the Indian Ocean world.

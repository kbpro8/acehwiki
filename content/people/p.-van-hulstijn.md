**P. van Hulstijn** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Korte Verklaring]]

[[P. van Hulstijn|Van Hulstijn]] summarised its double effect: it was a formula "which on the one hand guaranteed a state of affairs equivalent to the introduction of direct rule, yet moderated the consequences of our intervention, and on the other offered an unsought opportunity to leave a great part of the administrative task in the hands of the existing native organisations."

### From [[1926]]

In **[[1926]]** the great [[Teukoe Keumangan]] of [[Seunagan]] acknowledged to [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] that [[T. Radja Tampo']] had indeed been on the *pasar* at [[Djeuram]], but that an *[[oelèëbalang]]* who betrayed so holy a figure "could not remain a territorial chief in Seunagan". Du Croo also watched in [[1926]] a *toean* of the Swart school travel through his old district after ten years, to which the Acehnese "streamed together from far and near". [[P. van Hulstijn|Dr. P. van Hulstijn]]'s *Van Heutsz en de Buitengewesten* appeared in [[1926]]. The fright caused by the resistance of [[1925]]/1926 became the cause of the eventual construction of through roads and of the connecting roads between the north, east and west coasts.

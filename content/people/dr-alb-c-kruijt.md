**Dr. Alb. C. Kruijt** was a Dutch colonial-era author and scholar credited in the source document *DLP-84-Atjeh* with the biographical sketch of the *soldier-pastor* (Dutch: *soldaten-pastoor*) tradition in [[Atjeh|Aceh]], whose narrative combined personal observation with archival material to document the lives of the Catholic military chaplain [[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] and the Ambonese Protestant *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] during the closing decades of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]]. His account places the two chaplains at the centre of the spiritual care of the [[Indische Leger|Royal Netherlands East Indies Army (Dutch: *Indische Leger*)]] in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], and emphasises their devotion to both God and the Dutch crown (Dutch: *God en Koningin*). Kruijt's work thus occupies a small but telling place in the historiography of colonial military religion in [[Aceh|Atjeh]].

## Contribution to Acehnese Historiography

Kruijt's narrative, written in Dutch, combines personal observation with archival material to offer a sustained insight into the role of Christian clergy within the colonial military structure of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The sketch pairs the Catholic *soldaten-pastoor* [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]] — a Dutch Jesuit missionary who served for nearly thirty years on the war-footing of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir]] — with the Ambonese Protestant *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* (Dutch: indigenous religion teacher) [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]], who accepted the post at [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] in [[1894]] and remained there until his death in [[1937]]. The pairing is held up as the model of how a colonial garrison's spiritual care (Dutch: *geestelijke verzorging*) was actually done: not by the rulebook, but by two men of different confessions who recognised each other's authority and shared the work without disputing sacraments and doctrine. Kruijt's narrative is therefore a primary witness to the confessional accommodation that underpinned the moral life of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] garrison at [[Koeta-Radja|Kotta-Radja]] and along the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram postenlinie]].

## The Soldier-Pastor as a Type

Kruijt's biographical sketch is significant for codifying the type of the *soldaten-pastoor* — the chaplain whose ministry was exercised not from a presbytery but on the march, in the field hospital (Dutch: *militair hospitaal*), and at the bedside of the dying. The type is exemplified by [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]]'s years of accompanying the troops on field service along the [[Atjeh-tram|postenlinie]] chain, through ambush-prone jungle, and on the slow ox-cart convoys (Dutch: *ossenkarren*) that supplied the forward posts; and by [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]]'s parallel work at the military hospital of [[Pante Perak|Panteh Perak]] and at the school for soldiers' children that he established within the Dutch colonial fort of [[Peunajong|Peunajong]]. The sketch, by presenting the two chaplains as a paired type, fixes them in the colonial memory as the moral counterweight to the violence of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the subsequent [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification]] under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. Kruijt's narrative belongs to the wider body of colonial missionary historiography that shaped the post-war memory of the Atjeh campaign in the Netherlands.

**Pastoor Verbraak** was the Catholic military chaplain of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — the *Soldaten-Pastoor* — who, alongside the Ambonese *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]] (the *Soldaten-Dominee*), served as the geestelijk verzorger of the [[K.N.I.L.]] troops during the [[Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] era, accompanying expeditions and visiting the wounded in the Militair Hospitaal of [[Koeta-Radja]]. His partnership with Thenu is held up in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the model of how a colonial garrison's spiritual care was actually done — not by the book, but by two men of different confessions who recognised each other's gezag and shared the work without disputing sacramenten en geloofsleer.

## The Soldaten-Pastoor and the Soldaten-Dominee

"Dat was de Soldaten-Pastoor geweest, Thenu was de Soldaten-Dominee." The pairing is the book's. Both titles were, strictly, "volgens het boekje volkomen fout" — Thenu was technically only an *Inlands godsdienstleraar*, and Verbraak's formal title was military chaplain — but "een ieder kende hem die erenaam volledig toe." The two men worked as a pair: in the Van Heutsz time both went on expedities with the troops and were the most faithful visitors of the Militair Hospitaal.

## The sharing of the work

When one was at one place and the other elsewhere, "de een kwam dan bij de kudde van de ander, wanneer die afwezig was." Each gave his strengthening word to the other's flock — and did so without discussing sacramenten en geloofsleer. The book is explicit about the tone: the conversation was simply that of "een edel en hoogstaand mens." The model is offered as the colonial army at its best — the confessional division of the rulebook silently set aside by two men who recognised that a dying Javanese Catholic and a dying Ambonese Protestant needed the same thing from the man who came to their bed.

## Significance

Verbraak's significance in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is as the second half of the Thenu-Verbraak pair: the Catholic complement to the Ambonese Protestant dominee. Together they embody the book's argument that the [[K.N.I.L.]] in Aceh was held together less by regulation than by the patient daily work of men whose gezag ran ahead of and outside the rulebook. The fact that Verbraak's first name is not even given — that he is "Pastoor Verbraak" throughout — is itself a mark of the book's treatment: he is the type of the Soldaten-Pastoor, paired with the type of the Soldaten-Dominee.

**De Scheemaker** was a Dutch *assistent-resident* (assistant resident) of the Netherlands Indies administration, appointed in [[1876]] as the first civil officer charged with conducting political affairs on the north coast of [[Atjeh]], with his seat at the new Dutch establishment at [[Pedir|Pidir]].

## Appointment

Until 1876 political affairs on Atjeh's north coast had been entrusted to the commander of the war squadron on that coast. When the submission of [[Toengkoe Radja Pakik]] was converted into occupation, the civil and military commander in Atjeh proposed and the Netherlands Indies Government decided that at the capital of Pidir an assistant-resident should be stationed for the direction of political affairs of the whole coast. De Scheemaker went with a staff of one or more controllers destined for the service in Atjeh and dependencies, native clerks, a European clerk and 5 police servants; for his disposal were one armed boat — stationed in the mouth of the Pidir river for the control of imports — and one sloop's crew for communication with the roadstead. He arrived and took over the civil charge from the naval station commander a few days after the completion of the fortification, at the end of June [[1876]].

His house stood at 200 to 300 metres from the fortification by the beach, with a watch whose strength was raised to 22 men in July after rumours of a projected attack on the fortification and later settled at 16. On [[19 August]] he was charged with informing the princes of the other north-coast states — [[Endjoeng]] and [[Ajer Leboe]] with special warning — of the meaning of the [[Cancellation of friendship with Gigiëng (19 August 1876)|breach with Gigiëng]]. These events are recorded by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]).

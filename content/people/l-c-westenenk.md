**L. C. Westenenk** was a Dutch colonial official and ethnographer whose writings on the Sumatran interior — including Minangkabau studies and a [[1922]] article on "Rëntjong-schrift, II, Beschreven hoorns in het landschap Krintji" published in the *Tijdschrift van het Bataviaasch Genootschap* — documented the [[rëntjong-schrift]] (rencong / surat ulú) script family that includes the pre-Islamic writing systems of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], the [[Gajoland|Gayo]]-[[Alasland|Alas]] highlands, [[Krintji|Kerinci]] and the [[Rejang|Rejang]] country, and specifically the practice of inscribing buffalo horns as a documentation medium. Westenenk's work is cited in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] in connection with the Minangkabau buffalo-fight founding legend (where his *Land en volk van Minangkabau* and *Opstellen over Minangkabau* are listed alongside [[E. Dulaurier]] and [[A. Marre]] as authorities for the textual tradition), and his [[1922]] rëntjong-schrift article is listed in the *Lijst van geschriften* (bibliography) of *De Karbouw* (p. 268).

## The Rëntjong-schrift article (1922)

Westenenk's [[1922]] article "Rëntjong-schrift, II, Beschreven hoorns in het landschap Krintji," published in the *Tijdschrift van het Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen*, treated the pre-Islamic Sumatran script family — known variously as *rëntjong*, *rencong*, *ulú*, or *surat ulú* — that was used before the adoption of the Arabic-derived *jawi* script in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the adjacent highlands of central and southern Sumatra. The article's specific subject was the practice, attested in the [[Krintji|Kerinci]] landschap of interior Sumatra, of inscribing buffalo horns with this script — a documentation medium that survives in scattered specimens and that links the material culture of the buffalo to the literary culture of pre-Islamic Sumatra. The script family itself extends across the highland belt from [[Acheh|Atjeh]] through [[Gajoland|Gayo]]-[[Alasland|Alas]] to [[Krintji|Kerinci]] and the [[Rejang|Rejang]] country of south-western Sumatra, and is one of the principal evidences of a pre-Islamic Sumatran literate culture that was, in Atjeh itself, eventually displaced by Arabic *jawi* and by the European script.

## Minangkabau studies

Westenenk also wrote on Minangkabau — *Land en volk van Minangkabau* and *Opstellen over Minangkabau* — works cited in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s *De Karbouw* (p. 209, footnote) in the list of authorities for the Minangkabau buffalo-fight founding legend. The legend, in which the political fate of the Minangkabau country was decided by a single combat between a Javanese buffalo (a large, fierce beast) and a Minangkabau buffalo (a starved calf with iron spikes attached to its head, which the Javanese buffalo mistook for its mother and was killed by when it pressed its head into the calf's chest seeking milk), is attested in the [[Hikajat Radja-radja Pasei|Pasai chronicle]] and in a number of Sumatran and Javanese historical-literary texts, and Westenenk's Minangkabau writings represent the early-twentieth-century Dutch ethnographic documentation of it.

## Significance

Westenenk's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is twofold. First, his [[1922]] *rëntjong-schrift* article documented the pre-Islamic script family that included Atjeh's own rencong/ulú script, and specifically the practice of inscribing buffalo horns as a recording medium — a connection between the material culture of the buffalo and the literate culture of pre-Islamic Sumatra that is otherwise poorly attested. Second, his Minangkabau writings supplied early-twentieth-century ethnographic authority for the Minangkabau buffalo-fight founding legend, a text central to the comparative historical-literary tradition of Sumatra and the adjacent archipelago.

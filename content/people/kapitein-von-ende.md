**Captain von Ende** was a Dutch *kapitein* of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] and *commandant* of the [[Mandarsa Poeti]] post, who on his own initiative — *zonder toestemming* — slipped across the *sawah* on the night of [[14 February|14]]–[[15 February]] [[1875]] with part of the Mandarsa Poeti garrison and occupied the abandoned enemy fortification at [[Lamara Olejlo]] in the [[Surprise of Lamara Olejlo (14–15 February 1875)|surprise of Lamara Olejlo]], and who from [[Generaal Pel|Pel]] received, for the same action, both formal praise for his courage and a reprimand for his unauthorised initiative — "he had fully deserved both; achieved success made everything good."

## The Surprise of Lamara Olejlo

As *commandant* of Mandarsa Poeti, the old stone *gebedenhuis* some 1 600 paces south of the main wall that had been destroyed by 12 c.M. fire on [[5 October]] [[1874]] and occupied as a post on [[28 January]] [[1875]], von Ende had observed that the Atjehneezen left the fortification at the northern rand of [[Lamara]] each evening and returned in the morning. He *verified* the pattern personally by creeping across the *sawah* on a dark night with a few men. Without asking permission, he crossed the *sawah* in the night of [[14 February|14]]–[[15 February]] [[1875]] with part of the Mandarsa Poeti garrison and occupied the abandoned fortification. He at once reported to Pel and asked for reinforcements. Between eight and four o'clock the Atjehneezen arrived as usual — to considerable surprise they were met with [[Beaumont-geweer|Beaumont salvoes]]. Von Ende beat off their attempt to retake the works and held with his handful until reinforcements under overste van Dentekom arrived, then [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]]. Fighting continued all morning as the enemy streamed in from all sides.

## Praise and Reprimand

The whole Lamara Olejlo stelling remained permanently in Dutch hands. Three or four enemy *bentengs*, several cannons and *lillas* fell into Dutch hands — including a four-pounder that had fired on Mandarsa Poeti, which had no carriage but was ingeniously suspended on ropes. Von Ende received from Pel both formal praise for his courage and a reprimand for his unauthorised action. Borel's comment is that "he had fully deserved both; achieved success made everything good." The cost was one dead, some twelve severely wounded, and two officers wounded after the fight was already over.

## Borel's Judgement

Von Ende is, in [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s narrative, the prototype of the dashing Indische *kapitein* whose personal reconnaissance and personal initiative could achieve at no cost what a formal *colonne* operation might have purchased in blood. The contrast with the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|disaster at Longbatta]] six weeks earlier — where [[Majoor van der Meer|van der Meer]]'s misinterpreted orders and loss of cohesion on the *sawah* had cost a battalion — is sharp. Pel's simultaneous praise and reprimand is, in Borel's reading, the right response of a *bevelhebber* who values initiative but cannot publicly endorse its breach of discipline.

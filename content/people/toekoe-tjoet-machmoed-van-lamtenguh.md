**Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed van Lamtenguh** was an [[Achehnese|Atjehnese]] *[[Uleebalang|oeloebalang]]* of [[Lamtenguh]] — a *kampong* of the [[XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — who was entrusted by the Dutch with the safety of the terrain between the Dutch temporary post at [[Anagaloeng]] and the *blokhuis* of [[Blang Tjoet]] during the [[1896]] crisis. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as the half-brother of [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]], *oeloebalang* of the [[VII Moekims]], to whose command the Anagaloeng sector was reassigned after Baid himself "had played a double role" (*een dubbelzinnige rol gespeeld*).

## The 1896 context

In the early months of [[1896]], as the Dutch temporary outposts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] were being besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] following his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection from the Dutch alliance]], the security of the lines of communication between the outposts became a question of first importance. The Dutch had constructed a chain of *blokhuizen* (blockhouses) between Anagaloeng and the geconcentreerde linie, and the safety of the terrain between these *blokhuizen* was entrusted to allied Atjehnese *oeloebalang*s rather than to Dutch garrisons. Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed, as *oeloebalang* of Lamtenguh, was given responsibility for the sector between Anagaloeng and the *blokhuis* of [[Blang Tjoet]].

## The half-brother relationship and the "double role"

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] notes that Machmoed's assignment to the Anagaloeng sector was a consequence of the unreliability of his half-brother [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]]. The *oeloebalang* of the VII Moekims had, in the Dutch reading, played a *dubbelzinnige rol* — an ambiguous or double role — and could no longer be trusted with the defence of a sector so close to the Dutch perimeter. The transfer of the command to Machmoed was thus both a practical military measure and a political signal: it indicated that the Dutch were prepared to redistribute responsibilities within the kinship networks of the *oeloebalang* class when individual members proved unreliable. The same kinship logic had earlier been used against the Dutch, when the marriage alliances of [[Toekoe Oemar]] had extended his influence across the mukims of the Westkust.

## Significance

Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed's principal significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as an illustration of the way in which the Dutch, in the late *concentratie* period, sought to manage *oeloebalang* reliability by playing on the kinship structure of the Atjehnese aristocracy. His appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] is brief — a single clause in the narrative of the Anagaloeng siege — but it preserves a detail of the Dutch-*oeloebalang* alliance system that is otherwise difficult to recover.

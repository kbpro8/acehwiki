**Jan Grenier** was a Dutch vice-admiraal in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] who, as vice-admiraal of the fleet of [[Jacob van Neck|Jacob van Neck]] that left Texel on [[24 April]] [[1601]], arrived off [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on [[15 December]] [[1601]] in his single detached ship, witnessed the great fire of [[13 January]] [[13 January]] [[1602]] that destroyed some 200 houses and the Zeeland lodge "Zeelant", and sailed on [[19 February]] [[19 February]] [[1602]] to [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] — where he could buy no pepper because trade with foreigners in the west-coast ports was forbidden except at Atjeh — and then to [[Bantam|Bantam]]. Grenier is the principal Dutch witness of the destruction of the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil in Van der Woude's chapter "Atjeh. Djohor. Malaka." in his [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The detached arrival (December 1601)

Grenier was vice-admiraal of the [[Jacob van Neck|Van Neck]] fleet that left Texel on [[24 April]] [[1601]]. He became separated from the other ships — "van de andere schepen afgedwaald" — which by contrary wind could not reach Atjeh, "welke plaats zij ingevolge hun instructie gezamenlijk moesten aandoen". Grenier alone arrived off Atjeh on [[15 December]] [[1601]], some four months after the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]] had departed. He loaded a small quantity of pepper — "een klein quantum peper" — and waited.

## The fire of 13 January 1602

On [[13 January]] [[1602]] a great fire broke out in Atjeh, destroying some 200 houses; the Zeeland lodge "Zeelant" that the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland Company's mission of August 1601]] had founded burned down with the rest, "evenwel met weinig schade". Grenier witnessed the fire — the principal Dutch witness of the destruction of the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil. The fire marked the end of the Zeeland lodge "Zeelant" and the beginning of the VOC's preference for operating at Atjeh through visiting fleets rather than a permanent factory.

## The 1602 Tikoe visit and the Westkust closure

After the fire, Grenier sailed on [[19 February]] [[1602]] to [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] on the west coast of Sumatra, "waar hij geen lading opdeed omdat toen de handel met vreemdelingen in de kustplaatsen verboden was, uitgezonderd in Atjeh". The closure was the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s policy: he sought to centralise the European pepper trade at Atjeh, where he could control it through the sabander and the shore batteries. The policy was largely effective in the early Dutch-Indies period. Grenier then sailed for [[Bantam|Bantam]].

## Significance

Jan Grenier is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch witness of the destruction of the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil — the Zeeland lodge "Zeelant" — in the fire of [[13 January]] [[1602]]. The fire marked the end of the Zeeland lodge "Zeelant" and the beginning of the VOC's preference for operating at Atjeh through visiting fleets rather than a permanent factory. Grenier's subsequent visit to [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] on [[19 February]] [[1602]] — and his failure to buy pepper there because of the sultan's policy of centralising the European pepper trade at Atjeh — was the principal Dutch encounter with the sultan's commercial policy and the proximate cause of the Dutch decision to operate at Atjeh through visiting fleets rather than a permanent factory. Grenier's role in the Dutch-Atjeh relationship frames the transition from the pre-VOC period of separate Dutch factories to the VOC period of centralised commercial operations through visiting fleets.

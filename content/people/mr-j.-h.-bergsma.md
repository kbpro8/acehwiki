**Mr J. H. Bergsma** — Johannes Hermannus Bergsma ([[1832]]-[[1887]]) — was a Dutch liberal politician who served as minister of Colonies ([[1897]]-[[1901]]) in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]-predecessor cabinets. He is, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s retrospective, the *ethische politiek* minister who "nadrukkelijk niet wilde poseren als hervormer" — emphatically did not wish to pose as a reformer — and who has accordingly "vergausd en thans vergeten" — been vilified and is now forgotten. Yet his scepticism about the *ethische politiek* is, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s judgement, itself a form of ethics.

## The Bergsma question

[[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] preserves [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s answer to the *ethische politiek* consensus in the form of a thought-experiment that he posed to the [[Tweede Kamer]]:

> "Als men aan 50.000 Europeanen en aan 20 millioen inlanders vroeg, of zij gemeentelijke raden en gewestelijke raden wensen te hebben [zouden] van de Europeanen misschien een honderdtal die vraag met ja beantwoorden en [zouden] die 20 millioen inlanders zeggen: 'och, geef ons geen nieuwe bevelen, zorg er voor dat alles wat wij verdienen in onze handen komt, beschik niet te veel over onze tijd, laat ons werken voor ons zelven, leg ons geen te hoge belastingen op; wij willen wat billijk en nodig is gaarne betalen, mits de een niet meer moet opbrengen dan de ander, en laat ons dan verder ons zelf kalm ontwikkelen en tot rust komen. Dat zijn de wensen van de inlander'."

— If one were to ask 50,000 Europeans and 20 million *inlanders* whether they wished to have municipal councils and regional councils, perhaps a hundred of the Europeans would answer the question with yes, and those 20 million *inlanders* would say: 'oh, give us no new orders, see to it that all we earn comes into our hands, do not dispose too much of our time, let us work for ourselves, do not impose too high taxes on us; we will gladly pay what is fair and necessary, provided that one is not required to contribute more than another, and let us then further calmly develop ourselves and come to rest. Those are the wishes of the *inlander*.'

## Significance

[[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s thought-experiment is, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s retrospective, the type-specimen of the *ethische politiek* counter-argument from indigenous indifference. It places [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] in the company of the lesser-known but morally alert colonial politicians — the "vergseealsookte en thans vergeten figuren" — whose scepticism about the uplift agenda was, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s verdict, itself a form of ethical engagement.

## On Atjeh

[[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s ministry covered the [[1897]]-[[1901]] period — the immediate pre-[[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|Oemar-defection]] years and the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|post-concentratie]] recovery. The [[Troonrede of 1901|troonrede]] of [[1901]] that officially proclaimed the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical policy]] was, in part, a judgement on the comparative restraint of the [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] years.

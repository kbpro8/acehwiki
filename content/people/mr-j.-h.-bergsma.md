**Mr J. H. Bergsma** — Johannes Hermannus Bergsma ([[1832]]-[[1887]]) — was a Dutch liberal politician who served as minister of Colonies ([[1897]]-[[1901]]) in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]-predecessor cabinets. He is, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s retrospective, the *ethische politiek* minister who "nadrukkelijk niet wilde poseren als hervormer" — emphatically did not wish to pose as a reformer — and who has accordingly "vergausd en thans vergeten" — been vilified and is now forgotten. Yet his scepticism about the *ethische politiek* is, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s judgement, itself a form of ethics.

**Mr. J. H. Bergsma** was Minister of Colonies of the [[Netherlands]] immediately before [[J. T. Cremer|J. T. Cremer]], and appears in the record of the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[1897]] in two connections: as the minister who admitted that the cost of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] could not be stated, and as the author of the government's statement against mutilation of [[22 September]] [[1896]].

## The Bergsma question

[[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] preserves [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s answer to the *ethische politiek* consensus in the form of a thought-experiment that he posed to the [[Tweede Kamer]]:

> "Als men aan 50.000 Europeanen en aan 20 millioen inlanders vroeg, of zij gemeentelijke raden en gewestelijke raden wensen te hebben [zouden] van de Europeanen misschien een honderdtal die vraag met ja beantwoorden en [zouden] die 20 millioen inlanders zeggen: 'och, geef ons geen nieuwe bevelen, zorg er voor dat alles wat wij verdienen in onze handen komt, beschik niet te veel over onze tijd, laat ons werken voor ons zelven, leg ons geen te hoge belastingen op; wij willen wat billijk en nodig is gaarne betalen, mits de een niet meer moet opbrengen dan de ander, en laat ons dan verder ons zelf kalm ontwikkelen en tot rust komen. Dat zijn de wensen van de inlander'."

— If one were to ask 50,000 Europeans and 20 million *inlanders* whether they wished to have municipal councils and regional councils, perhaps a hundred of the Europeans would answer the question with yes, and those 20 million *inlanders* would say: 'oh, give us no new orders, see to it that all we earn comes into our hands, do not dispose too much of our time, let us work for ourselves, do not impose too high taxes on us; we will gladly pay what is fair and necessary, provided that one is not required to contribute more than another, and let us then further calmly develop ourselves and come to rest. Those are the wishes of the *inlander*.'

## Significance

[[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s thought-experiment is, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s retrospective, the type-specimen of the *ethische politiek* counter-argument from indigenous indifference. It places [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] in the company of the lesser-known but morally alert colonial politicians — the "vergseealsookte en thans vergeten figuren" — whose scepticism about the uplift agenda was, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s verdict, itself a form of ethical engagement.

## On Atjeh

[[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s ministry covered the [[1897]]-[[1901]] period — the immediate pre-[[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|Oemar-defection]] years and the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|post-concentratie]] recovery. The [[Troonrede of 1901|troonrede]] of [[1901]] that officially proclaimed the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical policy]] was, in part, a judgement on the comparative restraint of the [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] years.

## The admission on the cost of the war

[[H. van Kol]] told the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]] that with so extensive a bureaucracy "moest de minister Bergsma nog slechts 5 maanden geleden verklaren, dat men nòch bij het [[Ministerie van Koloniën|Ministerie van Koloniën]], nòch in Indië met juistheid kan opgeven hoeveel gelden speciaal voor Atjeh zijn uitgegeven." — the minister Bergsma had, only five months ago, to declare that neither at the Ministry of Colonies nor in the Indies can one state with precision how much money has been spent specifically on Atjeh. Van Kol thought it very desirable that the sum should once be accurately calculated so far as possible, "als een ernstige waarschuwing voor volgende geslachten, en voor alle Regeeringen, die nog door koloniale veroveringszucht worden bezield." — as a serious warning for following generations, and for all Governments that are still animated by colonial lust of conquest.

## The statement on mutilation of [[22 September]] [[1896]]

On [[17 November]] [[1897]] [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]], answering the charges of cruelty, quoted with full endorsement the words with which Bergsma had closed an extensive argument on [[22 September]] [[1896]] in answer to a question of [[Goeman Borgesius]]:

"Eens blijkt echter voldoende uit de gewisselde telegrammen, dat de Nederlandsche en de Indische regeering, beiden ten sterkste gekant zijn tegen het mutileeren, gelijk het terecht algemeen is afgekeurd. Mocht zich dit enkele geval hebben voorgedaan, hetgeen bij een leger uit verschillende elementen bestaande niet geheel onverklaarbaar is, dan zal zulks zeker niet meer gebeuren. De [[Governor-General of Netherlands India|Gouverneur-Generaal]] heeft het ten strengste verboden."

Cremer added: "Ik heb er niets aan toe te voegen; de tegenwoordige Regeering onderschrijft ten volle het toen gesprokene." — I have nothing to add to it; the present Government fully subscribes to what was then said.

## Continuity of policy

Cremer described himself in the same debate as the executor of a Royal authorisation obtained on the proposal of his predecessor, by which — in accordance with the proposals of the [[Governor-General of Netherlands India|Governor-General]] — a definite plan of action towards [[Acheh|Atjeh]] had been adopted, mentioned on page 7 of the [[Koloniaal Verslag]]. He also stated that fourteen successive Ministers of Colonies, thirteen predecessors and himself, had borne the responsibility for the war in its various phases, each for his own phase.

[[Bahlmann]] told the chamber that the chief reason the war had lasted so long was that at the accession of every new [[Governor-General of Netherlands India|Governor-General]] or new cabinet the system was changed, and that the appointment of Cremer was for him a guarantee that no such change would now occur.

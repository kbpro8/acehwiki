**Mr. Dr. Baron R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg** was a Dutch jurist and ornithologist who, with Ir. Jhr. [[F. C. van Heurn]], published the paper *[[Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh]]* on a collection of bird skins from [[Oostkust van Atjeh|East Atjeh]], and earlier, with the same collaborator, *Avifaunistische Studiën in de Gajo-landen* in the *Jaarberichten* of the [[Koloniaal Instituut]], no. II, [[1921]].

The [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oost-Atjeh]] paper describes material collected on the estates [[Aloer Djamboe]] in [[Tamiang]] and [[Alas Peurba]] south of [[Lho Seumawe]], and opens with the authors' argument that the richest field for tropical avifaunal collecting is the virgin primeval forest, since the typical forest species disappear immediately when the forest gives way to cultivation, while the birds of open marsh and cultivated country are already widely distributed and better known. The paper illustrates the argument from the Sumatran doves, from the common grey *[[Streptopelia suratensis tigrina]]* — so well adapted to cultivation that the birds were repeatedly run over by motor cars — to the shy *[[Poenei djamboe]]* (*Ptilinopus jambu*), which had to be sought in or near the original forest and of which the authors quote [[William Marsden]]'s description of [[1784]].

**Dr. Julius Jacobs** was a Dutch colonial physician and ethnographer, author of *[[Het familie- en kampongleven op Groot-Atjeh|Het familie- en kampongleven op Groot-Atjeh]]* — *Family and Kampong Life in Groot-Atjeh* — published in [[1894]]. The book is one of the principal pre-war sources for the social institutions of [[Groot-Atjeh]]: the *gampong* and *mukim* organisation, the household economy, and the legal condition of women, children, lepers and slaves.

## The Leprosy Account
Broersma cites Jacobs's account of the lepers in Groot-Atjeh in his chapter on the leper colony at [[Bajan|Bajan]]. Jacobs had recorded that *'de lijders in Groot-Atjeh onverbiddelijk werden verstooten'* — the sufferers in Groot-Atjeh were inexorably banished — and that they might withdraw to a particular kampong with sulphurous hot springs. The leper's immovable property fell to his family, and his wife was reckoned legally divorced from him. The leper-kampong thus formed *'een kleine maatschappij van rampzaligen'* — a small society of the wretched — in which, so far as the sufferers were able, fields, crafts and fishery were kept up. Jacobs noted that leprosy was then less frequent in Groot-Atjeh than on the eastern coast, where [[Pidië]] and the [[Pasé]]-streek were worse afflicted.

## The Pepper Harvest of 1893
Broersma's second use of Jacobs is for the pepper-harvest of [[1893]], when *'geen peper van beteekenis werd geoogst'* — no pepper of consequence was harvested — an item in the long wave of boom-and-bust that Broersma traces from [[J. A. Kruyt|Kruyt]]'s [[1873]] figure down to the disease-year of [[1918]].

## Significance
Jacobs's *Het familie- en kampongleven op Groot-Atjeh* is regularly cited alongside [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *Atjèh* for the ethnography of the period before the Dutch military consolidation, and is the principal medical-ethnographic source for the condition of lepers in pre-pacification Atjeh.

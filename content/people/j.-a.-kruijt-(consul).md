**J. A. Kruijt** — *Consul Kruijt* — was the Dutch consul at [[Djidda|Djidda] in the 1880s, who introduced the young scholar [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] at Djidda in [[August 1884|August]] [[1884]] and so enabled Snouck Hurgronje's [[Snouck Hurgronje's Mekka journey (1884-1885)|Mekka journey]] of [[1884]]–[[1885]], and who later, having been transferred to the consulship at [[Pinang|Pinang]], met Snouck Hurgronje again at Pinang in [[1889]] and delivered to him the Buitenzorg message of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1889]] that frustrated his Atjèh travel plan. The consul Kruijt is one of the recurrent figures of Snouck Hurgronje's early career — at Djidda in [[1884]]–[[1885]], and at Pinang in [[1889]] — and is the principal Dutch diplomatic facilitator of Snouck Hurgronje's Mekka journey.

## Note on Identity

The consul [[J. A. Kruijt (consul)|J. A. Kruijt]] of Djidda and Pinang, and the [[J. A. Kruijt|J. A. Kruijt]] who authored the [[De Atjeh-Oorlog (Kruijt, 1896)|brochure of 1896]] on the Atjeh-oorlog, are apparently different persons sharing the same name. The brochure author signs himself "*Oud Controleur-Generaal der Nederlanden te Pokkenhage*" (Former Controller-General of the Netherlands at Pokkenhage) — a senior rank in the Dutch colonial civil administration, distinct from the consular service. The shared name, however, suggests a possible family connection; the matter is not resolved by the present source.

## The Djidda Period (1884–1885)

When Snouck Hurgronje, having resolved to study Islam at its source, inquired of the secretary-general of foreign affairs at The Hague whether he might be attached to the consulate at Djidda, he was refused: in The Hague they had no ears for that. Then Snouck went to the consul Kruijt from Djidda, when that official was on leave at 's-Gravenhage; and that visit was a success: Kruijt would help the young scholar; he would introduce him at Djidda. Also Professor [[Van der Lith|Van der Lith]] at Leiden promised his help: he would, during Snouck's absence in Arabië, take over his courses at the Indische Instelling. The required leave was requested and obtained; and when the heer Kruijt returned to his unhealthy station, Snouck Hurgronje travelled with him. They arrived at Djidda on [[28 August]] [[28 August]] [[1884]].

Snouck Hurgronje stayed at Djidda a little half-year, and the heer Kruijt — the same Kruijt with whom he had travelled — was his patron and contact at the consulate. When the report that Dr. [[Huber|Huber]] had been murdered was received at Djidda, shortly before Snouck Hurgronje's arrival, France was not fully represented at Djidda. The vice-consul was on leave, and the first drogman — also a Frenchman — performed the office. Formerly, under similar circumstances, the Dutch consul Kruijt had often attended to the French interests; but now Kruijt too had been absent. Only in [[October 1884|October]] did the vice-consul [[De Lostalot|De Lostalot]] return to Djidda.

## The Pinang Period (1889)

Five years later, in [[1889]], Snouck Hurgronje — having received a special commission to go to Indië to study the institutions of Islam — sailed with a P & O boat to Pinang, with the intention of crossing from there, either as Abd al-Gaffār or as unofficial European, to the North Coast of Atjèh, avoiding the Dutch administrative posts. In Pinang, however, the Dutch consul Kruijt — the same Kruijt with whom he had been at Djidda four, five years earlier — came aboard to welcome the traveller, and handed him a Buitenzorg message of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1889]], that he must give up his plan to seek contact with Atjèh from Pinang; he had to travel directly to Batavia. The Buitenzorg message was the consequence of the advice of the *assistent-resident* [[Goossens|Goossens]] of Groot-Atjèh, who had persuaded the governor [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] that the visit would awaken distrust and jeopardise the pending submission of the Keumala-party.

## The Pinang Conversation

Snouck Hurgronje stayed a week or two at Pinang, where [[Maxwell|Maxwell]] was resident, with whom there was to be talked about the Atjèhers in the Straits and about his experiences on the West Coast of Atjèh six years earlier, when he had visited it in connection with the captivity by the *radja* of [[Teunom|Teunom]] of the crew of the English steamship *[[Nisero|Nisero]]* that had stranded at [[Panga|Panga]] and had been plundered and wrecked. From there Snouck travelled to [[Singapore|Singapore]], where he saw the Dutch consul [[Lavino|Lavino]], who did good business with the Atjeh-oorlog through his supplies of coal, victuals, and engineer materials.

## Significance

The consul J. A. Kruijt is the principal Dutch diplomatic facilitator of Snouck Hurgronje's Mekka journey of [[1884]]–[[1885]], and the principal Dutch diplomatic agent of the frustration of Snouck Hurgronje's Atjèh travel plan of [[1889]]. The recurrent presence of Kruijt — at Djidda in [[1884]]–[[1885]] and at Pinang in [[1889]] — places him at two of the principal junctures of Snouck Hurgronje's early career. The shared name with the [[J. A. Kruijt|J. A. Kruijt]] who authored the [[De Atjeh-Oorlog (Kruijt, 1896)|brochure of 1896]] on the Atjeh-oorlog suggests a possible family connection; the matter is not resolved by the present source.

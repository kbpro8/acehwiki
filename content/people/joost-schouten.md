**Joost Schouten** was a Dutch envoy of the [[Dutch East India Company]] (VOC) who negotiated the [[Dutch–Aceh Treaty of 1641]] with the newly enthroned [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Sultane of Aceh]] in February [[1641]]. His successful mission secured the Company crucial trading concessions on the West Coast of Sumatra, including toll reductions in pepper ports and the exclusion of other European nations.

## Mission to Aceh

Schouten was dispatched to Aceh shortly after Tajul-alam's accession. He was able to win her favour and conclude a treaty that gave the VOC free access to the pepper ports of [[Tikoe]], [[Priaman]], [[Padang]], and [[Indrapoera]]. This was a significant diplomatic achievement, as Aceh was a powerful independent state that had previously been hostile to Dutch merchants.

## Significance

Schouten's treaty laid the groundwork for later agreements ([[Dutch–Aceh Treaty of 1649|1649]], [[Treaty of 1650 on Perak Tin|1650]]) that would expand VOC privileges. His negotiations are notable for their tact and for securing commercial advantage without military confrontation. His role illustrates the importance of diplomacy in the VOC's expansion in Southeast Asia.

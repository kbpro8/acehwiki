**Sultan Jauhar Alain Shah** (also styled **Alaedin Jauhar al-Alam Shah**) was an Achehnese sultan who reigned from [[1802]] to [[1824]]. His name is one of the eight that appear on the *chab sikureu'eng*, the [[Sarakata|ninefold seal]] placed at the head of sultans' edicts, and his mediation (*afwah*) is invoked in the preamble of those documents.

The ninefold seal, as described by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]], comprises a large central circle containing the name of the reigning sultan, surrounded by eight smaller circles each containing the name of a celebrated predecessor. While the choice of the eight names rests with the reigning sultan, the names of the great sultans of the past and of the immediate predecessors of the reigning king are never omitted; great freedom is otherwise shown in selection. The four names that recur on virtually all such seals are [[Sultan Sayyidi al-Mukamnal]] (Alaedin al-Qahhar), [[Sultan Meukuta Alam]] (Iskandar Muda), [[Sultan Tajul-alam]] (Sapiatodin), and [[Sultan Ahmat Shah]].

Sultan Jauhar Alain Shah appears among the eight namesakes on the seal of the pretender-sultan [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Sultan Alaedin Muhamad Daud Shah Juhan]] (elected [[1879]]), and his mediation is explicitly invoked in the preamble of the letters patent (*sarakata*) which that sultan granted to [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] on 4 Rabi' al-Awwal 1307 H ([[1889]]). The same preamble invokes the mediation of [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Meukuta Alam Iskandar Muda]], [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam Sapiatodin]], [[Sultan Ahmat Shah|Alaedin Ahmat Shah]], [[Sultan Mahmut Shah|Alaedin Mahmut Shah]], [[Sultan Alaedin Muhamat Shah|Alaedin Muhamat Shah]], and [[Sultan Ibrahim Mansur Shah|Alaedin Manso Shah]].

Jauhar Alain Shah reigned during the late decadent period of the sultanate, when the effective authority of the port-kings was confined almost entirely to the [[Dalam]] (the Sultan's Court at [[Banda Acheh]]) and to a certain lordship over the neighbouring seas and harbours. He was succeeded by [[Sultan Alaedin Muhamat Shah]] ([[1824]]–[[1838]]) and ultimately by [[Sultan Ibrahim Mansur Shah|Sultan Mansur Shah]] ([[1838]]–[[1870]]).

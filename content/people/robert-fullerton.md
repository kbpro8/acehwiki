Robert Fullerton was a British East India Company official who served as Governor of [[Penang]] (Prince of Wales' Island) in the mid-[[1820]]s, in succession to [[W. E. Phillips]]. He is significant in the Achehnese record for two major minutes on [[Sumatra|Sumatran]] affairs: the [[Fullerton's minute on Acheh (21 March 1825)|minute on Acheh of 21 March 1825]] and the minute of [[16 March 1828]] on the projected [[Bellona mission to Sumatra's East Coast (1827-1828)|Dutch *Bellona* mission]], as well as for his [[Fullerton's letter to Sultan of Siak (7 February 1828)|letter to the Sultan of Siak]] of [[7 February 1828]] and his [[Fullerton's circular to nine Siak chiefs|circular to nine Siak chiefs]] of [[21 May 1828]].

## Governorship and the Acheh Question

Fullerton took up the governorship at a moment when the [[Acheh treaty of 1819]] was already an acknowledged [[dead letter]]. By the [[London dispatch of 4 August 1824]] the Court of Directors had observed that [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] had never possessed effective authority and that the treaty might «even now be fairly considered as a dead letter», while the [[Bengal inquiry to Penang (28 January 1825)|Bengal inquiry to Penang of 28 January 1825]] had requested the Penang government's advice on the possibility of replacing it with a purely commercial arrangement. Fullerton's reply, dated [[21 March 1825]] and concurred in by his council members [[W. A. Clubley|W. A. Clubley]] and [[R. Ibbetson|R. Ibbetson]] on [[7 April]], is printed as Appendix 10 of Van der Kemp's article. In it Fullerton observed that any attempt to establish European influence at Acheh without «completely subjugating the country» had proved «utterly impracticable», and warned that the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1824)]] placed the petty states of [[Deli]], [[Langkat]] and [[Siak]] — which carried on an extensive trade with Penang — at the mercy of any Dutch interference authorised by Article 2 of the treaty.

## The Said Akil Affair

Fullerton was governor during the latter part of [[Said Akil's Deli adventure (1824-1828)|Said Akil's adventure at Deli]]. After [[Sultan of Deli|the Sultan of Deli]] had written to Penang on [[27 August 1824]] complaining of Akil's behaviour and invoking the [[Acheh treaty of 1819]] (under which Deli was, he alleged, a dependency of Acheh), the [[Shahbandar of Deli|Shahbandar of Deli]] wrote again in February [[1828]] begging Fullerton to deliver the country from the «robber». Fullerton, after consulting the Malay translator [[John Anderson|John Anderson]], addressed a letter of [[27 August 1828]] to the Sultan, declaring that Penang «could not countenance any interference in the affairs of other countries by one of our own subjects» and that Said Akil, having proceeded to Deli contrary to the injunctions of the Penang government, was «placed without the protection of the British Government». The Bengal government, by dispatch of [[24 October 1828]], approved the measures Fullerton had taken.

## The Bellona Minute and the Siak Correspondence

In late [[1827]] Fullerton learned, through the former Singapore resident [[John Prince|John Prince]] at [[Batavia]], that [[Commissioner-General Du Bus|Commissioner-General Du Bus]] proposed to send the frigate *Bellona* to the ports of [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]] in order to induce the chiefs to take their salt from [[Riouw]] rather than from Singapore. In his minute of [[16 March 1828]] Fullerton observed that the Dutch would certainly use the exclusive right they claimed under the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1824)]] «to the full extent of their means», and that «the political influence so established will be rendered entirely subservient to the promotion of their own trade and deterioration of ours». To counter the design he had already, on [[7 February 1828]], addressed a letter from Singapore to [[Sultan of Siak|the Sultan of Siak]] expressing British anxiety to continue and promote the growing trade between Siak and the Straits settlements; and on [[21 May 1828]] he issued a [[Fullerton's circular to nine Siak chiefs|circular to nine Siak chiefs]] offering reciprocal freedom from duties and inviting their merchants to resort to [[Malacca]]. The Sultan of Siak's reply acknowledged receipt of the presents sent with Fullerton's letter and expressed his «ten times as great» anxiety to preserve the friendly relations between Siak and the British settlements.

## Assessment

Van der Kemp treats Fullerton as the most clearsighted of the British officials who succeeded [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] in the Sumatran correspondence. The historian prints Fullerton's minutes in extenso precisely because they afford, in his view, the most candid English exposition of the dangers to which the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1824)]] had exposed Netherlands India's commercial rivals on Sumatra.

**Captain Haga** — the brave Captain Haga — was a captain of the colonial army in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] who, in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), is named as the example of the *belabberen* (encircling-and-searching patrol) method's danger: the soldiers who were wounded or killed by friendly fire during a *belabberen* operation. Haga is the book's — and [[Musket]]'s — marker of the *belabberen*'s friendly fire: the brave captain who was wounded or killed by friendly fire during the encircling and searching of kampongs, kampong complexes, and isolated dwellings.

## The belabberen and friendly fire

"Sometimes on such occasions soldiers were wounded or killed by friendly fire, as for example the brave Captain Haga." Sometimes on such occasions — that is, during the *belabberen* (the encircling and searching) — soldiers were wounded or killed by friendly fire, as for example the brave Captain Haga. The *belabberen*'s friendly fire — the friendly fire that wounded or killed the soldiers who encircled and searched — is the book's — and Musket's — marker of the *belabberen*'s danger: the *belabberen* that was more decorative than effective and that could wound or kill the soldiers by friendly fire.

## Significance

Captain Haga is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the example of the *belabberen* method's friendly fire — the brave captain who was wounded or killed by friendly fire during a *belabberen* operation. The book's argument — that the *belabberen* was more decorative than effective and that it could wound or kill the soldiers by friendly fire — is the book's — and Musket's — marker of the *belabberen*'s danger. Haga is therefore the book's marker of the *belabberen*'s friendly fire: the brave captain who was wounded or killed by friendly fire.

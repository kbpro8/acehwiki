**E. B. Kielstra** (Eugène B. Kielstra) was a retired engineering major (*oud-majoor der Genie*) of the [[Indische Leger|Royal Netherlands Indies Army]] and later a member of the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal]] for the Dokkum constituency, who wrote the three-volume official history *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog met gebruikmaking der officieele bronnen, door het departement van Koloniën daartoe afgestaan* ("Description of the Atjeh War using the official sources placed at his disposal by the Colonial Department"), published at The Hague by Gebr. van Cleef between [[1883]] and [[1885]]. The work, accompanied by maps, served as Brooshooft's principal guide for his own [[Geschiedenis van den Atjeh-oorlog 1873–1886 (Brooshooft, 1886)|popular history]] ([[1886]]) and remained the standard contemporary military history of the first decade of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]]. In [[1902]] the bookseller Martinus Nijhoff offered the three stout volumes at the reduced price of 15 florins (original price 29 florins) in commemoration of the third centenary of the Dutch East India Company.

## Work

In addition to the war history, Kielstra published *Onder de Atjehers* ("Among the Acehnese") at Haarlem (Erven F. Bohn) and, in [[1886]], two articles on the governorship of [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]] in the *Indisch Militair Tijdschrift* (nos. 3 and 4). These articles provided Brooshooft with much of the material for his chapter on the civil-governor period.

Kielstra's three-volume *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* runs to roughly 1,700 quarto pages and, though invaluable as the first semi-official record, carries the story only to [[1880]]. Its comprehensiveness was precisely what led Brooshooft to offer a shorter, cheaper popular abridgement for schoolteachers and the general public.

## Political Stance

Kielstra was a defender of the military conduct of the war and critical of the premature transition to civil government. In his retrospective articles on Pruys van der Hoeven, written while the events were still fresh, he criticised the Governor for what he considered administrative over-confidence and a premature curtailment of military action — criticisms that would be echoed by later military writers such as [[J. B. van Heutsz|van Heutsz]].

**E. B. Kielstra** was a Dutch military historian and author of *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* ("Description of the Atjeh War"), published in three volumes at The Hague between [[1883]] and [[1885]], with maps. The work was offered in [[1902]] by the bookseller Martinus Nijhoff at the reduced price of 15 florins (original price 29 florins) in commemoration of the third centenary of the Dutch East India Company.

## Work

Kielstra's three-volume *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* provided a detailed contemporary account of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] (1873-c. 1900), one of the longest and most costly colonial conflicts in Dutch history. The work, accompanied by maps, served as a standard reference on the military operations in [[Atjeh]] during the first decade of the war, complementing the later and more famous studies by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] and others.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**E. B. Kielstra** was the Dutch historian whom author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] calls "the well-known writer on Atjeh" and "the excellent connoisseur of Atjeh's history", and whose judgements — on the self-contradicting obligations of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|London treaty of 1824]] and on the contrast between civilian Governor [[A. Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]]'s policy in Groot-Atjeh and in the Dependencies — anchored Langhout's assessment of the early decades of the [[Acheh War]].

### On the treaty of 1824

Of the London treaty of [[17 March]] [[1824]] — which prescribed that the Dutch Government would so regulate the relations with Atjeh that the native state, without losing anything of its independence, would not disturb the safety of trade and shipping — Kielstra remarked, in Langhout's words "rightly", that both obligations named in the agreement were absolutely in conflict with each other: by the signing, the Dutch authority had taken upon itself at most to "chastise"; the taking of further measures to prevent such deeds in the future had to remain behind. Langhout adds as the agreement's "principal peculiarity" that it was signed by a Netherlands-Indian governor-general.

### On the civil government of 1881–1884

The premature introduction of civil government in Atjeh was named by some the cause why in the years 1881–1884 almost all advantages gained were again lost. Kielstra pointed out the unfavourable contrast between the political policy followed by Pruijs van der Hoeven in Groot-Atjeh and the line of conduct which the same official followed toward the Dependencies, and explained it thus: "Along the coasts we tried to obtain influence, but we had no direct rule to exercise. There one had to negotiate, to persuade, there the matters had to be discussed, and there one saw the Indian official, acquainted with the native population, her customs and usages, in his full strength. In Groot-Atjeh, on the contrary, we had a territory conquered by force of arms, where in the first years the sword had to keep what the sword had won — something that same official did not perceive."

### Works cited

Langhout cites Kielstra's *Het Herstel van het Sultanaat in Atjeh* ("The Restoration of the Sultanate in Atjeh") in connection with Governor [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]]'s instruction ([[1886]]) to combine the consolidation of the [[Dutch Policy of 'Concentration'|concentration]] with the restoration of the Sultanate under Dutch protectorate.

**H. Wanting** was a Dutch journalist, editor of the *[[Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant]]*, who wrote the *voorrede* (foreword) to the [[1934]] [[Vereeniging SIMAVI|SIMAVI]] pamphlet *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]*, dated September [[1934]]. His foreword sets out the circumstances in which [[C. Graadt van Roggen]] came to write the three letters on the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] work of the ophthalmologist [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]], and states the purpose for which they were republished.

## The foreword

Wanting opened with the reflection that the manner in which human lives cross one another's course on this earth may with justice be called wholly capricious and incalculable, yet that meetings sometimes take place which, however brief, give occasion to so fruitful a contact and with such far-reaching consequences that one calls them, according to the standpoint of the observer, either a happy accident or a wise dispensation.

Such a meeting, he wrote, occurred when in February of the previous year the *[[Baloeran]]* of the [[Rotterdamsche Lloyd]] brought together on her homeward voyage two passengers who had never seen one another and who after a short association would never see one another again, and who were yet so dependent upon one another that even this fleeting acquaintance would bear uncommonly rich fruit.

## Purpose of publication

Wanting was explicit about the aim. It was not, he said, to beat the great drum for Tijssen's work — anyone who knew this physician and his modesty would not even feel such a thought arise in himself. Nor was it to collect money for his quiet but great work. It was rather "door deze mededeelingen van dr. Tijssen de plicht onzer Menschelijkheid naar voren te brengen, tot welks betrachting Simavi roept" — to bring forward, through these communications of Dr. Tijssen, the duty of our humanity, to the observance of which [[Vereeniging SIMAVI|Simavi]] calls — and thereby at the same time to fulfil an act of *piëteit* towards the young ardent idealist that Mr. C. Graadt van Roggen had been.

He closed by placing the responsibility on the living: "En dit ligt immers in de hand der levenden, die een plicht der menschelijkheid tegenover het lijdende Indië hebben te vervullen en wier ook nog zoo geringe steunverleening aan het breede doel van Simavi tevens zal zijn een posthume hulde aan hem, die met zoo warm hart de hier volgende brieven schreef." — And this surely lies in the hand of the living, who have a duty of humanity towards the suffering Indies to fulfil, and whose support of the broad aim of Simavi, however small, will at the same time be a posthumous tribute to him.

The foreword is signed *H. WANTING. September, [[1934]].*

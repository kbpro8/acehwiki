**Amat Leupon** was a Atjeh-moord perpetrator (1933) mentioned in [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]]'s *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu* in connection with [[Aceh]]. Kampong man; birthplace unknown (born in wandering period during the war).

## Role

[[Atjeh-moord]] perpetrator ([[1933]])

## Account from Jongejans

[[Achehnese geographical terms|Kampong]] man; birthplace unknown (born in wandering period during the war). Father [[Beureunoen]] was generous, depleted possessions, became 'moslimin' (kwaadwillige), took family to the woods, was shot by the Dutch 5 months later before [[Banta Ahmat|Amat]]'s eyes ([[Banta Ahmat|Amat]] then ~10-12 years old). Mother slightly weak-minded, became completely insane before death; her sister (whose husband had long been in a [[Leprozerie|leprozerie]]) was also completely insane. Youngest son died (probably of smallpox). [[Banta Ahmat|Amat]]'s mother did not regard him as '[[Vovo|vovo]]' (proper/full). Could not find a wife; his mother with others found him the deaf-mute [[Intjah]], with a bride price ([[Bruidschat|bruidschat]]) of one [[Tayl|tahil]] (~f 40.-), for which his mother had to sell a cow. Mother died a year later in poverty. Two daughters from this marriage; [[Banta Ahmat|Amat]] only knew the eldest; during his wife's second pregnancy he wandered, lived irregularly, committed thefts, earned a bad reputation. Repudiated his wife (accused her of infidelity). Briefly in service of the [[landschap]], dismissed for laziness and disobedience. In [[1931]] sentenced to 7 months prison for wounding someone with a bladed weapon. Remarried the elderly [[Fatimah]]; still married on day of attack. [[Fatimah]] ran up debts; [[Banta Ahmat|Amat]] always short of money. Could not recover his eldest daughter from first marriage. Began to brood, ate little, wanted to remarry first wife (now divorced from her second husband) - failed. Day before attack stayed out late; on morning of attack left home early; around 9 AM, in front of Captain [[Schmidt]]'s house at [[Lho'Soekon|Lho' Soekon]], calmly passed his victim, greeted him friendly, then dealt the fatal stabs. Driven by life-weariness (levensmoheid); committed [[Atjeh-moord|atjeh-moord]] to die and reach heaven.

## Significance

Amat Leupon appears in [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]]'s account as one of the figures shaping the history of [[Aceh]] during the period covered by the book.

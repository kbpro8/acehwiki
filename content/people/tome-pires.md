**Tome Pires** — **Tomé Pires** (c. [[1468]]–[[1540]]) — was the Portuguese apothecary and ambassador whose *Suma Oriental, que trata do Mar Roxo até aos Chins* (~[[1515]]) records the principal Portuguese-language account of the Javanese and Sumatran polities in the immediate pre-[[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aru war]] period. His account of [[Demak|Pate Rodin of Demak]], of [[Pati Oenoes|Pate Onus of Japara]], and of the Javanese commercial network that linked [[Malacca|Malacca]] to [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]], [[Palembang|Palembang]], [[Jambi|Jambi]] and [[Menangkabau|Menangkabau]] is the principal external witness to the early-sixteenth-century Javanese-Atjehnese sphere.

## The *Suma Oriental* (~1515)

Tome Pires's *Suma Oriental*, written in [[Malacca|Malacca]] around [[1515]] and rediscovered in the [[1940s]] (published in the Hakluyt Society series, 2nd series, no. LXXXIX), records the principal Portuguese account of the Javanese and Sumatran polities in the immediate aftermath of the [[Portuguese conquest of Malacca (1511)|Portuguese conquest of Malacca]] of [[1511]]. His account of the king of [[Java|Java]] — "called Batara Voiyaya", with the *viceroy and chief captain Guste Pate* holding the real power — and of the lords of [[Demak|Demak]], [[Tuban|Tuban]], and [[Japara|Japara]] is the principal external witness to the early-sixteenth-century Javanese political order.

## Pate Rodin of Demak and Pate Onus of Japara

Tome Pires's account of **Pate Rodin** (i.e. *Mansoer Ro-din*, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes — *aroe...din*) of [[Demak|Demak]] — "the chief *pate* in Java", whose father and grandfather had married many daughters to the chief *pate*s, and who "subjected all the land of [[Palembang|Palembang]] and of [[Jambi|Jambi]] and the island of [[Menangkabau|Monomby]] and many other islands over against [[Tandjoengpoera|Tanjongpoura]]" — fixes the Javanese commercial-military network that linked [[Malacca|Malacca]] to [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] and [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. His account of **Pate Onus** (i.e. [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes]]) of [[Japara|Japara]] — "the knight of whom the Javans speak because they say he is a great warrior in Java and very prudent" — and of his grandfather's rise from working-man in the islands of Lawé to the lordship of [[Japara|Japara]], [[Bengkalis|Bengka-lis]] and [[Tandjoengpoera|Tanjungpura]] — is the principal external witness to Pati Oenoes's career before the [[Pati Oenoes' attack on Malacca (1513)|1513 attack on Malacca]].

## The Sumatran connection

Tome Pires's account of the Javanese lords' Sumatran connections — [[Demak|Pate Rodin]]'s subjection of [[Palembang|Palembang]], [[Jambi|Jambi]], and [[Menangkabau|Monomby]] (Menangkabau), and [[Pati Oenoes|Pate Onus]]'s jurisdiction over [[Bengkalis|Bengka-lis]] and [[Tandjoengpoera|Tanjungpura]] — fixes the Javanese-Atjehnese commercial-military network against which the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese war on Aroe]] of [[1539]] is to be measured. The same network, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, is the political frame within which the [[Faletahan|Faletahan/Sunan Gunung Jati]] migration from [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] to [[Java|Java]] took place around [[1523]]–[[1524]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Tome Pires's importance lies chiefly in his *Suma Oriental* — the principal Portuguese account of the Javanese and Sumatran polities in the immediate pre-[[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aru war]] period — and in his record of [[Pati Oenoes|Pate Onus]]'s Sumatran jurisdictions ([[Bengkalis|Bengka-lis]], [[Tandjoengpoera|Tanjungpura]]). His account fixes the Javanese-Atjehnese commercial-military network against which the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese war on Aroe]] of [[1539]] is to be measured.

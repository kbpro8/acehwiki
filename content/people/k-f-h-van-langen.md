**K. F. H. van Langen** was a Dutch colonial official and geographer whose article "[[Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust (1888)|Atjeh's Westkust]]," published in [[1888]] in volume II of the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap*, supplied one of the earliest systematic Western geographical accounts of the West Coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and remained a standard reference for the region through the early decades of the twentieth century. Van Langen's study is cited in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] (p. 104) — together with [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (I, p. 120) and [[G. A. J. Wilken|Wilken]]'s *Verspreide Geschriften* (II, p. 488) — as one of the three principal authorities for the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Atjeh *adat* schimpstraf]] that paraded a wife-beater seated on a female buffalo, dressed in a head-cloth of dried banana-leaves and hung with wooden weapons and a chain of shells, to public mockery.

## Atjeh's Westkust (1888)

Van Langen's article — published in the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* (Royal Dutch Geographical Society's journal) in [[1888]], volume II — treated the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a geographical and administrative unit, and appears to have included ethnographic material on local Malay adat practices of the kind that [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] cites. The [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|buffalo-riding schimpstraf]] is a particularly vivid example: a man convicted of wife-beating was paraded through the village seated on a female buffalo, dressed in a banana-leaf head-cloth and hung with wooden weapons and a shell necklace, while the alternative form of the punishment required the delinquent to crawl on all fours for several days in public, wearing a nose-ring and eating grass "as if he were a buffalo." The detail that the punishment centred on the buffalo — symbolically converting the malefactor into the very animal he was thought to have abused his authority over — is preserved in Van Langen's account, and was passed on through [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] into the comparative ethnographic literature.

## Significance

Van Langen's chief significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is as the earliest named Western source for the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a describable territorial unit, and as one of the three authorities on which the standard account of the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Atjeh buffalo-riding schimpstraf]] rests. His 1888 article is also significant as a document of the early Dutch-colonial geographical literature on Atjeh, predating the great ethnographic syntheses of [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] (1893-94) and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] (1922-23) by a generation.

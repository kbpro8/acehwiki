**K. F. H. van Langen** (full name **Karel Ferdinand Hubert van Langen**) was a Dutch colonial official, geographer, and scholar of Acehnese government whose principal work — *De inrichting van het Atjehsche Staatsbestuur onder het Sultanaat* ("The Organisation of the Acehnese Government under the Sultanate") — published in the *Bijdragen van het Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië* for [[1888|1888]], and cited throughout [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *The Achehnese* simply as *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* — was the standard printed authority on the political institutions of the sultanate of [[Acheh|Aceh]]. He was also the author of an Acehnese dictionary, of an article on [[Acheh's Westkust|Aceh's West Coast]] in the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* for [[1888|1888]], and of remarks on Acehnese music that Snouck Hurgronje held to "require correction and expansion".

## The *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* (1888)

Van Langen's *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* — first printed in the *Bijdragen van het Kon. Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* for [[1888|1888]], pp. 381 et seq. — was based chiefly on native manuscript documents known as *sarakata* — edicts having the force of law ascribed to [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Sultan Meukuta Alam]] (Iskandar Muda, [[1607|1607]]–[[1636|1636]]) and to [[Shamsul-alam|Shamsul-alam]] ([[1726|1726]]–[[1727|1727]]). Van Langen supplemented these texts with oral tradition collected among the Acehnese, producing what remained, until Snouck Hurgronje's own study, the standard account of the Acehnese state. The *sarakatas* (royal edicts) of the sultans, including those bearing the [[Sarakata|ninefold seal]], were likewise first collected and published by Van Langen — a service that Snouck Hurgronje expressly acknowledged, even while collecting further, unpublished *sarakatas* during his own residence in Aceh, including the edict of [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Meukuta Alam]] dated [[1607|1607]], revived by [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Sapiatodin]] in [[1645|1645]], and the two further edicts of Meukuta Alam dated [[1635|1635]] and [[1640|1640]], which are explicitly described as "not included among those published by Van Langen".

## *Atjeh's Westkust* (1888)

Van Langen also contributed an article entitled "[[Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust (1888)|Atjeh's Westkust]]" to the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* (Royal Dutch Geographical Society's journal), 2nd series, Vol. V (p. 450 et seq.) and Vol. VI (longer articles, p. 38), in [[1888|1888]]. The article treated the [[Acheh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a geographical and administrative unit, and appears to have included ethnographic material on local Malay *adat* practices. It is cited by [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]] in his [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] (p. 104) — together with [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (I, p. 120) and [[G. A. J. Wilken|Wilken]]'s *Verspreide Geschriften* (II, p. 488) — as one of the three principal authorities for the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Atjeh *adat* schimpstraf]] that paraded a wife-beater seated on a female buffalo, dressed in a head-cloth of dried banana-leaves and hung with wooden weapons and a chain of shells, to public mockery. The alternative form of the punishment required the delinquent to crawl on all fours for several days in public, wearing a nose-ring and eating grass "as if he were a buffalo." The detail that the punishment centred on the buffalo — symbolically converting the malefactor into the very animal he was thought to have abused his authority over — is preserved in Van Langen's account, and was passed on through Snouck Hurgronje and Kreemer into the comparative ethnographic literature.

## Use as an Authority

Van Langen's *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* is one of the most frequently cited works in *The Achehnese*, Vol. I. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] refers to it for the traditions and conjectures respecting the origin of the Acehnese (pp. 384–389); for the organisation of the *sagis* and the formation of new *mukims* (p. 393); for the hereditary *banta* or right-hand man of the *uleebalang* (p. 398); for the list of "twelve *uleebalangs* who appoint and dethrone princes" (p. 404); for the institution of *wakeueh* lands (pp. 405 *et seqq.*); for the confidential secretaries of the sultan (p. 406, sub 5); for coinage in Aceh (pp. 427–435); for the laws relating to illicit intercourse (p. 469); and for the rules governing the determination of the fasting month (p. 456 *seq.*). It is from Van Langen that Snouck obtained the doggerel rhymes on the mutual relations of the Acehnese *sukeès* (tribes), which he quotes (with correction of some errors) from the *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* and the Acehnese Dictionary. These works made Van Langen a frequent point of reference, and Snouck Hurgronje cites him on almost every page dealing with institutions of government, land tenure, oath and ordeal, the *kawom* (tribal) system, and the *waqf* or pious endowments of [[Acheh|Aceh]].

## Criticism by Snouck Hurgronje

Snouck's reliance on Van Langen is not uncritical. The *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* is repeatedly cited as the source of statements which Snouck then qualifies or contradicts. The native traditions of the origin of the Acehnese, though collected by Van Langen, are unreliable: the writer's "efforts to reduce these data to one compact whole seem not justifiable in view of the small reliance that can be placed in them." The theory advanced in *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* (pp. 384–390) — that the division of the Acehnese people into *kawoms* was introduced on a sudden during the reign of a 16th-century [[Sultan of Acheh (16th century)|Sultan of Aceh]] on the basis of racial distinctions (a Mante-Batak *kawom*, a Hindu *kawom*, a half-caste *kawom* and one of more recent origin) — is dismissed as "unworthy of acceptance" and at variance with native tradition. Snouck Hurgronje instead represents the *kawom* distribution as the ancient and patriarchal form of social organisation, as opposed to the more recent territorial distribution into *mukims*.

On the *wakeueh* institution, "sundry kindred institutions are undoubtedly assigned to too late an epoch by Van Langen in his *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* pp. 405 seqq." On the question of the determination of the beginning of [[Ramadhan (fasting month)|Ramadhan]], Snouck again refers to Van Langen p. 456 *seq.*, while making clear his own preference for personal observation of the dispute between the upholders of calculation and those of the [[Ruya (moon-sighting)|*ru'ya*]] (moon-sighting).

A number of Van Langen's specific statements are corrected in turn: his assertion (in *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur*, p. 391) that the *teungku* in charge of the *meunasah* (prayer-house) was a kind of subordinate village headman is pronounced erroneous; his dating of certain *wakaf* (*waqf*) institutions to too late an epoch is criticised; and minor errors in his quotation of Acehnese doggerel in the *Achehnese Dictionary* (under the article [[Ja Sandang|Ja Sandang]]) are noted. The first chapter of Vol. I, in defending the need for a scientific study of Acehnese *adat*, takes Van Langen's work as the best of the available printed accounts, but treats it as no more than a starting-point.

## Lexicography and Ethnography

Van Langen's *Achehnese Dictionary* — alongside the later work of [[Dr. H. N. van der Tuuk|Dr. van der Tuuk]] and Snouck Hurgronje himself — remained one of the principal lexicographical sources for the language; the three "sentences of [[Meudeuha'|Meudeuha']]" are published in it. His *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* preserved data on court ceremonial, on the *peulieh beusoe* (licking of the hot iron) ordeal, and on the *kawom* poetry of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]] and [[XXV Mukims|XXV]] Mukims that would otherwise have been lost. Snouck Hurgronje nonetheless acknowledges Van Langen's careful preservation of the *sarakata* texts and the value of his ethnographic notes, including a description of the *bainglcotig* dress worn by escaped offenders and of the custom of dressing such offenders in the *bainglcotig*, iron *payee* jacket and weapons with unornamented hilts.

## Significance

Van Langen's chief significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Aceh]] history is twofold. As the author of *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur*, he is the earliest named Western scholar of the political institutions of the Acehnese sultanate, and the principal printed source on which Snouck Hurgronje's own ethnography builds. As the author of *Atjeh's Westkust* (1888), he is the earliest named Western source for the [[Acheh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a describable territorial unit, and one of the three authorities on which the standard account of the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Aceh buffalo-riding *schimpstraf*]] rests. His [[1888|1888]] article is also significant as a document of the early Dutch-colonial geographical literature on Aceh, predating the great ethnographic syntheses of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] ([[1893|1893]]-94) and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] ([[1922|1922]]-23) by a generation.

## Account from *Atjèh* by J. Kreemer (Deel II)

[[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] credits Van Langen with several foundational contributions to the study of Atjèh:

Van Langen authored the following works: *De inrichting van het Atjèhsche staatsbestuur* ([[1888]]), *Atjèh's Westkust* ([[1888]]), the *Handleiding voor de beoefening der Atjèhsche taal* ([[1889]]), and the *Atjèhsch-Nederlandsch Woordenboek* ([[1889]]). The *Handleiding* and *Woordenboek* together made Van Langen "de eerste ernstige student van de Atjèhsche taal" (the first serious student of the Atjèhnese language).

Van Langen published the *Adat Meukoeta Alam* (the adat-law edict attributed to [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Sultan Iskandar Moeda]]) from copies, providing the first printed version of this foundational Atjèhnese legal text. He also documented the *tjab sikoereuëng* (the ninefold seal of the Sultan of Atjèh) and provided important data on Atjèhnese coinage and metrology — data that Kreemer draws on for his own treatment of these subjects.

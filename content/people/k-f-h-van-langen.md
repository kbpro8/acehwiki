**K. F. H. van Langen** (full name **Karel Ferdinand Hubert van Langen**) was a Dutch colonial official, geographer, and scholar of Acehnese government whose principal work — *De inrichting van het Atjehsche Staatsbestuur onder het Sultanaat* ("The Organisation of the Acehnese Government under the Sultanate") — published in the *Bijdragen van het Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië* for [[1888|1888]], and cited throughout [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *The Achehnese* simply as *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* — was the standard printed authority on the political institutions of the sultanate of [[Acheh|Aceh]]. He was also the author of an Acehnese dictionary (*Woordenboek der Atjèhsche taal*, [[1889|1889]]), of a *Handleiding voor de beoefening der Atjèhsche taal* ([[1889|1889]]), of an article on [[Acheh's Westkust|Aceh's West Coast]] in the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* for [[1888|1888]], and of remarks on Acehnese music that Snouck Hurgronje held to "require correction and expansion". Kreemer, in turn, cites Van Langen frequently for information on coinage, weights, measures, and political institutions.

## The *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* (1888)

Van Langen's *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* — first printed in the *Bijdragen van het Kon. Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* for [[1888|1888]], pp. 381 et seq. — was based chiefly on native manuscript documents known as *sarakata* — edicts having the force of law ascribed to [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Sultan Meukuta Alam]] (Iskandar Muda, [[1607|1607]]–[[1636|1636]]) and to [[Shamsul-alam|Shamsul-alam]] ([[1726|1726]]–[[1727|1727]]). Van Langen supplemented these texts with oral tradition collected among the Acehnese, producing what remained, until Snouck Hurgronje's own study, the standard account of the Acehnese state. The *sarakatas* (royal edicts) of the sultans, including those bearing the [[Sarakata|ninefold seal]] — the *[[Tjab sikoereuëng|tjab sikoereuëng]]* — were likewise first collected and published by Van Langen — a service that Snouck Hurgronje expressly acknowledged, even while collecting further, unpublished *sarakatas* during his own residence in Aceh, including the edict of [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Meukuta Alam]] dated [[1607|1607]], revived by [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Sapiatodin]] in [[1645|1645]], and the two further edicts of Meukuta Alam dated [[1635|1635]] and [[1640|1640]], which are explicitly described as "not included among those published by Van Langen".

Van Langen also published the *[[Adat Meukoeta Alam|Adat Meukoeta Alam]]* — a *sarakata* / royal edict attributed to Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Moeda]] — from copies that survived the loss of the originals (captured at the mosque of [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] in [[1879]], lost in the [[1886]] flood). His publication preserved this important document for scholarship.

## *Atjeh's Westkust* (1888)

Van Langen also contributed an article entitled "[[Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust (1888)|Atjeh's Westkust]]" to the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* (Royal Dutch Geographical Society's journal), 2nd series, Vol. V (p. 450 et seq.) and Vol. VI (longer articles, p. 38), in [[1888|1888]]. The article treated the [[Acheh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a geographical and administrative unit, and appears to have included ethnographic material on local Malay *adat* practices, including marriage by force and bridewealth practices. It is cited by [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]] in his [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] (p. 104) — together with [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (I, p. 120) and [[G. A. J. Wilken|Wilken]]'s *Verspreide Geschriften* (II, p. 488) — as one of the three principal authorities for the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Atjeh *adat* schimpstraf]] that paraded a wife-beater seated on a female buffalo, dressed in a head-cloth of dried banana-leaves and hung with wooden weapons and a chain of shells, to public mockery.

## The Atjèhnese language

Van Langen was the first serious student of the Atjèhnese language. His *Handleiding voor de beoefening der Atjèhsche taal* and *Woordenboek der Atjèhsche taal* (both [[1889|1889]]) were issued by the Koninklijk Instituut. These works preceded [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *Atjèhsche taalstudiën* ([[1900]]) and *Studiën over Atjèhsche klank- en schriftleer* ([[1893]]), which "opened a new era for Atjèhnese language knowledge." The *Achehnese Dictionary* — alongside the later work of [[Dr. H. N. van der Tuuk|Dr. van der Tuuk]] and Snouck Hurgronje himself — remained one of the principal lexicographical sources for the language; the three "sentences of [[Meudeuha'|Meudeuha']]" are published in it. A *Wdbk* (woordenboek) is also cited for Westkust terms (*poekat*, *gisa baploeng*, *gisa baloeëm*, *reuban*, etc.).

## Coinage and metrology

Van Langen's works provided important information on Atjèhnese coinage — the *[[deureuham|deureuham]]*, *[[keuëh|keuëh]]*, *[[djampaj|djampaj]]*, *[[goepang|goepang]]*, *[[boesö|boesö]]* — and weights and measures: the *[[arè|arè]]*, *[[gantang|gantang]]*, *[[naléh|naléh]]*, *[[goentja|goentja]]*, *[[koejan|koejan]]*, *[[kati|katö'é]]*, *[[pikoel|piköj]]*.

## Use as an Authority

Van Langen's *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* is one of the most frequently cited works in *The Achehnese*, Vol. I. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] refers to it for the traditions and conjectures respecting the origin of the Acehnese (pp. 384–389); for the organisation of the *sagis* and the formation of new *mukims* (p. 393); for the hereditary *banta* or right-hand man of the *uleebalang* (p. 398); for the list of "twelve *uleebalangs* who appoint and dethrone princes" (p. 404); for the institution of *wakeueh* lands (pp. 405 *et seqq.*); for the confidential secretaries of the sultan (p. 406, sub 5); for coinage in Aceh (pp. 427–435); for the laws relating to illicit intercourse (p. 469); and for the rules governing the determination of the fasting month (p. 456 *seq.*). It is from Van Langen that Snouck obtained the doggerel rhymes on the mutual relations of the Acehnese *sukeès* (tribes), which he quotes (with correction of some errors) from the *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* and the Acehnese Dictionary.

## Criticism by Snouck Hurgronje

Snouck's reliance on Van Langen is not uncritical. The *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* is repeatedly cited as the source of statements which Snouck then qualifies or contradicts. The native traditions of the origin of the Acehnese, though collected by Van Langen, are unreliable: the writer's "efforts to reduce these data to one compact whole seem not justifiable in view of the small reliance that can be placed in them." The theory advanced in *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* (pp. 384–390) — that the division of the Acehnese people into *kawoms* was introduced on a sudden during the reign of a 16th-century [[Sultan of Acheh (16th century)|Sultan of Aceh]] on the basis of racial distinctions — is dismissed as "unworthy of acceptance" and at variance with native tradition. Snouck Hurgronje instead represents the *kawom* distribution as the ancient and patriarchal form of social organisation.

On the *wakeueh* institution, "sundry kindred institutions are undoubtedly assigned to too late an epoch by Van Langen in his *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* pp. 405 seqq." A number of Van Langen's specific statements are corrected in turn: his assertion (in *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur*, p. 391) that the *teungku* in charge of the *meunasah* (prayer-house) was a kind of subordinate village headman is pronounced erroneous; his dating of certain *wakaf* (*waqf*) institutions to too late an epoch is criticised; and minor errors in his quotation of Acehnese doggerel in the *Achehnese Dictionary* (under the article [[Ja Sandang|Ja Sandang]]) are noted.

## Significance

Van Langen's chief significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Aceh]] history is twofold. As the author of *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur*, he is the earliest named Western scholar of the political institutions of the Acehnese sultanate, and the principal printed source on which Snouck Hurgronje's own ethnography builds. As the author of *Atjeh's Westkust* (1888) and the *Woordenboek* (1889), he is the earliest named Western source for the [[Acheh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a describable territorial unit, the earliest serious student of the Atjèhnese language, and one of the three authorities on which the standard account of the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Aceh buffalo-riding *schimpstraf*]] rests. Kreemer's account, in turn, stresses Van Langen's preservation of the *Adat Meukoeta Alam* and his documentation of Atjèhnese coinage and metrology — the technical infrastructure of the sultanate's economic life that the *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* made accessible to later scholarship.

## Account from *Atjèh* by J. Kreemer (Deel II)

[[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] credits Van Langen with several foundational contributions to the study of Atjèh:

Van Langen authored the following works: *De inrichting van het Atjèhsche staatsbestuur* ([[1888]]), *Atjèh's Westkust* ([[1888]]), the *Handleiding voor de beoefening der Atjèhsche taal* ([[1889]]), and the *Atjèhsch-Nederlandsch Woordenboek* ([[1889]]). The *Handleiding* and *Woordenboek* together made Van Langen "de eerste ernstige student van de Atjèhsche taal" (the first serious student of the Atjèhnese language).

Van Langen published the *Adat Meukoeta Alam* (the adat-law edict attributed to [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Sultan Iskandar Moeda]]) from copies, providing the first printed version of this foundational Atjèhnese legal text. He also documented the *tjab sikoereuëng* (the ninefold seal of the Sultan of Atjèh) and provided important data on Atjèhnese coinage and metrology — data that Kreemer draws on for his own treatment of these subjects.

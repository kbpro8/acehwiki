**Darlang** — *Toean Darlang* (in the Acehnese idiom of the old hunter [[Datoek]]) — was a Marechaussee commander and patrol commander of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] in [[Atjeh|Aceh]], one of the great patrol commanders of the *prang* (the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]). In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Darlang is the eleventh-named in [[Musket]]'s list of the Marechaussee commanders — "Gilles Graafland, Van Daalen, Campioni, Christoffel, Van der Maaten, Scheepens, Watrin, Webb, Colijn, Geertsema Beckering, Darlang and the sergeants Nussy, Salowono, Pongoh … a colourful row" — and one of the *sobat kras* (heavy friends) whom [[Datoek]] names in the chapter *Jagerslatijn*.

## The list of Marechaussee commanders

Darlang is the eleventh-named in Musket's list. The names of these famous tough guys and Marechaussee commanders "rang through the Indies like carillon music," and their "stories of brave deeds, adventures, and peculiarities went the rounds, as if they were legends."

## Datoek's sobat kras

"I was a 'heavy friend' with Toean Kristoppel, and Toean General Van Heutsz, and Toean Scheepens, and Toean Darlang, and Toean Boreel, and Toean Governor Swart. Very many, and all very brave." Datoek's list of his *sobat kras* includes *Toean Darlang* — Darlang. The *Toean Darlang* — the Acehnese idiom for Darlang — is the book's — and Datoek's — memory of the patrol commander who was a heavy friend of the old hunter.

## Significance

Darlang is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as one of the Marechaussee commanders and one of Datoek's *sobat kras*. The book's argument — that the names of these fellows of the old time rang through the Indies like carillon music and that their stories went the rounds, as if they were legends — is the book's frame for the colonial memory of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. Darlang is therefore one of the book's fellows of the old time: the *Toean Darlang* who was a heavy friend of the old hunter.

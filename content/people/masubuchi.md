**Masubuchi** was the Japanese officer — initially a major, later promoted to lieutenant-general — who, as head of the *Masubuchi-kikan* (the Japanese military administration organisation for [[Atjèh|Atjèh]]), was the principal architect of the Japanese occupation of the residency from March [[1942]] until the reorganisation of the occupation apparatus in the later years of the war. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats Masubuchi as "an extremely capable leader" of the Japanese political apparatus in Atjèh, whose combination of military authority, political intelligence, and personal capability gave the occupation its initial character.

## The M-organisatie and the Masubuchi-kikan

The [[M-organisatie|M-organisatie]] — the Japanese military administration organisation for Atjèh — was, by Piekaar's glossary definition, "under Major (later Masubuchi-kikan Lieutenant-General) Masubuchi." The *kikan* (Japanese: 機関, "agency" or "organisation") took its name from its head, in the standard Japanese practice of naming administrative organisations after their commanders. Masubuchi's promotion from major to lieutenant-general over the course of the occupation reflected both the expanding scope of his responsibilities and the Japanese military's recognition of his effectiveness.

## Background and capability

Piekaar introduces Masubuchi in connection with the F-organisatie — the fifth-column apparatus that preceded the invasion — and notes that "in the Japanese Masubuchi, whom we shall get to know better in the second part of this work, this organization found for Aceh an extremely capable leader." The phrase — "this organization found for Aceh an extremely capable leader" — places Masubuchi at the head of the Japanese political apparatus in Atjèh, the successor to the F-organisatie's pre-invasion network. His capability, in Piekaar's reading, was the principal reason the Japanese occupation of Atjèh took the form it did.

## The establishment of Japanese authority

The first period of the occupation — what Piekaar designates as § 1 of Chapter VII, "The establishment of Japanese authority (March – September 1942)" — was Masubuchi's period. Under his direction, the F-organisatie's fifth-column network was transformed into the administrative apparatus of the occupation: the governing commissions established by Said Aboe Bakar and the other F-propagandists were regularised, the Japanese administrative hierarchy was installed, and the *gun-chó* and *son-chó* system of native administration was put in place. The transition from fifth-column to occupation administration was, in this sense, Masubuchi's principal achievement.

## The Asia Timoer Raja and Indonesia Merdéka periods

The subsequent periods of the occupation — the [[Asia Timoer Raja|Asia Timoer Raja]] period (September [[1942]] – September [[1944]]) and the [[Indonesia Merdéka|Indonesia Merdéka]] period (September [[1944]] – August [[1945]]) — were conducted under Masubuchi's continued direction, with the propaganda shift from the Greater East Asia idea to the Indonesia Merdéka idea marking the principal ideological adjustment. The [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] — the Islamic Council for the Welfare of Greater East Asia in Atjèh, established on [[10 March]] [[1943]] — was one of the principal institutional innovations of Masubuchi's administration, and reflected his recognition of the political weight of the Atjèhnese religious movement.

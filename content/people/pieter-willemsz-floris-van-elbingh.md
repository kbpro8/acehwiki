**Pieter Willemsz. Floris van Elbingh** (also spelled **van Elbinck**) was a Dutch merchant who resided in [[Aceh]] in [[1604]] and collected Malay and Arabic manuscripts there on behalf of the Leiden orientalist [[Thomas Erpenius]], forming the core of the Erpenius collection that later passed to [[Cambridge University Library]].
 
## Activity in Aceh
 
According to [[Ph. S. van Ronkel]]'s investigation of the Cambridge manuscripts ([[1896]]), at least three of the four Erpenius Malay manuscripts bear an ownership note indicating they belonged to Floris van Elbingh. Van Ronkel states that the merchant "vertoefd" (stayed/sojourned) in Aceh in [[1604]] and "waarschijnlijk handschriften voor Erpenius heeft verzameld" (probably collected manuscripts for Erpenius). The manuscripts contain predominantly Islamic religious texts: the story of Joseph (Qur'an Sura 12), the death of Hasan and Husayn (a fragment of the *Hikajat Muhammad Hanafiah*), a cosmogony, a commentary on Sura 18 (al-Kahf) with the Seven Sleepers, a tale of a king with five viziers (a *vorstenspiegel* translated from Persian), two Arabic texts with interlinear Malay translation (the *Burda* of [[al-Busiri]] and a fragment of [[al-Ushi]]'s *Bad' al-Amali*), and the Arabic text of Sura 58 (al-Mujadila) with prayers showing Javanese usage notes. Also included were a brief Dutch-Malay wordlist and a Telugu alphabet, reflecting Van Elbingh's broader linguistic interests.
 
## Connection to the Erpenius Collection
 
[[Thomas Erpenius]] (d. [[1624]]) was the preeminent Dutch Arabist of his generation. The manuscripts collected by Van Elbingh in Aceh formed part of Erpenius's "kleine verzameling oude Maleise manuscripten" (small collection of old Malay manuscripts) within his much larger collection of Oriental manuscripts. After Erpenius's death, negotiations for Leiden University to purchase the collection failed; it was auctioned in [[Antwerp]] in [[1625]]–[[1626]] and purchased by [[George Villiers, 1st Duke of Buckingham|George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham]]. His widow presented the manuscripts to [[Cambridge University Library]] in [[1632]], where they remained largely unexamined until [[E. G. Browne]] noticed them in [[1894]].
 
## Significance for Acehnese and Malay Manuscript Studies
 
Van Elbingh's 1604 sojourn in Aceh provides a firm *terminus ante quem* for the manuscripts he collected. The Burda translation manuscript edited by [[S. G. Evenhage]] in [[1955]] is one of these; its North Sumatran linguistic features and the separate discovery of an Acehnese Burda fragment in the [[Bataviaas Genootschap]] collection (MS B.G. 303) confirm the Acehnese milieu of production. Van Elbingh thus stands as the earliest documented Dutch collector of Malay manuscripts in Aceh, predating the systematic colonial collecting of the 19th century by two centuries.

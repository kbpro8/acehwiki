**Siraj al-Din Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Uthman al-Ushi** (Arabic *Sirāj al-Dīn Abū Muḥammad ʿAlī ibn ʿUthmān al-Ushī*) was a 12th-century Arab poet‑theologian, author of the creed poem *[[Bad' al-Amali]]* — also known as *Kasīda al-Lāmiyya fil-tawḥīd* ("the L‑poem on [divine] unity") — composed circa [[569]] AH / [[1173]] CE. His versified creed spread throughout the Muslim world, reaching the [[Malay Archipelago]] where a 16th‑century Acehnese manuscript preserves an interlinear Malay translation of the poem alongside the contemporary Malay version of the *[[Burda (poem)|Burda]]*.

## Composition and Title

The poem consists of fewer than seventy rhymed verses that end each line with the Arabic letter *lam* (ل), giving it the alternative name *Kasīda al-Lāmiyya fil-tawḥīd* ("the L‑poem on [divine] unity"). The opening line — *yakūlu 'l-ʿabdu fī badʾi 'l-amālī* ("the believer says at the start of the dictation") — explains the alternative titles *Bad' al-Amali* ("The Beginning of the Dictation") and *Amali*. According to the commentary preserved in the manuscript (see [[Hajji Khalifa|Hādjdji Khalifa]], ed. [[Flügel]] IV:558 no. 9524), the poem was compiled around [[569]]/[[1173]] by Siraj al-Din. Al-Ushi was a Hanafi jurist and theologian, and the *Bad' al-Amali* is his best-known work, condensing the principal articles of Sunni creed into a verse form suitable for rote memorisation in the elementary curriculum of the *madrasah*.

## Manuscript Transmission to the Malay World

The poem reached the Malay‑Indonesian world through at least two distinct manuscript witnesses:

1. **The Acehnese manuscript** (DLP‑54, folios 31–35) contains an interlinear Malay translation of the poem, demonstrating the text's diffusion into [[Aceh]] by the 16th century. The translation shows a higher density of Arabic lexical items than the contemporary Malay translation of the *[[Burda (poem)|Burda]]* preserved in the same codex — a feature reflecting the creed's denser Arabic theological vocabulary and the translator's strategy of retaining key technical terms rather than paraphrasing them.

2. **The Cambridge copy** (catalogue no. 3116 (1) and 5823 (2)) contains al-Ushi's work alongside the *Burda* and other devotional poems, evidencing the broader transmission of Arabic theological poetry to [[Southeast Asia]] through Malay scholarly networks. The Cambridge witness attests the regular pairing of credal (*Bad' al-Amali*) and devotional (*Burda*) Arabic verse in the curriculum of Malay-speaking Muslims.

## Significance

Al-Ushi's creed poem became a staple of Islamic education throughout the Muslim world, valued for its memorability. Its presence in both the Acehnese and Cambridge Malay manuscripts illustrates the region's integration into the broader Arabic‑Islamic literary sphere and the role of Malay translations in transmitting theological knowledge to the Indonesian archipelago. The poem was widely memorised and circulated across the Muslim world and was the subject of numerous commentaries in Persian, Turkish, Hindustani, and other languages of the Islamic commonwealth (see [[Brockelmann]] for a catalogue of twenty-nine such commentaries).

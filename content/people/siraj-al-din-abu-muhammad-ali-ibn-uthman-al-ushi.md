**Siraj al-Din Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Uthman al-Ushi** was a 12th‑century Arab poet‑theologian, author of the creed poem *[[Bad' al-Amali]]* (also known as *[[Kasida al-Lamiyya fil-tawwidd]]* or *Bad' al‑amaht*), composed circa [[569]]/[[1173]]. His versified creed spread throughout the Muslim world, reaching the Malay archipelago where a 16th‑century Acehnese manuscript preserves an interlinear Malay translation of the poem.

## Composition and Transmission

The poem consists of fewer than 70 rhymed verses that end each line with the letter *I* (Arabic *al‑lam*), giving it the name *Kasida al‑Lamiyya fil‑tawwidd* (“the I‑poem on creed”). The opening line “*yakalu ‘I‑cabdu ft bad’i ‘I‑amāli*” (“the believer says at the start of the dictation”) explains the alternative titles *Bad' al‑amaht* and *Amāli*. According to the commentary on the manuscript (see Hādjdji Khalifa, ed. [[Flügel]] IV:558 no 9524), the poem was compiled around [[569]]/[[1173]] by Siraj al‑Din.

## Acehnese Manuscript

The Acehnese manuscript (folios 31‑35) contains an interlinear Malay translation of the poem, demonstrating the text’s diffusion into Aceh by the 16th century. The translation shows a higher density of Arabic lexical items than the contemporary Malay translation of the *[[Burda]]*.

## Significance

Al‑Ushi’s creed poem became a staple of Islamic education, valued for its memorability. Its presence in Aceh illustrates the region’s integration into the broader Arabic‑Islamic literary sphere and the role of Malay translations in transmitting theological knowledge.

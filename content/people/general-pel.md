**General Pel** was the senior Dutch commander (opperhevelhebber) of the colonial forces in [[Atjeh]] during the [[1875]]–[[1876]] expeditionary campaign. He led the expeditionary forces of the [[Korps Mariniers]], directed operations from the main camp at [[Kota Radja]], ordered the punitive burning of villages such as [[Pakan Badak]] on [[3 January]] [[1876]], and oversaw the construction of fortified **bentings** (bentengs) at sites including [[Bockit Daroe]] and other positions throughout the campaign (see pages 135‑136 of the *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers*). His orders also shaped the march towards the [[Atjeh-rivier]] and the retreat from the [[Beradin]] pass in favour of the [[Blang Kala]] route.

## Command Decisions and Operations (1875–1876)

Pel received explicit instructions from the Dutch colonial government to subdue the IX Moekims. On [[27 January]] [[1876]] he wrote to [[Lieutenant Verhey]] that the marine detachment should not be withdrawn from the coast, fearing a negative impression for the Dutch presence. He ordered the construction of a fortified **benteng** at [[Bockit Daroe]] on [[29 January]] [[1876]] and directed the subsequent march towards the [[Atjeh-rivier]]. The same month, on [[3 January]] [[1876]], he commanded the burning of houses in [[Pakan Badak]] after the local population raised a white flag, describing the action as necessary to suppress “traitorous” behaviour.

## Relations with Sub‑commanders and Marine Officers

Pel coordinated closely with a range of Dutch officers:

- **Lieutenant‑Colonel Engel**, **Lieutenant Cambier**, and **Captain Cramer von Baumgarten**, who commanded infantry, marine, and artillery units respectively.
- Marine officers **Lieutenant Verhey**, **Lieutenant Mirandolle**, and **Major Diepenheim**, who were involved in the execution of the expedition’s marine components.

He inspected the troops before their departure from Aceh on [[5 February]] [[1876]], praising their conduct despite difficult terrain and disease‑ridden conditions.

## Later Activities

After the conclusion of the 1875–1876 campaign, General Pel returned to [[Batavia]] where he continued to serve within the Dutch colonial administration, though specific subsequent postings are not detailed in the available sources.

## Significance

General Pel’s leadership exemplifies the Dutch high‑command’s reliance on decisive, often harsh, measures—such as village burnings and rapid route changes—to subdue Acehnese resistance. His coordination of both land and marine forces and his involvement in the construction of fortified positions illustrate the integrated military strategy employed during the later phase of the Aceh War.

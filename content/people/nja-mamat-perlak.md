**Nja Mamat Perlak** was the Acehnese leader named by the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|J. T. Cremer]] in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]] as the commander of an attack at [[Edi]] reported in the days immediately preceding the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]].

## The reference

Cremer used the case to show the impossibility of the commission proposed by [[Van Kol's motion of 16 November 1897|Van Kol's motion]], which was to report on the possibility of ending the war. To do so, he argued, the commission would have to speak with the leaders of the resistance: "Deze strijd toch heeft geen leider maar leiders, want wij hebben op Atjeh te doen met benden over het geheele land verspreid. Aan de eene zijde heeft men b.v. [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]], aan de andere [[Panglima Polim]] en vele anderen. Dezer dagen hebben wij nog gehoord van een aanval in Edi onder aanvoering van Nja Mamat Perlak. Met al deze heeren zal de commissie te spreken hebben. Mij komt deze taak voor de commissie niet benijdenswaardig voor."

## Name and territory

The name as printed associates him with [[Perlak]], the district on the eastern seaboard of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] whose ruler had recognised Dutch sovereignty since [[1885]]\u2013[[1886]], and which was named in the same debate by [[Pieter Jelles Troelstra|Troelstra]] as the object of Dutch petroleum and tobacco concession applications. The attack he led was at [[Edi]], on the same coast.

The source records nothing further of his career, title or fate.

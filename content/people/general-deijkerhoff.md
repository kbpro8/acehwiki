**Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff** — also spelled **Deijkerhoff** in Kruisheer's text — was a *Generaal-Majoor* (Major-General) of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|K.N.I.L.]] and the senior Dutch military officer in command of the Atjeh operations in [[1894]] and [[1895]], immediately before the [[1896|1896 crisis]] with which *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* (1913) is concerned. He is the principal figure held responsible, in the closing *Overzicht* of *ATJEH '96*, for the political-military arrangement with [[Teukoe Oemar|Teuku Umar]] that produced the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]].

## The Panglima Prang Besar arrangement (1893–1895)

The [[Panglima Prang Besar|Panglima Prang Besar arrangement]] — by which [[Teukoe Oemar|Teuku Umar of Lepong]] was sworn in as *Panglima Prang besar van het Gouvernement* and given a paid government legion of 250 men — was the brainchild of Deykerhoff. The *Inleiding* of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* records that "Generaal Deijkerhoff moet die illusie gewekt hebben" — General Deijkerhoff must have awakened that illusion — that the arrangement was permanent. The illusion was broken by [[J. A. Vetter|the Leger-commandant]] during his November [[1895]] visit to Atjeh, who gave Deykerhoff to understand that the *Opperbestuur* (the supreme colonial government in [[Buitenzorg]]) would not, in any case, enter into the agreement. The contradiction between Deykerhoff's promises to Umar and the government's actual intentions is the proximate cause of the rupture.

## Kruisheer's later judgment

The closing *Overzicht van onzer vroegere betrekkingen met Atjeh* of *ATJEH '96* contains a passage that summarises the colonial judgment of Deykerhoff's policy:

> *Wat gebeurde er verder — zal men mij vragen — tijdens het bestuur van Generaal Deijkerhoff over Atjeh, hoe ontwikkelde zich zijn stelsel in 1894 en 1895, hoe was het verloop daarvan?*

— *What happened, the reader will ask, during General Deijkerhoff's command of Atjeh, how did his system develop in 1894 and 1895, what was its course?* The implied answer is unflattering: the system rested on the personal alliance with a single warlord, and when that warlord defected, the political structure collapsed.

## The dismissal of April 1896

Deykerhoff was honorably relieved as *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh* on [[7 April]] [[1896]] — recorded in the wiki as the [[Arrival of Vetter and Dismissal of Deykerhoff (7 April 1896)|arrival of Vetter and dismissal of Deykerhoff]] — in the immediate aftermath of Umar's defection. The *Koloniaal verslag* for [[1896]] explained the dismissal by reference to the collapse of the arrangement: the defection of Toekoe Djohan had given such cause that everything gained since [[1893]] came, as it were, to stand on loose screws. His portrait is one of the four frontispiece portraits of the *1e Deel* of *ATJEH '96*.

## Significance

Deykerhoff is the colonial figure most directly responsible, in the retrospective judgment of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]*, for the political-military arrangement that produced the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]]. His portrait as one of the frontispieces of *ATJEH '96* reflects his seniority in the [[1893]]–[[1895]] command structure; his appearance in the closing *Overzicht* reflects his responsibility for the crisis that followed.

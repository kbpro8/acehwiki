**A. Pruys van der Hoeven** (full name **Abraham Pruys van der Hoeven**, born [[1829|1829]]) was a Dutch colonial administrator — last [[Resident|Resident]] of [[Palembang|Palembang]] — who was appointed Government Commissioner together with Major-General [[K. van der Heyden|K. van der Heyden]] in [[1880|1880]] to draft the new organisation of the *gewest* (region) of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (Aceh and Dependencies), and who on [[6 April|6 April]] [[1881|1881]] was appointed the first civil [[Governor of Atjeh|Governor of Atjeh]] and Dependencies, with Colonel [[J. F. Haus|J. F. Haus]] taking the military command. An experienced administrator of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] who had previously served as Resident of [[Lampongs|Lampongs]] and Palembang, Pruys van der Hoeven's appointment marked the formal transition of Atjeh from a military command to a regular administrative *gewest* of the [[Netherlands Indies|Netherlands Indies]]; on [[27 December|27 December]] [[1881|1881]] he solemnly transferred the [[Meusigit Raja|Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] to the Acehnese people in the name of the Dutch government. His governorship lasted until [[1883|1883]], after which he served as a member of the [[Council of the Indies|Council of the Indies]] ([[1886|1886]]–[[1893|1893]]).

## Early Career

Pruys van der Hoeven began his career at sea before entering colonial service in [[1854|1854]]. He served as Political Agent during the [[Redjang|Redjang]] expedition ([[1859|1859]]), Resident of [[Lampongs|Lampongs]] ([[1870|1870]]), and Resident of [[Palembang|Palembang]] ([[1873|1873]]). He was known for his diplomatic skills and his successful handling of complex situations in the Outer Possessions, and he was involved in expeditions into [[Djambi|Djambi]].

## The Path to the Governorship

The appointment of Pruys van der Hoeven as Governor of Atjeh was the culmination of a complex political and administrative process:

1. **Early interest (1877).** [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]] had identified Pruys van der Hoeven as a potential governor as early as [[1877|1877]].

2. **Van Goltstein's negotiations.** Pruys van der Hoeven negotiated terms with [[W. van Goltstein|Van Goltstein]] in the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]], demanding a salary equal to the military governor's ([[1,500 guilders per month|1,500 guilders per month]] and [[50 guilders per diem|50 guilders per diem]]), as well as a pension equal to the highest pension of a Council of the Indies member.

3. **Royal authorisation (October 1879).** Van Goltstein received royal authorisation in [[October|October]] [[1879|1879]] to "make known to the Governor-General that it is left to him to entrust the civil administration in Aceh to a civil governor under such conditions as he deems necessary."

4. **Commissionership (January 1880).** Pruys van der Hoeven was initially appointed Government Commissioner alongside [[K. van der Heyden|K. van der Heyden]] in [[January|January]] [[1880|1880]], with instructions to "jointly devise and propose what is necessary to achieve a definitive organization of the region." The discharge of Van der Heyden and the appointment of Pruys van der Hoeven in his place was one of the last administrative acts of [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Governor-General Van Lansberge]].

## The Commission, 1880–1881

For the definitive regulation of the administration in [[Aceh|Atjeh]], Van der Heyden was placed in commission with Pruys van der Hoeven as Government Commissioners. They were charged, in joint consultation, "to arrange and propose everything necessary to arrive at an adequate organisation of the *gewest* Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, in connection with the political situation and the relation of the various Onderhoorigheden to our administration." The assistant-resident of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] (Great Atjeh) was attached to them as secretary for that assignment.

Pruys van der Hoeven arrived in Atjeh on [[21 February|21 February]] [[1880|1880]] and visited in succession the territory occupied by the Dutch, both in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and in the *vazal staten* (vassal states). On [[10 July|10 July]] [[1880|1880]] the commissioners declared their task ended.

## The Administrative Organisation of March 1881

After an extraordinary meeting of the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië]] (Council of the Netherlands Indies) with both commissioners had taken place for further discussion, and the Governor-General had taken several decisions in preparation, the Governor was yet charged with the submission of new proposals regarding the organisation of the inland administration in Great Acheh, based on an investigation into the existing situation and the institutions under the sultans. In accordance with the proposals submitted, the definitive administrative organisation of Atjeh came into force in [[March 1881|March 1881]]. According to this organisation, the administration of the *gewest* Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden would rest with a Governor, whose residence would be at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. Under him would stand three assistant-residents and ten *controleurs* (controllers). The assistant-residencies were:

1. that of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] with Kota Radja as chief place, in which direct Dutch administration was introduced;
2. that of the [[Noord- en Oostkust|Noord- en Oostkust]] (North and East Coast) with [[Telok Semawe|Telok Semawé]] as chief place, with *controleurs* at [[Edi|Edi]], [[Samalanga|Samalanga]], and [[Segli|Segli]];
3. that of the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]] (West Coast of Atjeh) with [[Malaboeh|Malaboeh]] as chief place.

## The Civil Governorship, 6 April 1881

By the Governor-General's decision, by virtue of authority already granted by the King in [[1879|1879]], it was decided to separate the civil and military commands and to entrust the civil administration in Atjeh to a civil Governor. Lieutenant-General Van der Heyden was honourably discharged from his functions, and Pruys van der Hoeven was appointed civil Governor; the military command was entrusted to Colonel [[J. F. Haus|J. F. Haus]] of the infantry. Both assumed their functions on [[6 April|6 April]] [[1881|1881]].

In office, Pruys van der Hoeven established a civil administration for Atjeh, replacing the military system. He created a structure with a secretary, a resident, assistant-residents for the north and east coasts, and *controleurs* for [[Great Aceh|Great Aceh]]. He maintained indigenous courts under European supervision. He faced, however, ongoing resistance from Acehnese chiefs, periodic violence, and the legacy of military rule.

## The Solemn Transfer of the Meusigit Raja (27 December 1881)

The [[Meusigit Raja|Meusigit Raja]] — the great mosque of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] — whose first stone had been laid on [[9 October|9 October]] [[1879|1879]] in the presence of Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]], all the civil and military authorities, and many Acehnese chiefs and clerics, was completed in [[1881|1881]]. On [[27 December|27 December]] [[1881|1881]] it was solemnly transferred, in the name of the Dutch government, to the Acehnese people by the Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies, Pruys van der Hoeven. The mosque had been brought about by [[Jan van Swieten|Generaal Van Swieten]] and its first stone laid by Van Lansberge; its solemn transfer to the Acehnese by Pruys van der Hoeven thus completed the chain of Dutch civil and military authorities under whose auspices the mosque had been built.

## Relationship with Van der Heyden

The relationship between Pruys van der Hoeven and [[K. van der Heyden|K. van der Heyden]] (also spelled *Van der Heijden* in some sources) was tense from the start:

1. **Initial cooperation.** Pruys van der Hoeven expressed willingness to work with Van der Heyden, stating he would "gladly give all honor to the General as long as he is convinced that he acts in the spirit of the Government."
2. **Competition.** Pruys van der Hoeven was seen by some as trying to undermine Van der Heyden's position, leading Van Goltstein to warn against "giving him the life impossible."
3. **Resolution.** Pruys van der Hoeven emerged as the victor when Van der Heyden was replaced in [[1881|1881]].

The discharge of Van der Heyden — "which set many pens in motion, and also gave rise to discussions in the [[Staten-Generaal|States-General]]" — was controversial.

## Later Career and Legacy

Pruys van der Hoeven served as Governor of Atjeh until [[1883|1883]], when he was pensioned. He later served as a member of the [[Council of the Indies|Council of the Indies]] ([[1886|1886]]–[[1893|1893]]).

He is remembered for:

- Establishing civil governance in Atjeh, a crucial transition from military rule;
- His diplomatic skills in dealing with Acehnese leaders;
- His role in the controversial replacement of Van der Heyden; and
- His solemn transfer of the [[Meusigit Raja|Meusigit Raja]] to the Acehnese people on [[27 December|27 December]] [[1881|1881]].

The [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] was, however, by no means over at the time of his appointment, and was to continue for more than two decades in the form of guerrilla resistance, especially in the upper lands of [[Pedir|Pedir]] and the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]].

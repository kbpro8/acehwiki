**A. Pruys van der Hoeven** was the first civil Governor of [[Aceh]], appointed in [[March]] [[1881]] to replace General [[K. van der Heijden]] and establish civil governance in the war-torn region. Born in [[1829]], he was an experienced colonial administrator who had previously served as Resident of [[Palembang]] and [[Lampongs]], and had been involved in expeditions into [[Djambi]].

## Early Career

Van der Hoeven began his career at sea before entering colonial service in [[1854]]. He served as Political Agent during the [[Redjang]] expedition ([[1859]]), Resident of [[Lampongs]] ([[1870]]), and Resident of [[Palembang]] ([[1873]]). He was known for his diplomatic skills and his successful handling of complex situations in the Outer Possessions.

## Appointment and Terms

Van der Hoeven's appointment as Governor of Aceh was the culmination of a complex process:

1. **Early interest**: Van Bosse had identified Van der Hoeven as a potential governor as early as [[1877]].

2. **Van Goltstein's negotiations**: Van der Hoeven negotiated terms with Van Goltstein in the [[Netherlands]], demanding a salary equal to the military governor's ([[1,500 guilders per month]] and [[50 guilders per diem]]), as well as a pension equal to the highest pension of a Council of the Indies member.

3. **Royal authorization**: Van Goltstein received royal authorization in [[October]] [[1879]] to "make known to the Governor-General that it is left to him to entrust the civil administration in Aceh to a civil governor under such conditions as he deems necessary."

4. **Commissionership**: Van der Hoeven was initially appointed as Government Commissioner alongside Van der Heijden in [[January]] [[1880]], with instructions to "jointly devise and propose what is necessary to achieve a definitive organization of the region."

## Governance as Governor

Van der Hoeven took office in [[March]] [[1881]]:

1. **Civil governance**: He established a civil administration for Aceh, replacing the military system.

2. **Organization**: He created a structure with a secretary, a resident, assistant residents for the north and east coasts, and controllers for [[Great Aceh]].

3. **Indigenous institutions**: He maintained indigenous courts under European supervision.

4. **Challenges**: He faced ongoing resistance from Acehnese chiefs, periodic violence, and the legacy of military rule.

## Relationship with Van der Heijden

The relationship between Van der Hoeven and Van der Heijden was tense from the start:

1. **Initial cooperation**: Van der Hoeven expressed willingness to work with Van der Heijden, stating he would "gladly give all honor to the General as long as he is convinced that he acts in the spirit of the Government."

2. **Competition**: Van der Hoeven was seen by some as trying to undermine Van der Heijden's position, leading Van Goltstein to warn against "giving him the life impossible."

3. **Resolution**: Van der Hoeven emerged as the victor when Van der Heijden was replaced in [[1881]].

## Later Career

Van der Hoeven served as Governor of Aceh until [[1883]], when he was pensioned. He later served as a member of the [[Council of the Indies]] ([[1886]]-[[1893]]).

## Legacy

Van der Hoeven is remembered for:
- Establishing civil governance in Aceh, a crucial transition from military rule.
- His diplomatic skills in dealing with Acehnese leaders.
- His role in the controversial replacement of Van der Heijden.

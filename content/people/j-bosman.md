**J. Bosman** was a Dutch first lieutenant of infantry in [[Atjeh]] in [[1876]], the senior surviving officer of the detachment destroyed in the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] of [[13 February]] [[1876]], and himself one of its last victims — he died of cholera during the following night, before he could be questioned a second time.

## 13–14 February 1876

Bosman marched with Captain [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|J. M. E. Van Swieten]] from [[Kota Radja]] as leader of one of the sections of the 60-man reinforcement bound for the post at [[Atoeh|Atoë]]. When the enemy first fired from the nipah bush by [[Lamsajoen|Lamsajoeng]] and the march's cohesion was lost as the captain quickened the pace, it was Bosman who repeatedly called after him: *"not so hard at the front of the march."* Repeatedly the soldiers faced right-about at his word to give volleys; when the klewang charge broke the troop, Bosman brought about thirteen survivors — the whole of what remained of the detachment — into the post at Atoë by flight.

The next day Major [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|B. E. Mekern]] rode with his column through the field to take the evidence. He reported — on the basis of Bosman's information, the spread of the bodies and his own inquiry — the whole story of the march to [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]]'s official record *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]). But he added that the shaken officer's answers could no longer be valued, and, as the night took him with cholera, First Lieutenant Bosman died next day — still the only officer who had seen the whole disaster from inside and could speak of it.

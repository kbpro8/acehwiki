**Leonard Werner** was a Luxemburger who served as interpreter (*tolk*) for the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits]] in [[1602]]–[[1603]]. His presence in the embassy suite — a German-speaking subject of the Spanish Netherlands translating between Malay and Dutch for the three Atjeh envoys [[Abdoel Hamid]], [[Sri Mohammed]] and [[Mir Hassan]] — illustrates the cosmopolitan linguistic pool on which the early Dutch-Asian trade relied.

## Role in the Atjeh embassy of 1602

When the three Atjeh ambassadors — the senior envoy [[Abdoel Hamid]], the Admiral of Atjeh [[Sri Mohammed]], and the nobleman [[Mir Hassan]] — sailed for Europe on the returning Zeeland ships, [[Leonard Werner]] accompanied them as interpreter. Godee Molsbergen's wording — "Een Luxemburger, Leonard Werner, diende als tolk" — gives his origin as Luxembourg and his function as *tolk* (interpreter/translator). The Luxemburger's role would have been to render the Sultan's Malay correspondence and the ambassadors' Malay speech intelligible to the Dutch court, and vice-versa.

## The audience at Grave

The three Atjeh envoys were received by [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]] at his siege camp before Grave. After a meal, the Prince received them surrounded by a brilliant company of nobles and military commanders. The ambassador — probably [[Sri Mohammed]] as the senior surviving envoy, after the death of [[Abdoel Hamid]] at Middelburg in August [[1602]] — greeted the Prince in the Mohammedan manner, returned the Prince's earlier letter marked with the King's seal, and presented the Sultan's two letters and the costly gifts. Werner would have been the linguistic bridge for all of this.

## Tours and cannon-shots at the siege

After the audience, the Atjeh envoys were escorted by the Counts Ernst and Lodewijk of Nassau to view the siegeworks before Grave — "de belegeringsserrkuige, dat dinghen waren, weert om se te sien" (the siege engines, things worth seeing) — whereupon the principal ambassador discharged two *cartouwen* (cannon-shots) at the city in memory of the occasion. Werner would have translated the technical explanations of the siege-works for the envoys.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, Werner is a small but telling figure — a Luxemburger serving as the linguistic hinge between the Atjeh sultanate and the House of Orange at the dawn of the seventeenth century. His presence in the embassy of [[1602]] is a reminder that the Dutch-Atjeh encounter was already mediated by a polyglot European world in which a German-speaking subject of the Spanish Netherlands could find employment interpreting Malay for the United Provinces.

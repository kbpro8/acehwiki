**Atta (Acehnese leader)** was the *opperhoofd* (chief) of an Acehnese rebel band that operated from the **Corentij** district of [[Acheh]] and later from the **Wikkie‑kreek** region during the Dutch colonial expedition to [[Berbice (colony)|Berbice]] in the mid-[[1760s]]. Dutch marine journals record that Atta “had zich met een talrijke negerbende neergezet op de plantage ‘Goed land en Goed Fortuyn’ nabij Hardenbroek” and that his forces, composed largely of **Negers** (enslaved Africans), were a principal target of Dutch punitive raids. After a clash with [[Kapitein Perrin de Salleneuve|Kapitein Perrin de Salleneuve]], Atta withdrew to the Wikkie‑kreek, later regrouping with an estimated **1200–1500 men**.

## Context and Early Activity

Atta’s followers had earlier settled along the [[Wimbia‑kreek]] but returned to the plantation [[JJhannaa]] seeking Christian employment, indicating a fluid relationship between African slave populations and indigenous Acehnese rebels. The rebel band’s base in the Corentij district placed them at the frontier of Dutch colonial expansion in the Acehnese hinterland, linking local sociopolitical structures to the broader Dutch imperial project in South America.

## Military Activity

- On [[18 January]] [[1764]] Colonel [[Kolonel De Salve|Colonel De Salve]] dispatched Captain [[Perrin de Salleneuve|Kapitein Perrin de Salleneuve]] with a detachment of thirty‑three men to attack Atta’s position. The expedition failed: “Perrin was een dapper officier, echter onervaren in de vechtwijze in de jungle,” resulting in Perrin’s death and the capture of several of his men.  
- Following the defeat, Atta’s band continued to harass Dutch posts, prompting further reinforcements and a shift in Dutch strategy. Colonel De Salve opted for containment rather than outright extermination, offering temporary leniency in exchange for surrender.  
- Atta’s forces engaged in intermittent skirmishes with troops under [[Luitenant‑Colonel Douglas|Luitenant‑Colonel Douglas]] and [[Maajoor De Brauw|Major De Brauw]]. On [[21 March]] [[1764]] a coordinated Dutch offensive was planned to crush his remaining strongholds, but the governor ultimately permitted a more conciliatory approach, hoping to induce voluntary submission.

## Resistance and Dutch Response

The rebel leader’s ability to regroup after the loss of Perrin’s detachment and to field a sizable force of up to fifteen hundred men demonstrates both the resilience of Acehnese resistance and the strategic importance the Dutch placed on neutralising his band. The offering of leniency by Colonel De Salve reflects a pragmatic Dutch policy of containment when outright military defeat proved costly.

## Significance

Atta’s resistance exemplifies the involvement of Acehnese actors in broader anti‑colonial struggles that extended beyond the Indonesian archipelago, highlighting the trans‑oceanic dimensions of eighteenth‑century resistance. His leadership also illustrates the interplay between African slave populations and indigenous rebel movements in the Acehnese hinterland, underscoring the complex social fabric that Dutch authorities had to confront.

**Sultan Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah** (reigned [[1870]]–[[1874]]) was the **last ruling Sultan of [[Aceh|Atjèh]]**. His single seal is reproduced on the spine of [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s work *Atjèh* (1922–23). He died without children when the [[Kraton Atjeh|kraton]] fell on [[24 January]] [[1874]].

## Account from *Atjèh* by J. Kreemer (Deel II)

Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah succeeded his father [[Sultan Mantsoer Sjah|Mantsoer Sjah]] ([[1838]]–[[1870]]) as Sultan in [[1870]]. His reign coincided with the diplomatic crisis that led to the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]] in [[1873]]. He died without children when the kraton fell to Dutch forces under [[Generaal van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]].

Kreemer notes that Sultan Mahmoed Sjah's single seal (*tjab soengöë*) appears on the spine of his monograph — a deliberate choice to commemorate the last ruling Sultan alongside the first queen (whose ninefold seal appears on the cover).

## Family connections

The prominent woman **Pö Tjoet Meurah** (Tjoet Nja' Meurah) first married Sultan Mahmoed Sjah, then Toeankoe Mahmoed (brother of [[Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala|Toeankoe Asem]]). Pö Tjoet Meurah was the daughter of Teukoe Pakèh Dalam (former Oelèëbalang of XII Moekim's Pidië), and her aunt had married Sultan Mantsoer Sjah.

## The end of the sultanate

After the kraton fell, the pretender Sultan [[Moehamat Dawöt (Pretendent Sultan)|Moehamat Dawöt]] fled to [[Keumala|Keumala]] in Pidië and continued to claim the sultanate until his submission in [[1903]] and banishment in [[1907]].

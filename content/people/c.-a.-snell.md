**C. A. Snell** — *Ridder van de [[Militaire Willemsorde]]* — was one of [[W. Muurling]]'s two well-known Atjeh lieutenants in the [[Gajò Loeös]], and the officer who on [[5 August]] [[1914]] destroyed the united bands of [[Nja' Ali]] and [[Nja' Asém]] at [[Koeala Daja]] in the sub-division [[Tjalang]]. He was later pensioned as a colonel.

## The Gajò Loeös

Snell served with [[Van Eck]] under captain Muurling when the 1st Division of *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* was sent to the Gajò Loeös after setbacks of the infantry garrison, and "soon restored order." In May [[1911]], while Muurling accompanied the [[Kedjoerön Petiambang]] and fourteen chiefs to the fair exhibition at [[Koeta Radja]], Snell temporarily conducted the administration — "and he, while acting, brought in eight more men from the resistance and four muzzle-loaders."

## Koeala Daja, [[5 August]] [[1914]]

During the [[Resistance in Daja and Lhong (1914)|resistance in Daja and Lhong]], the relentless pursuit in the plain of Lhong drove the band of Nja' Asém either into the caves of the limestone hills or into the *[[landschap]]* [[Daja]], where another *marechaussée* column was pursuing the band of Nja' Ali. Nja' Asém fled to Daja and joined Nja' Ali's band.

"It proved a short-lived joy, for already on 5 August the marechaussee commander in Daja, Lieutenant Snell, after a neatly prepared action, surprised the bands assembled at the holy grave near the mouth of the Daja river; fifteen band members fell, among them both band leaders Nja' Ali and Nja' Asém, while the repeating carbines lost by the Daja column and the overseer's Mauser rifle were recaptured."

That blow broke the last resistance: on [[9 August]] five more men of the Lhong band reported themselves and on [[18 August]] the remaining nine came to offer their submission, handing in the missing double-barrelled sporting gun.

## Account from the Maréchaussée Jubileum-nummer ([[1930]]) — "Old Hand"

the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* carried a long interview with Snell under the title *Een boom met "Old Hand"*, conducted shortly before the corps's fortieth anniversary. It gives his initials as **L. A. Snell** and describes him as an officer of sixteen years' service in [[Atjeh]], most of it with the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]*, in which he had served in every rank.

### Decorations and promotion

He is described as *ridder* of the *[[Militaire Willemsorde|Militaire Willems-Orde]]*, twice *Eervol Vermeld*, and bearer of the *Eereteeken voor Belangrijke Krijgsbedrijven* for Atjeh [[1906]]–[[1910]], Atjeh [[1911]]–[[1914]], [[Westkust Resistance (1925-1927)|Westkust Atjeh 1925–1927]] and Nieuw-Guinea [[1907]]–[[1915]]. The knight's cross was presented to him at [[Bandung|Bandoeng]] in April [[1927]]. He was promoted major on [[2 May]] [[1926]], lieutenant-colonel on [[21 December]] [[1926]], and by [[1929]] was colonel commanding the 2nd Regiment of Infantry at Bandoeng, with Captain [[L. N. W. Vogelesang]] as his adjutant.

### The Westkust command

When in [[1925]] "het op de Westkust van Atjeh opnieuw begon te spoken" and it was decided to send experienced *rimboe*-officers from [[Java]], attention fell at once on Captain Snell, "een old hand" as the press called him — a nickname he kept in the army. Greeted with joy by his old comrades, he "maakte met zijn steeds van een glimlach vergezeld gaande bevelen een eind aan het verzet." It was during that action that Captain [[J. Paris]] fell in the [[Gevecht waarin kapitein Paris sneuvelde (Westkust van Atjeh, 1925-1927)|engagement]] in which command passed to [[Kromo-di-Koro]] and then [[Suwoek]].

### On patrol work and post life

Snell described the *tempo-doeloe* of his entry into the *Atjehsche Garde* as a second lieutenant, learning "het vak" on wanderings with a handful of *maréchaussées*; the following of the enemy's trail to surprise him in his hiding place; and the bivouacs in downpours on the mountains, where no tents could be pitched, the wounded carried in *tandoe*, the men waiting for dawn plagued by hunger and cold, a rock or a branch for a resting place, "soms werd de nacht ook wel eens doorgebracht, hangende aan een wortel." All misery was borne with resignation and no complaint was ever heard.

Of post life he said it was full of variety and emotion: the Queen's birthday, Christmas, New Year, the *Inlandsch Nieuwjaar* and weddings celebrated with the revellers in "een blauwe atilla met witte pantalon," the bivouac band in front, coming to invite the officers. "Er heerschte een toon van saâmbindende kameraadschap, zonder dat één oogenblik de discipline werd verstoord; wij begrepen elkander volkomen."

### The men he named

Asked about the old *vuurvreters*, Snell said he saw pass before him "den zoo beminden [[Overste Scheepens|Scheepens]], den levenslustigen [[Th. J. Veltman|Veltman]], den altoos vroolijken [[Darlang]], den onverzettelijken [[Christoffel]], de stoere [[Geertsema Beckering|Geertsema]], Beckering, [[G. F. B. Watrin|Watrin]], [[Schmidt]] en zoovele anderen" — men carefree in the *soos* but of rare energy in the *rimboe*, "gepaard aan bovenmenschelijk doorzettingsvermogen en waardeering van zijn tegenstander. Ik heb Atjeh en zijn bewoners lief gekregen!"

He named as the great pioneers [[Jhr. Graafland|Graafland]], "den vader der Maréchaussées," with [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]], [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]], [[Van der Maaten]] and above all [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]; then insisted that "de eenvoudigen van ziel" be rescued from oblivion too, listing [[Marks]], [[F. Ch. Frank|Frank]], [[Mosselman]], [[Heynen]], [[Emond]], [[Rugebregt]], [[Salawono]], [[Kasir]], [[Wowor]], [[Nussy]] and [[Kromo-di-Koro]] as "de dappersten onder de dapperen."

### On the meaning of the corps and on the Atjehnese

Snell pointed to the corps's work of peace — the roads traversing Atjeh, the engineering works, the order created "in de vroeger door onderlinge veeten van elkander gescheiden staatjes." His closing reflections were sombre: hatred still reigned in the hearts of the former enemies, and could it be otherwise among a people with as much *onafhankelijkheidszin* as no other in the Archipelago? Despite decades of struggle its mentality had not changed: "Het is onderworpen, doch niet verzoend." "Wij hebben het Atjehsche Volk leed, zeer veel leed berokkend. Onze plicht is het dat weer goed te maken" — hatred must give way to appreciation and friendship, but that was a process running over generations, and the task fell to the younger men, demanding self-control, tact, and the execution of orders "zonder onnoodig bloedvergieten." "De Atjeher heeft zijn onafhankelijkheid met groote dapperheid verdedigd… een dapper vijand verdient de hulde."

### The vaandel

Snell called the coming [[Uitreiking van het vaandel aan het Korps Maréchaussée (2 April 1930)|presentation of the colour]] the fulfilment of a long-cherished wish, and travelled to Atjeh for the sixth time to attend — "gelukkig niet om de gepantserde vuist te hanteeren." The interviewer overheard him telephone an old comrade about the voyage: "Zorg er voor, dat er aan boord van de [[Tasman (ship)|Tasman]] een voldoende voorraad ijskoud bier aanwezig is, want een goed Maréchaussée heeft een gezonden dorst!"

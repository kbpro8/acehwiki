**Heredin Mahomet** was the [[Acheh|Achehnese]] commander who led the fleet that captured the [[Aroe Kingdom]] in late [[1539]], described by [[Mendez Pinto's Aroe mission (1539)|Mendez Pinto]] as the Sultan of Acheh's brother-in-law and governor of the kingdom of [[Barus]].

## Command

Pinto records that three days after his own arrival at Aroe, the Achehnese fleet arrived "sans danger à la rivière de Panetican, où par lors estoit le Roy d'Aaru avec six mille hommes du pays". The fleet was under *Heredin Mahomet, beau-frère du mesme Roy, marié avec une sienne soeur, & Gouverneur du Royaume de Baarros*.

De Roo accepts this as evidence that by [[1539]] [[Barus]] on Sumatra's west coast was already under Achehnese control, governed by a trusted kinsman of the sultan.

## Role in the fall

Heredin Mahomet's forces, numbering about 130 vessels according to Pinto, besieged the fortified town of Aroe. After initial resistance by the raja's 6,000 men, a *Cacis* (religious teacher) bribed by Acheh opened the gate, allowing the town to fall and the raja to be killed.

The conquest secured for Acheh the strategic base from which to dominate the [[Strait of Malacca]], interdict Portuguese shipping to [[Sunda]], the [[Moluccas]] and [[China]], and reap the tolls formerly collected at Aroe's *pabgjan*.

## Significance

Heredin Mahomet's appointment as commander of the Aroe expedition — as both the sultan's kinsman by marriage and the governor of [[Barus]] on the west coast — reflects the scale of Acheh's [[1539]] regional ambitions: the same polity that controlled Barus in the west was extending its reach to dominate the east-coast Strait passage. His appointment is the staff history's principal evidence that the [[1539]] campaign was a strategic project of the sultan's inner circle, not merely a border raid.

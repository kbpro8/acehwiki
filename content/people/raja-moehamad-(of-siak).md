**Raja Moehamad** — also written *Radja Moehamad*, *R. Moehamad* — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the Siak sultan whose disputes with his brother [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] over the Siak succession were the immediate occasion of the Dutch East India Company's (the *Oost-Indische Compagnie*'s) [[1755]] establishment of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post on the [[Siak|Siak River]], and whose [[1759]] destruction of that post was the immediate occasion of the long Dutch intervention in the Siak succession.

## The dispute with Raja Alam

The Bureau records [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]'s dispute with his brother [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] in a single, dense footnote. "In [[1755]] verleende de O. I. C. hulp aan Radja Moehamad, in zijne twisten met zijn broeder R. Alam, over de opvolging in Siak" — "In 1755 the O. I. C. gave help to Radja Moehamad, in his disputes with his brother R. Alam, over the succession in Siak." The dispute was, in the Bureau's framing, a fraternal succession dispute — the standard form of Siak dynastic conflict — and the Compagnie's support for [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] was, in return for the right to establish the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post, the first Dutch intervention in the Siak succession.

## The 1755 establishment of the Poelau Gontong post

The Compagnie's support for [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] was, the Bureau records, immediately rewarded by the establishment of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post. "Als loon voor deze hulp werd in [[1755]] toestemming tot het oprichten van eene loge met versterking op Poelo Gontong in de Siakrivier verleend" — "As reward for this help, in 1755, permission for the erection of a lodge with fortification on Poelo Gontong in the Siak River was granted." The [[1755]] establishment was, in the Bureau's framing, the formal beginning of the Dutch commercial presence on the [[Siak|Siak River]] — the foundation that would, within four years, be destroyed by the very sultan it had been meant to support.

## The 1759 destruction of the post

The [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post was destroyed in [[1759]] by [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]. The Bureau records: "Waarschijnlijk oorzaak van het verraad van [[1759]], was onoordeelkundig optreden der Compagnie's gevolmachtigden, toen dezelfde R. Moehamad den geheelen post liet uitmoorden" — "Probable cause of the treachery of 1759, was unjudicious conduct of the Compagnie's envoys, when the same R. Moehamad had the entire post massacred." The Bureau's attribution of the destruction to "onoordeelkundig optreden" — "unjudicious conduct" — of the Compagnie's envoys is the principal explanation recorded for the sultan's act.

## The death and the succession

[[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] died, the Bureau records, in [[1760]]. "Deze laatste overleed in [[1760]], doch zijn opvolger Radja Ismail, werd in [[1761]] overwonnen en verdreven naar Pelalawan" — "This latter [Radja Moehamad] died in 1760, but his successor Radja Ismail, was in 1761 overcome and driven out to Pelalawan." [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]'s death, in other words, opened the Siak succession to his successor [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]], who was in turn overcome and driven out by the Dutch-backed [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] in [[1761]].

## Significance

[[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the Siak sultan whose reign spans the [[1755]] establishment and the [[1759]] destruction of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post — the political event that inaugurates the long Dutch intervention in the Siak succession. His destruction of the post in [[1759]] was, in the Bureau's framing, the archetypal instance of the Dutch East India Company's "onoordeelkundig optreden" on the East Coast, and the political event that transformed the Siak sultanate from a Dutch client state into an independent polity — a transformation that would, in turn, be reversed by the Dutch-backed restoration of [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] in [[1759]]–[[1761]].

**Jacob Compostel** was a senior merchant (*Opperkoopman*) of the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] who, at the time of the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury embassy of 1641]], served as *Opperhoofd* (chief) of the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|Company's lodge in Atjeh]]. He was a member of [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s legation suite — the second-ranking Dutchman in the ceremonial audience of [[1 July]] [[1641]] — and is the only one of the six-man embassy whom the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] pointedly omitted from the gift of an [[Atjehsche feestjas|Atjeh ceremonial robe]].

## Role in the Soury legation

When the Soury embassy made its formal entry into the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh river]], Compostel — already in residence as *Opperhoofd* of the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|lodge]] — greeted the ships together with the chief eunuch of the Sultana and the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] (harbour-master), who brought the *tjap* or passport required to come ashore. He then took his place in the six-man legation suite of the formal audience of [[1 July]] [[1641]], in the capacity of *Opperkoopman* and current *Opperhoofd* of the Atjeh lodge.

The full suite, in order of precedence, was: *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]; *Opperkoopman* Compostel, *Opperhoofd* of the Company's lodge in Atjeh; the *Opperkoopman* [[Pieter Willemz|Pieter Willemz]], Compostel's recently-arrived replacement from Batavia; the *Secretaris* [[Jan van Riebeeck|Jan van Riebeeck]]; and two *Onderkooplieden* (junior merchants).

## The omitted feestjas

After the formal audience, the Sultana sent the legation an *[[Atjehsche feestjas|Atjehsch gewaad]]* with three golden rings on the collar, "zooals nog nimmer tevoren aan een ambassade was gezonden" — such as had never before been sent to an embassy. Compostel alone did not receive such a robe. Godee Molsbergen's gloss is: "vermoedelijk omdat Hare Majesteit hem als niet tot het gezantschap behoorend rekende" — presumably because Her Majesty did not consider him part of the embassy. The Sultana was making a fine distinction: Compostel was the *resident* chief of the lodge, not a member of the visiting embassy, and his ceremonial obligations ran to the lodge rather than to the ambassadorial party.

## Hand-over to Pieter Willemz

Compostel was at the moment of the Soury embassy in the process of handing over his post to [[Pieter Willemz|Pieter Willemz]], who had arrived from Batavia as his replacement. The presence in the legation suite of both the outgoing and the incoming *Opperhoofd* — Compostel and Willemz — was no doubt deliberate, allowing the Sultana and her court to see the formal transfer of the Company's local representation.

## Significance

Compostel is significant in the Atjeh record as the *Opperhoofd* of the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|lodge]] at the moment of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]]'s accession — the local face of the VOC during the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury mission]]. The Sultana's pointed omission of him from the [[Atjehsche feestjas|feestjas]] gift is a small but telling example of Atjeh court ceremonial precision: the robe went to the embassy, not to the resident chief. After his hand-over to [[Pieter Willemz|Willemz]] he disappears from the Atjeh story.

**Andrea Furtado de Mendoça** (also *André Furtado de Mendonça*) was a Portuguese admiral who, in [[1601]], commanded an armada sent from Goa with multiple purposes: to combat piracy, to confirm Portuguese power on Ceylon (where they occupied Jaffanapatnam on the island's north point), and — most importantly from the Dutch perspective — to drive the Netherlands from the Archipelago. The Mendoça armada's call at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] was the proximate cause of the Sultan's decision to release the Dutch prisoner [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] and to send the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|embassy to Prince Maurits]] in [[1602]].

## The Mendoça armada of 1601

"Van Goa uit zonden ze onder Andrea Furtado de Mendoça een armada tot bestrijding van den zeeroof, vervolgens tot bevestiging van de Portugeesche macht op Ceylon, waarbij ze Jaffanapatnam op Ceylon's Noordpunt bezetten, en ten slotte, wat het voornaamste moest gerekend worden, tot verdrijving der Nederlanders uit den Archipel" — From Goa they sent, under Andrea Furtado de Mendoça, an armada to combat piracy, then to confirm Portuguese power on Ceylon (in the course of which they occupied Jaffanapatnam on Ceylon's north point), and finally — what was to be considered the principal purpose — to drive the Netherlands from the Archipelago.

The multiple objectives of the armada — anti-piracy, Ceylon consolidation, and Dutch expulsion — illustrate the breadth of Portuguese strategic concerns in the Indian Ocean at the turn of the seventeenth century. The Ceylon occupation of Jaffanapatnam was a permanent gain; the anti-piracy and Dutch-expulsion objectives would not be achieved.

## The Sultan of Atjeh's reaction

The Mendoça armada's arrival in Atjeh waters altered the Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]]'s calculations. "De Sultan van Atjeh vreesde dat Portugal met zijn vijand, den Sultan van Djohor, tegen hem samenspande. Ook het verzoek om op een der eilandjes een Spaansch-Portugeesch fort te bouwen, ontstemde hem" — The Sultan of Atjeh feared that Portugal was conspiring with his enemy the Sultan of [[Johor|Djohor]] against him. The Portuguese request to build a Spanish-Portuguese fort on one of the islands also displeased him.

The Sultan's fear of a Portuguese-[[Johor|Djohor]] alliance against him, and his displeasure at the Portuguese fort request, together formed the political context in which the four Zeeland ships — arriving in August [[1601]] with [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]]'s letter — were so welcome. The Sultan agreed to release [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] "en zijn vrouw" and to compensate him for his material losses, and sent the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|three-ambassador embassy]] to Europe on the returning Zeeland ships. The Mendoça armada thus, paradoxically, produced the diplomatic opening between Atjeh and the Dutch Republic.

## Mendoça sails on to Bantam

When the Spanish-Portuguese armada had left Atjeh, "voer De Mendoça naar Bantam" — De Mendoça sailed to Bantam. The Dutch-Portuguese naval contest then shifted to the Sunda Strait. The last great Dutch expedition before the founding of the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] — thirteen ships under the Admirals [[Jacob van Heemskerck|Jacob van Heemskerck]] and [[Wolphert Harmensz|Wolphert Harmensz]] — encountered a strong enemy armada in Bantam bay on Christmas Day [[1601]]: eight large galleons and twenty-two galleys. The Breede Raad (Broad Council) decided "dat men de Portugesche armade met courage zou bezoeken, vertrouwende op de hulpe des Allerhoogsten" — that the Portuguese armada should be visited with courage, trusting in the help of the Most High. Three Dutch ships and two yachts attacked thirty enemy vessels. The battle, begun on Christmas Day, ended in a complete Dutch victory; on New Year's Day [[1602]] the heavily-reduced armada was in flight, Bantam was relieved, and the respect of the natives for the Dutch was greatly increased.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, Mendoça is significant as the Portuguese admiral whose armada — by alarming the Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]] and displeasing him with the fort request — produced the diplomatic opening between Atjeh and the Dutch Republic that became the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|1602 embassy to Prince Maurits]]. The Mendoça armada thus, paradoxically, accelerated the very Dutch-Atjeh alliance it had been (in part) sent to prevent. The episode is a measure of how Portuguese strategic overreach in the Indian Ocean in [[1601]] opened the door to the Dutch-Atjeh relationship that would dominate the next four decades.

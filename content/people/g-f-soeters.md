**G. F. Soeters** — *Luitenant-Kolonel* (Lieutenant-Colonel) of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] — was the commandant of the [[12e Bataljon]] and one of the principal Dutch field commanders of the second semester of [[1896]] in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *[[ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel]]*. As *commandant der 2e colonne* at the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] of [[29 June]] [[1896]], Soeters commanded the three-bataljon force — [[7e Bataljon|70e Bataljon]], [[12e Bataljon]] and [[14e Bataljon|14e Bataljon]] — which formed the eastern arm of the three-colonne night attack on the *benteng* (fort) of [[Ana' Galong]]. His later command record in the second semester of [[1896]] includes the [[Reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims (24 July 1896)|reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims of 24 July 1896]] — the principal Dutch probe into that *sagi* (territorial confederation (sagi)) in the post-De Moulin period — and the [[Trip to XXII Moekims (23–27 August 1896)|trip to the XXII Moekims of 23–27 August 1896]], in which the [[XXII Moekims]] were systematically traversed and their political disposition assessed.

## The 2e Colonne at the Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June [[1896]])

The composition of the 2e colonne at the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] is recorded by Kruisheer in detail. *2e colonne: Comd. Luit.-Kol. G. F. Soeters. Kap. H. C. Kronouer. 1e Luit.-Adj. H. C. van Bremen. Chef v/d. Staf. Adjudant. Acht ordonnansen. Half. 70 Bat. Inf. Comd. Maj. J. R. Jacobs. Sterkte: 9 off. en 277 ond. off. en minderen. 12e Bat. Inf. Comd. Kap. J. A. W. Weustmann. Sterkte: 14 off. en 487 ond. off. en minderen. 14 Bat. Inf. Comd. Majoor F. C. Thomson. Sterkte: 14 off. en 467 ond. off. en minderen. Artillerie. 1e en 3e sectie 1 Bergbatterij: Comd. Kap. G. J. E. Nauta. Sterkte: 2 off., 57 ond. off. en minderen, 8 off.- en rijpaarden, 24 trek- en draagdieren. Genietroepen. Een detachement: Comd. Kap. W. A. J. T. Zelle. Sterkte: 53 ond. off. en minderen. Cavalerie. Twee pelotons: Comd. Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy. Sterkte: 3 off. en 50 minderen. Trein. Per bataljon: een ambulance. Per compagnie: een mandoer en 24 dragers.* Soeters's colonne thus comprised some 36 officers and 1,231 *ond. off. en minderen* (NCOs and men) of infantry alone, plus a section of the 1e Bergbatterij, a detachment of genietroepen, two pelotons of cavalerie, and the customary ambulances and transport — a force nearly equal in strength to the entire pre-reinforcement garrison of Koeta-Radja at the start of the year.

The role assigned to the 2e colonne in the operation was to *hare terugtocht op den linker- [oever der Atjeh-rivier]* — to cover its retreat on the left bank of the Atjeh-rivier — while the [[J. B. van Heutsz|1e colonne under Van Heutsz]] stormed the *benteng* (fort) of Ana' Galoeng by surprise, and the [[C. J. Laceulle|3e colonne under Laceulle]] covered the right bank. To this end, *de 2e colonne moest daartoe eene opname-stelling in den Oostrand van Ana Baté en Glieng kiezen, die in het noorden aangeleund was legen den Ana' Galoengdijk en verder een deel van den noordrand van Kling bezetten* — the 2e colonne was to take up a position in the eastern edge of Ana Baté and Glieng, leaning in the north against the Ana' Galoengdijk and further occupying part of the northern edge of Kling. The hour of departure of the 2e colonne was fixed at 5:30 AM from [[Lambaroe]] — *extra-treinen zorgden voor het overbrengen naar dien post* (extra trains provided the transport to that post). The 2e colonne's role was thus to form the eastern cordon of the surprise attack — to prevent the enemy's escape eastward and to support the 1e colonne's attack by occupying the high ground east of the *benteng* (fort).

## The Reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims (24 July [[1896]])

After the death of [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor De Moulin]] on [[7 July]] [[1896]] and the restoration of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] as *wd. Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* on [[11 July]] [[1896]], the Dutch command entered the slow phase of reconnaissance and consolidation which characterised the second semester of [[1896]]. On [[24 July]] [[1896]], *idem in de XXVI Mockims door eene colonne onder Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters* — a reconnaissance was conducted in the XXVI Moekims by a colonne under Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters. The reconnaissance was the principal Dutch probe into the [[XXVI Moekims]] in the post-De Moulin period, and was undertaken in conjunction with the [[Reconnaissance in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb|reconnaissance in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] ordered by Stemfoort on [[11 July]] [[1896]]. The XXVI Moekims — the easternmost *sagi* (territorial confederation (sagi)) of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — had been the scene of [[Tjoet Nyak Din|Tjoet Nja Din]]'s family lands and the centre of [[Toekoe Nanta Tocha|Toekoe Nanta Tocha]]'s influence; the reconnaissance was thus both a military and a political probe, designed to ascertain the disposition of the *hoeloebalang* (territorial chief (uleebalang)) class and the extent of [[Teuku Umar|Umar]]'s following in that region.

## The Trip to the XXII Moekims (23–27 August [[1896]])

The principal command exercised by Soeters in the second semester of [[1896]] was the [[Trip to XXII Moekims (23–27 August 1896)|trip to the XXII Moekims of 23–27 August 1896]] — a five-day expedition which systematically traversed the [[XXII Moekims]] and assessed the political disposition of that *sagi* (territorial confederation (sagi)). The expedition formed part of the broader Dutch strategy of the second semester of [[1896]] — the slow extension of effective control beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) by means of repeated *machtsvertoon* (shows of force) and reconnaissance, in preference to the permanent occupation of points beyond the line. The *XXII Moekims* were the *sagi* (territorial confederation (sagi)) of [[Panglima Polim]] — one of the principal Acehnese commanders of the war — and the reconnaissance was thus a direct engagement with the political base of one of the principal adversaries of the Dutch.

## The Construction of Blang Bintang Camp (7 September [[1896]])

On [[7 September]] [[1896]], *begin van den bouw van een kampement op de Blang Bintang (Tjot Mantjang)* — the beginning of the construction of a camp at Blang Bintang (Tjot Mantjang). The construction of this camp — which would serve as the advanced base for the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]] and for the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]] of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]] — was a significant departure from the policy laid down in the *Notulen der conferentie van 18 Juni [[1896]]*, which had specified that *tot een postenstelsel, behalve dat van onze geconcentreerde stelling, niet mag worden overgegaan*. The construction of Blang Bintang was thus a *voorloopige* (provisional) occupation, justified by the explicit authorisation of [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|the Governor-General]]'s telegram of [[18 July]] [[1896]] for the temporary occupation of points beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line). Soeters's role in the construction of the camp — and in the subsequent operations based upon it — places him at the centre of the Dutch forward movement of the second semester of [[1896]].

## Significance

Soeters's significance in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* is that of the principal Dutch *veldcommandant* of the second semester of [[1896]] — the officer whose command record, alongside that of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[C. J. Laceulle|Laceulle]], defines the operational tempo of the period. His command of the 2e colonne at the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] placed him at the eastern arm of the most ambitious Dutch operation of the second semester; his reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims on [[24 July]] [[1896]] was the principal Dutch probe into that *sagi* (territorial confederation (sagi)) in the post-De Moulin period; and his trip to the XXII Moekims of [[23 August|23]]–[[27 August]] [[1896]] was the most extensive Dutch engagement with the political base of [[Panglima Polim]] in the second semester of the year. His name appears in the *Dagorders* granting military decorations for Atjeh '96, where *de kapiteins der Infanterie ... J. A. M. M. Schauroth* and others are honoured, but the bulk of the officer honours for the second semester went to the junior officers of the surprise attack — the [[W. Stoop|Luitenants Stoop]], [[C. Wagener|Wagener]], [[G. K. Dijkstra|Dijkstra]] and [[K. J. C. Rijnen|Rijnen]] of the snelladers compagnie.

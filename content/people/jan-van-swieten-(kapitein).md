**Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)** was a Dutch *kapitein der infanterie* who must not be confused with his namesake [[Jan van Swieten|generaal Jan van Swieten]], the *opperbevelhebber* of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]]. He commanded the small detachment of two officers and fifty-seven men (ten of them unarmed) that was massacred on the open *sawah* near [[Lamsajoen]] on [[13 February]] [[1876]] during the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|catastrophe at Atoeh]], the blackest single day of the *aanvallende* phase of the [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The Atoeh Catastrophe

On [[13 February]] [[1876]] [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] launched his three-*colonne* operation against the [[III Moekims]]: the *rechter-colonne* under [[Majoor B. E. Mekern]] upstream along the left bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]], the *midden-colonne* under [[Majoor Jeltes]] from [[Kota Alam]] along the right bank against Pango and [[Longbatta-Mesigit]], and the *linker-colonne* under [[Majoor Diepenheim]] to cover the flank. The same day [[Kolonel Meijer]]'s column conquered [[Pango]] and a post was *opgericht*. Kapitein van Swieten, marching from [[Kota Radja]] with his detachment to reinforce the [[Atoeh]] post, was attacked on the *drassige sawah* near Lamsajoen, almost at his destination. The Atjehneezen sprang from the *boschrand*; their charge, in Borel's comparison, was "als eene woeste onverwachte charge van cavallerie," and struck before most of the soldiers could *verzamelen*. Bijna allen sneuvelden "met wonden overdekt"; only a few escaped by flight. The bodies lay on the field; the *wapens*, *munitie* and ambulance materials were carried off by the enemy.

## Borel's Judgement

[[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] uses the catastrophe as a textbook example of the danger of marching in dispersed order over a soaked *sawah* along a wooded edge. He asks "hoe eene vrij aanzienlijke, achterladegewapende afdeeling op open terrein en bij klaarlichten dag kon overvallen worden" — and answers by recalling the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|rampspoedige terugtocht of kolonne van der Meer]] of [[31 December]] [[1874]], when the same loss of order on a *doorweekte sawah* had delivered a whole battalion to the Atjehneezen. On hearing the news [[Majoor B. E. Mekern]] changed route, marched on Atoch, *zuiverde* the terrain, and recovered the bodies of van Swieten's detachment, which were "voor schending bewaard" and "met de hun verschuldigde eer ter aarde besteld." Borel treats the disaster as a failure of march discipline, not as a reflection on the kapitein's personal courage.

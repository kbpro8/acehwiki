**Th. E. R. Kraaijenhoff** was a *controleur* first class in the Dutch colonial civil service, serving as the officer for relations with the native states of northern [[Sumatra]] at the time of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|First]] and [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|Second Aceh Expeditions]]. He accompanied [[F. N. Nieuwenhuizen|Government Commissioner Nieuwenhuizen]] as part of the political staff of the first expedition and was one of the few Dutch officials with direct prior knowledge of the Acehnese court and its leading figures. Kraaijenhoff offered to carry Nieuwenhuizen's first letter to the Sultan in person — an offer that was refused — and warned [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]] that the Dutch had been misinformed about the strength of Acehnese resistance. His judgment, later vindicated by events, was that peaceful negotiation rather than military force was the appropriate instrument for bringing Atjeh under Dutch influence.

## Role in the First Expedition

Kraaijenhoff joined the Government Commissioner's staff at [[Pinang]] on [[16 March]] [[1873]], along with his team of native assistants including [[Sidi Tahil]], [[Datoe Setia Aboe Hassan]], and [[Toekoe Mohamad Arifin]]. He had previously spoken with the Sultan of Aceh and knew the leading figures of the court personally, including [[Toengkoe Kadli Malikoel Adil|Toengkoe Kali]], the acting *bandhara*.

At [[Pinang]], Kraaijenhoff received intelligence about the hostile mood in Atjeh and the substantial importation of arms. He concurred with Nieuwenhuizen and Captain [[J. W. Binkes|Binkes]] that immediate bombardment might be desirable. However, his own assessment of the situation was more nuanced than the Commissioner's.

## The Offered Mission

When the Commissioner's squadron arrived on the roadstead of Atjeh on [[22 March]] [[1873]], Kraaijenhoff immediately offered to carry the Commissioner's first letter to the Sultan himself. As he had previously spoken with the Sultan and knew him personally, he believed he would be in a position to discuss the matter seriously and was confident that he could bring the Sultan and his father-in-law, the acting *bandhara* [[Toengkoe Kadli Malikoel Adil|Toengkoe Kali]], to reason.

The Commissioner, however, refused — deeming it too dangerous for Kraaijenhoff's life. Kraaijenhoff did not share this fear; it grieved him that his offer was not accepted. He had, in fact, less confidence in the use of arms than in his own means of persuasion.

## Warning to General Köhler

Kraaijenhoff also warned [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]], the military commander of the first expedition, that the Dutch had been misinformed about many things in Atjeh. The guidebook distributed to the officers, he said, had understated the strength of the Acehnese defences. Based on notes he had gathered during various journeys, Kraaijenhoff estimated that the Acehnese could field up to 50,000 able-bodied men — the *sagie* of the [[XXII Moekims]] alone reckoned at 30,000. This assessment was dramatically at variance with the optimistic figures that had guided the first expedition's planning.

## Kraaijenhoff's Assessment

Kraaijenhoff believed from the outset — as [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] later confirmed — that if the Commissioner had sent Kraaijenhoff to speak personally with the Sultan, to inform him about his interests, to promise the protection of the Indies Government, and to propose a tenfold increase in the Sultan's income, the negotiations would not have been fruitless. The opposition of the *sagie* chiefs would have been quickly overcome if the Sultan had been assured of Dutch military protection.

[[Van Swieten]] wrote: "The Controleur Kraaijenhoff had the same impression from the start. He offered the Government Commissioner to bring his letter to the Sultan himself, whom he had spoken with before and thus knew. He would thereby, he said, be in a position to speak seriously about the pending matter and he believed he would succeed in bringing the Sultan and his father-in-law, the acting state administrator Toengkoe Kadli Malikoel, to reason."

## Legacy

Kraaijenhoff's offered mission — refused on grounds of personal safety that Van Swieten considered unfounded — represents one of the critical missed opportunities of the Aceh War. The Controleur's intimate knowledge of the Acehnese court and his personal relationship with key figures could have provided the human contact that the diplomatic process fatally lacked. His warning about the true strength of the Acehnese forces was also vindicated by events.

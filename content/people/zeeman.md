**Zeeman** was the ophthalmological authority whose warning against oversimplified explanations of *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* was quoted by Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] as the starting point of his own argument against a purely dietary theory of the disease.

## The warning

In the early period of the study of xerophthalmia, Gomperts wrote, investigators formed all too simplistic conceptions of its cause, attributing the deficiency disease exclusively to vitamin-A-poor food; over the years, through the perfection of investigative methods — the demonstration of vitamin A in the blood — and through more extensive investigation of cases, that simple conception could no longer be maintained.

"Niemand minder dan Zeeman waarschuwde tegen al te eenvoudige voorstellingen onder de volgende bewoordingen: *Nieuwe proeven houden ons af van een al te eenvoudige voorstelling*" — none less than Zeeman warned against all too simple representations in the following words: new experiments hold us back from an all too simple representation.

Gomperts used the warning to introduce his own demonstration, from the harvest data of [[1936]] and [[1937]], that the distribution of xerophthalmia across the *moekims* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] could not be explained by food supply alone.

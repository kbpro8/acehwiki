**Dr. J. H. de Haas** — not to be confused with the [[De Haas|De Haas]] named in the K.N.I.L. literature — was a Netherlands-Indies physician, co-author with Mevrouw [[Van Stockum]] and Ir. [[Meulemans]] of the finding that the milk of indigenous mothers on [[Java]] was far lower in vitamin A than that of European women, and the physician whose demonstration of six xerophthalmic children at the [[C.B.Z.]] occasioned [[Mulock Houwer]]'s definition of *[[Keratomalacie|keratomalacie]]*.

## The mothers' milk study

The investigations of Mevrouw Van Stockum, Dr. J. H. de Haas and Ir. Meulemans among indigenous mothers on Java established that the vitamin A content of their milk was much lower than that of European women — a finding Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] applied to [[Groot-Atjeh]] in explaining the concentration of *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* in the youngest age group.

## The clinical demonstration

At a clinical evening at the C.B.Z. De Haas demonstrated six non-European children with xerophthalmia. It was on that occasion that Professor Mulock Houwer remarked that not every corneal ulceration in xerophthalmia might be called keratomalacia, since keratomalacia was a very typical clinical picture in which the cornea melted away entirely in a few days — a definition Gomperts subsequently declined to accept.

## On the diagnostic difficulty

De Haas feared that [[Sie Boen Lian]]'s important observation on the *[[Vlekken van Bitôt|vlekken van Bitôt]]* — that in adults these spots were not necessarily diagnostic of xerophthalmia — would make the diagnosis difficult. Gomperts did not share the fear so far as children showing all the typical conjunctival changes were concerned.

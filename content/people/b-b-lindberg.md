**B. B. Lindberg** was a Dutch mining engineer who, with [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]], carried out the [[1922]] mineralogical survey of the Residency of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] whose joint report is the principal printed source for Atjeh gold, iron-ore, copper and lead-zinc occurrences in [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]). The three names are uniformly cited together in the standard form "JANSEN, LINDBERG and WOLVEKAMP, 1922", which suggests a single field-party operating under the authority of the [[Netherlands Indies Geological Survey]] or the [[Bureau of Mines in Indonesia]].

## The 1922 Atjeh survey

The contributions recoverable from Van Bemmelen's citations ascribable to the Jansen–Lindberg–Wolvekamp party cover the principal Atjeh mineral occurrences:

- The **primary gold** of the [[S. Assai]] (11 gr Au/ton) and [[S. Meranging]] (10.8 gr Au/ton), the only two assays of 35 samples taken from Atjeh quartz-veins that yielded positive results.
- The **iron-ore** localities of the Residency of Atjeh: [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] (with the [[1920]] reserve of 2,600,000 tons at 58–64 % Fe, suspended "on account of low market-prices"), [[Gle Mon Ampeuet]], [[Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)|Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)]] (700 m of iron ore, pyrite and pyrolusite, thickness 8–17 m), [[Kr. Rigaih]] and [[Alu Talu]] (scattered ore-fragments at eruptive-rock contacts), [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]] (bog-iron estimates), [[A. Pentuwa Gade]] and [[Kuto Buloh]].
- The **copper** occurrences of Atjeh (Pulu Bras, Gle Broë, Aer Talu, Beutong, Aloe Baru, Aer Si Hajo, Muara Sipongi).
- The **lead-zinc** assays at [[Krueng Heureueng|Krueng Heureueng]] (76.1 % Pb, 920.0 gr/t Ag, 2.2 % Zn) and [[Krueng Isep|Krueng Isep]] (65.13 % Pb, 680 gr/t Ag, 8.30 % Zn, 0.5 % Fe, 0.15 % SiO₂).

For a fuller account of the contents of the survey see the entry on [[P. J. J. Jansen]].

## Significance

Lindberg's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is co-extensive with that of the Jansen–Lindberg–Wolvekamp report as a whole: he is one of the three Dutch engineers whose [[1922]] field-work supplied the baseline mineralogical description of Atjeh, and whose name accordingly appears in the source-line of nearly every mineral-locality entry for Atjeh on this wiki. His individual contributions cannot be separated from those of his colleagues on the basis of the printed citations, but the recurrence of his name as co-author of the report is itself the historical fact that matters.

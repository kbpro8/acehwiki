**Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat** — also spelled *Tocangkoe Mohammad Dawat*, from the Acehnese/Malay *Tuanaku* ("our lord") *Mohammad Dawat* — was the **ex-pretendent Sultan van Atjeh** — the ex-pretender Sultan of Atjeh — who, after his capture by the Dutch, was held in exile for thirty-two years and died at [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester-Cornelis]] (now [[Jatinegara|Jatinegara]], a suburb of [[Batavia|Batavia]]) on [[7 February]] [[1939]]. His death in exile marks the close of the line of Atjehnese pretender-sultans in the colonial period. He is the subject of an article by [[R. G. Doorman|R. G. Doorman]], *[[De gevangeneming van den ex-pretendent Sultan van Atjeh, Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat (Doorman, 1939)|De gevangeneming van den ex-pretendent Sultan van Atjeh, Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]]* (*De Indische Gids*, vol. 61, [[1939]], p. 308 *e.v.*), recorded as No. 563 in [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]]'s *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939]]* (The Hague, [[1940]]).

## Title and name

The title **Toeanangkoe** — Van Meurs's spelling, with the Doorman article's spelling **Tocangkoe** — is a Dutch rendering of the Acehnese/Malay *Tuanaku* or *Tuan-aku*, literally "our lord," a royal title borne by Atjehnese princes and pretenders. The personal name **Mohammad Dawat** is known elsewhere in the corpus in the related forms [[muhammad-dawot-shah|Muhammad Dawot Shah]], [[teuku-muda-dawot|Teuku Muda Dawot]], and [[tuanku-muhamat-dawot|Tuanku Muhamat Dawot]]; the variation in spelling reflects the wider variation in the recording of Atjehnese royal names in Dutch colonial sources, and the relationships among these forms — whether they denote the same individual, closely related claimants, or a sequence of pretenders — are among the questions that the corpus's entries on the late Atjehnese sultanate seek to clarify.

The designation **ex-pretendent Sultan van Atjeh** — ex-pretender Sultan of Atjeh — indicates that Mohammad Dawat had been, at some point before his capture, put forward as a claimant to the Atjehnese sultanate. The Atjehnese sultanate had been in abeyance since the Dutch conquest of [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in the late nineteenth century, but the royal line continued to command loyalty in the interior, and pretenders to the sultanate were a recurrent feature of the late phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and of the post-war colonial order.

## Capture and exile

The Doorman article records the **gevangeneming** — the capture — of Mohammad Dawat, and the circumstance of his subsequent exile. The duration of the exile — thirty-two years, ending with his death on [[7 February]] [[1939]] — places the beginning of the exile in [[1907]]. The year [[1907]] was a pivotal one in the late Atjeh-oorlog: it was the year of the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] — the formal inquiry into the governorship of [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|van Daalen]] — and of the [[1907-1908-aceh-resistance|1907–1908 flare-up of resistance]] that van Daalen's harsh policies had provoked. The capture and banishment of the pretender Sultan in that year suggests that the Dutch response to the late-war resistance included the removal of the royalist figurehead around whom the resistance might have coalesced, and that Mohammad Dawat's exile was part of the consolidation of Dutch authority in the closing phase of the Atjeh-oorlog.

The place of exile — [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester-Cornelis]], a suburb of [[Batavia|Batavia]] — was the standard destination for political exiles from the outer possessions of the Netherlands Indies, and the choice of Meester-Cornelis rather than a remoter place of internment indicates that Mohammad Dawat was held under the relatively lenient conditions of suburban Batavian exile rather than under the harsher conditions of a prison or a remote island.

## Death on 7 February 1939

Mohammad Dawat died at [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester-Cornelis]] on [[7 February]] [[1939]], after thirty-two years of exile. His death — recorded in the Doorman article and in Van Meurs's survey — marks the close of the line of Atjehnese pretender-sultans in the colonial period. The date [[7 February]] [[1939]] is, in the corpus, the terminus ad quem of the colonial-period Atjehnese sultanate, and the only record of the death of a pretender-sultan in the corpus. The year [[1939]] — the year of the [[Bij het derde eeuwfeest van de martelaren van Atjeh (1138-1938)|third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs]] and of the publication of the [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] edition of the [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh]] — was thus the year in which the last Atjehnese pretender-sultan died in exile, and the year in which the Atjehnese past was, in several registers, being memorialised in Dutch colonial scholarship.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat is significant as the last Atjehnese pretender Sultan of the colonial period — the figure whose death in exile on [[7 February]] [[1939]] closed the line of royal claimants that had persisted since the Dutch conquest of [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]]. His capture in [[1907]] and his thirty-two-year exile place him at the intersection of the late Atjeh-oorlog and the colonial politics of the sultanate, and his death in the same year as the [[Bij het derde eeuwfeest van de martelaren van Atjeh (1138-1938)|third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs]] gives his figure an additional resonance in the memorial landscape of late-colonial Atjeh. The entry complements the corpus's existing entries on the Atjehnese sultanate and its pretenders — including [[muhammad-dawot-shah|Muhammad Dawot Shah]], [[teuku-muda-dawot|Teuku Muda Dawot]], and [[tuanku-muhamat-dawot|Tuanku Muhamat Dawot]] — and the question of the relationship among these forms remains open in the corpus.

**Sultan Ali Mughayat Shah** — known in the Atjehnese tradition under his birth-name **Raja Ibrahim** — was, in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history of [[Acheh|Atjèh]], the first powerful ruler of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Atjèh sultanate]], reigning from approximately [[1514]] to [[1530]], under whom Atjèh freed itself from its vassalage to [[Pidië|Pidië (Pedir)]] and became the dominant power on the north coast of [[Sumatra]]. His reign marks the moment at which Atjèh, hitherto an insignificant harbour-state, emerged as the successor to the older Muslim kingdom of [[Pasei|Samudra-Pasè]] and the rival of [[Malaka|Portuguese Malaka]].

## Atjèh as a vassal of Pidië

Before Ali Mughayat Shah's accession, Kreemer records, Atjèh — "waaronder niet te verstaan is het land, doch de havenplaats van dien naam, het tegenwoordige [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] met omgeving" — "by which is meant not the land, but the harbour-place of that name, the present Koeta Radja with its surroundings" — was a statelet of little consequence, tributary to the neighbouring north-coast kingdom of [[Pidië|Pidië]]. The rise of Atjèh was prepared, Kreemer argues, by the Portuguese monopoly policy: "Hun monopoliepolitiek, vooral ter wille van de peper en de zijde, was een der oorzaken van de opkomst van Atjèh" — "Their monopoly policy, especially regarding pepper and silk, was one of the causes of the rise of Atjèh." The Portuguese, established at [[Malaka]] since [[1511]], sought to suppress free trade everywhere they held influence, including at [[Pasei|Pasè]]. The consequence was that trade diverted to other harbours — in this case Atjèh — which then threw off its dependence on Pidië.

## Conquests and the founding of the sultanate

Kreemer records the principal conquests of Ali Mughayat Shah's reign in a single compressed passage: "in ± [[1520]] lijfde hij het op de Westkust gelegen rijk [[Daya|Daja]] in, omstreeks [[1524]] veroverde hij, als gezegd, [[Pidië|Pidië]] en [[Pasei|Pasè]]. Zelfs voerde hij oorlog tegen [[Aru|Aroe]], toenmaals een machtig Bataksch rijk, dat zich van Atjèh tot aan de [[Rokan River|Rokan-rivier]] uitstrekte" — "in approximately 1520 he annexed the kingdom of Daya on the West Coast; around 1524 he conquered, as said, Pidië and Pasè. He even waged war against Aroe, then a powerful Batak kingdom that stretched from Atjèh to the Rokan river." The expulsion of the Portuguese from Pasè in [[1524]] is recorded as a direct consequence of these conquests.

## Significance

In Kreemer's periodisation of Atjehnese history, Ali Mughayat Shah's reign opens the period of Atjèh's "opkomst" — rise — which runs from approximately [[1500]] to [[1550]], preceding the "gulden eeuw" — golden century — of [[1550]]–[[1650]]. He is the ruler under whom Atjèh ceases to be a dependency and becomes a sultanate in its own right, the political successor to [[Pasei|Samudra-Pasè]] and the commercial successor to the Portuguese-thwarted trade of the north coast. His wars against [[Aru|Aroe]] and his annexation of [[Daya|Daya]] on the West Coast establish the two axes of Atjehnese expansion — eastward along the [[Sumatra's East Coast|Oostkust]] and westward along the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust]] — that would define the sultanate's imperial geography for the next century.

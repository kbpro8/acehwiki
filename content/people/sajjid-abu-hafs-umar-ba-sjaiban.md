**Sayyid Abū Ḥafṣ ʿUmar b. ʿAbdallāh Bā Shaybān** — in Dutch scholarship **Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban** — was a Sufi shaikh of Ḥadrami descent but born in India, the spiritual master of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and of [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]]. He was initiated into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] by [[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]] at [[Surat]] and lived chiefly at [[Bijapur|Bījāpūr]], where he enjoyed the protection of the ruler Muḥammad ʿĀdilshāh. He died in [[1656]] at [[Bilgram|Bilgrām]]. His role in the spiritual genealogy of two of the principal Malay-Indonesian Sufis of the seventeenth century makes him one of the key links between the Ḥadramī-Indian Sufi network and the Achehnese-Makassarese world.

## Origin and training

Bā Shaybān was of Ḥadrami descent — that is, of the Arab families of the Ḥaḍramawt valley in South Arabia — but born in India. He studied in Arabia but later settled again in India, where he was received into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] by [[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]] at [[Surat]]. The Rifāʿiyya — founded by the saint [[Ahmad Rifa'i]], whose tomb at [[Mocha]] is reverenced — was the silsila into which Bā Shaybān initiated both [[Raniri]] and [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]], and through which the authority of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian world.

## Residence at Bījāpūr

Bā Shaybān lived chiefly at [[Bijapur|Bījāpūr]] — the capital of the ʿĀdilshāhī sultanate of the Deccan — where he enjoyed the protection of the ruler Muḥammad ʿĀdilshāh. The ʿĀdilshāhī court of Bījāpūr was in this period one of the principal centres of Arabic and Persian scholarship in India, and the protection that Bā Shaybān enjoyed there is a measure of his standing as a scholar and a Sufi. He died in [[1656]] at [[Bilgram|Bilgrām]], in present-day Uttar Pradesh.

## The two Malay-Indonesian disciples

Two of the principal Malay-Indonesian Sufis of the seventeenth century studied with Bā Shaybān. The first is [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]], who — as [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] records in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises — "was initiated into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]]" by Bā Shaybān, having been his disciple before his departure for [[Acheh]] in [[1637]]. The second is [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]], who — as Voorhoeve notes — "in [[1644]] left [[Makassar|Celebes]] for [[Mecca]]" and "is among the disciples of the same Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān; he also numbers Raniri among his teachers." The two Malay-Indonesian Sufis, who never met one another in Acheh, were nonetheless joined in the same spiritual genealogy through their common Indian shaikh.

## Significance

Bā Shaybān is one of the key figures of the Ḥadramī-Indian Sufi network from which the orthodox reaction of [[Acheh]] was to draw its principal Malay-Indonesian agents. His initiation of [[Raniri]] into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] gave Raniri the authority of an orthodox Sufi shaikh by which he was able to confront the [[Wujudiyyah]] mystics of [[Hamzah Pansuri]]'s school at the court of [[Iskandar Thani]] in [[1637]]. His initiation of [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]] in the same silsila extended the same authority to the Makassarese world, where Shaikh Yūsuf was to play a role in the religious and political history of [[Makassar]] in the second half of the seventeenth century. Bā Shaybān's death in [[1656]] removes the last of the spiritual masters who had formed Raniri in the Gujarati-Ḥadramī network of the Rifāʿiyya.

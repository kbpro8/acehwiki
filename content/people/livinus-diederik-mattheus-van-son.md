**Livinus Diederik Mattheus van Son** ([[3 February]] [[1845]], [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]] – [[20 December]] [[1917]], [['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]]) was a Dutch colonial infantry officer of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands-Indian Army]] who, as a 2nd and 1st lieutenant of the 9th Battalion Infantry and the *expeditionnaire Bataillon Barisan van Madura*, took part in both the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first]] and the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expeditions]] of [[1873]] and [[1874]] — earning the *Eereteeken voor belangrijke krijgsbedrijven* with clasp and the [[Atjeh-medaille|Atjeh-medaille]] struck from cannon captured in Atjeh — and who, after promotion to captain in [[1881]] and an instructional tour with the Barisan of [[Bangkalan|Bangkalan]] (Madura), retired in [[1885]] and settled at [['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]]. His family's later connection with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — through the death of his daughter [[Leonie van Son|Leonie]] at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in [[1904]] and the birth of his granddaughter [[Yvonne Caroline Brewer|Yvonne Brewer]] at [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] the same year — gives his biography a multi-generational Atjeh dimension rare in the colonial record.

## Family and antecedents

Van Son was the son of **Theodorus Cornelis van Son** ([[23 February]] [[1814]], [[Breda|Breda]] – [[18 June]] [[1883]], [[Nieuwer Amstel|Nieuwer Amstel]]) — a clerk (*kantoorbediende*) of [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]] — and **Elisabeth Geertruida Maria Rijken** ([[6 June]] [[1818]], [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]] – [[11 December]] [[1891]], [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]]). Through his father he descended from the older line of the [[Van Son genealogy|Van Son family]], which traced itself to the Tilburg *Van Broechovens* of the fourteenth century. The genealogy — the [[Geschiedenis en genealogie van het geslacht Van Son (1951)|*Geschiedenis en genealogie*]] of H. J. A. van Son — places Livinus Diederik Mattheus in the generation XIa of Tak B (the "Breda–Amsterdam" branch).

## Commission and the Aceh expeditions (1873–1874)

Van Son departed for the [[Netherlands East Indies|Nederlandsch-Indië]] in [[1869]]. On [[16 March]] [[1869]] he was appointed by the [[Governor-General of the Netherlands East Indies|Governor-General]] 2nd lieutenant of infantry in the *Garnizoens Bataillon der Moluksche eilanden* (Garrison Battalion of the Moluccas). He was promoted to 1st lieutenant on [[21 August]] [[1873]].

In both ranks he took part in the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first]] (March–April [[1873]]) and the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second]] (November [[1873]] – January [[1874]]) expeditions against [[Acheh|Atjeh]], serving with the 9th Battalion Infantry and the *expeditionnaire Bataillon Barisan van Madura*. The genealogy — a family record, not an operational history — preserves only the decorations earned: the *Eereteeken voor belangrijke krijgsbedrijven* with clasp (the "Decoration for Important War-Operations," with clasp for the campaign), and the [[Atjeh-medaille|Atjeh-medaille]] — explicitly identified as "*vervaardigd uit het in Atjeh veroverde geschut*" ("manufactured from the artillery captured in Atjeh"). The phrase is the only documentary evidence in the source that cannon taken from the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] or its dependencies in [[1874]] were struck into a commemorative medal for the troops.

## Promotion to captain and instructorship at Madura (1881–1885)

On [[22 March]] [[1881]] Van Son was promoted to captain of infantry. He was subsequently appointed *kapitein-instructeur* (instructor-captain) with the Barisan of [[Bangkalan|Bangkalan]] on [[Madura|Madura]] — a training command for indigenous troops of the colonial army. The post was a typical mid-career slot for an infantry captain who had seen active service in Atjeh.

He was pensioned on [[7 October]] [[1885]] "by reason of completed service" (*wegens volbrachte diensttijd*), returned to the Netherlands, and settled at [['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]].

## Marriage and the family's Atjeh connection

Van Son married at [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]] on [[8 September]] [[1875]] **Carolina Weynschenk** ([[27 May]] [[1855]], [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]] – [[1 December]] [[1912]], ['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]]), daughter of George Lodewijk Weynschenk — a *landheer en grooterfpachter* (landowner and great-tenant) of Djocjacarta — and Wilhelmina Frederika Krämer, herself the daughter of Ds. Johann Friedrich Krämer, a [[Batavia|Batavia]] predikant, and Catharina Jacoba Charlotte de Gignoux.

Of the six children of the marriage, two had a documented connection to [[Acheh|Atjeh]]:

1. **[[Leonie van Son|Leonie van Son]]** (born [[Palembang|Palembang]], July [[1877]]; died [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] (Atjeh, Sumatra), [[17 November]] [[1904]]) — who married at [[Salatiga|Salatiga]] (Java) on [[26 June]] [[1899]] [[Otto Louis Brewer|Otto Louis Brewer]], the future titular major-general of artillery of the [[Koninklijk Nederlands-Indisch Leger|K.N.I.L.]], and who died at Koeta Radja five years later, possibly in connection with her husband's Atjeh posting. The genealogy records her death-place explicitly as "*Koeta Radja (Atjeh, Sumatra)*."
2. **Yvonne Caroline Brewer** — daughter of [[Leonie van Son|Leonie]] and [[Otto Louis Brewer|Otto Louis Brewer]] — born at [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] (groot Atjeh) on [[1 Augustus]] [[1904]]. Yvonne's birth at Indrapoera, only a few months before her mother's death at Koeta Radja, places the Brewer–Van Son household in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in the early months of [[1904]].

The Van Son family's multi-generational connection with Atjeh — the father's campaigns of [[1873]]–[[1874]], the son-in-law's artillery service, the daughter's death and the granddaughter's birth in the same year [[1904]] — is a micro-document of the colonial penetration of Atjeh: the soldier of [[1873]] becomes, in the next generation, the resident family of [[1904]].

## Other children

The other children of the marriage included **Frederika Elisabeth Carolina van Son** (born [[Palembang|Palembang]], [[22 June]] [[1876]]) — who married at ['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] on [[12 October]] [[1907]] Dr. [[Herman George Heyting|Herman George Heyting]], future [[Resident|Resident]] of [[Banjoemas|Banjoemas]]; **Adeline van Son** (born [[Palembang|Palembang]], [[10 October]] [[1878]]) — who married at ['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] on [[27 October]] [[1908]] her brother-in-law [[Otto Louis Brewer|Otto Louis Brewer]], the widower of her sister Leonie; **Mr. Egbert Louis Matheus van Son** (born [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]], [[19 October]] [[1880]]), who became vice-president of the [[Raad van Justitie|Raad van Justitie]] at [[Soerabaja|Soerabaja]]; **Maria van Son** (born [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]], [[27 February]] [[1882]]), who married at ['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] on [[11 October]] [[1906]] Jan Willem Herman Hamilton of Silvertonhill, a retired lieutenant-colonel of the Jagers; and **Mr. Louis van Son** (born [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]], [[7 February]] [[1884]]), who became a senior official of the [[Netherlands East Indies|Netherlands-Indian]] Government.

## Death and retirement

Van Son died at [['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] on [[20 December]] [[1917]], thirty-two years after his retirement from the army. His widow Carolina Weynschenk had predeceased him by five years, dying at ['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] on [[1 December]] [[1912]].

## Significance

Van Son's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history lies in three particulars. First, his presence in both the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first]] and [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second]] Aceh expeditions makes him one of the documented company of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in its opening campaigns — a company that includes [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]] (who fell in the first expedition) and the officers of [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s command in the second. Second, his recorded receipt of the [[Atjeh-medaille|Atjeh-medaille]] struck from captured Atjehnese cannon is a rare documentary witness to the medal's manufacture from enemy metal — a fact generally asserted in the medal's standard descriptions but seldom so explicitly stated in a primary genealogical record. Third, the multi-generational Atjeh connection of the Van Son family — campaigns in the [[1870s]], family residence and death in [[1904]] — exemplifies the way the Atjeh war-zone, opened by the military, became in the next generation a site of colonial family life.

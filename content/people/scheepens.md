**Scheepens** — *overste Scheepens* — was the Dutch colonel of maréchaussée who, in [[1913]], held both the *divisiecommando* and the *civiel gezag* (civil authority) at [[Sigli]]. He was one of the most popular and best-loved men among the Dutch in Atjeh, *door zijn troep op de handen gedragen* (carried on the hands by his troops), and his *gaaf karakter* was, Zentgraff writes, *ook door den vijand gewaardeerd* (appreciated also by the enemy).

## Wound in the Gajoetocht

Scheepens had been wounded in the [[Gajoetocht (1904)|Gajoetocht]] under [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] in [[1904]]. When the wound was still unhealed he was operated on again in Holland; after his recovery he was invited to a *diner ten Hove* at the palace. His wife, *op het punt moeder te worden* (about to become a mother), had had to excuse herself. At table, after inquiring after her condition, Queen Wilhelmina said: *"Ik hoop dat het een stamhouder zal zijn"* — "I hope it will be a male heir." Scheepens answered: *"Majesteit, ik zal m'n vrouw zeggen dat het op hoog verlangen een stamhouder moet zijn"* — "Your Majesty, I shall tell my wife that on high desire it must be a male heir." *De Koninklijke wensch ging in vervulling; er kwam een zoon* — the royal wish was fulfilled; a son was born.

## The moesapat stabbing, 10 October 1913

On [[10 October]] [[1913]] an American tourist, [[Mary Ware|Mrs. Mary Ware]], making a round-the-world journey, was at Sigli; Scheepens had arranged everything for her to see something of typical Atjehsche life. That day he came home only towards midday and had still to go to the *moesapat* (the Atjehsche landraad) for an unpleasant case. An Atjehsche kampong-man had struck the son of the *oeléëbalang* of Titeuë because that son had *zich aan zijn eigendom of aan zijn vrouw vergrepen* (intruded on his property or his wife). In the eyes of the Atjehsche chiefs the kampong-man was *zeer schuldig* (very guilty): the old lords could do almost anything to the little man. *Ons recht stond daar heel anders tegenover* — our law stood quite otherwise against it. Scheepens, as president of the moesapat, said: *"Drie maanden krakal is meer dan voldoende"* — three months in chains is more than enough. The *oeléëbalang*, *woedend omdat zijn afrekening dreigde te mislukken*, cried *"Itoe saja tida tarima!"* — I do not accept it! — and Scheepens made an end with: *"Het blijft drie maanden; abis!"* The *oeléëbalang* *greep zijn rentjong en stak hem Scheepens in de zijde* — seized his rentjong and stabbed Scheepens in the side. Atjehsche oppassers fell on the *oeléëbalang* with their klewangs and killed him — *Atjehers die hun eigen hoofd doodden in woede over zijn aanval op een blanke: een Kaphé* — Atjehers who killed their own chief in fury at his attack on a white man: a *Kaphé*.

## The special train and the death

A special express-train — *het speelgoederammetje*, the plaything-tram of the Atjehtram — was despatched from Koetaradja with the famous surgeon Dokter Dubinsky; the machinist was *met een flink touw aan z'n machine gebonden* (tied to his engine with a stout rope) so that he could not be thrown off, and he made *eene recordrit, eenig in de annalen van Atjeh* — a record-run, unique in the annals of Atjeh. Mary Ware ran *bareheaded* to the hospital. Scheepens was operated on the same afternoon. Mrs. Scheepens, *in stille wanhoop rondgaande*, was watched by his friends Grashuis and Van Galen Last. After some days the doctor warned that the end was near. On his last morning at *kwart over vijf* — 5:15 AM — Scheepens died. *Naast haar stonden onbeweglijk de twee officieren wien de tranen uit de oogen druppelden* — beside her stood unmoving the two officers from whose eyes the tears dripped.

## Burial at Peutjoet

His widow spoke over his body: *"Je bent nooit bang geweest voor den dood; je noemde hem: de groote rust. Het was altijd je verlangen op Peutjoet te liggen bij je oude kameraden, Webb, Campioni…"* — You were never afraid of death; you called it: the great rest. It was always your longing to lie at [[Peutjoet]] among your old comrades, Webb, [[Campioni]]… *Heel Atjeh was er bij toen Scheepens zijn laatsten rit maakte naar Peutjoet* — All Atjeh was there when Scheepens made his last ride to Peutjoet. Mary Ware kept up a correspondence with Mrs. Scheepens at The Hague and crossed the ocean once more to visit her.

## Significance

Scheepens's death is the symbol of the tragic crossing of two systems of law — *adat* against colonial code — at the moment of the colonial regime's sharpest transition. His widow's wish that their son should become an officer was fulfilled; his *eeresabel*, given by the Queen, two bullets taken from his body, his *Militaire Willemsorde*, and his portrait with the *typische kop* are still in the family's keeping, the *kostelijkst reliquie*.

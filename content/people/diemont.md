**Diemont** — *Generaal Diemont* — was a Dutch officer of the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army ([[K.N.I.L.|K.N.I.L.]]) who served as Governor of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] from [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]] to [[June 1877|June]] [[1877]], and whose brief tenure saw the completion of [[Pel's Plan|Pel's Plan]] in Groot-Atjeh, the offensive operations against the coastal statelets (notably the [[Simpang Olim expedition (November 1876)|Simpang Olim expedition]] of [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]]), and the visit of the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] in [[March 1877|March]] [[1877]] that crystallised the Dutch civil-government illusion.

## Appointment (November 1876)

When the Netherlands Indies government at Batavia became convinced that more vigorous action must be taken against the still-recalcitrant coastal statelets, and since [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] thought otherwise on the matter, he was replaced in [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]] by the general Diemont. To Diemont was entrusted the further completion of [[Pel's Plan|Pel's Plan]] in Groot-Atjeh and the offensive operations on the coasts. The troop strength had risen to over 8,000 by the end of [[1876]]; he could therefore undertake the work in [[January 1877|January]] [[1877]] and bring it to an end. This naturally resulted again in the establishment of new posts, so that there were now over 50.

## The Coastal Operations (1876–1877)

In [[June 1876|June]] [[1876]] a post was established at [[Pedir|Pedir]], and the *radja* received ƒ50,000 as damages for the 1873 chastisement. In [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]] [[Simpang Olim|Simpang-Olim]] — with little loss and with the cooperation of Atjehese auxiliaries — was chastised and taken, the *radja* deposed, another appointed in his place, and three posts established. New articles in the *onderwerpingsacte* stipulated, among other things, that the Sultan's share of the revenues would henceforth belong to the Dutch. This was later also applied to the other statelets.

With the submission of [[Simpang Olim|Simpang-Olim]] — the most powerful state of the East Coast and of pure Groot-Atjehse origin — the East Coast was now wholly under Dutch administration, except for [[Langsar|Langsar]], which was later easily brought to reason. The impression of this small expedition was the cause that now also with other statelets on the [[Noordkust|North Coast]], among others with [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]], more contact was obtained. Later the posts in Simpang-Olim were again withdrawn, and there and elsewhere, thanks to the Dutch's want of adequate supervision, an unsound situation arose and persisted. Yet in [[1877]] there was still an expedition to this statelet, which was crowned with complete success.

## The Van Lansberge Visit (March 1877)

In [[March 1877|March]] [[1877]] the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] visited [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. The impression he received of everything was excellent. [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] with their surroundings bore the marks of prosperous settlements. The enemy kept quiet and was cut off from the sea. Prominent chiefs, including a member of the Sultan's family, sought contact with the administration. The coast was Dutch. The people were weary of the war. His Excellency was thereby most pleasantly strengthened in his optimistic view that the war was as good as over.

After his departure, the already-mentioned expeditions on the [[Oostkust|East]] and [[Westkust|West Coasts]] took place. The expedition to Simpang-Olim went with General Diemont on leave to Java. The colonel [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] took over his functions temporarily, and was in [[January 1878|January]] [[1878]] definitively appointed *Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*.

## Significance

Diemont's governorship is a transitional phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], bridging the acting governorship of [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] and the definitive governorship of [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]]. The completion of [[Pel's Plan|Pel's Plan]] under his governorship, the offensive operations on the coasts (notably the [[Simpang Olim expedition (November 1876)|Simpang Olim expedition]]), and the visit of the *Gouverneur-Generaal* Van Lansberge together mark the high point of the Dutch optimism that would be dispelled by the civil-government period of [[1881]]–[[1884]] and the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[1884]].

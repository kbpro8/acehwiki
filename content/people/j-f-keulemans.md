**J. F. Keulemans** was a Dutch businessman and civil servant who served as head of the Department of Cultural Affairs (*Dep. van Cult. Zaken*) of the [[Negara Sumatera Timur]] (N.S.T.) from its establishment on 20 February [[1948]] until his honourable discharge on 1 May [[1950]]. Before the Second World War he had been a businessman (*zakenman*) in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]].

## Career in the N.S.T.

Upon the founding of the [[Negara Sumatera Timur]] on 20 February 1948, Keulemans was placed in charge of the Department of Cultural Affairs, which encompassed Education, Indonesian Culture, Public Health (*Volksgezondheid*), and Social Affairs. The Dutch-language newspaper *Het Nieuwsblad voor Sumatra* described him as "een harde werker, een door en door intègre en humaan bewindsman, een organisator en bouwer" ("a hard worker, a thoroughly upright and humane administrator, an organiser and builder").

After Japan's capitulation, Keulemans had on 6 November [[1946]] participated as secretary in the leadership of the Medan branch of the Red Cross (*Rode Kruis*). During the ensuing eighteen months he accomplished an enormous amount of work for the organisation. Among his published outputs were the annual reports for the years [[1946]] and [[1947]].

## Departure

When it became clear that the [[Negara Sumatera Timur]] would be dissolved and absorbed into the unitary Republic of Indonesia — a process in which the pre-war residencies of [[Atjeh]], [[Tapanuli]], and East Sumatra (*Oost-Sumatra*) were to form the autonomous province of North Sumatra (*Nd.-Sumatra*) — Keulemans, who felt no affinity for Indonesian citizenship (*warganegaraschap*), requested and was granted honourable discharge effective 1 May [[1950]]. He departed for the Netherlands on 9 May [[1950]] aboard the *Cuanza* with his wife.

He was succeeded as acting head of the department by [[Tengku M. Bahar]], a member of the College of Delegates of the Provisional Council of the N.S.T.

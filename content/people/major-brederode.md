**Major Brederode** was a Dutch army officer who, according to the source, co‑led the landing of 450 marines and soldiers on the island of [[Chappey]] during the 1667 Dutch expedition against the English fleet. The operation took place under the overall command of Lieutenant‑Colonel [[François Palm]] and Colonel Dolman.

## Military background
Brederode hailed from the noble Brederode family, long associated with the Dutch military. In the early [[1660s]] he served with the VOC’s marine contingent in the East Indies, taking part in naval patrols off the coast of [[Aceh]] to suppress piracy and enforce Dutch trade monopolies.

## Role in the Thames raid
During the 1667 Thames raid, Brederode oversaw the organization of infantry and marine units that stormed the English fortifications at [[Sheerness]] and later at [[Chatham]]. His leadership contributed to the swift capture and demolition of English war material.

## Connection to Aceh
Brederode’s earlier service in the Indian Ocean placed him in direct conflict with Acehnese forces, providing him with experience in combined land‑sea operations that proved valuable during the European campaign.

**A. J. B. Kessler** was a Dutch industrialist who served as the first Director of the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] (Royal Dutch Petroleum Company), the petroleum company founded in [[1890]] on the basis of the [[1884]] concession secured by *Zijlker* in the *Landschap* (self-governing territory) of [[Langkat]] on the Sumatra East Coast, immediately adjacent to [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. Kessler is named in Ir. [[F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]]'s chapter *Mijnbouw* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) as the man who, "through almost superhuman effort," succeeded in raising the Koninklijke's production at its [[Pangkalan Brandan]] refinery in Langkat to a level that allowed the company to pay a modest dividend in [[1895]].

## Role in the Koninklijke

According to Van der Ploeg's account, the Koninklijke built its refinery at Pangkalan Brandan in Langkat but was immediately forced to compete on the world market with American lamp-oil, "even if in the beginning [it remained active] on the nearby markets of Singapore and Penang." The first years were difficult. Kessler, as the Koninklijke's first Director, secured the help of [[H. W. A. Deterding|H. W. A. Deterding]] — then agent of the *Nederlandsche Handel Maatschappij* in [[Penang]] — for the sale of lamp-oil. Deterding "saw at once the great importance of a good sales organisation, which he therefore, in those days a novelty, set up under his own management." Tankers and other transport innovations were pressed into service to maintain the Koninklijke's competitive position.

Kessler's death in [[1901]] opened the way for the eventual merger of the Koninklijke with the Shell Transport and Trading Co. of Sir Marcus Samuel, brokered by Deterding (who after Kessler's death "took the reins of the Koninklijke alone in hand"). The merger came into effect in [[1903]], with the founding of the "Asiatic" sales organisation; in [[1906]] the Bataafsche was founded as the production arm and the Anglo-Saxon as the transport arm. The Koninklijke–Shell concern that emerged from this series of mergers — today's [[Royal Dutch Shell]] — was thus built on the foundations laid by Kessler in the Langkat oilfields of the Sumatra East Coast.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Kessler's significance is the placement of the [[Langkat]] oilfield — directly adjacent to Atjeh — at the head of the modern Indonesian petroleum story. The Koninklijke's Pangkalan Brandan refinery in Langkat, brought into production under Kessler's directorship, was the operational base from which the late-colonial Dutch petroleum industry expanded into the wider Indies; the same refinery's competition with American oil on the Singapore and Penang markets placed the Atjeh-adjacent East Coast of Sumatra at the intersection of Dutch, American and British commercial interests in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

**Gomies** was an Ambonese Marechaussee of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] who served in the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]-[[1911]] — one of the seven who climbed the steile waterral under fire in the overval on the schuilplaats of the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]]* in [[May 1911]], and one of the six (with [[Nussy]], [[Kamagi]], [[Womor]], [[Paliaman]], [[Liklikwatil]] and [[Rampisela]]) chosen for the omtrekking with the colonne's *beste mensen*. He was shot at from "vijf pas afstand" and missed — "Over geluk viel niet te klagen."

## The waterral climb (May 1911)

In the overval on the schuilplaats of the Tiriō bende in [[May 1911]], Gomies was one of the seven who climbed the steile waterral under fire: "*[[Nussy]], Gomies, [[Liklikwatil]], [[Enoch]], [[Kamagi]], [[Paliaman]] en [[Oesoel]] klommen er tegenop*." On the rand, Gomies was shot at from "vijf pas afstand" — five paces — and missed: "Over geluk viel niet te klagen" — no complaint about luck.

## The omtrekking

Gomies was one of the six — with [[Nussy]], [[Kamagi]], [[Womor]], [[Paliaman]], [[Liklikwatil]] and [[Rampisela]] — chosen by [[Schmidt]] for the omtrekking with the colonne's *beste mensen*. The omtrekking was the flanking movement that surrounded the bende's ladang; the six *beste mensen* were the colonne's most trusted soldiers.

## The levensgevaarlijke afdaling

Gomies was one of the five — with [[Van Drongelen]], [[Nussy]], [[Enoch]] and [[Kamagi]] — who made the *levensgevaarlijke afdaling* along the steile wand that gained the M. 95 (a *[[karabijn (M. 95)|karabijn]]) and the small boy — the son of *[[Tgk. Tjhi Majét]]*.

## Significance

Gomies is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as one of the named Ambonese Marechaussees of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] — one of the seven who climbed the waterral, one of the six chosen for the omtrekking, and one of the five who made the *levensgevaarlijke afdaling*. The book's argument is that the colonne method depended on the *beste mensen* — the most trusted soldiers — and that Gomies was one of them.

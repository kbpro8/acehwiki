**Lowe** was an *officier van gezondheid* 1e klasse (medical officer first class) of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]] who served in [[1879]] as chief of the medical service of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] during the expedition to [[Lampanas]], [[Lamtoba]] and [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen]], as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]). His establishment comprised a second medical officer — the Dane [[Nöckedved]] — twenty orderlies, a military pharmacist (attached for the chemical testing of the well-water, on account of the rumours that the population of the district was skilled in the use of poisons), and a train of *tandoes* (doolies) for the carriage of the sick.

## The campaign

On the [[March from Lampanas to Lamtoba (28–29 July 1879)|march into the Lamtoba valley]] on [[29 July]] [[1879]], when shots from a steep height on the left wounded a European fusilier and two convict labourers attached to the mortars, the wounded were at once dressed by Lowe. In the occupied valley his duties widened with the appearance of epidemic disease: when, in the refugee capture of the night march, several of the sixty-seven prisoners brought in were found covered with an eruption, the Eerstaanwezend Officier van Gezondheid was called to examine it and pronounced it to be smallpox (*pokken*) — the appearance of which in the valley was at once reported by him through the column-commander to the Militaire Bevelhebber, and which led first to the removal of the sufferers from the bivak and ultimately to the order for the whole column's withdrawal and quarantine.

During the [[Return march from Selimoen to Lamtoba (18–20 August 1879)|return march from Selimoen]], Lowe accompanied — together with the chief of staff, [[W. Cool]] — the rear division, where the ambulance's claims mounted with every hour of the climb; at the close-range affair in the brushwood he received, at a few paces' distance, the fire of the hidden Achehnese skirmishers at the same moment as the chief of staff. On the final departure from Lamtoba on [[22 August]] he accompanied the first, fast-marching division of sick, devising — since the number of bearers and *tandoes* no longer sufficed — the carriage of the sick in blankets arranged for the purpose.

In the [[Quarantine at Lampanas (August–September 1879)|quarantine camp at Lampanas]] the doctor held, the authors record, a daily exact inspection of the bivak; nevertheless the sick-rate rose with each day. Of the column's 2,234 men, 769 were within a month dead (39), evacuated (346), in the ambulance (127) or quarter-sick (267) — the statistical reckoning on which the book's sombre verdict on tropical campaigning rests.

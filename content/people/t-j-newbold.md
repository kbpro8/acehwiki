**T. J. Newbold** was a British colonial official and author of *Political and Statistical Account of the British Settlements in the Straits of Malacca* (London, [[1839]]), a work cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] in his discussion of the Achehnese calendar. He is one of a number of European writers on the Malay world whose works Snouck Hurgronje draws upon, qualifies, or corrects in the course of *The Achehnese*.

In his account of the [[Eight-year cycle (Achehnese calendar)|eight-year cycle]] by which the Achehnese compute the day of the week on which each month begins, Snouck Hurgronje notes that the Achehnese year-letters correspond with those "found in some Arabic handbooks, which Newbold cursorily refers to as in use among the Malays (British Settlements in the Straits of Malacca, II p. 336)". Snouck Hurgronje had earlier encountered the same year-letters in an almanac described by [[Dr. A. B. Cohen Stuart]] in a Sumatran publication. None of the calendars given in that article, however, entirely agrees with the Malay-Achehnese calendars; such an agreement may be presumed to be probable "in the case of that mentioned by Newbold, for the Achehnese derive their Malay lore principally from the Straits. Newbold, however, gives no particular whence this might be decided."

The reference is characteristic of Snouck Hurgronje's method: he acknowledges the pioneering work of earlier writers on the Malay world, while at the same time noting the limitations of their accounts. Newbold's work remains a valuable early nineteenth-century source for the Malay Peninsula and the Straits Settlements, but is here cited only for its passing mention of a calendar whose details he does not give.

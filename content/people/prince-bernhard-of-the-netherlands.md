**Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands** was a member of the Dutch royal family who, on [[27 August]] [[1935]], was appointed **kapitein der mariniers à la suite** of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]]. The royal appointment was intended to reinforce the close relationship between the Dutch monarchy and the marine corps, which had long served as a symbol of the Dutch presence in the [[Dutch East Indies]], including the province of [[Aceh]]. The honour reflected the importance the Dutch crown placed on maintaining a strong colonial military force in the archipelago.

## Royal Patronage and Colonial Context
The appointment of Prince Bernhard was part of a broader policy in the 1930s to modernise the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]] and to showcase royal support for the forces stationed in the East Indies. By linking the royal house directly to the marines, the Dutch government sought to underscore the legitimacy of its colonial rule over territories such as [[Aceh]].

## Significance
Prince Bernhard’s ceremonial role highlighted the intertwining of the Dutch monarchy with colonial military institutions, a relationship that would later be tested during the Japanese invasion of the Dutch East Indies in [[1942]].

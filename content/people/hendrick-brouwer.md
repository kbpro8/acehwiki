**Hendrick Brouwer** (c. 1581-1643) was a Dutch navigator and administrator in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] who, as commander of two ships at [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] in [[1612]], drafted the "Secrete Instructie" of [[4 April]] [[1612]] recommending the destruction of the Bandanese nutmeg trees as the means to coerce the Bandanese into submission — the policy that [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] would later execute in the [[1621]] conquest — and would go on to become Governor-General of the VOC from [[1632]] to [[1636]]. Brouwer is the principal Dutch strategic advisor of the Banda chapter of Van der Woude's [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The 1612 Secrete Instructie

Brouwer, "zich met twee schepen voor Banda bevindende", drafted the "Secrete Instructie" on [[4 April]] [[4 April]] [[1612]]. The instruction, addressed to his superiors, recommended the construction of a fort at [[Lontor|Lontor]] on [[Groot-Banda|Great Banda]] — "om daer mede een toon te hebben, van meester te sijn ouer beyde partijen" — and the destruction of the Bandanese nutmeg trees as the means to coerce the Bandanese into submission: "te weeten de nooteboomen te vernielen, destrueren, ende omhouden, datt lichtelijcker ende met minder peryckel sall connen geschieden dat hun vaste nesten te ouerweldighen, daer sij doch de vlucht wt nemen alsmen die vermeestert, waerdoor weynich voordeel op haer becomen".

The instruction's argument was the working-out of the [[Heeren Zeventien|Heeren Zeventien]]'s secret instructions of [[11 April]] [[1608]] and [[4 May]] [[1609]]. Brouwer wrote the instruction "oock kennisse hebbende van de intentie der heeren Bewinthebberen" — with knowledge of the bewindhebbers' intention — and his advice "kwam daarmee al bijzonder goed overeen". The instruction also recommended the blockade of the Banda islands, though Brouwer acknowledged that this could only be done with jachts that were not available.

## The strategic argument

Brouwer's strategic argument — the destruction of the Bandanese nutmeg trees — was the principal Dutch strategic innovation of the Banda chapter. The argument inverted the usual commercial logic: instead of seeking to control the trees in order to harvest their produce, Brouwer proposed to destroy the trees in order to coerce the Bandanese into submission. The argument was based on the observation that the Bandanese, whose food supply was already precarious, could not survive the destruction of their nutmeg trees — their principal commercial asset — and would be forced to submit. The argument would be executed by [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] in the [[1621]] conquest: the Bandanese were depopulated, the nutmeg trees were parcelled out into *perkenier* plantations worked by imported slaves, and the Banda islands became the principal Dutch nutmeg monopoly.

## The later career

Brouwer would go on to become Governor-General of the VOC from [[1632]] to [[1636]]. His Governor-Generalship, which Van der Woude does not pursue, was marked by the consolidation of the Dutch position in the East Indies and the expansion of the Dutch trade with Japan and Formosa. Brouwer's earlier role as the strategic advisor of the Banda chapter — and the author of the "Secrete Instructie" of [[4 April]] [[1612]] — frames him as the principal Dutch strategic thinker of the early VOC period between [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] (whose *Discours* of [[1608]] had anticipated the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme) and [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] (whose [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]] would complete it).

## Significance

Hendrick Brouwer is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch strategic advisor of the Banda chapter and the principal author of the "Secrete Instructie" of [[4 April]] [[1612]] that recommended the destruction of the Bandanese nutmeg trees as the means to coerce the Bandanese into submission. The instruction — written "oock kennisse hebbende van de intentie der heeren Bewinthebberen" — was the working-out of the [[Heeren Zeventien|Heeren Zeventien]]'s secret instructions of [[1608]]-[[1609]] and the proximate intellectual cause of the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] conquest of Banda in [[1621]]. Brouwer's role in the Dutch-Atjeh relationship was indirect: by [[1612]] the strategic centre of Dutch-Indies policy had shifted from Atjeh to Banda, and the Banda experience — not the Atjeh experience — would define the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme.

**Hoek** was a nineteenth-century Dutch author whose monograph *Herstel* ("Restoration") — cited by [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] as *Hoek's „Herstel"* — was one of the standard printed sources for the diplomatic acts marking the restoration of the Netherlands East Indies to Dutch sovereignty after the British interregnum. As the title implies, the work concentrates on the years around [[1816]]–[[1817]], when the colonial administration was being handed back by Britain in execution of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1814)|Convention of London of 1814]].

## Cited by Van der Kemp

Van der Kemp invokes Hoek in a footnote on page 37 of his article on [[Raffles' Atjeh-overeenkomst van 1819|Raffles's Acheh agreement of 1819]]. The note concerns the [[Convention of 24 January 1817|convention of 24 January 1817]], an Anglo-Dutch instrument that sought to settle the practical questions arising from the restoration. Van der Kemp writes that the text of the convention "is to be found on page 170 of Hoek's *Herstel* and on page 334 of [[H. D. Levyssohn Norman|Levyssohn Norman's]] *Britsche Heerschappij*", placing the two works side by side as the printed authorities for the document.

The same footnote refers the reader to Van der Kemp's own earlier treatise on the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1814)|1814 treaty]] (*Bijdragen*, vol. XLVII, 1897, p. 278), so that Hoek's *Herstel* forms part of the documentary apparatus on which the article's reconstruction of the [[1817]] settlement rests.

## Significance

Hoek's role in the present article is that of a documentary source. The convention of [[24 January]] [[1817]] was the immediate diplomatic background to Raffles's mission to [[Acheh]] in [[1819]], for it was this convention that the British subsequently invoked when contesting the scope of Dutch rights on Sumatra. By citing Hoek alongside Levyssohn Norman, Van der Kemp points his readers to the two Dutch compilations in which the relevant treaty texts were available in print. Hoek belongs, with Levyssohn Norman, [[L. W. G. N. Netscher|Netscher]], [[L. J. P. J. Jeekel|Jeekel]] and [[J. E. de Sturler|De Sturler]], to the network of late-nineteenth-century Dutch colonial historians whose editorial labours made possible Van der Kemp's own archival researches.

**Fujiwara** — the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] name for the Japanese intelligence officer (probably **Major Iwaichi Fujiwara** of the Imperial Japanese Army) who, after the [[Fall of Penang|fall of Penang of 19 December 1941]], organised the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] in [[Malakka|Malakka]] from Indonesians present there, and whose initial "F" gave the movement its name. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Fujiwara is the principal Japanese intelligence officer of the pre-invasion Atjeh rising, and the founder of the wider **Fujiwara-kikan** that would later re-emerge in [[1945]] as the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] under [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]].

## Identification

The [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history]] identifies Fujiwara through the interrogation of a Menadonese soldier at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]] in late February [[1942]]. The Menadonese had boasted of his admiration for the Japanese; under sharp interrogation he confessed "dat hij door een aantal Atjehers te zijn overgehaald toe te treden tot een wijdvertakte z.g. F. beweging. … Later werd bekend, dat dit een afkorting was van Fujiwara, een Japanner, die in Malakka een georganiseerde colonne had uit aldaar aanwezige Indonesiërs. Reeds na de val van Penang (19 December 1941) maakte Fujiwara met de opbouw van deze organisatie een aanvang."

The staff history's identification of Fujiwara as the "Japanner" who founded the F-beweging in Malakka after the [[Fall of Penang|fall of Penang]] is the principal Dutch printed identification of the F-beweging's organiser. The identification is consistent with the post-war recognition of **Major Iwaichi Fujiwara** (藤原岩市, 1909–1986) as the founder of the *Fujiwara-kikan* — the Japanese intelligence network that, under Fujiwara and later [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]], would govern the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Jappentijd]] occupation of Atjeh.

## The F-beweging's membership insignia

The staff history records that "alle tot deze beweging toegetredenen zouden door de Japanners als hun vrienden en medewerkers worden beschouwd en het insigne (witte band met rode letter F.) zou bij hun komst op zichtbare wijze worden gedragen." The white armband with the red letter F was thus the visible insignia by which F-beweging members were to identify themselves to the Japanese on their arrival — an insignia whose meaning was unknown to the Dutch command at Kotaradja until the Menadonese's interrogation.

## Significance

Fujiwara is, in the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the principal Japanese intelligence officer of the pre-invasion Atjeh rising, and the founder of the wider *Fujiwara-kikan* that would govern the Japanese occupation of Atjeh through the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Jappentijd]]. The staff history's emphasis on Fujiwara's role in the *opbouw* of the F-beweging after the [[Fall of Penang|fall of Penang]] — and on the Dutch command's initial incomprehension of the letter "F." — frames Fujiwara as the principal Japanese intelligence officer whose concealed operation determined the operational shape of the Atjeh campaign. The Fujiwara–[[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] succession is the staff history's principal instance of the institutional continuity of the Japanese intelligence network in Atjeh from [[1941]] to [[1945]].

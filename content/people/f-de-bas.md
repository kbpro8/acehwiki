**F. de Bas** was a major of the General Staff (Majoor van den Generalen Staf) who, in No. 1 of the *Militaire Spectator* of [[1886]] (pp. 10-11), came forward to a certain degree as a champion of the conduct of general [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] in Atjeh — one of the last public moves in the old controversy between the apologists of the [[Afwachtende houding|wait-and-see posture]] of [[1874]] and its critics, to which [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]]'s pamphlet of the same year had given new fuel. Verstege names him, together with the *Militaire Spectator* itself — the oldest military journal of the Netherlands, which had taken sides for the waiting posture "from the very beginning" — in his demonstration that the champions of the passive posture forgot that the military history could not point to a single example in which a militant, freedom-loving people had been brought to submission by mere passive waiting (see [[Afwachtende houding]]).

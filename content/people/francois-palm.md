**François Palm** was a Dutch [[person|colonel]] of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Mariniers]] who served both in the East Indies and in European wars.  He commanded the 64‑gun ship *[[Devvenerr]]* during the 1667 Dutch expedition against the English fleet on the River Thames, later became colonel of the newly formed [[Regiment de Marine]] and fought in the defence of the [[Holland Waterline]] during the French invasion crisis of [[1672]], and distinguished himself at the [[Siege of Naarden (1674)|Siege of Naarden]] where he led the half‑marine regiment that stormed the [[Huizerpoort]] and the contre‑escarp, sustaining a wound from a grenade and witnessing the death of two of his sons.  His career illustrates the fluid movement of Dutch marine officers between colonial campaigns against the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] and major European conflicts.

## Early VOC service and East Indies actions
Palm began his naval career aboard VOC vessels in the East Indies, taking part in minor actions against the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] in the mid‑[[1660s]].  This experience in combined naval‑land operations prepared him for later amphibious assaults in Europe.

## Thames raid of [[1667]]
In the spring of [[1667]] Palm was appointed “commandant” of *[[Devvenerr]]*, which sailed toward [[Gravesend]] and participated in the landing at [[Chatham]] on [[19 June]] [[23 June]] [[1667]].  He coordinated the landing of 450 marines and soldiers on the island of [[Chappey]] (modern [[Chatham]]) and oversaw the destruction of English warships during the raid on the River Thames.

## Service in the [[Regiment de Marine]]
After the Thames expedition Palm joined the newly formed [[Regiment de Marine]].  In [[1672]] he was promoted to colonel of the marine regiment and tasked with defending the [[Holland Waterline]] against the French invasion threat.  He fought in the battles of [[Woerden]], [[Naarden]] and [[Seneffe]], contributing to the successful repulsion of French forces.

## Siege of Naarden (1674)
During the [[Siege of Naarden (1674)|1674 siege]], Colonel Palm led the half‑marine regiment that stormed the [[Huizerpoort]] and the contre‑escarp.  Contemporary accounts note that he was wounded by a grenade and that two of his sons were killed in the fighting (see page [[277]] of the source).  In recognition of his bravery, the [[Kazerne van Naarden]] was later named after him.

## Later career and Aceh context
Palm’s later career continued to involve deployments to the East Indies, where the Dutch Marine Corps protected merchant vessels of the [[Dutch East India Company]] calling at [[Aceh]].  His expertise in siege warfare was later applied to fortifications during the mid‑nineteenth‑century [[Aceh War (1873–1904)|Aceh War]], linking his 17th‑century experience to later colonial conflicts.

## Significance
François Palm’s trajectory—from VOC actions in Southeast Asia to key roles in the Second Anglo‑Dutch War, the defence of the Dutch Republic, and the Siege of Naarden—exemplifies the interconnected nature of Dutch maritime and military history.  His service bridges the early Dutch‑Aceh confrontations of the 1660s with the later colonial wars of the 19th century, highlighting the continuity of Dutch marine expertise across continents and centuries.

**Habib Abdul Rachman El-Zahir** — also written *Abdurrahman Zahir* — was an Arab religious leader and diplomat of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] who, before the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|first Dutch expedition]] of [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]], travelled to [[Constantinople|Konstantinopel]] to seek Ottoman assistance for the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]]; who, returning empty-handed to [[Penang|Penang]], helped to constitute the so-called *[[Atjehsche Raad van Achten|Atjehsche Raad van Achten]]* (Atjeh Council of Eight) that managed Atjeh's external affairs; and who, after the [[Conquest of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the kraton]] of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] to the Dutch in [[January 1874|January]] [[1874]], returned to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[August 1876|August]] [[1876]] to take an active part in leading the resistance, landing on the [[Oostkust|East Coast]] and traversing the coast to raise money and recruits for the *heiligen oorlog* (holy war) against the Dutch. His surrender and exile to [[Jeddah|Djeddah]] in [[October 1878|October]] [[1878]], on a pension of 30,000 guilders a year, marked the end of the first phase of organised religious resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]].

## The Mission to Constantinople (1872–1873)

Before the outbreak of the [[Aceh War|war]], the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] had entered into negotiations with foreign powers to obtain assistance against the impending Dutch attack. Habib Abdul Rachman el-Zahir, an Arab *Habib* (a descendant of the Prophet Muhammad through his daughter Fatimah), was despatched to [[Constantinople|Konstantinopel]] to seek the assistance of the [[Sultan of Turkey|Sultan of Turkey]] — the Ottoman Sultan, as Caliph of Islam, being the natural addressee of such an appeal from a Muslim state threatened by a Christian power. The mission was undertaken already before the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|first expedition]]. It failed: the Ottoman Empire, itself under diplomatic pressure from the European powers, declined to intervene on Atjeh's behalf. Habib Abdul Rachman returned empty-handed to [[Penang|Penang]].

## The Atjehsche Raad van Achten (1873)

At Penang, while the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|second Dutch expedition]] was being assembled at [[Batavia|Batavia]], the so-called *[[Atjehsche Raad van Achten|Atjehsche Raad van Achten]]* (Atjeh Council of Eight) was constituted. This body, of which Habib Abdul Rachman was a leading member, managed the external affairs of Atjeh — that is, it continued to seek foreign assistance and to conduct diplomatic correspondence on Atjeh's behalf. Peace proposals made by it to the Dutch were rejected.

## The Return to Groot-Atjeh (August 1876) and the Holy War

In [[August 1876|August]] [[1876]] Habib Abdul Rachman returned to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. When his proposals for foreign intervention had been rejected, he had landed on the [[Oostkust|East Coast]] and, traversing the coast, had collected money and recruited men for the *heiligen oorlog* (holy war) against the Dutch. He now took an active part in the further leadership of the resistance, in concert with the Atjehese chiefs and the religious leaders.

## The Invasion of June 1878 and the Surrender of October 1878

In [[June 1878|June]] [[1878]], under the governorship of [[General van der Heijden|General van der Heijden]], Habib Abdul Rachman openly invaded the Dutch *stelling* in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] with a great force, took possession of the mountain passes, and besieged [[Kroeng Raba|Kroeng Raba]]. The settled population fled. Van der Heijden was forced to return in haste with his troops from the coast. The insecurity within the Dutch line increased steadily; women and children, even some of the Dutch camps, had to be brought within the walls of the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]].

The Netherlands Indies government was compelled to acknowledge that no question could any longer be of a conciliatory, purely defensive posture. It instructed Van der Heijden to drive Habib Abdul Rachman out or to render him harmless, and sent him the necessary reinforcements, so that by mid-[[August 1878|August]] [[1878]] there were again over 10,000 troops in Atjeh. The enemy, for his part, was reinforced by bands streaming in from the coasts — "onze kusten, waar onze vlag woei" (our coasts, where our flag flew), as Kruijt bitterly notes. First he was driven from [[Kroeng Raba|Kroeng Raba]] and out of the passes, and then out of the *stelling*.

Habib Abdul Rachman soon had enough of it. He opened negotiations and submitted in [[October 1878|October]] [[1878]]. He was taken to [[Jeddah|Djeddah]] by a Dutch warship, received 30,000 guilders a year income, and, at the time of Kruijt's writing in [[1896|1896]], was still living in enjoyment of this ample annual pension.

## Significance

Habib Abdul Rachman el-Zahir is the principal religious-diplomatic figure of the early phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. His mission to [[Constantinople|Konstantinopel]] represents the Atjehese attempt to invoke the Ottoman Caliphate against Dutch aggression — an attempt that, if it had succeeded, would have internationalised the conflict and might have prevented the Dutch conquest. His role in the [[Atjehsche Raad van Achten|Atjehsche Raad van Achten]] at [[Penang|Penang]] represents the Atjehese attempt to conduct their own external affairs after the failure of the Ottoman mission. His return to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[August 1876|August]] [[1876]] and his *heiligen oorlog* of [[June 1878|June]] [[1878]] represent the religious-military leadership that, after the death of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] in [[January 1874|January]] [[1874]], sustained the resistance in the absence of a central political authority.

His surrender in [[October 1878|October]] [[1878]] and his comfortable exile on a Dutch pension in [[Jeddah|Djeddah]] represent the Dutch policy of buying off dangerous enemies — a policy that [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]] would later identify as recurrent and problematic, and that would be applied again, with far more catastrophic consequences, to [[Teuku Umar|Teuku Umar]] in [[1893]]–[[1896]].

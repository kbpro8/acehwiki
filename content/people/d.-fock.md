**D. Fock** — Dirk Fock ([[1858]]-[[1941]]) — was a Dutch liberal politician who served as minister of Colonies ([[1908]]-[[1909]]) and, later, as governor-general of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipelago ([[1921]]-[[1926]]). His colonial ministry covered the [[Avondpost "Wekeer" articles (October 1907)|"Wekeer" affair]] of October [[1907]] and the [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|Van Heutsz report]] of June [[1908]]; his conduct of the parliamentary response to both is the immediate political frame of the [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] dismissal.

## The "Wekeer" response

When, in October [[1907]], *[[De Avondpost]]* published the [[Avondpost "Wekeer" articles (October 1907)|"Wekeer" articles]] exposing needless cruelties committed against the Atjehnese population under [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s command, [[D. Fock|Fock]] — then minister of Colonies — advised the chambermembers "hun tijd niet te verspillen aan deze artikelen" — not to waste their time on these articles. The governor-general, he said, had full confidence in [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]; both he and [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] considered reinforcement of the troop strength in Atjeh unnecessary. The inspection tour that governor-general [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] undertook in December [[1907]] — at [[D. Fock|Fock]]'s request — was, however, to reverse both judgements.

## On the Commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart

[[D. Fock|Fock]]'s dry verdict on the [[Commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart ([[1902]])|commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart]] — the commission installed in [[1902]] whose report, when it finally appeared in [[1914]], filled no fewer than 33 volumes — was that "naar het meer dan volmaakte [was] gestreefd" — it had aspired to more than perfection. More modest, but at least as useful, in [[D. Fock|Fock]]'s judgement, were the *economisch-historische bijdragen* that the [[Ministerie van Koloniën]] published in [[1904]] under the editorship of [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]], [[D. Fock|Fock]] himself and [[Kielstra]].

## On Lohman's *adat*-law proposals

During the debate on the revision of the *adat*-law provisions for the indigenous population, [[D. Fock|Fock]]'s only support in the [[Tweede Kamer]] came from [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.) and [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] (a.r.). When the amendment of his party-colleague [[Van Idsinga]] — broadly containing what [[Lohman (De Savornin Lohman)|Lohman]] wanted — was moved, [[D. Fock|Fock]] took it over with the words: "aangezien de voorsteller (Van Idsinga) en ik (Fock) het amendement in dezelfde zin opvatten" — since the proposer and I construe the amendment in the same sense. [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] drily notes that "[[D. Fock|Fock]] with this not-very-clear phrase covered his retreat".

## Fock and Snouck Hurgronje

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] was [[D. Fock|Fock]]'s advisor at the [[Ministerie van Koloniën]]. The *adat*-law disagreement between [[Lohman (De Savornin Lohman)|Lohman]] and [[D. Fock|Fock]] was, in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]]'s judgement, "voornamelijk van formele aard" — mainly of a formal nature — since in either case everything would depend on practical implementation.

## Significance

[[D. Fock|Fock]]'s colonial ministry is the political hinge of the post-[[1904]] Atjehnese period. His initial defence of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] in October [[1907]] and his reluctant acquiescence in the [[Van Heutsz's inspection tour of Atjeh (December 1907)|inspection tour]] of December [[1907]] frame the fall of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] and the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] course-correction of [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908]].

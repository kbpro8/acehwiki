**W. A. Coblijn** — *gepensionneerd Kolonel van het Oost-Indisch Leger* (retired Colonel of the Netherlands Indies Army) — was a Dutch military writer, veteran of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], and the author of the polemical pamphlet *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]* ('s-Gravenhage: [[De Gebroeders van Cleef]], [[1893]]), in which he reviewed the twenty years of war since [[1873]], attributed the failure of the Dutch arms not to a *fatum* (destiny) but to the interference of *de politiek* (politics), and set out a detailed [[Coblijn's programme for the subjugation of Atjeh|programme for the subjugation of Atjeh]] with a reserve-brigade cantoned in the [[Padangsche Bovenlanden]] and supported by fast cruisers. In [[1879]], as Majoor commanding the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie]], he marched as voorhoede-commandant of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] from [[Lampanas]] to [[Lamtoba]] and [[Selimoen]] — the tocht of which [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis* ([[1889]]) gives the eyewitness account.

## Service in the campaign of 1879

Coblijn wrote as an eyewitness of the war. In the closing tactical argument of his pamphlet he records of the operations of [[1879]], under the command then renewed in Atjeh: *bij onzen langen tocht van Lampannas naar het dal van Lamtoba en vandaar naar Selimoen* (on our long march from Lampannas to the valley of Lamtoba and thence to [[Selimoen]]) the troops with a few companies repeatedly searched kampongs and rendered the captured weapons unusable. The passage places him in the columns that operated west of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in the campaign year 1879, the year of the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|operations against the XXII Moekims]] and the [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|operations against the XXVI Moekims]] of generaal [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heijden]].

### The Lamtoba–Selimoen tocht

That "long march" Coblijn made as Majoor of the 6e Bataljon, and *Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis* records his part in it. On the [[March from Lampanas to Lamtoba (28–29 July 1879)|march of 28–29 July 1879]] he commanded the voorhoede, deliberately made stronger than custom demanded and furnished with Mineurs, mountain artillery and an ambulance under [[Nöckedved]], "since in mountain terrain a column files man behind man along narrow footpaths and support comes late." He again commanded the voorhoede of the [[Punitive expedition against Kepaja Doewa (August 1879)|punitive expedition against Kepaja Doewa]] in early August. In the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|march to Selimoen]] of [[13 August]] [[1879]] it was Coblijn who, when the voorhoede lay bunched under the enemy's fire before the marsh-crossing and the earthen trench at the ruined kampong of [[Kepaja Doewa]], "gave last tot stormen" — sending the 1st platoon of the 2nd Company of the 6e Bataljon, with Kapitein [[Clerkson]] at its head, to turn the work through the dammed-up spring-marsh and the bamboo abatis, while he joined himself to the 2nd platoon holding the front: the position was carried with three wounded. The next day, [[14 August]], he likewise let the voortroep — a company strong — storm the fire of [[Lam-Lepong]], after which the kampong was burned. On the [[Evacuation of the Lamtoba valley (21–23 August 1879)|evacuation of the valley]] of [[22 August|22]]–[[23 August]] Coblijn commanded the second, slower division — the 6e Bataljon, the section of cannon, the Mineurs and the vivres train — which, having had to leave part of the stockpiled provisions behind for want of bearers, completed the march on the 23rd, reuniting the whole column in the beach bivouac of Lampanas.

## Military writings

Coblijn had earlier published *De Indische Infanterie* (The Indies Infantry), a *geschrift* (treatise) which he cited in *Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen* as having most strongly disapproved, years before, of the withdrawal into a more concentrated position. He was also the translator into Dutch of Von der Goltz's *Das Volk in Waffen*, which he cited as the door ons uit het Duitsch vertaalde boek *Het gewapende volk* (The Nation in Arms), the work from which he drew the military lessons — including the maxim of the German Kaiser, that of Moltke, and the military writers Derrécagaix and Horsetzky — with which he combatted the anti-militarist argument of Mr. [[S. van Houten]] in the *Vragen des Tijds*.

## The pamphlet of 1893

In *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]* — bearing as its motto the sentence *"When work of this kind is to be done, it should be done promptly"* — Coblijn rejected the proposition of oud Vice-Admiraal [[Lieutenant P. ten Bosch|P. Ten Bosch]], who in his work *De sluiting der Atjehsche kust* (The closing of the Atjeh coast) had written that *er een fatum op den Atjeh-oorlog rust* (a destiny rests upon the Atjeh war). Against philosophical resignation Coblijn placed the responsibility of men: the war had been opened unprepared in [[1873]], with only two battalions armed with Beaumont rifles, because the expedition's leaders reckoned three battalions together with the Barisans of [[W. E. Kroesen|Kroesen]] sufficient and made no provision for reserve or replenishment. He condemned the condemning of the leaders of the first expedition by the [[Enquête-commissie on the First Atjeh Expedition (1873)|enquête-commissie]], the strategic dispersion under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] and [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heijden]], the triple échec of [[Batoe Iliq Expedition (July 1880)|Batoe Iliq]] in [[1880]], the experiment of the [[Civil government period|civil government]] under [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]] and [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], the passive concentration after the [[Government decisions of 19 August 1884|decisions of 19 August 1884]], and the piecemeal attacks on [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|Kaloet]] in 1892. Against the latter's newspaper critics he cited [[Hoe moet Atjeh gepacificeerd worden? (Scherer)|Scherer]]'s [[Scheepvaartregeling (1892)|scheepvaartregeling]] and, in a naschrift written while the pamphlet was already at the printer's, the brochure *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* of majoor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]].

## Coblijn and Van Heutsz

Coblijn acknowledged the importance of Van Heutsz's brochure but differed from its programme on the essential point: where Van Heutsz adopted — in improved form — the scheepvaartregeling of Resident Scherer as the first of four means of subjugation, Coblijn held that the work *kan geschieden* (can be done) only by the sword, without untimely interference of politics. With the late ex-Governor-General [[O. van Rees|Van Rees]] he endorsed the demand that the [[Issuance of firearms to Acehnese|issue of firearms to Acehnese]] be stopped for good, and he quoted from the brochure Van Heutsz's own figures — that at the beginning of [[1891]] already some thousands of rifles stood under license in native hands, about eighty per cent breech-loaders, and that cartridges were being supplied in hundreds of thousands from the war magazine — together with Van Heutsz's words: *Dwaze illusie!* and *Misschien zullen wij ze nog eenmaal bloedig moeten terughalen* (Foolish illusion! ... Perhaps we shall one day have to take them back bloodily).

## Significance

Coblijn is significant as one of the most outspoken Dutch military publicists on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] before the definitive conquest. His pamphlet of [[1893]], printed at Haarlem by Ruigrok & Co. and preserved, stamped, in the library of the Second Chamber of the [[Staten-Generaal]], belonged to the same discussion of that year as the brochure of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]: both officers demanded an end to the tactics of the defensive, and both looks to a solution by force. Coblijn's work is distinguished by its bitterness against the parliamentary and ministerial management of the war, its extensive citation of the pamphlet literature of the preceding decade, and its detailed tactical and organisational programme — the only full-length printed statement of the *partij van de daad* (party of action) written by a veteran of the campaign of 1879.

**H. Geurtjens** was a Dutch Catholic missionary-scholar credited in the source document *DLP-84-Atjeh* with the descriptive section on Acehnese material culture, whose catalogue of agricultural tools, household items, and weapons preserved a detailed record of the artefacts of daily life in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] at the close of the [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification]] era and the transition to civil government under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Governor-General Van Heutsz]]. His observations, written in Dutch and assembled in the early twentieth century, document the blend of indigenous craftsmanship and European influence in Acehnese society at a moment when the opening of the [[Atjehweg|Great Atjeh Road (Dutch: *Groote Atjehweg*)]] and the [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajoweg]] was beginning to accelerate the integration of the residency (Dutch: *gewest*) into the wider economy of the [[Dutch East Indies|Netherlands-Indies]] state.

## Contribution to Acehnese Material Culture

Geurtjens catalogued a wide range of objects — from sugar-cane hand presses (Dutch: *suikerriethandpersen*) to checkerboards (Dutch: *damspel*) and blunderbusses (Dutch: *donderbussen*) — illustrating the material assemblage of an Acehnese household in the early colonial period. His descriptions cover agricultural implements such as the rice-pounder (Dutch: *rijststampers*), household vessels in brass and coconut shell, weaving equipment for the dark self-woven garments worn especially by the [[Gajo|Gajo]] highlanders, and the weapons — klewang, rencong, and the long flintlock muskets (Dutch: *snaphanen*) — that remained in everyday use long after the formal end of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[1904]]. The catalogue is the more valuable because Geurtjens recorded not only the form of each object but its Acehnese name and its place in the daily round of the village (Dutch: *kampung*), thereby preserving a lexical record of the material vocabulary of pre-electrification Atjeh.

## The Missionary-Ethnographer Tradition

Geurtjens's work belongs to the tradition of missionary-ethnographer writing on the Netherlands Indies, in which Catholic and Protestant clergy stationed in the *Buitengewesten* produced some of the most detailed contemporary accounts of indigenous societies. Like the work of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] on the religious and social institutions of [[Acheh|Acheh]], Geurtjens's catalogue was assembled in the field, from direct observation, and was addressed to a metropolitan readership that had little other access to the material realities of the residency. The catalogue thus complements the more famous institutional histories of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] by documenting the artefacts through which Acehnese life was actually carried on — the cooking pots, the betel-quid sets (Dutch: *sirihpruimen*), the prayer-mats of the village prayer house (Dutch: *missigit*), and the wood carving (Dutch: *snijwerk*) that adorned the more substantial houses of the *banda* villages around [[Banda Acheh|Banda Acheh]]. His record is one of the small but significant sources for the material history of Atjeh in the transition from war-footing to civil administration.

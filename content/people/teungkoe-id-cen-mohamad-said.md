**Teungkoe Id-cen / Mohamad Said** — also written *Mohamad Said*, *Mohammad Said* — was, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the overall commander of the Achehnese *stelling* east of the old [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* on [[Tjot Karieng]] during the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|battle of Kroeng-Kali on 23 June 1896]], the culminating engagement of the first semester of [[1896]]. Kruisheer describes him as "*candehoold-eigenly Mohamad Said*" — that is, his actual name was Mohamad Said — and identifies him as "from the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] / [[III Moekims Lam-Rebo]] area", a territorial origin which places him in the eastern sector of [[Groot-Atjeh]] where the principal June [[1896]] operations unfolded. His command at Tjot Karieng brought together the eastern Achehnese forces — including [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah]] — in a defensive *stelling* which the Dutch *maréchaussée*, *bergartillerie* and infantry were forced to assault in the course of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing operation of 22–23 June 1896]].

## Territorial Origin and Command Authority

Kruisheer's identification of Teungkoe Id-cen / Mohamad Said as "from the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] / [[III Moekims Lam-Rebo]] area" places him in the eastern sector of Groot-Atjeh, where the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] formed the territorial base of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and where the [[III Moekims Lam-Rebo]] formed the southern part of the Tjot Karieng *stelling*. The combination of these two territorial identifications suggests that Teungkoe Id-cen was a figure whose religious-military authority crossed *moekim* boundaries, and his designation as *algemeen aanvoerder* — overall commander — of the Achehnese *stelling* east of the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* indicates that he had been given command authority over Achehnese forces from multiple *moekims*. The southern part of the Tjot Karieng *stelling* "belonged to" the III Moekims Lam-Rebo, and the eastern *stelling* on Tjot Karieng was the principal Achehnese defensive position which the Dutch *2e Colonne* under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] assaulted on [[23 June]] [[1896]] in the course of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing operation]]. The fact that an *algemeen aanvoerder* had been appointed at all is significant: it suggests that the Achehnese had developed a unified command structure for the eastern *stelling*, in contrast to the more fragmented command patterns which characterised the western IV/VI Moekims operations.

## The 23 June 1896 Battle of Kroeng-Kali

The [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|23 June 1896 battle of Kroeng-Kali]] was the culminating engagement of the first semester of [[1896]]. The operation involved two Dutch colonnes: the 1e Colonne (sea-landing) under [[G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. Hansen]] which landed at [[Ladoeng]] in the early hours of [[23 June]] [[1896]] and marched over [[Glé Broeë]] to the [[XXVI Moekims]] plain, and the 2e Colonne (land) under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] which advanced from [[Lam-Njong]] at 6:45 AM. The 2e Colonne's advance brought it to the old [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* — a square earthen redoubt with 15 m sides, walls 1.5 m high and 0.75 m thick, with a 4 m-wide *bamboe-doeri pagger* and a small swivel gun — which was stormed by the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] of the [[5e Bataljon]], with 2e Luit. [[Heyligers]] the first to enter (with *hoornblazer* [[van Spanje]] and fusilier [[Veeges, No. 31895|Veeges No. 31895]]). Teungkoe Id-cen's command of the eastern *stelling* on Tjot Karieng thus placed him at the centre of the culminating Dutch tactical success of the first semester of [[1896]]. The Achehnese forces under his command — including [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah]] — retreated after the storming of the Kroeng-Kali *benteng*, with [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] retreating to [[Lam-Tobah]].

## Significance

Teungkoe Id-cen / Mohamad Said's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the unified Achehnese command authority which the eastern sector had developed by June [[1896]] — the *algemeen aanvoerder* whose territorial origin in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb / III Moekims Lam-Rebo area gave him cross-moekim authority, and whose command at Tjot Karieng placed him at the centre of the culminating Dutch tactical success of the first semester. His identity as a religious teacher confirms the continuing religious-military character of the Achehnese command, and his survival through the engagement (Kruisheer does not record his death) suggests that he continued to play a role in the second-semester resistance.

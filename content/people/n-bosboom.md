**N. Bosboom** was the Dutch Minister of War who, in [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]], appointed [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Generaal-Majoor C. J. Snijders]] to the rank of General and to the office of [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht]] for the duration of the [[First World War|First World War]] mobilisation. Bosboom had earlier considered Snijders' older brother [[W. G. F. Snijders|Luitenant-Generaal b.d. W. G. F. Snijders]] for the supreme command, but judged that the latter's known deafness was too great an obstacle. Bosboom later recorded his argumentation and his relationship with Snijders in his book *In moeilijke omstandigheden*, which the [[1944]] biography of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] cites as a principal source for the first two mobilisation years.

## The Appointment of August 1914

Bosboom's *In moeilijke omstandigheden* describes, with reference to Clausewitz and Von der Goltz, his ideal image of the *veldheer* — "strategische kennis, vrucht van de studie der krijgsgeschiedenis, en strategische bekwaamheid, die gezond verstand, algemeene ontwikkeling, fantasie, intuitie, spontaneïteit in zich sluit, en een krachtig lichaamsgestel" — and his deliberations over which senior officer should be entrusted with the supreme command if war broke out. He had thought of [[W. G. F. Snijders|W. G. F. Snijders]] (Inspecteur der Infanterie b.d. since [[1905]]), an officer of great strategic ability and of character "dat hij alleszins voor het opperbevel over onze strijdkrachten berekend mocht worden geacht." His "bekende hardhoofdigheid" was the obstacle. Bosboom ultimately decided on the younger brother, the [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chef van den Generalen Staf]], who was appointed by *Koninklijk Besluit* of [[9 Augustus|9 August]] [[1914]] No. 7.

**Kolonel Stemfoort** — *J. W. Stemfoort*, *Kolonel der Infanterie* — was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] colonel of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] who, in [[1896]], commanded the Dutch expeditionary reinforcements sent to [[Atjeh]] in response to [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] from the Dutch alliance. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as the commander of the *suppletie-troepen* (reinforcement troops) from [[Padang]] and two battalions from [[Batavia]], each approximately 20 officers and 500 men strong, who landed at [[Oleh-leh]] at the outbreak of the [[1896]] crisis.

In Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, Stemfoort is further identified as the principal Dutch field commander of the first semester of [[1896]] — the officer who arrived with [[J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] on [[7 April]] [[1896]] aboard the steamer *Carpentier* and who was *voorloopig belast met het Civiel en Militair Gezag* (temporarily charged with the Civil and Military Authority) of Aceh after Vetter's departure on [[28 June]] [[1896]]. In the second semester of [[1896]] — the principal subject of *[[ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel]]* — Stemfoort served a **second tenure** as *wd. Civiel en Militair Gouverneur*, from [[11 July]] [[1896]] to [[17 November]] [[1896]], following the [[Death of Generaal-Majoor De Moulin (7 July 1896)|death of Generaal-Majoor J. J. K. de Moulin]] by sunstroke on [[7 July]] [[1896]]. His combined command record, across both tenures and the field operations of the first semester, is the most extensive of any Dutch officer in either volume of the work.

## The [[1896]] expedition

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, in response to [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]], the Dutch government dispatched reinforcements to Atjeh under the command of Kolonel Stemfoort. The reinforcements consisted of *suppletie-troepen van Padang en twee Bataljons van Batavia, ieder ongeveer 20 officieren en 500 man sterk* — *reinforcement troops from Padang and two battalions from Batavia, each approximately 20 officers and 500 men strong* — which *stapten te Oleh-leh aan wal* — *stepped ashore at Oleh-leh*. Three further battalions stood ready on [[Java]] to be sent to that *labyrinth of muddy rice-fields and overgrown kampongs with unintelligible names*, as the album characterises the theatre of operations.

## The arrival of General Vetter

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, alongside the dispatch of Stemfoort's reinforcements, the Dutch government dispatched General [[General Vetter|Vetter]] to Atjeh as *Regeerings-Commissaris* (Government Commissioner). The album's reading of the dual appointment is that *men in het paleis van den Gouverneur-Generaal de zaken op Noord-Sumatra ernstig inziet* — *one in the palace of the Governor-General sees the situation in North Sumatra as serious* — and that the dual appointment was intended to signal a change of policy from the *concentratie-stelsel* (concentration system) of General [[Deykerhoff]]. The album records that Vetter's first act was the dismissal of the Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies — a sign that the Dutch intended to follow a different policy from that of Deykerhoff.

## The cost of the relief expeditions

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Stemfoort's reinforcements, having landed at [[Oleh-leh]], were employed in the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] and the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]]. The album's narrative of these engagements notes that the *strijd hevig was, dien Kolonel Stemfoort aanvoerde, maar ook, dat de overwinning was aan ons* — *the battle was fierce that Colonel Stemfoort commanded, but also that the victory was ours* — and that the two posts were relieved *ten koste van Kapitein Geluk en zeven minderen, die sneuvelden, en van de Luitenants ten Seldam en Hockstein, die met 23 minderen werden gewond* — *at the cost of Captain Geluk and seven subordinates who fell, and of the Lieutenants ten Seldam and Hockstein who, with 23 subordinates, were wounded*.

## Arrival, Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit, and the April Operations (per *ATJEH '96*)

Stemfoort arrived with Vetter on [[7 April]] [[1896]] aboard the steamer *Carpentier* (Kruisheer also gives the name *Bantam*), together with [[J. F. Breijer|Majoor Breijer]] and the [[9e Bataljon]]. His immediate task was the relief of the besieged posts of [[Biloel]] (under [[P. H. van der Wedden|Kapitein Van der Wedden]]) and [[Lam-Koenjit|Lamkoenjit]] (under [[K. H. J. Creutz Lechleitner|1e Luit. Creutz Lechleitner]]), and he commanded the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief column of 8 April 1896]] — a multi-column operation with the [[3e Bataljon]] (under [[K. W. Steinmetz|Maj. Steinmetz]]), the [[6e Bataljon]] (under [[H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]]), the [[9e Bataljon]] (under [[H. Bendien|Bendien]]), the [[12e Bataljon]] (under [[G. F. Soeters|Maj. Soeters]]), the [[14e Bataljon]] (under [[G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. Van der Noordaa]]), two *bergbatterijen* (mountain batteries), a cavalry detachment, engineers and ambulances, with [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Lt.-Col. Van Vliet]] as *Chef van den Staf* (Chief of Staff). The relief operation broke the sieges and prepared the evacuation of both posts on [[12 April]] [[1896]], and Stemfoort continued with the [[20 April 1896 multi-column operation|three-colonne operation of 20 April 1896]] which completed the wholesale evacuation of the Dutch buitenlinie (outer line). Through April and May, Stemfoort directed the systematic *tuchtigingen* (punitive expeditions) against the regions which had joined Umar: the [[tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Goet on 14 April 1896]], the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop on 17 April 1896]], the [[night surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe on 28–29 April 1896]], and the [[Excursie to Moekim Lamtengah (21 May 1896)|excursie to Moekim Lamtengah on 21 May 1896]].

## The Three-Day Offensive of 23–25 May [[1896]] and the June Tuchtigingen (per *ATJEH '96*)

The culminating operation of Stemfoort's first-semester command was the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] against [[Teuku Umar|Umar]]'s stelling at [[Lam-Asan]], [[Lam-Pisang]], [[Tjot Tjako]], [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Lam-Tjroeng]] and [[Lam-Poelau]]. The operation involved four colonnes plus reserve, with [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] commanding the *bergcolonne* which stormed [[Tjot Tjako]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]] (with 2e Luits. [[Wagener]] and [[Vis]] and the *maréchaussée* (military police (maréchaussée)) [[Wongsodikromo, No. 33780|Wongsodikromo]] and [[Todikromo, No. 28097|Todikromo]] the first to reach the summit at 5:43 PM), and with [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]]'s 4e compagnie storming the *versterking* (fortification) Belang on [[21 May]] [[1896|21 May]] and entering [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]] right after 1e Luit. [[M. D. J. de Jongh|de Jongh]]. Stemfoort personally inspected the Lam-Asan position on [[23 May]] [[1896]] and issued the orders for the [[25 May]] [[1896|25 May]] attack on Boekit Seboen/Lam-Tjroeng/Daman/Lam-Poelau. After the three-day offensive, Stemfoort directed the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims on 9–10 June 1896]] (with two colonnes under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] and [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]), the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik on 16–17 June 1896]] (with three colonnes under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]]), and the land column of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing of 22–23 June 1896]] — the operation which culminated in the storming of the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* (fort) by [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]]'s compagnie on [[23 June]] [[1896]], with 2e Luit. [[Heyligers]] the first to enter.

## First Restoration as Civiel en Militair Gouvernur (28 June [[1896]])

On [[28 June]] [[1896]], with the departure of Vetter as Regeeringscommissaris per stoomschip *Coen* for [[Batavia]], the office of Civiel en Militair Gouvernur was restored, and Stemfoort was appointed (provisionally) to the office. [[K. F. H. van Langen|Resident van Langen]] was simultaneously relieved of his temporary functions as *Civiel Bestuurder van het gewest* (Civil Administrator of the territory). Stemfoort's appointment represented the institutional closure of the Vetter mission and the formal transfer of the Aceh command back to the regular colonial military structure. The closure of the first semester of [[1896]] under Stemfoort's command confirmed that the Dutch counter-offensive had achieved its principal objectives — the breaking of the sieges, the destruction of Umar's stelling at Lam-Pisang, the death or dispersion of his principal sub-commanders, the *tuchtiging* (punitive expedition) of the Lam-Krak and V Moekims Montassik regions, and the storming of the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* — even as Umar himself remained at large in [[Lepong]].

Stemfoort's first tenure as *wd. Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* was, however, exceptionally brief. On [[5 July]] [[1896]] the newly-appointed governor [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor J. J. K. de Moulin]] arrived at [[Oleh-leh]] aboard the steamer *Reaal* and formally assumed the office, and Stemfoort departed for [[Batavia]] on the same steamer — his first tenure having lasted just seven days (28 June – 5 July [[1896]]).

## Second Tenure as wd. Civiel en Militair Gouverneur (11 July – 17 November [[1896]])

Stemfoort's second tenure as *wd. Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* — the principal subject of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* — began on [[11 July]] [[1896]], four days after the [[Death of Generaal-Majoor De Moulin (7 July 1896)|death of Generaal-Majoor J. J. K. de Moulin]] by sunstroke during his first *excursie* (reconnaissance (excursie)) to the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims (Kroeng-Raba)]] on [[7 July]] [[1896]]. Stemfoort had been en route to Batavia when De Moulin's death required his recall, and he *aanvaardt kolonel Stemfoort het gezag* — assumed the authority — on [[11 July]] [[1896]]. The Governor-General [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] then sent a telegram of [[18 July]] [[1896]] — *eene nadere uiteenzetting zijner bedoelingen omtrent de verdere actie* — which laid down the *[[Beheersching|beheersching]]* (domination) programme that would govern Dutch operations for the next four months: *De XXV Moekims moeten in haar geheel door ons beheerscht worden, wij moeten de woelgeesten en de hen volgende benden onschadelijk maken, minstens verdrijven...*

The four months and six days of Stemfoort's second tenure — covered in detail in *Hoofdstuk III* (pp. 1–144) and the opening of *Hoofdstuk IV* (pp. 145–160) of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* — were characterised by the slow, methodical extension of Dutch control from the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) into the [[XXVI Moekims]], [[XXII Moekims]], [[IV Moekims]] (Kroeng-Raba / Lok-Nga), and the [[Selimoen]] highlands. The principal operations and innovations of Stemfoort's second tenure include:

- [[11 July]] [[1896]] — [[Reconnaissance in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb (11 July 1896)|Reconnaissance in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]].
- [[15 July]] [[1896]] — [[Occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga) (15 July 1896)|Occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga)]] — the first permanent Dutch forward position beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) in the second semester.
- [[18 July]] [[1896]] — Telegram of [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General Van der Wijck]] — the *beheersching* (domination programme) programme which supplemented the *pacificatie* (pacification) framework of the [[Notulen der conferentie van 18 Juni 1896|conference of 18 June 1896]].
- [[24 July]] [[1896]] — [[Reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims (24 July 1896)|Reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims]] by [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]].
- [[29 July|29]]–[[30 July]] [[1896]] — [[Trip to Teungkoe di Lengkong and Pièng (29–30 July 1896)|Trip to Teungkoe di Lengkong and Pièng]] — diplomatic contact with the religious leader [[Habib Lengkong]] of the [[Oostergebergte]].
- Late July [[1896]] — [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] *verlaat Lepong en vestigt zich in Lohong* — leaves Lepong and settles in [[Lohong]].
- [[10 August|10]]–[[14 August]] [[1896]] — [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|Excursie to Lepong and Lohong]] — established that Oemar was in Lohong, that his band had been reduced to 100 men, and that he had fallen out with the *kedjoeroean* (council of headmen) of Lohong.
- [[23 August|23]]–[[27 August]] [[1896]] — [[Trip to XXII Moekims (23–27 August 1896)|Trip to XXII Moekims]] — five-day reconnaissance by a colonne under [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]].
- Late August [[1896]] — [[Occupation of Samahani (late August 1896)|Occupation of Samahani]] by a *bataljon infanterie met hulp-wapens* — the third Dutch forward position beyond the *geconcenteerd linie*.
- [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]] — [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|First Selimoen Expedition]] — five-day reconnaissance-in-force into the [[Selimoen]] highlands by a colonne under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].
- [[7 September]] [[1896]] — [[Construction of Blang Bintang Camp (7 September 1896)|Construction of Blang Bintang Camp]] — *begin van den bouw van een kampement op de Blang Bintang (Tjot Mantjang)* — the second permanent Dutch forward position.
- Mid-September to [[28 October]] [[1896]] — [[Pasenstelsel (September–October 1896)|Introduction of the Pasenstelsel]] — the pass system for the regulation of movement between the Dutch-held and Acehnese-held territories.
- [[28 October]] [[1896]] — [[Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri (28 October 1896)|Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri]] and [[Capture of Kota Ba Teuē (28 October 1896)|Capture of Kota Ba Teuē]] — the most significant Dutch military victory of October [[1896]].
- [[30 October|30]]–[[31 October]] [[1896]] — [[Trip to Ladoeng and Kroeng Raja (30–31 October 1896)|Trip to Ladoeng and Kroeng Raja]].
- [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]] — [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|Second Selimoen Expedition]] — the last major Dutch operation of the second semester.
- [[8 November]] [[1896]] — [[Bridge over the Kroeng Pengapit (8 November 1896)|Bridge over the Kroeng Pengapit]] — constructed by the *Genietroepen* (Engineers).

The second tenure closed on [[17 November]] [[1896]] — *overgave en overname van het bestuur, Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort keert naar Java terug* — when the newly-appointed [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet]] arrived at Koeta-Radja and the [[Handover of Command from Stemfoort to Van Vliet (17 November 1896)|handover]] took place. Stemfoort returned to Java, and Van Vliet formally assumed the governorship. A portrait of Kolonel Stemfoort appears in *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* opposite page 83 — the second of the six portraits bound into the volume, and the visual record of the officer whose command defined the second semester of [[1896]].

## Significance

Stemfoort's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the immediate Dutch military response to [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] — the commander of the reinforcements that allowed the Dutch to undertake the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief expeditions]] of April [[1896]]. His appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] is brief, but it establishes the scale of the Dutch reinforcement effort at the outbreak of the [[1896]] crisis — some 1040 men of the two Batavia battalions, plus the Padang *suppletie-troepen*, with three further battalions held in reserve on Java.

His significance in *ATJEH '96* (1e Deel) is that of the principal Dutch field commander of the first semester of [[1896]] — the officer whose command record covers every major Dutch operation from the relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit on [[8 April]] [[1896]] to the storming of the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* (fort) on [[23 June]] [[1896]]. His personal inspection of the [[Lam Asan]] position on [[23 May]] [[1896]] and his receipt of a letter of thanks from the Regeeringscommissaris after the operation confirm his direct involvement in the culminating engagement, and his appointment as provisional Civiel en Militair Gouvernur on [[28 June]] [[1896]] confirmed the institutional closure of the Vetter mission. His command record, as enumerated in the master article list, is the most extensive of any Dutch officer in the first volume.

His significance in *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* (2c Deel) is that of the *wd. Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* whose second tenure — from [[11 July]] to [[17 November]] [[1896]] — characterised the second semester of [[1896]]. Under Stemfoort's second tenure, the Dutch extended their effective control from the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) into the [[XXVI Moekims]], [[XXII Moekims]], [[IV Moekims]] (Kroeng-Raba / Lok-Nga), and the [[Selimoen]] highlands; introduced the [[Pasenstelsel (September–October 1896)|pasenstelsel]] for the regulation of movement; and undertook the most ambitious operations of the second semester — the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]], the [[Capture of Kota Ba Teuē (28 October 1896)|capture of Kota Ba Teuē]], and the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]]. Stemfoort's second tenure was thus the bridge between the immediate crisis of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|Umar defection]] and the appointment of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] in [[1898]] — the slow, methodical *pacificatie* (pacification) of the second semester of [[1896]] which prepared the ground for the decisive *korte oorlog* phase.

## Advisory role on garrison reorganisation (1897)

H. T. Damsté's *Drie Atjeh-Mannen* records that Stemfoort, together with [[J. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]], advised on the disposition of Dutch forces in Atjeh during [[1897]]. Stemfoort's proposed reorganisation of the Atjeh garrison — submitted to [[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] for consideration alongside Van Vliet's counter-proposal — came down to four points: abolishing the posts at [[Pakan Kroeng Tjoet|Pakan Kroeng Tjoet]], [[Boekit Karang|Boekit Karang]], [[Roempit|Roempit]], [[Sirón|Sirón]], Lamreng, [[Blang|Blang]], [[Lamsih|Lamsih]], and [[Olehleh-Oost|Olehleh-Oost]] and moving [[Kota Pohama|Kota Pohama]] to [[Kroeëng Raja|Kroeëng Raja]]; keeping a half battalion at Loknga and a battalion at Tjot Mantjang; stationing two battalions at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] to secure order there and in the 7 Moekims Baid, plus a battalion at [[Selimoen|Selimoen]] to destroy the war party in the upper [[XXII Moekims]]; and conducting an expedition to the 7 Moekims Pedir and [[Peukan Baroe|Peukan Baroe]]–[[Peukan Sot|Peukan Sot]]. Van Vliet preferred instead to keep a half battalion at [[Samagani|Samahani]] and one and a half battalions at Indrapoeri until the [[Tramweg aanleg in Atjeh (1897)|tramway]] to Indrapoeri was constructed. Both Van Vliet and Stemfoort agreed that the [[Lhong expedition (January 1897)|Lhong expedition]] was unavoidable to check the growing influence of [[Toekoe Oemar|Teukoe Oemar]], though Van der Wijck would have preferred to postpone it.

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart (1943)

Van Heutsz, "Atjeh's maarschalk Voorwaarts", stood for the time being under Stemfoort and moreover opposed to him, Stemfoort being "not inclined to seize and pursue [[Teukoe Oemar|T. Oemar]] immediately and unceasingly."

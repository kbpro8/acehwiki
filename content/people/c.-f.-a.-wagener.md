**C. F. A. Wagener** was a Dutch 1st lieutenant of the *[[Korps Marechaussee|Korps Marechaussée]]* who was wounded in the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Anak Galoeng]] on [[29 June]] [[1896]] and killed a year later, on [[25 September]] [[1897]], while leading the *marechaussée* pursuit on the far side of the [[Glé Betoes]].

## At Anak Galoeng, [[29 June]] [[1896]]

[[P. H. R. Beuming]] names Wagener among the officers of the *marechaussée* who took part in the night attack, describing him as the officer *die nimmer op 't appel mankeerde, als er iets bijzonders te doen viel* — who never failed to answer the roll when anything special was afoot.

In the fighting inside the *[[Benting|benting]]* he received a gunshot wound through his left hand and was at the same moment set upon with the *[[Klewang|klewang]]*; Lieutenant [[Rijnen]] struck the assailant down. At the same instant Rijnen himself was wounded by a shot in the hip and a *klewang* blow across the back, whereupon Wagener shot the enemy down with two well-aimed revolver shots *en redde op zijn beurt zijn collega van een wissen dood* — and in his turn saved his colleague from certain death.

## Death on the Glé Betoes, [[25 September]] [[1897]]

On [[25 September]] [[1897]], while the patrol of Captain [[J. N. C. Baron van Heerdt]] was engaged at the [[Goeah Teroes]] cave, a forced labourer was sent back to [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] for reinforcement. Three brigades of *marechaussée* immediately marched out under Wagener's command *om te trachten aan de andere zijde van den Glé Betoes den vijand op te vangen* — to try to catch the enemy on the other side of the Glé Betoes.

The brigades returned the following day. After a violent fight in the mountains the enemy had escaped, carrying his dead and wounded with him. The *marechaussée* patrol had likewise a sad loss to lament: *Luitenant Wagener en 2 marechaussée's sneuvelden* — Lieutenant Wagener and two *marechaussées* were killed.

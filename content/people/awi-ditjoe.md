**Awi Ditjoe** — also **Awi Titjoe** — was the *hoeloebalang* (war-chief) of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] who, in chapter VIII of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]], sailed with 4,000 fighters in a hundred vessels to [[Pasej-Samodra|Samoedra]], presented himself as the envoy of the king of Sjahroe'n Noewi, smuggled four champions each inside a chest, and sprang them on the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]] in his court — capturing him and carrying him back to Sjahroe'n Noewi, where he was made to "tend chickens" (*menggombala ayam*). The episode — the central set-piece of the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] chronicle narrative — is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the *bedekte taal* record of the long rivalry between the [[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]] complex and [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej-Samudra]].

## The chronicle episode

In chapter VIII of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]], as [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] recounts, the king of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] — "a very great kingdom with many champions and countless people" — was told that [[Pasej-Samodra|Samoedra]] was a thriving trading-place. He asked his war-chiefs: "is there no one among you who can capture for me that radja of Samoedra?" [[Awi Ditjoe]] — "a very strong and brave man" — volunteered, on condition of being given 4,000 champions. The king gave him the 4,000 and a hundred vessels.

Sailing to [[Pasej-Samodra|Samoedra]] under pretence of trade, Awi Ditjoe presented himself as the king of Sjahroe'n Noewi's envoy, then — before the audience — smuggled four champions each inside a chest, with the order: "when you have come before the king of Samoedra, open the chest from inside, come out, and seize the radja". The chests were brought with great ceremony; the king's letter was read; the champions sprang out and seized the radja of Samudra. The war-chiefs of Samudra fell upon the men of Sjahroe'n Noewi, but the latter threatened: "if you attack us, we will kill your radja". The people of [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasaj]] held back; Awi Ditjoe sailed home with his captive.

## The "tending chickens"

On arrival at Sjahroe'n Noewi, Awi Ditjoe presented the captive radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samoedra]] to his king, who "was much pleased, bestowed on the conquerors fitting ceremonial garments", and ordered the captured king of Samudra to "*menggombala ayam*" — "tend chickens". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes this as *menggembala Pasaj semajam* — "to tend the like of Pasaj" — i.e. to be made the keeper of one's own kind: a *bedekte taal* form for the humiliation of being reduced from king to peasant-keeper, but also an allegorical comment on the original unity of the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]-[[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]] complex, of which the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] king was now keeper.

## The recovery of the radja

The humiliation drove [[Sidi Ali Ghiyat oed'dir|Sidi Ali Ghiyat oed'dir]] and the elders of [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasaj]] to fit out a ship, buy Arab merchandise (for "in those days the people of Pasaj understood Arabic"), and dispatch [[Sidi Ali Ghiyat oed'dir|Sidi Ali]] in person to Sjahroe'n Noewi. After three embassies — each bearing a fabulous golden gift (a golden tree with gemstone fruit, a golden chess-set, two golden ducks on a golden bowl of water) — the king of Sjahroe'n Noewi, on being asked "what is your desire?", received the answer: "we ask for our king, the tender of the chickens". The king returned him, observing "because he is a Muslim".

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Awi Ditjoe is the principal named actor of the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] chronicle episode — the *hoeloebalang* whose capture of the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]] is the central set-piece of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe|Sadjarah Malajoe's]] chapter VIII, and whose "tending chickens" humiliation encodes, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the long [[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]]–[[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] rivalry that the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] finally closed.

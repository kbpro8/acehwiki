The most famous Sultan of Acheh, posthumously known as Meukuta Alam ("Crown of the World"), who reigned [[1607]]-[[1636]]. His conquests included Johor ([[1613]]) and Pahang ([[1618]]), and he made several attempts to drive the Portuguese from Malacca, culminating in his most celebrated naval expedition in [[1628]] with a fleet of enormous proportions.

He is the central heroic figure of the Hikayat Malem Dagang. In the legend-poem, he is portrayed as receiving the defecting prince Raja Raden and his brother Si Ujut from Malacca, granting them territory, and then leading a great naval expedition after Si Ujut's treachery. He is sometimes anachronistically credited by the Achehnese with introducing Islam to Acheh, though Islam pre-dates him by two centuries. His tomb in the ancient gampong Kuta Alam is revered, and the name Meukuta Alam is probably preserved in that gampong's name.

His successor had the disciples of the heretical mystic Hamzah Pansuri put to death and his books burned, an episode of religious persecution that occurred in the generation after Iskandar Muda's reign.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Meukuta Alam

**Meukuta Alam** — literally "the Crown of the World" — was the posthumous name of the Sultan of [[Acheh]] known in life as **Eseukanda Muda** (Iskandar Muda), who reigned from [[1607]] to [[1636]]. His ordinances, real or supposed, are known in Achehnese tradition as the [[Adat Meukuta Alam]], and his person stands at the centre of the popular legend which traces the descent of the [[Panglima Polem|house of Polem]] from the sultans. For his full biography and edicts, see [[Sultan Meukuta Alam]]; this article treats of those references to him that occur in Chapter I of Vol. I, where he is invoked as the type of the great sultan of the past.

## The "Meukuta Alam" edicts

In his discussion of the actual powers of the port-kings, [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] observes that even the edicts ascribed by general consensus to Meukuta Alam "confine themselves within very narrow limits, although that prince had at his command a considerable fleet and a small standing army." The demands made on the uleebalangs by the port-kings in the most flourishing period of their rule — the second half of the 16th and the first half of the 17th century — were extremely moderate, and bear witness partly to the want of power on the part of these princes to control the government of the interior, partly to the small interest they seem to have taken in any such interference. The edicts of Meukuta Alam, moreover, "are not evidence of a state of things that ever actually existed, but simply the expression of the wishes of the king and his councillors as to what they would see carried out."

## The legend of the first Panglima Polem

The Meukuta Alam of tradition also figures in a legend which purports to relate the origin of the Polem family. The great Meukuta Alam, so the story runs, once suffered from a venereal disease, and in order to cure it had recourse to the remedy — held in much repute among natives — of having intercourse with a healthy woman. For this purpose he employed a black slave, and did in fact recover from his disease. The slave however became pregnant; and, as in Acheh great weight is given to descent from the mother's side, the sultan was distressed at the prospect of having to acknowledge as his son the child of a black woman. He accordingly sent her forth into the jungle — that is, into the district of the [[XXII Mukims]] — and the son, whose life was spared by the highlanders, became the first [[Panglima Polem]], chief of what is in many respects the most important sagi of Acheh.

Snouck dismisses this tradition — of which there are also other versions less insulting to the honour of the Polem — as containing "not a single grain of historic truth." The title of "elder brother" (*po lua*), he suggests, probably typified the original relation between the powerful sagi-chief and the sultan, just as the official title of "grandfathers" (*ne*) was borne by [[Teuku Ne' Raja Muda Seutia]] of [[Meura'sa]] and [[Teuku Ne' Peureuba Wangsa]] of the [[IX Mukims]] of the XXV.

## Place on the ninefold seal

Meukuta Alam appears, *par excellence* the prince of old Acheh, on the [[Sarakata with chab sikureu'eng|ninefold seal]] (*chab sikureu'eng*) of the sultans. The choice of the eight names inscribed in the circular spaces surrounding the central name rests with the reigning sultan; but "those of Eseukanda Muda (Meukuta Alam), *par excellence* the prince of old Acheh, and of the immediate predecessors of the reigning king are never omitted, but great freedom of choice is shown as regards the rest." His mediation is also invoked in the formal preambles of royal letters patent, alongside that of [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam Sapiatodin]], [[Sultan Ahmat Shah|Alaedin Ahmat Shah]] and others.

**Habib Long** — identified in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as a former *Bengaloesche koewachter* (Bengali cattle-keeper) — was the leader of a bandit group encamped at the *kampong* of [[Lamkrak]] in [[1896]], whose presence in the sector immediately south of the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* became one of the immediate occasions of the [[1896]] crisis. He is named in the album's narrative of the events leading up to [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] as the chief enemy of [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan]] in the Lamkrak sector, and his refusal to be dislodged by Djohan — and Djohan's subsequent refusal to dislodge him — was the proximate cause of the breach between Djohan and the Dutch.

## Background

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] introduces Habib Long as a *zekere Habib Long, vroeger een Bengaloesche koewachter* — "a certain Habib Long, formerly a Bengali cattle-keeper" — who, by early [[1896]], had gathered a *bende* (gang) at Lamkrak. The album does not record how a former Bengali cattle-keeper came to bear the honorific *Habib* — properly a descendant of the Prophet — nor how he came to be at the head of an armed band in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The inconsistency is itself notable: the Atjehnese usage of *Habib* as an honorific could, by the late nineteenth century, be applied to figures whose claim to Prophetic descent was dubious or manufactured, and the album's identification of Long as a *Bengaloesche koewachter* is a deliberate Dutch deflation of his religious pretensions.

## Operations in the Lamkrak sector

The album records that Habib Long and his *bende* had, in the months preceding [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]]'s defection, carried out a series of attacks in the territory of [[Toekoe Nja Din|Nja Din]] — the wife of [[Toekoe Oemar]] who effectively governed the [[VI Moekims]]. In these attacks Long's band burned houses, murdered a village head and his wife, and killed several of Oemar's best fighters. The incursion was, in the Dutch reading, a provocation by the religious-party *Moslemin* against the *oeloebalang* party that Oemar represented — and it was the immediate occasion of Oemar's appointment by the Dutch to "purify" the [[XXV Moekims]] of *Moslemin* bands.

## The breach with Toekoe Djohan

The immediate occasion of [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]]'s defection was his refusal to march on Lamkrak. The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after the Dutch had decided that Habib Long must be dislodged from Lamkrak, Djohan *vond redenen, te weigeren, op te trekken tegen Lamkrak* — "found reasons to refuse to march on Lamkrak." The album characterises the refusal as *te onbegrijpelijker* — the more incomprehensible — given that Habib Long's band had recently carried out the outrages in the territory of Nja Din and had killed several of Oemar's own fighters. The refusal was, however, the proximate signal of the defection: it was followed by Djohan's flight, the desertion of the Dutch-allied *hoofden*, and the siege of the Dutch outposts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]].

## Significance

Habib Long's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the catalyst — perhaps inadvertently — of the [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|1896 defection]]. His presence at Lamkrak, and the Dutch decision to use [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]] to dislodge him, placed Djohan in a position where his refusal to act became the open signal of his defection. The album's deflation of his religious status — its identification of him as a *Bengaloesche koewachter* rather than as a true *Habib* — is also a document of the Dutch colonial practice of distinguishing "genuine" *Habibs* such as [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] from the broader class of self-styled religious adventurers who operated on the fringes of the Atjehnese war.

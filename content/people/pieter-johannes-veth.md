**Pieter Johannes Veth** (1814–1895) was a Dutch professor of Oriental languages at the University of Leiden, the doyen of Dutch Arabic and Malay studies in the middle decades of the nineteenth century, and the author of the 1873 monograph *Atchin en zijne betrekkingen tot Nederland* ("Acheh and its Relations to the Netherlands"), which is cited at three points by [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] in his 1900 article on the [[Acheh treaty of 1819|Raffles–Coombs convention]].

## Veth's Two Acheh Writings

Van der Kemp distinguishes between two of Veth's treatments of the Acheh question. The earlier is an article published in *De Gids* of 1850 (14th year, part 1, p. 166, note 2), in which Veth reproduced the principal features of [[John Anderson|Anderson]]'s account of the two Achehnese pretenders and of the divergence of opinion between [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] and [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Bannerman]] over the Acheh mission of 1819. The later is the self-standing monograph *Atchin en zijne betrekkingen tot Nederland*, published at Haarlem in 1873, in which Veth treated the same subject "independently" (pp. 86 ff.). Both writings earned Van der Kemp's respect as the standard Dutch scholarly accounts of the Acheh succession dispute and the British intervention of 1819.

## The 1850 Article in *De Gids*

Veth's 1850 article, though confined to a long footnote, performed a useful bibliographical function: it made available to Dutch readers the gist of Anderson's English-language narrative, drawn from Anderson's *Acheen* (1840), pp. 137–138. The article gave particular prominence to the conflict between Raffles, who insisted on treating with the "old king" [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] at Pedir, and Bannerman, who, supported by [[Captain Coombs|Captain Coombs]], maintained that the commission's instructions required it to treat with the ruling party at Acheh itself. Van der Kemp cites Veth's summary with approval and uses it as an anchor for his own longer narrative of the Penang–Calcutta quarrel of March 1819.

## The 1873 *Atchin* Monograph

Veth's 1873 monograph, *Atchin en zijne betrekkingen tot Nederland*, is cited twice by Van der Kemp. The first citation is in the context of Van der Kemp's account of the antecedents of the Acheh succession dispute. The 1786 letter of the [[Sultan Alauddin Muhammad Shah Jauhar|sultan of Acheh]] to [[Warren Hastings|Warren Hastings]], printed as Bijlage I of Van der Kemp's article, is identified by Veth as the work of the sultan whom he names, in *Atchin* p. 66, "Alaoe'd-din Mahmoed Shah Djohan"; this sultan, Veth had noted, came to the throne in 1781 and died in 1795. The second citation occurs in Van der Kemp's discussion of the [[Raja Akil]] of Siak, whom Veth had given an "attractive biographical sketch" in *Borneo's Westerafdeling*, volume II, pp. 150 ff.; the same Raja Akil, Van der Kemp cautions, is not to be confused with the relatively unimportant [[Said Akil]] of the Deli correspondence.

## Veth's Place in Dutch Acheh Scholarship

Van der Kemp places Veth alongside [[Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] and [[J. H. P. E. Kniphorst|Kniphorst]] as one of the more reliable Dutch scholars of the Acheh question. Veth's importance is twofold. As a Leiden orientalist, he possessed the linguistic equipment necessary for the serious study of Malay and Arabic sources, an equipment that the colonial administrators whose compilations Van der Kemp otherwise uses could not always claim. As a contributor to *De Gids*, moreover, he belonged to the Dutch liberal intelligentsia of the mid-nineteenth century, and his Acheh writings were shaped by the same reformist concerns — the emancipation of the colonial press, the reform of the cultivation system, the assertion of Dutch metropolitan control over colonial policy — that animated his better-known essays on the administration of the East Indies.

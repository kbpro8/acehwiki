**Van Lansberge** — Governor-General — was the Governor-General of the [[Nederlands-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] who, in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), is named as the Governor-General who attended the laying of the first stone of the [[Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] on [[9 October]] [[1879]]. Van Lansberge is the book's marker of the Governor-General's presence at the first stone of the Meusigit Raja: the colonial state's highest representative at the laying of the first stone of the mosque that [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]] had brought about as the colonial state's gift to the Acehnese population.

## The First Stone of the Meusigit Raja (9 October 1879)

"The mosque, whose first stone was laid on 9 October 1879 in the presence of Governor-General Van Lansberge, all the civil and military authorities, and many Acehnese chiefs and clerics, was completed in 1881 and on 27 December of that year was solemnly transferred, in the name of the Dutch government, to the Acehnese people by the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies, Pruys van der Hoeven." The first stone of the mosque — in the presence of Governor-General Van Lansberge, all the civil and military authorities, and many Acehnese chiefs and clerics — was laid on [[9 October]] [[1879]]. The mosque was completed in [[1881]] and on [[27 December]] [[1881]] was solemnly transferred to the Acehnese people by the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies, [[Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]], in the name of the Dutch government.

## Significance

Van Lansberge is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the Governor-General who attended the laying of the first stone of the [[Meusigit Raja]] on [[9 October]] [[1879]]. The book's argument — that the first stone was laid in the presence of the Governor-General, all the civil and military authorities, and many Acehnese chiefs and clerics — is the book's marker of the colonial state's consent and cooperation in the rebuilding of the Meusigit Raja. Van Lansberge is therefore the book's marker of the colonial state's highest representative at the first stone of the Meusigit Raja.

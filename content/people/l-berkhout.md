## Overview

L. Berkhout was a Dutch colonial administrator who served as *Controleur van de onderafdeeling Sinabang* (Controller of the Sinabang sub-district) in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] during the early twentieth century. He was present at the *bestuursvergaderingen* (administrative meetings) held at [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[19 January]] [[1914]] and [[20 January]] [[1914]], which were convened by Gouverneur [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. N. A. Swart]] to discuss the proposed *bestuursreorganisatie* (administrative reorganization) of the Netherlands Indies as it pertained to the Gouvernement. Although Berkhout attended both days of the proceedings, no recorded remarks appear under his name in the *notulen* (minutes), indicating that he was present as an observer or listener rather than as an active participant in the recorded discussion. His attendance nonetheless demonstrated the Gouverneur's commitment to including officials from even the most geographically remote parts of the Gouvernement in the consultation process, ensuring that the reform proposals were communicated across the entirety of Atjeh's administrative apparatus.

## The Onderafdeeling Sinabang and the Island of Simeulue

The *onderafdeeling* (sub-district) of Sinabang encompassed the island of Simeulue—then known in Dutch colonial records as *Simaloer*—which lay off the western coast of Sumatra in the Indian Ocean. Sinabang was the *hoofdplaats* (chief town) of the island and served as the administrative center from which the *Controleur* exercised Dutch authority over the local population. The island was relatively isolated from the main theaters of the Atjeh conflict, and its inclusion in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] reflected the broad territorial scope of the Gouvernement, which extended well beyond the northern tip of Sumatra proper to encompass offshore islands and coastal territories along the western seaboard. Gouverneur [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. N. A. Swart]] noted during the Koeta-Radja meetings that while part of the *onderafdeeling* [[Singkel]] fell within the *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory) alongside [[Groot-Atjeh]], the remainder of the Gouvernement consisted of *Inlandsche Zelfbesturen* (Native Self-Governments), a classification that likely applied to the more remote territories such as Simeulue. The island's population had historically maintained its own distinct cultural and social patterns, relatively insulated from the prolonged conflict that had devastated mainland Atjeh, and Dutch administrative control was exercised with a lighter hand than in the intensively governed zones of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## The Bestuursreorganisatie and the Peripheral Territories

The *bestuursreorganisatie* (administrative reorganization) discussed at the Koeta-Radja meetings in January 1914 posed particular questions for the outlying territories of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], including the island of Simeulue. The central reform proposal involved the potential abolition of the Gouvernement as a separate administrative entity and its incorporation into a new *gewest* (region) to be called [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]], which would also incorporate territories from the Residencies of the Oostkust van Sumatra and Tapanoeli. For officials like Berkhout, who administered territories on the periphery of the Gouvernement, the reorganization raised questions about whether their jurisdictions would remain under the same provincial structure or be transferred to different administrative units. The distinction between the *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory) and the *Inlandsche Zelfbesturen* (Native Self-Governments) was a central theme of the discussions, as the reform program aimed to standardize administrative structures across the Indies while respecting the unique conditions that had developed in Atjeh over decades of military governance. The fate of the peripheral *onderafdeelingen* like Sinabang depended on how the boundary between direct and indirect rule would be redrawn under the new administrative regime.

## The Controleur in Remote Island Administration

The role of the *Controleur* in a remote island posting such as Sinabang differed in significant ways from that of a *Controleur* in the intensively governed zones of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. On Simeulue, the *Controleur* was often the sole European representative of Dutch authority for extended periods, responsible for a wide range of administrative, judicial, and occasionally diplomatic functions that in larger centers would be distributed among multiple officials. The isolation of the posting required a degree of self-reliance and adaptability that was not always necessary in more centrally located positions, and the *Controleur* had to maintain effective working relationships with local indigenous leaders who exercised considerable de facto autonomy under the umbrella of Dutch suzerainty. Berkhout's journey from Sinabang to [[Koeta-Radja]] for the January 1914 meetings would have been a substantial undertaking, likely involving a sea voyage of several days, underscoring the logistical challenges inherent in governing a far-flung colonial territory and the importance that Gouverneur [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. N. A. Swart]] attached to ensuring comprehensive participation in the reform consultations.

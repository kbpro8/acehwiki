**Van Daalen** — *Toean Obers Van Daalen* (Toean Obers = the Acehnese idiom for the colonel) — was a Marechaussee commander of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] in [[Atjeh|Aceh]], the figure whose name — in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]) — is associated with the [[Colonne van Daalen 1904|Van Daalen Column]] and with the reputation of a man "for whom a human soul had no value." Van Daalen is the second-named in [[Musket]]'s list of the Marechaussee commanders, and the figure whose reputation the old hunter [[Datoek]] — a close comrade of Van Daalen — gives in the chapter *Jagerslatijn*.

## The List of Marechaussee Commanders

Van Daalen is the second-named in Musket's list: "Gilles Graafland, Van Daalen, Campioni, Christoffel, Van der Maaten, Scheepens, Watrin, Webb, Colijn, Geertsema Beckering, Darlang and the sergeants Nussy, Salowono, Pongoh … a colourful array." The list is the book's catalogue of the famous fighters and Marechaussee commanders whose names rang through the Indies like carillon music.

## The Datoek Judgement

"I know Toean Obers Van Daalen very well. That was a very great lord, 'a man whose life has no price!'" The old hunter [[Datoek]] — a close comrade of Van Daalen — reports: "I know Toean Obers Van Daalen very well. That was a very great lord, 'a man whose life has no price.'" The Datoek judgement — "a man whose life has no price" — is the book's — and the Acehnese memory's — judgement on Van Daalen: a man for whom a human life had no value. The judgement is the book's — and Datoek's — register of the Van Daalen Column reputation: the column that had been conducted without mercy.

## The Mercy for Animals

"But, sir, that Toean Van Daalen was not without tenderness where animals were concerned." Van Daalen could get angry when he saw Acehnese going to the market carrying chickens by the legs, with the head down. The detail — Van Daalen's mercy for chickens — is the book's small humanising of the Van Daalen Column commander: the man for whom a human life had no value had mercy for chickens.

The book's argument — voiced through Datoek — is that the Van Daalen Column reputation ("a man whose life has no price") was the colonial army's — and the Acehnese memory's — judgement on Van Daalen, but that the man himself was not without tenderness where animals were concerned. The detail of the chickens is the book's — and Datoek's — small marker of the humanising of the Van Daalen Column commander.

## Significance

Van Daalen is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the Marechaussee commander whose reputation — "a man whose life has no price" — is the book's — and the Acehnese memory's — judgement on the Van Daalen Column. The book's argument — that the Van Daalen Column reputation was the colonial army's judgement on Van Daalen, but that the man himself was not without tenderness where animals were concerned — is the book's small humanising of the Van Daalen Column commander. Van Daalen is therefore the book's marker of the Van Daalen Column reputation: the very great lord for whom a human life had no value, but who had mercy for chickens.

## Account from *Dispereert Niet Vol. XI* (1946) — Van Daalen

**Van Daalen** was a Dutch military officer who commanded the **[[Gajo Expedition]]** of [[1904]], which brought the highland regions of **[[Aceh]]**—the Gajo and Alas territories—under Dutch control. The expedition was one of the most remarkable military campaigns of the **[[Aceh War]]**, involving a small force crossing extreme terrain and fighting a series of battles against determined defenders.

### The Expedition

Van Daalen departed in **[[February]] [[1904]]** with a force of approximately 200 *marechaussees*. His mission was to traverse the Gajo highlands, a territory larger than the Netherlands that had never been explored by Europeans. The region was protected by high mountains and nearly impenetrable forests, with only a few passes guarded by fortified *bentengs*.

### Achievements

Van Daalen's force:

- Crossed mountain passes comparable to the Alps
- Defeated a series of fortified positions, each guarded by thousands of defenders
- Survived near starvation, at one point subsisting on a captured python
- Persevered despite heavy losses

The expedition lasted **163 days**, ending when Van Daalen reached **[[Tarotoeng]]** in the **[[Tapanoeli]]** residency on **[[18 June]] [[1904]]**.

### Legacy

Van Daalen's expedition completed the pacification of Aceh, eliminating the last significant area of resistance. His leadership and the endurance of his men were celebrated in Dutch military history.

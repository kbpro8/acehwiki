**Alexander Willem Frederik Idenburg** was a Dutch colonial administrator who served as [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië|Governor-General of the Netherlands East Indies]] during the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade covered by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914*. His portrait, alongside those of [[J. B. van Heutsz|his predecessor Van Heutsz]] and [[H. Colijn|H. Colijn]], appears as one of the three frontispiece figures of the volume — the three men "whose names are so closely bound up with the preparation and execution of the measures that have led to the enormous progress of the Buitenbezittingen in the said period."

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Idenburg

In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's framing, [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] — together with [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[H. Colijn|Colijn]] — is one of the three principal architects of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "enormous progress" of the Buitenbezittingen, including the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] and the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch effective authority across the outer possessions. The Bureau notes that all three "granted permission for the inclusion of their likenesses" in the volume — a courtesy that frames the volume itself as, in part, a tribute to their work.

The portrait caption identifies [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] as "Gouverneur-Generaal van Ned.-Indië; Oud-Minister van Koloniën; Oud-Gouverneur van Suriname" — that is, as a colonial-administrative figure whose career spanned the principal Dutch colonial postings of his generation. His tenure as Governor-General coincided with the consolidation phase of Dutch rule in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — the phase that saw the [[1912]] creation of fifteen new *onderafdeelingskassen* in [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]], the [[1913]] approval of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] extensions toward [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]], and the [[1914]] promulgation of the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]].

## Significance

[[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] occupies a symbolic position in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account as the presiding Governor-General of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress." His appearance alongside [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] (the military conqueror of Atjeh) and [[H. Colijn|Colijn]] (the principal administrative architect of the post-pacification order) frames the volume as a tribute to the three men who, in the Bureau's view, between them brought the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] to its completion and translated it into a colonial-wide policy of effective authority.

**Habib Teupin Wan** — addressed in the submission-letter of the three great Atjehsche chiefs as *Penghoeloe (Heer) Habib Abdoerrachman Teupin Wan*, and called in the language of the Dutch soldiers *"The Holy One"* — was an Acehnese *sayyid* (descendant of the Prophet Muhammad), *de afstammeling van de profeet*, venerated as *kramat* (a living saint), who remained one of the principal religious figures of the Acehnese resistance to the end. He belonged properly to the VII Moekims Ateuë in [[Groot-Atjeh]], but when the Kompeuni had done its great work there he withdrew to [[Keumala]] and later lived in the mountains near [[Tangsé]]. He was killed on [[29 September]] [[1911]] by [[Schmidt]]'s brigade at the Aloeë Blang Djeuraloh, and his death, together with those of [[Tengkoe di Boekit]] and [[Tengkoe Majet di Tiro|Tgk. Majet]], closed the *epos der Tiro-oelama's*.

## The sayyid as saint

*De afstammeling van de profeet, Habib Teupin Wan, die als een heilige werd vereerd.* The phrase marks him as a *sayyid* — a descendant of the Prophet Muhammad through his daughter Fatimah. In Acehnese society such descent carried automatic religious prestige; and Teupin Wan's prestige had ripened into active veneration. He was *kramat* — a living saint — from whom, by respectable report, there went out such great powers that the hand raised against him immediately stiffened. Prominent families sent him their sons to learn the recitation of the Koran. After the disappearance of great men like [[Habib Abdoerrachman el Zahir]] and [[Habib Samalanga|Habib Samalanga]], the title of paramount religious authority in the resistance had passed to him. He refused all contact with the Dutch *bestuur* and military, and never ceased to admonish the holding-fast of the [[prang sabil|holy war]].

His son — *Habib Tjoet*, the little or younger Habib — was killed in the overval of [[21 May]] [[1910]] by which the brigades of Schmidt fell upon the Tiro-oelama's bende. After this the old Habib went his own way; earthly things had become small and insignificant in the light of the eternal.

## The killing, 29 September 1911

In [[September]] [[1911]] the Hari Raja fell on the 25th, and some of the Habib's men had gone into a kampong in Tangsé for the celebration. This leaked out; on [[27 September]] a detachment of 30 maréchaussée's under Schmidt marched out on the spurs of the returned followers of the Habib. On [[29 September]] the colonne moved along a little river, the Aloeë Blang Djeuraloh, cutting a path along a densely grown slope, and came upon the Habib's schuilplaats.

Beside the huts stood a group of fifteen Atjehers; at their head a greybeard, in one hand a *kris* and in the other a revolver. At his side stood men with a gun and a blunderbuss; the others were armed with klewangs. The bende had been alarmed by the flute-signal but had not fled. The old commander had weighed the matter for a brief moment, and in those seconds had measured out the term of his life. His choice was: to become *[[sjahid]]*. So stood the grey old man at the head of his men, head upright, eyes on the soldiers stepping out of the bushes — without a moment of hesitation; it was *his men* who began the fight, having devoted themselves with him to death. So fell Habib Teupin Wan, *"The Holy One"*, and with him eight others.

The *oeléëbalang*'s wife of Tangsé asked Schmidt, when he came back, how many dead and wounded the Kompeuni had received: *"Zelfs geen enkele gewonde"* — not even one wounded. The astonished woman answered: *"Dan moet uw eleumeë wel heel sterk zijn, want de Habib was zoo machtig dat, als iemand maar met den vinger naar hem wees, diens hand verstijfde."* — "Then your *eleumeë* must be very strong, for the Habib was so powerful that, if anyone but pointed a finger at him, his hand stiffened."

## Significance

Teupin Wan's significance is as the religious authority of the Tiriō bende — the saintly figure whose presence gave the band its charisma, and whose death stripped that charisma away. With the sayyid's death in [[September]] [[1911]], only the young *[[Tengkoe Tjhi Maät di Tiro|Tgk. Tjhi Maät]]* remained of the whole Tiri bende. The doctrinal structure of the resistance — the patriarch [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]], the sons, the saintly sayyid — had been destroyed.

**Stoop** — published in the volume as *gouvernements mijnningenieur Stoop* (governments' mining engineer Stoop) — was the Dutch colonial mining engineer who, after resigning from the colonial government's mining service, carried out the borings on Java that founded the [[Dordtsche Petroleum Maatschappij]] — the first indigenous Indonesian petroleum company. He is named in Ir. [[F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]]'s chapter *Mijnbouw* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]).

## Van der Ploeg's account

Van der Ploeg's wording: "Meanwhile the governments' mining engineer Stoop had, after leaving the service, carried out borings on Java which were crowned with success. Stoop immediately began a small factory to distil and refine the crude oil. He sold in 1889 the first 8000 cases of lamp-oil locally in this densely populated region. As packaging served old cases and tins of American oil, which had been bought up second-hand by Chinese. With this the first Indies oil business, the later Dordtsche Petroleum Maatschappij, had come into being."

## Distinction from the Koninklijke

Stoop's Dordtsche was, in Van der Ploeg's account, the Javanese counterpart to the Sumatra East Coast–based [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] of [[A. J. B. Kessler|Kessler]] and [[H. W. A. Deterding|Deterding]], whose [[1884]] concession in [[Langkat]] (immediately adjacent to [[Acheh|Atjeh]]) had been the foundation of the modern Indonesian petroleum industry. The two companies — the Javanese Dordtsche and the Sumatran Koninklijke — were to remain in parallel existence until their eventual merger into the [[Royal Dutch Shell]] concern, with the Dordtsche being absorbed in [[1911]] as the last of the roughly fifty Indies petroleum companies to be brought into the concern.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Stoop's significance is comparative: he was the founder of the Javanese branch of the late-colonial Indonesian petroleum industry, alongside [[A. J. B. Kessler|Kessler]] (the founder of the Sumatra East Coast branch) and [[J. H. Menten|Menten]] (the founder of the Borneo branch). The three founders together — Stoop, Kessler and Menten — bracket the late-colonial petroleum industry of the Indies at its three principal points of origin, of which the Sumatra East Coast point (in Langkat, adjacent to Atjeh) was the most consequential for the strategic significance of the Atjeh region.

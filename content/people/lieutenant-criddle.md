**Lieutenant Criddle** was a British naval officer in the service of the East India Company who commanded the Honourable Company's ship *Minto*. He carried out the secret mission dispatched by [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] from [[Penang]] harbour in January [[1819]] to warn [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] at [[Pedir]] of the impending Acheh commission, a mission that constitutes one of the more controversial episodes in the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission]] and is examined at length by P. H. [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] (1900).

## The Dispatch of the *Minto* to Pedir

When Raffles, on the point of departing for the [[Karimon Islands]] and ultimately for the founding of [[Singapore]], feared that the Dutch might strike their bargain with Sultan Djohar during his absence, he ordered the *Minto* to put immediately to sea for [[Pedir]] without informing either [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Bannerman]] or [[Captain Coombs|Coombs]]. The commandant, Lieutenant Criddle, "appears to have had no reason not to follow the order". He proceeded to Pedir, where he communicated to Sultan Djohar, in accordance with his instructions, that the commission would arrive within three or four weeks and that the king might rely upon the justice and good intentions of the British government. Sultan Djohar replied with characteristic restraint that the Dutch had made him no overtures, but promised to enter into negotiations with them if they did.

Criddle's mission was therefore the means by which Raffles sought to keep the [[Acheh]] business in his own hands during the period of his Karimon detour. The Calcutta government, in its report to London of 7 October [[1820]], afterwards pronounced the proceeding "contrary to the spirit of our instructions; and independently of that consideration was obviously improper without previous communication with his colleague". Raffles, in his defence addressed to [[O. F. Metcalfe|O. F. Metcalfe]] as Secretary to Government at Fort William on 30 September [[1819]], acknowledged the dispatch of the *Minto* and explained the considerations of time and Dutch interference that had prompted it.

## Raffles's Written Instructions to Criddle

Criddle's orders were committed to writing, and the letter from Raffles dated "At sea, 20th January 1819" to "Lieutenant Criddle commanding the Honorable Company's ship *Minto*" is printed by Van der Kemp in his third appendix. By its terms Criddle was directed to proceed to Pedir and there personally to inform "the Chief who is usually termed Rajah Lama" that "the Honorable Sir Stamford Raffles and Captain Coombs have been appointed to this mission with full power to make all arrangements of a political and commercial nature between the countries of Acheen and Great Britain". Criddle was further directed to enquire what communications had been received from the Dutch and whether any further attempt at negotiation on their part was expected, and a memorandum was enclosed defining the precise language he was authorised to use. Raffles's defence of 30 September [[1819]] stresses that "the very words, in which captain Criddle was alone authorized to express himself, were laid down for his guidance".

## The Plundered Brig

Criddle's mission also brought back to Penang a piece of intelligence which afterwards excited comment: a report that a vessel under British colours had recently been plundered on the Achehnese coast. Captain Coombs, on learning of this through a private note, considered the matter important enough to bring formally to the attention of the commission. Raffles, however, declined to enter into the subject, on the ground that Criddle's communication had been general; the lieutenant had stated merely that the King had informed him of a native vessel being recently plundered, and as this did not fall within the limits of his duty, "which has been successively and carefully defined, he merely reported the circumstance personally". The affair is one of the earliest recorded references to the [[Capture of British brig by Tunku Pakih|plunder of the British brig]] by [[Tunku Pakih]] of Pedir, an episode which subsequently became entangled with the fall of Tunku Pakih himself.

## Assessment

Van der Kemp treats Criddle as a faithful executor of orders rather than as an independent actor. His historical significance is that of the channel through which Raffles, in defiance of his colleague and of the Penang government, sought to maintain a separate line of communication with [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]]. The *Minto* mission is one of the chief grounds on which the Calcutta government afterwards found that Raffles had shown "an undue bias towards the interests of Jowhar ul Aulum, inconsistent with the impartiality requisite towards obtaining the best security for our interests".

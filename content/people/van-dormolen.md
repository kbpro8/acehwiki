**Van Dormolen** was a captain of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]] who, on the orders of major Van Aarsen, had executed a *tegenstoot* from the [[Koeta Boeloebenteng-stelling|Koeta Boeloebenteng-stelling]] (see Hoofdstuk IV of the staff history) and was still in the *terrein* in late March [[1942]] when he was joined by Lt [[M. C. Thomson|M. C. Thomson]] — fresh from the [[Lae Boetar|Lae Boetar]] capitulation — and Thomson's three Maréchaussee brigades. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the combined Van Dormolen–Thomson column's six-week evasion march from the Medan area to [[Pematangsiantar|Pematangsiantar]] is the longest individual escape-and-evasion episode of the Atjeh campaign after the [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|Van Zanten resistance]].

## The tegenstoot from Koeta Boeloebenteng

The Koeta Boeloebenteng-stelling was the Dutch defensive position north of [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]] (see Hoofdstuk IV), held in March [[1942]] by troops under Van Aarsen. When the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Overakker capitulation]] was imminent, major Van Aarsen ordered Van Dormolen to execute a *tegenstoot* — a counter-stroke — from the stelling. Van Dormolen did so, and remained in the *terrein* with his detachment after the stelling was abandoned.

Around [[30 March]] [[30 March 1942|30 March 1942]] Van Dormolen was joined by Lt [[M. C. Thomson|Thomson]], who had broken out of the [[Lae Boetar|Lae Boetar]] capitulation with three Maréchaussee brigades. Thomson briefed Van Dormolen on the Westkust situation; the two detachments were combined.

## The march to the Medan area

The combined column — initially 18 men (three officers and fifteen *onderofficieren en soldaten*) — moved northward into the [[Medan|Medanse]]. On [[8 April]] [[8 April 1942|8 April 1942]] the column reached a kampong on the *cultuuronderneming „Merijke"* in the Medanse. Van Dormolen contacted the still-serving Dutch *administrateur* of the onderneming, who agreed to supply food and shoes for the next day. On the morning of [[9 April]] [[9 April 1942|9 April 1942]] a patrol reported Japanese in a *ladang* opposite the kampong. Van Dormolen ordered the kampong evacuated; it was already surrounded, but the small group succeeded in slipping away.

Van Dormolen then noticed that he had left his *actetas* — containing *f 2000,—* — in the house. One soldier returned for it but was captured by the Japanese. After some minutes the soldier was heard calling to the group that it was surrounded and should return. From the tone of his voice Van Dormolen suspected coercion and refused to return; Thomson, later in captivity, confirmed that the soldier had been coerced by a Japanese with a pistol.

## The march to Pematangsiantar and surrender

The group — reduced to six men — moved on, "waarbij last werd ondervonden van verklikkers onder de bevolking." Whenever the group foraged in a kampong, a message was immediately sent to the Japanese. Because the Medanse was saturated with Japanese troops, the group resolved to make for Zuid-Sumatra, navigating mainly by compass for want of a good map. Near [[Pematangsiantar|Pematangsiantar]] the group met a *bevolkingspatrouille* with a Dutch-speaking Indonesian, who told them that the *zelfbestuurder* of the streek had an urgent message and begged Van Dormolen to come with him. The *zelfbestuurder*, "zeer hulpvaardig," explained that the Japanese had ordered every kampong to report the presence of Europeans on pain of severe punishment. He had not yet reported the group's arrival but was forced to do so because of the *verklikkers* in the bevolking. The *gezondheidstoestand* of several members was poor; Van Dormolen decided to report to the Japanese at Pematangsiantar.

On [[24 April]] [[24 April 1942|24 April]] the group — now six men — was taken by an auto supplied by the *zelfbestuurder* to Pematangsiantar. On [[26 April]] [[26 April 1942|26 April]] the group was moved to a prisoner-of-war camp at [[Medan|Medan]].

## Significance

Van Dormolen's *tegenstoot* from Koeta Boeloebenteng is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the only Dutch counter-attack of the central-Atjeh theatre, and the combined Van Dormolen–Thomson march is the staff history's principal case study of the impossibility of sustained Dutch evasion in the face of a population that — under Japanese threat of punishment — systematically reported Dutch movements. The capitulation at Pematangsiantar on [[24 April]] [[24 April 1942|24 April]] is the staff history's effective close of the Dutch evasion phase in northern Sumatra.

**Jacob Pieter van Braam** was a Dutch naval officer of the [[Oost-Indische Compagnie|Dutch East India Company]] who, as *commandeur* (commander) of a squadron sent from the Dutch Republic in [[1783]], led the [[1784]] expedition against the Bugis-Malay forces of the [[Riouw]] regent [[Radya Hadgè]] (Raja Haji) on the western coast of Sumatra and at [[Salangore]] and [[Riouw]] — the campaign that broke Bugis sea-power in the [[Straits of Malacca|Straits of Malaka]] and reimposed Dutch authority over the Riouw archipelago. His subsequent career culminated in his appointment as *schout-bij-nacht* (rear-admiral) in [[1788]].

## Early Career and the 1783 Voyage

In [[March 1783]] Van Braam received orders to lead a squadron of four line ships and two frigates from the Dutch Republic to the [[East Indies]]. The fleet sailed from the [[Texel]] on [[14 March]] [[1783]], stopped at the [[Cape of Good Hope]] for provisions, and arrived at [[Batavia]] on [[4 March]] [[1784]], where it awaited further instructions from Governor-General [[Alting]]. Van Braam had already distinguished himself at the [[Battle of Doggersbank]] of [[5 August]] [[1781]] — the engagement that, in the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] history's retrospective, formed the basis of his eventual promotion to *kapitein* (captain) — before he was given the East Indies command.

## The 1784 Expedition: Salangore, Pulau Mars, and Riouw

In [[April 1784]] the fleet left Batavia for the western coast of Sumatra. Operating from [[Malakka]] — where the Dutch had mustered their forces against the Bugis-Malay alliance under [[Radya Hadgè]] — Van Braam's squadron joined the campaign whose initial assault, on [[18 June]] [[1784]], saw the death of Radya Hadgè in the fighting around the Riouw approaches. The fleet then proceeded against [[Salangore]], arriving on [[20 July]] [[1784]] and capturing the fort on the east point of the island of [[Mars|Pulau Mars]]. After a series of engagements, Van Braam declared the local ruler deposed and installed [[Mohamed Ali]] of the Malay princely line as a client ruler.

The two extracts of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] history preserve slightly different emphases of the same campaign. The earlier extract (pp. 78–79) records the failed initial landing on Riouw — defended by Radya Hadgè and his allies — and the imposition of a naval blockade that was eventually lifted to allow Dutch merchant vessels to resume trade. The later extract (pp. 80–82) records the Salangore and Pulau Mars operations and the death of Radya Hadgè in the course of those engagements. The two extracts together describe a single coordinated campaign: the repulse of the initial landing on Riouw, the subsequent descent on Salangore, the death of the Bugis leader, and the reimposition of Dutch control.

## The Riouw Settlement and Return to the Netherlands

Following the Salangore campaign, Van Braam turned his attention to the pacification of [[Riouw]] itself. He issued an ultimatum to [[Radya Aly]] on [[28 October]] [[1784]] and concluded a peace treaty with the young prince [[Mahmoed]] on [[31 October]] [[1784]]. He returned to the Netherlands with his squadron on [[28 June]] [[1786]], where he received a monetary reward for the campaign.

## Later Service

Van Braam continued to serve in the Dutch navy after his return from the East Indies. He had distinguished himself at the [[Battle of Doggersbank]] ([[5 August]] [[1781]]) — the engagement that, in the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] history's account, earned him the rank of *kapitein* (captain) — and was subsequently appointed *schout-bij-nacht* (rear-admiral) in [[1788]]. The Doggersbank engagement had in fact preceded the East Indies command: the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] history's account of Van Braam's career folds the earlier battle into its retrospective of his later distinction.

## Significance

Jacob Pieter van Braam's [[1784]] expedition was, in the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] history's account, the decisive Dutch stroke against Bugis sea-power in the western archipelago — the campaign that avenged the Bugis attacks on [[Malakka]] and reimposed Dutch authority over [[Riouw]] and [[Salangore]] for the remainder of the VOC era. The expedition is one of the last major VOC naval operations of the eighteenth century, and the career that culminated in Van Braam's [[1788]] *schout-bij-nacht* appointment spans the final phase of the Company's maritime power. The *Journaal van Van Braam* — the commander's own journal of the expedition — is, in the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] history's framing, the principal documentary record of the Dutch effort to impose control over the western coast of Sumatra and the surrounding archipelago.

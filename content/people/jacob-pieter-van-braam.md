**Jacob Pieter van Braam** was a Dutch naval officer who served as a *commandeur* (commander) of a VOC expedition in the early 1780s. He commanded an escort fleet that departed from the Texel on [[14 March]] [[1783]] and arrived in [[Batavia]] on [[4 March]] [[1784]], later taking part in operations against the Acehnese‑aligned forces of the [[Riouw]] regent *Radya Hadgè*.

## Early Career and 1783 Expedition
Van Braam received orders in [[March 1783]] to lead a squadron of four line ships and two frigates from the Dutch Republic to the East Indies. The fleet sailed from the Texel, stopped at the [[Cape of Good Hope]] for provisions, and proceeded to Batavia, where it awaited further instructions from Governor‑General [[Alting]].

## Operations against Riouw and Aceh (1784)
In [[April 1784]] the fleet left Batavia for the western coast of Sumatra. Van Braam’s force attempted a landing on the island of Riouw, which was defended by the regent *Radya Hadgè* and his allies. After the initial landing was repulsed with heavy casualties, Van Braam’s squadron imposed a naval blockade that was later lifted, allowing Dutch merchant vessels to resume trade.

## Later Service
Following the 1784 campaign, Van Braam continued to serve in the Dutch navy, later attaining the rank of *kapitein* (captain) after distinguished conduct during the engagement at [[Doggersbank]] (see [[Battle of Doggersbank]]).

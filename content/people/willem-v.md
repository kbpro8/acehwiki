**Willem V, Stadholder of the Dutch Republic** was the last hereditary stadholder of the Dutch Republic, reigning from [[1751]] until his deposition in [[1795]]. He played a decisive role in the Dutch naval and colonial policies of the late eighteenth century, including the financing of a marine corps of 6 000 men (proposal of [[April 1781]]) and the awarding of honours after the [[Battle of Doggersbank]].

## Naval Policy and Marine Corps Expansion
In response to the increasing pressure from Britain during the Fourth Anglo‑Dutch War, the *Raad van State* (Council of State) submitted a proposal in [[April 1781]] to raise a marine corps of 6 000 men. Although the plan was never implemented, it reflects Willem V’s concern for strengthening Dutch naval power to protect overseas possessions such as the Sultanate of [[Aceh]].

## Honours after Doggersbank
Following the Dutch victory at the [[Battle of Doggersbank]] on [[5 August]] [[1781]], Willem V personally presented silver medals to the officers involved, including [[Johan Engelbert Frantzen]] and [[Jan Hendrik van Kinsbergen]]. These awards were intended to boost morale and underscore the importance of defending Dutch maritime interests, which extended to the East Indies.

## Political Turmoil
Willem V’s reign was marked by internal provincial disputes and the growing influence of the Patriot movement, which limited his ability to execute large‑scale naval reforms. Nevertheless, his patronage of the navy remained a key factor in the Dutch Republic’s capacity to project power toward Aceh and other Asian territories.

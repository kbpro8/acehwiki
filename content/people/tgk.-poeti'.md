**Tgk. Poeti'** was the notorious Acehnese religious resistance leader of the [[Meulaboh]] country who submitted in December [[1910]] with all his *teungkoes*, *panglimas* and sixty followers, but who in December [[1917]] organised the [[Attack on Gosenson at Djeuram (29 December 1917)|attack on lieutenant Gosenson]] at [[Djeuram]] with the intention of making resistance in [[Seunagan]] break out again, and was arrested and banished.

## Submission, [[1910]]

In March [[1910]] more than a hundred *uitgewekenen* reported themselves in the Meulaboh country, "among them were many adherents of Tgk. Poeti'." December of that year "brought the closing piece: at last Tgk. Poeti' submitted with all his religious teachers, war-leaders and sixty followers." Thanks to that strong and general improvement, various *landschappen* of the [[Westkust van Atjeh|west coast]] could be opened for industrial and agricultural undertakings in January [[1911]].

## The attack of [[1917]]

On [[29 December]] [[1917]] the bivouac commander of Djeuram, *1ste luitenant* [[G. F. P. Gosenson]], returning with a few *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* from an elephant hunt undertaken at the request of the inhabitants of [[Goenòng Balè]], was attacked by some young Acehnese of Seunagan. He was severely wounded by a *[[klewang]]* blow across the face, disfiguring him for life, and by a cut across the thigh; he and his men, all wounded, laid the three attackers low.

"The investigation conducted by Schmidt brought to light that the notorious Tgk. Poeti', through the agency of a certain clerk Hamit, had set the raid on foot with the intention of causing the resistance in Seunagan to break out again. Tgk. Poeti' was arrested and banished; order was not disturbed."

## Significance

[[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] singled out the Gosenson attack as "a serious attack, because it had a political background" — the one incident of the later pacification years genuinely political in origin, and therefore the exception that proved his general rule that such outbreaks arose from personal grievance and *[[maloe]]*.

**Teungku Hadji Hasan of Krueng Kalé** was a venerable Ulama, nearly seventy years old at the time of the events described by Henri J. H. Alers, resident at [[Krueng Kalé|Krueng Kalé]] in [[XIII Mukims Tungkob|Tungkob]] and possessed, in Alers's words, of "a great reputation as a religious teacher." He led an internal opposition within the [[Poesa|Poesa]] movement against the way the confiscated properties of the defeated *[[Uleebalang|oelèëbalang]]* class were being distributed after the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|social revolution]] of [[1945]]–[[1946]].

## Opposition to the appropriation of Uleëbalang property

Alers records that not only the remaining *oelèëbalang*s but also many within the Poesa itself objected to the fact that numerous Ulama had simply appropriated the property of murdered or dispossessed *oelèëbalang*s for themselves and were using it to build up their own position in trade and the economy. Such acts, the opposition argued, were unlawful acts of plunder — "*haram*" under Islamic law. This opposition, Alers writes, was led by "the venerable, nearly seventy-year-old Ulama of Krueng Kalé in Tungkob, named Teungku Hadji Hasan," together with [[Teungku Hasbullah Indrapura|Teungku Hasbullah Indrapura]]. On their advice, the central republican government supported the creation of the [[Madjelis Penimbang|Madjelis Penimbang]] ("House of Consideration"), the body to which all irregularly acquired properties were meant to be surrendered for redistribution.

**Geij van Pittius** — *majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius*, *den pas opgetreden chef van den staf* (the newly entered Chief of Staff) — was the Dutch major who, as Chief of Staff to [[General van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] in [[1878]], was the intellectual author of the June [[1878]] strategic reversal that abandoned the coastal expeditions of the *Onderhoorigheden* (Dependencies) and attacked the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi of the XXII Moekims]] directly — the reversal that opened the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] of July–October [[1878]], brought the submission of [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoel Rachman]] and [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Tekoe Moeda Baid]] on [[13 October]] [[1878]], and produced, by September [[1879]], the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order]] declaring that "*geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt*" was (the whole of Groot-Atjeh had been subjugated). Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* credits Geij van Pittius by name as the officer whose *vertoogen* (representations) brought the General to the decision; the citation is drawn from [[B. C. P. Kielstra|Kielstra]]'s *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* (III, noot, bladz. 249), Van Heutsz's principal secondary source throughout the tract.

## The 1877 advice: the Zuidoosterlinie into the XXII Moekims

Geij van Pittius had first proposed the *inrukken* — the moving-in on — the [[XXII Moekims]] as early as [[1877]], during the Atjeh visit of [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge]]. Van Heutsz's account, citing Kielstra, fixes the date and the advice:

> "Deze had reeds in 1877, tijdens de aanwezigheid te Atjeh van den Souverneur-Generaal van Lansberge, er op aangedrongen, de Zuidoosterlinie langs de Atjeh rivier te verleggen en daartoe de XXII Moekims in te rukken".

The advice was to shift the *Zuidoosterlinie* — the south-eastern line of Dutch posts that ran along the [[Atjeh rivier|Atjeh river]] — into the territory of the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi of the XXII Moekims]] and so to bring military pressure directly on the first of the three *Sagi* (federation) federations of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Van Lansberge had refused to follow the advice — "wellicht dewijl toen, om de vermelde redenen, van een verzoeningspolitiek, die veel goedkooper was, heil voor de toekomst verwacht werd" — probably because, for the reasons mentioned, hope was at that time placed in a *verzoeningspolitiek* (conciliation policy) that was much cheaper, and from which, for the future, salvation was expected. The *verzoeningspolitiek* of March [[1877]] — conciliation in Groot-Atjeh combined with armed coercion in the still-hostile *Onderhoorigheden* — had been the policy on which Van Lansberge had staked his March [[1877]] visit to Atjeh; Geij van Pittius's advice to abandon the conciliation of the Groot-Atjeh chiefs and attack the *Sagi*'s directly was, in March [[1877]], still unwelcome at the *Gouverneur-Generaal*'s (Governor-General's) level.

## The June 1878 persuasion of Van der Heijden

The advice was renewed in June [[1878]] — after the *Inval van Habib Abdoer Rachman (June 1878)* (the incursion of Habib Abdoer Rachman of June [[1878]]) had exposed the failure of the *verzoeningspolitiek* and had triggered, by telegram of [[13 June]] [[1878]], the *Gouverneur-Generaal*'s order to Van der Heijden to make an end of the resistance in Groot-Atjeh. Geij van Pittius, by then *pas opgetreden* — newly entered into office — as Chief of Staff, brought the General to the decision. Van Heutsz's account names Geij van Pittius by name:

> "Het waren vooral de vertoogen van den pas opgetreden chef van den staf, den majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius, die den bevelhebber tot dit besluit brachten".

The *besluit* — the decision — was the abandonment of the planned [[Pasangan]] expedition and the concentration of the full strength of the Atjeh garrison against the *Sagi* federations of the interior. Geij van Pittius's argument was, Van Heutsz records, both negative and positive: negative against the coastal expeditions, positive for the direct attack on Groot-Atjeh:

> "Thans overtuigde de chef van den staf hem, dat men door expeditiën naar de kuststaten het doel — het einde van den oorlog — niet kon bereiken, en dat alleen eene beslissing kon worden verkregen door in Groot-Atjeh zelf den vijand aan te tasten en te vervolgen, totdat zijne oorlogsbenden allerwegen verslagen en verdreven waren. Alleen op deze wijze zou afdoende gevolg kunnen worden gegeven aan den last der Regeering om Habib's benden te verdrijven niet alleen, maar ook onschadelijk te maken".

The argument was that the expeditions to the *kuststaten* — the coastal statelets — could not achieve the *doel* (the end of the war), and that a *beslissing* — a decision, a settlement — could only be obtained by attacking the enemy in Groot-Atjeh itself and pursuing him until his war-bands were everywhere defeated and driven out. Only in this way, Geij van Pittius argued, could effectual compliance be given to the *Regeering*'s (Government's) order to drive out and disable Habib's bands. The argument was accepted by Van der Heijden — "toen besloot de Generaal van der Heijden voorloopig van de expeditie naar Pasangan af te zien en met geheel zijne beschikbare macht voor goed te Groot-Atjeh aan het verzet een einde te maken" — and the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] opened in July [[1878]].

## The strategic significance

The reversal was, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the *keerpunt* (turning point) of the Atjeh-oorlog — the moment at which the colony, having spent four years dispersing its forces in coastal expeditions against the *Onderhoorigheden*, turned its full strength on the *Sagi*'s of the interior. The campaign that followed vindicated the reversal within three months: "binnen drie maanden na den aanvang van den eersten veldtocht in de XXII Moekims, de onderwerping van Habib Abdoel Rachman en van Tekoe Moeda Baid op den 13en October 1878 volgde" — within three months of the opening of the first campaign in the XXII Moekims, the submission of Habib Abdoel Rachman and of Tekoe Moeda Baid followed on [[13 October]] [[1878]]. The [[XXVI Moekims campaign (1879)|XXVI Moekims campaign]] of [[1879]] followed; the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|tocht naar Selimoen]] of September [[1879]] closed it; the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] declared the conquest complete.

The strategic principle Geij van Pittius had articulated — that the *doel* could not be reached by coastal expeditions and that only the direct attack on Groot-Atjeh could produce a *beslissing* — was the principle on which Van Heutsz would build his [[1893]] tract's central prescription. The rejection of the *verzoeningspolitiek* and the *cajoleering* (cajoling) decade that followed Van der Heijden's dismissal in early [[1881]] was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the political reversal of the Geij van Pittius strategic reversal: the colony returned to the dispersal of its forces among the *Onderhoorigheden*, and the war that Van der Heijden had on the verge of concluding became, after [[1881]], the chronic colonial ulcer of the next twelve years.

## Significance

Geij van Pittius's significance in the Aceh Wiki History record rests on three things: he was the intellectual author of the June [[1878]] strategic reversal — the abandonment of the coastal expeditions of the *Onderhoorigheden* and the concentration of the full strength of the Atjeh garrison against the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi's]] of the interior — that opened the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] and produced, within three months, the submission of [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoel Rachman]] and [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Tekoe Moeda Baid]] on [[13 October]] [[1878]]; he had first proposed the *inrukken* of the XXII Moekims — the shifting of the *Zuidoosterlinie* along the [[Atjeh rivier|Atjeh river]] into the territory of the Sagi of the XXII Moekims — as early as [[1877]], during the Atjeh visit of [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|G.-G. van Lansberge]], and the advice had been refused on the grounds of the *verzoeningspolitiek* of March [[1877]]; and his *vertoogen* — his representations to Van der Heijden — were the immediate occasion of the *besluit* of June [[1878]] that abandoned the planned [[Pasangan]] expedition and concentrated the full strength of the Atjeh garrison on the *Sagi* federations of the interior. The strategic principle Geij van Pittius articulated — that the *doel* could not be reached by coastal expeditions and that only the direct attack on Groot-Atjeh could produce a *beslissing* — is the principle on which Van Heutsz would build his [[1893]] tract's central prescription, and the rejection of which, after Van der Heijden's dismissal in early [[1881]], was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the political reversal of the Geij van Pittius strategic reversal.

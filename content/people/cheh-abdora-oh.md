**Cheh Abdora'oh**, also known as **Abdura'uf** or **Abdulra'uf** and called by the Achehnese honorific **Teungku di Kuala** ("the Teungku of the river-mouth"), is an Achehnese saint (*ulama*) whose memory is intimately bound up with one of the most persistent popular confusions in Achehnese historical tradition. Many Achehnese, at the time of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s writing, attributed to him the introduction of Islam into [[Acheh]], although it is "clearly established that this man lived no earlier than the 17th century" — many hundreds of years after the actual Islamisation of the country.

## The confusion as to the introduction of Islam

The misattribution is cited by Snouck Hurgronje as a measure of the reliability of native Achehnese historical traditions in general. It comes up in his discussion of the supposed formation of the three *sagis* (confederacies of *uleebalangships*) of Acheh proper — the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]], [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]] and XXV Mukims — during the reign of the sultana Nurul-alam Nakiatodin ([[1675]]–[[1677]]). Such traditions, Snouck Hurgronje warns, "are of very little value"; and as proof of their unreliability he points to the legend of Cheh Abdora'oh. The example is doubly instructive because it shows how the Achehnese tendency to refer all customary institutions to a small set of canonical figures works even in matters of religion.

## Tomb and veneration

Cheh Abdora'oh's tomb was long held in the highest reverence in Acheh. Snouck Hurgronje, in his catalogue of the most venerated saints of the country, identifies him with the **Teungku di Kuala** whose tomb "formerly enjoyed [the] highest reputation in Acheh". His association with the name *Abdurra'uf of Singkel* (i.e. the town of [[Singkel]] on the West Coast) ties him to that learned tradition of West-Coast Achehnese *ulamas* whose influence reached across the whole country. The veneration of his tomb in due course yielded pride of place to that of [[Teungku Anjong|Teungku Anjong (Sayyid Abu Bakar bin Husain Bil-Faqih)]] at [[Gampong Jawa]] — whose own posthumous reputation is sometimes anachronistically projected backwards into the reign of [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Meukuta Alam]] (1607–1636), although Teungku Anjong died in [[1782]] and so could not have been born during Meukuta Alam's lifetime.

## Place in the Achehnese hagiography

Cheh Abdora'oh is one of a class of Achehnese saints whose reputations rest less on documented deeds than on the devotional reflexes of the people. He is mentioned alongside such other holy figures as [[Teungku Anjong]] of [[Gampong Jawa]], [[Teungku di Bitay|the Teungku di Bitay]] at [[Bitay]] (associated with the colony of Syrian artisans), and [[Teungku di Buket]] the pioneer of [[Idi]]. The fact that popular imagination credited him with the very Islamisation of Acheh — a feat impossible on chronological grounds — is itself a mark of the depth of his reputation, and at the same time a warning against the uncritical use of Achehnese hagiological tradition as historical evidence.

**Johan Harmen Rudolf Köhler** was a Major-General in the [[Dutch East Indies Army]] who served as the Supreme Commander of the [[First Aceh Expedition]] in [[1873]]. He was killed in action during the assault on the [[Mesjid Raya Aceh]] in [[Banda Aceh]].

## Military Career and Command

Major-General Köhler was appointed supreme commander of the first expeditionary force assembled at Batavia in [[March]] [[1873]]. The expedition consisted of over 3,300 soldiers, 18 artillery pieces, and naval support aimed at forcing the [[Sultanate of Aceh]] to acknowledge Dutch sovereignty following the breakdown of diplomatic talks between [[Frederik Nieuwenhuijzen]] and [[Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX]].

## The First Aceh Expedition

Köhler led the landing forces at [[Pante Cermin]] on [[8 April]] [[1873]]. After securing [[Kota Mogat]], he directed the advance into the interior toward the capital. On [[10 April]] [[1873]], Köhler commanded the [[First Capture of Mesjid Raya]], but the extreme heat and fierce resistance forced his troops to temporarily withdraw from the burning mosque complex.

On [[14 April]] [[1873]], during the [[Second Capture of Mesjid Raya]], Dutch forces successfully stormed and reoccupied the fortified complex. Shortly after 9:00 AM, while inspecting the eastern face of the captured mosque fortification, Major-General Köhler was struck in the chest by an Acehnese sniper's bullet. He died within moments beneath a large banyan tree in the mosque courtyard.

## Impact of Death

Köhler's death sent shockwaves through the colonial administration and severely disrupted the command structure of the [[First Aceh Expedition]]. His successor, Colonel [[Eduard Cornelis van Daalen]], called a council of war following a failed assault on the [[Kraton of Aceh]] on [[16 April]] [[1873]], ultimately leading to the complete withdrawal of Dutch troops from Aceh later that month.

## Significance

General Köhler was the highest-ranking Dutch military officer killed during the early phase of the Aceh War. His death highlighted the formidable nature of Acehnese military resistance and led directly to the launching of the much larger [[Second Aceh Expedition]].

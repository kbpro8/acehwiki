**Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema** was the Langkat-appointed ruler of a fragment of the [[Bandahara|Bandahara]] lands in [[Tamiang|Tamiang]], whose disputed authority over the southern part of the Bandahara territory became the focal point of a long-running quarrel between the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] and the Radja Bandahara — a quarrel that ultimately drew the Dutch into the politics of Tamiang and led to the formal transfer of Langkat's claims over the Tamiang *landschapjes* to the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Netherlands-Indies Government]] in [[1878]].

## The Tjoet Gát inheritance

The Bandahara territory, on both banks of the Tamiang River, had been ceded by the two *Keudjeuroeën* of Karang and Moeda to the heir of a certain *Tjoet Gát* — a subject of the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] who had led the combined forces of the two Keudjeuroeëns to victory against adventurers from [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]]. Tjoet Gát himself fell in the battle; in gratitude, the two Keudjeuroeëns granted the Bandahara territory to his heir, who became the first *Radja Bandahara*. The [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] — Tjoet Gát having been his subject — used the occasion to proclaim his own suzerainty over the new *landschapje*, and on the death of the first Radja Bandahara, appointed his own nephew, Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema, to rule over part of the Bandahara territory.

## The war with Atjeh and Langsa

The Bandahara heir's response to this Langkat intervention was armed resistance, supported by [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] and [[Langsa|Langsa]]. The war that followed — between Langkat, on one side, and the Radja Bandahara (backed by Atjeh and Langsa) on the other — produced no decisive outcome. Years later, in [[1861]], the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] exploited the presence of Dutch warships off [[Poeloe Kompai|Poeloe Kompai]] to summon the Tamiang princes and induce them to appeal to the Dutch *Gouvernement* and to offer their submission. The Keudjeuroeën Karang and the Keudjeuroeën Moeda answered the summons, and at the same occasion recognised Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema as the legitimate ruler of the Bandahara fragment.

## Dutch confirmation and the 1878 transfer

In [[1865]] the Dutch Government issued formal acts of confirmation to the Keudjeuroeën Karang, to the Keudjeuroeën Moeda, and to Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema. In [[1878]] the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] formally transferred his rights over the Tamiang *landschapjes* to the Dutch *Gouvernement*, which then placed a *bestuursambtenaar* — a civil administrator — in the territory. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that Tamiang was incorporated into the *indirect bestuurd gebied* of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] only in [[1908]], having previously been administered as part of the *Oostkust van Sumatra* on the basis of Langkat's now-extinguished claims.

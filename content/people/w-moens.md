**W. Moens** — *oud-luitenant ter zee* (retired lieutenant of the navy) — was a Dutch naval officer and business traveller who, having taken part about 1893–1894 in the naval blockade of the [[Atjeh]] coast during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], returned to the country for a fourteen-day journey in November [[1913]] at the invitation of luitenant-generaal [[H. N. A. Swart]], Gouverneur van Atjeh, and published his observations as the feuilleton series *[[Reisindrukken van Atjeh (Moens, 1914)|Reisindrukken van Atjeh]]* in the *[[Algemeen Handelsblad]]*, reprinted in booklet form at Amsterdam by [[Jacob van Campen (publisher)|Jacob van Campen]] in [[1914]]. His account of *[[Moens' journey through Atjeh (November 1913)|the journey]]* — from [[Pangkalan Brandan]] over [[Koeala Simpang|Kwalla Simpang]], by the [[Atjeh-tram]] to [[Lho' Seumawe|Loh Semaweu]], [[Bireuën]], [[Sigli]] and [[Koeta-Radja]] — is one of the principal Dutch eyewitness descriptions of [[Atjeh]] in the first years of the definitive peace under the *[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]]*.

## Blockade memories

Moens had known Atjeh some twenty years earlier, when he served as a naval officer (*zeeofficier*) in the blockade of the coast — *veel moeite en weinig succes* (much trouble and little success), as he wrote. What the Dutch then held of Atjeh was very inconsiderable: a small plain around [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]], entirely ringed by a line of forts, and some five minimal strips enclosed by palisades scattered along the whole length of the coast. On the night patrols with the steam sloops he had worn his overcoat against the cool of the nights; the uniforms were then still blue, white uniform cloth not yet existing. The contrast between that war-time Atjeh and the pacified country he found in 1913 determined the whole tone of his *Reisindrukken*.

## The invitation of Gouverneur Swart

Moens left Holland in July [[1913]] for a business journey through other parts of the Indies. On the outward voyage, arriving at [[Sabang]], he met there luitenant-generaal Swart, whom he knew from former times. Swart pressed him to reserve fourteen days at the end of his business trip to take a look at Atjeh and to convince himself with his own eyes of the conditions now prevailing there. Moens first hesitated, but the governor's remark — that it pleased him when Hollanders came to convince themselves what Atjeh now was, so that more confidence in the conditions of that region might arise in the mother country — decided him to accept the proposal in principle.

## The journey of November 1913

In November 1913 Moens made his fourteen-day journey. He landed at [[Pangkalan Brandan]], went by steam launch over the [[Aru Bay|Aroe-baai]] to [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] and by road over [[Salahadji]] to [[Koeala Simpang|Kwalla Simpang]] on the [[Tamiang]] river, where the civiel-gezaghebber [[W. Wessel|de heer Wessels]] received him; he witnessed the placing of the last span of the [[Completion of the Tamiang bridge (November 1913)|Tamiang bridge]], visited the first native school on Acehnese soil and the rubber estate [[Soengei Lipoet]], and travelled by the [[Atjeh-tram]] over [[Langsa|Langsar]], [[Peureula]], [[Idi]] and [[Lho Soekon|Loh Soekoen]] to [[Lho' Seumawe|Loh Semaweu]], where assistent-resident [[M. C. Roos van Raadshoven]] supplied him with information. From Loh Semaweu he visited the maréchaussée-bivak of [[Boeloh Blang Ara]] and, in the company of the archaeologist [[De Vink]], the grave of a queen of [[Pasé]] at [[Koeta Kareuëng]]. From [[Bireuën]] he went over the [[Gajo-weg|Gajoe-weg]] as far as [[Kroeng Seumpoe]]. In the company of Gouverneur Swart and the ex-Minister of War [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]] he travelled on by extra train over [[Samalanga]] to [[Sigli]], where majoor [[Th. J. Veltman|Veldman]] received them and where Moens visited the courtroom in which, some weeks before, overste [[Scheepens]] had been mortally wounded. Over the watershed at [[Selimoen]] he reached [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]], where he stayed at the house of the Governor; after Colijn's departure he left for Europe by way of [[Sabang]].

## Judgement on the state of Atjeh

Moens closed his series of impressions with an extensive argument that the Atjeh war was over and that Dutch authority had been finally established. He enumerated seven proofs of the favourable political situation — the well-developed school attendance, the lively markets (*passars*), the confidence of the population who brought their most intimate affairs to the officials, the safety on the roads, the extension of native agriculture, the quiet departure of all officials for the independence festivities, and the general, subjective impression of calm — and quoted the resigned reasoning of the chiefs: *Het is het gouvernement nu ernst* (the government means it now), since it built railways and roads for millions and educated the children. To the ex-Minister Colijn, who in [[1908]] had been sent to Atjeh by the Governor-General as an expert, he put the question of the general state of the country, and the answer was: *Tot onherkenbaarwordens toe verbeterd!* (Improved to the point of unrecognisability!). His closing word was *een eeresaluut* (a salute of honour) to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]].

## Significance

Moens is significant as the author of the *[[Reisindrukken van Atjeh (Moens, 1914)|Reisindrukken van Atjeh]]*, the booklet in which the publisher — who signed herself *DE UITGEEFSTER* (the publisher, feminine) — sought to spread in wider circles the gladdening truth *Er is vrede op Atjeh, de Atjeh-oorlog is voorbij* (There is peace in Atjeh, the Atjeh war is over). The series had originally appeared as a feuilleton in the *[[Algemeen Handelsblad]]* and was reprinted at the request of several readers of that newspaper. The work is one of the few published Dutch travel accounts of Atjeh of the immediate post-pacification years, written by an eyewitness who could compare the country of [[1913]] with the blockaded coast of about twenty years earlier. The series closes with the signature *W. M.*, dated Januari 1914.

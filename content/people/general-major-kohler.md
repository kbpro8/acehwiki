**General-Major Köhler** was the senior Dutch army commander who led the initial expeditionary force against the [[Aceh Sultanate]] after the [[Declaration of War on Aceh (1873)]]. On [[5 April]] [[1873]] his troops arrived at the Aceh coast with a strength of 170 officers, 3 198 non‑commissioned officers and men, and 1 000 sailors, supported by the warships **Antwerpen**, **Marnix**, **Soerabaja**, and **Sumatra**.

Köhler coordinated the joint operations of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] and the regular infantry, overseeing the first amphibious landing at the Kraton (royal palace) area. He also ordered the evacuation of the **Mesigit** building on [[14 April]] [[1873]] after it had been recaptured by Acehnese forces, during which the commanding officer, [[Captain-terr zee Binkes]], was wounded.

## Leadership style
Contemporary reports praised Köhler for his willingness to integrate marine troops into the land army, despite initial resistance from the infantry who regarded the marines as “surrogate infantry.” His cooperation with marine officers such as [[C. J. Visser]] and [[B. H. Stenfert]] contributed to a relatively harmonious inter‑service relationship.

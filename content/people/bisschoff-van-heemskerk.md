**Bisschoff van Heemskerk** was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] *overste* (colonel) of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] who, in early [[March]] [[1896]], was the newly-installed post-commandant of [[Anagaloeng]] — the temporary post in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] whose siege was one of the principal engagements of the spring [[1896]] crisis. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as the officer who, on his installation as commandant, found it *noodig* (necessary) to put an end to the Atjehnese annoyance from the band that had been shooting at the post for some two weeks — and who accordingly ordered the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] under Captain [[Van Blokland|van Blokland]].

## The decision to send the patrol

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the immediate occasion of the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] was the shooting of Lieutenant [[Franson]] — who had been felling trees in the neighbourhood of the post and had received a bullet in his body — together with the general harassment of the post by a band of Atjehnese who had, *sedert een paar weken* (for some two weeks), been *op hun gemak* (at their ease) shooting at everyone they could hit. The album characterises Bisschoff van Heemskerk's decision as the response of a newly-installed post-commandant who found it necessary to put an end to the annoyance — and the patrol of [[7 March]] [[1896]] was the response.

## The aftermath of the patrol

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] — although it had been intended as a punitive expedition against the Atjehnese band — became itself the occasion of an Atjehnese attack on the relief column, in which Lieutenant [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]] was mortally wounded and Captain [[Van Blokland]] and several other officers were wounded. The engagement was the first major action of the [[1896]] crisis, and it was followed, in the next two months, by the sieges of the temporary posts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] and by the relief expeditions of [[April]] [[1896]].

## Significance

Bisschoff van Heemskerk's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the post-commandant whose decision to send the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] opened the spring [[1896]] crisis. His appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] is brief, but it establishes the chain of command at the post of Anagaloeng in the early days of the [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar defection]] crisis, and it identifies the immediate tactical occasion of the patrol — the shooting of Lieutenant Franson — that would, in turn, produce the first Dutch officer fatality of the spring.

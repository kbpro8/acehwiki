**A. L. J. Sunier** was a Dutch colonial commentator and author who in [[1946]] published the pamphlet *[[En tientallen millioenen|Nederlandsch Indië: Een Woord over den Huidigen Toestand]]* (*Netherlands Indies: A Word on the Present Situation*), a political tract defending the continuation of the Dutch colonial relationship with the Netherlands Indies at the moment when the [[Indonesian National Revolution|Indonesian independence movement]] had proclaimed the [[Republic of Indonesia]] following the [[Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies|Japanese occupation]]. Beyond his authorship of this pamphlet, biographical details of Sunier's career are not recorded in the source.

## *Nederlandsch Indië* (1946)

In his [[1946]] pamphlet Sunier argued that Dutch presence in and administration of the Netherlands Indies had been a net benefit to the archipelago and its people, and that the relationship should be preserved or reformed rather than dissolved. He invoked the example of leaders who combined military effectiveness with political vision, naming *"een Churchill, een Colijn, een Van Heutsz"* as models of what the moment required. His reference to [[Johannes van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] is notable: Sunier characterized Van Heutsz not primarily as the military man of the [[Aceh War]] but as *"de pacificateur van Adjeh"* — the pacifier of Aceh — placing the emphasis on political consolidation rather than armed conquest.

The pamphlet was published at a moment of profound uncertainty for the Dutch colonial project. The Japanese had surrendered in [[August 1945]] and [[Sukarno]] had proclaimed Indonesian independence on [[17 August]] [[1945]]; Sunier's tract thus entered a debate over whether the Netherlands would reassert sovereignty or accept independence.

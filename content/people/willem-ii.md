**Willem II** reigned as **King of the Netherlands** from [[1840]] to [[1849]], a period that saw the escalation of Dutch‑Aceh tensions leading to the first armed confrontations.  The *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pages [[489]]–[[490]]) lists Willem II among the sovereigns under whose authority the **Royal Netherlands Marine Corps** prepared for possible operations against **Aceh** following the 1845 **Treaty of London** (not to be confused with the 1824 treaty) which attempted to delineate spheres of influence in **Sumatra**.

## Diplomatic Strains and the 1845 Treaty

In [[1845]], a treaty negotiated by Dutch envoys attempted to limit Aceh’s control over the **Strait of Malacca**.  Willem II’s government rejected Aceh’s demands for unrestricted trade, prompting the dispatch of a marine squadron to **Banda Aceh** in [[1846]] as a show of force.  The *Mariniers* records on page [[489]] describe the fleet’s arrival and the subsequent negotiations.

## Marine Preparations

Marine units were assembled at **Willemsoord**, where they underwent jungle‑training and amphibious drills tailored for the **Aceh** coastline.  The *Mariniers* archive notes that Willem II personally inspected the troops in [[1847]], underscoring the monarch’s direct involvement in colonial military matters.

## Significance

Willem II’s assertive stance contributed to the breakdown of diplomatic relations with the **Aceh Sultanate**, setting the stage for the full‑scale **Aceh War** that erupted under his successor, **Willem III**.  His reign exemplifies the transition from diplomatic pressure to military intervention in Dutch‑Aceh policy.

**Jacobus Nienhuys** (died [[1927]], aged 91) was the Dutch pioneer who in [[1863]] established the first tobacco plantation at the mouth of the [[Deli River]] on [[Sumatra]]'s East Coast, inaugurating a commercial agricultural transformation that within sixty years would make the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]] the second most economically valuable territory in the Dutch colonial empire after [[Java]]. Described by Dr. T. Volker as the quintessential *voorrekker* (trailblazer), Nienhuys repatriated in [[1871]] but the industry he founded persisted and expanded far beyond anything he could have imagined at the time.

## Arrival and First Plantation

The political context of [[Sumatra]]'s East Coast in [[1863]] was one of general disorder: [[Atjeh]] was fully independent and hostile to Dutch authority, having threatened [[Deli]] with a naval force as recently as [[February 1863]]; the *landschappen* (petty states) of [[Deli]], [[Langkat]], and [[Serdang]] had only just recognized Dutch suzerainty in [[1862]] under the [[Resident van Riouw|Resident Netscher of Riouw]]; and the Batak interior remained *terra incognita* to Europeans. In [[1864]] a punitive expedition had to be mounted against the [[Tamiang|Tamiangsche landschapjes]] to restore order. Against this background, Nienhuys settled at the mouth of the [[Deli River]] and planted tobacco. The first harvest, in [[1864]], yielded 50 bales (*pakken*) with a gross return of ƒ 2,542.08.

In [[1863]] a supplementary agreement with the Sultan of [[Deli]] was concluded, binding the Sultan not to cede land to Europeans or other foreigners without the consent of the Resident. In the same year the Sultan halved the export duty on tobacco from $2.00 to $0.50, greatly assisting the first planters. In [[1864]] the colonial government placed a *Controleur* in [[Deli]] who, for lack of European accommodation, lodged with Nienhuys himself.

## Co-Founding the Deli Maatschappij

In [[1869]] Nienhuys, together with the [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij|Handel Maatschappij]] and the partners Janssen, Clemen, and Nienhuys, established the *[[Deli Maatschappij]]* — the first *naamloze vennootschap* (limited-liability company) in the Dutch East Indies. The Deli Maatschappij would become the anchor institution of the entire East Sumatran plantation economy, with its capital growing from ƒ 300,000 in [[1869]] to ƒ 30,000,000 by [[1927]].

## Legacy and Repatriation

Nienhuys repatriated in [[1871]], handing the practical leadership of the emerging plantation colony to organizers such as J. T. Cremer. He died in [[1927]] at the age of 91. Volker's tribute in *Van Oerbosch tot Cultuurgebied* (published the year of Nienhuys's death) reads: *"In den ouderdom van 91 jaar is deze voortrekker, een voorbeeld van moed en doorzettingsvermogen, in 1927 gestorven. Sumatra's Oostkust heeft reden zijn nagedachtenis hoog te houden!"* ("At the age of 91 this trailblazer, an example of courage and perseverance, died in 1927. Sumatra's East Coast has reason to hold his memory in high esteem!").

## Context: Acehnese Threat in Deli's Early Years

The founding years of Nienhuys's enterprise were shadowed by the threat of [[Acehnese raids on East Sumatra|Acehnese aggression]]. In [[1861]] two ships sailing under the Dutch flag were seized by Acehnese forces; in [[February 1863]], the very year Nienhuys arrived, an Acehnese fleet threatened [[Deli]] itself. The presence of Acehnese raiding parties in the northern reaches of the tobacco belt — attacking plantations such as [[Glen Bervi]], [[Tamaram]], and [[Ludwigsburg]] in [[1876]] — was a persistent menace to the industry Nienhuys had begun, and ultimately triggered the [[Expedition against Tamiang (1893)|Tamiang Expedition of 1893]].

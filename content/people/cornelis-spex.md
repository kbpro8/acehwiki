**Cornelis Spex** (also written **Specx**) was a Dutch navigator in the service of the Zeeland merchant **[[Balthasar de Moucheron|Balthasar de Moucheron]]** who commanded the yacht *'t Lam* in the [[Joris Spilbergh|Spilbergh]] fleet that left [[Middelburg|Middelburg]] on [[5 May]] [[1601]] — a yacht that became separated from the others near the [[Comoro Islands|Comoros]], was so damaged that Spex did not dare to continue the voyage through Indian waters, and put in at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in early September [[1602]] to place itself and its crew under the command of the English admiral [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]] already on the roadstead. Spex's *'t Lam* was the first ship of the Spilbergh fleet to reach Atjeh, and the crew's transfer to Lancaster's service was the proximate cause of the Anglo-Dutch joint operation that culminated in the [[Capture of the Portuguese carrack off Atjeh (October 1602)|capture of the Portuguese carrack of 3 October 1602]].

## The Moucheron fleet and the separation

The [[Balthasar de Moucheron|Moucheron]] fleet of three ships and the yacht *'t Lam* left Middelburg on [[5 May]] [[1601]] under the command of [[Joris Spilbergh|Joris Spilbergh]]. Near the [[Comoro Islands|Comoros]] *'t Lam* became separated from the others and was so badly damaged that Spex "het niet aandurfde de reis door de Indische wateren voort te zetten". He decided to put in at Atjeh, where he arrived in early September [[1602]], a few days before Spilbergh himself with the other two ships. On the roadstead he found [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]]'s four English ships already at anchor — the [[English East India Company|English East India Company]]'s first voyage to the East Indies.

## The transfer to Lancaster

Spex "deed het scheepje aan Lancaster over, die ook de bemanning in zijn dienst nam". The transfer was the proximate cause of the Anglo-Dutch joint operation that followed: when Spilbergh arrived a few days later and heard of Lancaster's plan to ambush the annual Portuguese carrack from the [[Koromandel kust|Koromandel coast]], he offered to join with one of his ships for an eighth of the prize, and the combined Anglo-Dutch squadron sailed from Atjeh on [[21 September]] [[1602]] to take up positions across the Strait of Malaka. Spex's *'t Lam* was thus the first Dutch vessel to join the English fleet at Atjeh and the immediate occasion of the Anglo-Dutch naval partnership of October [[1602]].

## Significance

Cornelis Spex is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch figure of the separation-and-reunion episode of the [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|Spilbergh visit of 1602]] and the proximate cause of the [[Capture of the Portuguese carrack off Atjeh (October 1602)|Anglo-Dutch joint operation of 3 October 1602]]. The transfer of *'t Lam and its crew to Lancaster's command marked the first formal Anglo-Dutch naval co-operation in the East Indies, and the crew's participation in the capture of the Portuguese carrack — under English command — established the precedent that the Spilbergh-Lancaster partnership would consolidate a few days later. Spex's role also marks the early-modern Dutch practice of transferring damaged ships and their crews to friendly commanders on the spot — a practice that the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] would later formalise in its regulations.

**Sjamsoeddin van Pasè** (*Sjamsoeddïn as-Samatranï*) was the leading heterodox mystic who was favoured by Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Iskandar Moeda]] of [[Aceh|Atjèh]] ([[1607]]–[[1636]]). His conflict with the orthodox scholar [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Noer-ad-dïn ar-Ranïrï]] led to the orthodox victory under Sultan [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar Thanï]], who had Sjamsoeddin executed and his works burned.

## The heterodox mysticism

Sjamsoeddin van Pasè was the leading figure of the heterodox mystic tradition — a pantheistic *Wujudiyyah* mysticism — that flourished at the court of Sultan Iskandar Moeda. Alongside the famous poet [[Hamzah Pansuri|Hamzah Pansoerï]] "of Baros," Sjamsoeddin represented the literary and spiritual high point of this tradition. He enjoyed full royal patronage and domicile at the kraton court.

## Account from *Atjèh* by J. Kreemer (Deel II)

[[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] records that Sjamsoeddin was favoured (*begunstigd*) by Iskandar Moeda, as was Hamzah Pansoerï. When [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Noer-ad-dïn ar-Ranïrï]] returned to Atjèh in [[1637]], he found the court under Sultan Iskandar Thanï still influenced by Sjamsoeddin's heterodox teachings.

Ar-Ranïrï's polemic led to the orthodox victory: Sjamsoeddin was executed by Iskandar Thanï (having died around [[1630]], his memory and works were condemned), and the works of the heterodox mystics — including those of Hamzah Pansoerï — were burned before the great mosque **Beit ar-rahman** (the future [[Meusigit Raja|Meusigit Raja]]). This destruction of the heterodox literature in the reign of Iskandar Thanï ([[1636]]–[[1641]]) represented a decisive shift in the religious culture of the Atjèhnese court.

## Significance

The conflict between Sjamsoeddin and ar-Ranïrï represents a pivotal moment in the religious history of Atjèh — the triumph of orthodox Shafi'ite Islam over the heterodox pantheistic mysticism that had flourished under Iskandar Moeda. This orthodox victory shaped the subsequent development of Atjèhnese Islamic institutions, and the memory of the burned books marked the end of Atjèh's golden age of Malay mystical poetry.

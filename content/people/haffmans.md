**Haffmans** was a member of the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]] who spoke during the [[Tweede Kamer debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|debate on Atjeh war origins]] in April [[1874]], arguing that the war was pre-arranged between the Government and the Indian administration and that the question was merely to find an occasion. Haffmans also opened the procedural debate on [[16 April]] by arguing against secret sessions, insisting that the *comité-général* procedure made open debate impossible given the manner in which the Government had submitted the documents.

## The procedural objection

Haffmans opened the debate on [[16 April]] [[1874]] by arguing that he saw no reason for closed-door deliberation, and had compelling reasons for treating the matter publicly. He noted that only a few of the submitted documents were non-secret, while the majority had been submitted under secrecy — a format that made open debate impossible. He asked whether, after the *comité-général* was concluded, the discussion of the interpellation would be continued in public session.

## The pre-arranged war argument

Haffmans argued that between the Government and the Indian administration there was already an *afspraak* (agreement) that a war with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] must come sooner or later, and the question was merely to find an occasion. "That agreement accepting, everything explains itself; without it, the war declaration is incomprehensible." He pointed to the telegram of [[18 February]] [[1873]] as "the spark that hit the powder," and noted that the war was decided in four days — from [[16]] to [[22 February]] — including the time to convene the council and consult the King.

He cited [[Loudon|Loudon]]'s letter of [[25 February]] [[1873]]: "Noodzakelijkheid der expeditie is een uitgemaakte zaak. Zelfs al mogt de verzekering worden ontvangen dat Amerika zich even weinig als Italië zal bemoeijen. [...] Het fait accompli is het eenig redmiddel."

## The manifest critique

Haffmans characterised [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|Nieuwenhuyzen]]'s entire approach as one in which "he fell, as they say, with the door in the house; he came equipped for war and brought the war. The asking of clarification and accountability that preceded was merely for form, an idle display." He argued that the modification of article 6 of the [[Instructie gouvernements-commissaris (1873)|instruction]] went much further than the Minister acknowledged, and that the entire proceeding constituted a war declaration rather than a negotiation.

## The motion to close

A modified motion by Haffmans to close the secret session and continue in open session while respecting the imposed secrecy was rejected 36–30.

**Isah** was one of the three female elephants of the colonial army's [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant transport]] in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — with [[Agam]] the male and her fellow female elephants [[Sarah]] and [[Rimba]]. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Isah is one of the four elephants — "Agam, the male, and Isah, Sarah, and Rimba, the female elephants" — that were disposed of in the time when the narrator was posted there.

## The female elephants and the European zoo

"One of the female elephants was sent to Medan, and it is this sturdy young lady that you see here on this snapshot. I still see the colossus slowly swaying forward along the main road. The future of the two other female elephants was also a European zoo." One of the female elephants was sent to Medan, and it is this sturdy young lady that the colonel's snapshot — the photo from the Rotterdam Diergaarde that opens the chapter — shows. "I still see the colossus slowly swaying forward along the main road." The future of the two other female elephants was also a European zoo — that is, the female elephants were sent to European zoos (including the Rotterdam Diergaarde) when the [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant transport]] was abolished.

## Significance

Isah is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as one of the three female elephants of the [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant transport]]. The book's argument — that the female elephants were sent to European zoos when the elephant transport was abolished — is the book's marker of the end of the former-times elephant transport: the female elephants who had served the colonial army in Aceh ended their days in European zoos, including the Rotterdam Diergaarde.

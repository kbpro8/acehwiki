**Queen Wilhelmina** — Wilhelmina ([[1880]]–[[1962]]), Queen of the Netherlands from [[1890]] to [[1948]] — was the sovereign whose reign spanned the final phases of Dutch colonial rule in [[Aceh]] and the [[Dutch East Indies]], the Japanese occupation of [[1942]]–[[1945]], and the opening of the Indonesian revolution. Her reign is significant for Acehnese history through her [[7 December]] [[1942]] speech in exile in London, the deployment of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]] to restore Dutch authority in Aceh after the liberation, and the commemoration of her silver jubilee in [[1926]] by the *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* and the [[Wilhelminapark]] at [[Weltevreden]] — site of the [[Atjeh Monument]].

## The Audience with Dominee Thenu ([[1934]])

When [[Dominee Thenu]]'s "veertig Atjeh-jaren" were over in [[1934]], he was sent on a *reis naar Nederland* (journey to the Netherlands) and was received by Koningin Wilhelmina. "*Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt*" — for a long time he refused to wash the hand the Queen had shaken. For more than ten days no one else — until [[H. N. A. Swart|General Swart]] — was permitted to shake that hand. The episode places the Queen, with Swart, in the small circle of figures whose touch the *Soldaten-Dominee* counted as sacramental.

## The 7 December 1942 Speech

The [[7 December]] [[1942]] speech of Queen Wilhelmina — delivered in exile in London — promised a post-war round-table conference on Indonesia. The speech gave its name to the [[Zeven December Divisie]] of the Indonesian revolution, raised in [[1946]]–[[1947]] from Dutch volunteers. The naming was therefore both a political reference (to the Queen's promise) and a military designation: the division named for the promise of negotiation was fighting the war that the negotiation would, in [[1949]], finally conclude.

## The Loyalty Triad

The *[[Marechausseelied]]* that Dominee Thenu taught stressed "*trouw aan Koningin, trouw aan het vaandel en trouw aan de vlag*" — loyalty to the Queen, loyalty to the standard, and loyalty to the flag. The triad was the *Soldaten-Dominee*'s framing of the colonial *soldaat*'s loyalty: the Queen, the standard, and the flag — the sovereign, the regiment, and the state.

## World War II and the Japanese Occupation

During the Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies ([[1942]]–[[1945]]), Aceh was incorporated into the Japanese administrative zone. After the liberation in [[1945]], Queen Wilhelmina authorised the deployment of marine units to restore Dutch authority in Aceh, as recorded in the *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pages 489–490).

In [[1946]], Wilhelmina signed a royal decree ordering the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Marine Corps]] to assist in rebuilding infrastructure in Aceh, including the repair of the [[Banda Aceh]] port and the re-establishment of civil administration. The decree emphasised "the duty of the Dutch to bring order and development to the former colonies," reflecting the Queen's commitment to maintaining Dutch influence.

## The Silver Jubilee and the *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* ([[1926]])

The *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* was published in January [[1926]] "*ter gelegenheid van het vijf-en-twintigjarig regeringsjubileum van Hare Majesteit de Koningin*" (on the occasion of the twenty-fifth anniversary of Her Majesty the Queen's reign). The foreword, written by the editor, explains that the work was produced by a special *Commissie van Redactie* (Commission of Editors) in the Indies on the occasion of the Queen's silver jubilee. The [[Volkslectuur]], under the direction of Dr. [[C. P. Gunning|C. P. Gunning]], was responsible for the publication.

## The Wilhelminapark at Weltevreden

The [[Atjeh Monument]] in [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], stood in the Wilhelminapark — a public park named after the Queen. The naming of this prominent public space after the Queen reflected the central place of the monarchy in the official culture of the Dutch East Indies, and the presence of the Atjeh Monument within it symbolically linked the Queen's reign with the [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] — the colonial achievement that the atlas commemorated as the hallmark of the modern Dutch East Indies.

## Significance

Queen Wilhelmina's reign marks the twilight of Dutch colonial presence in Aceh, transitioning from military conquest to post-war reconstruction and eventual decolonisation. Her royal directives illustrate the lingering involvement of the Dutch monarchy in colonial affairs up to the final years of the Dutch East Indies. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* her handshake — the touch that Thenu refused to wash — was the political honour accorded to the *Soldaten-Dominee* at his retirement, and the [[7 December]] [[1942]] speech gave its name to the division that would fight the war the speech had promised to conclude by negotiation. The *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* further underscores her central role in the official culture of the Dutch East Indies: the silver jubilee of her reign was the occasion for the colonial government's most ambitious publishing programme, and the Wilhelminapark at Weltevreden linked her name permanently to the monumental commemoration of the Pacification of Atjeh.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Queen Wilhelmina** was the young sovereign of the Netherlands referred to by [[G. B. Hooijer]] in *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]* as *onze jonge Koningin* — "our young Queen" — in his tribute to the soldiers of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]] serving in [[Atjeh]].

## Hooijer's tribute

Describing the men photographed inside the *benteng* of [[Kroeng Gloempang]] — Javanese in shoes they were not issued but could not do without in the heavily overgrown terrain, a tall young European with a bold face, two non-commissioned officers, a couple of sturdy Dutch boys without their uniform coats for the heat, and a native soldier at ease in a blue check shirt and short working trousers — Hooijer wrote that these were the types of the men who now upheld the Dutch flag in [[Atjeh]] as in all the Indies. *Zij met hun 30 à 40 duizend bewaren de rust onder de millioenen onderdanen, die onze jonge Koningin kan tellen in haar Indisch rijk* — with their thirty to forty thousand they preserved order among the millions of subjects whom the young Queen could count in her Indies empire.

This wonder, he continued, was possible thanks to their exertion, their courage and contempt of death, their indifference to danger and misery of every kind, and their cheerfulness in spite of the mind-killing isolation of the outposts. He closed the passage: *Eere aan hen!* — "Honour to them!"

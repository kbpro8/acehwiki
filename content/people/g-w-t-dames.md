**G. W. T. Dames** was a former Major of Infantry of the [[K.N.I.L.]] (*Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d.* — *buitengewoon dienst*, retired) who, in [[1954]], published *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* in 's-[[Gravenhage]] as a grateful literary memorial to the Ambonese soldiers with whom he had served. The book — part memoir, part regimental history, part ethnographic portrait of "[[Oom Ambon]]" — draws on Dames's own service at posts such as [[Bakongan]] in the dertiger jaren and at [[Brastagi]] in [[1947]], and is the principal printed source on the daily life, patrols, songs and ethos of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Royal Netherlands East Indies Army's Marechaussee corps in Aceh]] in the last decades of colonial rule.

## Career and the voice of the book

Dames writes in the first person throughout *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, identifying himself as a captain in the [[K.N.I.L.]] who served on the [[Westkust (of Atjeh)|Westkust of Atjeh]] in the [[1930s]] and saw action against Indonesian revolutionary forces around [[Brastagi]] in [[1947]]. He had the "Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d." after his name on the title page, indicating that he had retired from the infantry by the time of publication. His narrative is not a chronological autobiography but a series of thematic chapters — *Bij ons…*, *Van recruut tot pensioen*, *Moeilijkheden op den weg*, *Tangsi en Tante*, *De buitenpost*, *Ooms leger*, *Oom en de Bintang*, *Dominee Thenu*, *Thijs*, *De patrouille*, *Jappentijd*, *Onder Brits bewind*, *Onzekerheid*, *Tegen de rood-witten*, *Tamaela* — held together by the figure of "Oom Ambon," the archetypal Ambonese soldier of the colonial army.

The author met [[Dominee Thenu|the Soldaten-Dominee of Aceh]] for the first time at the end of [[1932]] on the beach of [[Bakongan]], when Thenu was already "vierenzestig" years old. He served at [[Bakongan]] in the dertiger jaren alongside [[Thijs de Fretes]] and [[Tanamal]] of the [[6e Divisie Marechaussee]], and was present when the kolonel visited [[Bakongan]] in [[1933]] and the zelfbestuurder of [[Troemon]] raised the matter of a rogue elephant. In [[1947]] he was "in actie" at [[Brastagi]] and omgeving, where the soldier [[Taris]] shot two [[pelapor]] at eighty-five and over one hundred metres.

## Method and purpose

Dames explicitly distances himself from the tall-story tradition of the colonial officer corps. The book's prologue opens with the famous Hotel de Boer anecdote — a captain of the [[1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren]] who, around [[1933]], told the *Soos* in [[Koeta-Radja]] a story (almost certainly embellished) of two Marechaussees with drawn [[klewang]] standing behind his beer glass at [[Hotel de Boer]] in [[Medan]] — and Dames uses the story to mark out the boundary between legend and "waarheid." "Wij willen naar waarheid vertellen van Oom Ambon," he writes; the book will not "de methode van bovengenoemde kapitein volgen" or "trachten te zien hoever men met fantasieën kan gaan."

The author's purpose, stated at the close of the prologue, is that "de lezers Oom Ambon uit deze regels leren kennen en hem de genegenheid toedragen, die schrijver dezes ertoe bewoog, zijn dankbare herinnering aan hem op papier te zetten." The book therefore takes in everything from recruit life at the [[depotbataljons]] on [[Java]] to the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] of [[1873]]-[[1904]], from the [[colonne Schmidt]] campaign against the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō Teungkoes]] in [[1910]]-[[1911]] to the Japanese occupation of [[1942]]-[[1945]] and the Indonesian revolution of [[1945]]-[[1949]].

## Sources and the *Nawoord*

In the *Nawoord* Dames acknowledges the printed sources he drew on for episodes he could not have witnessed himself, especially the chapters on the older [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and the decorations history. He names in particular the works of [[van Kijffler]] and [[Booms]], as well as [[Hotz]] (whose account of the [[colonne Schmidt]] he used for the [[Tiriō Teungkoes|Tiriō]] campaign), [[Schmidt]] himself (whose report on [[1910]]-[[1911]] was published), [[du Croo]], [[Zentgraaf]] and [[Generaal Swart|General Swart]]. The book is therefore not pure memoir but a synthesis of personal recollection and these older printed authorities, with Dames's own commentary threaded throughout.

## Significance

Dames's *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is the single richest Dutch-language portrait of the Ambonese professional soldier of the colonial army. Because it was written in [[1954]] — late enough to look back on the whole arc of the [[K.N.I.L.]] from the [[Aceh War]] to the [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]], early enough to be informed by living memory — it functions both as a primary source on the last generation of *[[Oom Ambon]]* and as a retrospective interpretation of what that figure meant. Its affectionate but clear-eyed portrait of the Ambonese soldier, of the [[tangsi]] culture that shaped him, and of the colonial regime he served, has been drawn on by every later historian of the [[K.N.I.L.]] and of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] in particular.

**J. van Swieten** was a retired Lieutenant-general of the Indies army who was restored to active service in [[1873]] to command the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]], served as civil and military commissioner for Atjeh, and presided over the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] and the [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation of annexation]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] on [[31 January]] [[1874]]. Earlier in his career, as major-general and governor of [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]], he had concluded the [[Treaty of 30 March 1857 with the Sultan of Aceh|tractaat of 30 March 1857]] with the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan of Atjeh]].

## The Tractaat of 1857

In [[1856]] the official [[J. F. Nieuwenhuizen|J. F. Nieuwenhuizen]] had opened negotiations with the Acehnese court; the following year Van Swieten, then major-general and governor of Sumatra's Westkust, brought to conclusion the tractaat of [[30 March]] [[1857]], ratified by the [[Governor-General of the Netherlands India|Governor-General]] on [[9 May]]. The treaty provided for "bestendige vrede, vriendschap en goede verstandhouding" between the two realms, free intercourse for the subjects of either, and the rights of the most-favoured nation. [[Article 4 of the Treaty of 1857 with Aceh|Art. 4]] bound both governments to suppress piracy and slave-raiding in their own or their influenced territories; the close of [[Article 6 of the Treaty of 1857 with Aceh|art. 6]] prescribed severe punishment for those who plundered stranded ships or maltreated castaways; [[Article 7 of the Treaty of 1857 with Aceh|art. 7]] required the sultan to recognise the governor of Sumatra's Westkust as the representative of the Governor-General and to address him in all matters of mutual interest.

The paper, however, "proved to be patient" — the sultan lacked the authority to make his subordinate chiefs observe the stipulations, and new cases of piracy and plundering of stranded ships continued to occur.

## Command of the Second Aceh Expedition

After the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] and the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]] in [[April 1873]], the Indies government set about preparing a second expedition that should leave nothing to chance. Troops were levied in the Netherlands, all were equipped with breech-loading rifles, the artillery was reinforced with newer breech-loading guns from Europe, and supplies of every kind — bridging material, headquarters, landing gear, ambulance, postal service — were assembled. The expeditionary force, 6354 infantry besides officers, 75 cavalry, 656 artillery with 74 guns, and 306 engineers besides 250 free natives, was placed under Van Swieten, "the retired Lieutenant-general J. van Swieten, restored to active service." [[G. M. Verspyck|Major-general G. M. Verspyck]] was appointed second-in-command; Van Swieten was at once civil commissioner and military commander, uniting in himself the political and military authority.

After delays caused by cholera on Java and aboard the transports, the fleet sailed in mid-November and arrived at the Atjeh roadstead between [[20 November|20]] and [[29 November]] [[1873]]. The landing on [[9 December]] took place east of the Atjehrivier rather than west of it as in the first expedition, both to avoid stronger resistance and to spare the kampong [[Marassa]], whose head, [[Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia|Toekoe Nek]], had offered his services. On [[13 December]] [[Kota Moesapi]] was occupied; on [[14 December]] the enemy's first line was carried by the storming of Tiban by Verspyck; on [[15 December]] the mouth of the Atjehrivier was reached.

## Capture of the Kraton and Proclamation

Van Swieten first attempted negotiation, sending two letters to the sultan offering terms similar to the [[Tractaat with Siak of 1858|Siak tractaat]] and proposing to wage war "in the manner of civilised nations." The bearer, the native [[Mas Soemo Widikdjo|Mas Soemo Widikdjo]], departed [[26 December]] and never returned — later it was learned that, after being held prisoner a few days, he was murderously killed at [[Longbatta]]. On [[6 January]] [[1874]] the [[Missigit|missigit]] of the Kraton was taken; on [[12 January]] the [[Taman|Taman]] or Kota Goenoengan, the sultan's fortified pleasure-garden south-west of the Kraton, was stormed and carried on the advice of [[Toekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia|Toekoe Nek]]. On [[24 January]] a reconnaissance discovered that the Kraton had been evacuated; the Dutch flag was hoisted to the strains of the *Wilhelmus*.

Two days later, on [[26 January]] [[1874]], the sultan died of cholera at Pagger Ajer. On [[31 January]] Van Swieten issued his [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation]]: "the people of Great Atjeh, after a brave defence and a stubborn struggle, has had to yield to the Dutch arms; the people conquered, the Kraton taken, the sultan dead, many chiefs fallen in the struggle; the land therefore, by right of conquest, belongs to the Netherlands-Indian government." The chiefs of the three [[Sagi|sagis]] and of the [[Onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]] were invited to make written submission; those who did so would have the blockade raised and trade, except in weapons of war, restored, on condition of flying the Dutch flag and providing their ships with proper passes.

## Van Swieten's Note of 22 April 1874

Before his departure Van Swieten laid down his policy in a note of [[22 April]] [[1874]] to his successor [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Colonel Pel]]. He wished the defence of [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] itself to be made as active as possible by ceaseless patrols, the establishment of [[kozakkenposten|kozakkenposten]], the offering of premiums for the laying down of armed Atjehers, and the levelling of enemy fortifications near Kota Radja. Apart from this the defence was to remain passive: "one must not seek the enemy in his well-built and defended positions, as long as they do not hinder us. His offensive strength is not great and will gradually diminish." He recognised that the enemy's defensive strength was considerable, given the nature of the terrain and the close-packed kampongs, and that if he did not submit, the Dutch should rely on the solidity of their positions to force him to it.

## Departure and Later Years

On [[25 April|25]] and [[26 April]] [[1874]] the troops whose continued presence was no longer needed embarked, and on [[26 April]] the supreme commander, the second-in-command, the general and general staff, with the troops no longer required, left the theatre of war. A force of 128 officers, 1788 Europeans, 176 Africans, 489 Ambonese and 698 natives remained under Pel's command. Van Swieten's later letter of [[19 July]] [[1874]] approved Pel's conduct and recommended the subjection of the IV Moekims, an eastward expansion from Kota Radja, and an attempt to bring the southern parts to submission.

## Account from *Dispereert Niet Vol. XI* (1946) — General Van Swieten

**General Van Swieten** was the commander of the second Dutch expedition to **[[Aceh]]** in [[1873]]–[[1874]], during the early phase of the **[[Aceh War]]**. He succeeded **General Köhler** after the failure of the first expedition and achieved the capture of the Acehnese capital, **[[Kutaradja]]**.

### The Second Expedition

Van Swieten was sent to Aceh in [[November]] [[1873]] with a significantly larger force than Köhler's expedition—approximately 13,000 troops. His mission was to achieve what Köhler had failed to accomplish: the permanent occupation of Aceh's capital and the establishment of Dutch authority.

### The Capture of Kutaradja

Van Swieten's forces landed on Aceh's north coast and advanced on the capital. After a hard fight, the Great Mosque was again captured, and this time the Dutch forces consolidated their position. The *kraton* (royal palace) was evacuated by the sultan and his followers, who fled into the interior. The sultan died soon after of cholera.

### Aftermath

Van Swieten declared Aceh directly governed territory and established Kutaradja as the administrative capital. He then returned to Java, leaving a garrison behind to maintain order. However, the "victory" was illusory: Acehnese resistance continued, and the war would drag on for decades.

### Significance

Van Swieten's expedition marked the first successful Dutch occupation of Aceh's capital, but it failed to end the war. His decision to leave a garrison without adequate pacification measures laid the groundwork for the prolonged guerrilla warfare that followed.

**D. W. N. de Boer** was a Netherlands-Indies colonial official and jurist who served as *Voorzitter* (chairman) of Atjehnese *[[Moesapat|moesapat]]* and *[[Landschapsgerecht|landschapsgerechten]]* — the native and territorial courts of [[Aceh|Atjeh]] — and who, after his return to the Netherlands, published at [[Scheveningen]] in November [[1931]] the monographic study *[[Het recht op den grond in Atjeh (De Boer)|Het recht op den grond in Atjeh]]* ("The right to the soil in Atjeh"), printed in pages 59-80 of a colonial periodical and preserved as offprint DLP-141 in the library of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde]].

## Judicial service in Atjeh

De Boer's knowledge of Atjehnese land law was gathered in the course of judicial service. He described his study as a *resume* of data collected "te hooi en te gras" — haphazardly, as occasion offered — over many years while sitting as chairman of Atjehnese *moesapats* and *landschapsgerechten*. Throughout the study he introduced institutions from the standpoint of the bench: the *[[Atra peunoelang|atra peunoelang]]*, for instance, he presented as "een bezitsvorm, dien de Voorzitter van Moesapat of Landschapsgerecht mede nog al eens ontmoet" — a form of possession that the chairman of a *moesapat* or *landschapsgerecht* not infrequently encounters.

His observations covered the whole of the *[[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|gewest]]*: [[Groot-Atjeh]] as the directly governed territory, the *onderafdeeling* (subdivision) [[Sigli]] in the *afdeeling* (district) [[Pidie|Pidië]], the *landschappen* (territories) [[Aree|Aréë]], [[Ndjong]], [[Keumangan]] and [[Pidie|Pidië-proper]] within it, the *landschap* (territory) [[Sawang]] in the *onderafdeeling* [[Lho' Seumawe|Lho' Seumaweh]] of the *afdeeling* [[Noordkust|Noordkust van Atjeh]], the [[VI Moekims Peukan Bada|VI moekims Peukan Bada]] in Groot-Atjeh, and [[Idi]] on the east coast.

## Scholarly position

De Boer wrote within the framework of Netherlands-Indies *[[Adatrecht (Van Vollenhoven)|adatrecht]]* scholarship, citing [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]* and [[C. van Vollenhoven|Van Vollenhoven]]'s *Adatrecht* repeatedly and by page. He nevertheless departed from Van Vollenhoven on two points. Against Van Vollenhoven's statement (*Adatrecht*, p. 196) that "een verbleekt of slapend beschikkingsrecht bestaat hier niet" — a faded or sleeping right of avail does not exist here — De Boer held that this was "niet juist" for the *[[Zelfbesturende landschappen|zelfbesturende landschappen]]*. And against Van Vollenhoven's treatment of the Atjehnese grazing usage as "het eigenaardige recht," possibly a survival of a village *[[Beschikkingsrecht in Atjeh|beschikkingsrecht]]*, De Boer argued that it was no right at all but a purely economic usage, "'n nuchtere uitwisseling van economische goederen" — a sober exchange of economic goods.

His governing thesis was that the [[Gampong|gampong]] played no role whatever in Atjehnese rights in land, a proposition he stated at the head of the study and reasserted in its central sections.

## Purpose of publication

De Boer explained his motive for publication in the closing paragraph of the study: he had wished to print the material only "in de hoop dat ook deze bescheiden bijdrage voor de studie van het Adatrecht van nut zal kunnen zijn, al zou ze alleen maar aanleiding geven tot critiek, correctie, commentaar" — in the hope that even this modest contribution to the study of *adatrecht* might prove useful, if only by giving occasion for criticism, correction and commentary. He justified his recording of obsolete institutions such as the *[[Haq thilangkob|haq thilangkob]]* on the ground that the noting-down of "adatrechterlijke antiquiteiten" could be of use for comparative or explanatory study of the *adatrecht* of other regions.

## Significance

De Boer's study is one of the principal practitioner accounts of Atjehnese land law, and the source of much of the recorded technical vocabulary of Atjehnese rights in soil, clearance, share-cropping, pledge and mortgage. Because he wrote from the bench, his account preserves the procedural and evidentiary dimension of the law — what a plaintiff had to prove, what remedies a losing party retained — which purely descriptive ethnographies of Atjeh generally omit.

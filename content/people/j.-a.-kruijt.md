**J. A. Kruijt** — full name *Johannes Antonius Kruijt*, signing himself in [[De Atjeh-Oorlog (Kruijt, 1896)|his 1896 brochure]] simply as "De Schrijver" — was a Dutch colonial official who served as *Oud Controleur-Generaal der Nederlanden te Pokkenhage* (Former Controller-General of the Netherlands at Pokkenhage) and, in [[August]] [[1896]], authored the historical brochure *[[De Atjeh-Oorlog (Kruijt, 1896)|De Atjeh-Oorlog: Kort Overzicht van Oorsprong, Verloop en Gevolg]]*, published at 's-Gravenhage by [[Loman & Funke|Loman & Funke]] as a volume of the *[[Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut|Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut]]*. Krruyt's brochure is one of the earliest sustained historical critiques of the Dutch conduct of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] from [[1873]] to [[1896]], written in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] of [[March]] [[1896]].

## Office and Position

Kruijt identifies himself on the title page of his brochure as *Oud Controleur-Generaal der Nederlanden te Pokkenhage* — that is, Former Controller-General of the Netherlands at Pokkenhage. The office of *Controleur-Generaal* was a senior rank in the Dutch colonial civil administration, denoting an official of the highest grade in the *Binnenlands Bestuur* (Interior Administration) of the [[Netherlands Indies|Nederlandsch-Indië]], ordinarily charged with the supervision of a major administrative division. Kruijt's signature of himself as *Oud* (Former) Controleur-Generaal indicates that he had retired from the colonial service by the date of publication. His presence at *Pokkenhage* — almost certainly a misspelling or OCR corruption of *'s-Gravenhage* (The Hague), the seat of the Dutch central government — places him at the metropolitan centre of Dutch colonial policy-making at the time of writing.

## Authorship of *De Atjeh-Oorlog* (1896)

Kruijt is the author of the seventy-nine-page brochure *[[De Atjeh-Oorlog (Kruijt, 1896)|De Atjeh-Oorlog: Kort Overzicht van Oorsprong, Verloop en Gevolg]]*, dated "Den Haag, Augustus 1896" and published at 's-Gravenhage by [[Loman & Funke|Loman & Funke]] as a volume of the *[[Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut|Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut]]*. The brochure is a sustained polemical indictment of the Dutch conduct of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] from [[1873]] to [[1896]], organised as a chronological narrative in thirteen chapters covering each successive Dutch governor and military commander of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

In the *Inleiding* (Introduction) Kruijt explains that, in consequence of the "jongste gebeurtenissen" (recent events) — that is, the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] — public attention had been renewed on Atjeh, and that "in dagbladen, tijdschriften en brochures de Atjeh-zaak weer druk besproken wordt" (in newspapers, journals and brochures the Atjeh question is again being hotly debated). He invokes the complaint of [[Nederburgh|Nederburgh]] (writing in [[1886]]) that the Dutch public brain "wordt meer verbijsterd dan verlicht door den strijd der deskundigen" (was being more bewildered than enlightened by the quarrel of the experts), and the March [[1893]] testimony of [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] that the Dutch public was unacquainted with the purpose, actions and significance of events in Atjeh.

## Argument and Influence

Kruijt's central argument is that the Dutch failure in Atjeh was not the failure of any single system, but rather the consequence of inconsistency: "geen enkele maatregel consequent is doorgevoerd en volkomen tot zijn recht is kunnen komen" (no measure was carried through consistently and brought fully to its right). He identifies a recurring pattern of half-measures, contradiction between the Dutch government at home and the colonial administration in Batavia and Atjeh, "ongeloofelijk optimisme" (incredible optimism) in the reports from Atjeh, and a structural failure to recognise that the centre of gravity of the war lay on the kusten (coasts) rather than in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]].

His prescription, articulated in the *Besluit* (Conclusion), is for a war footing, the maintenance of the Dutch *stelling* (defensive position) in Groot-Atjeh with active defence, the rigorous and uncompromising application of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] on the coasts, and the avoidance of all "concessiën of vergunningen" (concessions or permissions) and "bijzondere vriendjes" (special friends) until complete submission had been obtained. The diagnosis was vindicated by the subsequent success of the consistent scheepvaartregeling and the marechaussée campaigns under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s governorship from [[1898]].

## Significance

Kruijt's brochure is one of the earliest sustained historical critiques of the entire Dutch conduct of the Atjeh War. It was written before the publication of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]* ([[1906|1906]]) and before the ultimate Dutch victory under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s governorship from [[1898]]. As an *Oud Controleur-Generaal*, Kruyt wrote with the institutional authority of a retired senior colonial official; his decision to publish his critique in the *Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut* series placed it in the institutional context of the [[Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] of Amsterdam. The brochure's tone — at once scholarly and polemical, annotated with personal judgements on the principal actors — reflects the genre of the colonial brochure of the late nineteenth century.

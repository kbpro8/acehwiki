**Toengkoe Bintara Kamangan** — his princely name **Potjoet Osman Bintara** — was the prince of [[Gigiëng]], the little state on the north coast of [[Atjeh]] whose rivers shared a mouth with his constant rival, the prince of [[Pedir|Pidir]]. His submission of [[20 April]] [[1874]] had been the first trade-opening success of the Dutch blockade; his breach of faith and going over to the war party in [[1876]] brought the Dutch [[Cancellation of friendship with Gigiëng (19 August 1876)|cancellation of the friendship of 19 August 1876]].

## Submission and trade

During the Second Expedition, living in continuous hostility with the prince of Pidir — mainly, the Dutch thought, over border claims and the taxes levied at the shared river mouth — Toengkoe Bintara offered his submission on receipt of the *pembrian radja*; trade with his state was reopened; and the wartime need of the resistance in Groot-Atjeh quickly made his harbours, together with Pidir's, the principal supply places of the whole war party. The Dutch flag waved at his capital from [[1874]].

## The bad faith

Since that submission he never once gave proof of his good disposition. With the return of [[Habib Abdoerrachman el Zahir|Habib Abdoer Rachman]] and the rising of the war party in [[1876]], reports repeatedly reached the Dutch that he had joined it; in July certainty came from three sides — the Pidir head [[Radja Mangkoeta]], his own moekim chief [[Toekoe Imam of Gigiëng]], and the Dutch political agent at Penang, [[Lavino]] — that he had invited the heads of his realm to collect money for the *[[Prang sabi|prang sabil]]* (holy war) against the Netherlands, had dismissed the well-disposed [[Toekoe Moeda Rajat]] as market master of Gigiëng and put the hostile [[Toekoe Hasjim]] in his place, and was himself on the way to fight in the [[XXII Moekims]]. His own two letters to the Dutch authorities, delivered in August, announced, under their usual ambiguous and confused phrasing, his departure for Groot-Atjeh.

## The breach

On [[19 August]] [[1876]] Hr. Ms. steamship Marnix anchored on the Gigiëng roadstead with the proclamation of the civil and military commander: the friendship with the prince was openly ended, his harbours were closed to trade, and the Dutch flag was hauled down. The breach lasted two years, during which his people repeatedly alarmed and even attacked the Dutch fortification at [[Segli]]; in [[1878]] an expedition sent from Groot-Atjeh brought his second submission.

Meijer treats his case as the standing illustration of Dutch weakness among the coastal princes before permanent establishments existed there.

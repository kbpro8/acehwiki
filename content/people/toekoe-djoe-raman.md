**Toekoe Djoe Raman** was the Achehnese chief of the kampong [[Thingkun]], outside the valley of [[Lamtoba]] at the foot of the [[Goudberg]], who came into the Dutch bivak at Lamtoba during the final week of the occupation, in August [[1879]], and willingly offered to follow the column — being connected by marriage with the house of [[Panglima Polem]] — as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## Arrival with a gift

During the absence of the main column on the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]], on a morning in mid-August [[1879]], Toekoe Djoe Raman arrived in the bivak at Lamtoba, bringing with him a *sappi* (a head of cattle) as a gift for the column-commandant. He was summoned, together with the other wakils and kampong-heads of the valley, to appear again before the commander on the following day.

## Kinship with Panglima Polim

The book records the family relation that marked the chief's significance: a younger sister of Toekoe Djoe Raman was married to [[Radja Koelala|Radja Koeala]], the son of Panglima Polim. It was with an eye to this connection that, when the hostages for the valley were nominated on [[21 August]] [[1879]], the column-commandant let him know that he desired him to follow the troops — "toonde zich dadelijk bereid de colonne te volgen": he showed himself immediately ready to follow the column. While the Rijksbestierder [[Panglima Banten]] fled in the morning of [[22 August]] and had to be replaced by his captured son, Toekoe Djoe Raman marched with the troops as a willing companion.

## Significance

The willing adherence of a chief allied by marriage to Panglima Polim's house was one of the quiet political gains of the Lamtoba occupation: it extended the network of the submitted into the very family of the most obdurate of the war-leaders of [[Groot-Atjeh]], and it illustrates Generaal [[General van der Heijden|van der Heijden]]'s policy of binding the chiefly class of the valley in person to the new order.

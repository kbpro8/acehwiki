**Hendrick van Bergel** was a Dutch commercial officer in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] who served as governor of [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] from [[1609]] to [[1610]] — the period immediately following the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|murder of Verhoeff of 22 May 1609]] — and was the principal Dutch commercial officer at Banda during the critical transition from the [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] fleet's coercive monopoly policy to the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force. Van Bergel is the principal Dutch governor of Banda in Van der Woude's [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]] before [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]].

## The appointment and the eeuwig verbond

Van Bergel was appointed governor of Banda after the death of **Jacob de Bitter** (the first governor, who had died of wounds sustained in the attack on Selamon in July [[1609]]). The appointment followed the conclusion of the *eeuwig verbond* (eternal covenant) of [[10 August]] [[10 August]] [[1609]], by which [[Banda-Neira|Banda-Neira]] was ceded to the VOC in perpetuity and the Bandanese chiefs were placed under the jurisdiction of Fort Nassau. Van Bergel's task was to enforce the covenant: to ensure that the Bandanese delivered their spices only to the Dutch, to suppress the sluikhandel (clandestine trade) with the English and the Javanese middlemen, and to maintain the Dutch position at the fort.

## The conduct of the governorship

Van Bergel's conduct of the governorship was the subject of controversy. The Bandanese continued to resist the Dutch monopoly, often selling their spices to the English at [[Poeloe Ai|Ai]] and [[Run|Run]] or to Javanese and Makassarese middlemen. The Dutch position at Fort Nassau remained precarious: the Bandanese "buiten de muren of op zee voortdurend gevaar liepen". Van Bergel's correspondence with the [[Heeren Zeventien|Heeren Zeventien]] shows the difficulties of the position: "Banda moet 't ondergebracht worden ende moeten aldaer in 't veld domineren", he wrote, anticipating [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]].

When [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]]'s [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] expedition of March [[1610]] arrived, Van Bergel sailed to [[Bantam|Bantam]] in June [[1610]] to consult with [[Jacques l'Hermite|Jacques l'Hermite]]. L'Hermite "ernstig afkeurde" — seriously disapproved — of Van Bergel's conduct: he was of the opinion that his landgenoot "de Engelschen hadden moeten verjagen, dan zouden de Bandaneezen wel opnieuw vrede hebben moeten sluiten". Van der Woude notes that L'Hermite's criticism missed the point: "Het schijnt niet tot l'Hermite doorgedrongen, dat zijn landgenooten machteloos waren tegen den sluikhandel en dat zij in den toestand van gewapenden vrede op Neira en openlijken oorlog op de andere eilanden vrijwel de gevangenen waren van hun eigen fort".

## The transfer to Van der Voort

Van Bergel transferred the governorship to **Willem van der Voort** in June [[1610]] and sailed with two ships to [[Bantam|Bantam]]. His transfer marked the end of the first phase of the Dutch governorship of Banda — the phase of enforcement of the *eeuwig verbond* — and the beginning of the second phase — the phase of escalating conflict with the Bandanese and the English that would culminate in [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s conquest of [[1621]]. Van der Voort's governorship ([[1610]]-[[1611]]) and the governorship of **Dirck Pietersz. van de Sande** ([[1613]]-[[1614]]) would continue the escalation.

## Significance

Hendrick van Bergel is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch governor of Banda in the period immediately following the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|murder of Verhoeff of 22 May 1609]] and the principal Dutch commercial officer at Banda during the critical transition from the [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] fleet's coercive monopoly policy to the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force. His conduct of the governorship — and the controversy it provoked with [[Jacques l'Hermite|l'Hermite]] — frames the institutional tension between the Bantam factorship and the Banda governorship that would define the early VOC administration. Van Bergel's correspondence with the [[Heeren Zeventien|Heeren Zeventien]], anticipating [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]], also frames the intellectual continuity between the [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] fleet's coercive monopoly policy and the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme. Van Bergel's role in the Dutch-Atjeh relationship was indirect: by [[1609]] the strategic centre of Dutch-Indies policy had shifted from Atjeh to Banda.

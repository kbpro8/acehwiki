**J. H. Kromhout** was a Dutch Majoor-Ingenieur (Major-Engineer) of the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs]] who, in [[1873]], devised an optical-telegraph system intended for use by the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] in the forthcoming [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]]. The system was studied at the Mineurskamp at [[Zeist|Zeist]] in June [[1873]] by the four engineer officers selected for the [[Koninklijk Besluit of 10 June 1873 No. 33|detachment to Atjeh]], including the young [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede-Luitenant C. J. Snijders]] and his colleague J. W. N. Cramer, but was ultimately set aside in the field in favour of the optical-telegraph system of the [[Dutch Navy|Dutch Navy]].

## The 1873 Optical-Telegraph Project

After the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] under [[J. H. R. Köhler|Generaal-Majoor Köhler]] in [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]], the Dutch government prepared a [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] and called for volunteer engineer officers from the metropolitan army. By [[Koninklijk Besluit of 10 June 1873 No. 33|Koninklijk Besluit of 10 June 1873 No. 33]] four engineer officers were selected, the youngest of whom was [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]. Before departure, Snijders and his fellow selectee J. W. N. Cramer were instructed at the Mineurskamp at [[Zeist|Zeist]] for some days in the application of the new Kromhout optical-telegraph system, "welks toepassing bij de voorgenomen expeditie zou kunnen worden gebruik gemaakt."

The biographer D. van den Berg records that the instruction took place at Zeist, and that Snijders on arrival in the Indies was first employed at the Hoofdbureau der Genie at [[Weltevreden|Weltevreden]] to draft a memorandum on the "seinstelsel-Kromhout." When it became clear that the [[Dutch Navy|navy]]'s system would be used in the field, Snijders was posted to the Staf der Genie at [[Surabaya|Surabaya]] instead, and the Kromhout system played no further part in the Atjeh operations.

## Significance

Kromhout's system belongs to the early history of military field telegraphy in the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] and of Dutch engineer efforts to provide the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] with the command-and-control means needed for the protracted [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The episode also illustrates the recurring inter-service rivalry between the Genie and the Marine in the colonial expeditions: the navy's system was adopted without prior consultation with the engineer officers who had been instructed in the rival system.

Kromhout is one of a series of nineteenth-century engineer officers — together with [[W. Cool|W. Cool]], [[W. Rooseboom|W. Rooseboom]], and others — who rose from the technical ranks of the Genie to staff and command positions. Van den Berg's biography of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] notes that the future Sous-Chef and Chef van den Generalen Staf closed the line of "een vijftal genie-officieren (merkwaardigerwijze allen bij het wapen aangekomen binnen een tijdsbestek van 16 jaar)" who reached important staff functions, of whom Kromhout was the earliest (aanst. 1856).

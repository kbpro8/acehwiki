**Captain Smith** was a British sea-captain who, in March [[1819]], brought to [[Schout-bij-nacht Wolterbeek|schout-bij-nacht Wolterbeek]] at [[Batavia]] an alarming — and, as it proved, false — report of British military movements against the [[Karimon Islands|Carrimons]] and against Acheh itself. He is mentioned once in [[P. H. van der Kemp]] (1900), in Appendix 4, which reproduces the private letter of [[Timmerman Thyssen]] to Wolterbeek dated 16 March 1819.

## The Thyssen letter of 16 March 1819

Thyssen, then on station in the neighbourhood of [[Riouw]] and [[Lingga]], had received intelligence via [[Carnegy|the heer Carnegy]] — who was actively making enquiries at [[Penang]] on Thyssen's behalf — to the effect that two ships with three hundred troops had sailed from Penang, destined for the Carrimons with the intention of taking possession of those islands; and that [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] was about to depart with another three ships and four hundred men for [[Acheh|Adcheen]]. Thyssen himself did not believe the second part of the report, and predicted that Raffles would soon join his friends at the Carrimons and attempt an attack on [[Lingga]] rather than Acheh. He warned Wolterbeek that "these waters swarm with pirates," that the [[Tommegong of Singapore|Tommegong of Singapore]] had at sea nine well-armed pengojabja, and that he had no frigates or large ships at hand to inspire the native rulers with confidence in Dutch power.

## The Smith report and its correction

The postscript to Thyssen's letter, dated 18 March 1819, records the corrective intelligence brought by [[Captain Robson]]:

> "P.S. 18 Maart, met vreugde kan ik UHEG. melden, dat de komst van kapitein Robson het geheele nieuws door kapitein Smith meedegebragt heeft geannuleerd, en dat de scheepen, die hij te Carrimon moest aantrefven, scheepen zijn, die van Pulo Penang over Benkoelen derwaarts bestemd waaren, zij moesten te Benkoelen ammunitie en andere benodigdheden inladen, alzo de heer Raffies de Gouverneur Bannerman van niet konde bekomen wat hij wenschte, en dat uit zijn Gouvernement daarin heeft voorzien."

In other words: Captain Robson's arrival entirely cancelled the news that Captain Smith had brought. The ships that Smith believed were destined for the Carrimons were, in fact, ships sent from [[Penang]] by way of [[Bencoolen]] to those islands; they had to load ammunition and other supplies at Bencoolen, because Raffles — unable to obtain from [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Governor Bannerman]] at Penang what he wanted — had been supplied from his own government at Bencoolen. The riddle was finally solved when news arrived from Penang that Raffles had given up his intended seizure of the Carrimons and had settled at [[Singapore]] instead.

## Significance

The Smith-Robson episode is interesting to Van der Kemp as the immediate intelligence background to Raffles's movements in February-March 1819 — the very weeks in which the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission]] was concluding the [[Treaty of Pedir]] at [[Pedir]]. Thyssen, the senior Dutch naval officer on the spot, was receiving contradictory reports from passing captains; the colony's intelligence picture was, in consequence, of a piece with the political disarray in the Strait of Malacca that followed Raffles's detour to [[Karimon Islands|Karimon]] and his founding of Singapore. Captain Smith's report, though false, was sufficiently credible at the time to alarm Thyssen into calling for a powerful Dutch squadron to be sent to the region.

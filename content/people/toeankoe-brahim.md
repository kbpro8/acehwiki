**Toeankoe Brahim** — also written *T. Brahim Montassik*, *Toekoe Brahim* — was, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, the son of [[Toeankoe Hasjim]] and the former guardian of the [[Pretender Sultan|pretender Sultan]] who, in late May [[1896]], "went with many followers over the [[VII Moekims Baid]] to [[Groot-Atjeh]], settling at [[Anao-Galong]]" alongside the pretender Sultan's younger brother [[Toeankoe Machmoed]]. He is also identified as a band leader in the [[V Moekims Montassik]] — one of the eight hostile chiefs ([[T. Brahim Montassik|Brahim]], [[Pang Arab]], [[Panglima Nja Makam]], [[Pang Sech]], [[Habib Samalanga]], [[Teungkoe Soepi|Teungkoe Soepi]], [[Teungkoe Loeboek|Loeboek]] and [[Teungkoe Lam-Oee|Lam-Oee]]) whose fortifications provoked the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 punitive expedition]] — and Kruisheer records that his "family lived at [[Lam-Pasei]]". The deserter [[Sergeant Carli]] was sent on his behalf to [[Kroeng Gloempang]] in late March [[1896]] with a letter demanding that the post be handed over in exchange for free retreat and retention of weapons — an early and aggressive assertion of Achehnese military pressure on the Dutch buitenlinie.

## Guardianship of the Pretender Sultan and Family Position

Kruisheer's identification of Brahim as the son of [[Toeankoe Hasjim]] and the former *voogd* of the [[Pretender Sultan|pretender Sultan]] places him in the inner circle of the dynastic party. The guardian was the figure responsible for the pretender Sultan's person and upbringing, and the role carried significant political weight within the dynastic network. The fact that Brahim was the *former* voogd suggests that the role had been transferred or relinquished by the spring of [[1896]], but his continuing presence alongside the pretender Sultan's younger brother [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] in the movement to Groot-Atjeh indicates that he remained a central figure in the dynastic party. The identification of Brahim as a *bendehoofd* in the V Moekims Montassik also indicates that he combined his dynastic role with active military command, and his family's residence at [[Lam-Pasei]] — a kampong in the V Moekims Montassik — gave him a territorial base within the southern sector of the western *sagi*. The combination of dynastic guardianship, territorial command, and family residence in the V Moekims Montassik made Brahim one of the most well-connected figures in the Achehnese coalition of the spring of [[1896]].

## The Sergeant Carli Letter and the 16–17 June Tuchtiging

The earliest concrete action associated with Brahim in *ATJEH '96* is his dispatch of the deserter [[Sergeant Carli]] to the Dutch post at [[Kroeng Gloempang]] with a letter demanding the post's surrender. Kruisheer records that Carli "sent a letter from the enemy side to Kroeng Gloempang, on behalf of Toekoe Brahim der V Moekims Montassik, demanding the post be handed over in exchange for free retreat and retention of weapons". The demand — surrender in exchange for free retreat with weapons — was a standard formula of Achehnese siege diplomacy, and its dispatch in late March [[1896]], in the immediate aftermath of Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection on 29 March]] [[1896]], reflects the confidence which the defection had given to the Achehnese side. The demand was, of course, refused, and the Dutch buitenlinie was progressively evacuated through April [[1896]]; but the dispatch of the letter itself established Brahim as one of the most aggressive Achehnese commanders in the immediate post-defection period. By mid-June [[1896]], Brahim's fortifications in the V Moekims Montassik had become sufficiently threatening to provoke the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 16–17 June 1896]] — a major two-day operation under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] with three colonnes (under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]]) — which burned Lam-Soet, stormed the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng*, and forced the retreat of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] to [[Lam-Tobah]]. The 2 May 1896 *diversie* to Montassik under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] had also inflicted "5 killed, 12 wounded" on Brahim's bands, an earlier indication of the intensity of the fighting in his sector.

## Significance

Toeankoe Brahim's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the intersection of dynastic authority and territorial military command — the *voogd* of the pretender Sultan who was also a *bendehoofd* in the V Moekims Montassik, with a family residence at Lam-Pasei and an aggressive record of Achehnese siege diplomacy. His dispatch of the [[Sergeant Carli]] letter to Kroeng Gloempang in late March [[1896]] was one of the earliest Achehnese military-diplomatic initiatives of the post-defection period, and his continuing presence among the hostile *bendehoofden* of the V Moekims Montassik through mid-June [[1896]] confirms that he was one of the most persistent Achehnese commanders of the first semester of [[1896]].

**Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje** ([[8 February]] [[1857]]–[[1936]]) was a Dutch orientalist, Islamic scholar, and government adviser whose analysis of the religious-political dynamics of [[Atjeh]] shaped Dutch military strategy during the final phase of the [[Aceh War]]. As adviser for Arabic and indigenous affairs to the government of [[Atjeh]], his reports and recommendations—especially the [[1892]] *Verslag omtrent den religieus-politieken toestand in Atjeh*—provided the intellectual framework that guided [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s active subjugation policy.

## Early studies and Mecca

Before his first mission to [[Atjeh]], [[Snouck Hurgronje]] had spent time in [[Mecca]] ([[1884]]–[[1885]]), where he established contact with [[Atjeh]] pilgrims and observed the pan-Islamic networks through which religious resistance ideas circulated. This experience gave him insight into the spiritual motivation behind [[Atjeh]]'s resistance to Dutch rule. During his residence in [[Mecca]] he produced the second volume of his study *Mekka*, which remains a foundational work on Meccan ritual life.

## First mission to Atjeh (1891-1892)

On the instructions of the [[Indische Regeering]], [[Snouck Hurgronje]] arrived in [[Atjeh]] in [[July 1891]] with a commission to study the religious element in the country's political conditions. He remained until [[February 1892]] and produced the report *Verslag omtrent den religieus-politieken toestand in Atjeh*. The report concluded that:

1. The court of [[Keumala]]—with everything belonging to it—should be left aside as a matter indifferent to the course of events.
2. The actively hostile party should not be negotiated with, but struck very hard, since its own teaching and self-interest compelled it to submit only to overwhelming force.
3. The government should strive to restore confidence in its intentions by promoting agriculture, industry, and trade.

## The Oema affair and the rejection of his advice

After completing his [[1892]] report, [[Snouck Hurgronje]] travelled back to [[Batavia]] to await more favourable circumstances. His recommendations found little support at first. The [[Gouverneur]] of [[Atjeh]] at the time, [[Generaal Van Teyn]], and the newly appointed [[Gouverneur]] [[Deykerhoff]] (from [[January 1892]]) rejected his conclusions in favour of the alliance-policy centred on [[T. Oema]]. [[Deykerhoff]] had unlimited confidence in [[Oema]], despite the lack of any service record justifying such trust. The subsequent [[1896]] defection of [[Oema]]—who turned his Dutch-supplied modern rifles against his former patrons—confirmed [[Snouck Hurgronje]]'s warnings.

Following the [[1896]] debacle, the [[Indisch Besluit]] of [[14 September]] [[1896]] No. 2 adopted many of [[Snouck Hurgronje]]'s ideas as official policy. In [[1898]] he was formally appointed Adviser for Arabic and Indigenous Affairs, with authority to travel to [[Atjeh]] whenever his duties required.

## Collaboration with Van Heutsz (1898-1904)

In [[March 1898]], [[Snouck Hurgronje]] joined [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in [[Atjeh]] as part of the new strategic team. Their cooperation produced the [[Pidië Expedition]], the capture of [[Keumala]] ([[14 June]] [[1898]]), and the systematic campaign against [[Panglima Polim]]. Although their views were not identical—[[Snouck Hurgronje]] held that pacification by controlling the coast alone was impossible, since administrative influence could not be exercised in lands where the government could not show itself—both men recognised that sustained military pressure combined with ethnographic understanding was the only viable path.

[[Snouck Hurgronje]] drafted the three-article *korte Atjèhverklaring* (short declaration) together with [[Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]]. This declaration replaced earlier, more elaborate loyalty oaths and became the legal basis for indirect rule in the [[Onderhoorigheden]].

## Intellectual framework and publications

[[Snouck Hurgronje]]'s major work, *De Atjehers* ([[1893]]–[[1894]]), remains the standard ethnographic and historical account of [[Atjeh]]. Its English translation, *The Achehnese* ([[1906]]), was prepared during [[Snouck Hurgronje]]'s second stay in [[Atjeh]] ([[1899]]–[[1903]]). The work documents the structure of [[Atjeh]] society—the [[gampong]] as the smallest territorial unit, the rise of the [[moekim]] around the mosque, the authority of the [[oelèëbalangs]], the relationship between [[adat]] (customary law) and [[hoeköm]] (Islamic law), and the role of the [[sarakata]] (royal edicts). It continues to be the principal source for studies of [[Atjeh]].

## Significance

[[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] is recognised as one of the most effective collaborations between scholarship and military strategy in colonial history. His analysis that no mere treaty with a sultan could pacify a country divided among independent chiefs shaped Dutch expectations for the negotiations of the [[Korte Verklaring]]. His inclusion—alongside [[Van Heutsz]] and [[Generaal Van der Heyden]]—in the colonial atlas of "Great Men" of the late colonial period attests to the public recognition of his contribution to the consolidation of Dutch authority over [[Atjeh]].

## Account from *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*

Most influential scholar of Aceh. Stayed in Aceh [[1892]]-[[1893]] to advise on political situation. Advised: never negotiate with the enemy, only strike them, never let them recover - then show peaceful intentions. His advice was implemented by [[Generaal J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. Recommended [[Generaal J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] for command of Aceh. Author of '[[Snouck Hurgronje's Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners (1903)|Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners]]' (The [[Gajo-land (Gayo Land)|Gayo]] Land and its Inhabitants) and other standard works. Died [[1936]].

*Source: J. Jongejans, Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu (Baarn: Hollandia Drukkerij N.V., c. 1938), pp. 17, 18, 20, 40, 41, 47.*

**Toeankoe Abdul Medjid** — also written *Tuanku Abdul Majid* — was an Atjehese royal pretender to the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] whose repeated submissions to the Dutch — in [[June 1887|June]] [[1887]], again in [[May 1885|May]] [[1885]], and on subsequent occasions — and whose successive Dutch pensions and allowances made him, in Kruijt's judgement, the most striking instance of the Dutch policy of buying off dangerous enemies. He is described as a Sultanstelg (pretender to the Sultanate) who had already deceived the Dutch before [[May 1885|May]] [[1885]] and who would later "steeds om den tuin geleid" (continually lead [the Dutch] around the garden — i.e., deceive them).

## The May 1885 Negotiations

Already in [[May 1885|May]] [[1885]] the Dutch administration again began to negotiate with a second Sultanstelg, Toeankoe Abdul Medjid, "die ons voor dien tijd reeds bedrogen had en later steeds om den tuin geleid heeft" (who had already deceived us before that time and later continually deceived us), about his submission. The negotiations took place under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]]'s governorship, in the period of the *aajoleeren* (cajoling) — as [[Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] would later aptly call it — when various chiefs within and outside the line were paid by the Dutch.

## The June 1887 Submission

Under [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]]'s governorship, [[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]] requested a meeting at [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]] and already in [[June 1887|June]] [[1887]] the aforementioned Sultanstelg Toeankoe Abdul Medjid submitted, and — besides a gift of ƒ25,000 — enjoyed a monthly allowance of ƒ400.

## The 1890s West Coast Activity

Under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s governorship, Toeankoe Abdul Medjid, paid by the Dutch, long held house on the [[Westkust|West Coast]], robbing and plundering. His brother [[Pangeran Hoessin|Pangeran Hoessin]] sometimes joined in. Finally picked up by the [[Koninklijke Marine|Marine]] — who should have hanged him directly — he came to [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and was also taken into favour again. This was the unworthy, humiliating, and dangerous policy in the last years of Deijkerhoff's governorship that brought on the Dutch the deep, intense contempt of friend and foe alike.

## Significance

Toeankoe Abdul Medjid is the most striking instance in Kruijt's brochure of the Dutch policy of buying off dangerous enemies by pensions and allowances. His successive submissions — in [[May 1885|May]] [[1885]] and [[June 1887|June]] [[1887]] — and his successive Dutch payments — a gift of ƒ25,000 and a monthly allowance of ƒ400, then his continued pension in the 1890s — furnish the measure of the cost of the Dutch policy of conciliation; his continued depredations on the [[Westkust|West Coast]] under Dutch pension furnish the measure of its futility. His case is the precedent for the more catastrophic policy of arming [[Teuku Umar|Teuku Umar]] that would culminate in the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]].

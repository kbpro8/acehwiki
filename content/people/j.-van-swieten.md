**J. van Swieten** was a lieutenant general of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] and special diplomatic envoy who concluded the [[Treaty of 1857 between Netherlands and Aceh]], and who subsequently served as Commander-in-Chief of the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] between December [[1873]] and April [[1874]].

## Diplomatic Mission of 1857

In [[1857]], [[J. van Swieten]] visited [[Kraton of Aceh]] as an envoy of the [[Dutch East Indies Government]], negotiating a bilateral treaty of commerce and friendship with the [[Aceh Sultanate]]. This experience led colonial authorities to assume in [[1873]] that he possessed unmatched personal expertise regarding Aceh.

## Command of the Second Expedition (1873-1874)

Called out of retirement after the defeat of the [[First Aceh Expedition]], General [[J. van Swieten]] commanded an armada of over 13,000 troops. Landing at [[Koerong Raba Bay]] in December [[1873]], his forces executed the [[Attack on Koerong Raba]], captured [[Meuraxa]] and [[Pekan Badak]], and occupied [[Kraton of Aceh]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] ([[Capture of the Aceh Kraton]]).

## Telegrams and Historiographical Controversy

Following the palace's capture, [[J. van Swieten]] transmitted the [[General van Swieten War Telegrams]] predicting general submission, citing the [[Raja of Gighen Letter of Submission]] and alliances with [[Teuku Nek of Meuraxa]]. 

When resistance continued under [[Panglima Polem]], anonymous critics writing as "Brutus" ([[Open Letter by Brutus to General Knoop]]) attacked his cautious strategy. The [[1876]] study *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* defended [[J. van Swieten]], proving through official dispatches in the [[Commander's Journal of the Second Aceh Expedition]] that his deliberate operations saved troops from cholera ([[Field Hospital and Cholera Epidemics in Aceh]]) and logistical collapse.

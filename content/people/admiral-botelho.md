**Admiral Botelho** was the Portuguese naval commander whose victory over the [[Atjeh's attack on Malakka (1629)|Acehnese fleet sent by [[Iskandar Muda of Atjeh|Iskandar Muda]] against [[Malakka|Malakka]] in [[1629]] broke the back of Acehnese naval power in the [[Straits of Malacca|Straits]] and forced the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] to take up the anti-Portuguese role alone. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* he is named only as "the Portuguese admiral Botelho" (*den Portugeeschen admiraal Botelho*) — the sole Portuguese individual mentioned by name in the Westkust-Malakka narrative.

## The 1629 battle

The Compagnie had watched Iskandar Muda's 1629 expedition against Malakka with undisguised satisfaction. "Nothing could be so welcome to her as that Atjeh and the Portuguese should exhaust each other in a mutual struggle" (*niets kon haar zoo welkom zijn, dan dat Atjeh en de Portugeezen elkaar in een onderlingen strijd uitputten*). The hope was disappointed: "The Atjehers were however totally defeated by the Portuguese admiral Botelho" (*De Atjehers werden echter door den Portugeeschen admiraal Botelho totaal verslagen*).

The phrase *totaal verslagen* — totally defeated — indicates that the Acehnese fleet was effectively destroyed as a fighting force. Stapel does not give casualty figures or the size of the opposing fleets, and Botelho's first name is not given in the text.

## Consequences

The 1629 disaster broke the back of Acehnese naval power in the Straits. Iskandar Muda would never again be able to threaten Malakka directly, and the burden of confronting Portuguese Malacca fell henceforth on the Compagnie alone. From [[1633]] the Malaccan waterway was "more closely watched" by the Compagnie, and the [[1640]]–[[1641]] siege that finally took the city for the Compagnie could proceed without serious Acehnese help.

## Significance

Botelho is, in the structure of Stapel's narrative, the man whose victory over Atjeh made possible the Compagnie's later conquest of Malakka. By destroying the Acehnese fleet in [[1629]] he eliminated Atjeh as a serious naval contender in the Straits, and he bequeathed to the Compagnie the strategic problem of confronting Portuguese Malacca alone. The [[1641]] fall of Malakka is, in this sense, the long-term consequence of Botelho's victory.

The fact that Botelho is the only Portuguese individual named in the Westkust-Malakka narrative is itself significant. The Compagnie's Dutch sources name him because his victory over Atjeh was a Dutch strategic interest — even though he was a Portuguese enemy — and because the scale of his victory made it impossible to omit. The lone Portuguese name in a Dutch colonial atlas is the index of a single, decisive naval engagement.

**Mohamad Arief** — *hoofddjaksa* (chief prosecutor) of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur|Civil Administration]] of [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] in [[1896]] — was the senior native justice official of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|Dutch colonial government]] in the Residency, who was responsible, in early [[1896]], for the investigation of the uniforms (*baadjes*) that [[Teukoe Oemar|Teuku Umar / Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan]] had had sewn for his government legion. The investigation was one of the immediate triggers of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar of 29 March 1896]].

## Role in the 1896 crisis

Arief was, in [[1896]], the *hoofddjaksa* of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur|Civil Administration]] of [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]]. The *hoofddjaksa* was the senior native justice official of the colonial government in the Residency, responsible for the investigation of crimes and the preparation of prosecutions. The [[First Letter of Teuku Umar to the Governor of Atjeh (Bijlage II)|first letter of Teuku Umar]] to the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur|Governor of Atjeh]] (Bijlage II of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]*) records that:

> *Heeft de hoofddjaksa Mohamad Arief een onderzoek ingesteld naar de baadjes, die ik liet naaien.*

— *the hoofddjaksa Mohamad Arief has instituted an investigation into the uniforms that I had had sewn.* The investigation treated the uniforms as if they were an unauthorised military force rather than the legitimate uniform of a government ally, and was one of the immediate causes of Umar's rupture with the government.

## Significance

Arief is the senior native justice official of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur|Civil Administration]] of [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]] in [[1896]] and one of the immediate triggers of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]]. His investigation of the uniforms of Umar's government legion was, in Umar's own account, one of the principal grievances that led to his defection.

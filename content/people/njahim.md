**Njahim** was an Acehnese whom, according to [[H. van Kol]] speaking in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[17 November]] [[1897]], the governor and military commander [[Karel van der Heijden|Van der Heyden]] "liet onschuldig doodschieten" — caused to be shot dead though innocent.

## The allegation

The charge was made in a single sentence which linked three acts to Van der Heyden: that he had Njahim shot though innocent; that he wrote in pencil in the margin of a [[Krijgsorder for the VI, XXII and XXVI Moekims|krijgsorder]] for the [[VI Moekims]], [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] the words "er worden geen krijgsgevangenen gemaakt" (no prisoners of war are to be taken); and that he was with his command *"kassi mandie!"* so cruel that [[H. van Kol|Van Kol]] preferred not to communicate its meaning to the chamber, "om geen kreet van afschuw in Nederland te doen opgaan."

The source gives no further particulars — no date, no place, no indication of Njahim's status, and no account of the proceedings, if any, by which he was condemned.

## Historiographical caution

The allegation rests solely on Van Kol's parliamentary statement of [[17 November]] [[1897]]. Neither the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] nor any other speaker addressed it. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] replied in general terms that he fully endorsed the condemnation of cruelties "indien metterdaad die feiten zoo zijn," and that if such facts were concretely and clearly proved to the chamber, no member of any party would hesitate to condemn them.

**K. H. Westendorp** was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] lieutenant of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] who was mortally wounded in the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] near the Dutch temporary post of [[Anagaloeng]] in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and died of his wounds on [[11 March]] [[1896]]. He is the first of the officers named on the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]]'s roll of the fallen of the spring [[1896]] crisis — *Gevallenen, hun Roeping Vervuld!* — *Fallen, their calling fulfilled!* — and his portrait is one of the six reproduced in the album's closing plates.

## The patrol of 7 March 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the patrol was sent out on [[7 March]] [[1896]] under Captain [[Van Blokland|van Blokland]] and two lieutenants, with the object of proceeding westward from the extreme temporary post of [[Anagaloeng]] to deal with a band of Atjehnese who had, for some two weeks, been shooting at everyone they could hit from that direction. Lieutenant [[Franson]], who had been felling trees in the neighbourhood of the post, had already received a bullet in his body, and the newly installed post-commandant, the overste [[Bisschoff van Heemskerk]], had judged it necessary to put an end to the annoyance.

The patrol — 72 men strong — was left relatively unmolested on the march out, but on the return march it was attacked by various bands and was hard-pressed to hold its own. Although it was only some 350 paces from Anagaloeng, it had to hold its ground for five long hours in order not to leave the dead and wounded in the enemy's hands, and no help could reach it from the post because only 28 men had been left behind in the *benting*. The commandant at [[Senelop]] heard the rumour of the engagement and hurried with 40 men under lieutenants Westendorp and [[Ketjen]] towards Anagaloeng; by taking up a flanking position and drawing the Atjehnese fire upon himself, he gave Van Blokland the opportunity to retire.

## Death

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records the casualties of the relief in a single sentence: *Luitenant Westendorp en drie minderen waren zwaar, kapitein van Blokland, luitenant Stakman, luitenant van Hasselt, de officier van gezondheid van der Meer, de adjudant-onderofficier Kruger en 18 minderen licht gewond* — "Lieutenant Westendorp and three subordinates were severely [wounded], Captain van Blokland, Lieutenant Stakman, Lieutenant van Hasselt, the medical officer van der Meer, the adjutant-non-commissioned-officer Kruger and 18 subordinates lightly [wounded]." The album's roll of the fallen, in the chapter *Gevallenen, hun Roeping Vervuld!*, dates Westendorp's death to [[11 March]] [[1896]] — four days after the patrol — and notes that he was *gestorven aan zijn wonden* — *dead of his wounds*. Of the twelve killed on the patrol, three had to be left on the field, and one man — unobserved to fall — was missing from the roll.

## Burial at Kota Potjoet

The album records that the fallen officers of the spring [[1896]] operations — Westendorp, [[J. W. Vuijk|Vuijk]], [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]], [[H. L. Maarschalk|Maarschalk]], [[J. B. Zon|Zon]], [[H. O. W. L. Kramer|Kramer]] and [[C. G. A. C. Krohne|Krohne]] — were buried at the cemetery of [[Kota Potjoet]], whose entrance bears the inscription *Aan onze Kameraden, gevallen op het Veld van Eer* — "To our comrades, fallen on the Field of Honour."

## Significance

Westendorp's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the first-named fatality of the spring [[1896]] crisis — the moment at which the *concentratie-stelsel* collapsed under the weight of [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]]. His appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] — both in the narrative of the patrol and in the roll of the fallen — establishes the chronology of the crisis: the patrol of [[7 March]] was the first major engagement after the defection, and Westendorp's death four days later was the first Dutch officer fatality.

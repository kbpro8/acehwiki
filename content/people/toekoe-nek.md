**Toekoe Nek** was the *hoofd* (chief) of [[Marassa]] and, after the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] of [[24 January]] [[1874]], the only *bondgenoot* of the Dutch in Groot-Atjeh. He submitted out of *eigenbelang* and *dwang* — his *gebied* lay "tusschen 2 vuren," between the Dutch at [[Kota Radja]] and the hostile [[VI Moekims]] — but he defended his submission with arms in hand, resisting Atjehneesche attacks on [[26 April|26]]–[[27 April]] [[1874]] and on [[19 May]] [[1874]], and earning, in [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s judgement, the gratitude of "the most" that the Dutch owed to any Atjehneesche chief.

## The Submission and Its Strategic Consequences

Toekoe Nek's *gebied* of Marassa lay on the coast south-east of the Kraton, and his submission secured for the Dutch the *zeehoofd* at [[Oeleh Leh]] and the line of communication between the sea and the Kraton. The [[VI Moekims]] — the confederation of six *mukims* adjacent to Marassa, led by [[Toekoe Nanta]] — were hostile to him and to the Dutch, and would have cut Marassa off from the sea had they been able. On [[4 May]] [[1874]] [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]], accompanied by the *commandanten* of artillery and *genie*, adsistent-resident Kroesen and controleur Tadema, met Toekoe Nek at his home. The conference "liep zeer bevredigend af": the Dutch could erect as many posts as they wanted in Marassa. Pel decided to *bezet* Merdoewati, Lampasej, [[Blang Oe]] (headquarters), and Oeleh Leh, with a *batterij* at Oedjoeng Planggan. Borel clarifies that the so-called *strategische driehoek* of [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] is misnamed — the triangle Kwala Atjeh–Kota Radja–Oeleh Leh was Pel's work, and it was made possible only by Toekoe Nek's submission.

## The Defence of Marassa

The Atjehneezen attacked Marassa on the very night of [[26 April|26]]–[[27 April]] [[1874]], immediately after van Swieten's [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)|departure from Atjeh]]. Their goal was to punish Toekoe Nek for his submission and "ons, door het bezetten van Marassa, aan die zijde van de zee af te snijden." The Marassanen resisted *dapper*; seven kampongs with about eighty houses were burned, but the enemy failed to take Marassa. On [[19 May]] [[1874]] the enemy attacked again: Merdoewatie and Blang Oe were *gealarmeerd en beziggehouden*, while *talrijke benden* invaded Toekoe Nek's *gebied*, burning "weder eenige huizen." The Marassanen again had it "zwaar te verantwoorden" but "verdedigden zich zóó dapper, dat de vijand tot staan gebracht werd." The Oeleh Leh *bezetting* also stood an *ernstigen aanval* and "sloeg dien wakker af." "De nieuw opgerichte post te Oeleh Leh redde ons dus van de ramp om van de kust te worden afgesneden." At the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] of [[28 June]] [[1874]] the enemy tried again to persuade Toekoe Nek to turn against the Dutch; he refused.

## Borel's Judgement

"Wij zijn dan ook de meeste dankbaarheid aan Toekoe Nek schuldig," Borel writes. His submission was the political foundation of the [[Strategische driehoek (Atjeh)|strategische driehoek]], and his armed defence of his *gebied* spared the Dutch the worst consequences of van Swieten's premature departure. Dutch aid to Toekoe Nek against his *buren* was, in Borel's reading, the immediate trigger of Pel's abandonment of the *afwachtende houding* in mid-[[1874]].

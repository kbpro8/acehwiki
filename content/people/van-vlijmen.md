**Van Vlijmen** was the member of the [[Tweede Kamer]] who in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November]] [[1897]] gave his unqualified adhesion to everything that had been done and was being done in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], and who pressed for a substantial increase in the number of *[[Recherchevaartuigen|recherchevaartuigen]]* on the Acehnese coast.

## On the justice of the war

Van Vlijmen conceded to [[H. van Kol|Van Kol]] that every war was a national disaster and an evil, but held that such disasters were not always to be averted, and declined to go back into the past. What the causes of the war were had been sufficiently shown, he said, by [[Bahlmann]]. For himself he did not regard the war as unjust, "daar waar wij inderdaad moesten optreden tot verdediging van lijf en goed van de Nederlanders, wier schepen door de Atjehers werden geroofd." — there where we indeed had to act in defence of the life and property of the Netherlanders whose ships were robbed by the Acehnese.

## On the state of the Atjeh valley

Considering the matter without prejudice, he believed one might say that in the past year a better condition had been reached in Atjeh. The whole [[Atjeh valley|Atjeh-vallei]] was at rest and the population had returned to its rice fields. Here and there exceptions might exist, but on the whole he thought the condition in the valley could be called satisfactory, and that in consequence of the establishment of the three bivouacs of [[Tjot Mautjang]], [[Indrapoeri]] and [[Lhoknga|Loknga]]. By those three *kampementen* the Dutch commanded the whole valley and at the same time held in awe the still hostile chiefs who had withdrawn into the mountainous terrain, because those posts lay in their immediate neighbourhood.

The princes on the coast, he thought, would now also begin to understand that the Dutch were in earnest with the maintenance of their authority. In the previous year he had indicated that it would be useful to set some of the so-called friends to rights; [[Segli]] had indeed now had a lesson, and he hoped other friends might soon receive an admonition, so that the friendship might be confirmed.

## His withdrawn motion of [[1896]]

Van Vlijmen recalled that the motion he had proposed the previous year, and withdrawn on the advice of [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] — then a private member — had in his view had some effect. More than before, attention had been directed to the coast, to the *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]*, to the import of war contraband, and to the taking over of the taxes.

In the [[Koloniaal Verslag]] he found it recorded that the [[Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|governor of Atjeh]] did not consider the time come to take over the rights or the taxes of the coastal princes. So be it, he said, but the communication was at least a proof that the matter had not fallen into the book of forgetfulness; and the appointment of a clerk of the *scheepvaartregeling* at [[Lhokseumawe|Selok Semawé]] to be a brother of the Nederlandschen Leeuw also served him as proof that the Dutch officials on the coast were awake and that the *scheepvaartregeling* was at that moment no fiction.

## The recherchevaartuigen

He looked with confidence above all to Minister [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] at work, who in the previous year had told him from the members' bench that he was a supporter of the taking over of the taxes of the coastal princes; and he rejoiced also at the request for three *recherchevaartuigen*. He would rather have seen ten requested, considering what the [[Voorloopig Verslag on the Indies budget for 1898|Voorloopig Verslag]] reported: that precisely the rivers and creeks frequently gave opportunity to small *prauwen* to bring in contraband, and that Dutch warships could not adequately watch against it. He instanced the case cited in the [[Koloniaal Verslag]] of the bringing in of war contraband at [[Simpang Anas]]. One could not therefore, he thought, be quick enough in building search vessels; only when those vessels were in action would the enemy begin to feel Dutch power through the obstruction of the import of war material.

Van Vlijmen voted against [[Van Kol's motion of 16 November 1897|Van Kol's motion]].

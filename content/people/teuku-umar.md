**Teuku Umar** was a charismatic Acehnese military leader, noble chieftain (*uleebalang*), and national hero from West Aceh who played a central role in the resistance against Dutch colonial forces during the late nineteenth century, famous for his complex tactical alliance and subsequent defection from the Dutch administration.

## Early Resistance and Maritime Incidents

Teuku Umar gained prominence as a warlord along the West Coast of Aceh. In [[1886]], he was involved in the seizure of the British merchant vessel *Hok Canton* off Rigas, securing a ransom of 25,000 dollars from colonial authorities for the release of European captives.

## Tactical Alliance with the Dutch (1893–1896)

In [[August]] [[1893]], Teuku Umar executed a strategic submission to Dutch Governor [[C. Deijkerhoff]] in [[Banda Aceh]]. He pledged allegiance to the Netherlands East Indies government and was granted the title *Teuku Umar Johan Pahlawan*, along with command of a colonial-funded indigenous legion of 250 soldiers. Using Dutch weaponry, ammunition, and financial backing, Teuku Umar led operations that successfully captured large portions of the [[XXV Mukims]], [[XXVI Mukims]], and [[XXII Mukims]], expanding the colonial perimeter beyond the [[Concentrated Line|Concentratie-Stelsel]].

## The Treason of Teuku Umar (1896)

In [[March]] [[1896]], in the famous [[Treason of Teuku Umar]], he defected back to the Acehnese resistance with 800 modern rifles, heavy stockpiles of ammunition, and 180,000 guilders in cash. This event completely shattered the Dutch policy of reconciliation and forced the colonial army to abandon all forward outposts established since [[1893]], returning to the defensive [[Concentratie-Stelsel]]. His defection also compelled the Dutch to deploy the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]] in active offensive counter-insurgency warfare. Teuku Umar continued guerrilla warfare until his death in action off [[Meulaboh]] in [[1899]].

**Teuku Umar** — also written *Toekoe Oemar*, and after his pardon of [[30 September]] [[1893]] styled *[[T. Djohan Pahlawan|T. Djohan Pahlawan]]* with the rank of *[[Panglima Prang Besar|Panglima Prang Besar]]* (Great War Commander) — was an Atjehese chieftain of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] whose career, in the words of [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]], "van zijn standpunt beschouwd, zijn slim overleg, zijn diplomatisch talent, zijn groote krijgslist slechts bewonderen" (considered from his standpoint, one can only admire his shrewd deliberation, his diplomatic talent, his great military cunning). He was the master of the [[Westkust|West Coast]] of Atjeh from [[1886]] onwards; submitted to the Dutch in [[December 1892|December]] [[1892]]; was armed and paid by them as their principal Atjehese auxiliary from [[August 1893|August]] [[1893]] to [[March 1896|March]] [[1896]]; and then, in the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], threw off the mask, withdrew into the mountains with his bands, and joined the Atjehese resistance, precipitating the final crisis of the war that would be answered by the despatch of [[Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] and the resumption of full Dutch military operations.

## Master of the West Coast (1886–1892)

By [[1886]] [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] was master on the [[Westkust|West Coast]] of Atjeh, levying taxes, pressing the population, and murdering, robbing, and burning where resistance was offered. In [[June 1886|June]] [[1886]] he captured the steamship *[[Hok Canton|Hok Canton]]*; for 25,000 dollars the Dutch bought the prisoners free with difficulty; [[Kemala|Kemala]] received its share. He was among the principal leaders who, in the period of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] (1884–1886), bound themselves afresh under oath against the Dutch, together with the later Dutch "friend" [[Nja Makam|Nja Makam]].

Already in [[1887]]–[[1889]], under the governorship of [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]], Toekoe Oemar and [[Toekoe Nja Makam|Toekoe Nja Makam]] sought to be taken into favour by the administration and had meetings within the line (in secret, of course) with Dutch emissaries. In [[August 1888|August]] [[1888]], when the Sultan suddenly appeared in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and met with [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]], [[Imam Longbattah|Imam Longbattah]], and [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]], the Sultan appointed Toekoe Oemar as *panglima* (*Emir El Bahr*) of the West Coast as far as [[Singkel|Singkel]].

## The Pardon of 30 September 1893

The circumstances of Toekoe Oemar's pardon were these. Against the exactions, extortions, and acts of violence to which the population outside the line was subjected by the religious party — whose head was still [[Tengkoe Mohamed Amin|Tengkoe Mohamed Amin]], in [[1894]] appointed by the Sultan as Kadhi — the *Chalif El Alam* first began to resist more and more, and when these also extended to the [[4 and 6 Moekims|4 and 6 Moekims]], Toekoe Oemar opposed them and inflicted some painful losses on the Muslemin. He then asked again to be taken into favour by the administration, and the administration put this in prospect, provided he first gave adequate proofs of good disposition by driving out the Muslemin.

In [[December 1892|December]] [[1892]] the capable *Assistent-Resident* [[Kroesen|Kroesen]] came to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], and with him the submission and use of Toekoe Oemar was again raised and carried out. Many believe, Kruijt notes, that this was largely his work, because before him [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]] had always advised a waiting attitude. Helped by Atjehers from within the line — with people, supplies, rifles, and ammunition (from the Dutch, of course) — Toekoe Oemar's operations were crowned with brilliant success. With the cooperation of Dutch troops, Dutch artillery, and Dutch rijksdaalders, he successively took various points, and on [[10 August]] [[10 August]] [[1893]] also the notorious [[Kaloet|Kaloet]], almost without a blow. He blew them away or walked in. On the proposal of Deijkerhoff, he was officially pardoned on [[30 September]] [[30 September]] [[1893]], under the name of *[[T. Djohan Pahlawan|T. Djohan Pahlawan]]* appointed *[[Panglima Prang Besar|Panglima prang besar]]* (Great War Commander), and he and his auxiliaries were granted allowances, food, weapons, and ammunition. He received a fine house and dressed henceforth as a chief officer.

## The "Pahlawan-klucht" (1893–1896)

Thereafter Toekoe Oemar went on, right and left, with great ease and little loss, fighting and conquering. Many viewed this *[[Pahlawan-klucht|Pahlawan-klucht]]* (Pahlawan farce) in the Atjeh drama with great concern. It had all the appearance as if Toekoe Oemar, by agreement with the enemy, was regulating his operations according to a fixed plan, of which again the occupation of numerous temporary posts outside the line — in effect the creation of a second line — was the consequence. Large and small chiefs outside the line were liberally paid by the Dutch. Naturally, then, within the first, the old line, and even beyond it, it was very safe, and this safety was praised and used to prove how far the Dutch were advancing under this system.

Had Toekoe Oemar, with his men, for Dutch money, held that whole second line and thereby provided for the safety of the first and the keeping open of the 1,000-metre free strip — as was first the plan — then much could have been said for it. But no: the principal points had to be occupied by the Dutch's own troops, would have been temporarily assigned to Toekoe Oemar's operations, leading to dispersal of forces and weakening, and in between, in numerous blockhouses, the Dutch allies (?) were quartered. Dutch posts, patrols, and transports were then again regularly fired upon and harassed, often with the Dutch's own unexploded grenades and cartridges; even communication between the two lines was repeatedly broken, and reliefs had to take place by stealth.

As Toekoe Oemar became more demanding, his zeal also diminished. His interest, after all, lay in maintaining, for the time being, the *status quo* on his side. He corresponded with the Sultan and other great gentlemen of the opposition; he even sent them money and gifts; yes, the enemy opposite the Dutch received his share of Dutch rijksdaalders. The Dutch sat on a crater. The situation, should Toekoe Oemar turn against the Dutch again, would simply be terrible, much worse than ever before. Warning voices went up from all sides. For it was universally believed, also among the Atjehers and beyond, that Toekoe Oemar was deceiving the Dutch, that the Dutch were playing a high game, putting everything on one card. But of course, one could not prove it.

## The Defection of 29 March 1896

Deijkerhoff proposed in [[1895]] to make permanent and equip the hitherto temporary posts outside the line. And indeed he managed to get it through at Batavia. Expansion again. But first, with the help of Toekoe Oemar, the [[Lamkrah|Lamkrah]] region — a hotbed of fanaticism, where the Dutch had never been master, where Dutch troops had recently suffered very serious losses — had to be cleared of enemies. There Toekoe Oemar seemed to feel little inclination, whether he did not dare, or he could no longer get enough money out and considered the suitable moment now come to throw off the mask for the umpteenth time. Yet he finally promised to march. He received at the end of [[March 1896|March]] the necessary funds, a number of breech-loading rifles, cartridges, rations, and so on. The combined operation of Dutch troops and the Atjehese auxiliaries was to take place on [[29 March]] [[29 March]] [[1896]].

Fortunately his plan — when the Dutch troops had marched out in mass outside the line, to throw himself on the exposed [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] — was discovered in time, at the eleventh hour. It cost effort to convince Deijkerhoff. The marching out of the troops was countermanded, and so a catastrophe was prevented, in which the surprise attack on [[Lombok|Lombok]] in [[August 1894|August]] [[1894]] would have been child's play. Finding himself discovered, Toekoe Oemar withdrew with his bands into the mountains and fortified himself there mightily; most of the paid friends (?) outside the line joined him. The Dutch posts lying outside the line were surrounded and cut off. Reinforcements had to be telegraphed for immediately to Java. These came at once.

## Kruijt's Judgement

Kruijt's judgement of the defection is severe on the Dutch and admiring of Toekoe Oemar:

> "Welnu, wij kunnen, van zijn standpunt beschouwd, zijn slim overleg, zijn diplomatisch talent, zijn groote krijgslist slechts bewonderen, om ons des te meer te ergeren over de verregaande lichtgeloovigheid en blindheid van het bestuur in Atjeh." (Well, from his standpoint, we can only admire his shrewd deliberation, his diplomatic talent, his great military cunning, the more to be vexed at the extreme credulity and blindness of the administration in Atjeh.)

He had "ons gebruikt" (used us), Kruijt writes, rather than the Dutch using him. The fault lay not in the policy of using Atjehers against Atjehers — *Verdeel en heersch* (divide and rule) — but in the half-measures and credulity with which it was executed. "Vooral de verstrekking van massa's geweren en patronen was onverantwoordelijk roekeloos" (Above all, the supply of masses of rifles and cartridges was irresponsibly reckless), Kruijt wrote, citing the warnings of the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[Van Rees|Van Rees]] and many after him, who had been convinced that from every rifle supplied to Atjehers the Dutch would later themselves be fired upon.

## Significance

Toekoe Oemar's defection of [[29 March]] [[29 March]] [[1896]] is the immediate occasion of Kruijt's brochure. It exposed the failure of the Dutch policy of using Atjehese auxiliaries against Atjehese enemies — the policy of *Verdeel en heersch* (divide and rule) — and inaugurated the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in which the Dutch reverted to full military operations under [[Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as government commissioner, and ultimately to the systematic pacification under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]]. Toekoe Oemar himself would be killed in action against the Dutch on [[11 February]] [[1899]] at [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]] — though this is outside the scope of Kruijt's 1896 brochure.

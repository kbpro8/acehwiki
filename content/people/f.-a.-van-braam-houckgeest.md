**F. A. van Braam Houckgeest** was a Dutch marine officer who became **colonel‑commandant of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]]** in **[[November]] [[1882]] and subsequently commanded marine forces during several operations on the [[Atjeh]] coast, notably the 1890 expedition to the [[Edi River|Edi]] area. His earlier service included campaigns in **[[Africa]]** and **[[Guinea]]**, but he is chiefly remembered for his role in the **[[Blockade of Atjeh coast|blockade]]** from **[[1876]]** to **[[1908]]**.  

During his tenure the corps was reorganised by the **[[Royal Decree of 18 September 1880|Koninklijk Besluit van 18 September 1880]]**, and he oversaw the increase in personnel authorised by the **[[Royal Decree of 26 February 1883|Koninklijk Besluit van 26 Februari 1883]]**. Van Braam Houckgeest’s leadership was praised in contemporary naval reports, which highlighted the effectiveness of marine landing divisions under his command.  

## Military Career  

- **[[November]] [[1882]]**: Appointed colonel‑commandant of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] after the dismissal of the marine garrison at **[[Hellevoetsluis]]** earlier that year.  
- **[[8 March]] [[1890]]**: Led the marine landing division that disembarked from the warship **[[Hendrik der Nederlanden (ship)|Hendrik der Nederlanden]]** at **[[Edi River|Edi]]**, contributing to the defeat of Acehnese forces at **[[Pedawa Pondong]]**.  
- **[[June]] [[1890]]**: Coordinated the arrival of the warships **[[Samarang (ship)|Samarang]]** and **[[Bennkoeenn (ship)|Bennkoeenn]]**, which provided artillery support during the left‑bank assault on the **[[Edi River|Edi]]**.  

## Legacy  

Van Braam Houckgeest is commemorated in Dutch naval historiography as a capable commander who helped maintain the Dutch presence in the far‑eastern overseas territories during a period of intense resistance.

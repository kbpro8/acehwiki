**W. F. Palmer van den Broek** was a major of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]] who commanded the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] in Atjeh from [[July 1941|July 1941]] and, in that capacity, became the senior Dutch field commander of the central-Atjeh guerrilla during the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account of the Atjeh campaign (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Palmer van den Broek is the most-cited Dutch field commander: the officer to whom Colonel [[G. F. V. Gosenson|Gosenson]] successively entrusted the suppression of the [[Seulimeum|Seulimeumse]] sabotage, the command of the *oorlogsdetachement Sabang*, and the leadership of the central-Atjeh guerrilla in the [[Tangse|Tangse]]–[[Geumpang|Geumpang]]–[[Takengon|Takengon]]–[[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]] theatre.

## Command of the 2de Garnizoensbataljon and transfer to the Maréchaussee

Before his appointment to the Maréchaussee, Palmer van den Broek had commanded the [[2de Garnizoensbataljon|2de Garnizoensbataljon]] at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]]. In [[July 1941|July 1941]] he was appointed commander of the *Korps Maréchaussee*, succeeding in that post the officer under whom the Korps had been reorganised through the [[1940]]–[[1941]] reductions. He handed the 2de Garnizoensbataljon to Lt-Col [[C. J. Bekkers|C. J. Bekkers]], "die tevoren nimmer eerder in Atjeh gediend" — a transfer the staff history implicitly faults for the loss of Atjeh-experience at the top of the Kotaradja garrison.

## Command of the Oorlogsdetachement Sabang (December 1941 – February 1942)

On [[17 December]] [[1941]], with the outbreak of war with Japan, Palmer van den Broek was sent to [[Sabang|Sabang]] to take command of the *[[Oorlogsdetachement Sabang|Oorlogsdetachement Sabang]]*, formed by combining the two Sabang brigades already there with a full infantry company sent from Kotaradja and a second company, all "ruime mate voorzien van automatische vuurwapens." He remained in command at Sabang through the heaviest Japanese air raids of January–February [[1942]].

In mid-February [[1942]] the *Oorlogsdetachement Sabang* was dissolved under the revised operational plan agreed with General [[Overakker|Overakker]], and Palmer van den Broek was summoned to the Territoriaal Commandant's post at [[Takengon|Takengon]] for consultations. The new plan placed him in command of the central-Atjeh guerrilla: with about twenty reorganised Maréchaussee brigades at [[Lawe Sigalagala|Lawe Sigalagala]], [[Kotatjane|Kotatjane]] and [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]], he was to attack the Japanese in the flank and rear once they had penetrated the Brastagi–Kabandjahe highlands. On [[23 February]] [[1942]] this plan was modified in favour of immediate dispatch to Kotaradja to suppress the [[Seulimeum|Seulimeumse]] rising.

## Suppression of the Seulimeumse (February 1942)

On arrival at Kotaradja, Palmer van den Broek was ordered by Gosenson "orde en rust in de XXII moekims te herstellen en in dit gebied het verrichten van sabotagedaden tegen te gaan, de sedert het plegen van de sabotagedaden gevluchte hoofden op te sporen (o.a. de oudste zoon van de overleden [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]]), het bestuur van de XXII moekims op zich te nemen." The staff history credits him with restoring an outward calm within days: "door dag en nacht te patrouilleren en hinderlagen te leggen slaagde majoor Palmer van den Broek er binnen enkele dagen in, verbetering in de toestand te brengen." The *gevluchte hoofden* — including the eldest son of the late Panglima Polem — remained untraceable. The staff history observes drily that the restored calm "was slechts een schijnsucces": "bepaalde verzetskernen waren nergens ontmoet en geen der gevluchte hoofden had men in handen kunnen krijgen."

## The March 1942 central-Atjeh guerrilla and capitulation at Takengon

After the [[12 March]] [[1942]] Japanese landings at [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]], [[Sabang|Sabang]], [[Ujong Batëë|Ujong Batëë]], [[Kuala Bughah|Kuala Bughah]] and [[Tandjong Tiram|Tandjong Tiram]], Palmer van den Broek commanded the *3de hoofdgroep* in the [[Bireuën|Bireuën]]–[[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]]–[[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]]–[[Takengon|Takengon]] theatre. His instructions were to fight a delaying action along the [[Bireuën|Bireuën]]–Takengon road through three successive stellingen, manned by brigades of the IIIde and Vde divisie Maréchaussee: the first under Lt [[W. van Pesch|van Pesch]] about 10 km south of Bireuën, the second under Captain [[F. Wetzels|Wetzels]], the third under Captain [[L. A. Boucherie|Boucherie]].

On [[19 March]] [[1942]], with the Japanese within 30 km of Takengon, Palmer van den Broek judged further resistance useless and "besloot te capituleren, na alle transportmiddelen te hebben vernield." He issued written orders — in both Dutch and Malay — that all Dutch and indigenous soldiers still at large must surrender; any soldier arrested in uniform carrying arms would be treated as a *franc-tireur* and shot without trial. The order was distributed from the internee camp at Takengon. With this capitulation, the central-Atjeh theatre was effectively closed. The staff history's Nabeschouwing singles out Palmer van den Broek for having "de troep weten te inspireren" and for having "getrouwen om zich heen verzameld" to continue the hopeless struggle — a rare note of praise.

## Significance

Palmer van den Broek is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the figure who carries the operational weight of the Dutch defence. He is the officer dispatched successively to Sabang, to Seulimeum and to central Atjeh, and at each post he is presented as competent and energetic; the failure of his guerrilla is attributed not to his command but to the demoralisation of the indigenous soldaten, the desertion of the inheemse militairen, and the hostility of the Atjehnese population. His [[19 March]] [[1942]] capitulation order at Takengon is the formal Dutch act that closed the central-Atjeh campaign.

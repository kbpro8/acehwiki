**General Booms** was a Dutch *generaal* and military historian whose "zeer juist geschreven critiek" of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Dutch expedition against Atjeh]] — a critique that [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] cites as one of the principal contemporary judgements on [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]]'s conduct of the war — forms part of the documentary scaffolding of *Onze vestiging in Atjeh*.

## The Critique of the Second Expedition

Borel introduces Booms in the opening chapter of his book, alongside [[Generaal Knoop|generaal Knoop]] and [[Luitenant-kolonel de Rochemont|gep. lt.-kol. de Rochemont]], as one of the named critics whose published judgements of the second expedition he cites to buttress his own indictment of van Swieten. The critique is described as "zeer juist geschreven" — very justly written — and is offered by Borel as an independent corroboration of the verdict he himself reaches by participant-observation. The principal points of the critique converge with Borel's own: that van Swieten left the [[Padangsche brigade|third brigade]] at Padang instead of landing it at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai]] for a pincer movement against the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]]; that he ordered the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|storming of the Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] "tegen alle Indische traditiën in, zonder noemenswaardige voorbereiding, alleen in front"; that he recalled the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]] and one company of vesting-artillerie to Java; and that he declared the war ended on [[23 April]] [[1874]] and departed on [[26 April]] [[1874]] leaving a "zeer gebrekkige stelling."

## Borel's Use of Booms

Borel's use of Booms is characteristic of his method: he invokes the named critics of the second expedition — Booms, Knoop, de Rochemont — not as substitutes for his own participant-observation but as independent authorities whose published judgements corroborate the conclusions he himself has reached at the guns' mouth. The convergence is significant, because Booms (a general officer), Knoop (a general officer) and de Rochemont (a retired *luitenant-kolonel*) represent the professional military consensus of the Indies army, against which van Swieten's partisans had tried to defend his conduct by appealing to Pel's [[Pel's letter to Jan van Swieten|particulier schrijven]] attributing his *succes* to van Swieten's *beleid*. Borel explains Pel's letter as *nederigheid, erkentelijkheid* and *deferentie*, and treats the Booms-Knoop-de Rochemont consensus as the more reliable measure.

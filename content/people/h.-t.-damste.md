**H. T. Damsté** — full name *Hendrik Thijs Damsté* — was a Dutch colonial official and author who served two Atjèh periods in the Netherlands Indies civil service, lived through "the period-Van Heutsz almost entirely", and in [[September 1936|September]] [[1936]] published the biographical essay *[[Drie Atjeh-mannen (1936)|Drie Atjeh-mannen]]* — the principal biographical source on [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s Atjèh mission. Drawing on his personal knowledge of the Atjèh period-Van Heutsz and on archival letters from the estates of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], and the general [[Van der Maaten|Van der Maaten]], Damsté's essay corrected, supplemented, and brought to life the published record of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]].

## Service in Atjèh

Damsté served two Atjèh periods in the Netherlands Indies civil service, in the course of which he lived through "the period-Van Heutsz almost entirely" — that is, the governorship of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] from [[1898]] onwards, the period of the final Dutch pacification of Atjèh. His personal knowledge of the period gave his later biographical writing an authority that the published record alone could not provide. He had access to important letters from the archives of Professor Snouck Hurgronje, General Van Daalen, and General Van der Maaten, and he corresponded with many of the surviving principals of the period-Van Heutsz.

## The Atjèh-history in the Koloniaal Tijdschrift

Since [[1916]] Damsté had been publishing occasional Atjèh-history in the *Koloniaal Tijdschrift* — the principal Dutch metropolitan journal of colonial affairs. The articles in the *Koloniaal Tijdschrift* were the foundation on which the [[Drie Atjeh-mannen (1936)|Drie Atjeh-mannen]] essay of [[1936]] was built; the essay itself, with its sustained biographical treatment of Snouck Hurgronje, Van Heutsz, and Van Daalen, was the culmination of the Atjèh-history project.

## Drie Atjeh-mannen (1936)

Damsté's principal publication is the biographical essay *[[Drie Atjeh-mannen (1936)|Drie Atjeh-mannen]]* ("Three Atjeh Men") of [[September 1936|September]] [[1936]] — the same month as the death of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] on [[26 June]] [[26 June]] [[1936]]. The essay treats the three principal Dutch figures of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]]: Snouck Hurgronje, Van Heutsz, and Van Daalen. The Snouck Hurgronje section — by far the longest and most detailed in the extant file — is the principal biographical source on Snouck Hurgronje's Atjèh mission, drawing on the author's personal knowledge and on the archival letters. The Van Heutsz and Van Daalen sections are not in the extant file (the essay closes with the words "Wordt vervolgd" — to be continued).

## Method and Tone

Damsté's method is that of the biographer who, while he admires his subjects, refuses to retouch their portraits. "We will gladly see heroes," he writes in the opening, "but I do not go and model them myself! I retouch nothing, I do not practise beauty-surgery." Where the subjects are beautiful or great, the task can be completed with the more love; but "the smallness in the great, the ugly in the beautiful?... where it is necessary they will be signalled; where it is not necessary, I will gladly be silent over it." This method gives the essay its distinctive character — at once admiring and unsparing, with attention to the smallnesses (the vanity of [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]]'s "haardos" (head of hair), the smallness of [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s contempt for Snouck Hurgronje's advice) that the more hagiographic accounts omit.

## Significance

Damsté is, with [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]] (author of the [[De Atjeh-Oorlog (Kruijt, 1896)|brochure of 1896]]) and [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]] (author of *ATJEH '96* of [[1913]]), one of the principal Dutch biographical-historical sources on the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]]. His personal knowledge of the period-Van Heutsz and his access to the Snouck Hurgronje, Van Daalen, and Van der Maaten archives give his writing an authority that the published record alone cannot provide. His method — admiring but unsparing, attentive to smallness as to greatness — gives his portraits a life and relief that the more formal accounts lack. The *[[Drie Atjeh-mannen (1936)|Drie Atjeh-mannen]]* essay is the principal biographical source on Snouck Hurgronje's Atjèh mission.

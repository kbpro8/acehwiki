**Majoor B. E. Mekern** was a Dutch *majoor* of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who commanded the *rechter-colonne* — the 13th battalion, part of the [[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|Barissans]], and one section of artillery — on [[13 February]] [[1876]] in [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]]'s three-*colonne* operation against the [[III Moekims]]. He is the officer who, on hearing of the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|catastrophe at Atoeh]], changed route, marched on Atoch, *zuiverde* the terrain and recovered the bodies of [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]]'s massacred detachment for proper burial.

## The Three-Colonne Operation against the III Moekims

Pel granted the troops only a couple of days' rest at [[Kota Radja]] after the conquest of Atoeh of [[3 February]] [[1876]] and the operations that had put the entire [[XXV Moekims]] in Dutch hands. On [[13 February]] [[1876]] he advanced in *oostelijke richting* with three *colonnes* against the III Moekims. The *rechter-colonne* under B. E. Mekern — the 13de bataljon, part of the Barissans and one section of artillery — was to march *stroomopwaarts* along the left bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]]. The *midden-colonne* under [[Majoor Jeltes|Jeltes]] — the 12de bataljon, the remainder of the Barissans and two sections of artillery — was to advance from [[Kota Alam]] along the right bank against Pango and the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]]. The *linker-colonne* under [[Majoor Diepenheim|Diepenheim]] was to cover the flank. On [[13 February]] [[1876]] [[Pango]] was conquered and a post *opgericht*.

## Recovery of the Atoeh Dead

On the same day [[13 February]] [[1876]] the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|catastrophe at Atoeh]] took place. Kapitein van Swieten, marching from Kota Radja with two officers and fifty-seven men (ten unarmed) to reinforce the [[Atoeh]] post, was attacked on the *drassige sawah* near [[Lamsajoen]]. The Atjehneezen sprang from the *boschrand*; their charge, in [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s comparison, was "als eene woeste onverwachte charge van cavallerie." Bijna allen sneuvelden "met wonden overdekt"; only a few escaped by flight. The bodies lay on the field; the *wapens*, *munitie* and ambulance materials were carried off by the enemy. On hearing the news, B. E. Mekern changed route, marched on Atoch, *zuiverde* the terrain, and recovered the bodies of van Swieten's detachment. They were "voor schending bewaard" and "met de hun verschuldigde eer ter aarde besteld" — preserved from desecration and buried with the honour due to them.

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — the besieged column of Kajoe-Lē

Meijer prints, in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]), Mekern's continuing part after the catastrophe: on [[14 February]] he received Pel's disposition to take a stand on the northeast edge of kampong [[Kajoe Lē|Kajoe-Lē]] with his column of 305 bayonets and 2 sections of artillery, to keep the road safe between Mesigit Pagar-Ajer and Atoë by continuous strong patrols, and to choose a site for the fortification there. His daily patrols met constant enemy fire, in which Captain [[Van der Pauwert]] fell on [[15 February]], Second Lieutenant [[L. H. Kooy]] was wounded on [[18 February]] and Captain [[Witewaall van Stoetwege|Jhr. Witewaall van Stoetwege]] was mortally wounded on [[27 February]]. When the route by Lambaroe became completely unsafe, he was ordered to march west around behind Lamsajoeng; when that way too was cut, his column stood isolated at Kajoe-Lē with the enemy in heavy force at Lamrong, Lamsajoeng and Lambaroe, short of ammunition for field and fortress guns and of vivres — his telegraphic-style report carried to Kota Radja through the enemy by the two Bugis couriers on the morning of [[6 March]] is printed by Meijer in full, with the request that receipt be answered by two signal rockets at seven in the evening. His position was relieved the following day when Lieutenant-Colonel [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|stormed Lambaroe]]. In late July he led the 3-company column of the south-east line that, with 165 European and 165 native non-commissioned officers and men, searched the ground along the southern mountains to Boekit Daroe as part of the July sweeps, returning over Lamkoenjit, Lamrong and Lamsajoeng to Lambaroe.

## Significance

B. E. Mekern's intervention retrieved what could be retrieved from the worst single day of the *aanvallende* phase. His name is paired in Borel's narrative with that of his kinsman [[Majoor F. J. E. Mekern|F. J. E. Mekern]], mortally wounded on [[26 December]] [[1875]] at the very opening of the offensive; between them the two Mekerns bookend the high-water mark of Pel's *aanvallende* campaign.

**J. F. Haus** was a colonel of the infantry who was appointed the first military commandant of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] under the civil governor [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|A. Pruys van der Hoeven]] on [[6 April]] [[6 April]] [[1881]], following the [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|separation of the civil and military commands]] by the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]]. The appointment, together with the discharge of [[K. van der Heyden|Lieutenant-general Van der Heyden]] from his functions, was one of the last administrative acts of Van Lansberge, who had himself requested discharge for health reasons.

## The Separation of Commands, 6 April 1881

By the Governor-General's decision, by virtue of authority already granted by the King in [[1879]], it was decided to separate the civil and military commands and to entrust the civil administration in Atjeh to a civil governor. The Lieutenant-general Van der Heyden was honourably discharged from his functions, and [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|A. Pruys van der Hoeven]] was appointed civil governor; the military command was entrusted to Colonel J. F. Haus of the infantry. Both assumed their functions on [[6 April]] [[6 April]] [[1881]].

## Significance

The separation of the civil and military commands was the formal transition of Atjeh from a military command to a regular administrative gewest of Netherlands India. Colonel Haus, as the first military commandant under the civil governor, was responsible for the conduct of military operations in Atjeh, which — notwithstanding the [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|formal establishment of civil government]] — continued throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the form of guerrilla resistance, especially in the upper lands of [[Pedir|Pedir]] and the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]].

**H. van Kol** was a Dutch socialist politician and member of the [[Tweede Kamer]] (Lower House of Parliament) for the [[Sociaaldemokratische Arbeiderspartij]] (SDAP), who served from [[1897]] and became the most vocal parliamentary opponent of the [[Aceh War]] (*Atjeh-oorlog*). He represented the Enschede district and was one of the first two social democratic members of the Dutch parliament.

## Opposition to the Aceh War

Van Kol first spoke against the Aceh War during the general budget debates following his election. On [[1 December]] [[1897]], he argued that the war was driven by "annexatie-geest" (annexationist spirit) and "de zucht om nog meer landen te veroveren" (the desire to conquer even more territories). He stated that the war had been started "meer dan een kwart eeuw geleden" (more than a quarter century ago) and that its end was still not in sight.

He described the consequences of the war in stark terms: "Vreeselijk waren de gevolgen van dien zoo misdadigen, roekeloozen eersten stap. Tal van menschen verloren het leven, millioenen gouds werden verspild; wreedheden werden bedreven, moord, verraad en brandstichting werden gepleegd, tal van gruweldaden bedreven." (Terrible were the consequences of that so criminal, reckless first step. Many people lost their lives, millions of gold were squandered; cruelties were committed, murder, betrayal and arson were perpetrated, numerous atrocities committed.)

He concluded: "Wij zijn dus verplicht, in naam van menschelijkheid en recht, zoo snel mogelijk een einde te maken aan den Atjeh-krijg; de [[Atjeh-kanker|Atjeh-kanker]] moet worden uitgesneden." (We are therefore obliged, in the name of humanity and justice, to make an end to the Aceh war as soon as possible; the Aceh cancer must be cut out.)

## The Motion of 1897

Van Kol was the primary mover behind a motion submitted in [[1897]] calling for an impartial commission to investigate the Aceh War — see [[Van Kol's Aceh War Motion (1897)]]. The motion was co-sponsored by [[Troelstra]] and [[Van der Zwaag]], the other two socialist members of the SDAP. It stated that the war should be considered a "nationale ramp" (national disaster) and called for information about the war's significance to be shared with the nation. It questioned whether continuing the war was desirable and possible after 24 years without an end in sight.

The motion was defeated by a vote of 72 to 3, with only the three socialists supporting it.

## Economic critique of the war

Van Kol continued to attack the war in subsequent budget debates. He exposed the economic interests that benefited from its continuation — see [[Economic interests in the Aceh War]] — stating that officers, shipping companies, building material suppliers, food suppliers, contractors, manufacturers, and transport entrepreneurs all had a stake in the war's prolongation. He further argued that European planters and mining companies were pressing for the submission of Aceh, "die azen op de groote rijkdommen van den bovengrond en den ondergrond van Atjeh" (who were preying on the great riches of the surface and sub-surface of Aceh). He claimed that companies had already been formed and capital collected, and that the Acehnese must be subjugated "om kapitalistische hebzucht te kunnen verzadigen" (to satisfy capitalist greed).

He also exposed the practice of sending [[Javanese convicts in the Aceh War|Javanese convicts]] to serve as soldiers in the war, condemning it as "elke beschaafde natie onwaardig" (unworthy of any civilized nation).

## Other colonial critiques

Beyond Aceh, Van Kol was a persistent critic of Dutch colonial policy. He spoke against heavier taxation of the Javanese, called for the abolition of all forced cultivation (*dwangcultuur*), opposed the usury of Arabs and Chinese, demanded better indigenous education, and supported freedom of the press, association, and assembly for the Indo people. He also opposed the extension of Dutch authority in New Guinea, predicting it would bring new costs and wars, a prediction he claimed was borne out when three officers were killed there in [[1900]].

## Later career

Van Kol continued his anti-war advocacy during the [[1901]] Indische begrooting (Indies budget) debates, speaking against what he called "Imperialisme" and "veroveringszucht" (conquest fever), which he said was being prophesied by Dr. [[Abraham Kuyper]]. He noted that the Aceh War remained "de melkkoe van allerlei goudzoekers" (the cash cow of all sorts of gold diggers) and that the minister was a fervent advocate of its continuation. See [[Indische begrooting]].

## Significance

H. van Kol was the most prominent Dutch parliamentarian to consistently oppose the Aceh War and expose the economic motivations behind it. His speeches and motions provide a contemporary socialist critique of Dutch imperialism and colonial capitalism, framing the Aceh War not as a civilizing mission but as a project driven by capitalist greed and the desire for territorial expansion. His analysis of the "Aceh cancer" and his call for its surgical removal captured the moral and political outrage of the anti-war movement.

**Faletahan** — more properly **Sjech Noeroe'd din Ibrahim ibn Maulana Israil**, later titled **Sunan Gunung Jati** (Javanese **Sunan Goenoeng Djati**) — was a Paseier, born at [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] on the north coast of [[Sumatra]], who left his father-city in [[1521]] when the Portuguese took it, travelled to [[Mecca]] for two or three years of Islamic study, returned briefly to [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]], and then crossed to [[Java]] where, around [[1523]]–[[1524]], he married a sister of the king of [[Japara|Japara]] — the king whom [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies with [[Pati Oenoes]] / [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]] — and founded the [[Cirebon|Cirebonese]] branch of the [[Demak|Demak sultanate]]'s *wali sanga*. His migration is the principal documented movement of a north-Sumatran religious figure into the Javanese Islamic establishment in the early sixteenth century, and a key link in the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]–[[Acheh|Atjeh]]–Java triangle.

## Departure from Pasej (1521)

In the chapter *Over Soenan Kalidjaga*, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] — citing [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Dr Hoesein Djajadiningrat]]'s *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* — records Faletahan as "a Paseier by birth [who] left his father-city in [[1521]] when the Portuguese entered there. He went to [[Mecca]] and devoted himself for two or three years to the study of his faith, Islam. Then he returned to his birthplace, but left it again soon after". The year [[1521]] is the date of the fall of [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] to the Portuguese — the political event that broke the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej-Samudra]] sultanate and opened the way for the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s absorption of the north Sumatran coast.

## Migration to Java (c. 1523-1524)

"He came to [[Japara|Japara]]", De Roo de la Faille continues, citing Hoesein, "(of which the year is to be set at [[1523]] or [[1524]])". There, "with his religious teaching he reaped much success, so much so that he was given a sister of the king of the land as wife". The king in question, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, can be no other than [[Pati Oenoes]] — the Javanese commander of the [[1513]] attack on Malacca, then king of [[Japara|Japara]] — whom De Roo de la Faille further identifies with [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]] / [[Soenan Kalidjaga]] of the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]]. Faletahan's marriage, on this reading, was the dynastic link by which a north-Sumatran Paseier entered the Javanese Islamic establishment.

## Foundation of Cirebon

Faletahan's later career — his move to [[Cirebon|Tjarabon]], his settlement at the river "Kali Djaga", his adoption of the title **Sunan Gunung Jati**, and his eventual rule over [[Cirebon|Cheribon]] — is the founding act of the Cirebonese sultanate. In [[1527]], with the help of his brother-in-law the king of [[Demak|Demak]], he seized Hindu-Javanese [[Sunda Kelapa|Soenda Kalapa]] — the event that made Pate Unus "king of Sunda". He is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the principal conduit by which the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] Islamic tradition — itself a product of the long Pasej-Atjeh connection — entered the Javanese *wali sanga*.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Faletahan matters as the principal documented human link between the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] religious establishment of north Sumatra — the same establishment that produced [[Abdurra'uf of Singkel]] and the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s religious class — and the Javanese Islamic world of the early sixteenth century. His departure from [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] in [[1521]] coincides with the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s absorption of the north Sumatran coast; his migration to Java, with the Javanese *wali* tradition's incorporation of a Paseier. He is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the historical kernel of the [[Sadjarah Banten]]'s story of "the Moor Faletahan from Pase".

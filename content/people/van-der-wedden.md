**Van der Wedden** was a Dutch officer of the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army ([[K.N.I.L.|K.N.I.L.]]) who, with [[Christoffel|Christoffel]], [[Colijn|Colijn]], [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], [[Van Loenen|Van Loenen]], [[Van der Maaten|Van der Maaten]], and [[Swart|Swart]], was one of the military figures who stood brilliantly at [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s side during the period-Van Heutsz — the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]] that brought the war to its military conclusion in [[1904]].

## Place in the Period-Van Heutsz

Van der Wedden is named by [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] as one of the military figures who stood brilliantly at Van Heutsz's side during the period-Van Heutsz. The period-Van Heutsz, Damsté writes, was the work of many hands, of whom Van Heutsz was the principal but not the only one. "*Zonder Snouck Hurgronje ware er geen Van Heutsz geweest; en Christoffel, Colijn, Van Daalen, Van Loenen, Van der Maaten, Swart, Van der Wedden, — om in alphabetische orde eenige militaire namen te noemen, — zij als zoo vele anderen hebben hem voortreffelijk gediend.*"

## Significance

Van der Wedden is one of the principal military figures of the period-Van Heutsz — the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]] — and is named by Damsté, alphabetically seventh (last) among the seven military figures who stood brilliantly at Van Heutsz's side. Together with the other military figures of the period-Van Heutsz, Van der Wedden executed the policy that [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] had prescribed and [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had commanded, and so helped to bring the war to its military conclusion in [[1904]].

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Kapitein Van der Wedden** was a Dutch captain of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]] who commanded the temporary post of [[Tjot Rang]] in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and who appears on three of the plates of *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]*.

## Command at Tjot Rang

On the plate of [[Tjot Rang]], Hooijer identified the commandant of the garrison of three officers and seventy-five men standing on the rampart, beside a bench erected among flowers, dressed in *nachtbroek en kabaai* — night-trousers and a loose jacket. The post lay in an old Achehnese fortification on a height by the *[[Passer|passer]]* of Tjot Rang, with the usual vertical wire entanglement reinforced by a horizontal one, earthen ramparts and two bastions armed with 8 cm. pieces.

Life at Tjot Rang, Hooijer wrote, was roughly the same as at [[Toengkoeb]]: no trouble was had from the enemy, and the few rifle-shots fired were aimed at the wild pigs roaming the neighbourhood, which when hit were brought in with cheering and eaten with relish.

## At Biloel and Kroeng Gloempang

Van der Wedden was also identified among the officers photographed at [[Biloel]], and on the plate of the interior of [[Kroeng Gloempang]] he appears on the right of the long passage between the barracks that the garrison had labelled *Heerengracht*, with [[Kapitein Schadée|Schadée]] on the left.

**Massa-an** was an Achehnese chief, the brother of [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed|Toekoe Moeda Daud]] of [[Selimoen]], named among the principal hostile chiefs who still held out after the Dutch occupation of Selimoen on [[15 August]] [[1879]], as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## The council in the missigit of Selimoen

On the day before the arrival of the Dutch columns — as Toekoe Moeda Daud reported to his new allies — Massa-an had been at Selimoen together with [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]], [[Radja Koelala|Toekoe Radja Koeala]], the [[Imam of Longbatta|Iman Longbattah]] and other principal hostile chiefs, assembled in the *missigit* (mosque) of the kampong to deliberate on what was to be done. Perceiving the impossibility of defending Selimoen against the Dutch troops, the chiefs withdrew to [[Reng-Reng]], in the VII Moekims of the landschap [[Pedir]], a place amid wildly overgrown mountains which the troops could reach only after three day-marches. The defection of Massa-an's own brother to the Dutch, and his escort of the Lubeck column to [[Indrapoeri]], left the little company of the missigit council — with the pretendant [[Toewankoe Daoed]] watched by [[Toewankoe Haschim]] and the chiefs at [[Kemala]] — as the last fixed points of the military resistance in Groot-Atjeh.

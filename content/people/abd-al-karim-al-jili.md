**ʿAbd al-Karīm al-Jīlī** — in Dutch scholarship **al-Djili** — was a Sufi author of the post-Ibn ʿArabian tradition, who died around 832 H / [[1432]] C.E. He is the author of the [[al-Insan al-Kamil|*al-Insān al-kāmil*]] ("The Universal Man"), one of the principal expositions of the metaphysics of the unity of being (*waḥdat al-wujūd*) in the tradition of [[Ibn Arabi|Ibn ʿArabī]]. The work is identified by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] as one of the principal sources of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]]: Raniri's notice on the Barāhima is a literal translation of *al-Insān al-kāmil*, ed. Cairo 1293, II, p. 81 r. 33–82 r. 1.

## The *al-Insān al-kāmil*

The [[al-Insan al-Kamil|*al-Insān al-kāmil*]] is a systematic exposition of the Sufi doctrine of the "Universal Man" — the *al-insān al-kāmil* — who is at once the cosmic archetype of the human being and the perfect mirror in which the divine names and attributes are manifested. The work develops, in the tradition of Ibn ʿArabī's *Fuṣūṣ al-ḥikam* and *al-Futūḥāt al-Makkīya*, the metaphysics of the divine names, theophanies, the levels of existence, and the relation of the microcosm to the macrocosm. Its cosmological scope and its sustained articulation of the doctrine of the unity of being made it one of the most authoritative Sufi treatises in the post-Ibn ʿArabian tradition.

## Source of the *Tibjān*

[[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]]'s identification of the *al-Insān al-kāmil* as a source of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] is one of the most original contributions of his [[1955]] edition. Raniri's notice on the Barāhima — the so-called "followers of Ibrāhīm" who, according to Raniri, "say that they belong to the religion of the prophet Ibrāhīm and are his descendants. They worship idols, and a part of them dwells in Gujarat" — is shown by Voorhoeve to be a literal translation of *al-Insān al-kāmil*, ed. Cairo 1293, II, p. 81 r. 33–82 r. 1. The classification of the Barāhima among the scriptuaries, which is unique to al-Jīlī among the sect-writers, is transmitted by Raniri into his Malay encyclopaedia.

## The controversy with Shamsuddin of Pasai

The [[Djawahir al-'ulum|*Jawāhir al-ʿulūm*]] of Raniri records that [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] — the Wujūdiyyah mystic whose works Raniri refutes in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] — also appealed to the *al-Insān al-kāmil*, but that he interpreted the text, in Raniri's view, incorrectly. The *al-Insān al-kāmil* thus occupies a remarkable place in the controversy between Raniri and Shamsuddin: a work to which both orthodox and heterodox appealed, but which each read differently. The same work that supplied Raniri with his account of the Barāhima was, in Shamsuddin's reading, a warrant for the [[Wujudiyyah]] position that Raniri sought to refute.

## Citations in Raniri's other works

The *al-Insān al-kāmil* is cited explicitly twice in the [[Hall al-zill|*Ḥall aẓ-ẓill*]] and repeatedly in the [[Djawahir al-'ulum|*Jawāhir al-ʿulūm*]] of Raniri. In both works it is named as an authority — a fact that indicates the standing of al-Jīlī's treatise in the orthodox Sufi canon as Raniri received it. The same work that supplied Raniri with his account of the Barāhima also supplied him with the metaphysical vocabulary by which he distinguished the orthodox Sufi position from that of the heretical Wujūdiyyah.

## Significance

ʿAbd al-Karīm al-Jīlī is, through his *al-Insān al-kāmil*, one of the principal sources of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri and one of the principal authorities cited in Raniri's other Malay religious writings. His work is also one of the principal authorities cited by [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] — Raniri's Wujūdiyyah opponent. The fact that the same Sufi canon was appealed to by both sides of the Achehnese controversy is a measure of the difficulty of distinguishing the orthodox from the heterodox position in the scholastic Sufism of the seventeenth century, and of the role of commentary and interpretation — rather than the choice of authorities — in the religious controversy of Acheh.

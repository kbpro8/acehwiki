**Soenan Kalidjaga** — also **Raden Sahid**, in the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] the son of the *tumenggong* of Wilatikta, in the [[Sadjarah Banten]] **Raden Sa'id** — is, in the Javanese *wali sanga* tradition, the most popular of Java's saints, and in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s conflation the same person as [[Pati Oenoes]] of [[Japara|Japara]] and [[Demak|Demak]]. His traditional career — the conversion by [[Soenan Bonang|Pangeran Bonang]] at the *pasar* of [[Djatisaripa|Djatisaripa]]/[[Djatiswara|Djatiswara]], the *tapa* at the river *Kali Djaga* in [[Cirebon|Tjarabon]], the marriage of his younger sister to [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]], and the building of the [[Demak|Demak mosque]] — is the principal Javanese hagiographic material that [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads through the *[[bedekte taal]]* lens.

## The conversion story

The [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] tells that Raden Sahid, the wild son of the *tumenggong* of Wilatikta, "was a wild fellow, given to gambling and idling; he came as far as the neighbourhood of [[Japara|Japara]]. When he had lost, he went out robbing, waited for people in the forest of [[Djatisaripa|Djatisekar]], to the north-east of [[Lasem|Lasem]]". One day [[Soenan Bonang|Soenan Bonang]] came through that forest; Raden Sahid held him up to rob him, but Soenan Bonang told him: "if later someone passes here, dressed entirely in black and with a *wora-wari* flower behind his ear, a red *kembang spatu*, you must plunder him". Three days later Soenan Bonang himself returned in that guise; Raden Sahid held him up; "Soenan Bonang became his *sa-kawan* — his companion"; Raden Sahid repented, and after two years of *tapa* he settled at the river *Kali Djaga* in [[Cirebon|Tjarabon]], whence his name.

## Decoding the conversion story

[[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads the conversion story as *bedekte taal*: *Bonang* — the name of the mysterious *pangeran* who converts Raden Sahid — is the name of a *desa* in [[Rembang|Rembang]] south of [[Lasem|Lasem]], but "meant was [[Worawari|Worawari]] on [[Sumatra]]". [[Rembang|Rembang]] is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the Javanese remembrance of the *Ari-ke-mbang* of the Sumatran Overwal — the land of the [[Kalang|Kalang's]], Javanese people who were not "real" Javanese but [[Djawaka|djawa-ka]] of *lang-ka*, the Sumatran country from which they had been brought as war-booty. The "pasar of [[Djatisaripa|Djatisaripa]]" — at which Raden Sahid's conversion takes place — is read as *djati-pa-sar d-ipa*, "the Heiland with the *pasar* of the *Heer des Rijkdoms*"; and the place to which Raden Sahid is sent — *Poelo Oepih*, "the island of the ancestors" — is read as [[Poelo Kampi|Poelo K—oe—mpi]], "the same remarkable hilly islet in the bay of [[Aroe-baai|Aroe]] close under the mainland that the Controleur S.B. [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] of Alkemade described so picturesquely in [[1888]]".

## Marriage to Sunan Gunung Jati's line and the Demak mosque

In the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten]], Raden Sahid — converted by [[Soenan Bonang|Pangeran Bonang]] at the *pasar* — was directed to seek the *ngélmoe sadjati* (the True Knowledge) from "the *dara poetin* of *1-ang mangkoe*" at [[Poelo Oepih|Poelo Oepih]], which De Roo de la Faille decodes as [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]. His later marriage of his younger sister to [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]] — the [[Faletahan|Paseier]] who crossed to Java after the [[1521|fall of Pasej]] — is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the dynastic link that brought the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] Islamic tradition into the Javanese *wali* world.

The [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] further records that when the *wali*s built the [[Demak|Demak mosque]], Soenan Kalidjaga — having gone to *tirakat* at Pamantingan — returned late, gathered the *tatal* (shavings) into a bundle, raised the *saka goeroe* (main pillar) in a single act, and from the heavens received a bundle containing the *saléndang* of the Prophet — which he sewed into the *kjahi goendil*, the leather over-garment that later Javanese kings were to wear at their installation and on the field of battle.

## Identification with Pati Oenoes

The boldest claim of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s chapter is that Soenan Kalidjaga and [[Pati Oenoes]] of [[Japara|Japara]]/[[Demak|Demak]] are the same person. The convergences — both associated with the *pasar* of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]], both connected with the [[Demak|Demak mosque]], and the *patya-oenoes* (market-master's toll) reading of the title *Pati Oenoes* — produce, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, a single historical figure: a Javanese commander who attacked Malacca in [[1513]], gave his sister to a [[Faletahan|Paseier]] in [[1523]]–[[1524]], and built the [[Demak|Demak mosque]] before his death in the [[Demak's attack on Pasoeroewan (1546)|1546 attack on Pasoeroewan]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Soenan Kalidjaga's significance lies chiefly in the [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] decoding of his conversion story — which ties the most popular of the Javanese *wali*s to the [[Aroe|bay of Aroe]] and to the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]–[[Acheh|Atjeh]] religious tradition — and in his sister's marriage to [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]], which is the dynastic link between the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] and Javanese Islamic establishments. His identification, by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], with [[Pati Oenoes]] also makes him, in the *bedekte taal* reading, the *"Coming Man"* of the [[ngélmoe sadjati]] tradition.

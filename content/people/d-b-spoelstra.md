**D. B. Spoelstra** — properly Dr. D. B. Spoelstra — was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] chemist of the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] in [[Amsterdam]] who, in [[1930]], performed the fractional distillation of the Atjeh-turpentine samples that established the chemical basis of the improved quality of the factory-scale product. He is named in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] as the author of the chemical investigation that identified the fourfold reduction in carene content — from 15% in the [[1924]] trial samples to about 4% in the [[1930]] factory product — as the chemical basis of the improved odour of the new Baleq turpentine.

## The chemical investigation

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that, in order to determine whether the changes in the chemical properties of the new product — relative to the trial samples of [[1924]] — were accompanied by a change in chemical composition, Dr. Spoelstra subjected a larger quantity of the new turpentine to a fractional distillation. The distillation was performed on 2500 g of oil (sample *H.M. no. 1607*) under a pressure of 30 mm, and the resulting fractions were examined individually. The first four fractions — totalling 2046 g — consisted of d-α-pinene in, by all observable constants, pure form. The fifth fraction — 270 g — was also principally pinene, so that the actual pinene content was judged to lie between 85% and 90%. The sixth fraction — 227 g — was subjected to further fractional distillation, and from it Spoelstra isolated 93 g (approximately 4%) of Δ3-carene in nearly pure form: boiling point 169–173 °C at 760 mm, density d⁴ = 0.8636, refractive index n² = 1.4723, optical rotation α +12.2°. The identification was confirmed by the preparation of the nitrosate, which after recrystallisation melted at 146 °C.

The carene content of approximately 4% in the [[1930]] factory product — against approximately 15% in the [[1924]] trial samples — was the chemical basis of the improved odour of the new Baleq turpentine. The same investigation isolated a small quantity of sesquiterpenes (3.6 g, or 0.15%), which after distillation over sodium showed the constants characteristic of bisabolene.

## Other publications

The list of *Berichten van de Afdeeling Handelsmuseum* preserved at the back of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that Spoelstra was the author, with A. Reclaire, of *Bericht* No. 25 (*Kajoeputih-oil (Cajeputolie)*, [[1926]]), No. 31 (*Over de bepaling van het z.g. total-geraniol-gehalte in Java-citronellolie en over het analytisch onderzoek van die olie in het algemeen*, [[1927]]), No. 36 (*Verdere mededeelingen over de bepaling van het z.g. total-geraniol-gehalte in Java-citronellolie en over het analytisch onderzoek van die olie in het algemeen*, [[1928]]) and No. 54 (*Over de cineolbepaling in kajoeputiholie*, [[1930]]); and the author of *Bericht* No. 41 (*Onderzoekingen over Bambang-olie en Kajoepoetih-olie*, [[1929]]). The range of his publications — from Atjeh-turpentine to Java-citronella oil, from cajeput oil to *barnes-kamfer* — indicates that he was the Koloniaal Instituut's principal chemical researcher on essential oils.

## Significance

Spoelstra's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the chemist whose fractional distillation of the Atjeh-turpentine samples established the chemical basis of the improved quality of the factory-scale product. His identification of the fourfold reduction in carene content — from 15% to 4% — as the chemical basis of the improved odour is the principal scientific result of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn evaluation of 1930]], and his work on the chemical composition of Atjeh-turpentine is the principal published scientific investigation of the subject.

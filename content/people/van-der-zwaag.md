**Van der Zwaag** was a Dutch socialist politician and member of the [[Tweede Kamer]] for the [[Sociaaldemokratische Arbeiderspartij]] (SDAP) from [[1897]], who joined his colleagues [[H. van Kol]] and [[Troelstra]] in opposing the [[Aceh War]]. He was one of the three socialist members who supported the [[1897]] motion calling for an impartial commission to investigate the war.

## Anti-war advocacy

Van der Zwaag co-sponsored Van Kol's [[1897]] motion on the Aceh War — see [[Van Kol's Aceh War Motion (1897)]] — which was ultimately defeated by a vote of 72 to 3. He consistently voted with his socialist colleagues against all military expenditures, including budgets for the army and navy. In debates on military spending, he argued that the true enemy was domestic poverty and that money should be spent on combating what he called "den steeds voortkankerende armoede" (the ever-spreading poverty) before spending on foreign defence.

## Criticism of the penal system

Van der Zwaag was particularly active in criticizing the Dutch penal system, especially the "celstelsel" (cell system) and the use of preliminary detention (*voorloopige hechtenis*). He brought cases to parliamentary attention of individuals who had spent many months in pre-trial detention before being acquitted. He argued that the criminal justice system was biased against the poor and often failed to deliver justice.

## Socialist solidarity

Although less prominent than Troelstra or Van Kol in the Aceh War debates, Van der Zwaag consistently voted with his socialist colleagues on all colonial and military matters. He supported the party's positions on universal suffrage, workers' rights, anti-militarism, and opposition to colonial expansion.

## Significance

Van der Zwaag was a dedicated member of the first socialist parliamentary group in the Netherlands. His consistent anti-war and anti-militarist votes helped establish the socialist position on the Aceh War as principled opposition to all military expenditures. His work on the penal system complemented the party's critique of the violence and injustice of the colonial state.

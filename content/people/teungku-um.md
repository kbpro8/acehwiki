**Teungku Um** — also *Teungku Umar* — was the Atjehnese war-leader who, after several years of co-operation with the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] — during which he was officially granted clemency, permitted to maintain a legion of 250 men at Dutch expense, and given a further 1,000 rifles, much ammunition and money — threw off the mask in [[1896]] and turned his modern-Dutch arms against his former patrons. The [[1896]] defection is the type-specimen, in the source's narrative, of the Dutch misreading of Atjehnese warlord politics.

## The Deykerhoff-Pijnacker Hordijk trust

The source records that the governor of Atjeh, [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]], and the governor-general [[Pijnacker Hordijk|Pijnacker Hordijk]] "meenden de raadgevingen van Snouck Hurgronje niet te moeten volgen. Tegen diens waarschuwing in schonken zij hun vertrouwen aan Teungku Um, die officieel begenadigd werd, een legioen van 250 man op onze kosten mocht onderhouden en later nog 1000 geweren kreeg, veel ammunitie en geld" (believed they should not follow Snouck Hurgronje's advice. Against his warning they placed their trust in Teungku Um, who was officially granted clemency, permitted to maintain a legion of 250 men at our expense, and later received a further 1,000 rifles, much ammunition and money). The [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] warning that [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]] and [[Pijnacker Hordijk|Pijnacker Hordijk]] overrode is the [[1892]] advice to ignore the sultan's party and to strike the enemy sensibly rather than to negotiate with him.

## The 1896 defection

The source records: "Korte tijd daarna (1896) wierp Teungku Um het masker af en hadden onze soldaten te vechten tegen met modern geweren bewapende Atjehers" (A short time later (1896) Teungku Um threw off the mask, and our soldiers had to fight against Atjehnese armed with modern rifles). The [[1896]] defection is, in the source's narrative, the consequence of the [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]]-[[Pijnacker Hordijk|Pijnacker Hordijk]] decision to arm and subsidise a warlord against the explicit advice of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]].

## The 1880 antecedent

[[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]] had first appeared in the source's narrative in the [[1880]] context as one of several bandit-chiefs whose attacks resumed after the false dawn of that year: "Het duurde niet lang, of de aanvallen van bendehoofden, o.a. van de later bekend geworden Teungku Um begonnen weer" (It did not take long before the attacks of bandit-chiefs, among others the later-famous Teungku Um, began again). The [[1880]] antecedent — a decade and a half before the [[1896]] defection — establishes that [[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]] was a known quantity to the Dutch when [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]] and [[Pijnacker Hordijk|Pijnacker Hordijk]] chose to arm and subsidise him.

## The Van der Wijck reversal

The governor-general [[Van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] (1893-99) "begreep, dat Snouck Hurgronje's adviezen gevolgd moesten worden" (understood that Snouck Hurgronje's advice had to be followed). The [[J. B. van Heutsz|overste Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] "had dit begrepen" (had understood this) too. In [[1896]] — the year of the [[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]] defection — [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was charged with the direction of military operations in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. The [[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]] defection and the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] appointment together mark the [[1896]] turning-point of the war.

## Significance

[[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]]'s [[1896]] defection is, in the source's narrative, the consequence of the Dutch decision to arm and subsidise an Atjehnese warlord against the explicit advice of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]. The defection — together with the contemporaneous [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] appointment — marks the [[1896]] turning-point of the [[Atjehoorlog|Atjehoorlog]].

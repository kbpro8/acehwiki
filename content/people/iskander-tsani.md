**Sultan Iskander Tsani** (also spelled *Iskandar Thani*; in Acehnese tradition *Iskandar Thaib*) was Sultan of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the late 1630s and early 1640s, of Pahang origin, whose death in [[1641]] triggered the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Dutch embassy of Pieter Soury]] and the accession of his widow as the first of the ruling [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultanas of Atjeh]]. His reign was brief but commercially consequential: he was the *besteller* (commissioning buyer) of a parcel of jewels that the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] shipped to Atjeh in [[1641]] with [[Pieter Soury|Soury]] — a consignment whose difficult sale and ultimate return to Holland left the Company with what Godee Molsbergen calls "een leelijken schadepost" (an ugly loss).

## Origin

Iskander Tsani was, in Godee Molsbergen's account, "een Pahangschen prins" — a prince of [[Pahang]] on the Malay Peninsula — who had come to Atjeh by the well-trodden path of captured or hostage Malay royal kin moving through the Atjeh court. The Atjeh sultans had long interfered in Malay peninsula politics, particularly against Portuguese [[Malakka]]; Pahang princes had been part of that political web since the time of [[Iskander Muda]].

## The 1641 orders

The Sultan had placed an outstanding order with the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] for jewels; *Justus Schouten* had been in Atjeh the year before to receive it. The stones were cut to *[[Atjehsche mode|Atjehsche mode]]* — the Atjeh-style cut — and set in Holland to the Sultan's design. As Godee Molsbergen notes, this presented a problem: "dit slijpsel en deze zetting in andere Indische kwartieren niet gewild waren" — this cut and setting were not wanted in any other quarter of the Indies — so the consignment had to be placed in Atjeh or the Company would have "een strop" (a noose, i.e. a loss) on its hands. The order was still unfilled when the Sultan died.

## Death and silence

The Sultan's death in [[1641]] produced a striking silence at the Atjeh court. When the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury embassy]] was being entertained at the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]]'s *lusthof* (pleasure-garden) on [[14 July]] [[1641]], after songs had been sung in honour of [[Iskander Muda]] — "den afstammeling, naar men geloofde, van Alexander den Grooten" — the late Sultan was passed over in total silence: "Over den pas overleden Sultan, een Pahangschen prins, sprak men nooit en deed alsof hij nooit aan de regeering was geweest" — One never spoke of the recently-deceased Sultan, a Pahang prince; one acted as if he had never reigned.

The dynamics of this silence — typical of the Atjeh court's treatment of unpopular or short-lived rulers — left the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] with a diplomatic and commercial problem: the Company could not forget the *besteller* of the jewels, [[Iskander Tsani]], as easily as the Atjeh nobles forgot their own Sultan.

## The unfinished jewel order and Soury's negotiations

The Sultana who succeeded him was not liable for the order placed by her late husband; the Atjeh treasury would not authorise the 15,000 *[[Tayl|tayl]]* (= 5,000 guilders) it would have cost. To get the *Rijksgrooten* to permit the purchase required "heel wat bezoeken" — many a visit — and "menig geschenk" — many a gift — delivered by *Secretaris* [[Jan van Riebeeck|Jan van Riebeeck]] to their houses. After long haggling a price was agreed for the greater part of the parcel; for the future, the import of jewels by the Company was forbidden in Atjeh. In later years the Bewindhebbers (Directors) again tried to sell jewels in Atjeh, in vain; the stones had to be sent back to Holland and the whole order produced an ugly loss.

## Marriage and succession

Iskander Tsani's most lasting historical effect was the widow he left behind. She — at about forty years of age — wished to take a Dutchman as prince-consort, a proposal over which both the *Rijksgrooten* and the High Government at Batavia vetoed. The *Rijksgrooten* chose her as Sultan Iskander Tsani's successor: she ruled in her own right under the title *Sultan* (Godee Molsbergen insists, "Sultane beteekent slechts eerste vrouw van een Sultan" — *Sultana* only means first wife of a Sultan; the queens regnant bore the title *Sultan* in their own right). Her accession opened the long era of female rule in seventeenth-century Atjeh.

## Significance

Iskander Tsani is significant in [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history as the last of the warrior-sultan line and the hinge on which the sultanate turned from the masculine polity of [[Iskander Muda]] to the female rule of the next four decades. His outstanding jewel-order with the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] is also a small but vivid instance of how a sultan's personal taste — the *Atjehsche mode* cut — could shape Company commerce, and of how the death of a single prince could leave a European multinational holding an unsellable consignment and a diplomatic problem.

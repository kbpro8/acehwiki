**Toekoe Oemar** (modern Acehnese *Teuku Umar*) was an [[Achehnese|Atjehnese]] military leader of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] who, in the years [[1893]]–[[1896]], entered into an alliance with the Dutch — serving as their *bondgenoot* (ally) against his own countrymen — and then, in [[1896]], defected back to the Atjehnese side, where he resumed the leadership of the resistance until he was killed in action at [[Melaboeh|Melaboeh]]. His defection and death mark the most consequential single trajectory of the late phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], and they precipitated the appointment of [[General Vetter|General Vetter]] with *onbeperkte volmacht* (unlimited authority) and the eventual subjugation of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

Toekoe Oemar also appears in the Dutch marine records as a local Acehnese figure associated with the **1896** expeditionary activities in [[Atjeh]]. The source lists "Toekoe Oemar, 1896" among the operations, suggesting that Oemar was either a local leader who negotiated with the Dutch or a participant in a specific action that year.

## The Dutch alliance (1893–1896)

The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] school-history textbook of F. Roorda records the alliance in a single line: "*1893-'96. Toekoe-Oemar werd onze bondgenoot*" ("1893–96: Toekoe-Oemar became our ally"). The schoolroom text does not specify the terms of the alliance, the theatre of Toekoe Oemar's cooperation with the Dutch, or the immediate occasion of his defection. The textbook's next clause — "*Hij bleek echter onbetrouwbaar, want in 1896 sloot hij zich weer bij zijn landgenooten aan*" ("He proved, however, untrustworthy, for in 1896 he rejoined his countrymen") — fixes the defection as a moral-rather-than-military fact: the Dutch schoolroom memory of Toekoe Oemar is the memory of a man who broke his word.

## The 1896 defection

Toekoe Oemar's [[1896|1896]] defection from the Dutch alliance to the Atjehnese side triggered the next phase of the war. The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook records the Dutch response in a single sentence: "*Generaal Vetter werd daarna met onbeperkte volmacht naar Atjeh gezonden. Hij behaalde groote voordeelen; doch pas aan van Heutz gelukte het, Groot Atjeh te onderwerpen*" ("General Vetter was thereupon sent to Atjeh with unlimited authority. He gained great advantages; but only to Van Heutsz did it succeed to subdue Groot Atjeh"). The defection thus led, in the schoolroom chronology, directly to the appointment of [[General Vetter|Vetter]] and, ultimately, to the command of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] under which [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] was finally subdued.

## Death at Melaboeh

The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook records Toekoe Oemar's death in a single clause: "*Toekoe Oemar sneuvelde bij Melaboeh*" ("Toekoe Oemar fell at Melaboeh"). The textbook does not specify the date of the engagement or the immediate circumstances of his death. [[Melaboeh|Melaboeh]] — a place on the west coast of Atjeh — is thus fixed in the schoolroom memory as the field of his death.

## Account from the Dutch Marine Corps record

The Dutch marine record of [[1896]] lists "Toekoe Oemar, 1896" among the operations of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]] in Atjeh, suggesting that Oemar was either a local leader who negotiated with the Dutch or a participant in a specific action that year. The marine record thus corroborates, from an independent institutional source, the [[1896]] date of Toekoe Oemar's engagement with the Dutch — the year of his defection recorded in the *Na 1795* schoolroom text. Further details about his role in the marine record are sparse; the entry therefore records the known association while noting the limited information.

## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**Toekoe Oemar** — Teuku Umar ([[1854]]-[[1899]]) — was the Atjehnese war-leader who, after several years of co-operation with the Dutch in [[Groot-Atjeh]], turned against his former patrons in [[1896]] and thereby unhinged the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentratie-stelsel]] of [[1884]]-[[1896]]. His [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] is the immediate trigger of the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-[[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] conquest of Atjeh.

### Co-operation with the Dutch

Before [[1896]] [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar (Teuku Umar)]] was, with [[Teungku Malem]] and others, one of the principal Atjehnese allies of the Dutch in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The *concentratie-stelling* around [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] was sustained in part by alliances of this kind; the Dutch forces, confined to a "Noordoostpolder"-sized area, depended on indigenous auxiliaries to extend their effective reach. The earlier period of co-operation is reflected in the [[1893]] *Submission of Teuku Uma to the Dutch Government* and the so-called *Uma period* of Dutch-Atjehnese relations.

### The 1896 defection

In [[1896]] [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] defected. The immediate consequence was that the *concentratie-stelling* around [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] was "niet meer geheel zeker" — no longer wholly secure. The Dutch-held area in [[Groot-Atjeh]], already comparable in extent to a "Noordoostpolder", shrank further. The parliamentary debate that followed the defection produced the canonical liberal and anti-revolutionary speeches of [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]], [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] and [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] on the failure of pre-[[J. B. van Heutsz|Heutsz]] Atjeh policy.

### The 1898-1904 pursuit

After the defection the resistance leaders did not submit. They withdrew, first to [[Pedirse|Pedir]], and were pursued: first to [[Pedirse|Pedir]], then to the coasts, and finally — in [[1904]] — to the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]] and [[Alasland|Alaslanden]]. The [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]] of [[1904]] was the culmination of this pursuit.

### The Cremer footnote

[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] (lib.) offered, in a footnote preserved by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]], the observation that what had complicated the recovery of [[Groot-Atjeh]] after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|the defection of Toekoe Oemar]] was the fear of the population that the Dutch troops would again withdraw, and that it would then be at the mercy of a figure like [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]]. Once the population was given confidence that the Dutch would not leave again, "hadden wij het spel half gewonnen" — we had half won the game.

### Significance

[[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]]'s [[1896]] defection is the political-military hinge of the Atjehnese war. The post-[[1896]] policy of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — the recovery of [[Groot-Atjeh]], the pursuit of the resistance chiefs to [[Pedirse|Pedir]] and thence to the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]] and [[Alasland|Alaslanden]] — is the direct consequence of the collapse of the pre-[[1896]] alliance system that his defection revealed.

## Significance

Toekoe Oemar's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history lies principally in the documentary fact that his defection from the Dutch alliance in [[1896|1896]] is, in the Dutch schoolroom memory fixed by the [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook, the proximate cause of the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the appointment of [[General Vetter|Vetter]] with unlimited authority, and ultimately the command of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] that subdued [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. His career is the document par excellence of the ambiguous Atjehnese position in the late phase of the war: an Atjehnese leader capable of fighting on both sides, whose ultimate loyalty — fixed in his [[1896|1896]] defection and his [[Melaboeh|Melaboeh]] death — was to the Atjehnese cause. The Dutch schoolroom memory of him — as the man who "proved untrustworthy" — is the inverse of the Atjehnese memory, in which his defection back to his countrymen and his death in their cause make him a national hero.

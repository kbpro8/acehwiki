**Al-Busiri** was the Arab poet **Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Busiri** (d. [[1294]]) who composed the *Burda* (Poem of the Mantle), a celebrated Arabic qaṣīda in praise of the Prophet Muhammad; a sixteenth‑century Malay translation of the work attests to its diffusion into the Malay literary sphere, including Aceh.

## The Burda
The *Burda* (Arabic: البردة) is a qaṣīda that recounts the poet’s miraculous healing after reciting verses in praise of the Prophet. It has been widely copied, commented upon, and translated into numerous languages, among them Malay. The poem’s enduring popularity is reflected in both the Arabic original and its many translations.

## Malay Transmission
The presence of a sixteenth‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* (referenced in the file name) attests to the poem’s popularity in the Malay world. Such translations were often used in Sufi circles and courtly settings across Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula, influencing local devotional literature. The OCR text preserves this translation alongside commentary that explains the theological and poetic significance of the work.

## Connection to Aceh
Aceh, as a major centre of Islamic scholarship in Southeast Asia, adopted the *Burda* into its religious and literary repertoire. Malay translations of the poem would have been known in Acehnese suraus and pesantren, contributing to the shared Islamic literary culture of the archipelago.

## Significance
Al-Busiri’s *Burda* became a cornerstone of devotional literature across the Islamic world, and its Malay transmission illustrates the poem’s role in shaping a pan‑Malay Islamic literary tradition that linked Aceh to broader networks of Sufi learning and Malay-language textual production.

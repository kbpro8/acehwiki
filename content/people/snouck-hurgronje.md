**C. Snouck Hurgronje** was Dutch scholar of Islam, advisor for native affairs, whose spelling of Atjeh place names after Atjeh pronunciation became officially adopted, and author of *Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners* ([[1903]]) and extensive Atjeh work serving as guide for administrators and researchers.

## Works referenced by Broersma

Broersma notes Snouck Hurgronje established spelling of Atjeh place names reflecting Atjeh pronunciation, while Europeans usually followed pronunciation that became current among military, leading to traditional wrong pronunciation deviating from officially accepted correct spelling.

His book *Het Gajo-land en zijn bewoners* ([[1903]]), composed from communications of educated Gajo'ers and officer-administrators, is described as directional rope for administrators and investigators. Beside it, his Gajo-Dutch dictionary where [[Dr. Hazeu]] laid final hand, much more than dictionary.

Broersma also refers to Snouck Hurgronje for position of *banta* in Atjeh landscape comparable with *patih* in principality or regency on Java (deel I p. 96), and for sacred rank of *sajjids* or *sajets* whose word replaced in Arab by *habib* meaning beloved, having religious nobility and great influence, sometimes also in Atjeh.

Broersma cites Snouck Hurgronje's later critique in *Tijdschrift van het Kon. Aardr. Genootschap* [[1924]] afl. 4 regarding administrative period [[1908]]-[[1918]] as part of evidence arbitrary handling of moneys during Swart period.

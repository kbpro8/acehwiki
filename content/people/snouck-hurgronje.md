**Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje** (1857–1936) was a Dutch orientalist and Islamologist, the leading Dutch scholar of Achehnese society and religion at the close of the nineteenth century, and the author of the two-volume *De Atjehers* (Leiden, 1893–1894), cited by [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] in his 1900 article on the [[Acheh treaty of 1819|Raffles–Coombs convention]] as the standard authority on the political and social constitution of the sultanate of Acheh.

## *De Atjehers* (1893–1894)

Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers* — the title means "The Achehnese" — was the product of years of intensive study of the language, religious literature, and political organisation of the sultanate of Acheh, undertaken partly in connection with the Dutch military expedition against Acheh that had begun in 1873. The first volume of the work appeared in 1893, the second in 1894. It rapidly established itself as the indispensable scholarly account of Achehnese society, and was a frequent reference point for Dutch colonial officials and historians for at least a generation.

## Citation in Van der Kemp

Van der Kemp cites *De Atjehers* on his thirteenth page, during his discussion of the political constitution of the sultanate at the time of Raffles's mission in March 1819. The Raffles–Coombs commission, on anchoring before the Achehnese capital, found that "no sultan" was present; authority was exercised, in the words of the Calcutta government's report to London, "in name by the council of the Sagis, but in reality by Panglima Polim aided by his adherents." The Sagis in question were the heads of the three territorial divisions — the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]], the [[XXV Mukims|XXV]], and the [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI]] Mukims — that surrounded the capital; and from the end of the seventeenth century, Van der Kemp notes, the sultanate had been under the tutelage of the heads of these highland districts. [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]] — the title, literally "Lord Elder Brother," borne for many generations by the head of the XXII Mukims — was the chief among the Sagi heads. Van der Kemp's footnote refers the reader to *De Atjehers*, volume I, pages 2 and 139, for the institutional background of these arrangements.

## Snouck Hurgronje and the Dutch Colonial Project

Snouck Hurgronje's scholarly work on Acheh was carried on in close conjunction with the Dutch colonial administration. His advice to the colonial government on the conduct of the Acheh War, and his conviction that the resistance of the Achehnese was religiously rather than politically motivated, exercised a profound influence on Dutch policy in the closing phase of the war. Van der Kemp, writing in 1900, cites *De Atjehers* as a scholarly authority, but the broader context of Snouck Hurgronje's relationship to the colonial project is one that the reader of the 1900 article is expected to take for granted. Together with [[Pieter Johannes Veth|Professor Veth]] and [[J. H. P. E. Kniphorst|Kniphorst]], Snouck Hurgronje represents, in Van der Kemp's apparatus, the tradition of Dutch scholarly engagement with Acheh that stood behind the official colonial policy of his day.

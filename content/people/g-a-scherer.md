**G. A. Scherer** was a Dutch official who as Resident of [[Atjeh]] during the [[Civil government period|civil government period]] pleaded for the maintenance and extension of the civil administration, and later — after the scheepvaartregeling had been set in the place of the bankrupt blockade — expounded the new system in a lecture before the Indisch Genootschap (Indies Society), *[[Hoe moet Atjeh gepacificeerd worden? (Scherer)|Hoe moet Atjeh gepacificeerd worden?]]* (How must Atjeh be pacified?), a text that [[W. A. Coblijn|Coblijn]] made one of the principal targets of his pamphlet *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]* and that majoor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] quoted for its four-point programme in his brochure *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*.

## The favourable state after the XXII and XXVI Moekims

In his lecture Scherer described, with the optimism that Coblijn set out to refute, the favourable condition of Atjeh after the occupation of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII Moekims]] and the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] in the campaigns of [[1879]]: officials could travel alone in the IVth and VIth Moekims and along the [[Kroëng Raba|Kroeng Raba]]; vaccination had been accepted by the population of [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]]; and new kampongs were being laid out by the inhabitants on points designated by the government. For Coblijn the *gebeurtenissen van 30 Juni 1880 in Samalanga* — the [[Samalanga ambuscade (30 June 1880)|surprise of the Samalanga garrison's military walk]] and the ensuing triple échec of [[Batoe Iliq Expedition (July 1880)|Batoe Iliq]] — proved how lamentably the Resident had been deceived in this optimistic supposition.

## Civil government and the old adage

Scherer argued, in the formula that Coblijn contested at length, that it did not stand to reason that every able officer of the [[Indische Leger|Indies army]] was also an able administrator — the adage by which the demand for a civil functionary at the head of the Atjeh government had been defended since [[1881]]. Coblijn replied with the roll of the Dutch soldier-administrators: [[Andreas Victor Michiels|Michiels]], [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]], [[Colonel C. A. de Brauw|De Brauw]], [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]], Andresen, [[W. E. Kroesen|Kroesen]], [[G. M. Verspyck|Verspyck]], Happé, [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]], [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]], [[Demmenie]], [[Van Teijn]] — and with the example of the British administrators of India, from Clive to the Lawrences, Outram, Edwardes, Lumsden, Davidson, Durand and Nicholson. The question belonged to the controversy of the [[Civil versus military government in Atjeh|civil versus military government of Atjeh]].

## The scheepvaartregeling and the four-point programme

In the same lecture Scherer indicated accurately what was to be understood by his [[Scheepvaartregeling (1892)|scheepvaartregeling]] (shipping regulation), the system that after [[1892]] replaced the blockade of the Atjeh coast; Coblijn quoted it and objected that it was a *sprong in het duister* (a leap in the dark). In the *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* of 1893, Van Heutsz quoted Scherer's programme in four points: 1. the scheepvaartregeling; 2. to side with the weak against the strong; 3. to isolate [[Groot-Atjeh]]; 4. to pacify Groot-Atjeh with or without the Sultan — and refuted the second and third points. Scherer's judgement of the military situation appeared also in the letter of [[Toekoe di Tiroe|Tengkoe di Tirou]] that had come into his hands, in which the chief said it had been shown that the Atjehers could not stand against the Dutch in the open field: *de tijger niet naar de weide komt om de grazende runderen aan te vallen* (the tiger does not come to the meadow to attack the grazing cattle) — a saying Coblijn cited to illustrate how well the enemy had understood the Dutch position.

## The visit to Batoe Iliq

Coblijn related that Scherer, some years after the expedition of 1880, visited the kampong [[Batoe Iliq]] on [[30 June]], accompanied by a controleur, by the agent Persijn of the Atjeh supplies, and by a health officer; the visit was made under the protection of [[Toekoe Tjibik]], who had broken with the religious party and had burned the houses of the priests at Batoe Iliq. The Resident there saw with his own eyes that the famous fortified kampong stood abandoned, dominated at 150 metres by the adjoining hills — from which side, as the garrison admitted, it would have been taken at once had the assault of Van Lübtow's day been followed up. For Coblijn the visit was the unsolicited official confirmation of his criticism of the [[Batoe Iliq Expedition (July 1880)|Batoe Iliq operations]].

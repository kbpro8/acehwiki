**Sultan Sayyidi al-Mukamnal** — pronounced in Achehnese **Sidi Meukamay**, and identical with the monarch known in Achehnese tradition as **Alaedin al-Qahhar** — was one of the greatest sultans of Acheh. The native chronicles variously give his reign as from [[1530]] to [[1552]] or [[1557]], while others place it between [[1540]] and [[1570]]. He is one of the four monarchs whose names recur on virtually every *chab sikureu'eng* — the [[Sarakata with chab sikureu'eng|ninefold seal]] of the sultans of Acheh.

The ninefold seal, as described by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]], consists of a large central circle bearing the name of the reigning sultan, surrounded by eight smaller circles each containing the name of a celebrated predecessor. The four names that recur on almost every seal are those of the rulers to whom the former prosperity of Acheh and her adat-institutions were generally ascribed: [[Sultan Sayyidi al-Mukamnal|Sidi Meukamay]] (Alaedin al-Qahhar), [[Sultan Meukuta Alam]] (Iskandar Muda, [[1607]]–[[1636]]), [[Sultan Tajul-alam]] (Sapiatodin, the first sultana, [[1639]] or [[1641]]–[[1675]]), and [[Sultan Ahmat Shah]] (the first prince of the present dynasty, [[1723]] or [[1726]]–[[1735]]).

Alaedin al-Qahhar is remembered in Achehnese tradition as a conqueror and consolidator, the second of the great sultans who raised Acheh to its position as a leading Mohommedan power of the Eastern Archipelago. The embassy which he sent to the [[Embassy from Acheh to the Sultan of Turkey|Sultan of Turkey at Stambul]] — bearing pepper as tribute and returning with the celebrated cannon Lada Sichupa — is one of the best-known legends of his reign, and is said to have given rise to the special importance attached by the Achehnese to the [[Maulud (Mo'lot)|feast of the Maulud]]. His name is mentioned in the same breath as Iskandar Muda's, the latter being the prince of old Acheh par excellence.

The chronological details of the reigns of the Achehnese kings are, as Snouck Hurgronje observes, "very variously given in the different native authorities"; the footnote to the description of the ninefold seal records that "some have it that Sidi Meukamay reigned from 1540 to 1570 A.D." This uncertainty need not detain us, since the political importance of Alaedin al-Qahhar lies less in the precise dates of his reign than in the enduring place he holds in the Achehnese royal imagination. His mediation (*afwah*) is invoked in the preamble of the sultans' letters patent alongside [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Meukuta Alam Iskandar Muda]], [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam Sapiatodin]], [[Sultan Ahmat Shah|Alaedin Ahmat Shah]], [[Sultan Mahmut Shah|Alaedin Mahmut Shah]], [[Sultan Alaedin Muhamat Shah|Alaedin Muhamat Shah]], [[Sultan Jauhar Alain Shah|Alaedin Jauhar al-Alam Shah]], and [[Sultan Mansur Shah|Alaedin Manso Shah]].

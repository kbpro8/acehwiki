**De Goeje** — *Professor De Goeje*, full name *Michaël Jan de Goeje* — was the Dutch orientalist, professor at Leiden, who was the teacher of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and the principal scholarly patron of Snouck Hurgronje's early career. On a walk along the Leiden singels, Snouck Hurgronje floated with De Goeje the idea of going to Arabië; De Goeje had then found the idea rather foolish — in those days no orientalist went to the East. De Goeje valued Snouck Hurgronje's presence at the Leiden Indische Instelling as a matter of the highest importance, for the institution had to compete with a similar municipal institution at Delft. When, after a couple of years of teaching, the trek to Arabië became too strong for Snouck Hurgronje, De Goeje's opposition gave way; and when Snouck Hurgronje's Mekka plan succeeded, De Goeje became seriously anxious about his reckless pupil and wrote to him in disapproval, well knowing that it would avail nothing.

## The Teacher

De Goeje was the teacher of Snouck Hurgronje at Leiden, and the principal scholarly patron of his early career. Snouck Hurgronje's theological and Semitic studies at Leiden from [[1874]] — which led to the doctorate on [[24 November]] [[24 November]] [[1880]] on the dissertation "*Het Mekkaansche Feest*" — were conducted under De Goeje's guidance; and it was on a walk along the Leiden singels with De Goeje that Snouck Hurgronje first floated the idea of going to Arabië. De Goeje had then found the idea rather foolish — in those days no orientalist went to the East.

## The Leiden Indische Instelling

What De Goeje found important was the Leiden Indische Instelling — the municipal institution for the training of East Indian officials — which had to compete with a similar institution at Delft. Snouck Hurgronje's presence at the Leiden institute, as the teacher of "*godsdienstige wetten en gewoonten der mohammedanen*" (religious laws and customs of the Mohammedans), was seen by De Goeje as a matter of the highest importance. When, after a couple of years of teaching, the trek to Arabië became too strong for Snouck Hurgronje, the competition between the two institutions was one of the considerations that weighed against the journey.

## The Mekka Anxiety

When Snouck Hurgronje's Mekka plan succeeded — when, after a half-year at Djidda, he was able to enter Mekka on [[21 February]] [[21 February]] [[1885]] as the Muslim convert "Abd al-Gaffār" — De Goeje became seriously anxious about his reckless pupil, and wrote to him in disapproval, well knowing that it would avail nothing, that Snouck would not let himself be stopped.

## Significance

De Goeje is the teacher and principal scholarly patron of Snouck Hurgronje's early career. His opposition to the Arabië journey — and his championship of Snouck Hurgronje's presence at the Leiden Indische Instelling — was one of the principal considerations that weighed against the journey; and his anxiety about the Mekka plan, when it succeeded, is one of the personal notes in the history of Snouck Hurgronje's Mekka journey.

**Andreas Victor Michiels** ([[1797]]–[[1849]]) was a Dutch general who distinguished himself throughout a long military career in the [[Netherlands India|Dutch East Indies]] and was killed in action on [[Bali]] in [[1849]]. He entered military service at the age of seventeen and eventually attained the rank of General, having passed through all intermediate ranks.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — Michiels

The atlas describes him in the context of the two monuments depicted on the same plate: "Andreas Victor Michiels werd in 1797 geboren en trad reeds op 17-jarigen leeftijd in den militairen dienst. Na zich op alle mogelijke manieren onderscheiden te hebben en tot den Generaalsrang te zijn opgeklommen, sneuvelde hij in 1849 op Bali." (Andreas Victor Michiels was born in 1797 and entered military service at the age of 17. After distinguishing himself in every possible way and having been promoted to the rank of General, he fell in battle in 1849 on Bali.)

The atlas further notes that the [[Atjeh Monument]] in the Wilhelminapark at [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], "werd opgericht ter gedachtenis aan de pacificatie van Atjeh" (was erected in memory of the pacification of Atjeh). Michiels himself did not campaign in Atjeh — he died in [[1849]], twenty-four years before the first Dutch expedition to Atjeh in [[1873]] — but the monuments are presented together in the atlas as symbols of Dutch military achievement in the archipelago.

## Monuments

Two monuments are associated with Michiels and the broader military heritage commemorated in the atlas:

1. **The Michiels Monument** (*Het Michielsmonument*) at the Waterlooplein in [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], directly commemorating General Michiels.
2. **The Atjeh Monument** (*Het Atjeh-monument*) in the Wilhelminapark at [[Weltevreden]], erected in memory of the [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]].

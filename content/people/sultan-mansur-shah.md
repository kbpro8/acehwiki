**Sultan Mansur Shah** (also styled **Alaedin Manso Shah**, or simply **Manso Shah**) was an Achehnese sultan who reigned from [[1838]] to [[1870]]. He was the father of [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Sultan Alaedin Muhamad Daud Shah Juhan]], the last sultan recognised by the Achehnese. His name appears on the *chab sikureu'eng* — the [[Sarakata with chab sikureu'eng|ninefold seal]] of the sultans — and his mediation (*afwah*) is invoked in the preamble of sultans' letters patent (*sarakata*).

The ninefold seal, as [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] explains, consists of a large central circle bearing the name of the reigning sultan, surrounded by eight smaller circles each containing the name of a celebrated predecessor. The choice of the eight names rests with the reigning sultan, though the names of the great sultans of the past and of the immediate predecessors of the reigning king are never omitted. The four names that recur on virtually every seal are [[Sultan Sayyidi al-Mukamnal]] (Alaedin al-Qahhar), [[Sultan Meukuta Alam]] (Iskandar Muda), [[Sultan Tajul-alam]] (Sapiatodin), and [[Sultan Ahmat Shah]].

Mansur Shah's appearance among the eight namesakes on his son's seal reflects his position as the immediate predecessor of the pretender-sultan. The preamble of the letters patent granted by Daud Shah Juhan to [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] on 4 Rabi' al-Awwal 1307 H ([[1889]]) explicitly invokes the mediation of "His late Majesty Alaedin Manso Shah", alongside [[Sultan Meukuta Alam]], [[Sultan Tajul-alam]], [[Sultan Ahmat Shah]], [[Sultan Mahmut Shah]], [[Sultan Alaedin Muhamat Shah]], and [[Sultan Jauhar Alain Shah]].

Mansur Shah's reign fell entirely within the decadent period of the sultanate. He is apparently identical with the [[Ibrahim Manso Shah]] named elsewhere in the text as reigning [[1858]]–[[1870]], during whose reign the holder of the [[XXVI Mukims]] panglimaship died. The sultan's authority was then so weak that he could not enforce his chosen candidate ([[Teuku Muda Lampaseh]]) against the wishes of the people of the district, and the eventual compromise produced the [[Disputed succession to the panglimaship of the XXVI Mukims|double panglimaship]] of Teuku Lampaseh and [[Teuku Nya' Banta|Teuku Lamreueng]]. His son was elected sultan in [[1879]] as Sultan Alaedin Muhamad Daud Shah Juhan and ruled — at least nominally — from the sultan's fortress at [[Keumala]] until his [[Submission of Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|submission to the Dutch]].

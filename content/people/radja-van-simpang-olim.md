**The Radja of Simpang Olim** was an Acehnese local ruler whose personal jewellery and regalia were discovered in his abandoned house in [[1877]] and acquired for the collection of the [[Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen|Batavian Society's]] treasure room in [[Koeta Radja|Batavia]].

## Abandonment and discovery

The 1928 treasure-room guide describes the circumstances plainly: the items were *"sieraden van den gewezen radja van Simpang Olim, aangetroffen in diens verlaten woning in 1877"* — "jewellery of the former radja of Simpang Olim, found in his abandoned dwelling in 1877." The word *gewezen* (former) and *verlaten* (abandoned) together indicate that the radja had fled or been driven from his residence, and that by the time the Dutch or their Acehnese allies entered the house, it was already empty of its occupant.

The year [[1877]] falls within the first phase of the [[Aceh War]], which had begun in [[1873]] with the Dutch declaration of war and first assault on the palace at [[Koeta Radja|Kutaraja]]. By 1877, Dutch military expeditions were penetrating into the interior of [[Great Acheh|Greater Atjeh]], and many local rulers faced the choice between submission to Dutch authority and flight. The abandonment of the radja's house at Simpang Olim likely reflects the disruptive impact of the war on the traditional Acehnese political order, as [[Uleebalang|uleebalangs]] and lesser radjas were either displaced by military operations or chose to withdraw rather than submit.

## The jewellery assemblage

The radja's personal ornaments, catalogued in Cabinet 3 (Atjeh), items 2–42, form a remarkably comprehensive assemblage of Acehnese personal adornment. They include:

- A **dagger** (*dolk*) with black coral grip decorated with [[Spinsbek|spinsbek]] work
- A **small cap** (*mutsje*) of multicoloured yarn with a golden, star-shaped ornament on the crown
- **Rings** of gold, silver, and [[Spinsbek|spinsbek]] work, some set with stones, some plain
- **Belts** of gold chain with die-shaped clasps, and belly bands (*buikband*) of twisted silver cords
- **Boxes** of chased, gilt silver and of gold and [[Spinsbek|spinsbek]], for betel lime (*kalkdoosje*) and other purposes
- A remarkable pair of **gold spectacles with extendable parts** (*gouden bril met uitschuifbare verlengstukken*)
- An **ankle ring** of [[Spinsbek|spinsbek]], multifaceted
- **Amulets** and amulet chains, including silver chains with black stones in silver wire
- A **crown ornament for a kopiah** (*koepiah*), of gold with enamel
- A **hair pin** in the form of a kopiah crown ornament, described as a rare model (*zeldzaam model*)
- **Bracelets** and **ankle rings** in various styles
- A **silver ornament for the end of a chain or rope** used as a support for climbing house stairs, reflecting the Acehnese tradition of elevated stilt houses
- **Children's jewellery**, including a bracelet of chains with a gold ornament

## Significance

The collection is significant both as a record of Acehnese courtly personal adornment and as evidence of the upheavals of the early [[Aceh War]]. The quality and variety of the pieces suggest that even a relatively minor radja maintained a substantial collection of precious-metal jewellery, underscoring the importance of personal ornament as a marker of status and authority in pre-colonial and early-colonial Acehnese society. The gold spectacles, in particular, are an unusual and intriguing item, suggesting either actual use or a purely ceremonial function as a marker of learning and authority.

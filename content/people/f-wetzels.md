**F. Wetzels** was a captain of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]] Maréchaussee who commanded the IIIde divisie and, in that capacity, the **second stelling** of [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]]'s three-stelling defence of the [[Bireuën|Bireuën]]–[[Takengon|Takengon]] road in mid-March [[1942]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Wetzels's stelling — held by five brigades drawn from the IIIde and Vde divisie — was the second of the three positions at which the Japanese advance was delayed.

## The second stelling

The second stelling was located approximately 25 km behind the [[First stelling south of Bireuën (15 March 1942)|first stelling]] held by Lt [[W. van Pesch|Van Pesch]]. It was held by a total of five brigades from the IIIde and Vde divisie, under Wetzels as commander of the IIIde divisie. The stelling's orders were "'s vijands nadering door guerrilla-acties te vertragen" — to delay the Japanese advance through guerrilla actions rather than to fight a set-piece defence. The third stelling, halfway between Bireuën and Takengon, was held under Captain [[L. A. Boucherie|Boucherie]].

## Operations

The staff history gives few operational details of the second stelling, noting only that Wetzels "agerende troepen werden tot 19 Maart hinderlagen langs de weg gelegd." When [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]] ordered the capitulation of Takengon on [[19 March]] [[19 March 1942|19 March 1942]], "werden deze troepen krijgsgevangen gemaakt" — that is, the troops operating behind Wetzels's stelling were captured along with the rest of the Takengon garrison.

## Significance

Wetzels's command is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the middle element of the three-stelling defence — and the one whose *guerrilla-acties* are most directly continuous with the pre-war doctrine of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] in Atjeh. The staff history's emphasis on the doctrinal continuity — "zoals tevoren in vredestijd reeds was beoefend" — frames Wetzels's stelling as the operational expression of the Maréchaussee's *kleine oorlog* tradition, set against the *gevechtsopstelling* of Van Pesch's first stelling.

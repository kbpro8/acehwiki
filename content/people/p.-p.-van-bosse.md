**P. P. van Bosse** — Pieter Philip van Bosse ([[1809]]-[[1879]]) — was a Dutch liberal politician who served two terms as minister of Colonies ([[1872]]-[[1875]]; [[1879]]) and is the minister under whose signature the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] was concluded with [[Great Britain|England]]. His "zeer geruststellende verklaringen" — very reassuring statements — to the [[Tweede Kamer]] on the consequences of the treaty would prove, within two years, to have been gravely mistaken.

## The Sumatra-tractaat

The [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] between the [[Netherlands]] and [[Great Britain|England]] provided that the whole of [[Sumatra]] — including the independent [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate — would fall within the Dutch sphere of influence. In exchange the [[Netherlands]] conceded to British subjects the same commercial rights throughout Sumatra as those enjoyed by Dutch subjects. Minister [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]] delivered to the [[Tweede Kamer]] "zeer geruststellende verklaringen" — very reassuring statements — about the consequences of the instrument he had just signed.

## The 1897 retrospective

More than two decades later, in the [[1897]] debate on credits for [[Nieuw Guinea]], [[W. J. van Welderen Rengers|Van Welderen Rengers]] (lib.) recalled the [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]] assurances of [[1871]] as the type-specimen of the danger of "the first step" in colonial affairs. In [[1873]] — two years after the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] — the [[Netherlands]] declared war on the [[Sultan of Acheh|sultan of Atjeh]]. The [[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]] assurances of [[1871]] had been the original "geruststellende verklaring" against which every subsequent ministerial reassurance on colonial extensions was thereafter measured.

## Significance

[[P. P. van Bosse|Van Bosse]]'s signature of the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] is the diplomatic hinge on which the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] turns. His reassurances to the [[Tweede Kamer]] are, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] narrative, the type-specimen of ministerial over-confidence in colonial affairs.

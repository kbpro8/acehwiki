**Gudjarāt** — in English **Gujarat** — is the region of north-western India whose coastal towns were, in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, the principal nodes of the Indian Ocean network that linked the religious scholarship of India with the Malay-Indonesian world. Gujarat is the region of origin of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and of his uncle [[Muhammad Djailani Hamid]]; the region of the religious activity of [[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]] and [[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs]]; and the immediate source of the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] silsila that was carried to [[Acheh]] and [[Makassar]] by Raniri and [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]].

## The coastal towns and the Indian Ocean network

The coastal towns of Gujarat in this period had a very international population. Ships regularly sailed thence to the Strait of Malaka; among the mercenary troops of the sixteenth century were "Javanese" — that is, in this context, Indonesians. The Malay language was certainly not unfamiliar in the harbour towns, and the family of Raniri — Indian Arabs of mixed blood, who maintained contact with the motherland by correspondence and study-journeys, and who stood in relation with Indonesia — had its centre in the coastal towns of Gujarat. To learn Malay, as [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] records, Raniri "had no need of distant travel: there were Malays enough in Gujarat, and his uncle [[Muhammad Djailani Hamid]] will have spoken that language too."

## The religious families of Gujarat

The Indian-Arab religious families of mixed blood — the al-Aydārūs family, the family of [[Muhammad al-Addars|al-ʿAddārs]], and the family of Raniri himself — formed the network that carried the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago. The family of [[Abd al-Qadir al-Aidaros|ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs]] had come from South Arabia to India in [[1551]]; the mother of ʿAbd al-Qādir was an Indian. [[Muhammad al-Addars|Al-ʿAddārs]] was born at [[Tarim]] in [[1561]] and came to Gujarat at the age of nineteen, succeeding his famous grandfather as religious teacher first at [[Ahmadabad]] and later at [[Surat]]. The family of Raniri — descended, like the al-Aydārūs and al-ʿAddārs families, from Arabs of mixed blood — was centred at [[Ranir]] near Surat.

## The first Gujarati scholar in Acheh

The first recorded link of the family of Raniri with [[Acheh]] is the visit of his uncle [[Muhammad Djailani Hamid]] between [[1580]] and [[1583]]. The visit established the precedent by which a Gujarati scholar of Arab descent could seek his fortune at the Achehnese court, and was the immediate experience on which Raniri himself drew, more than half a century later, when he left for Acheh in [[1637]].

## Significance

Gujarat is the region of origin of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and the immediate source of the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] silsila that was carried to [[Acheh]] and [[Makassar]] by Raniri and [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]]. The Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network — centred in the coastal towns of Gujarat, with nodes at [[Surat]], [[Ahmadabad]], [[Bijapur|Bījāpūr]], and [[Bilgram|Bilgrām]] — was the principal channel by which the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, and the immediate background of the orthodox reaction of Acheh against the [[Wujudiyyah]] of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] and [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]].

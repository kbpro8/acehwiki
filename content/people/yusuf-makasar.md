**Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar** — in Dutch scholarship **Sjaich Jusul Makasar** — was a Makassarese Sufi who in [[1644]] left [[Makassar|Celebes]] for [[Mecca]], where he became — like [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] before him — a disciple of [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] in the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]]. He is named by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises as one of the two principal Malay-Indonesian Sufis of the seventeenth century who were formed in the Gujarati-Ḥadramī network of the Rifāʿiyya; he also numbers Raniri himself among his teachers.

## The journey to Mecca

In [[1644]] — the very year in which [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] completed the [[Sirat al-Mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] and returned from [[Acheh]] to [[Ranir]] — Shaikh Yūsuf left [[Makassar|Celebes]] for [[Mecca]]. The journey brought him, like Raniri before him, to the discipleship of [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] — the Ḥadramī-Indian shaikh of the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] who lived chiefly at [[Bijapur|Bījāpūr]] and died in [[1656]] at [[Bilgram|Bilgrām]]. Yūsuf al-Makassar also numbers Raniri himself among his teachers — a remarkable fact, since it implies either that the two met during Raniri's Achehnese period (which would require Yūsuf to have passed through Acheh on his way to Mecca in 1644) or that they met after Raniri's return to [[Ranir]] in 1644, possibly during Yūsuf's transit through India on his way to or from Mecca.

## The Ḥadramī-Indian-Malay-Indonesian network

Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar belongs, with [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]], to the small group of Malay-Indonesian Sufis of the seventeenth century who were formed in the Ḥadramī-Indian network of the Rifāʿiyya through the discipleship of [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Bā Shaybān]] and [[Muhammad al-Addars|al-ʿAddārs]]. This network was the principal vehicle by which the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago in the seventeenth century; and the fact that two of its principal Malay-Indonesian disciples — Raniri in [[Acheh]] and Yūsuf in [[Makassar]] — were to play leading roles in the religious and political history of their respective sultanates is a measure of the network's importance for the Islamic history of the region.

## Significance

Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar is, with [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]], one of the two principal Malay-Indonesian disciples of [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] in the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]]. His journey to [[Mecca]] in [[1644]] — the year of Raniri's return from [[Acheh]] to [[Ranir]] — is one of the principal chronological coincidences in the religious history of the Malay-Indonesian world in the seventeenth century, and his numbering of Raniri among his teachers establishes a direct link between the orthodox reaction of Acheh and the religious history of Makassar in the second half of the seventeenth century.

**Jacob van Heemskerck** (1567-1607) was a Dutch navigator and vice-admiraal who, as vice-admiraal of the fleet of [[Jacob van Neck|Jacob van Neck]]'s first voyage, conducted the first Dutch visit to the [[Banda-eilanden|Banda islands]] in March [[1599]], captured a Portuguese galley at the mouth of the river of [[Djohor|Djohor]] in [[1603]] after a fight in which the 750 Portuguese crew were granted their lives, and concluded the formal Dutch-Djohor commercial treaty of [[3 April]] [[1603]] with the sultan of [[Djohor|Djohor]] — the treaty that inaugurated the Dutch-Djohor military alliance of [[1603]]-[[1613]]. He is, after the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers]] and [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]], the principal Dutch commander of the early Atjeh-Djohor period.

## The first Dutch visit to Banda (March 1599)

Van Heemskerck left [[Ambon|Ambon]] on [[11 March]] [[1599]] with two ships of the [[Jacob van Neck|Van Neck]] fleet, anchored off the north coast of [[Banda-eilanden|Great Banda]] on [[15 March]] [[1599]], and concluded a temporary commercial treaty with the **sabander** of **Ortatan** on [[18 March]] [[1599]]. The Dutch were assigned a house on shore for their merchandise — the first Dutch factory on Banda. Van Heemskerck left two factors and eighteen men at [[Lontor|Lontor]] and [[Neira|Neira]] with a cargo worth over ƒ 38,000 in money and goods — the principal instance of the early Dutch trust in the Bandanese before the conflicts of the [[1600s]]. The factors were **Adriaen van Veen** and **Augustijn Stalpaert van der Wiele**, the latter of whom later wrote an important memorandum on the Indies trade.

## The Djohor expedition (1602-1603)

In July [[1602]] Van Heemskerck, then vice-admiraal of the fleet of [[Wolfert Harmensz.|Wolfert Harmensz.]], set out from **Djaratan** for [[Djohor|Djohor]], bearing a letter and gift for the sultan. Driven by current and contrary wind to [[Patani|Patani]], he received the visit of the brother of the sultan of Djohor, then regent of [[Siak|Siak]] on Sumatra, who asked his help against the Portuguese. From mid-November [[1602]] to end-February [[1603]] Van Heemskerck cruised the vicinity of **Timoean** island to intercept the Portuguese galley from Macao. When one of the Macao galleys had already reached the Strait of Malaka, he sailed on the sultan of Djohor's invitation to the river, where a second galley lay at anchor. The galley hoisted sail and, after a fight, surrendered on condition that the 750 Portuguese crew be granted their lives.

Van Heemskerck kept his word. The prisoners were allowed to depart unhindered for [[Malaka|Malaka]], and Van Heemskerck received thank-you letters from the Malaka commandant **Fernao d'Albuquerque**, the Malaka council, and the captain of the captured galley, **Sebastiao Serrao**. The sultan of Djohor received Van Heemskerck "met de grootste welwillendheid", and on [[3 April]] [[1603]] Van Heemskerck sailed for [[Bantam|Bantam]] with the captured galley, leaving the factor **Jacob Buys** with assistants and merchandise at the sultan's court. The sultan sent his own envoy with Van Heemskerck to [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]].

## The commercial treaty and the Dutch-Djohor alliance

The treaty of [[3 April]] [[1603]] inaugurated the Dutch-Djohor military alliance that would frame Dutch policy in the Strait of Malaka until the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack on Djohor of 1613]]. The alliance was confirmed by the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|first and second accords of Matelieff of 17 May and 23 September 1606]] and was the strategic framework within which the Dutch attempted to take Malaka from the Portuguese. Van Heemskerck's courtesy in releasing the 750 Portuguese prisoners — and the sultan's evident satisfaction with it — established the diplomatic standard that subsequent Dutch commanders at Djohor were expected to meet.

## The capture of the Portuguese galley in the Strait of Malaka

The capture of the Portuguese galley at the mouth of the river of Djohor in [[1603]] was the first major Dutch naval prize in the Strait of Malaka and the proximate cause of the Dutch-Djohor alliance. Van Heemskerck's conduct of the affair — the conditional surrender, the courteous release of the prisoners, the formal exchange of letters with the Malaka authorities — became a model for later Dutch-Portuguese naval engagements in the Strait. The strategic effect was to confirm the sultan of Djohor's preference for the Dutch over the Portuguese and to lay the groundwork for the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]].

## Significance

Jacob van Heemskerck is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch commander of the early Atjeh-Djohor period and the principal architect of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance of [[1603]]-[[1613]]. The first Dutch visit to [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] in March [[1599]] established the early Dutch trust in the Bandanese that the conflicts of the [[1600s]] would destroy; the treaty of [[3 April]] [[1603]] with the sultan of [[Djohor|Djohor]] inaugurated the Dutch-Djohor military alliance that the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]] would exploit and the [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|Verhoeff expedition of 1608]] would fail to sustain. Van Heemskerck's courtesy in releasing the Portuguese prisoners — and the sultan's satisfaction with it — established the diplomatic standard that subsequent Dutch commanders at Djohor were expected to meet, and his conduct of the affair was the principal instance of the early Dutch chivalric code in naval warfare in the East Indies. He died in battle against the Spanish at the [[Battle of Gibraltar|Battle of Gibraltar]] in [[1607]] — a death that, in the Dutch Republic's naval mythology, sealed his reputation as one of the great Dutch admirals of the early seventeenth century.

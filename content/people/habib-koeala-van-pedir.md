**Habib Koeala van Pedir** — also recorded under the personal name **Said Anajatoellah** — was an Afghan adventurer and [[Habib (concept)|Habib]] (descendant of the Prophet Muhammad) from the north-coast residency of [[Pedir]] whose death during the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lam-Krak Moekims on 9–10 June]] [[1896]] is recorded in A. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* as one of the most significant Achehnese losses of that operation. Kruisheer describes him as an "Afghan adventurer" who "held considerable standing in the [[Pedir]] area" — a description which positions him as one of the Central/South Asian Muslim fighters who came to [[Aceh]] in the closing years of the nineteenth century to participate in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and whose religious authority (as a Habib) and foreign origin gave them a distinctive role within the Achehnese coalition. His presence in the [[Lamkrak|Lam-Krak]] mukims during the June [[1896]] Dutch *tuchtiging* suggests that he had moved south from [[Pedir]] to participate in the consolidated Achehnese defence of the western mukims following [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]].

## The Afghan Habib and Pedir Standing

The identification of Habib Koeala as an "Afghan adventurer" is unusual in *ATJEH '96*, where the other Habibs — [[Habib Lhong]] (a Kling), [[Habib Samalanga]], [[Habib Lengkong]] — are not given a specific national origin beyond the generic Hadhrami/Arab descent implied by the title. The explicit Afghan identification suggests that Koeala was a Pathan or other Central Asian Muslim who had travelled to Aceh to participate in the resistance, and his presence in the [[Pedir]] area indicates that the north-coast residency had become a centre of foreign Muslim participation in the Aceh War. Kruisheer's observation that Koeala "held considerable standing in the Pedir area" suggests that his religious authority as a Habib had been acknowledged by the local Achehnese population, and that his death would have been felt as a significant loss in the Pedir network. The choice of the [[Lamkrak|Lam-Krak]] mukims as his operational theatre is also significant: the [[Lamkrak|Lam-Krak]] mukims — the four "eigentliche" Lam-Krak mukims of [[Lamkrak|Lam-Krak]], [[Lam-Lheuë]], [[Ateuh]] and [[Kroeng Mak]], together with the adjacent [[Ana Batee]], [[Sibreh]] and [[Ana Glé]] — had been the target of the cancelled [[30 March]] [[1896]] Van Vliet operation which Umar's defection had aborted, and their [[9 June|9]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] *tuchtiging* was therefore both a delayed realisation of the original March plan and a direct assault on one of the principal concentrations of foreign Muslim fighters in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Death in the Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak, 9–10 June 1896

The [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lam-Krak Moekims on 9–10 June]] [[1896]] was one of the largest Dutch operations of the first semester of [[1896]], involving two colonnes — the 1e Colonne under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] (with the [[3e Bataljon]], [[6e Bataljon]], [[7e Bataljon]], a *bergbatterij*, the *genietroepen* and 12 brigades *maréchaussée* under [[Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland|Kapitein Graafland]]) and the 2e Colonne under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] (with the [[9e Bataljon]], [[12e Bataljon]] and [[14e Bataljon]]) — under the overall command of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]]. The operation included the [[Battle of Ateuh Raja / Lam-Krak / Lam-Djamé (9 June 1896)|battle of Ateuh Raja on 9 June 1896]], in which the 9e Bataljon under [[J. F. Th. Veeren|Lt.-Col. Veeren]] attacked in "one continuous aanval", and the [[Combat at Baha (9 June 1896)|combat at Baha on 9 June 1896]], in which the 3e Bataljon's 3e compagnie and the *maréchaussée* talso the *koeta Baha* with five of its defenders shot trying to flee. Koeala's death occurred during this two-day operation, although the specific engagement in which he was killed is not identified by Kruisheer; the loss of a Habib of his standing was, however, significant enough to be recorded as a named casualty. The burning of the Lam-Krak kampongs on [[10 June]] [[1896]] — by both colonnes, with the 2e Colonne marching "in *gevechtsformatie* in *breed front* through [[Lam-Lheuë]] toward [[Ana Batee]], burning everything in the area" — concluded the operation and confirmed that the Lam-Krak concentration of foreign Muslim fighters had been substantially broken.

## Significance

Habib Koeala van Pedir's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the Afghan Muslim adventurer who, under the religious authority of the Habib title, came to Aceh to participate in the resistance and was killed in one of the largest Dutch operations of the first semester of [[1896]]. His presence in the [[Lamkrak|Lam-Krak]] mukims confirms that the foreign Muslim participation in the Acehnese coalition was concentrated not only in the western IV/VI Moekims (where the Kling [[Habib Lhong]] had been killed at [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]]) but also in the Lam-Krak mukims, where the cancelled March operation was finally executed in June. His death, together with the broader destruction of the Lam-Krak kampongs, marked a significant degradation of the foreign Muslim fighter presence in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

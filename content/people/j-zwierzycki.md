**J. Zwierzycki** was a Dutch mining geologist of the [[Netherlands Indies Geological Survey]] whose [[1922]] compilation of the *geological map 1,000,000, sheet I* — covering [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West coast of Atjeh]] — became the standard cartographic basis for all subsequent colonial-era geological descriptions of northern [[Sumatra]], and whose [[1922]] report on the coal, iron and other mineral localities of Atjeh remains the principal source for [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]) in its treatment of Atjeh. Zwierzycki's name is attached, in the standard scholarly citation form "ZWIERZYCKI, 1922", to nearly every coal locality in the Atjeh list and to the canonical inventory of iron-ore occurrences in the Residency of Atjeh.

## The 1:1,000,000 geological map of Atjeh

The most widely cited of Zwierzycki's Atjeh contributions is the *geol. map 1,000,000, sheet I*, which Van Bemmelen reproduces (in adapted form) as Fig. 28 of *The Geology of Indonesia* under the title "Basin of [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]] (West coast of Atjeh)". The map distinguishes Alluvium, Neogene (Pliocene, Miocene), Paleogene (Oligocene, Eocene), Permo-Carboniferous, andesitic effusiva, andesite, basalt, diabase, gabbro, serpentine and granite, and it carried — at the date of its compilation in [[1922]] — the most complete picture then available of the surface geology of the West-Atjeh coastal plain and its immediate hinterland. Van Bemmelen notes one cartographic correction: the "Losir (Löser)" shown as a volcano on Zwierzycki's map is "not volcano, as has been indicated on this map, see volume I" — a caution against treating the printed sheet as infallible, but also a mark of how thoroughly the map had become the reference frame for discussing Atjeh geology.

## The 1922 inventory of Atjeh coal localities

In the same year Zwierzycki published the inventory of Sumatran coal localities that Van Bemmelen reproduces, with only minor additions, as Table 13 of the coal chapter. For Atjeh specifically, the inventory lists:

- *East coast of Atjeh*: Ajer Djambu Batang (No. 1), Pendens River (No. 2), Mouth Woini Orong (No. 3, "Seams a few inches thick"), Ajer Baturong (No. 4).
- *West coast of Atjeh*: Downstream Kr. Kluet (No. 13, "Very thin seams"), South of Tapatuan (No. 14, "Seam 3 ft thick... 10 places"), NW Krueng Teunom (No. 15, "Seam 2½ ft thick"), Krueng Raja (No. 16, "5 seams of brown coal of 2–9 ft"), Krueng Antjong (No. 17, "3 seams of 2–5 ft"), East of Peureu Moue (No. 18), Near Simpane Peust (No. 19), and a further locality (No. 20).

Each entry is cited in the standard form "ZWIERZYCKI, 1922, p. 37" (or p. 38, p. 160, p. 161, etc.), with the older Rinaud references ([[1885]]) preserved where Zwierzycki was drawing on Rinaud's earlier survey.

## The 1922 iron-ore inventory of Atjeh

Zwierzycki's report is also the principal source for Van Bemmelen's list of Atjeh iron-ore localities. The catalogue in *The Geology of Indonesia* (Fig. 41 and key) lists eight Atjeh localities — [[Kr. Geuenteuet|Kr. Geuenteuet]] (No. 1, cited to Hövig [[1918]] p. 87 and Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp [[1922]] pp. 131–132), [[Gle Mon Ampeuet|Gle Mon Ampeuet]] (No. 2), [[Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)|Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)]] (No. 3), [[Kr. Rigaih|Kr. Rigaih]] (No. 4), [[Alu Talu|Alu Talu]] (No. 5), [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]] (No. 6/7), [[A. Pentuwa Gade|A. Pentuwa Gade]] (No. 8), and [[Kuto Buloh|Kuto Buloh]] (No. 9) — and the great majority are cited, alongside Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp, to Zwierzycki's 1922 report. The same 1922 publication is also the source for the [[1920]] reserve estimate of 2,600,000 tons of 58–64 % Fe ore at Kr. Geuenteuet.

## Other cited works

Van Bemmelen cites Zwierzycki on several other Atjeh and northern-Sumatran topics:

- On the geological map of Tapanuli (Blad VII, [[1922]], p. 49), in connection with gold-bearing quartz-veins along the A. Pendung.
- On the cinnabar occurrence at Sungei Salak in the upper Merangin region of Djambi (ZWIERZYCKI, [[1930]], p. 129).
- On the granodiorite contact at [[Gunung Serot]] on Semangko Bay in the Lampong Districts (Zwierzycki, Exploration to geol. map, Kota Agung, p. 27).
- On the Radjabasa iron-ore deposits (Zwierzycki, Expl. to sheet I of the 1:200,000 geol. map, Telok Betung, p. 28).

The recurrence of Zwierzycki's name across these northern-Sumatran localities suggests a geologist who, in the years around [[1922]]–[[1930]], was effectively the Geological Survey's senior field-mapper for the Barisan Range and adjacent coasts, and whose reports accordingly formed the substrate on which all later colonial mineralogical work on Atjeh was built.

## Significance

Zwierzycki is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because his [[1922]] map-sheet and report constitute, in effect, the first systematic geological description of Atjeh as a territorial unit. Earlier reports by Rinaud ([[1885]]) and Verbeek had addressed individual districts or localities; Zwierzycki's map and inventory brought them into a single frame. For the historian of Atjeh's colonial economy — for the study of coal-exploration, iron-ore prospecting, the gold-bearing rivers of the Westkust, the basin of Meulaboh, and the broader mineral geography of the residency — Zwierzycki's 1922 work is the indispensable printed source.

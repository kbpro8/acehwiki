**Levyssohn Norman** was a nineteenth-century Dutch historian of the Netherlands East Indies whose work *Britsche Heerschappij* ("British Rule") — cited in full by [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] as *Levyssohn Norman's „Britsche Heerschappij"* — was one of the standard reference works on the period of British interim administration in the archipelago, that is, the years of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1814)|1814 London Convention]] and the British government of Java under [[Raffles|Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles]] before the colony was returned to the Netherlands in [[1816]].

## Cited by Van der Kemp

Van der Kemp cites Levyssohn Norman in a footnote on page 37 of his article. The footnote concerns the text of the [[Convention of 24 January 1817|convention of 24 January 1817]] between the British and Dutch authorities, by which the two colonial powers sought to settle the outstanding questions left by the restoration of the Dutch possessions after the Napoleonic Wars. Van der Kemp remarks that "one finds this convention on page 170 of Hoek's *Herstel* and on page 334 of Levyssohn Norman's *Britsche Heerschappij*", placing the two works side by side as the printed sources in which the text of the convention is to be consulted.

The same note cross-refers to Van der Kemp's own earlier treatise on the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1814)|1814 treaty]] (in *Bijdragen*, vol. XLVII, 1897, p. 278), so that Levyssohn Norman functions as part of a documentary apparatus alongside [[Hoek]] and Van der Kemp's own earlier publications.

## Significance

Levyssohn Norman's role in the present article is that of a documentary source. His *Britsche Heerschappij* provided later Dutch scholars with the printed texts — and a narrative frame — for the years in which the British exercised direct rule in Java and influence over the surrounding Malay world. These were precisely the years that produced Raffles's abortive [[Acheh treaty of 1819|Acheh agreement]] and the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs commission]], which Van der Kemp's article examines in detail. Without such sourcebooks, the reconstruction of the diplomacy of [[1817]]–[[1819]] would have been far harder. He is mentioned in the same breath as [[Hoek]], [[L. W. G. N. Netscher|Netscher]], [[L. J. P. J. Jeekel|Jeekel]] and [[J. E. de Sturler|De Sturler]], the network of Dutch colonial historians on whose editorial labours Van der Kemp relied.

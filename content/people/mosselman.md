**Mosselman** — *oud-brigadecommandant Mosselman*, *vermaard* in Zentgraff's phrase — was one of the most celebrated Dutch *brigadecommandanten* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussée]] in the last phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. He is the principal narrator of the chapter *"Hoe Tjoet Ali sjahid werd"* — though actually his great *krijgsbedrijf* was the *overval* of [[25 October]] [[1910]] at the Kroeëng Peutoeë, for which he received the *Willemsorde 3e klasse*.

## The 22-25 October 1910 patrol

On [[22 October]] [[1910]] Mosselman was ordered out of [[Lho Soekon]] at 9 p.m. to investigate a *sterke bende* reported in the night of 20–21 October west of Lho Reuhat. With him were 16 maréchaussée's — *telden wij 18 karabijnen* (we counted 18 carbines, including his *panglima* and himself). He took the bende's *sporen* southward through the *Boekit-paja's*, then eastward; *eind oktober 1910, op den derden dag van dien tocht, vlak op de versche bekas zat in een zijriviertje van de Kroeëng Peutoeë* — on the third day of that march, right on the fresh tracks in a side-rivulet of the Kroeëng Peutoeë, *hoe de vluchtelingen ook hadden getracht, de sporen te doen verdwijnen* (howsoever the fugitives had tried to make the tracks disappear). On [[25 October]] [[1910]] his brigade overvalled the bende. *In het geheel liet de vijand 7 dooden, waaronder het bendehoofd en eene vrouw, die aan het gevecht deelnam, in onze handen en werden buit gemaakt: geweer M. '95, Beaumontkarabijn, munitie, blanke wapens, het zegel en de lijfwapens van Tgk. Sjech di Paja Bakong, alias Tgk. Seupot Mata.* — In all the enemy left 7 dead in our hands, including the bende-head and a woman who took part in the fight, and there were captured: a M.95 rifle, a Beaumont carbine, ammunition, blanke wapens, the seal and the personal weapons of [[Tengkoe Sjech di Paja Bakong|Tgk. Sjech di Paja Bakong, alias Tgk. Seupot Mata]]. The woman was [[Tjoet Meuthia]] — *de Parel* — mother of [[T. Radja Sabi|het adelaarsjong]]. The boy, then ten, was in the beek fishing when *"Kaphé datang"* was cried; his *bewaker* snatched him up and ran into the bosch: *"Saja tida lihat lagi iboe"* — I have not seen my mother again.

## The Willemsorde 3e klasse

For this *krijgsbedrijf* Mosselman *kreeg er de Willems-orde 3de klasse voor* — received the Military Willems-Order 3rd class. The official report and his own narrative of the patrol — *„I would, it were evening, Hall, and all well"* (Shakespeare, after the march) — are Zentgraff's principal source for the chapter.

## Limbar's mental-health crisis

Mosselman's *keutjhi* (corporal) Limbar — *mooi gedecoreerd* (beautifully decorated) — *begon-ie een beetje raar te doen* (began to act a bit strange) after long years in the *kleine oorlog*. At [[Padang Tidji]] he stood before the mirror in *goudpapier* on his white pantalon, *veel tressen en krulsen*, *boven z'n beteekenis opgedirkt* (above his station dressed up) — *"als een Indisch hoofdcommissaris van politie in groot-tenue, zooiets tusschen een Groot-Admiraal en een veldmaarschalk."* Limbar: *"Apa sergeant tida tahoe saja sekarang djendral?"* — Sergeant doesn't know that I am now a general? Mosselman's command: *"Rechts om! Geef acht!"* (Limbar hesitated one second and snapped to attention). *"Sekarang djendral mesti klein tenue. Ingerukt.... marsch!"* — Now the general must be in undress. Dismissed... march! At a later patrol, when Limbar — being punished for losing the bende's *bekas* — came before Mosselman with the karabijn in his hand and a strange cold look, *„Kenapa sekarang korporaal Limbar tida lakoe lagi?"* — Why does corporal Limbar no longer function? — Mosselman's command *„Zet af karabijn!"* (Limbar set it on the ground), *„Aan den schouder... karabijn! Ingerukt... marsch!"* brought him back from the edge. *De opkomende waanzin liet haar greep op den korporaal los* (the rising madness released its grip on the corporal). Limbar was *afgekeurd* and when his *Militaire Willemsorde* came, *belooning voor zijn schitterende daden*, he lay in the *gekkenafdeeling* at [[Koetaradja]], where Mosselman visited him; he had made himself a *ridderorde* out of a tin lid, *en deze droeg Limbar, die in bed lag, op de borst*. He died not long after at the *krankzinnigengesticht te Buitenzorg* — *het einde van een braaf keutjhi* — the end of a brave corporal.

## The wild pig at Koeta Tjané with Willem van Leuven

In the Alas-dal near Koeta Tjané Mosselman, *terugkeerend naar zijn brigade*, heard *geritsel* in a suikerriettuintje and saw a *groot varken* that *regelrecht op hem afkwam* — so that he was *verplicht* (obliged) to shoot it. *Willem van Leuven|Willem* — the *slagersjongen*-turned-sergeant — was summoned: *"Willem, hier is een tjèlèng; maak er eens wat van"* — Willem, here is a pig; make something of it. Willem came, looked at the animal *met halfgesloten oogen*, *stelde zijn oogen op het grootste diaphragma*, looked at Mosselman: *"Een echt wild varken"* — a real wild pig. The animal was hung, opened, and boned *naar de regelen der kunst* (according to the rules of the art), and the next day *groote zijden spek, een paar vingers dik, en een paar hammen in den rook* (great flitches of bacon, a couple of fingers thick, and a couple of hams in the smoke) hung in the bivak. Kapitein Muurling *was content met deze verklaring* (was content with this explanation). *Eerlijk gezegd geloof ik er geen woord van* — Honestly I don't believe a word of it, Zentgraff comments; *ik denk dat Mosselman, die zich terdege herinnert dat de Chinees dagen later met een claim is gekomen (natuurlijk afgepoeierd), het verhaal zoo dikwijls heeft gedaan dat hij het nu zelf gelooft, of dat hij bang is voor „navordering"* (I think that Mosselman, who thoroughly remembers that the Chinese came days later with a claim — fended off, of course — has told the story so often that he now believes it himself, or that he is afraid of a reassessment).

## Significance

Mosselman is the type of the old *brigadecommandant* of the Atjeh-oorlog: a sergeant who, *na lange jaren het leven in den kleinen oorlog te hebben meegemaakt*, knew his brigade as a *trimoerti*, who could bring a mad corporal back from the edge with a single command, and who ran a *bende* of *Tiro-oelama's* to ground at the Kroeëng Peutoeë. *Het is opmerkelijk, maar wanneer je nu, eene kwart eeuw later, Mosselman over dat varken spreekt dan draait hij subiet en met groote snelheid datzelfde verhaal af* — even now, a quarter-century later, Mosselman, asked about that pig, reels off the same story with great speed.

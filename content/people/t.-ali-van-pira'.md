**T. Ali van Pira'** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Tgk. di Barat]]

In June [[1911]] came reports of the vanished Teungkoe again: he had moved with some adherents from his hiding-places in the Gajò mountains back to the upper [[Keureutòë]]. That meant an immediate setback — "Tgk. di Barat bléé Tgk. di Barat." The report for June recorded: "his presence in Keureutòë revealed itself at once in the defection of the former band-leader [[T. Ali van Pira']], son-in-law of Tgk. di Paja Bakong, with eight followers, all boys of sixteen to eighteen." Only some six nights did the fugitives stay away before reporting themselves again; general opinion said that they had come to the Teungkoe without firearms and had been rejected by him.

### From [[Tgk. Sjeh di Paja Bakong]]

The death of Tgk. Sjeh di Paja Bakong, following that of [[Pang Nanggròë]] a month earlier, brought the decisive turn in [[Keureutòë]]: in [[1910]] seventy-one resistance men were killed there, fifty-one taken prisoner and eighty submitted, and [[1911]] brought "the great improvement," the monthly reports opening habitually with the phrase "the political situation remained favourable." ("the political situation remained favourable") His son-in-law [[T. Ali van Pira']], a former band-leader, defected with eight followers in June [[1911]] but reported himself again after six nights.

### From [[The resistance in Atjeh 1908–1912]]

In June [[1911]] came news of Tgk. di Barat again, returned from the Gajòland to the upper Keureutòë; his presence at once revealed itself in the defection of the former band-head [[T. Ali van Pira']], son-in-law of Tgk. di Paja Bakong, with eight followers, all boys of sixteen to eighteen — who stayed away only six nights before reporting themselves again, general opinion saying they had come to the Teungkoe without firearms and been rejected. In December "the situation was favourable": [[H. Behrens|Behrens]] and his men were on the Teungkoe's track.

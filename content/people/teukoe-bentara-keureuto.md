**Teukoe Bentara Keureuto** — the chief bearing the title *Bentara* of the *landschap* [[Keureuto|Keureuto]] in the *onderafdeeling* [[Lho Soekon|Lho Soekon]] — was the *[[Oeléebalang|oeléebalang]]* whom Broersma met at [[Blang Djeroeën|Blang Djeroeën]]. He rules a large *landschap* and receives accordingly f 600 a month from the *landschapskas*, in addition to f 125 for his car. He maintains a small lorry for the transport of persons and goods and fetches, for example, fish daily from the coast.

## Character and Family
Broersma describes him as *'een krachtig en kundig hoofd'* — *a powerful and capable chief.* *'In vriendelijkheid put hij zich niet uit, doch hij verstaat het antwoorden te geven op vragen over het volksbestaan, die hout snijden'* — *In friendliness he does not exhaust himself, but he knows how to answer questions about the life of the people that are solidly true.* His son attends the *Mulo*-school at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and in his holidays helps his father in the work of government. The choice of a Dutch-speaking *Mulo* for the son of an *oeléebalang* of the interior is, in Broersma's view, a sign of the same modernising tendency visible in the houses of Peureula and Peusangan.

## The Landschap
The *landschap* of Keureuto lies in the western part of the *onderafdeeling* Lho Soekon, in the hill-country where the memory of [[Christoffel's expedition to Keureuto (c. 1908)|Christoffel's expedition]] — the *apitan rimoeeng*, or *tiger-captain* — is still fresh. The eastern half of the *onderafdeeling* is the great swamp of [[Paja Tjitjem|Paja Tjitjem]], but the western half is a fertile rice-plain watered by the Keureuto river, and the chief draws an income from the *hacil* on the pepper and pinang of the hills as well as from the *padi* of the plain.

## Significance
Teukoe Bentara is the type of the *krachtig en kundig hoofd* of the interior — a chief not effusively friendly but solidly competent, who manages his *landschap* with a lorry and a *Mulo*-school son. Broersma's portrait of him is the counterweight to his portrait of the *'verlichte hoofden'* of Peureula and Peusangan: the same modernising tendency is visible, but in a harder, less showy form.

**Nja' Ali** — also **Si Ali of [[Oenga]]** — was the Acehnese who murdered the Civil Authority [[W. van Kregten]] and two soldiers at [[Lam No]] on [[20 May]] [[1914]] and thereby became, in a few weeks, a band-head of reputation in the [[Daja]] country. He was killed by lieutenant [[C. A. Snell]] at the holy grave near the mouth of the [[Daja-rivier]] on [[5 August]] [[1914]].

## Motive

[[H. N. A. Swart]]'s own investigation, conducted personally in a district he knew through and through, established the cause: "a certain Si Ali of [[Oenga]] had long had good reason to be aggrieved at the *[[oelèëbalang]]* of [[Lambeusdë]], because the latter stood in an illicit understanding with Ali's wife. More than once matters had come to an outburst between the two, and the fact that Si Ali did not proceed to an attack on this chief earlier must be attributed to the fact that an Acehnese does not readily raise his hands against a territorial chief."

Shortly before the murder he had been dismissed, at the instigation of the Civil Authority [[W. van Kregten|Van Kregten]], from his post as *mandoer* of a coconut garden; at the inspection which led to that dismissal he must have been pointed to his duty somewhat roughly by the Civil Authority. Du Croo added the note: "Van Kregten was a quiet, calm, well-balanced man."

"Taking the Acehnese national character into account, he now needed to let nothing restrain him from playing his own judge."

## The band

The easiest way to play his own judge was to gather about him a band of men who likewise had grievances against the administration or their chiefs. He found them in [[Tgk. Machmoed van Lam No]], a former *teungkoe-meunasah* who had quarrelled with his *oelèëbalang* over his share in the *[[zakat]]*; in [[Si Seuna]], whose wife had shortly before been unlawfully taken from him by the same *oelèëbalang*; and in [[Pang Abaih]], a former band-head "for whom the life of the jungle still evidently held a certain attraction." The four gathered some youths, also *kaom*-fellows, about them.

## The murder of [[20 May]] [[1914]]

The plan to withdraw had already been formed when, in the night of [[19 May|19]]–[[20 May]], the extremely careless manner in which the Civil Authority and his escort chose and occupied their night quarters on the *keudé* at Lam No — the weapons of both soldiers, who were in two different unwalled rooms, lying on the floor — gave them a fine chance to make a name and to provide themselves with weapons.

That night the band held a meeting, and in the early morning of [[20 May]], about half past five, when the soldiers had even left their weapons unguarded to go and drink coffee and lieutenant Van Kregten was innocently winding his puttees, Si Ali, Tgk. Machmoed, Pang Abaih, Si Seuha and a certain [[Pang Hitam]] suddenly attacked him "with the well-known result."

After the murder of the Civil Authority and the two soldiers, Si Ali sought the *oelèëbalang* of Lambeusdë, who had hidden himself so well that he was not found and escaped.

The band fled to the mountains with the weapons of the murdered soldiers and was pursued by several brigades.

## Reputation and death

The two clashes in the Daja country between the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* patrols and the murderers cost the Dutch two killed and seven wounded against six of the band laid low and one severely wounded brought in; three band members reported themselves. That action, du Croo observed, "had not been an unqualified success — they had suffered heavy losses and lost several carbines, whereby the band leader Si (Nja') Ali at once acquired a great reputation, and that is and was in Atjeh a dangerous and highly contagious phenomenon."

His reputation drew [[Nja' Asém]] and the Lhong band to him. On [[5 August]] [[1914]] lieutenant Snell, after a carefully prepared action, surprised the united bands at the holy grave near the mouth of the Daja river; fifteen band members fell, among them both band-heads Nja' Ali and Nja' Asém, and the repeating carbines lost by the Daja column and the overseer's Mauser rifle were recaptured.

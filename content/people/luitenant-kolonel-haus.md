**Luitenant-kolonel Haus** was a Dutch lieutenant-colonel of infantry in [[Atjeh]] in [[1876]], commander of the [[Westerlinie|western line]] of posts (Olehleh, Lampasei, Blang-Oë, Sinangri, Lampagar, Pakan Badak, Boekit Seboen, Djempit, Ketapan Doewa and [[Boekit Daroe]]) during the consolidation period of that year. He gave the order for the [[Gli Taroem night ambush (8-9 August 1876)|night ambush in the Gli Taroem pass]] of 8–9 August [[1876]].

## The order of 8 August 1876

When, at the beginning of August, reports from a prisoner and from spies established that enemy parties nightly carried rice bought in the IV and VI Moekims through the pass of [[Glitaroem|Gli Taroem]] and the other mountain paths to the hungry [[XXII Moekims]], and word came on 8 August that a large enemy transport would most probably pass that night, Haus judged that a favourable opportunity of surprising an enemy convoy must not go unused. He detailed from the garrisons of [[Djempit]], [[Ketapan Doewa]] and [[Boekit Daroe]] a force of 3 officers and 120 non-commissioned officers and men, prescribed their orders in writing — every post an officer and 40 bayonets, union at the ravine, mutual support, a recognition signal agreed beforehand, the return signal by gun fire from the posts — and gave his further intentions to the young First Lieutenant-adjutant [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. Van Heutsz]], who was to accompany the force. The ambush's tragic issue by friendly fire is told at the event; his written order is printed verbatim by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]).

The commander of the western line in 1876 bore the same name as — and was presumably identical with — Colonel [[J. F. Haus]], the first military commandant of Atjeh appointed at the separation of the civil and military commands of April [[1881]].

**Teukoe Mamat of [[Lambeusdë]]** was the Acehnese chief who, according to the investigation conducted by [[H. J. Schmidt]], stood behind the [[Resistance in Daja and Lhong (1914)|resistance in Daja and Lhong]] of [[1914]] as its secret instigator, and who was arrested on [[2 September]] [[1914]] and banished from the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh]].

## Swart's warning

At Schmidt's departure for the affected district, [[H. N. A. Swart]] had said: "remember, you must keep an eye on Teukoe Mamat of Lambeusdë!" — for, as [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] observed, "in all such Acehnese affairs there is always something or someone pulling the strings behind the scenes and therefore deserving more than ordinary attention; the difficulty, however, is to find that something or that someone."

## The intrigue

T. Mamat was a relative of the *[[oelèëbalang]]* of the [[IV Moekims Lhong]], with whom he was on bad terms, and a personal enemy of the district head of the *moekim* Lhong. He was also the brother-in-law of [[Nja' Asém]], whose fall from esteem after his wife's death had thrown him off balance.

"And now T. Mamat had reasoned thus: if I can secretly stir up a revolt, then both the district head and the territorial chief will fall into discredit with the administration, because they neither prevented the revolt nor gave warning of it."

Du Croo's comment: "A typical Acehnese intrigue — whether an innocent European official became its victim, or the deceived villagers lost their lives in it, left T. Mamat wholly unmoved. But… this time he was playing against a master who had not much left to learn in the field of Acehnese intrigue."

## Execution of the design

Through T. Mamat's intervention seven men of the Lhong band reported themselves before the end of July [[1914]]; Nja' Asém apparently considered this too high a toll and fled with his men to [[Daja]] to join the band of [[Nja' Ali]]. Both band-heads were killed by lieutenant [[C. A. Snell|Snell]] at [[Koeala Daja]] on [[5 August]].

T. Mamat had indeed "co-operated properly in bringing the wicked rebels back," for which a reward had been promised him, on condition that he should prove to have played a wholly pure role. Feeling entirely safe and expecting the *oelèëbalangschap* as his reward, "he had indeed walked the fire out of his sandals."

"Maar toen volgde het onderzoek, and that investigation was led by a man who needed no interpreter and who knew too much of Atjeh and the Acehnese to go astray, and… on [[2 September]] T. Mamat was arrested and banished from the Government of Atjeh, while the incompetent territorial chief was dismissed and replaced by another member of the family."

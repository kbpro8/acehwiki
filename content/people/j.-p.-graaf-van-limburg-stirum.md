**J. P. graaf van Limburg Stirum** — Jan Pieter, count (graaf) Van Limburg Stirum ([[1863]]-[[1936]]) — was a Dutch liberal politician who, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s retrospective, supplied the most lapidary formulation of the moral ambiguity of the modern colonial enterprise. The remark is preserved alongside [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s thought-experiment as a counter-weight to the consensus of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] state.

## The "rijk aan wensen, arm aan tevredenheid" formulation

[[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] cites [[J. P. graaf van Limburg Stirum|Van Limburg Stirum]]'s view of the relation of the modern colonising powers to the indigenous populations in the following form:

> "Zij leggen straten, spoorwegen aan en doen allerlei wonderbare dingen die het land rijk maken. Maar feitelijk maken zij hen rijk aan wensen en arm aan tevredenheid".

— They lay streets, railways, and do all kinds of wonderful things that make the land rich. But in fact they make them rich in wishes and poor in contentment.

## Significance

[[J. P. graaf van Limburg Stirum|Van Limburg Stirum]]'s formulation is, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s retrospective, the canonical liberal statement of the moral paradox of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] state — that the very uplift of the colonial population, by raising its expectations, also raised its discontent. The formulation is preserved alongside [[Mr J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]]'s thought-experiment as a counter-weight to the consensus that irrigation, education and regional councils were, by their nature, blessings. "Wie zou hier durven kiezen, wie oordelen?" — who would dare choose here, who judge? — is [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s comment.

## On Atjeh

[[J. P. graaf van Limburg Stirum|Van Limburg Stirum]]'s observation bears directly on the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] administration of Atjeh, where the construction of roads, telegraph lines, irrigation works and (after [[1904]]) administrative posts was the principal material content of the *ethische politiek* in action. The paradox he identifies — that improvement generates discontent — would prove characteristic of the Atjehnese post-conquest decades.

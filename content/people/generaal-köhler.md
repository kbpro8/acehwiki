**Generaal Köhler** was a Dutch *generaal-majoor* of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who commanded the [[First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)|first Dutch expedition against Atjeh]] in April [[1873]] and was killed outside the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] when his assault was repulsed by the Atjehneezen. In [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s narrative his failure is "slechts een voorspel" of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]]'s drama — the bloody lesson that forced the Dutch government to assemble a vastly stronger force under [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] eight months later.

## Command of the First Expedition

Köhler led the first Dutch landing against the [[Atjeh (Borel account)|sultanate of Atjeh]] in April [[1873]], his column advancing from the strand directly upon the Kraton of the sultan. The operation, conceived as a punitive blow after the breach of Dutch-Atjeh relations, was undertaken without proper reconnaissance of the formidable *borstwering* (earthen parapet), *bamboe-doeri versperringen* (bamboo-thorn barricades) and *gracht* (moat) that protected the sultan's palace-fortress. Dutch troops advancing on the open ground before the walls were met by a "moorddadig vuur" and could gain no footing. Köhler himself fell, struck down outside the Kraton, and with him perished the best chance of a quick Dutch success on the day. The repulse humiliated Dutch prestige in the Indies; "door een tweede échec zou ons prestige in Indië geheel vernietigd zijn," Borel records of the prevailing sentiment.

## Legacy and Borel's Judgement

The news of the catastrophe "maakte een onuitsprekelijken indruk" in both Indië and Nederland and engaged the entire Dutch nation with the Atjeh question. Borel treats Köhler's defeat as a foretaste of the disasters that would follow under van Swieten, and as the immediate cause of the [[Atjeh-enquête|onzalige enquête]] and of the government's decision to send out a second, much larger expedition in November [[1873]]. The lesson Borel draws is the same he presses throughout his book: that the Atjehneezen, "uit eene geheel bijzondere stof gekneed," would yield only to the *kracht der wapens* pursued to its conclusion, and that any Dutch commander who under-estimated them, as Köhler had done at the Kraton's walls, was bound to repeat his fate.

**Kolonel Simbolon** was the territorial commander of Territorium I, Bukit Barisan, in the Indonesian National Army, entrusted in [[October]] [[1953]] with the reconquest of Atjeh from the rebel forces of the [[Negara Islam Indonesia]] (N.I.I.) following [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]]'s proclamation of Atjeh's accession to the N.I.I. on [[21 September]] [[1953]]. His frank public statement on [[3 October]] [[1953]] — that the crisis in Atjeh could not be solved by weapons alone — became one of the defining public acknowledgements of the rebellion's political depth.

## An ironic assignment

Henri J. H. Alers's *Om een rode of groene Merdeka* calls the reconquest of Atjeh, given the scale of the rebel gains by late September 1953, "a nearly hopeless task," and notes a certain irony in the fact that Simbolon, given this "unenviable" assignment, had himself declared himself for "green" — the Islamic side — during the [[17 oktober-affaire|17 October 1952]] controversy on Java. Nonetheless, Alers writes, Simbolon "tackled the problem vigorously" and achieved more success than had been hoped.

## Simbolon's statement of 3 October 1953

H. C. Beynon's [[Nederland staat terecht in Indonesië]] records Simbolon's statement of [[3 October]] [[1953]] — the same day government forces reached [[Seulimeum]] from [[Kutaradja]] — in which the territorial commander offered an unusually candid assessment of the situation: *"De toestand in Atjeh is zeer ernstig; het is niet mogelijk deze kwestie met wapenen alleen op te lossen"* ("The situation in Atjeh is very serious; it is not possible to resolve this question with weapons alone"). The remark acknowledged what the government in [[Djakarta]] was reluctant to admit publicly: that the [[Atjèh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia (1953)|1953 rebellion]] drew on deep grievances that military reconquest alone would not address.

## The reconquest of Atjeh (October–November 1953)

The government counter-offensive, conducted under Simbolon's command, unfolded through October and November [[1953]]: [[Seulimeum]] was reached from [[Kutaradja]] on [[3 October]], and the hamlet of [[Bajeunen]], some 20 kilometres from [[Langsa]], on the same date; [[Meureudu]] was retaken on [[10 October]] after heavy fighting; [[Blangkedjeren]] and [[Bireuën]] were occupied on [[20 October]]; and [[Takengon]] was finally recaptured on [[24 November]] [[1953]] in a pincer operation mounted from Blangkedjeren and Bireuën. Troop reinforcements were also sent to the West Coast. By the end of November 1953, Alers records, all the major towns and connecting roads of Atjeh were once more in government hands, though fierce attacks on them continued, and the rebellion — no longer holding a contiguous territory with open lines to the outside world — had been reduced to the scale of "roving bands in the interior," much as the parallel insurgencies in West Java and South Celebes.

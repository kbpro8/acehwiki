**Hadji Aboe** was a man of the kampong Lambaroe in the [[VI Moekims]], identified by the colonial investigation as a participant in the [[Koewala Pantjoer patrol ambush (7 April 1876)|ambush of 7 April 1876]] on the Dutch patrol returning from the beach of Koewala Pantjoer, and named afterwards as the enemy who gathered the refugees of his burnt kampong into a fresh band in the summer of [[1876]].

## The ambush and the burning of his house

The strict inquiry conducted by the controleur [[Michielsen]] after 7 April could prove only that this certain Hadji Aboe had taken part in the ambush that left five Dutch wounded dying within hours. He had made himself scarce; the only punishment that reached him was burning his house.

## After May

The two friendly kampong heads who visited the Lampagar camp on the evening of 2 May told the officers — in the words reproduced by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]) — that they suspected Hadji Aboe, wishing to avenge the burning of his house, of gathering the refugees of his kampong with the purpose either of troubling the friendly people or of attacking the post of Lampagar itself. The investigating official afterwards learnt that the plotting of the night attack belonged to [[Panglima Moeda of Piëng]]; but at the beginning of August [[1876]] new reports reached the Dutch that about 600 enemies under Hadji Aboe had gathered in the mountains between the IV and VI Moekims with the purpose of attacking Lampagar again — and named the signatories of the report as the heads [[Hadji Soliman of Lampagar|Hadji Soliman]] and [[Hadji Laoet]].

**Panglima Polem** ("Lord Elder Brother") is the hereditary title of the chief (*panglima sagi*) of the [[XXII Mukims]] of [[Great Acheh]]. The bearer is regarded as the doyen of the chiefs of the three sagis, on account of the antiquity of his lineage, the bravery of the men of his sagi, and its wealth. His sagi really numbers many more mukims than the name implies — increase of population having given rise to the formation of new districts — yet the traditional name has survived. The same is true, though in a less degree, of the [[XXV Mukims]], while the [[XXVI Mukims]] continue to correspond with their ancient name.

## The legend of origin

A tradition represents the Polems as sprung from the sultans. The legend, as given in Vol. I, runs that the great [[Meukuta Alam]] once suffered from a venereal disease, and in order to cure it had recourse to the remedy — held in much repute among natives — of having intercourse with a healthy woman. For this purpose he employed a black slave, and did in fact recover; the slave however became pregnant. As in Acheh great weight is given to descent from the mother's side, the sultan was distressed at the prospect of having to acknowledge as his son the child of a black woman, and sent her forth into the jungle — that is, into the district of the [[XXII Mukims]]. According to the legend, the son, whose life was spared by the highlanders, became the first Panglima Polem, chief of what is in many respects the most important sagi of Acheh.

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] dismisses this tradition — of which there are also other versions less insulting to the honour of the Polem — as containing "not a single grain of historic truth." The title of "elder brother," he suggests, probably typified the original relation between the powerful sagi-chief and the sultan; just as the official title of "grandfathers" (*ne*) was borne by [[Teuku Ne' Raja Muda Seutia]] of [[Meura'sa]] and [[Teuku Ne' Peureuba Wangsa]] of the [[IX Mukims]] of the XXV.

## Place in the marriage with the State

The three panglima sagi were the guardians of Acheh, which was represented as a bride that continually renewed her youth. They gave her in marriage to whom they would after mutual consultation, usually selecting the bridegroom from the family of his predecessors; yet they did not shrink upon occasion from the introduction of a new dynasty or even the choice of a foreigner, as in the case of the Sultans of Arab descent. The bridegroom had to pay to each of the chiefs a sum of 500 dollars as a wedding gift (*jinamee* or *peunamee*). The three guardians, however, "did not succeed in retaining the supremacy over the federate dominions which they had possessed when this metaphorical marriage-contract was concluded."

The Panglima Polem is repeatedly cited as the type of the powerful sagi-chief who, while owing nominal allegiance to the sultan, treated him with studied contempt. The father of the lately deceased Panglima Polem, like the father of the present [[Teuku Nanta Seutia]], chief of the [[VI Mukims of the XXV]], entered the royal dwelling in foul clothing and addressed the sultan as *gata* (the equivalent of *tuan ku*, "my lord"), remarking "we are not wont to say *deelat*." Of the Panglima Polem it is said that he often disregarded for months all invitations to the [[Dalam]], and finally, as a token of goodwill, journeyed to the Sultan's frontier and had a heavy gong beaten to announce his presence, after which he returned home.

## The kali of the sagi

The hereditary kali of the XXII Mukims — [[Teungku Tanoh Abee]] — by reason of his learning esteemed himself too highly to act as a sort of acolyte of the Panglima of his sagi, and refused to appear at the call of the Panglima Polem. This powerful uleebalang was thus constrained, when need arose, to seek the help of teungkus of inferior qualifications — the chief of those who rendered service in this way being [[Teungku di Lheue]], an ulama of inferior repute — while his hereditary kali devoted his time to study and the instruction of his disciples.

In [[Pidie]] there are federations of the same kind as that of the Panglima Polem; just as Acheh has its XXII Mukims of Panglima Polem, so we find in Pidie the XXII Mukims of [[Bentara Keumangan]].

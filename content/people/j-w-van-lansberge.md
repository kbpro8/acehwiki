**J. W. van Lansberge** was a Dutch diplomat and colonial administrator who served as [[Governor-General of the Netherlands India|Governor-General of Netherlands India]] from [[26 March]] [[1875]] until [[12 April]] [[1881]], succeeding [[J. Loudon|Mr. J. Loudon]] and presiding over the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. His governorship saw the rise of [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman Zahir]] as the principal Acehnese leader, the campaigns of generals [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]], [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]], [[A. J. E. Diemont|Diemont]] and [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]], the submission of the [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] in [[1879]], and the establishment of a civil government in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1881]].

## Appointment

Van Lansberge had been the King's extraordinary envoy and minister plenipotentiary in Belgium when he was appointed Governor-General by Royal Decree of [[17 December]] [[1874]], the same decree that granted Loudon's honourable discharge. He assumed office on [[26 March]] [[1875]]. His predecessor's fall had been caused by the [[conversiequaestie]]; Van Lansberge's first months were occupied in managing its aftermath, including the advice of the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad van Indië]] of [[17 October]] [[1879]] that codification of the agrarian ordinances was not a matter of urgent necessity.

## The Conversie Quaestie

Although [[H. D. Levyssohn Norman|Levyssohn Norman]] had submitted a draft general ordinance on [[1 February]] [[1875]], Van Lansberge's government declined to push the conversion of communal into individual land ownership further. The Raad van Indië's advice of [[17 October]] [[1879]] — that there was no need for codification and that amendments had already been or were less pressingly needed — was endorsed by both Governor-General and Opperbestuur. The reports of [[1878]] and [[1880]] showed that the native population, left to itself, nowhere spontaneously undertook conversion; the government's role was now limited to issuing instructions to the heads of regional administration.

## The Aceh War under Van Lansberge

When Van Lansberge arrived, the war had been formally suspended since the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the kraton]]. [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] returned to Atjeh on [[8 November]] [[1875]] with instructions of [[10 November]] [[1875]] authorising him to break with [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s purely defensive policy. Pel's campaigns of [[December 1875]]–[[February 1876]] carried the Dutch through the [[VI Moekims]], [[IV Moekims]] and [[IX Moekims]] of the [[XXV Moekims|Sagi der XXV Moekims]], and were pushing into the [[XXVI Moekims]] when Pel died suddenly of an arterial rupture at the kampong Tonga on [[24 February]] [[1876]]. He was succeeded by [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]], who arrived on [[10 March]] [[1876]], and after him by [[A. J. E. Diemont|Diemont]] on [[6 November]] [[1876]].

The arrival of [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman Zahir]] in March [[1876]] — a sayyid who had been first rijksbestuurder under the sultanate and had previously offered his services to the Dutch in vain — gave new life to the resistance. After Diemont's expedition against [[Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh]] in November-December [[1876]], Van Lansberge visited Atjeh in person in [[March 1877|March-April 1877]] to inspect the theatre of war. His visit led to a new phase of the war: the abandonment of offensive operations, the reorganisation of the inland administration of the [[XXV Moekims]], and a policy of conciliation. The expedition to [[Samalanga]] under [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] in [[August 1877]] was the last large operation before Van der Heyden became governor of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden on [[15 January]] [[1878]].

## The Submission of the XXII and XXVI Moekims

Under Van der Heyden's governorship the war was carried to its first conclusion. After the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman on 13 October 1878]], the campaigns of [[1879]] brought about the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|submission of the XXII Moekims]] — including the [[Capture of Indrapuri (24 March 1879)|capture of Indrapuri]] on [[24 March]] [[1879]], the [[Capture of Gleiêng (9 July 1879)|fall of Gleiêng]] on [[9 July]] [[1879]] — and the [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|reduction of the XXVI Moekims]] in early July. The [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of August [[1879]] completed the conquest of Great Acheh. In a cabinet letter of [[21 March]] [[1879]] Van Lansberge instructed Van der Heyden that "the war must now come to an end," whether by submission or by such punishment of the enemy that no doubt could remain about Dutch overlordship.

## Civil Government and Resignation

After the close of large-scale operations, Van Lansberge initiated the definitive administrative reorganisation. [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven]], last resident of [[Palembang]], was appointed government commissioner together with Van der Heyden to draft the new organisation of the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden; [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. T. H. der Kinderen]] was sent out as commissioner for the reorganisation of justice in the buitenbezittingen. In March [[1881]] the definitive organisation came into force: a governor at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], three assistant-residents (Groot-Atjeh, Noord- en Oostkust with headquarters at [[Telok Semawe|Telok Semawé]], and Westkust with headquarters at [[Malaboeh]]), and ten controleurs.

By authority already granted by the King in [[1879]], Van Lansberge decided to separate the civil and military commands. Van der Heyden was honourably discharged from his functions, [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]] was appointed civil governor, and [[J. F. Haus|Colonel J. F. Haus]] took the military command. Both assumed their functions on [[6 April]] [[1881]]. This discharge — the last administrative act of Van Lansberge — provoked much discussion and was debated in the [[Staten-Generaal|States-General]]. For health reasons Van Lansberge himself had requested discharge in the spring of [[1881]], which was granted by Royal Decree of [[24 November]] [[1880]]; on [[12 April]] [[1881]] he handed over his dignity to his successor.

## Other Reforms

Van Lansberge's term also saw the introduction of the [[personeele belasting|personeele belasting]] and [[patentrecht|patentrecht]] for Europeans and foreign Orientals in Netherlands India (in force from [[1 January]] [[1879]]), the great harbour works at [[Tandjong Priok]] (begun in [[1876]]), the construction of the state railways on Java, the appearance of the koffiebladziekte in [[1876]] in Sumatra's Westkust and in [[1879]] on Java, and the expedition against the Bataks in Toba in [[1878]].

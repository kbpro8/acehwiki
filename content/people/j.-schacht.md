**Professor Joseph Schacht** (1902–1969) — cited by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] as **Prof. J. Schacht** — was a German-born Islamic scholar, one of the leading western scholars of Islamic law and of the manuscript traditions of Arabic scholastic literature. He is acknowledged by Voorhoeve in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises for his guidance in the reading, explanation, and collation of the manuscripts of the [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*Tamhīd*]] of [[Abu Syakur as-Salimi|Abū Shakūr as-Sālimī]] — the principal source of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]].

## Guidance in the collation of the *Tamhīd*

Voorhoeve records, in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition, his "great debt to Professor J. Schacht for his guidance in the reading, explanation, and collation of the manuscripts of the last chapter of the *Tamhīd*." The last chapter of the *Tamhīd* — *Fīʾs-sunna waʾl-jamāʿa waʾr-radd ʿalāʾl-bidʿa* — is the source from which Raniri translated more than a quarter of the text of the *Tibjān*. Schacht's guidance was therefore indispensable to the identification of the *Tamhīd* as the principal source of the *Tibjān* — the most original contribution of Voorhoeve's edition.

Schacht is also cited in Voorhoeve's apparatus criticus for a number of emendations to the text of the *Tamhīd*. He is recorded, for instance, as the authority for the emendation of *Halāliyya* (in the manuscripts of the *Tamhīd*) to *Hallājiyya* — a sect of those who follow the doctrine of [[al-Hallaj|al-Ḥallāj]] — at p. 27 r. 13 of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]]. He is also recorded as the authority for the identification of the dogma of the *Mafrūʿiyya* of the Jabriyya as a doctrine of *kasb* — the acquisition of human action by divine creation — originally signifying "responsibility," but in Malay interpreted as *usaha*, "effort."

## Significance

Schacht's guidance in the collation of the *Tamhīd* is one of the principal channels by which the source-study of the *Tibjān* was carried through. The identification of the *Tamhīd* as the principal source of the *Tibjān* — established by Voorhoeve with Schacht's guidance — is the most original contribution of the [[1955]] edition, and the one on which the modern understanding of the textual genealogy of Raniri's Malay encyclopaedia of religions and sects depends.

**Pierre Cambronne** was the French general to whom the saying *“La garde meurt, mais ne se rend pas!”* — the guard dies, but does not surrender — is poetically attributed, and which the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* placed on its masthead as the motto of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]*.

## The motto in the jubilee number

The special number carried the phrase beneath the title *Het Korps Maréchaussée in Atjeh — De Atjehsche Garde, 1890 - 2 April 1930*. The editorial explained the association: the name *Maréchaussée* was in the Netherlands-Indies army "meer dan een klank," and the word "doet denken aan het Cambronne dichterlijk toegeschreven woord: *La garde meurt, mais ne se rend pas!*" — calls to mind the words poetically attributed to Cambronne. "Het Korps Maréchaussée is onze Garde," the editors added — the Corps Maréchaussée is our Guard.

The styling of the corps as *De Atjehsche Garde* — the Atjehnese Guard — derives from the same comparison with the Imperial Guard of Napoleon.

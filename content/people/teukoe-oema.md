**Teukoe Oema** — also rendered *Teuku Umar* or *Teuku Uma* — was, in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the Atjèhnese chief of the [[XXII Mukims|VI Moekim's]] who began the [[1880s]] as an uncertain Dutch ally and ended as one of the most zealous enemies of the Netherlands in the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]]. His conversion — provoked by the disastrous Dutch employment of him in the [[Nisero affair (1883-1885)|Nisero affair]] of [[July 1884]] — is, in Kreemer's narrative, a key episode in the Dutch loss of the [[XXII Mukims]] and the rise of the *ulama*-led resistance.

## Descent and early position

Kreemer identifies Teukoe Oema as "een schoonzoon van het vroegere hoofd der VI Moekim's Teukoe Nanta Seutia" — "a son-in-law of the former head of the VI Moekim's Teukoe Nanta Seutia." He held the [[XXII Mukims|VI Moekim's]] district of [[Great Acheh|Groot-Atjèh]] in the early [[1880s]], during the governorship of [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] ([[1883]]–[[1884]]), when the Dutch were attempting to play the Atjèhnese chiefs against each other. "Herhaaldelijk werden met hem onderhandelingen gevoerd, aanvankelijk verklaarde hij zich bereid onze partij te kiezen, maar in het begin van 1884 keerde hij geheel om, om in Maart weder aanraking met ons bestuur te zoeken" — "Repeatedly negotiations were conducted with him; at first he declared himself willing to take our side, but at the beginning of 1884 he turned completely around, only to seek contact with our administration again in March."

## The Nisero affair and the conversion

The decisive break came in the [[Nisero affair (1883-1885)|Nisero affair]]. Governor [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], seeking to liberate the crew of the wrecked English ship *Nisero* from the [[Teunom|Radja of Teunöm]], employed Teukoe Oema as a Dutch agent. "Hiertoe werd deze den 3en juli 1884 met 32 volgelingen ingescheept; toen hij met zijn volk aan land werd gezet, werden de sloeproeiers plotseling met den klewang aangevallen en op twee na allen vermoord. Oema maakte zich meester van de wapens en munitie, welke in de sloep aanwezig waren en deed zich sedert weder als een onzer ijverigste vijanden kennen" — "For this purpose he was shipped on 3 July 1884 with 32 followers; when he and his people were put ashore, the boat-rowers were suddenly attacked with the klewang and all but two murdered. Oema took possession of the weapons and ammunition that were in the boat, and from then on made himself known as one of our most zealous enemies."

The ambiguous syntax of Kreemer's account leaves open the question of whether Oema himself ordered the slaughter of the boat-rowers or whether the attack was the act of an Atjèhnese party on the shore that Oema then exploited. In either case, the seizure of the Dutch weapons and ammunition, and Oema's open enmity thereafter, transformed the military situation in the [[XXII Mukims]].

## Significance

Teukoe Oema's conversion is, in Kreemer's narrative, the moment at which the Dutch policy of playing the Atjèhnese chiefs against each other collapsed in the [[XXII Mukims]]. His seizure of Dutch weapons and his open enmity thereafter supplied the *ulama*-led resistance with a military chief of the first rank, and his example — of a chief who had tried the Dutch alliance and rejected it — reinforced the message of [[Teungku Tiro|Teungkoe Tirö]] and the other *ulama* that the Dutch were "met geen overmacht meer te keer te gaan" — "no longer to be countered by superior force." The [[Oema-periode (1893-1896)|Oema-period]] of [[1893]]–[[1896]] takes its name from his later prominence.

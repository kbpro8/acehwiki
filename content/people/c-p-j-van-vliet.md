**C. P. J. van Vliet** — also written *overste van Vliet* — *Luitenant-Kolonel van den Generalen Staf*, was the Chief of Staff of the Dutch military command in [[Aceh]] throughout the first semester of [[1896]], as recorded in A. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*. Van Vliet's role combined operational planning with personal reconnaissance under fire: he planned the cancelled [[30 March 1896 Lamkrak operation|Lamkrak operation of 30 March 1896]] which [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection aborted, personally conducted reconnaissances under fire on [[21 April]], [[30 April]] and [[4 May]] [[1896]], commanded the *colonne Lamtih* on [[23 May]] [[1896]] during the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]], served as *Chef van den Staf* of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 expedition]] and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|22–23 June 1896 expedition]], and was sent on [[12 June]] [[1896]] with [[J. J. Hissink|luit. ter zee 1e klasse Hissink]] to reconnoitre the [[Westkust]] for a possible landing at [[Lepong]]. His combination of staff planning and field reconnaissance made him one of the most versatile and continuously active Dutch officers of the spring [[1896]] operations.

## The Cancelled Lamkrak Operation of 30 March 1896

Van Vliet's first major appearance in *ATJEH '96* is as the planner of the [[30 March 1896 Lamkrak operation|Lamkrak operation of 30 March 1896]] — a multi-column operation intended to clear the four "eigentliche" Lamkrak mukims ([[Lamkrak|Lam-Krak]], [[Lam-Lheuë]], [[Ateuh]] and [[Kroeng Mak]]) of hostile bands. The operation, which had been prepared with the cooperation of [[Teuku Umar]]'s legion, was cancelled when Umar defected on [[29 March]] [[1896]] — three days before the planned attack. The composition of the cancelled column is recorded in detail: 3 companies of the [[3e Bataljon]] (under [[K. W. Steinmetz|Maj. Steinmetz]]), the [[6e Bataljon]] (under [[H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]]), the [[12e Bataljon]] (under [[G. Ilgen|Overste Ilgen]]), the [[14e Bataljon]] (under [[G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. Van der Noordaa]]), two sections of under [[A. Bangert|Kapitein Bangert]], the under [[N. Plantenga|Luitenants Plantenga]] and [[A. S. Ruzette|Ruzette]], and 4 brigades *maréchaussée* — an expeditionary force of approximately 1,500 men. The cancellation of the operation, on the eve of its execution, was the first Dutch operational consequence of Umar's defection, and Van Vliet's role as its planner placed him at the centre of the institutional shock which the defection represented.

## Personal Reconnaissances under Fire and the May–June Operations

Throughout April, May and June [[1896]], Van Vliet combined his staff duties with personal reconnaissance under fire. He "personally conducted reconnaissances under fire" on [[21 April]], [[30 April]] and [[4 May]] [[1896]] — the same dates as the [[20 April 1896 multi-column operation|20 April 1896 operation]], the [[night surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe]] and the [[4 May 1896 expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb|4 May 1896 expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]. He commanded the *colonne Lamtih* on [[23 May]] [[1896]] during the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] — the same operation in which [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s *bergcolonne* stormed [[Tjot Tjako]] and [[P. Linck|Linck]]'s 4e compagnie stormed the *versterking Belang*. He served as *Chef van den Staf* of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 expedition]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] (with three colonnes under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]]) and of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|22–23 June 1896 expedition]] (with the sea-landing 1e Colonne under [[G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. Hansen]] and the land 2e Colonne under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]). On [[12 June]] [[1896]] he was sent with [[J. J. Hissink|luit. ter zee 1e klasse Hissink]] and the commander of [[H. M. Madoera|H. M. Madoera]] ([[R. Reinders|luit. ter zee 1e kl. Reinders]]) aboard the *gouvernementsstoomer Albatros* to reconnoitre the [[Westkust]] for a possible landing at Lepong; the reconnaissance returned the following evening with an "unsatisfactory" result, and the expedition was postponed due to weather.

## Significance

Van Vliet's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the *Chef van den Staf* who combined operational planning with personal field reconnaissance — a combination which gave him an unusually comprehensive view of the Dutch war effort in the first semester of [[1896]]. His planning of the cancelled [[30 March 1896 Lamkrak operation|Lamkrak operation of 30 March 1896]] placed him at the centre of the institutional shock of Umar's defection, and his subsequent reconnaissances under fire on [[21 April]], [[30 April]] and [[4 May]] [[1896]] demonstrated a personal courage and operational engagement which the balso explicitly documents. His role as *Chef van den Staf* of the major June operations, and his reconnaissance to the Westkust on [[12 June]] [[1896]], confirm that his responsibilities extended across the full breadth of the Dutch theatre of operations.

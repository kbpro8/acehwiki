**C. P. J. van Vliet** — also written *overste van Vliet* — *Luitenant-Kolonel van den Generalen Staf*, was the Chief of Staff of the Dutch military command in [[Aceh]] throughout the first semester of [[1896]], as recorded in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]*. He was later promoted to *Kolonel* (Colonel) and appointed [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] van [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in November [[1896]] — the principal subject of *Hoofdstuk IV* (pp. 145–160) of *[[ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel]]*. His tenure as governor, which lasted from [[17 November]] [[1896]] until [[1898]] — when he would be succeeded by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — was the bridge between the *pacificatie* (pacification) of the second semester of [[1896]] and the decisive *korte oorlog* phase which would be launched by Van Heutsz.

## The Cancelled Lamkrak Operation of 30 March [[1896]]

Van Vliet's first major appearance in *ATJEH '96* is as the planner of the [[30 March 1896 Lamkrak operation|Lamkrak operation of 30 March 1896]] — a multi-column operation intended to clear the four *eigentliche* Lamkrak mukims ([[Lamkrak|Lam-Krak]], [[Lam-Lheuë]], [[Ateuh]] and [[Kroeng Mak]]) of hostile bands. The operation, which had been prepared with the cooperation of [[Teuku Umar]]'s legion, was cancelled when Umar defected on [[29 March]] [[1896]] — three days before the planned attack. The composition of the cancelled column is recorded in detail: 3 companies of the [[3e Bataljon]] (under [[K. W. Steinmetz|Maj. Steinmetz]]), the [[6e Bataljon]] (under [[H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]]), the [[12e Bataljon]] (under [[G. Ilgen|Overste Ilgen]]), the [[14e Bataljon]] (under [[G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. Van der Noordaa]]), two sections of under [[A. Bangert|Kapitein Bangert]], the under [[N. Plantenga|Luitenants Plantenga]] and [[A. S. Ruzette|Ruzette]], and 4 brigades *maréchaussée* — an expeditionary force of approximately 1,500 men. The cancellation of the operation, on the eve of its execution, was the first Dutch operational consequence of Umar's defection, and Van Vliet's role as its planner placed him at the centre of the institutional shock which the defection represented.

## Personal Reconnaissances under Fire and the May–June Operations

Throughout April, May and June [[1896]], Van Vliet combined his staff duties with personal reconnaissance under fire. He *personally conducted reconnaissances under fire* on [[21 April]], [[30 April]] and [[4 May]] [[1896]] — the same dates as the [[20 April 1896 multi-column operation|20 April 1896 operation]], the [[night surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe]] and the [[4 May 1896 expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb|4 May 1896 expedition to XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]]. He commanded the *colonne Lamtih* on [[23 May]] [[1896]] during the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] — the same operation in which [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s *bergcolonne* stormed [[Tjot Tjako]] and [[P. Linck|Linck]]'s 4e compagnie stormed the *versterking Belang*. He served as *Chef van den Staf* (Chief of Staff) of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 expedition]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] (with three colonnes under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]]) and of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|22–23 June 1896 expedition]] (with the sea-landing 1e Colonne under [[G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. Hansen]] and the land 2e Colonne under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]).

On [[12 June]] [[1896]] he was sent with [[J. J. Hissink|luit. ter zee 1e klasse Hissink]] and the commander of [[H. M. Madoera|H. M. Madoera]] ([[R. Reinders|luit. ter zee 1e kl. Reinders]]) aboard the *gouvernementsstoomer Albatros* to reconnoitre the [[Westkust]] for a possible landing at Lepong; the reconnaissance returned the following evening with an *unsatisfactory* result, and the expedition was postponed due to weather. His combination of staff planning and field reconnaissance made him one of the most versatile and continuously active Dutch officers of the spring [[1896]] operations.

## Appointment as Civiel en Militair Gouverneur and the Instruction of November [[1896]]

The appointment of Kolonel Van Vliet to the governorship of Atjeh was made by the *Opperbestuur* (Supreme Government) in the autumn of [[1896]], in succession to the late [[J. J. K. de Moulin|Generaal-Majoor De Moulin]] (who had died of sunstroke on [[7 July]] [[1896]]) and the acting governor [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] (who had served since [[11 July]] [[1896]]). The appointment was accompanied by the issue of the [[Bijlage III — Instruction for Civiel en Militair Gouverneur Van Vliet|Instruction for the Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van A. en O., den kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet]] — preserved as *Bijlage III* of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* (pp. 168–172). The instruction explicitly withdrew and replaced all prior policy documents — Vetter's instruction of [[28 June]] [[1896]] (Bijlage I), the conference of [[18 June]] [[1896]] (Bijlage II), and the telegram of [[17 July]] [[1896]] — and consolidated the policy framework within which the Van Vliet governorship would be conducted.

The instruction laid down the policy framework for the Van Vliet governorship in detail. The principal provisions included the maintenance of the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) as the *kern* of the Dutch occupation; the regulation of the *pasenstelsel* (pass system) for the control of movement between the Dutch-held and Acehnese-held territories; the regulation of the *hoofden* (chiefs) and the *toekenning van traktementen of geldelijke toelagen* (granting of salaries or monetary allowances) to them; the prohibition on contact with the *pretendent-sultan*; and the regulation of the *belastingen* (taxes) collected without legal basis in the gewest. The instruction further specified that *het beperken van de gemeenschap tusschen de buiten de linie wonenden, met het gebied daarbinnen, ongewenscht [is]* (the restriction of communication between those living outside the linie and the area within is undesirable), but that *het binnenkomen en naar buiten terugkeeren mag echter alleen plaats hebben, langs onze posten, waar de wapens tijdelijk worden afgelegd* (entering and returning outside may only take place through our posts, where weapons are temporarily laid down).

## Arrival and Assumption of the Governorship (8–17 November [[1896]])

[[Arrival of Van Vliet as Civiel en Militair Gouverneur (8 November 1896)|Van Vliet arrived at Koeta-Radja on 8 November 1896]] — *aankomst van den nieuw benoemden Civiel- en Militairen Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden den kolonel C. J. P. van Vliet op 8 November* — nine days before the [[Handover of Command from Stemfoort to Van Vliet (17 November 1896)|formal handover of command]] of [[17 November]] [[1896]]. His arrival opened *Hoofdstuk IV* of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* and marked the beginning of the transition from [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s second tenure to the Van Vliet governorship.

The formal handover of command took place on [[17 November]] [[1896]] — *overgave en overname van het bestuur, Kolonel J. W. Stemfoort keert naar Java terug* — at which Stemfoort returned to Java and Van Vliet formally assumed the governorship. A portrait of Kolonel Van Vliet appears in *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* opposite page 145 — the opening page of *Hoofdstuk IV* — and is the third of the six portraits bound into the volume, marking the formal transition from the post-De Moulin transitional period (covered in *Hoofdstuk III*) to the Van Vliet governorship (covered in *Hoofdstuk IV*).

## The Operations of November–December [[1896]]

Under Van Vliet's new governorship, the Dutch undertook the following operations in November–December [[1896]]:

- [[12 November]] [[1896]] — [[Reconnaissance in Lepong (12 November 1896)|Reconnaissance in Lepong]] — five days after the formal handover.
- [[29 November]] [[1896]] — [[Trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah (29 November 1896)|Trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah]] near [[Bat Dilip]] — to recover the artillery lost on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]].
- December [[1896]] — [[Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh (December 1896)|Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh]] — *de heerschende cholera belet de actie* — prevents the *colonne tegen Oemar* (column against Umar) from taking the field.

The Van Vliet governorship — which would last until [[1898]], when Van Vliet would be succeeded by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — was thus a transitional period, between the *pacificatie* (pacification) of the second semester of [[1896]] and the decisive *korte oorlog* phase which would be launched by Van Heutsz. The cholera epidemic of December [[1896]] prevented the *colonne tegen Oemar* (column against Umar) from taking the field, and the strategic opportunity which the August intelligence on [[Teuku Umar|Oemar]]'s vulnerable position in [[Lohong]] had presented was lost.

## The 1897 governorship: Lhong, the Sigli expedition, and Van Heutsz

H. T. Damsté's *Drie Atjeh-Mannen* supplies a second-source account of Van Vliet's governorship (there rendered "J. J. van Vliet") through [[1897]], marked by strained relations with [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and his gradual displacement as [[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]]'s confidence shifted toward Van Heutsz as the future governor of Atjeh. On [[22 September]] [[1896]], Van der Wijck wrote to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] that Van Vliet would have to declare, before his appointment, "that he with full conviction accepts the present situation with all its consequences." Van Vliet arrived at [[Oeléëlheu|Oeléëlheu]] on [[8 November]] [[1896]] and took over command and administration on [[17 November]], declaring that he would "relentlessly pursue the hostile bands... and gradually put an end to the resistance against our authority."

Van Vliet's first political report was not cheerful: the weather was poor, the terrain muddy, cholera cases appeared, and Van Heutsz had already predicted on [[12 September]] that the operation lacked offensive character. On [[1 January]] [[1897]] an expedition was sent to [[Lhong]] to capture [[Toekoe Oemar|Teukoe Oema]], but an Atjehsche guide misled the column and the capture failed; *De Locomotief* of [[16 January]] placed the blame squarely on the guide. Van der Wijck nonetheless defended Van Vliet in his letter to Snouck of [[17 February]] [[1897]], writing that Van Vliet "has, in my opinion, shown to conduct a wise policy."

The [[Sigli expedition (August 1897)|Sigli expedition]] of August [[1897]] was the defining operation of Van Vliet's later governorship: Van Heutsz led the operation while Van Vliet observed from a watchtower at [[Sigli]]. When cholera broke out among the troops and Van Vliet considered postponing, Van Heutsz's insistence ("Waarom zouden wij hier blijven afwachten...") prevailed, and on [[6 August]] the bentengs at [[Peukan Baroe|Peukan Baroe]]–[[Peukan Sòt|Peukan Sòt]] were stormed and taken.

Van Vliet appreciated Van Heutsz's military judgement — consulting him on all matters concerning the [[XXII Moekims]] — but found Van Heutsz's personality a barrier to confidential relations. Van der Wijck wrote to Snouck on [[25 September]] [[1897]] that Van Vliet recognised Van Heutsz "in his full value," but that "the personality of the latter presents an obstacle to standing on a confidential footing with him," and that Van Vliet and [[Generaal Vetter|general Vetter]] had both refrained from reporting Van Heutsz's "great character defects." Van Vliet's position weakened further after Vetter's departure and the arrival of [[H.N.A. Swart|general Swart]] as army commander. On [[30 September]] [[1897]] Van Heutsz received extraordinary promotion to colonel and appointment as chief of staff, and Van Vliet reportedly told confidants that Van Heutsz would probably succeed him as governor within four months — a prediction confirmed by Van Heutsz's eventual succession in 1898.

## Significance

Van Vliet's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the *Chef van den Staf* (Chief of Staff) who combined operational planning with personal field reconnaissance — a combination which gave him an unusually comprehensive view of the Dutch war effort in the first semester of [[1896]]. His planning of the cancelled [[30 March 1896 Lamkrak operation|Lamkrak operation of 30 March 1896]] placed him at the centre of the institutional shock of Umar's defection, and his subsequent reconnaissances under fire on [[21 April]], [[30 April]] and [[4 May]] [[1896]] demonstrated a personal courage and operational engagement which the balso explicitly documents. His role as *Chef van den Staf* (Chief of Staff) of the major June operations, and his reconnaissance to the Westkust on [[12 June]] [[1896]], confirm that his responsibilities extended across the full breadth of the Dutch theatre of operations.

His significance in *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* is that of the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* (Civil and Military Governor) who closed the transitional period opened by the death of [[J. J. K. de Moulin|De Moulin]] and the second tenure of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] — the governor under whom the *pacificatie* (pacification) policy of the second semester of [[1896]] was consolidated, and under whom the Dutch forward movement was paralysed by the [[Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh (December 1896)|cholera epidemic of December 1896]]. His governorship — which would last until [[1898]], when he would be succeeded by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — was the bridge between the *pacificatie* (pacification) of the second semester of [[1896]] and the decisive *korte oorlog* phase which would be launched by Van Heutsz.

**Panglima Tibang Moehamad** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the *sjahbandar* (harbourmaster) of [[Acheh|Atjèh]] — was the principal envoy of the Atjèhnese embassy sent to the resident of [[Riouw|Riouw]] in late [[1872]] or early [[1873]], bearing a letter from the [[Sultan of Atjeh|Sultan]] requesting the postponement of the announced Dutch visit to Atjèh. The embassy — consisting of Tibang Moehamad and four [[uleebalang|oelèëbalangs]] — is, in Kreemer's narrative, a key episode in the diplomatic prelude to the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]].

## The embassy to Riouw

Kreemer records that the Indische Regeering had decided, at the end of August [[1872]], to send a peaceful embassy to Atjèh. "De vredelievende strekking der zending werd in de instructie der commissie zeer duidelijk op den voorgrond gesteld" — "The peace-loving tendency of the mission was set out very clearly in the instruction of the commission." The commissioners — the resident of Riouw D. W. Schiff, the official for the Malay language H. von de Wall, and the controleur Krayenhoff — were to arrive at least two months after advance notice had been given to the Sultan. This was done at the end of October; the letter was received and would later be answered.

But the mission never fulfilled its charge. "In dezelfde maand, dat ze zich daarvan kwijten zou, vervoegde zich een Atjèhsch gezantschap (bestaande uit den sjahbandar van Atjèh Panglima Tibang Moehamad en vier oelèëbalang's) bij den resident van Riouw met een brief van den Soeltan, waarin verzocht werd het aangekondigd bezoek nog eenige maanden uit te stellen" — "In the same month in which it would discharge itself of the task, an Atjèhnese embassy (consisting of the sjahbandar of Atjèh Panglima Tibang Moehamad and four oelèëbalangs) presented itself to the resident of Riouw with a letter from the Sultan, in which was requested that the announced visit be postponed for some months."

## The Sultan's letter

The Sultan's letter — quoted by Kreemer — gave his reason: "want het is nu een maand geleden, dat ik een brief gezonden heb aan den Soeltan van Turkije, waarop ik nog geen antwoord heb ontvangen. Wanneer ik eenig antwoord van den Soeltan van Turkije ontvang, zal ik den Grooten Heer een brief schrijven" — "for it is now a month since I sent a letter to the Sultan of Turkey, to which I have not yet received an answer. When I receive an answer from the Sultan of Turkey, I will write a letter to the Great Lord [i.e., the Dutch sovereign]."

## The discovery at Singapore

The Indische Regeering granted the postponement. While the Atjèhnese envoys were still at Riouw, the Regeering learned that in early [[January 1873]] an Atjèhnese delegate had been sent to the French government seeking help. "De gezanten hieromtrent ondervraagd toonden zich 'ondubbelzinnig ten hoogste verbaasd'" — "The envoys, questioned on this, showed themselves 'unequivocally most astonished.'" A Dutch warship carried the embassy back to [[Singapore|Singapore]] and thence to Atjèh at the end of January [[1873]]. After their departure from Singapore it emerged that the envoys had visited the consuls of [[United States of America|America]] and [[Italy|Italy]] "hun voorstellende tractaten met Atjèh te sluiten, omdat Atjèh door Nederland bedreigd werd" — "proposing to them to conclude treaties with Atjèh, because Atjèh was threatened by the Netherlands." Kreemer notes that Tibang Moehamad's biography was published by J. A. Vink in the *Indisch Militair Tijdschrift* of [[1892]], part II.

## Significance

The Tibang Moehamad embassy is, in Kreemer's narrative, the last Atjèhnese diplomatic initiative before the war. The Sultan's letter — with its revelation that he had written to the [[Sultan of Turkey|Sultan of Turkey]] seeking help against the Dutch — convinced the Indische Regeering that further negotiation was pointless, and led directly to the dispatch of [[F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen|F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen]] as Regeeringscommissaris and the [[Declaration of war on Atjèh (26 March 1873)|declaration of war]] of [[26 March]] [[1873]].

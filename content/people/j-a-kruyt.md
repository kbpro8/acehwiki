**J. A. Kruyt** — also rendered **J. A. Kruijt**, with the OCR-garbled form **J. A. Kniijt** also occurring in the text — was a Dutch author whose work *Atjeh en de Atjehers* ("Aceh and the Acehnese") is cited repeatedly by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in Vol. I of *The Achehnese* as one of the standard sources on the country and people of [[Aceh]]. His name appears in the footnotes of the Introduction as one of the small body of Dutch writers whose observations on Acehnese life and history had been published before Snouck Hurgronje's own investigations.

## The Work: *Atjeh en de Atjehers*

*Atjeh en de Atjehers* is the work by which Kruyt is known to scholarship. Cited in three distinct connections by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in Vol. I of *The Achehnese* — on the letters patent issued under the Sultan's nine-fold seal (p. 58), on the weight to be attached to civil wars in Aceh (p. 144), and on Acehnese material culture and weapons (p. 56) — the work is treated as a standard reference for the formal documents, political history, and material culture of [[Aceh]].

## Citations in *The Achehnese*

### Letters patent and the ninefold seal

The first specific citation of Kruyt in Vol. I occurs in the chapter on the official documents of the Acehnese sultanate, where [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] refers to *Atjeh en de Atjehers* (p. 58) in connection with the formal letters patent issued under the Sultan's nine-fold seal. The reference occurs in the long note appended to Snouck Hurgronje's analysis of the letters patent given to the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]]. This document, composed in a mixture of Malay and bad Arabic, opens with a long invocation of blessings — on the [[Muhammad (prophet)|Prophet]], on his four companions [[Abu Bakr]], [[Omar]], [[Uthman]], and [[Ali]], on "the glorious Pole of the world" [[Abdul-Qadir Jilani|Shaikh Abdul-Qadir Jilani]], and on the whole line of deceased sultans whose names also appear in the *chab sikureu'eng* (the nine-fold seal). It is dated Monday, the 4th of Rabi' al-Awwal 1307 Hijrah — equivalent to the [[28 October]] [[1889]] — and is issued in the name of [[Sultan Alaedin Muhamad Daud Shah Juhan|Sultan Alaedin Muhamat Dawot Shah Juhan]] by his "Keureukon Katiboy Muluk" or royal secretary (see [[Keureukon Katiboy Muluk]]). For comparison of the formal features of such documents, Snouck Hurgronje refers the reader to Kruyt's work.

### Civil wars in Aceh

In a footnote relating to the [[Aceh War|Achehnese War]], Snouck Hurgronje commends "a very correct estimate of the weight to be attached to civil wars in [[Aceh|Acheh]]" to be found in *Atjeh en de Atjehers* (p. 144). The remark bears on the essentially local and personal character of Acehnese hostilities: wars in [[Aceh]], as Snouck Hurgronje elsewhere observes, were "of small importance and little blood was spilt," and cannot be compared in scale or consequence to the wars of European powers.

### Acehnese material culture and weapons

For an account of Acehnese weapons, Snouck Hurgronje refers the reader to [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]]'s article on Aceh's west coast in the *Tijdschrift van het Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap*, together with Kruyt's *Atjeh en de Atjehers* (p. 56) and an appendix to the *Notulen van het Bataviaasch Genootschap* of [[1892]]. The same work is therefore treated as a standard reference for the description of Acehnese dress and weaponry.

## Place in the Dutch colonial literature on Aceh

Kruyt's *Atjeh en de Atjehers* belongs to the literature produced by Dutch officials and observers in the closing decades of the nineteenth century, alongside the *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* of [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]] and the writings of [[G. H. Niemann|Niemann]] and [[Coolsma]]. These works, together with the early monographs of [[L. W. C. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] and the later researches of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] himself, form the principal Dutch-language corpus on Acehnese society.

## Variant spellings

The variant spellings **Kruyt** and **Kruijt** — both of which occur in the text of *The Achehnese* — are alternative renderings of the same author's name. The OCR-garbled form **Kniijt**, which appears in the indexed Vol. I (p. 225) of *The Achehnese*, likewise refers to the same author.

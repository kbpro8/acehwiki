**The Radja van Samalangan** was the Acehnese ruler of [[Samalanga|Samalangan]] on the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] who, according to [[H. van Kol]] speaking in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[17 November]] [[1897]], was taken prisoner by the Dutch "op verraderlijke wijze" — in a treacherous manner — after having been promised rifles.

## The allegation

Van Kol's words were: "Op verraderlijke wijze nam men den radja van Samalangan gevangen, dien men geweren beloofde." — In a treacherous manner the radja of Samalangan was taken prisoner, to whom rifles had been promised. He named the case first in a series of three treacherous arrests, the others being that of the head of [[Bintara Peunang]] and, in the month before the debate, that of [[Toekoe Njak Amat]] near [[Kroeng Raja]]. The three were adduced as instances of Dutch breaches of the *[[Lois et coutumes de la guerre|lois et coutumes de la guerre]]*, alongside the shooting of [[Njahim]], the taking of the daughter of [[Wakil Joesoef]] as a hostage and the killing of [[Joed Mohmed Lamtengah]].

The source gives neither the date nor the personal name of the radja concerned. [[Samalanga|Samalangan]] had been the object of repeated Dutch expeditions from the [[Expedition to Samalanga (August 1877)|expedition of August 1877]] onward and was long a centre of resistance on the north coast.

## Reception

The Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] did not answer this allegation specifically. In his general reply he denied cruelties committed in cold blood, and on [[17 November]] associated himself with [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s statement that concretely proved cases would find no defender in any party of the chamber, quoting the words of [[Mr. J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] of [[22 September]] [[1896]] on the government's opposition to mutilation.

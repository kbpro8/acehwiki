**T. Rajoet** — also written *T. Tjihiq*, *T. Tjihik*, *Toekoe Rajoet*, modern *Teuku Rajoet* — was the hereditary territorial chief of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] whose kinship with [[Teuku Umar]] — he was Umar's brother-in-law, "*ouden broeder (eigenlijk zwager)*" (older brother, actually brother-in-law) — and whose territorial command in the southern sector of [[Groot-Atjeh]] made him, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, one of the most significant Achehnese military figures of the first semester of [[1896]]. Rajoet "lived at [[Lepong]]" — Umar's own *landschap* — and "possessed three houses (Lam-Pasei, Ba Tjiri, Tjot Loãt)", a territorial dispersion which gave him military reach across the V Moekims Montassik and the adjacent mukims. He "commanded ~500 men with 200 *geweren*" at [[Adjé]], and the "*Toekoe Rajoet*" fort near [[Kajoe Koenjit]] was named after him — a *benteng* which was stormed by the *maréchaussée* brigades Türpitz and Reis during the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik on 16 June 1896]].

## Kinship with Umar and Residence at Lepong

The kinship between Rajoet and Umar — explicitly identified by Kruisheer as brother-in-law through the parenthetical "*eigenlijk zwager*" — placed Rajoet within the inner kinship circle of the post-defection Achehnese coalition. His residence at [[Lepong]] (Umar's *landschap*) rather than within his own V Moekims Montassik is a strategically significant detail: it indicates that Rajoet had relocated from his territorial base to Umar's operational centre, and that his command of ~500 men with 200 *geweren* at [[Adjé]] (a kampong with a *benteng* in the southern sector) was exercised from Lepong as a base. The three houses — at [[Lam-Pasei]], [[Ba Tjiri]] and [[Tjot Loãt]] — represent the geographic spread of his territorial possessions: Lam-Pasei was the family residence of [[Toeankoe Brahim]] (another of the hostile V Moekims Montassik *bendehoofden*), Ba Tjiri was a kampong in the V Moekims Montassik, and Tjot Loãt was a kampong traversed by the Dutch *colonnes* on 16 June 1896. The combination of kinship with Umar, residence at Lepong, and dispersed territorial possessions gave Rajoet a uniquely central position within the Achehnese coalition of the spring of 1896 — he was simultaneously Umar's brother-in-law, a territorial chief in the V Moekims Montassik, and a military commander with a substantial force at Adjé.

## Command at Adjé and the Toekoe Rajoet Fort

The most concrete military detail which Kruisheer records about Rajoet is his command of "approximately 500 men with 200 *geweren*" at [[Adjé]] — a force which represented one of the largest single Achehnese concentrations in the V Moekims Montassik. The 200 *geweren* out of 500 men — a 40% firearms ratio — was substantial by Achehnese standards and suggests that Rajoet's force was among the better-equipped of the Achehnese bands; the *benteng* at Adjé itself had *versterkingen* at its north and south ends, and it was a key objective of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 16 June 1896]]. The "*Toekoe Rajoet*" fort near [[Kajoe Koenjit]] — named after Rajoet himself — was a separate *benteng* which was stormed by the *maréchaussée* brigades Türpitz and Reis during the same operation, and its fall represented the destruction of one of the principal Achehnese fortified positions in the southern sector. Kruisheer does not record Rajoet's own fate in the engagement, but the storming of his namesake fort and the broader Achehnese retreat from the V Moekims Montassik in mid-June 1896 indicate that his territorial command was substantially degraded by the end of the first semester of 1896.

## Significance

T. Rajoet's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the kinship-territory-command nexus which characterised the Achehnese coalition at its strongest — the *hoeloebalang* who was simultaneously Umar's brother-in-law, a territorial chief with three dispersed residences, and a military commander with a substantial force at Adjé and a namesake fort at Kajoe Koenjit. The fall of the Toekoe Rajoet fort to the *maréchaussée* brigades Türpitz and Reis on 16 June 1896 represented one of the most concrete Dutch tactical successes of the southern sector *tuchtiging*, and the degradation of Rajoet's command was a key strategic outcome of the mid-June operations.

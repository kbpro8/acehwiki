The great mystic saint, died A.D. [[1182]], a younger contemporary of Abdul-qadir Jilani and founder of the wide-spread Rifa'iyyah order. He is revered in Acheh, where his name is invoked in the rapa'i performance — a self-wounding dervish display whose performers claim descent from him, though it has degenerated into theatrical conjuring.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Ahmad Rifa'i

**Ahmad Rifa'i** was a master of mysticism (*tasawwuf*) from whom the Achehnese religious performances called [[Rapa'i]] take their name, and who is "of course always invoked" at those performances. He is named by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] alongside the great founders of mystic orders, of whom the most celebrated in Acheh is [[Abdul-Qadir Jilani]].

The *Rapa'i* (the Achehnese form of the Arabic *rabā'ī*, the "fourth" [of the mystic recitations]) are religious performances in which the performers wound themselves with knives, sear their bodies with red-hot chains, and similar feats, while the bystanders chant religious formulas. They belong to the same class of Achehnese religious phenomena as the *like* and *rateb* (the equivalents of the Arabic *dikr* and *ratib*), and the *sadati-pantons*. Snouck Hurgronje groups them with those "curious proceedings which in Acheh are included under the terms *like* and *rateb*", at which "the help of these holy men and especially of [[Abdul-Qadir Jilani|Abdul-Qadir Jilani]] is invoked".

Ahmad Rifa'i is described as a "master of mysticism" — that is, a teacher or eponym of a tariqah (mystic order) — rather than as a departed *wali* (saint) in the strict Achehnese sense. The tariqahs, as Snouck Hurgronje observes, "so popular in other parts of Sumatra do not flourish in Acheh, though sundry Achehnese hajis have enrolled themselves as members of a tariqah in [[Mecca]]". The spiritual successors of the founders of mystic orders in Acheh, who teach mystic practices and formulas, "also enjoy much respect, but are not very numerous". The invocation of Ahmad Rifa'i at the Rapa'i performances is thus one of the comparatively rare survivals of organised mystic practice in Achehnese religious life.

The name Ahmad Rifa'i has been variously associated in the broader Mohammedan world with several figures, including Ahmad al-Rifaʿī (d. [[1182]] CE), the eponymous founder of the Rifaʿiyya tariqah whose disciples are famous in many lands for their fire-handling and self-wounding demonstrations. The Achehnese Rapa'i performances described by Snouck Hurgronje — in which the performers "wound themselves with knives" and "sear their bodies with red-hot chains" — strongly suggest an Achehnese survival or adaptation of the Rifaʿī dhikr.

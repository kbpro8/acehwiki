**Mohammed Aroef** was the *radja* of [[Singkel]] on the western coast of [[Sumatra]] who in [[1838]], when Dutch warships went out to extend Dutch power at [[Baros]], [[Tapoes]] and Singkel, appealed to the Dutch to leave him to live on in peace. His words were quoted by [[H. van Kol]] in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]] as an illustration of the character of Dutch expansion at the expense of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and its dependencies.

## The appeal of [[1838]]

Van Kol related that in [[1838]] warships went out to extend Dutch power in Baros, Tapoes and Singkel. The radja of Singkel, Mohammed Aroef, requested to be left to live on in rest, for — in the words attributed to him — *"wij zijn een vrij volk en doen niemand kwaad" — we are a free people and do no one harm* (we are a free people and do no one harm). He also found it *"zeer onbillijk, zich eens anders goed toe te eigenen, alleen omdat men meer geld en meer volk voor den oorlog heeft" — most unjust, to appropriate another's property merely because one has more money and more people for war* (most unjust to appropriate another's property merely because one has more money and more people for war).

But to an appeal to right and equity, Van Kol said, the Dutch government remained deaf: [[Baros]] was occupied in [[1839]] and Singkel in [[1840]].

## Aftermath

In [[1848]] the Acehnese attempted to retake Singkel from the Dutch but were not strong enough for it. Fighting occurred in [[1848]]\u2013[[1849]] and again in [[1851]], and a treaty was concluded with [[Troemon]] in order to make it independent of Atjeh — that is, in Van Kol's characterisation, to raise it in revolt against its liege lord. A further expedition was sent to Singkel in [[1865]].

## Significance

Mohammed Aroef's appeal is one of the few indigenous voices from the western dependencies of Atjeh preserved verbatim in the Dutch parliamentary record of the war. Van Kol used it, alongside the passage from [[E. B. Kielstra|Kielstra]]'s *[[Kielstra's Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog (1883-1885)|Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog]]*, to establish that the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]] was the culmination of a half-century of Dutch encroachment rather than a response to Acehnese aggression.

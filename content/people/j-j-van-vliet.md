**J. J. van Vliet** was a Dutch colonial officer who served as colonel and governor of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from [[8 November]] [[1896]] to [[1898]], succeeding the interim administration after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[1896]]. Van Vliet's governorship was marked by strained relations with [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], the conduct of the [[Lhong expedition (January 1897)|Lhong expedition]] and the [[Sigli expedition (August 1897)|Sigli expedition]], and his gradual displacement as [[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]]'s confidence shifted toward [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as the future governor of Atjeh.

## Appointment and arrival

On [[22 September]] [[1896]], [[C. A. van der Wijck|the landvoogd Van der Wijck]] wrote to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] that Van Vliet would have to declare, before his appointment, "that he with full conviction accepts the present situation with all its consequences." Van Vliet arrived at [[Oeléëlheu|Oeléëlheu]] on [[8 November]] [[1896]] and took over command and administration on [[17 November]], declaring that he would "proceed on the path now taken, and relentlessly pursue the hostile bands, in order thereby to protect the peace-wishing and well-disposed population against molestation from those bands, and gradually to put an end to the resistance against our authority."

## First political report and the Lhong expedition

Van Vliet's first political report was not cheerful. The governor "saw and painted clouds" — and clouds were indeed present in December and early January: the weather was poor, the terrain muddy, and cholera cases appeared. Military action slackened and the enemy raised its head again. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had predicted it on [[12 September]]: what the operation lacked under Van Vliet was offensive character. [[K. F. H. van Langen|Van Langen]] said the same thing afterward.

On [[1 January]] [[1897]], an expedition was sent to [[Lhong]] to capture [[Toekoe Oemar|Teukoe Oema]] if possible or otherwise to inflict as much damage as possible. The capture failed, and the damage was chiefly on the Dutch side. The plan had been to surprise Oema at [[Geunteuët|Geunteuët]]; but an Atjehsche *jōt* (guide) misled the column, so that Geunteuët was only reached at dawn. It proved deserted: the enemy sat in the hills behind and inflicted five dead and nine wounded. In the days following, the enemy made things worse still.

*De Locomotief* of [[16 January]] commented: "Wij zeiden het vroeger reeds: de tocht naar Lhong is mislukt. De schuld van het mislukken ligt natuurlijk niet bij den colonne-commandant [...] de schuld lag bij den gids." The expedition lasted until [[14 February]]. Oema had moved to [[Bojen-Daja]] and the troops returned to [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]]. The *keudjroečn* (district chief) of Lhong was taken along; he had cooperated insufficiently and was fined 30,000 dollars, held at [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] to stimulate payment.

[[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] defended Van Vliet in his letter to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] of [[17 February]] [[1897]], writing: "Van Vliet has, in my opinion, shown to conduct a wise policy; the troops had to be spared for health reasons, and maintaining this as governor of Atjeh as long as necessary testifies not only to moral strength but also to the awareness that nothing else could be done." Van der Wijck also expressed concern about the annual supplement of 2,000 men from Holland for [[1897]], calling it "our weak point."

## The mobile column and growing confidence

In February [[1897]], the mobile column of [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] was expanded, with troops from [[Samagani|Samahani]] placed under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. The favorable effect radiating from [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] was recorded in Van der Wijck's letter of [[17 May]] [[1897]] to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]], which also contained a significant indication of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s growing credit at Buitenzorg. On [[24 May]] [[1897]], [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] received a new royal recognition: appointment as Knight of the Military Willemsorde 3rd class, "for having distinguished himself in the military operations against Atjeh in the months March through November [[1896]]."

## The Sigli expedition (August 1897)

The [[Sigli expedition (August 1897)|Sigli expedition]] of August [[1897]] was the defining operation of Van Vliet's governorship. On [[30 July]] and [[1]]-[[2]]-[[4 August]], troops were transported from Atjeh: battalions of infantry, 12 brigades of [[Korps Marechaussee|marechaussee]], and detachments of other weapons. Their task was to cleanse the immediate surroundings of [[Sigli]]: [[Peukan Baroe|Peukan Baroe]]-[[Peukan Sòt|Peukan Sòt]]. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was to lead the operation; Van Vliet watched from an observation tower at Sigli.

During the expedition, cholera broke out among the troops, and Van Vliet considered postponing the action. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] replied: "Waarom zouden wij hier blijven afwachten, wie cholera zullen krijgen en wie niet?" — Van Heutsz's logic won over Van Vliet's prudence. When the action proceeded on [[6 August]], Van Vliet stood on a watchtower at Sigli observing how Van Heutsz conducted the operation. Van Heutsz did it well: after bombardment by warships and then by artillery from Sigli, all the bentengs were stormed and taken.

On [[15 August]], the expedition returned to [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]]. *De Locomotief* of [[14 August]] wrote: "Overste van Heutsz heeft [...] getoond, dat hij de ware manier om den Atjeher te lijf te gaan nog niet vergeten is."

## Strained relationship with Van Heutsz

The relationship between Van Vliet and Van Heutsz was consistently strained. Van Vliet appreciated Van Heutsz — as he said, he consulted Van Heutsz on all matters concerning the XXII Moekims because "he who must execute the plans should first have a voice in them, and I attach much value to Van Heutsz's judgment" — but found that Van Heutsz's personality was a barrier to confidential relations. [[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] wrote to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] on [[25 September]] [[1897]]: "I could demonstrate to you that colonel Van Vliet recognises overste Van Heutsz in his full value, but the personality of the latter presents an obstacle to standing on a confidential footing with him."

[[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] further noted that Van Heutsz owed it to Van Vliet and [[Generaal Vetter|general Vetter]] that his "great character defects" did not count against his good qualities as troop commander, for both of them had refrained from reporting those defects. Van Heutsz himself claimed he was being spied upon by Van Vliet.

The press recorded the tension. A *Java-bode* correspondent wrote from [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] on [[4 September]] [[1897]]: "Bij elke koempoelan van officieren, minus gouverneur en chef van den staf, is het Van Heutsz voor en Van Heutsz na." *De Locomotief* reported that no higher authorities attended [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s farewell; only the commander of the naval squadron came to escort him to the Pakketvaart ship.

## Departure and aftermath

Van Vliet's position weakened after [[Generaal Vetter|general Vetter]] departed and [[H.N.A. Swart|general Swart]] took over as army commander. On [[30 September]] [[1897]], [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] received extraordinary promotion to colonel and appointment as chief of staff of the Indian army. The motivation read: "for excellent military deeds performed while at Atjeh from [[5 February]] to [[26 July]] [[1897]], and in particular for his action as commander in the battles of [[6]]-[[10 August]] [[1897]] in the moekims Peukan Baroe, Peukan Sòt (Sigli)."

Van Vliet continued as governor, working with [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] (now in Batavia) on preparations for the [[Pidië-expedition (1898)|Pidië expedition]] planned for [[1898]]. Both colonels — Van Heutsz in Batavia and Van Vliet in Atjeh — prepared for the next phase. Van Vliet reportedly told confidants that Van Heutsz would probably succeed him as governor within four months.

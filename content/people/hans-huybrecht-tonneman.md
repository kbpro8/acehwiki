**Hans Huybrecht Tonneman** was a Dutch admiraal in the service of the [[Middelburgsche Compagnie|Middelburgsche Compagnie]] who, as captain of the *Middelburg* and admiraal of the fleet of "twee groote en twee kleine schepen" that left Zeeland on [[28 January]] [[1601]] and arrived off [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on [[23 August]] [[1601]], conducted the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]] that produced the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil, the unconditional release of [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] and his surviving companions, and the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits (1601)|first Achehnese embassy to the Netherlands]]. Tonneman is the principal Dutch naval commander of the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of 1601]].

## The 1601 fleet to Atjeh

Tonneman's fleet of "twee groote en twee kleine schepen" left Zeeland on [[28 January]] [[1601]] and reached Sumatra on [[23 August]] [[1601]] after a prosperous voyage via **Andschuan** (one of the [[Comoro Islands|Comoro Islands]]) and [[Ceylon|Ceylon]]. At Andschuan the local ruler gave them a letter of recommendation to the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]; at Ceylon they took on refreshment. The fleet arrived off Atjeh bearing a letter from [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]] and gifts in money and merchandise. [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]], still in captivity at Atjeh, heard the salute guns fire and "ondervond alras dat de houding van den vorst te zijnen gunste veranderde" when the sultan ignored Portuguese advice to send the Dutchmen into the mountains for safety.

## The reception and the lodge "Zeelant"

The Zeelanders were received solemnly by the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]], who ordered Houtman's chains struck off — "die hij acht maanden lang had gedragen" — and released him and his companions without ransom. The merchants [[Gerard le Roy|Gerard le Roy]] and [[Laurens Bicker|Laurens Bicker]] conducted the commercial negotiations, persuaded the sultan that [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]]'s seizure of pepper from Arab junks in January [[1601]] had exceeded his commission, and undertook to compensate the Arab claimants. The Zeelanders obtained leave to settle two commiezen and three assistants on shore at a lodge that they named **"Zeelant"** — the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil. The sultan dispatched his own embassy to Prince Maurits — the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits (1601)|Atjeh embassy of 1601]].

## The return voyage

On [[29 November]] [[29 November]] [[1601]] the two loaded ships sailed for the Netherlands with the Achehnese embassy aboard; off [[St Helena|St. Helena]] they were saluted by cannon-fire from a richly laden Portuguese galleon, which they captured after a two-day fight and brought home to the United Provinces. The two other ships of the Zeeland fleet sailed the same day for [[Bantam|Bantam]] by way of the west coast of Sumatra, where they evaded a Portuguese fleet sent against them by [[André Furtado de Mendoza|André Furtado de Mendoza]] and continued to the [[Molukken|Molukken]].

## Significance

Hans Huybrecht Tonneman is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch naval commander of the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]] and the principal Dutch figure of the diplomatic rapprochement that the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman ambush of 1599]] and the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|Van Caerden affair of 1600-1601]] had foreclosed. The mission's success — the lodge "Zeelant", the release of [[Frederik de Houtman|Houtman]], the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits (1601)|Atjeh embassy of 1601]] — was the principal Dutch diplomatic achievement of the pre-VOC period and the framework within which the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the VOC in March 1602]] would consolidate the Dutch position at Atjeh. The capture of the Portuguese galleon off [[St Helena|St. Helena]] on the return voyage was the principal naval prize of the mission and the proximate cause of the rising prestige of the Zeeland interest in the United Provinces.

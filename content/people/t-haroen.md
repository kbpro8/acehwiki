**T. Haroen** was an Acehnese resistance leader who, after the destruction of the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]]* in [[1911]], operated a resistance band "op de Noordkust" — on the North Coast of [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. Some forty *onverzoenlijken* (irreconcilables) — the survivors of the Tiriō bende — "sloot zich aan bij de bende van *[[Teukoe Dagang]]* en bij die van T. Haroen, die op de Noordkust opereerde."

## The Noordkust band

"een veertigtal onverzoenlijk en sloot zich aan bij de bende van T. Dagang en bij die van T. Haroen, die op de Noordkust opereerde." The forty irreconcilables who survived the destruction of the Tiriō bende split between the band of [[Teukoe Dagang]] (in the Tangsé area) and the band of T. Haroen (on the Noordkust). The two bands operated in different theatres: Dagang in the bergland of Tangsé, Haroen on the Noordkust.

## The pairing with Teukoe Dagang

The pairing of T. Haroen with [[Teukoe Dagang]] is the book's register of the disintegration of the Acehnese resistance after the destruction of the Tiriō bende. Where the Tiriō bende had been a coherent religious resistance under a single patriarchal family, the post-Tiriō resistance was split between regional *hoofden* like Dagang and Haroen, each operating in his own theatre. The book's quiet point is that the religious coherence of the Tiriō resistance was, after its destruction, replaced by the regional incoherence of the Dagang-Haroen resistance.

## Significance

T. Haroen is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the Acehnese resistance leader who operated a band on the Noordkust after the destruction of the Tiriō bende. The book's argument is that the post-Tiriō resistance was split between regional *hoofden* — Dagang in the Tangsé bergland, Haroen on the Noordkust — and that the religious coherence of the Tiriō resistance was, after its destruction, replaced by the regional incoherence of the Dagang-Haroen resistance.

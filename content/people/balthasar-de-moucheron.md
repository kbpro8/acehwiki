**Balthasar de Moucheron** (also written **Balthazar de Moucheron**; c. [[1552]]–[[1630]]) was a Zeeland merchant of [[Middelburg|Middelburg]] — a South-Netherlander of French descent (*Zuid-Nederlander van Fransche afstamming*) and "den ondernemenden Zeeuw" (the enterprising Zeelander) of Van der Woude's portrait — who outfitted the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage of 1599]] to [[Aceh|Atjeh]] on the ships *De Leeuw* and *De Leeuwin*, dispatched [[Joris van Spilbergen|Joris Spilbergh]]'s fleet of three ships and the yacht *'t Lam* to [[Aceh|Atjeh]] in [[1601]] (which arrived in [[1602]]), and was one of the founding *Bewindhebbers* (directors) of the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]]'s Zeeland chamber in [[1602]]. He is the principal Dutch commercial figure of the pre-VOC Atjeh trade and the central merchant of the [[Moucheron's firm|Moucheron firm]] that anchored the Zeeland interest in the early Dutch-Indies commerce.

## Background and the Pre-VOC *Voorcompagnieën*

De Moucheron's commercial career unfolded in the so-called *voorcompagnieën* (pre-VOC companies) period — the small independent Dutch trading companies that sailed to the East Indies in the years [[1595]]–[[1602]] before their consolidation into the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]]. The Moucheron firm was the principal Zeeland interest in the early Dutch-Indies trade and the chief rival of the Amsterdam companies in the pre-VOC period. Van der Woude treats Moucheron as the principal instance of the Zeeland merchant's risk-tolerance in this period: "*den ondernemenden Zeeuw*".

## The Houtman Brothers' Voyage of 1598–1599

After [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]]'s return from the first Dutch voyage to [[Bantam|Bantam]] in [[1597]] and the subsequent legal disputes, Moucheron — who "*waarschijnlijk, en tot zijn nadeel, geen geloof sloeg aan de geruchten over en processen tegen hem*" ("probably, and to his detriment, gave no credence to the rumours and proceedings against him") — gave Houtman command of two ships, *De Leeuw* and *De Leeuwin*, which left [[Vlissingen|Vlissingen]] on [[25 March]] [[1598]] with the English pilot [[John Davis|John Davis]] as *stuurman* (helmsman/sailing-master). Trusting the experience of the De Houtman brothers — Cornelis having commanded the first Dutch voyage of [[1595]]–[[1597]] — Moucheron took them both into his service. Davis served as pilot on the secret instructions of the [[Robert Devereux, 2nd Earl of Essex|Earl of Essex]], and his reports would later inform the [[English East India Company]]'s own early voyages, including [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]]'s.

The voyage to the Indies took fifteen months. The Madagascar population fled at the reappearance of the rough Dutchmen — this was the second Dutch landing there, the first having already left a bad reputation under [[Cornelis de Houtman|De Houtman]]'s own first expedition. The fleet reached Atjeh in [[June]] [[1599]], anchoring off the roadstead on [[24 June]] [[1599]]. The good understanding with the Sultan lasted only briefly. With poisoned fruits and drinks, later with a *kris*-attack, the Atjehnese sought to be rid of the foreigners. The voyage ended in the ambush of [[11 September]] [[1599]] in which many were killed — including [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] — and many taken prisoner, including [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] (who would remain captive for twenty-six months). See [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)]].

The ships that escaped Atjeh made for [[Pedir|Pedir]], where they beat off an Atjehnese naval attack; they then touched at the [[Nicobars|Nicobars]] and, after much misfortune, reached Middelburg in the summer of [[1600]]. De Moucheron suffered heavy losses from the expedition.

## The Spilbergh Fleet of 1601–1602

Moucheron then outfitted the fleet of three ships and the yacht *'t Lam* under [[Joris van Spilbergen|Joris Spilbergh]], which left Middelburg on [[5 May]] [[1601]]. Near the [[Comoro Islands|Comoros]] the yacht became separated from the others and was so damaged that [[Cornelis Spex|Cornelis Spex]] put in at Atjeh in early September [[1602]] and placed himself under [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]]'s command. Spilbergh himself arrived a few days later with the other two ships, having called at [[Ceylon|Ceylon]] on the way and conducted the first Dutch embassy to the King of Kandi. Spilbergh joined Lancaster in the [[Capture of the Portuguese carrack off Atjeh (October 1602)|capture of the Portuguese carrack of 3 October 1602]] for an eighth of the prize. The Spilbergh voyage was the closing Dutch visit to Atjeh of the pre-VOC period — and, like the opening Houtman voyage of [[1599]], it was outfitted by Moucheron.

## The Founding of the VOC (1602)

Moucheron was one of the founding *Bewindhebbers* of the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]]'s Zeeland chamber in [[1602]]. The charter of [[20 March]] [[1602]] listed him among the first Zeeland *Bewindhebbers*:

> "De Camere van Zeelandt by Adriaen Hendircxsz. ten Haeff, Jacob Boreel, Jehan Lambertsz. Coole, Jacob Pietersz. de Weert, Cornelis Meunicx, Adriaen Bommené, Laurens Bacx, Everhardt Becquer, Arnoult le Clerck, Arnoult Verhouve, Gherardt van Schoonhove, Nicolaes Pieterssz., Balthazar van Vlierden, ende Balthzar de Moucheron."

With the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the VOC]], the separate Zeeland companies — including the Moucheron firm — were wound up, and their assets and personnel were absorbed into the united company. The absorption marked the institutional end of the pre-VOC period and the beginning of the unified Dutch-Indies trade.

## John Davis and the English Spy-Work

John Davis — the English pilot who had served as *stuurman* on the Houtman voyage — turned his spy-material to English advantage. As the Dutch had once wrung the secrets of the India trade from Portugal, England now sought profit from Dutch experience. The De Moucheron expedition was a key part of that intelligence-gathering: Davis's reports would inform the [[English East India Company]]'s own early voyages, including [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]]'s. The Davis episode is a reminder that the early Dutch-Indies trade was carried on under the watchful eye of rival European powers, and that the same voyages that opened Dutch commerce with Atjeh also opened the English path to the Indian Ocean.

## Significance

For [[Aceh|Atjeh]]'s history, Balthasar de Moucheron is significant as the merchant-prince whose expedition of [[1598]] produced the [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)|first Dutch fatality at Atjeh]] and the [[Frederik de Houtman|first Dutch captivity at Atjeh]] — the two events that, taken together, set the tone for the Dutch-Atjeh relationship for the next decade. He is also a reminder that the early Dutch India trade was not exclusively Amsterdam: the South-Netherlander of French descent, operating out of Zeeland, was a typical figure of the pre-VOC *wilde vaart* (free trade) generation. In Van der Woude's narrative, Moucheron is the principal Dutch commercial figure of the pre-VOC Atjeh trade and the central merchant of the Zeeland interest in the early Dutch-Indies commerce. The [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman voyage of 1599]] — the first Dutch visit to Atjeh — was outfitted by Moucheron; the [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|Spilbergh voyage of 1601]]–[[1602]] — the closing Dutch visit to Atjeh of the pre-VOC period — was also outfitted by Moucheron; and the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the VOC in 1602]], which absorbed the Moucheron firm into the Zeeland chamber, marked the institutional end of the pre-VOC period. Moucheron's role in the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship is thus both foundational and transitional: he opened the trade and he shepherded it into the unified company that would carry it forward.

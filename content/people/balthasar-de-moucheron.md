**Balthasar de Moucheron** (c. 1552-1630) was a Zeeland merchant of [[Middelburg|Middelburg]] — "den ondernemenden Zeeuw" — who outfitted the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage of 1599]] to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on the ships *De Leeuw* and *De Leeuwin*, dispatched [[Joris Spilbergh|Joris Spilbergh]]'s fleet of three ships and the yacht *'t Lam to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1601]] (which arrived in [[1602]]), and was one of the founding Bewindhebbers of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s Zeeland chamber in [[1602]]. He is the principal Dutch commercial figure of the pre-VOC Atjeh trade and the central merchant of the Moucheron firm that anchored the Zeeland interest in the early Dutch-Indies commerce.

## The Houtman brothers' voyage of 1599

The [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage]] — the first Dutch voyage to Atjeh — was outfitted by Moucheron. After [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]]'s return from the first Dutch voyage to [[Bantam|Bantam]] in [[1597]] and the subsequent legal disputes, Moucheron — who "waarschijnlijk, en tot zijn nadeel, geen geloof sloeg aan de geruchten over en processen tegen hem" — gave Houtman command of two ships, *De Leeuw* and *De Leeuwin*, which left [[Vlissingen|Vlissingen]] on [[25 March]] [[1598]] with the English pilot [[John Davis|John Davis]] as stuurman. The ships anchored off Atjeh on [[24 June]] [[1599]]. The voyage ended in the ambush of [[11 September]] [[1599]] in which Cornelis was killed and [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik]] taken captive.

## The Spilbergh fleet of 1601

Moucheron then outfitted the fleet of three ships and the yacht *'t Lam under [[Joris Spilbergh|Joris Spilbergh]], which left Middelburg on [[5 May]] [[1601]]. Near the [[Comoro Islands|Comoros]] the yacht became separated from the others and was so damaged that [[Cornelis Spex|Cornelis Spex]] put in at Atjeh in early September [[1602]] and placed himself under [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]]'s command. Spilbergh himself arrived a few days later with the other two ships, having called at [[Ceylon|Ceylon]] on the way. Spilbergh joined Lancaster in the [[Capture of the Portuguese carrack off Atjeh (October 1602)|capture of the Portuguese carrack of 3 October 1602]] for an eighth of the prize.

## The founding of the VOC (1602)

Moucheron was one of the founding Bewindhebbers of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s Zeeland chamber in [[1602]]. The charter of [[20 March]] [[20 March]] [[1602]] listed him among the first Zeeland Bewindhebbers: "De Camere van Zeelandt by Adriaen Hendircxsz. ten Haeff, Jacob Boreel, Jehan Lambertsz. Coole, Jacob Pietersz. de Weert, Cornelis Meunicx, Adriaen Bommené, Laurens Bacx, Everhardt Becquer, Arnoult le Clerck, Arnoult Verhouve, Gherardt van Schoonhove, Nicolaes Pieterssz., Balthazar van Vlierden, ende Balthzar de Moucheron." With the founding of the VOC, the separate Zeeland companies — including the Moucheron firm — were wound up, and their assets and personnel were absorbed into the united company.

## The character of the Moucheron firm

The Moucheron firm was the principal Zeeland interest in the early Dutch-Indies trade and the chief rival of the Amsterdam companies in the pre-VOC period. Moucheron's readiness to take on Cornelis de Houtman — despite the disputes — and his outfitting of the Spilbergh fleet after the Houtman disaster show both his enterprise and his resilience. Van der Woude treats him as the principal instance of the Zeeland merchant's risk-tolerance in the pre-VOC period: "den ondernemenden Zeeuw". His firm's absorption into the VOC in [[1602]] marks the institutional end of the pre-VOC period and the beginning of the unified Dutch-Indies trade.

## Significance

Balthasar de Moucheron is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch commercial figure of the pre-VOC Atjeh trade and the central merchant of the Zeeland interest in the early Dutch-Indies commerce. The [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman voyage of 1599]] — the first Dutch visit to Atjeh — was outfitted by Moucheron; the [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|Spilbergh voyage of 1601]]-[[1602]] — the closing Dutch visit to Atjeh of the pre-VOC period — was also outfitted by Moucheron; and the founding of the VOC in [[1602]], which absorbed the Moucheron firm into the Zeeland chamber, marked the institutional end of the pre-VOC period. Moucheron's role in the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship is thus both foundational and transitional: he opened the trade and he shepherded it into the unified company that would carry it forward.

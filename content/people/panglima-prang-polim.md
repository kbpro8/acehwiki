**Panglima Prang Polim** was the former *legeraanvoerder van de Sultan* — the commander-in-chief of the Sultan of [[Atjeh|Aceh]]'s army — who eventually surrendered to *[[Kapitein Colijn|kapitein Colijn]]* and, in [[1910]]-[[1911]], was one of the three *voornaamste Atjehse hoofden* who, at the request of [[Generaal Swart]], wrote the letter urging the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō Teungkoes]] to submit. The letter was refused. His signature on that letter is the book's marker of the political closing of the Acehnese Sultanate: the Sultan's own former army commander, having come in to the Dutch, now asked the last religious resisters to come in too.

## The surrender to Colijn

Polim "zich tenslotte had gemeld bij kapitein *[[Colijn]]*." The kapitein in question is almost certainly the later Dutch statesman H. Colijn, who served in Aceh as a young officer. The phrase "zich tenslotte had gemeld" — had eventually reported — indicates that Polim held out long after the formal end of the Sultanate, and came in only when further resistance at the head of the Sultan's army was impossible. His surrender was therefore a major political event: the Sultan's legeraanvoerder acknowledging the Dutch overlord.

## The letter to the Tiriō Teungkoes

At [[Generaal Swart]]'s request, "de drie voornaamste Atjehse hoofden" wrote the Tiriō Teungkoes a letter urging submission. The three were "twee leden van de Sultansfamilie, de *[[Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala]]*, benevens Panglima Prang Polim, de vroegere legeraanvoerder van de Sultan, die zich tenslotte had gemeld bij kapitein Colijn." The letter is the book's clearest example of Swart's preferred method — to bring moral and political pressure on the remaining resisters through the most authoritative Acehnese leaders.

The Teungkoes "bespraken de brief, doch besloten tot het einde te vechten; hen zou geschieden naar Allah's wil." The refusal is doctrinal: the religious resisters would not accept the authority of the former Sultan's army commander to override their doctrine of *[[prang sabi]]*. The letter therefore failed — but its existence is the book's marker of the moment at which the formal Acehnese political establishment had come over to the Dutch side, leaving the religious resisters alone.

## Significance

Polim's significance in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is as the figure through whom the book marks the political closure of the Acehnese Sultanate. The Sultan's own army commander, having surrendered to Colijn, now urges the last resisters to surrender — and is refused on doctrinal grounds. The episode is the book's clearest statement that the Aceh War, in its last phase, was no longer a war between states (the Sultanate was gone) but a war between a colonial army and a religious doctrine.

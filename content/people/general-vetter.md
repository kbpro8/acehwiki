**General Vetter** was a Dutch colonial general of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands-Indian Army]] who, in the aftermath of the [[1896]] defection of [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] from the Dutch alliance to the Atjehnese side, was sent to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] with *onbeperkte volmacht* (unlimited authority) — a plenipotentiary military command — and "*behaalde groote voordeelen*" ("gained great advantages"), though the final subjugation of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] was reserved for his successor [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. His command is the immediate Dutch response to the Toekoe Oemar defection, and it bridges the central and final phases of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The command with unlimited authority

The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] school-history textbook of F. Roorda records Vetter's appointment in a single line: "*Generaal Vetter werd daarna met onbeperkte volmacht naar Atjeh gezonden. Hij behaalde groote voordeelen; doch pas aan van Heutz gelukte het, Groot Atjeh te onderwerpen*" ("General Vetter was thereupon sent to Atjeh with unlimited authority. He gained great advantages; but only to Van Heutsz did it succeed to subdue Groot Atjeh"). The phrase *onbeperkte volmacht* — literally "unlimited authorisation" — is the constitutional key of the command: it conferred on Vetter a plenipotentiary military authority that the regular Atjeh command did not possess, and it is the documentary mark of the political-military decision, in [[1896]] or shortly thereafter, to break the deadlock of the [[Concentration system in Atjeh|concentration system]] by a renewed offensive.

## The limits of his success

The textbook's evaluation of Vetter's command — "*groote voordeelen*" ("great advantages") — is paired with the limitation that the final subjugation of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] was reserved for [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. Vetter's command thus occupies, in the schoolroom memory, the position of the partial success that prepared the way for the definitive conquest. The schoolroom text does not specify the particular operations of Vetter's command, the territory recovered, or the engagements fought; it preserves only the qualitative judgment of "great advantages" and the chronological placement before Van Heutsz.

## Significance

Vetter's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history lies in the documentary fact that his appointment with unlimited authority, in the immediate aftermath of the [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] defection, marks the Dutch decision to break the [[Concentration system in Atjeh|concentration system]] and resume the offensive. His "great advantages" — though they did not complete the subjugation of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] — were the operational preparation for the final conquest by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. His command thus bridges the central and final phases of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], and his name — though overshadowed in the schoolroom memory by Van Heutsz's — is the documentary mark of the Dutch operational recovery of the late [[1890s]].

**Vetter** — *Luitenant-Generaal Vetter*, *J. A. Vetter* — was a Dutch lieutenant-general of the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army ([[K.N.I.L.|K.N.I.L.]]) who, in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] of [[March 1896|March]] [[1896]], was despatched to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] as *Regeeringscommissaris* (Government Commissioner) with the expedition of reinforcements from Java, and who oversaw the resumption of full Dutch military operations against the Atjehese resistance, the honourable discharge of [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]], and the counter-offensive that drove [[Teuku Umar|Teuku Umar]] from his positions and dispersed the enemy bands.

## Appointment as Regeeringscommissaris (March 1896)

When, on [[29 March]] [[29 March]] [[1896]], the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] exposed the failure of the Dutch policy of arming Atjehese auxiliaries, the Dutch posts lying outside the line were surrounded and cut off. Reinforcements had to be telegraphed for immediately to Java. These came at once. The military commander Vetter accompanied the expedition as *Regeeringscommissaris*, and Deijkerhoff was honourably relieved of his office. The reason for discharge, according to the *Java-bode*, was: the disguising of the truth in the reports.

## The Counter-Offensive (March – April 1896)

As soon as the necessary troops, despatched with great promptness from Java, had arrived, vigorous action was taken against the enemy — who stood against the Dutch in thousands, including the Sultan and numerous bands from the coasts. First, the surrounded posts were relieved, gradually dismantled, and the troops concentrated within the line. Then Toekoe Oemar was driven from his positions. And finally, everywhere outside the line, the enemy bands were defeated and scattered and the possessions of the Dutch's opponents destroyed.

## The Army's Reputation Restored

The army had gloriously maintained its old renown and shown that the Dutch still always possessed the power to punish. The terror of Dutch arms was again mightily felt among the Atjehers, and in short time the Dutch's shaken prestige was restored. Many of the enemy fell, and among them prominent and influential chiefs and leaders. Even the Sultan nearly fell into Dutch hands. But the Dutch too suffered heavy losses in the fighting. The taming of known hotbeds of resistance, the clearing of enemy strongholds, and the dispersal of armed bands continued.

## The Question of "What Now?"

Kruijt's brochure closes with the question that Vetter's appointment as *Regeeringscommissaris* raised but did not answer: "*Wat nu? of Wat dan?*" (What now? or What then?). The events in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] after the defection of Toekoe Oemar the reader may take as known. Kruijt's *Besluit* (Conclusion) is the prescription for Vetter's future conduct of the war: the establishment of a war footing; the maintenance of the Dutch *stelling* in Groot-Atjeh with active defence; the rigorous and uncompromising application of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] on the coasts; and the avoidance of all concessions, licences, and "special friends" until complete submission had been obtained.

## Significance

Vetter's appointment as *Regeeringscommissaris* in [[March 1896|March]] [[1896]] marks the end of the Dutch experiment with Atjehese auxiliaries and the resumption of full Dutch military operations. The counter-offensive he oversaw in the spring of [[1896]] — driving Toekoe Oemar from his positions, relieving the surrounded posts, and dispersing the enemy bands — restored the prestige of Dutch arms that the defection of Toekoe Oemar had shattered, and prepared the way for the systematic pacification that [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] would carry through after [[1898]]. His appointment also marked the displacement of the civil-and-military governor system: Vetter as *Regeeringscommissaris* superseded the Governor of Atjeh and reported directly to the Governor-General in Batavia, an arrangement that anticipated the centralised command structure under which the final pacification would be conducted.

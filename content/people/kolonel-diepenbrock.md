**Colonel Diepenbrock** was a Dutch *overste* (colonel) of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who commanded the column that conquered [[Lemboe]] and [[Berauw]] on [[7 November]] [[1874]] in the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]], the operation that finally restored to Dutch hands the kampong briefly held and "onbegrijpelijk genoeg" abandoned on Christmas [[1873]]. He was wounded in the action, in which, contrary to expectation, the enemy's resistance was light.

## The Conquest of Lemboe

Six weeks after the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] of [[28 September]] [[1874]], [[Generaal Pel|Pel]] again marched out against the kampongs Lemboe, Berauw and [[Kota Alam]]. Lemboe — "the key to the dike" along the [[Pedir-dijk|Pedir-dijk]] — had been in Dutch hands on [[25 December|25]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]] during the [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|bloody reconnaissances]] and was then immediately abandoned by [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s order, a major error compounded when the same ground had to be re-fought eleven months later. The enemy had since heavily fortified the kampong. At four o'clock on the morning of [[7 November]] [[1874]] two half-battalions and a field-artillery battery under Diepenbrock marched. The attack came from [[Penajoeng]] and Langkroek across the open *sawah*. Contrary to expectation, the resistance was light. The western part of Lemboe (Berauw) with the main dike fortifications fell, and the operation cost only Diepenbrock and twenty men or coolies wounded, and one officer's servant killed.

## Borel's Judgement

The conquest of Lemboe is, in [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s narrative, an exemplary Pel operation: bold, well-planned, and cheaply bought. Three posts were immediately *opgericht* on the conquered ground. But the action also underlines Borel's indictment of van Swieten: had Lemboe been held in December [[1873]] instead of abandoned, the entire [[Pedir-dijk]] could have been made secure, the enemy's first line of investment around [[Kota Radja]] would have been broken nine months earlier, and the second conquest of the kampong would not have been necessary. Diepenbrock's wound is, in this reading, one of the "wrange vruchten" of the *afwachtende houding*.

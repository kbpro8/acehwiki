**Generaal Diemont** was a Dutch *generaal* of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who served as *bevelhebber in Atjeh* under whose administration the sea-expedition to the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raya]] was undertaken by [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]] in [[1876]]. In [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s account he appears principally as the superior officer under whom the eastern, sea-borne half of [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]]'s secret *afsluitingslinie* was attempted — and failed of its object.

## The Kroeng Raya Sea-Expedition

Pel's plan, conceived at his [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Buitenzorg planning session]] of July [[1875]] and endorsed by [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]], had been to extend the Dutch line from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast — already reached by the column that marched through the [[Blang Kalla]] kloof on [[15 January]] [[1876]] — to the mouth of the Kroeng Raya on the north coast, so closing Atjeh from the sea. After Pel's [[Death of Generaal Pel (24 February 1876)|sudden death at Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]] and the reversion of his successor [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] to the *afwachtende houding*, the closure was to be attempted from the sea. Under Diemont's *bestuur* Van der Heijden *debarkeerde* a Dutch force at the mouth of the Kroeng Raya; the troops, Borel concedes, *dapper gestreden* have. But the *tocht* "leidde tot geene bevredigende uitkomst," and it did not link up with the Tongah–[[Kwala Gigieng]] line that [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|lt.-kol. Engel]] had reached on [[26 February]] [[1876]].

## Significance

The failure of the Kroeng Raya expedition under Diemont meant that the Pel plan was never fully executed. The western end of the line at Loenga on the Kroeng Raba was held, and the north-eastern end at Kwala Gigieng was secured by Engel's post and by the island post at [[Kota Pohama]] in the [[Gigieng lagune|Gigieng lagune]], but the long stretch of coast between them — including the mouth of the [[Kroeng Tjoet]], which the Atjehneezen used as a contraband supply channel to the [[XXVI Moekims]] — remained only partially closed. Borel's broader judgement falls not on Diemont personally but on the *afwachtende* system under which, after Pel's death, every opportunity to complete the closure was allowed to slip.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In the [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] places Diemont as the fourth name in his catalogue of the eleven Governors of Atjeh — *van Swieten, Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont, van der Heijden, Pruijs van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias, Demmeni, van Teijn, Pompe van Meerdervoort, Deijkerhof* — and the Governor who, within days of his November [[1876]] appointment, sent the military expedition that forced [[Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh]] to recognise Dutch sovereignty, and who, in the beginning of [[1877]], put the last hand to the [[Pel (governor)|Pel]] plan's coastal line before being evacuated sick on [[30 June]] [[1877]] and ceding the governorship to [[Van der Heijden|kolonel K. van der Heijden]].

### Succession of Wiggers van Kerchem (November 1876)

Diemont's appointment was the direct consequence of the dismissal of his predecessor. [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Generaal-Majoor Wiggers van Kerchem]] had refused to send Dutch troops to the support of [[Kerti]] in its war with [[Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh]], preferring to arm and subsidise [[Tekoe Moeda Augkasa]] of [[Merbou]] as a proxy. [[van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]], who "door het met de wapenen dwingen van erkenning onzer souvereiniteit, hoopte de voor die weerspannige staatjes nog steeds voortdurende dure blokkade te kunnen opheffen" (by forcing recognition of our sovereignty with weapons, hoped to be able to lift the costly blockade that had been continuously maintained against those recalcitrant statelets), replaced Wiggers van Kerchem with Diemont precisely so that the armed coercion could be carried out. Van Heutsz records the succession in one sentence that fixes both the dismissal and the appointment:

> "De Gouverneur-Generaal echter, die, door het met de wapenen dwingen van erkenning onzer souvereiniteit, hoopte de voor die weerspannige staatjes nog steeds voortdurende dure blokkade te kunnen opheffen, liet toen den Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem vervangen door den Generaal Diemont, die, onmiddelljkk na zijn op- treden in November 1876, door het zenden van eene militaire expeditie, Simpang Olim en Tandjong Semantoh, tot erkenning van onze souvereiniteit dwong en tot regeling van de destursaangllgenneeenn daar een controleur plaatste, gedekt en gesteund door een militaire bezetting." — The Governor-General, however, who, by forcing recognition of our sovereignty with weapons, hoped to be able to lift the costly blockade maintained against those recalcitrant statelets, then had Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem replaced by Generaal Diemont, who, immediately upon taking office in November 1876, by sending a military expedition, forced Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh to recognise our sovereignty and to regulate their relations, placing a *controleur* there, covered and supported by a military garrison.

Diemont's first act as Governor was thus the [[Simpang Olim expedition (November 1876)|expedition against Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh]] — the very operation Wiggers van Kerchem had declined to undertake. The statelets were forced to recognise Dutch sovereignty; a *controleur* was installed to regulate their affairs, supported by a military garrison.

### The completion of Pel's plan (early 1877)

Diemont's second achievement as Governor was the completion of the [[Pel (governor)|Pel]] plan's coastal line in the beginning of [[1877]]. Pel had died in the early months of [[1876]], before his plan — the occupation of the entire coastal strip of [[Groot-Atjeh]] from [[Kroeng Raja]] to [[Kroeng Raba]] and the closing of the five *kwala's* (Loé, [[Gighen]], Atjeh, Tjangkoel and Pantjoer) — had been fully carried out. Van Heutsz's verdict on the strategic delay between Pel's death and the plan's completion under Diemont is the closest he comes to a measured assessment of Wiggers van Kerchem's interregnum:

> "Door 's Generaals ontijdigen dood evenwel, kwam er groote vertraging in de uitvoering van zijn plan, waaraan wat Groot-Atjeh betreft, eerst de laatste hand werd gelegd in het begin van 1877 onder Generaal Diemont, terwijl op de kusten geen posten werden opgericht te Gighen en te Melaboeh, zooals in het plan lag." — Through the general's untimely death, however, great delay entered the execution of his plan, to which, as regards Groot-Atjeh, the last hand was only laid in the beginning of 1877 under Generaal Diemont, while on the coasts no posts were erected at Gighen and at Melaboeh, as the plan had called for.

The plan was substantially complete by February [[1877]] — "Men dacht toen nog altijd, dat het door het bezetten van Koeala Loé aan den vijand te Groot-Atjeh zoo goed als onmogelijk was geworden" (it was still thought then, that by the occupation of Koeala Loé it had become virtually impossible for the enemy in Groot-Atjeh to obtain supplies) — but the posts that had been planned for [[Gighen]] on the [[Noordkust]] and [[Melaboeh]] on the [[Westkust]] were never established under Diemont's governorship. The Pel plan's coastal line was complete in its essentials; it was not complete in its details.

### The 1877 strategic inheritance

Diemont's governorship fell in the same months as G.-G. van Lansberge's *verzoeningspolitiek* (conciliation policy) of March [[1877]] — the policy of conciliation in [[Groot-Atjeh]] combined with armed coercion in the recalcitrant Onderhoorigheden. Van Heutsz summarises the hybrid policy that Diemont was sent to execute: "Het stelsel: Verzoenende politiek op Groot-Atjeh en in de Onderhoorigheden, die ons gezag erkend hadden en met ons bevriend waren; door gewapend optreden dwingen tot onderwerping van de nog weerspannige Onderhoorigheden" — The system: conciliatory politics in Groot-Atjeh and in the Onderhoorigheden that had recognised our authority and were friendly to us; by armed intervention to force the submission of the still-recalcitrant Onderhoorigheden. The policy opened with the [[Telok-Kroet]] negotiations of [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polem]] and [[Tekoe Moeda Baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]] — negotiations Van Heutsz reads as insincere, designed only "om tijd te winnen" (to gain time) — and produced, in October [[1877]], van Lansberge's letter to the Governor of Atjeh announcing that "Naar alle kenteekenen te oordeelen, is in de Atjehsche zaak thans een belangrijk keerpunt bereikt" (Judging by all signs, an important turning-point has now been reached in the Atjeh affair). The "keerpunt" proved illusory: the submissions of [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polem]] and [[Tekoe Moeda Baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]] were not made, and the [[1878]] *inval* (raid) of [[Habib Abdoel Rachman]] would expose the failure of the conciliation policy.

### Medical evacuation (30 June 1877) and succession by Van der Heijden

Diemont's governorship ended with his medical evacuation on [[30 June]] [[1877]]. Van Heutsz's footnote records the succession in a single sentence — the only place in the 1893 tract where Diemont's departure is dated:

> "(1) Hier was intusschen de Generaal Diemont, die 30 Juni 1877 wegens ziekte geevacueerd werd, eerst tijdelijk en later definitief in het bestuur vervangen door kolonel K. v. d. Heijden." — Here meanwhile the Generaal Diemont, who was evacuated on 30 June 1877 because of illness, was first temporarily and later definitively replaced in the administration by colonel K. v. d. Heijden.

The succession was at first *tijdelijk* (temporary) and only later *definitief* (definitive). The new Governor — [[Van der Heijden|kolonel K. van der Heijden]], the future conqueror of the [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] — would inherit, with the office, the *verzoeningspolitiek* that Diemont had been sent to execute and the [[Pel (governor)|Pel]] plan's coastal line that Diemont had substantially completed. Diemont's seven months as Governor had carried out the armed coercion of the east coast and finished the coastal closure of Groot-Atjeh; he left the office before either policy could be tested against the resistance of the [[Sagi]] federations.

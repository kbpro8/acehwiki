A religious leader of Samalanga who, after Teungku Tiro's death, obtained a letter under the royal seal authorizing his leadership of the holy war. He imposed strict seclusion penalties on followers who entered Dutch-controlled territory and fought alongside the followers of Mat Amin and Teungku Kuta Karang.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Habib Samalanga

**Habib Samalanga** was a sayyid religious leader, born in [[Acheh]] and therefore of less consideration than those sayyids who came direct from Arabia, who came to [[Great Acheh]] during the Dutch-Achehnese War to take part in the direction of the jihad, especially in the [[XXVI Mukims]]. He became the chief rival of [[Teungku Tiro (Muhamat Amin)|Teungku Tiro]] for the leadership of the religious party, and after Teungku Tiro's death in January [[1891]] obtained from the court at [[Keumala]] a nine-fold seal (*chab sikureung*) similar to that which had been in the possession of the deceased *ulama*. This symbol of authority, however, could not raise the sayyid to the elevation which Teungku Tiro had attained without any such symbol.

Habib Samalanga's learning must have been below the average and would never have excited remark had it not been for his religious nobility. Just like Teungku Tiro in his sphere of action, he gathered in the *ka sabil* and other contributions to meet the expenses of the war. In order to appear in the eyes of his followers as at least the equal — and if possible the superior — of "the Teungku," he had to distinguish himself from the latter in some particular way. This he did by the practice of **tapa** — seclusion from the world. He would remain isolated (*kaleuiit*) for from seven to forty successive days in a cave at the source of the Krueng (river) Daroy, a place which had from ancient times been chosen for such devotions. His followers also had occasionally to submit to a similar course of *tapa*; it was in fact one of the ordinary punishments imposed by him for various offences.

A Javanese whose tomb is now an object of veneration at [[Ulee Lheue]], known by the name of [[Teungku Lam Guha|Teungku Lam Guha]], had formerly done *tapa* for successive years in this same cave of the Daroy, so that Habib Samalanga's choice of the spot placed him in a tradition of hermitical sanctity already established in Achehnese memory.

Habib Samalanga also differed from Teungku Tiro in his opinions respecting those Achehnese who either lived within the Dutch *linie* or went thither to trade. During the greater portion of the period of his activity Teungku Tiro declared such persons to be little better than unbelievers, and did not raise a hand in protest when his troops robbed them of their property or even of their life — refraining, however, from giving open orders in this spirit, since he would have had in that case to reckon with *uleebalangs* of distinction who were guilty of the same offence. It was only when [[Teuku Uma]] had convinced him that it was in many respects desirable for the chiefs and notables within the *linie* to keep in touch with the court, that Teungku Tiro at length changed his tactics and received the Keumala pilgrims in a friendly manner. Habib Samalanga, on the other hand, **consistently taught that all submission to or intercourse with the infidels was a sin**, though not one which made the offender a complete outlaw. Whoever was brought before him convicted of this sin was condemned to isolation in the cave, there to do penance for some days and prepare himself for conversion from his heresy.

After Teungku Tiro's death the friends of Habib Samalanga succeeded in obtaining for him from the court the nine-fold seal — the *chab sikureung* — similar to that which had been in the possession of the deceased *ulama*. But the symbol could not by itself confer the authority: the sayyid never attained the elevation which Teungku Tiro had reached without any such symbol. Habib Samalanga died in December [[1901]], closing the chapter of sayyid-led resistance in Great Acheh.

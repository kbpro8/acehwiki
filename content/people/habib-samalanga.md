A religious leader of Samalanga who, after Teungku Tiro's death, obtained a letter under the royal seal authorizing his leadership of the holy war. He imposed strict seclusion penalties on followers who entered Dutch-controlled territory and fought alongside the followers of Mat Amin and Teungku Kuta Karang.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Habib Samalanga

**Habib Samalanga** was a sayyid religious leader, born in [[Acheh]] and therefore of less consideration than those sayyids who came direct from Arabia, who came to [[Great Acheh]] during the Dutch-Achehnese War to take part in the direction of the jihad, especially in the [[XXVI Mukims]]. He became the chief rival of [[Teungku Tiro (Muhamat Amin)|Teungku Tiro]] for the leadership of the religious party, and after Teungku Tiro's death in January [[1891]] obtained from the court at [[Keumala]] a nine-fold seal (*chab sikureung*) similar to that which had been in the possession of the deceased *ulama*. This symbol of authority, however, could not raise the sayyid to the elevation which Teungku Tiro had attained without any such symbol.

Habib Samalanga's learning must have been below the average and would never have excited remark had it not been for his religious nobility. Just like Teungku Tiro in his sphere of action, he gathered in the *ka sabil* and other contributions to meet the expenses of the war. In order to appear in the eyes of his followers as at least the equal — and if possible the superior — of "the Teungku," he had to distinguish himself from the latter in some particular way. This he did by the practice of **tapa** — seclusion from the world. He would remain isolated (*kaleuiit*) for from seven to forty successive days in a cave at the source of the Krueng (river) Daroy, a place which had from ancient times been chosen for such devotions. His followers also had occasionally to submit to a similar course of *tapa*; it was in fact one of the ordinary punishments imposed by him for various offences.

A Javanese whose tomb is now an object of veneration at [[Ulee Lheue]], known by the name of [[Teungku Lam Guha|Teungku Lam Guha]], had formerly done *tapa* for successive years in this same cave of the Daroy, so that Habib Samalanga's choice of the spot placed him in a tradition of hermitical sanctity already established in Achehnese memory.

Habib Samalanga also differed from Teungku Tiro in his opinions respecting those Achehnese who either lived within the Dutch *linie* or went thither to trade. During the greater portion of the period of his activity Teungku Tiro declared such persons to be little better than unbelievers, and did not raise a hand in protest when his troops robbed them of their property or even of their life — refraining, however, from giving open orders in this spirit, since he would have had in that case to reckon with *uleebalangs* of distinction who were guilty of the same offence. It was only when [[Teuku Uma]] had convinced him that it was in many respects desirable for the chiefs and notables within the *linie* to keep in touch with the court, that Teungku Tiro at length changed his tactics and received the Keumala pilgrims in a friendly manner. Habib Samalanga, on the other hand, **consistently taught that all submission to or intercourse with the infidels was a sin**, though not one which made the offender a complete outlaw. Whoever was brought before him convicted of this sin was condemned to isolation in the cave, there to do penance for some days and prepare himself for conversion from his heresy.

After Teungku Tiro's death the friends of Habib Samalanga succeeded in obtaining for him from the court the nine-fold seal — the *chab sikureung* — similar to that which had been in the possession of the deceased *ulama*. But the symbol could not by itself confer the authority: the sayyid never attained the elevation which Teungku Tiro had reached without any such symbol. Habib Samalanga died in December [[1901]], closing the chapter of sayyid-led resistance in Great Acheh.

## Account from Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — Habib Samalanga in the Spring of 1896

In Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, **Habib Samalanga** is identified as a [[Habib (concept)|Habib]] — a descendant of the Prophet Muhammad — whose trajectory illustrates the pattern by which initially-neutral religious figures were drawn into the Achehnese resistance in the wake of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. Kruisheer records that Samalanga was "initially neutral in early May [[1896]]" — one of a list of chiefs (alongside [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]], [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]], [[Toekoe Moesa Anak Baté]], [[Toekoe Kerkoen]], [[Teungkoe Soepi]], [[Teungkoe Lam-Oee]] and others) whose neutrality was documented in the spring [[1896]] Dutch intelligence picture — but that by mid-June [[1896]] he was a *bendehoofd* (band leader) in the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and "one of the hostile chiefs whose fortifications provoked the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|16–17 June 1896 punitive expedition]]". Kruisheer also records that he "reportedly nested himself in [[Lamsoet]] with 50–60 men", a defensive posture which the Dutch *tuchtiging* (punitive expedition) of mid-June [[1896]] was intended to dislodge.

### From Neutrality to Bendehoofd in the V Moekims Montassik

The transition from "initially neutral" to "bendehoofd in the [[V Moekims Montassik]]" in the space of approximately six weeks (early May to mid-June [[1896]]) reflects the gravitational pull which Umar's defection exerted on the religious and political elites of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The [[V Moekims Montassik]] — the southernmost of the western *sagi* (territorial quarter) of Groot-Atjeh — was, by mid-June [[1896]], the principal concentration of hostile band leaders in the Dutch theatre of operations; Kruisheer's list of these chiefs includes, in addition to Samalanga, [[T. Brahim Montassik|Toekoe Brahim]], [[Pang Arab]], [[Panglima Nja Makam]], [[Pang Sech]], and the *Teungkoes* [[Teungkoe Soepi|Soepi]], [[Teungkoe Loeboek|Loeboek]] and [[Teungkoe Lam-Oee|Lam-Oee]] — eight *bendehoofden* in total. Samalanga's presence in this list, given his Habib status, indicates that the religious authority of the Prophet's descendants was actively being mobilised in support of the resistance, and his name's connection to [[Samalanga]] (a coastal *landschap* on the north coast east of [[Pedir|Pedir]]) suggests that he, like other Habibs (cf. [[Habib Koeala van Pedir]]), was a north-coast figure who had come south to participate in the Groot-Atjeh operations.

### Nesting at Lamsoet and the Mid-June Tuchtiging

Samalanga's choice to "nest himself in [[Lamsoet]] with 50–60 men" is strategically significant. [[Lamsoet]] was a Dutch *buitenpost* in the [[III Moekims Kerkoen]] — one of the posts which the Dutch had originally established to control the river crossing between [[Lambaroe]] and [[Ana' Galong]] and which had, from [[1878]] to [[1885]], been the origin of many raids. The post had been evacuated as part of the [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|April 1896 outside-line evacuation]], and Samalanga's occupation of the position with 50–60 men was a deliberate re-fortification of a former Dutch strongpoint by a Habib commander. The 50–60 men he had with him represented a significant but not overwhelming force, and their "nesting" in Lamsoet was the kind of fortified presence which the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 16–17 June 1896]] — under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] with three colonnes (under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]]) — was explicitly designed to dislodge. The *tuchtiging* burned Lamsoet's *versterkingen* (fortifications) on [[17 June]] [[1896]] and cleared the eastern sector of the V Moekims Montassik, though Samalanga himself is not recorded as killed or captured in the engagement.

## Significance

Habib Samalanga's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the religiously-authoritative Habib who, despite an initially neutral stance, was drawn into the resistance by the gravitational pull of Umar's defection and the progressive Dutch pressure on the western *sagi* mukims. His occupation of the former Dutch post at [[Lamsoet]] is a particularly evocative detail, because it represents the literal re-fortification of a Dutch strongpoint by a Habib commander — a symbolic reversal of the colonial *buitenlinie* through the agency of religious authority. His presence among the eight *bendehoofden* of the V Moekims Montassik confirms the integration of the Habib network into the broader Achehnese military command structure of mid-June [[1896]].

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Habib Samalanga** was an Achehnese religious war-leader associated with [[Samalanga]] on the north coast of [[Atjeh]], already dead by [[March]] [[1894]], whose sister commanded the fortified position of [[Senelop]] in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] against the forces of [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]].

## The defence of Senelop

Hooijer recorded that at [[Senelop]] *de zuster van wijlen Habib Samalanga* — the sister of the late Habib Samalanga — held the command and had, with fifteen other fanatical women, devoted herself to death. The struggle that [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] had to wage there on [[12 March]] [[1894]] was accordingly severe, and he succeeded in taking the position only after placing himself at the head of his troops with his sunshade in hand and running in upon the fortification together with [[Toekoe Hoessin Longbattah]]. Dutch troops occupied Senelop three days later.

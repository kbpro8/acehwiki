**H. N. A. Swart** was a Dutch military officer and colonial administrator who served as Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] from [[1908]] onward, restoring conciliatory governance after the harsh policies of his predecessor [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] had provoked the [[Atjéh-enquête|1907–1908 inquiry]] and a fierce flare-up of armed resistance. He was the final military governor of [[Atjeh|Aceh]], the commander who in [[1918]] at last "een rustig gebied kon overdragen aan een civiel Gouverneur" — thereby ending what G. W. T. Dames calls "de slepende ziekte: de [[Atjeh-oorlog|Atjeh-oorlog]]" — and who throughout his career "altijd en overal het meest voelde voor breken van het verzet zonder bloedvergieten": who most preferred to break resistance without bloodshed.

## Early career and the case against Van Daalen

Swart rose through the ranks during the [[Aceh War]], serving under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and earning praise for his energy and sound judgment. [[H. Colijn]], who worked closely with him, described Swart as a "goed verstand, vlot en krachtig werker" (good sense, fluent and powerful worker) with a talent for handling people. When Van Heutsz proposed [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] as his successor in the Aceh governorship in [[1904]], Colijn privately favoured Swart as the more suitable candidate, though he conceded that Van Daalen's seniority and military experience made him the inevitable choice. Van Heutsz himself acknowledged Swart's abilities but initially hesitated to promote him because of his relatively junior rank.

## Service in Borneo and Celebes

In [[1905]], Van Heutsz appointed Swart as civil and military resident of the [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo]] (South and East Division of Borneo), where he successfully suppressed a long-standing rebellion by reorganising the military patrols and restoring order without relying on unreliable Dayak auxiliaries; by [[1906]] the area was pacified. He was later appointed Governor of [[Celebes]] and its dependencies, where he oversaw the pacification of [[Boni]], [[Loewoe]], and other recalcitrant states, implemented the [[Korte Verklaring]], and established *landschapskassen*. His methodical approach earned him the respect of both the local population and the central government.

## Succession to the Aceh governorship, 1908

Following the [[Atjéh-enquête]] and Van Daalen's resignation, Van Heutsz turned to Swart as the obvious successor, at a moment when the territory was still shaken by the 1907–1908 flare-up of armed resistance that Van Daalen's harsh policies had provoked. Swart took office in [[1908]] and immediately reversed Van Daalen's punitive measures: he restored the authority of the *oelèëbalangs*, reduced the arbitrary fines and released unjustly imprisoned chiefs, and fostered cooperation between the administration and local leaders, while continuing the economic and infrastructural development Van Daalen had initiated, now within a framework of trust rather than coercion.

His response was double in character: first to demonstrate, in the early years of his administration, that Dutch *overmacht* (overwhelming power) remained unshakeable; then, having established that fact, to pivot to a strategy of reconciliation and trust-building. The *volksleiders* who had been held down by force were "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde gebracht" — increasingly brought to the Dutch side by conviction rather than coercion — and the result was the *periode van rust* that allowed the *tal van maatregelen ter bevordering van den economischen vooruitgang*, the many measures for the promotion of economic progress, described in *De Buitenbezittingen*.

## The letter from the three hoofden (1910–1911)

Swart's preferred method in the closing years of the Aceh resistance was to bring moral and political pressure on the remaining resisters through the most authoritative Acehnese leaders rather than military force alone. At his request, "de drie voornaamste Atjehse hoofden" — two members of the Sultan's family, including *[[Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala]]*, and the former Sultan's legeraanvoerder *[[Panglima Prang Polim]]* (who had surrendered to [[H. Colijn|kapitein Colijn]]) — wrote a letter urging the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō Teungkoes]] to submit. "De Teungkoes bespraken de brief, doch besloten tot het einde te vechten; hen zou geschieden naar Allah's wil." The diplomatic track failed; the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910–1911)|colonne Schmidt]] was needed to finish the work militarily.

The episode is the clearest example of Swart's preference for negotiation even when he had the military power to crush. He mobilised the most authoritative remaining Acehnese political and religious voices before resorting to the final military solution — a reflection of the *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* (conciliatory and confidence-building policy) that defined his governorship.

## Cultural initiatives

Swart's governorship is also associated with the promotion of native craft industries. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that "op initiatief van Gouverneur Swart" a half-dozen *handwerkscholen* — handicraft schools — were founded in [[Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir]], where pupils received regular instruction in useful and decorative handicrafts, lace-making, weaving, and similar skills, typically taught by Menangkabau girls trained as assistant teachers on Sumatra's West Coast. The aim was to influence "de ontwikkeling der Atjehsche vrouw" — the development of the Acehnese woman — and through her the condition of the entire people.

## The handover of 1918

The cumulative effect of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910–1911)|colonne Schmidt]], the killing of the last Tiriō leaders, and the steady pressure of the patrouilles was that by [[1918]] Swart could at last transfer a peaceful Aceh to a civilian Gouverneur. "Daardoor kon Generaal Swart in 1918 eindelijk een rustig gebied overdragen aan een civiel Gouverneur. Er was een eind aan de slepende ziekte: de Atjeh-oorlog." The forty-five-year war — from the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of [[1873]] to the handover of [[1918]] — was over. The political closure that Swart achieved was distinct from the military closure of [[1911]]; the seven years between them are the years in which the colonial army held a pacified country until the civil administration was ready to take over.

## Consolidation and assessment

By the time *De Buitenbezittingen* was compiled, Swart could point to a territory in which, "na de hardnekkigen strijd," the population was "vrijwel in zijn geheel" working with Dutch power, and long-neglected agriculture was being taken in hand again. The cautious and gradual replacement of military by civil administrators, and the accompanying reduction of troop strength, were "een krachtig bewijs" — powerful proof — of the consolidation that, after years of stubborn struggle, had come "betrekkelijk snel en onverwacht" (relatively quickly and unexpectedly). By the time Van Heutsz left office in [[1909]], Aceh was largely peaceful, the success widely attributed to Swart's conciliatory governorship.

## Swart and Dominee Thenu

One personal anecdote from G. W. T. Dames's *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* places Swart in an unexpected register. When [[Dominee Thenu]], after his retirement in [[1934]], was received in audience by [[Koningin Wilhelmina|Queen Wilhelmina]] in the Netherlands, he was so taken by the honour that he refused to wash the hand she had shaken. "Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt." For more than ten days, the only other hand Thenu allowed to shake his was that of Generaal Swart. The episode places Swart, alongside the Queen, in the small circle of figures whose touch the *Soldaten-Dominee* counted as near-sacramental — a quiet marker of the esteem in which the rank-and-file of the colonial army held their general.

## Later career

After his term in Aceh, Swart continued to serve in senior colonial positions. He later became Vice-President of the [[Council of the Indies]] (Raad van Indië) and played a key role in shaping colonial policy in the 1910s.

## Significance

Swart's success in Aceh demonstrated that military capability alone was insufficient — a governor needed tact, patience, and a genuine respect for local institutions. His appointment marked the definitive shift from the coercive approach of Van Daalen to the collaborative model pioneered by Van Heutsz, and his governorship is often seen as the final consolidation of Dutch rule in Aceh after decades of conflict. In the narrative of *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, Swart stands as the quiet counter-weight to Van Heutsz: where Van Heutsz is the conqueror whose *systeem* finished the war at the tactical level, Swart is the general who closed it at the political level.

**H. N. A. Swart** was a Dutch military officer and colonial administrator who served as Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] from [[1908]] onward, restoring conciliatory governance after the harsh policies of his predecessor [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] had provoked the [[Atjéh-enquête|1907–1908 inquiry]] and a fierce flare-up of armed resistance. He also served earlier as Resident of the [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo]] and later as Governor of [[Celebes]], earning throughout a reputation as a capable, decisive, and tactful administrator.

## Early career and the case against Van Daalen

Swart rose through the ranks during the [[Aceh War]], serving under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and earning praise for his energy and sound judgment. [[H. Colijn]], who worked closely with him, described Swart as a "goed verstand, vlot en krachtig werker" (good sense, fluent and powerful worker) with a talent for handling people. When Van Heutsz proposed [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] as his successor in the Aceh governorship in 1904, Colijn privately favoured Swart as the more suitable candidate, though he conceded that Van Daalen's seniority and military experience made him the inevitable choice. Van Heutsz himself acknowledged Swart's abilities but initially hesitated to promote him because of his relatively junior rank.

## Service in Borneo and Celebes

In 1905, Van Heutsz appointed Swart as civil and military resident of the [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo]] (South and East Division of Borneo), where he successfully suppressed a long-standing rebellion by reorganising the military patrols and restoring order without relying on unreliable Dayak auxiliaries; by 1906 the area was pacified. He was later appointed Governor of [[Celebes]] and its dependencies, where he oversaw the pacification of [[Boni]], [[Loewoe]], and other recalcitrant states, implemented the [[Korte Verklaring]], and established *landschapskassen*. His methodical approach earned him the respect of both the local population and the central government.

## Succession to the Aceh governorship, 1908

Following the [[Atjéh-enquête]] and Van Daalen's resignation, Van Heutsz turned to Swart as the obvious successor, at a moment when the territory was still shaken by the 1907–1908 flare-up of armed resistance that Van Daalen's harsh policies had provoked. Swart took office in 1908 and immediately reversed Van Daalen's punitive measures: he restored the authority of the *oelèëbalangs*, reduced the arbitrary fines and released unjustly imprisoned chiefs, and fostered cooperation between the administration and local leaders, while continuing the economic and infrastructural development Van Daalen had initiated, now within a framework of trust rather than coercion.

His response was double in character: first to demonstrate, in the early years of his administration, that Dutch *overmacht* (overwhelming power) remained unshakeable; then, having established that fact, to pivot to a strategy of reconciliation and trust-building. The *volksleiders* who had been held down by force were "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde gebracht" — increasingly brought to the Dutch side by conviction rather than coercion — and the result was the *periode van rust* that allowed the *tal van maatregelen ter bevordering van den economischen vooruitgang*, the many measures for the promotion of economic progress, described in the rest of *De Buitenbezittingen*.

## Cultural initiatives

Swart's governorship is also associated with the promotion of native craft industries. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that "op initiatief van Gouverneur Swart" a half-dozen *handwerkscholen* — handicraft schools — were founded in [[Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir]], where pupils received regular instruction in useful and decorative handicrafts, lace-making, weaving, and similar skills, typically taught by Menangkabau girls trained as assistant teachers on Sumatra's West Coast. The aim was to influence "de ontwikkeling der Atjehsche vrouw" — the development of the Acehnese woman — and through her the condition of the entire people.

## Consolidation and assessment

By the time *De Buitenbezittingen* was compiled, Swart could point to a territory in which, "na de hardnekkigen strijd," the population was "vrijwel in zijn geheel" working with Dutch power, and long-neglected agriculture was being taken in hand again. The cautious and gradual replacement of military by civil administrators, and the accompanying reduction of troop strength, were "een krachtig bewijs" — powerful proof — of the consolidation that, after years of stubborn struggle, had come "betrekkelijk snel en onverwacht" (relatively quickly and unexpectedly). By the time Van Heutsz left office in 1909, Aceh was largely peaceful, the success widely attributed to Swart's conciliatory governorship.

## Later career

After his term in Aceh, Swart continued to serve in senior colonial positions. He later became Vice-President of the [[Council of the Indies]] (Raad van Indië) and played a key role in shaping colonial policy in the 1910s.

## Significance

Swart's success in Aceh demonstrated that military capability alone was insufficient — a governor needed tact, patience, and a genuine respect for local institutions. His appointment marked the definitive shift from the coercive approach of Van Daalen to the collaborative model pioneered by Van Heutsz, and his governorship is often seen as the final consolidation of Dutch rule in Aceh after decades of conflict.

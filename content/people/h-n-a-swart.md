**H. N. A. Swart** — *Hendrik Nikolaas Albert Swart* — known in the Acehnese idiom of the old hunter [[Datoek]] as *Toean Gouverneur Swart* — was a Dutch **lieutenant-general of the infantry** (*Luitenant-Generaal der Infanterie*) of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|K.N.I.L.]] and colonial administrator who, as **Civil and Military Governor of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]]** from [[1908]] to [[1918]], restored conciliatory governance after the harsh policies of his predecessor [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] had provoked the [[Atjéh-enquête|1907–1908 inquiry]] and a fierce flare-up of armed resistance, and who finally handed a peaceful Aceh over to a civilian Governor in [[1918]] — thereby ending what G. W. T. Dames calls "de slepende ziekte: de [[Atjeh-oorlog|Atjeh-oorlog]]" ("the lingering disease: the Aceh War"). He was the final military governor of [[Atjeh|Aceh]], the *Aceh-connoisseur* whose ten-year administration — "order and peace were consolidated in that province" — was the phase that "brought the [[Aceh War]] to an end," and who throughout his career "altijd en overal het meest voelde voor breken van het verzet zonder bloedvergieten" — "always and everywhere most preferred to break resistance without bloodshed".

> **Note on nomenclature**: One source — the *Notulen der bestuursvergaderingen* of [[1914]] — gives the initials as "H. N. N. A. Swart" (with two N's); all other sources, including Dames, Lamster, and the Encyclopaedisch Bureau, give "H. N. A. Swart". The biography *Generaal Swart* by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] and [[Schmidt|Kolonel Schmidt]] identifies him as **Hendrik Nikolaas Albert Swart**. The double-N form is almost certainly a transcription or OCR variant of the same name.

## Early Career and the Case Against Van Daalen

Swart rose through the ranks during the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]], serving as a young officer under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and earning praise for his energy and sound judgment. [[Hendrikus Colijn|Hendrikus Colijn]], who worked closely with him, described Swart as a "goed verstand, vlot en krachtig werker" ("good sense, fluent and powerful worker") with a talent for handling people. When Van Heutsz proposed [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] as his successor in the Aceh governorship in [[1904]], Colijn privately favoured Swart as the more suitable candidate, though he conceded that Van Daalen's seniority and military experience made him the inevitable choice. Van Heutsz himself acknowledged Swart's abilities but initially hesitated to promote him because of his relatively junior rank.

## Service in Borneo and Celebes

In [[1905]], Van Heutsz appointed Swart as civil and military resident of the [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo|Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo]] (South and East Division of Borneo), where he successfully suppressed a long-standing rebellion by reorganising the military patrols and restoring order without relying on unreliable Dayak auxiliaries; by [[1906]] the area was pacified. He was later appointed Governor of [[Celebes|Celebes]] and its dependencies, where he oversaw the pacification of [[Boni|Boni]], [[Kingdom of Loewoe|Kingdom of Loewoe]], and other recalcitrant states, implemented the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] (Short Declaration), and established *landschapskassen* (territorial treasuries). His methodical approach earned him the respect of both the local population and the central government.

## Succession to the Aceh Governorship, 1908

Following the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] and Van Daalen's resignation, Van Heutsz turned to Swart as the obvious successor, at a moment when the territory was still shaken by the [[1907]]–[[1908]] flare-up of armed resistance that Van Daalen's harsh policies had provoked. Swart took office in [[1908]] and immediately reversed Van Daalen's punitive measures: he restored the authority of the *oelèëbalangs* (Acehnese territorial chiefs), reduced the arbitrary fines and released unjustly imprisoned chiefs, and fostered cooperation between the administration and local leaders, while continuing the economic and infrastructural development Van Daalen had initiated, now within a framework of trust rather than coercion.

His response was double in character: first to demonstrate, in the early years of his administration, that Dutch *overmacht* (overwhelming power) remained unshakeable; then, having established that fact, to pivot to a strategy of reconciliation and trust-building. The *volksleiders* (people's leaders) who had been held down by force were "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde gebracht" — "increasingly brought to the Dutch side by conviction rather than coercion" — and the result was the *periode van rust* (period of rest) that allowed the *tal van maatregelen ter bevordering van den economischen vooruitgang* ("the many measures for the promotion of economic progress") described in *De Buitenbezittingen*.

## The Letter from the Three Hoofden (1910–1911)

Swart's preferred method in the closing years of the Acehnese resistance was to bring moral and political pressure on the remaining resisters through the most authoritative Acehnese leaders rather than military force alone. At his request, "de drie voornaamste Atjehse hoofden" ("the three principal Acehnese chiefs") — two members of the Sultan's family, including [[Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala|Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala]], and the former Sultan's *legeraanvoerder* (army commander) [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]] (who had surrendered to [[Hendrikus Colijn|kapitein Colijn]]) — wrote a letter urging the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō Teungkoes]] to submit. "De Teungkoes bespraken de brief, doch besloten tot het einde te vechten; hen zou geschieden naar Allah's wil" — "The Teungkoes discussed the letter, but resolved to fight to the end; what happens to them will be according to Allah's will." The diplomatic track failed; the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] was needed to finish the work militarily.

The episode is the clearest example of Swart's preference for negotiation even when he had the military power to crush. He mobilised the most authoritative remaining Acehnese political and religious voices before resorting to the final military solution — a reflection of the *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* (conciliatory and confidence-building policy) that defined his governorship.

## The Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913–1914)

As the highest-ranking Dutch colonial official in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] during the critical period of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)|Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913–1914)]], Swart played a central role in articulating the unique governance challenges that the territory posed to the broader reform agenda. His detailed explanations of Atjeh's indigenous administrative structures and his candid assessments of the prospects for *zelfbestuur* (self-governance) in the residency made him an indispensable voice in the *bestuursvergaderingen* (administrative meetings) convened by Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff|S. de Graaff]] at [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] on [[19 January]] [[1914]] and [[20 January]] [[20 January|20 January]] [[1914]].

### Views on Atjeh's Governance

Swart declared that the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] could not have local *zelfbestuur* in the standard form because of the territory's distinctive administrative composition. He explained that only [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and part of the *onderafdeeling* (sub-district) [[Singkel|Singkel]] constituted *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory), while the remainder of the governorate consisted of *Inlandsche Zelfbesturen* (Native Self-Governments, also referred to as *landschappen*). This meant that the conventional model of decentralised governance being proposed for other parts of the Netherlands East Indies could not be applied uniformly in Atjeh without significant modifications.

Regarding the appointment of indigenous officials, Swart stated that *erfelijkheid* (heredity) in Atjeh *hoofd* (head) appointments existed but was increasingly being set aside in favour of *bekwaamheden* (competence) as the decisive factor. He explained that while the local population was consulted and their recommendations were heard, these could be overridden if the *rechthebbende* (claimant) lacked the required qualifications for the position. Swart expressed confidence that with proper *opleiding* (training), within a small number of years the necessary *Inlandsche bestuurskrachten* (Native administrative forces) would be obtained in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. However, he also raised probing questions about the proposed administrative structure, asking whether the *ambtelijk element* (bureaucratic element) in the proposed [[Gewestelijke Raad Noord-Sumatra|Gewestelijke Raad Noord-Sumatra]] (Regional Council of North Sumatra) would be overly dominant, and whether the rank of [[Civiel Gezaghebber|Civiel Gezaghebber]] (Civil Authority Holder) would be open to military officers with ambition — questions that reflected his concern about maintaining an appropriate balance between civilian and military authority in a territory where the two had long been intertwined.

### The Sagi System Explanation

One of Swart's most significant contributions to the *bestuursvergaderingen* was his detailed explanation of the indigenous administrative hierarchy of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], prompted by De Graaff's inquiry about institutions comparable to Palembang's *marga*'s. Swart described a three-tiered system comprising three *[[sagi|sagi]]*'s, which he compared to Javanese *regentschappen* (regencies); *[[peleebalangschap|peleebalangschap]]pen*, which he compared to *districten* (districts); and *[[moekimschap|moekimschap]]pen*, which he compared to *onderdistricten* (sub-districts). The comparison to Javanese administrative units was deliberate, as it allowed officials whose experience was primarily in Java to grasp the scale and function of Atjehnese institutions through analogy with the more widely understood *desa* and *regentschap* system of Java.

Swart closed the [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta-Radja]] meetings by thanking Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff|S. de Graaff]] for the "*heldere, zakelijke uiteenzetting*" ("clear, business-like explanation") of the reform plan. He expressed hope that this "*grootsche plan*" ("grand plan") would soon be realised, signalling his general support for the reorganisation effort despite the reservations he had articulated about its application to Atjeh's unique circumstances.

## Cultural Initiatives

Swart's governorship is also associated with the promotion of native craft industries. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that "op initiatief van Gouverneur Swart" ("on the initiative of Governor Swart") a half-dozen *handwerkscholen* — handicraft schools — were founded in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir|Pedir]], where pupils received regular instruction in useful and decorative handicrafts, lace-making, weaving, and similar skills, typically taught by Menangkabau girls trained as assistant teachers on [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's West Coast]]. The aim was to influence "de ontwikkeling der Atjehsche vrouw" — "the development of the Acehnese woman" — and through her the condition of the entire people.

## The Ten-Year Administration and the Handover of 1918

In Merac's *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* ([[1947]]), Swart is the figure whose ten-year administration consolidated order and peace in Aceh. "The history of Aceh, for example, repeatedly showed how the most effective ally of the Acehnese against the Government was the Government itself, which constantly struck a new direction, which constantly changed systems of administration, and only during the ten-year administration of the Aceh-connoisseur, General Swart, were order and peace consolidated in that province." The same volume records Buyckjens's testimony: "Under the administration of Governor Swart the Aceh War came to an end… then gradually regulated conditions entered, and everywhere peace, order, and security began to prevail."

The cumulative effect of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]], the killing of the last Tiriō leaders, and the steady pressure of the patrouilles was that by [[1918]] Swart could at last transfer a peaceful Aceh to a civilian Gouverneur. "Daardoor kon Generaal Swart in 1918 eindelijk een rustig gebied overdragen aan een civiel Gouverneur. Er was een eind aan de slepende ziekte: de Atjeh-oorlog." — "Thereby General Swart was able in 1918 at last to hand over a peaceful territory to a civilian Governor. There was an end to the lingering disease: the Aceh War." The forty-five-year war — from the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of [[1873]] to the handover of [[1918]] — was over. The political closure that Swart achieved was distinct from the military closure of [[1911]]; the seven years between them are the years in which the colonial army held a pacified country until the civil administration was ready to take over.

## Datoek's Sobat Kras

The old hunter [[Datoek|Datoek]] of [[Blang Rakal|Blang Rakal]] counted Swart among his *sobat kras* (heavy friends): "I was a 'heavy friend' with Toean Kristoppel, and Toean General Van Heutsz, and Toean Scheepens, and Toean Darlang, and Toean Boreel, and Toean Governor Swart." The *Toean Gouverneur* — the Acehnese idiom for the Governor — is the book's — and Datoek's — memory of Swart as the heavy friend of the old hunter: the *Toean Gouverneur* who was a *sobat kras* of the Acehnese hunter.

## Swart and Dominee Thenu

One personal anecdote from G. W. T. Dames's *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* places Swart in an unexpected register. When [[Dominee Thenu|Dominee Thenu]], after his retirement in [[1934]], was received in audience by [[Queen Wilhelmina|Queen Wilhelmina]] in the Netherlands, he was so taken by the honour that he refused to wash the hand she had shaken. "Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt" — "It was a long time before he washed the hand that had been pressed by the Queen." For more than ten days, the only other hand Thenu allowed to shake his was that of Generaal Swart. The episode places Swart, alongside the Queen, in the small circle of figures whose touch the *Soldaten-Dominee* (Soldiers' Dominee) counted as near-sacramental — a quiet marker of the esteem in which the rank-and-file of the colonial army held their general.

## Later Career and Consolidation

After his term in Aceh, Swart continued to serve in senior colonial positions. He later became Vice-President of the [[Council of the Indies|Council of the Indies]] (*Raad van Indië*) and played a key role in shaping colonial policy in the 1910s.

By the time *De Buitenbezittingen* was compiled, Swart could point to a territory in which, "na de hardnekkigen strijd" ("after the stubborn struggle"), the population was "vrijwel in zijn geheel" ("almost in its entirety") working with Dutch power, and long-neglected agriculture was being taken in hand again. The cautious and gradual replacement of military by civil administrators, and the accompanying reduction of troop strength, were "een krachtig bewijs" — "powerful proof" — of the consolidation that, after years of stubborn struggle, had come "betrekkelijk snel en onverwacht" ("relatively quickly and unexpectedly"). By the time Van Heutsz left office in [[1909]], Aceh was largely peaceful, the success widely attributed to Swart's conciliatory governorship.

## Significance

Swart's success in Aceh demonstrated that military capability alone was insufficient — a governor needed tact, patience, and a genuine respect for local institutions. His appointment marked the definitive shift from the coercive approach of Van Daalen to the collaborative model pioneered by Van Heutsz, and his governorship is often seen as the final consolidation of Dutch rule in Aceh after decades of conflict. In the narrative of *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, Swart stands as the quiet counter-weight to Van Heutsz: where Van Heutsz is the conqueror whose *systeem* finished the war at the tactical level, Swart is the general who closed it at the political level.

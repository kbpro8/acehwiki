**H. N. A. Swart** was a Dutch lieutenant-general who served as Civil and Military Governor of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] from [[1908]] onward, succeeding [[G. C. E. van Daalen|van Daalen]] at a moment when the territory was still shaken by the [[1907]]–[[1908]] flare-up of armed resistance provoked by his predecessor's harsh policies. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* credits Swart's *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* — his conciliatory and confidence-building policy — with having, "na de bewijzen onzer overmacht in de eerste jaren van zijn bewind gegeven," drawn the chiefs who had been coerced into submission "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde" (ever more, from conviction, to our side). His governorship inaugurated the period of relative peace during which the economic and administrative development described in the rest of the handbook became possible.

## Succession and policy

Swart inherited a tense situation. The hard-driven *represaille-maatregelen* and the *scherpe besnoeiing der adat-inkomsten* — sharp curtailment of the customary revenues of the native chiefs — imposed under van Daalen had produced the fiercest resistance since the turn of the century. Swart's response was double: first to demonstrate, in the early years of his administration, that Dutch *overmacht* (overwhelming power) remained unshakeable; then, having established that fact, to pivot to a strategy of reconciliation and trust-building. The *volksleiders* who had been held down by force were "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde gebracht" — increasingly brought to the Dutch side by conviction rather than coercion. The result was the *periode van rust* that allowed the *tal van maatregelen ter bevordering van den economischen vooruitgang* — the many measures for the promotion of economic progress — that the rest of *De Buitenbezittingen* describes.

## Cultural initiatives

Swart's governorship is also associated with the promotion of native craft industries. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that "op initiatief van Gouverneur Swart" a half-dozen *handwerkscholen* — handicraft schools — were founded in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir|Pedir]], where pupils received regular instruction in useful and decorative handicrafts, lace-making, weaving, and similar skills. The instruction was typically given by Menangkabau girls trained as assistant teachers on Sumatra's West Coast. The aim was to influence "de ontwikkeling der Atjehsche vrouw" — the development of the Acehnese woman — and through her the condition of the entire people.

## Consolidation and assessment

By the time the handbook was compiled, Swart could point to a territory in which, "na de hardnekkigen strijd," the population was "vrijwel in zijn geheel" working with Dutch power, and the long-neglected agriculture was being taken in hand again. The cautious and gradual replacement of military by civil administrators and the accompanying reduction of the troop strength were, in *De Buitenbezittingen*'s interpretation, "een krachtig bewijs" — powerful proof — of the consolidation that, after years of stubborn struggle, had come "betrekkelijk snel en onverwacht" (relatively quickly and unexpectedly).

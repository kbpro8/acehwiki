**T. W. G. Merz** was an Eerste-Luitenant der Infanterie of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] whose eyewitness account of the construction of the [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit Longbatta]] redoubt in [[January 1875|January]] [[1875]] is the principal contemporary source for [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede-Luitenant C. J. Snijders]]'s engineering work under fire during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Merz's lecture on the subject was delivered in [[1935]] and printed in the *Orgaan der Officiersvereeniging* of October [[1935]], on the occasion of the sixtieth anniversary of Snijders' appointment to the [[Militaire Willemsorde|Ridder der Militaire Willemsorde]].

## The Lecture and its Subject

In [[October 1935|October]] [[1935]] Merz lectured to the Officiersvereeniging on the Missigit Longbatta operations of [[31 December]] [[31 December]] [[1874]]–[[January 1875|January]] [[1875]], in which he had taken part as a young infantry officer. The lecture was printed in the *Orgaan der Officiersvereeniging* of October [[1935]]. It was later cited in extenso by [[C. A. Bannink|C. A. Bannink]] in his *Grepen uit den Atjeh-oorlog*, printed in the same *Orgaan* in [[1936]], and again in the [[1944]] biography of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] by [[D. van den Berg|D. van den Berg]].

Merz's account gives the technical detail of the redoubt — a langwerpige redoute traced around the captured mosque, with the Zuid-Oostelijke and Noord-Westelijke saillants worked up as cirkel-bastions; an entrance on the N.O. side closed by a travers with Friesche ruiters; a detached post of four men in the old Atjehese voorwerk to cover the cooks; and a planned horizontal wire entanglement surrounded by a two-metre-wide ranjoe (sharpened-bamboo) planting, every man being required to deliver 30 ranjoes a day in his free time. Of Snijders himself Merz wrote:

> "De Luitenant SNIJDERS der Genie, een braaf officier, kweet zich uitmuntend van zijn plicht; het vijandelijk vuur in het minst niet schuwende, altijd opgewekt en vroolijk, ging hij de zijnen voorbeeldig voor, zoodat binnen drie weken de wallen uit zandzakken bestaande, voltooid en de gebouwen gereed waren, waardoor ten minste iedereen behoorlijk onderkomen had. Waarna hij met zijne Mineurs en werklieden naar Kota Radja terugkeerde; ieder onzer speet het, dat deze brave, trouwe makker van ons ging; iedereen waardeerde hem in hooge mate om zijn moed en warmen kameraadschappelijken geest."

## Significance

Merz's lecture is a rare first-person infantry account of an engineer operation in the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — between the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the Kraton]] of [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] and the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|Campaign against the XXII Moekims]] of [[1879]] — and is the source from which the [[1944]] biographer of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] reconstructed the technical detail of the [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit Longbatta]] work. It is also one of the very few places where an eyewitness records the construction of a [[ranjoe|ranjoe]] obstacle and the daily quota system by which the planting was built up.

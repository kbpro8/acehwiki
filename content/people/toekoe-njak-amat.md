**Toekoe Njak Amat** was an Acehnese chief who, according to [[H. van Kol]] speaking in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[17 November]] [[1897]], had "naar allen schijn" (to all appearance) been arrested in a treacherous manner near [[Kroeng Raja]] in the month preceding the debate — that is, in October [[1897]].

## The allegation

Van Kol named the case as the third in a short series of treacherous arrests of Acehnese chiefs. He alleged that the [[Radja van Samalangan]] had been taken prisoner treacherously after being promised rifles; that the head of [[Bintara Peunang]] had likewise been arrested in a faithless manner; and that with Toekoe Njak Amat the same had to all appearance been repeated in the previous month at [[Kroeng Raja|Kroeng raja]].

The name is given in the source both as *Toekoe Nja Amat* and, in the same passage, in the form here adopted. The source supplies no further particulars of his district, his standing among the chiefs, the circumstances of the arrest or his subsequent fate.

## Context

The alleged arrest belongs to the same phase of the war as the [[Marine landing at Kroeng Raja (29 December 1896)|Dutch operations at Kroeng Raja]] on the north-eastern shore of [[Groot-Atjeh]], and to the wider Dutch effort after [[1896]] to detach or neutralise individual chiefs. Van Kol placed the case in his catalogue of breaches of the *[[Lois et coutumes de la guerre|lois et coutumes de la guerre]]*, alongside the killing of [[Njahim]] and the marginal note attributed to [[Karel van der Heijden|Van der Heyden]] on the [[Krijgsorder for the VI, XXII and XXVI Moekims|krijgsorder for the VI, XXII and XXVI Moekims]].

The Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] did not respond to this case specifically, replying only in general terms to the charge of cruelty.

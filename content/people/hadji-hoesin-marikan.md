**Hadji Hoesin Marikan** — by his own account formerly named **Shephard**, an Englishman or American by birth — was a renegade sailor living at [[Pedir|Pidir]] who, in [[1876]], kept the Dutch flag on the beach of the roadstead as the paid servant of the prince of Pidir, [[Toengkoe Radja Pakik]].

## The flag of the prince

[[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] describes him in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]): once a sailor on a merchant ship, he had come twelve years earlier to Atjeh ([[Samalanga]]), turned Muslim, married an Atjehnese woman, and later moved to Pidir, where he lived with his wife in a poor hut near the beach and was paid two and a half Spanish dollars per day by the prince to take care of the Netherlands flag. The Atjehnese princes, Meijer remarks — likening him to the comparable figure of *Svendsen* (Swinsen), the *ablanda hilan* ("renegade European") well known during the Second Expedition — preferred the flag to fly at the beach rather than before their residences, on the pretext that it would be better seen by the ships: the flag was the sign of the prince's outward submission, not of his disposition.

He stands as a curiosity of the north-coast diplomatic record: a Westerner in Atjehnese service guarding the enemy's flag in the year of the Dutch landing.

**Ignác Goldziher** (German form **Ignaz Goldziher**, often cited as **Dr. Ign. Goldziher**; [[1850]]–[[1921]]) was a Hungarian orientalist — the founding figure of modern European [[Islamic studies|Islamic studies]] — whose writings on Islamic law, tradition, saint-worship, and Arabic poetry are cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] throughout *The Achehnese* as among the standard European authorities on the gap between the theory and practice of Mohammedan law, on the Zahirite school of jurisprudence, and on the devotional literature of which the Malay translations of the [[Burda (poem)|Burda]] poem by [[Al-Busiri|Al-Busiri]] formed part. His treatise *Mohammedanisches Recht in Theorie und Wirklichkeit* ("Mohammedan Law in Theory and in Reality"), published in the *Zeitschrift für vergleichende Rechtswissenschaft* (vol. VIII), is invoked by Snouck Hurgronje alongside his own essays on the same theme; his essay on Moslem saint-worship in the *Muhammedanische Studien* (Halle [[1890]]) is described by Snouck Hurgronje as "the best sketch of the Moslim saint-worship in general" and is used to open his own discussion of Achehnese saint-worship.

## Biographical Note and a Source Discrepancy

The historically attested Ignác Goldziher was born in [[Budapest|Budapest]] in [[1850]] and died in [[1921]], having spent his career in Hungary and at the University of Budapest; he was a founding figure of European [[Islamic studies|Islamic studies]] and was long regarded, with [[Theodor Nöldeke|Theodor Nöldeke]] and [[Michael Jan de Goeje|Michael Jan de Goeje]], as one of the three great European orientalists of his generation. The present article follows the biographical dates and the Hungarian nationality given in the source file that draws directly on Vol. I of *The Achehnese*.

A second source file in the wiki, however, gives the name as **Heinrich Goldziher** with the dates [[1842]]–[[1927]] and describes him as a "Hungarian-born Dutch orientalist" who held academic positions at the [[University of Leiden|University of Leiden]] and died in Leiden. These biographical particulars — the forename "Heinrich," the dates 1842–1927, the Dutch residence, the Leiden death — do not match the historically attested Ignác Goldziher and appear to be erroneous; the same source file, however, lists "Ignác Goldziher" among its aliases and cites Goldziher's actual writings on Arabic poetry and on the *Burda*, both of which are works of Ignác Goldziher. The three source files are accordingly treated here as describing the same person; the "Heinrich" attribution is preserved in the alias list and noted here as a discrepancy in the source.

## Writings on Islamic Law and the Theory–Practice Gap

Goldziher's treatise *Mohammedanisches Recht in Theorie und Wirklichkeit* is cited by Snouck Hurgronje in connection with the question why Mohammedan law, "developed as it was for the most part in the schools, out of reach of all close connection with the real requirements of daily life", is so ill-fitted for the practical administration of justice. Snouck Hurgronje had himself treated this theme in two earlier papers — *Mohammedaansch recht en rechtswetenschap* (*Indische Gids*, [[1886]]) and *De fiqh en de vergelijkende rechtsvietenschap* (*Rechtsgeleerd Magazijn*, [[1886]]) — and he places Goldziher's contribution on the same level of authority. The Mohammedan law, in Snouck Hurgronje's summary, "greatly hampers the detection of crime, imposes impossible demands on witnesses and fails to take cognizance of historical changes". As the law itself excludes on principle all intrinsic reform, rulers everywhere have found themselves compelled to provide a practical method of administering justice by constituting themselves the judges in all cases, following partly the prevailing custom of the country and partly their own inclination.

## Significance for Achehnese Studies

The general thesis advanced by Goldziher and Snouck Hurgronje is the key to the latter's discussion of Achehnese judicial institutions. The twofold concession by which, in most Mohammedan countries, the *adat* (conceived as the ancient law of the land, altered but little by the spirit of Mohammedanism) and the *hukom* or religious law are made to govern side by side, "though in practice the former plays by far the larger part", is presented as a direct consequence of the impracticability of the religious law in its ideal form. In [[Acheh|Acheh]], even the jurisdiction in matters affecting the family has not been entirely made over to the *kali*; the latter's most usual duties are the declaration of the *pasah* (judicial dissolution of a marriage) and the acting as *wali* for maidens without *wali*s by kinship — and even these duties are performed by the *kali* only by the authority of the *uleebalang* specially given in each case.

## The Essay on Moslem Saint-Worship

Goldziher's essay on Moslem saint-worship, published in the *Muhammedanische Studien* (Halle [[1890]]), is described by Snouck Hurgronje as "the best sketch of the Moslim saint-worship in general" and is used to open his own discussion of [[Achehnese saint-worship|Achehnese saint-worship]] — including the cult of figures such as [[Teungku Anjong|Teungku Anjong]]. The essay furnished the comparative European framework against which Snouck Hurgronje measured the specifically Achehnese forms of saint-veneration.

## *Die Zahiriten* and the Zahirite View

Goldziher's *Die Zahiriten* ([[1884]]) is cited by Snouck Hurgronje for the Zahirite view on the question whether a person in a state of ritual impurity may touch the [[Quran|Quran]] — a question that bore on the daily devotional practice of the Achehnese and on the rules governing the handling of sacred texts in the prayer-houses and *meunasahs*.

## Writings on Arabic Poetry and the *Burda*

Goldziher's work on Arabic poetry and religious literature is cited in the source for a 16th-century Malay translation of the *Burda* of [[Al-Busiri|Al-Busiri]]. A Dutch-language commentary on his analysis of metaphorical language in Arabic poetry records that:

> "*Spot, smaad en schande worden in de Arabische poëzie dikwijls met een halsketen vergeleken…*" (Mockery, slander and shame are often compared in Arabic poetry to a necklace…)

The passage illustrates Goldziher's analysis of the motif of the necklace as a symbol of shame and disgrace in Arabic poetic imagery. His researches on the *Burda* are also cited through a French-language quotation of his commentary — "*les savants les plus distingués…*" — highlighting his role in introducing the poem to European academia. Goldziher's editions and commentaries helped establish the *Burda* as a central text for comparative studies of Islamic devotional literature.

Although Goldziher's primary focus was the Arab world, his methodological tools have been applied to the study of Malay and Achehnese Islamic literature. Scholars interpreting poetic imagery in the Malay translations of the *Burda* — such as those preserved in the seventeenth-century manuscript examined in the source — drew on Goldziher's insights. His work thus indirectly shaped the literary culture of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Sultanate of Acheh]] in the [[19th century|19th century]], where the *Burda* became part of the religious and poetic repertoire of the Achehnese elite.

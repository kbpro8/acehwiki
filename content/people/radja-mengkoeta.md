**Radja Mengkoeta** was a Batak headman, established near [[Tandjoeng Karang|Tandjoeng Karang]] in the Tamiang region, who secured from the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] a *[[Sarakata|sarakata]]* appointing him ruler over [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] during a period when the old Tamiang kingdom had fallen into *regeeringloosheid* — anarchy — following a war with a Javanese prince. *De Buitenbezittingen* describes him as a *Bataksch vorst* whose opportunistic appeal to the Sultan gave Atjeh a foothold in a country that would later become one of the most contested zones between the Acehnese, Langkat, and Dutch spheres.

## The anarchy and the sarakata

The oldest history of Tamiang, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, tells of a single kingdom from which the present five *landschapjes* of Tamiang descended. A period of anarchy, following a war with a Javanese prince, gave the Batak immigrants — settled near [[Tandjoeng Karang|Tandjoeng Karang]] — the opportunity to come to an understanding with the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Acehnese Sultan]]. The Sultan's *sarakata* to Radja Mengkoeta appointed him *vorst over Tamiang*, providing an Acehnese-derived legitimacy that the Batak chief lacked by descent.

## The Rijksraad of Datoeq Ampat Soekoe

To govern his new realm, Radja Mengkoeta appointed a Rijksraad — a council of state — of four *Datoeq ampat soekoe*, the "four soekoe chiefs," whose titles and offices would persist, in ever more fragmented form, through all the later partitions of Tamiang:

1. *Datoeq Imam Balei Mesoeara*
2. *Datoeq Hakim Sri Soeara*
3. *Datoeq Tandil Bongkal*
4. *Datoeq Panghoeloe*

A fifth, *Datoeq Laksamana*, was added later. This five-member council became the constitutional framework of the Tamiang lands, surviving the subsequent partition of the realm into the two *keudjeuroeëns* of Karang and Moeda and the further divisions that produced the five *landschapjes* of the colonial era.

## Succession and partition

After Radja Mengkoeta's reign, his kingdom was split into two: the territory of the [[Keudjeuroeën Karang|Keudjeuroeën Karang]] on the left bank of the Tamiang River and the territory of the [[Keudjeuroeën Moeda|Keudjeuroeën Moeda]] on the right bank. Both fragments retained a council of *Datoeq ampat soekoe*, with the Moeda council adding a fifth member, the *Panglima Besar*, alongside the original four. The partitions that followed — driven by war, the rise of the [[Bandahara|Bandahara]] line, and Langkat's claims of suzerainty — never erased the constitutional template Radja Mengkoeta had established.

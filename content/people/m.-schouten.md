**M. Schouten** — *Ridder van de [[Militaire Willemsorde]]* — was one of [[H. N. A. Swart]]'s longest-serving subordinates, who "served under the orders of Governor Swart in [[Borneo]], [[Celebes]] and [[Atjeh]]," was extraordinarily promoted from *onderluitenant* to officer for special merit, and was pensioned with the rank of captain.

## As one of Swart's Jannen

Schouten first appears as a sergeant evacuated from the 1st company of the 12th Battalion at [[Sigli]] in [[1899]], to whom a comrade wrote of the new captain: "And now we have only a little captain!" [[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] identifies him as "the later Lieutenant Schouten, one of Swart's disciples."

## In Atjeh under Swart

In the resistance years Schouten commanded the garrison of the bivouac [[Boeloh Blang Ara]] in the [[Lho' Seumawè]] sub-division, which "repeatedly had, and kept, his hands full."

In [[1916]] Schouten received reports so positive about the whereabouts of [[T. Radja Sabi]] — the last wandering irreconcilable of [[Keureutòë]], then a boy of fifteen — that he resolved, together with the *[[oelèëbalang]]* of [[Seuleuma]], to make one more attempt to bring him in: "the fighting time was over, and why should that boy go on wandering?" With the co-operation of all the chiefs and of [[T. Tjhi' Béntara]], the half-brother, who went along in person, a discussion was held "somewhere in the rimba." The condition set by the other side — that the Governor must first acknowledge that the boy at [[Koeta Radja]] was a substitute — proved a stumbling-block, and T. Radja Sabi continued to wander until [[1919]].

On [[11 March]] [[1918]] Schouten, by then Civil Authority at [[Lho' Soekon]], was attacked there in one of the [[Atjeh-moord|Atjeh murders]]; he soon recovered and the perpetrator was laid low.

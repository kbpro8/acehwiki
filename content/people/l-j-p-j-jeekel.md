**L. J. P. J. Jeekel** (rendered by [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] at one point as "Jeekers", evidently an OCR or compositor's slip) was a late-nineteenth-century Dutch scholar whose Leiden doctoral dissertation, *Het Sumatra-tractaat* ("The Sumatra Treaty"), was published in [[1881]]. The treatise remained, at the time Van der Kemp was writing in [[1900]], the standard monograph on the [[Sumatra Treaty of 1871|Sumatra Treaty]] — the agreement by which Britain released the Netherlands from the restraints imposed by Article 2 of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824 treaty]] and recognised the Dutch right to extend their power over the whole of Sumatra, including [[Acheh]].

## The dissertation

Jeekel's *Het Sumatra-tractaat* is cited in a footnote on page 56 of Van der Kemp's article, in connection with the publication of the treaty texts in the Dutch and Indies *Staatsbladen* of [[1872]] (Ned. Stbl. 1872 No. 18; Ind. Stbl. 1872 No. 94). Van der Kemp notes that the texts of the 1871 Sumatra Treaty and its associated instruments are to be found, *inter alia*, in Jeekel's proefschrift. The cross-reference forms part of the documentary scaffolding of Van der Kemp's discussion of how the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|Acheh agreement of 1819]] and Article 2 of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824 treaty]] between them structured Dutch and British relations with Acheh for the half-century from [[1824]] until [[1871]].

## Significance for Acheh studies

Van der Kemp's article treats the [[Sumatra Treaty of 1871|Sumatra Treaty]] as the diplomatic act that "released the Netherlands from England's tutelage" over Acheh, and he marshals Jeekel's dissertation as the principal Dutch monograph on the subject. The treaty is the indispensable hinge of any account of how the [[Acheh]] question developed from the dead-letter Acheh agreement of [[1819]] through the long British veto on Dutch expansion to the [[Acheh War|Aceh War]] that opened in [[1873]]. Jeekel's importance in the present article is therefore that of a source-citer who supplies the legal-diplomatic documentation of that hinge.

By [[1900]] the Sumatra Treaty and its consequences had become a live political question in the Netherlands — [[E. de Waal]] had devoted a volume of *Onze Indische Financiën* to it, and the question of British opposition to Dutch expansion on Sumatra was being openly discussed. Jeekel's dissertation of [[1881]] thus belongs to the same late-nineteenth-century scholarly enterprise as the works of [[H. D. Levyssohn Norman|Levyssohn Norman]], [[Hoek]], [[L. W. G. N. Netscher|Netscher]], [[J. E. de Sturler|De Sturler]] and [[Van Hogendorp]] — the editorial labours on which Van der Kemp built.

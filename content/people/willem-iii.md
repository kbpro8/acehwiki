**Willem III** ruled as **King of the Netherlands** from [[1849]] to [[1890]], presiding over the longest and most devastating **Aceh War** ([[1873]]–[[1904]]) in Dutch colonial history.  The *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pages [[489]]–[[490]]) records his name repeatedly (pages [[104]], [[111]]) as the sovereign who authorised the deployment of the **Royal Netherlands Marine Corps** to **Aceh**, the expansion of naval bases, and the issuance of numerous royal decrees directing the war effort.

## Outbreak of the Aceh War

In [[1873]], following a dispute over the **Dutch‑Aceh trade agreement** and the murder of Dutch officials, Willem III ordered a punitive expedition led by **General Johan Harmen Rudolf Köhler**.  Marine battalions embarked from **Willemsoord** and landed at **Kuta Raja** (modern **Banda Aceh**) in [[1873]], marking the formal start of the war.

## Royal Directives and Marine Operations

Royal decrees issued by Willem III in [[1874]] and [[1875]] mandated the establishment of a permanent marine garrison at **Banda Aceh** and the construction of the **Fort van den Bosch**.  The *Mariniers* archives (page [[104]]) list these orders alongside the names of the commanding officers, illustrating the king’s hands‑on involvement.

## Prolonged Conflict and Negotiations

Throughout the 1880s, Willem III oversaw multiple campaigns, including the **Kuta Raja Expedition** ([[1880]]) and the **Ulee Lheue Campaign** ([[1885]]).  The *Mariniers* records (page [[111]]) note that the king personally received reports from field commanders and authorized additional marine reinforcements in [[1888]].

## Significance

Willem III’s reign epitomises the Dutch colonial policy of “**pacification through force**” in **Aceh**, with the **Marine Corps** playing a central role in amphibious assaults, riverine operations, and the establishment of colonial administration.  The war’s high cost and prolonged resistance contributed to the eventual re‑evaluation of Dutch colonial strategy in the early 20th century.

**T. Tjhi Toenong** — also *Teukoe Tjoet Moehamat*, *T. Tjhi Moehamat*, and called by his party *balang tjoet baroh* — was the half-brother of [[T. Tjhi Bentara]] and the rival *oeléëbalang* of [[Keureutoe]]. While [[T. Tjhi Bentara|Tjhi Bentara]] was the *oeléëbalang baroh* (the new, the Dutch-appointed, sitting in the lower country), Tjhi Toenong was *oeléëbalang Toenong* — *Toenong* signifying that he held himself in the upper reaches, the free mountains, where he received his appointment from the pretendent-sultan with the [[Tjap sikoereuëng|tjap sikoereuëng]] (nine circles). He was, Zentgraff writes, the type of Atjehsche chief of that time, sympathetic and liked by the people, and his influence hung as a shadow over the authority of his half-brother.

## The raids of 1902-1903

In [[June]] [[1902]] he overvalled a detachment of infantry of thirty men under v. Steijn Parvé; this cost the Dutch 8 dead and wounded, the attacker 14 dead. In [[August]] [[1902]] he did better. From spion reports he knew by which path a transport, under cover of twenty bajonets, would march from [[Simpang Olim]]; he laid an ambush in the high *alang-alang* beside the path near Meunasah Djeuro. There his men suddenly fell upon the troop with the klewang and shattered it completely. In the first onset 7 soldiers fell and the commander and 2 men were wounded. Three Native soldiers took to flight; a brave European soldier, Fortier, held the poor remnant together until lieutenant [[Van Gheel Gildemeester|Van Gheel Gildemeester]] arrived with 40 men. The attackers had disappeared, taking five Dutch rifles.

The greatest blow T. Tjhi Toenong dealt the Dutch was in [[November]] [[1903]], when lieutenant Kok, with a patrol of forty-five bajonets from the keudé Sampoj Niët in two prauws with Atjehsche rowers, was to go up-river. Incautiously the next day's objective had been spoken of the evening before, and the keudé knew of it; so T. Tjhi Toenong could set the scene of the drama. The troop was crossing the *kall* when at midnight a few shots were fired from the bank at the prauws; on this signal the Atjehsche rowers capsized the little boats and escaped by swimming. All the soldiers were thrown into the water, so that 42 rifles were lost; lieutenant Kok and 28 soldiers were drowned. The mass-grave of this patrol is on the [[Keureutoe|Keureutoe-rivier]].

## Submission October 1904 and arrest March 1905

After [[Panglima Polim]] submitted in the second half of [[1903]], T. Tjhi Toenong knew that his hour too had come; on [[5 October]] [[1904]] he submitted with his followers at [[Lho Seumawé]]. He was treated with clemency and allowed to live in Keureutoe. At first it seemed as though this stormy nature had come to rest at last; he had a son by [[Tjoet Meuthia]] and worked with his half-brother. But his fighting blood and his warlike wife could not break with the old comrades in the mountains, and gradually he began to drift. Or perhaps, Zentgraff notes, his half-brother's faction made life difficult for him by intrigues. Who can say what was true and what was false in the accusations later brought against T. Tjhi Toenong, which led to his death and to the drama of the *adelaarsjong*?

The affair developed as follows. On [[26 January]] [[1905]] a patrol under sergeant Vollaers, with sixteen bajonets, bivakked in the *meunasa* of Meurandeh Paja east of [[Lho Soekon]]. All security measures had been neglected; armed Atjehers were let inside the *pagger* on the pretext of selling fruit and chickens, and a couple were even allowed up the ladder to where the commander lay reading a book. Suddenly the signal was given; the whole troop was cut down with the klewang, the sergeant first. Of the seventeen soldiers sixteen were killed. After the [[Meurandeh Paja massacre (1905)|Meurandeh Paja massacre]] the investigation pointed to T. Tjhi Toenong as one of the authors; he was arrested on [[5 March]] [[1905]] at Lho Seumawé by lieutenant Van Vuuren.

## Execution, 5 March 1905

He was condemned to death, and the sentence was to be the rope; but [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], then governor, found it improper that an Atjeher who had always been a brave fighter and commander should be hanged. He had a right to a more worthy death, and Van Daalen changed the sentence to death by the bullet. In the days before the execution T. Tjhi Toenong was still permitted to receive his wife. In the bitterness of those last days — he had not in a dozen fights been granted the crown of the *sjahid* — his hatred flared up again. He made [[Tjoet Meuthia]], then pregnant, swear that she would bring up their son, then about five, in hatred of the *kaphé*, and that after his execution she would marry [[Pang Nanggroë]]. This she swore to him, and he knew that a woman of her character would keep that oath. In that certainty T. Tjhi Toenong walked in the early morning to the sea-beach at Lho Seumawé, where he was placed before the firing-squad. He died as a man, and he will have had the conviction that the Prophet would grant him the blessings of paradise, as to all who walk his way.

## Significance

His execution set off the long drama of his son [[T. Radja Sabi|T. Radja Sabi, *l'Aiglon*]] and of [[Tjoet Meuthia]] and [[Pang Nanggroë]] — fourteen years of forest war in the mountains of Keureutoe. He was buried on the strand of Lho Seumawé; his grave, and that of T. di Boeah, one of his panglima's, is one of the monuments of the war.

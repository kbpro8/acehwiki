**Muhammad Radjab** was a Minangkabau youth whose autobiographical recollection, cited by [[Van Ronkel]], records the festive use of Arabic *kasidas*—notably the *Burda*—during a religious school ceremony in a Minangkabau village in the early [[20th]]‑century [[Indonesia]]. His narrative offers a rare first‑person perspective on the lived experience of Arabic devotional poetry, the role of *hadji*‑clad boys in processions, and the syncretic character of Islamic education in the region.

## Narrative Details

Radjab describes a procession in which boys dressed as *hadji* led the group while chanting verses from the *Burda* and other *kasidas*. The chanting underscored the cultural diffusion of Arabic poetic forms into local religious practice, illustrating how Islamic devotional literature was integrated into the educational rituals of early‑[[20th]]‑century Minangkabau society.

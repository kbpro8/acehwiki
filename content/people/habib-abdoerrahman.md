**Habib Abdoerrahman** — properly Sayyid (i.e. descendant of the Prophet) Abdurrahman Zair — was an Arab scholar and sayyid who served as first rijksbestuurder (chief minister) of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] under the sultanate, who during the early years of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] sought Dutch favour in return for a reward, and who, on the rejection of his offers, returned to Atjeh in [[March 1876]] as the principal organiser and leader of the Acehnese resistance until his [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission to the Dutch on 13 October 1878]]. He was exiled to [[Jeddah|Jeddah]] in [[November 1878]] with a personal annual pension of 12,000 dollars, and was remembered by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] as one of the principal leaders of the *prang Gompeuni*.

## Early Career under the Sultanate

The Habib was "a capable and ambitious Arab" who first appeared under the sultanate in Atjeh as a strict Mohammedan jurist. "By his learning, ability and descent he knew how to acquire a very great influence there, and had even become first rijksbestuurder." He figures already in the early months of the war: in [[September 1871]], when the controller [[E. R. Krayenhoff]] arrived aboard the *Djambi* with a letter for the new young sultan [[Aladin Mahmood Shah|Aladin Mahmood Shah]], the sultan reigned under the voogdij of "the Arabier Habib Abdoe'r Rachman," who assumed a high tone, demanded the return of former Acehnese territory on the east and west coasts of Sumatra, and threatened the intervention of [[England|England]], [[France|France]] and [[Turkey|Turkey]].

## Attempts to Negotiate with the Dutch

At the beginning of the war the Habib was absent on a mission to [[Constantinopel|Constantinople]] to invoke the help of the Sultan (of Turkey). He then returned to [[Penang|Penang]]. "From there he had at once encouraged the war-party, but at the same time opened negotiations with our government and offered his services in the war for a reward. As these offers were repeatedly rejected because he was distrusted, he decided to appear at the head of our opponents in Atjeh."

## Arrival in Atjeh, March 1876

"From Pulau Penang he went, completely disguised as a matelot, aboard a schooner that took him to [[Edi-Tjoet|Edi Tjoet]], whence he reached Great Acheh overland. On the way he collected funds for the war, while a number of fighters from the various coastal states followed him. Later he gradually acquired an important influence on the course of affairs." His arrival in March [[1876]], during the command of [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|General Wiggers van Kerchem]], gave new life to the war: "The coming of Habib Abdurrahman seemed to have given new life to the war."

## The Campaigns of 1878

By the spring of [[1878]] the Habib was once more on the offensive. In the night of [[7 June|7]]-[[8 June]] [[1878]] a band dared to attack the dwang-arbeiders hospital at [[Pantej Perak|Pantej Perak]], where it "made a real slaughter." As soon as [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] had departed for [[Gedong|Gedong]], news came of the Habib's intention to make a great attack with 2,000 men in the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] on the posts at [[Kroëng Raba|Kroëng Raba]], Boekit Seboen and [[Pakan Badak|Pakan Badak]]. On [[22 June]] [[1878]] Kroëng Raba was attacked by a great multitude of Atjehers, who shot at the garrison until [[24 June]] without venturing a storm. On [[23 June]] the Habib sent an Atjeher with a white flag and a letter inviting the post-commandant "to leave his position and fight with him in the open field," and on [[24 June]] a second letter offering free withdrawal on condition that the Dutch fortification was destroyed.

The government at Batavia ordered Van der Heyden to make an end of the situation and to drive the Habib and his bands out. On [[23 July]] [[1878]] the important campaign in the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] began, with the [[Capture of Montassik (28 July 1878)|capture of the missigit Montassik]] on [[28 July]]. In August and September, reconnaissances and patrols were constantly sent out to punish hostile kampongs, especially in the sawah of Lamkrak west of Montassik. The campaigns made an impression; on [[25 August]] [[1878]] the Habib himself made the first overture toward submission, soon followed by others.

## Submission, 13 October 1878

"On the afternoon of that day, about two o'clock, he arrived at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], where the governor-commander immediately received him and accepted his submission. The following day [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]], the head of the VII Moekims in the Sagi XXII, was also sworn in and recognised in his dignity. Their example was followed by several other chiefs."

The Habib "remained some weeks at Kota Radja. With a view to the operations already previously planned in the direction of Tjot Bada and Indrapuri, the very precise data he provided about the region south of Anak Galoeëng were of great value. After his submission he behaved always chivalrously and loyally towards us; in all respects he showed that he wished to contribute to ending the struggle. His letters or envoys to various chiefs, to urge them to submission, however, had little result, as most considered him a traitor to the national cause."

## Exile to Jeddah

"He departed on [[24 November]] [1878] with His Majesty's steamship *Curagao* to Arabia, where Jeddah was assigned to him as his residence, with a personal annual pension of 12,000 dollars." The choice of Jeddah was a political exile, removing him from the Atjeh theatre while honouring his rank as a sayyid by allotting him a residence near the holy places.

## Significance

The Habib's submission marks the turning-point of the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] in its first phase. With his removal, and the subsequent [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign against the XXII Moekims]] and [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|campaign against the XXVI Moekims]] of [[1879]], the first conquest of Great Acheh was completed. His role, however, was in some sense taken over by the religious leaders — the ulamas and teungkus — who, financed by the *sabil* share of the [[Jakeuet (zakat)|jakeuet]] or zakat, kept the resistance alive through the period of the [[Dutch Concentration Scheme|concentration scheme]] and produced such figures as [[Teungku Tiro|Teungku Tiro]] and [[Habib Samalanga|Habib Samalanga]] in the 1880s and 1890s.

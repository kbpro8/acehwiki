**F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the *Vice-President van den Raad van Indië* — Vice-President of the Council of the Indies — who was sent to [[Acheh|Atjèh]] as *Regeeringscommissaris* (Government Commissioner) to demand of the [[Sultan of Atjeh|Sultan]] an explanation for his "trouweloos gedrag" — faithless conduct — and to require of him the recognition of Dutch sovereignty. When Atjèh refused all explanation, Nieuwenhuyzen exercised his authority to declare war on [[26 March]] [[1873]], opening the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]].

## The road to war

Kreemer situates Nieuwenhuyzen's mission at the end of a long chain of provocations and failures. After the [[Sumatra Treaty of 1871|Sumatra-tractaat]] with Britain had released the Netherlands from its [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824]] obligation to respect Atjèh's integrity, the sultanate sought protection from foreign powers. An Atjèhnese embassy — consisting of the [[Panglima Tibang Moehamad|sjahbandar of Atjèh Panglima Tibang Moehamad]] and four [[uleebalang|oelèëbalangs]] — presented itself to the resident of [[Riouw|Riouw]] with a letter requesting that the announced Dutch visit be postponed, "want" — so the Sultan wrote — "het is nu een maand geleden, dat ik een brief gezonden heb aan den Soeltan van Turkije, waarop ik nog geen antwoord heb ontvangen" — "for it is now a month since I sent a letter to the Sultan of Turkey, to which I have not yet received an answer."

While the Atjèhnese envoys were still at Riouw, the Indische Regeering learned that in early [[January 1873]] an Atjèhnese delegate had been sent to the French government to seek help and protection. "De gezanten hieromtrent ondervraagd toonden zich 'ondubbelzinnig ten hoogste verbaasd'" — "The envoys, questioned on this matter, showed themselves 'unequivocally most astonished.'" A Dutch warship carried the embassy back to [[Singapore|Singapore]] and thence to Atjèh at the end of January [[1873]]. After their departure from Singapore it emerged that they had paid visits to the consuls of [[United States of America|America]] and [[Italy|Italy]], "hun voorstellende tractaten met Atjèh te sluiten, omdat Atjèh door Nederland bedreigd werd" — "proposing to them to conclude treaties with Atjèh, because Atjèh was threatened by the Netherlands."

## The declaration of war

"Wanneer men bedenkt, dat al ons streven der laatste jaren gericht was geweest op het regelen onzer betrekkingen met Atjèh in dier voege, dat vreemde vestiging er zou zijn uitgesloten, dan laat het zich verklaren, dat de berichten uit Singapore de ernstigste complicaties deden vreezen. Onze Regeering achtte langer dralen thans ongeoorloofd. Besloten werd den Vice-President van den Raad van Indië F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen als Regeeringscommissaris naar Atjèh te zenden om den Soeltan opheldering te vragen omtrent zijn trouweloos gedrag en van hem de erkenning onzer souvereiniteit te eischen. Maar Atjèh weigerde alle opheldering. Toen maakte Nieuwenhuyzen van zijn bevoegdheid gebruik om 26 Maart 1873 aan Atjèh den oorlog te verklaren" — "When one considers that all our efforts of recent years had been directed to the regulation of our relations with Atjèh in such a way that foreign settlement there would be excluded, then it is explicable that the news from Singapore gave rise to fear of the most serious complications. Our Government deemed further delay now impermissible. It was decided to send the Vice-President of the Council of the Indies F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen as Government Commissioner to Atjèh, to demand of the Sultan an explanation concerning his faithless conduct and to require of him the recognition of our sovereignty. But Atjèh refused all explanation. Then Nieuwenhuyzen made use of his authority to declare war on Atjèh on 26 March 1873."

## Prior career and selection as commissioner

Before his mission to Atjèh, Nieuwenhuyzen had served as *commissaris* at [[Banjermasin|Banjermasin]] and as resident of [[Riouw|Riouw]], where he had concluded the [[Siak-tractaat (1858)|Siak treaty]] that extended Dutch sovereignty over the entire east coast of Sumatra without a single soldier being involved. He was known as a follower of [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Van de Putte]] and his opinions; the [[Raad van Indie|Raad van Indie]]'s unanimous advice of the end of [[1870]] — warning against all the consequences of a war with Atjeh — had been signed by Nieuwenhuyzen himself. [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Minister Fransen van de Putte]] pointed to Nieuwenhuyzen when the Governor-General telegraphed that the original commissioners [[Schiff|Schiff]] and [[Van de Wall|Van de Wall]] were unavailable (one sick, the other unable to leave his post); the Minister's telegram of [[18 February]] [[1873]] stated: "Misschien is vice-president Raad van Indie in de tegenwoordige omstandigheden meest geschikt, daar welligt gouverneur Sumatra's Westkust ook niet weg kan." [[Loudon|Loudon]], however, had already asked Nieuwenhuyzen before receiving this telegram.

The Sultan of Atjeh had declared that he did not wish to have dealings with Nieuwenhuyzen, precisely because Nieuwenhuyzen had concluded the Siak treaty that altered Atjeh's borders in ways the Sultan had never recognised. [[Insinger|Insinger]] in the [[Tweede Kamer debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|Tweede Kamer debate]] called Nieuwenhuyzen "the most unwelcome person to come into contact with the Sultan of Atjeh," and argued that selecting this man was what one would do if one wished war, whereas the governor of Sumatra's Westkust was the man designated by the treaty of 1858 for all relations with Atjeh.

## The ultimatum of 22 March 1873

On [[22 March]] [[1873]], Nieuwenhuyzen delivered an ultimatum to the Sultan demanding recognition of Dutch sovereignty as the primary condition, with only a short period for compliance. A manifest was simultaneously sent ashore stating: "This manifest will be delivered to the Sultan today, and as soon as its receipt is confirmed, it will be further confirmed by the firing of some projectiles into or near the Sultan's residence, from which we are only 3,600 metres distant." The ultimatum changed the originally stipulated 14-day period to 24 hours — a modification that [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] later took "entirely upon myself," arguing that once blood had been shed, article 6 of the original instruction (which envisaged only sovereignty on paper) was no longer applicable and the right of establishment must be reserved. [[Haffmans|Haffmans]] in the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]] characterised the entire proceeding as one in which "the commissaris fell, as they say, with the door in the house; he came equipped for war and brought the war."

## The failed first expedition and its aftermath

Nieuwenhuyzen's mission ended in the disastrous [[First Atjeh expedition (March-April 1873)|first Atjeh expedition]]. [[Nierstrasz|Nierstrasz]] in the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]] called Nieuwenhuyzen "the truly guilty one" while noting that he lived in Arnhem on a pension of 12,000 guilders, whereas "the poor Van Daalen" — the colonel blamed for the first expedition's failure — could not even receive his pension.

**Dr. R. Roolvink** was a Dutch Malay scholar who, as [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] records in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises, recovered the long-lost Arabic manuscript of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq li-dafʿ az-zindīq*]] of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] in the collections of the Bataviaas Genootschap in Jakarta — KBG 420 Mal. (not in the catalogue). The recovered manuscript, designated **A** in Voorhoeve's apparatus, was repeatedly useful for the correction of the Maxwell manuscript of the *Ḥujjat*.

## The recovery of the Arabic manuscript of the *Ḥujjat*

The Arabic manuscript of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat*]] had been noted as early as [[1866]] by [[Herman Neubronner van der Tuuk|Van der Tuuk]] in *BKI* 13, p. 464, but had long been thought lost. Roolvink's recovery of the manuscript — KBG 420 Mal., not in the catalogue of the Bataviaas Genootschap — made it possible for Voorhoeve to collate the Arabic text with the Malay text of the Maxwell manuscript. Comparison showed that the copyist, working from a complete Malay exemplar, had transcribed only the Arabic words and sentences written in red ink, producing an incoherent text that nonetheless contains fewer copyists' errors than the Maxwell manuscript and was repeatedly useful for its correction.

## Other services

Roolvink is also acknowledged by Voorhoeve — alongside **Dr. Johns** in London — for having looked at the India Office Library manuscript Loth 1033 of the [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*Tamhīd*]] of [[Abu Syakur as-Salimi|Abū Shakūr as-Sālimī]] in Jakarta, where the passage missing from Raniri's exemplar (and from the Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 5664) is preserved.

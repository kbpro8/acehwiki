**G. F. W. Borel** was a Dutch *Kapitein der Artillerie* (Captain of Artillery), Ridder van de [[Militaire Willemsorde|Militaire Willemsorde]], and bearer of the Eresabel, who served in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] between [[1873]] and [[1878]] and wrote the critical military history *[[Onze vestiging in Atjeh (Borel, 1878)|Onze vestiging in Atjeh, critisch beschreven]]* ('s-Gravenhage: D. A. Thieme, [[1878]]). As a participant-observer he supervised the debarkation of the siege guns at [[Oeleh Leh]] and personally traced the new zuider-face battery on the bastion of [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] on the morning of [[14 August]] [[1874]], when the Atjehneezen fired a twelve-shot salvo that passed only inches from him and his officer. His book and his March [[1878]] article *[[Een vaderlandsch belang (Borel, 1878)|Een vaderlandsch belang]]* in *Het Vaderland* together form the principal published record of his thinking on the conduct of the war.

## Service in Atjeh

Borel served with the artillery of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] throughout the second phase of the Atjeh War. He was present at the siege works around the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] in January [[1874]] and remained with the [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]] after [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s departure in April [[1874]]. On [[14 August]] [[1874]] he laid out the new zuider-face battery on the bastion of the southern face of K. Radja — a battery first armed and fired on [[18 August|18]] and [[22 August]] [[1874]] — under point-blank Atjehnese fire; he records that the twelve-shot enemy salvo missed him and his officer "slechts met eenige duimen." He also supervised the debarkation of guns at the [[Oeleh Leh|zeehoofd]], an operation he describes as one of the most damagingly neglected of the whole occupation.

## The Book and the Brigade Proposal

Borel finished *Onze vestiging in Atjeh* at 's-Gravenhage on [[27 August]] [[1878]]. In the *Voorberigt* he declares that, with the war already in its sixth year, "het wel niet voorbarig heeten" to pronounce a critical judgement, and he acknowledges that his work is in many respects "gebrekkig en onvolledig," but insists that his aim was to represent "de feiten en toestanden naar waarheid, zonder overdrijving en zonder mij door eenige partijschap te laten leiden." The book is in fact strongly partisan: it is a sustained indictment of [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]]'s [[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]] after the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]], and an equally sustained vindication of [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]].

On [[1 March|1]], [[2 March|2]] and [[3 March]] [[1878]] Borel published in *Het Vaderland* a three-part article entitled *[[Een vaderlandsch belang (Borel, 1878)|Een vaderlandsch belang]]*, proposing the formation in the Netherlands of an expeditionary *[[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]]* of three to four thousand men — infantry, artillery and engineers — to be raised from Dutch volunteers, sent directly to Atjeh (not via Java's "ongezonde garnizoenen"), armed with steel [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]], and bound to a strict two-year engagement. He amplified the proposal in *De Militaire Spectator*, No. 7, jaargang [[1878]]. He estimated the cost at six million guilders for the first year and ten to fifteen million for the whole two years, to be raised as an *oorlogsleening* and ultimately repaid, he suggested, in Acehnese pepper.

## Intellectual Posture

Borel writes as a professional artilleryman and a moralist. He repeatedly invokes foreign campaigns as tactical analogues: the German pursuit after Sédan ([[1870]]), the Russian failure before Plevna ([[1877]]), the French expedition against Algiers ([[25 May]] [[1830]]) quoted from Camille Rousset's *L'Histoire de la guerre de Crimée*, and the English Abyssinian and Ashantee expeditions as proof that Europeans need no "acclimatiseren" in tropical climates. His characterisation of the [[Atjehnees volkskarakter (Borel)|Atjehnees volkskarakter]] — "woest, dapper, vrijheidlievend, onafhankelijk, fanatiek," "uit eene geheel bijzondere stof gekneed" — fixed a Dutch military stereotype even as he saluted the Atjehneezen as "een schitterend voorbeeld van wat een volk vermag dat zijne onafhankelijkheid wil bewaren." He closes the book with the wish that, if his brigade proposal is adopted, the words of Rousset on the Algiers expedition — "rien n'avait été négligé pour le succès de l'expédition" — may apply to the Dutch in Atjeh.

## Significance

Borel is the principal Dutch critical voice on the conduct of the Atjeh War between [[1873]] and [[1878]]. His book is one of the few primary sources that narrates Dutch reverses — the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|échec at Lampoe Oek]], the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|disaster at Longbatta]], the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|catastrophe at Atoeh]] — in full tactical detail, and his advocacy of [[Mobiele colonnes (Borel)|mobiele colonnes]] over the [[Posten-systeem in Atjeh|posten-systeem]] anticipates the later *maréchaussée* system associated with [[Johannes van Heutsz|van Heutsz]].

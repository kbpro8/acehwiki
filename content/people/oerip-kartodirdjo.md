**Oerip Kartodirdjo** — published in the volume as *mr. Oerip Kartodirdjo* — was an Indonesian legal scholar who, in the early 1920s, served as secretary of the Leiden commission of professors that produced the *[[Proeve-Oppenheim]]* — a *proeve van een staatsregeling voor Nederlandsch-Indië* (draft state regulation for the Netherlands Indies). His name is recorded in Mr. Dr. [[L. J. J. Caron|Caron]]'s chapter *De bestuursvormen* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]), which describes the *Proeve* as having been compiled by "a commission of professors in Leiden" to which, "besides Prof. Oppenheim, the professors [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], Van Vollenhoven, [[J. H. Carpentier Alting|Carpentier Alting]] and Kleintjes contributed, while an Indonesian, mr. Oerip Kartodirdjo, acted as secretary."

## Significance

The naming of Kartodirdjo as secretary of the *Proeve-Oppenheim* commission is, in the context of *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]*, the only explicit mention of an Indonesian contributor to the volume's account of late-colonial constitutional thought. His role as secretary to a commission of senior Dutch professors working on a draft constitution for the Indies — in the early 1920s, before the [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]] — places him at the intersection of Dutch colonial legal scholarship and the emerging Indonesian nationalist movement. The *Proeve-Oppenheim* itself was to exercise considerable influence on the Wet op de Indische Staatsregeling (Law on the Indies State Regulation) of [[1925]], though — as Caron notes — the final law fell short of the autonomous Indies government that the *Proeve* had envisaged.

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Kartodirdjo's significance is indirect: the *Proeve-Oppenheim* commission on which he served was attempting to constitutionalise an Indies administrative order whose foundations, in the Atjeh-derived [[Korte Verklaring|*korte verklaring*]] policy of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], lay in the Atjeh War and its aftermath.

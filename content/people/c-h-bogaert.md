**C. H. Bogaert** was a lieutenant-at-sea 1st class of the Dutch navy, commandant of the ship *Timor*, who was the principal agent in the submission of the east coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] to Dutch authority in [[1873]]-[[1875]]. "Bogaert was on his ship master of the coast, he had on shore and aboard continual contact with the chiefs, he did not only blockade, he regulated and governed on his own initiative; he was everywhere honoured and feared, and his name still lives on among the Atjehers."

## The Submission of the East Coast

Already long before the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]], on [[7 May]] [[7 May]] [[1873]], [[Edi|Edi]] on the east coast had hoisted the Dutch flag. Two years later the whole east coast, with the exception of [[Simpang Olim|Simpang Olim]] and [[Langsar|Langsar]], was brought under Dutch administration, "chiefly through the efforts of the commandant of the Timor, the lieutenant-at-sea 1st class C. H. Bogaert, although here and there not without struggle. Thus on [[21 September]] [[21 September]] [[Edi-Tjoet|Edi-Tjoet]] was punished, on [[22 October]] [[22 October]] Simpang-Olim, on [[4 November]] [[4 November]] the kampong Gloempang on the Djolok-rivier."

## Significance

Bogaert's role on the east coast in [[1873]]-[[1875]] illustrates the part played by individual naval officers in the extension of Dutch authority over the [[Onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]] of Atjeh. His combination of blockade, negotiation, and direct administration — without specific instructions from the commander-in-chief — made him, in the source work's phrase, "master of the coast," and his name was still remembered among the Atjehers at the time of writing.

**Aeilco Zijlker** was the Dutch entrepreneur who, in [[1883]], conducted the first successful petroleum test-borings near [[Pangkalan Brandan]] in the *Landschap* (self-governing territory) of [[Langkat]] on Sumatra's East Coast, thereby inaugurating the Dutch colonial petroleum industry. His [[1884]] concession from the Sultan of [[Langkat]] became the legal foundation of the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] when it was formed in [[1890]], the year in which Zijlker sold his petroleum business for ƒ371,000.

## Test-borings and discovery

Zijlker began prospecting for petroleum near [[Pangkalan Brandan]] in [[1883]], at a time when the region lay immediately adjacent to the still-contested territory of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and was part of the *Landschap* Langkat, governed by its own ruler. The borings established commercially exploitable quantities of petroleum. In [[1884]] Zijlker secured a formal concession from the Sultan of [[Langkat]], providing the legal title that underpinned all subsequent development.

Dr. T. Volker describes Zijlker's enterprise as one of the most significant early events in the economic history of Sumatra's East Coast, but notes that the industry he founded was exposed from the outset to "one of the serious dangers" (*"een van de ernstige bedreigingen"*) in the form of [[Acehnese raids on East Sumatra|Acehnese raiding parties]], which continued to threaten the northern plantation and petroleum belt until the pacification of [[1899]].

## Sale to the Koninklijke

By [[1890]], when the [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] was formally constituted on the basis of Zijlker's concession, he sold his petroleum business for ƒ371,000. The Koninklijke built its first refinery at [[Pangkalan Brandan]] and expanded production until by [[June 1892]] it was producing 18,000 cases of petroleum per month. Under [[A. J. B. Kessler|Kessler]] and subsequently [[H. W. A. Deterding|Deterding]], the Koninklijke grew into the dominant force in Dutch colonial oil — eventually merging with the Shell Transport and Trading Co. to form the entity now known as [[Royal Dutch Shell]].

## Significance

Zijlker's initiative at Pangkalan Brandan in [[1883]] placed Sumatra's East Coast — in immediate proximity to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — at the origin point of one of the twentieth century's largest petroleum concerns. The ƒ371,000 he received in [[1890]] was modest compensation for an enterprise that within seven years was exporting petroleum worth ƒ31,000,000 annually. His name is borne by a street in present-day Pangkalan Brandan.

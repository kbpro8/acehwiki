**Goossens** — *Assistent-Resident* in the Dutch colonial civil administration — was the Dutch official who, as *assistent-resident* of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjèh]] in [[March 1889|March]] [[1889]], persuaded the governor [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] to refuse permission to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]] to visit Atjèh incognito, on the ground that the visit would awaken distrust and jeopardise the pending submission of the [[Kemala-party|Keumala-party]]. The refusal, frustrated Snouck Hurgronje's Atjèh plan for two years, until his eventual residence at Atjèh from [[July 1891|July]] [[1891]] to [[February 1892|February]] [[1892]] under the next governor [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe van Meerdervoort]].

## The March 1889 Refusal

When Snouck Hurgronje, having arrived at [[Pinang|Pinang]] on his way to visit Atjèh incognito — either as Abd al-Gaffār or as unofficial European, avoiding the Dutch administrative posts on Atjèh's North Coast — was met by the Dutch consul [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]] with a Buitenzorg message of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1889]] that he must give up his plan and travel directly to Batavia, the immediate cause was the advice of the *assistent-resident* Goossens of Groot-Atjèh. The governor [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] had laid the matter before Goossens, and that official thought it better to fend off Snouck Hurgronje's visit.

Goossens had objections, and it was not difficult for him to persuade the governor that the political situation did not allow the execution of the travel plan. The journey would awaken distrust. Abd al-Gaffār's origin the Atjèhers would surely discover, and his attempt would be connected with the Dutch striving for contact with the pretendent-sultan and the heads of the war party; the pending submission of the Keumala-party, the probability of coming success, would be put in the balance — and that must not be! The governor said so on [[25 March]] [[25 March]] [[1889]] to Buitenzorg, whereupon a missive of the *gouvernements secretaris* of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] to the heer Snouck Hurgronje at Pinang meant the prohibition to remain there: he had to travel directly to Batavia.

## The Succession by Ruyssenaers

Goossens was succeeded as *assistent-resident* of Groot-Atjèh by [[Ruyssenaers|Ruyssenaers]], who — reading in the files of the botched visit — understood that a blunder had been made. He spoke of it with the general [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]], who now also began to grasp something of the value of a scholarly religious-political Atjèh-reconnaissance as Snouck Hurgronje had meant to make. The understanding would lead, in [[February 1891|February]] [[1891]], to Van Teijn's request for Snouck Hurgronje's "*spoedig mogelijk*" (as soon as possible) coming to Atjèh — the request that, after some delay, would result in the [[July 1891|July]] [[1891]]–[[February 1892|February]] [[1892]] residence.

## Damsté's Judgement

[[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]]'s judgement of Goossens is double-edged: "*Laten we hem loven, dien assistent-resident, die den heer Snouck Hurgronje zoo te zeggen de atjehsche deur voor de neus toegooide*" (Let us praise that assistant-resident, who so to speak slammed the Atjèhse door in the nose of the heer Snouck Hurgronje). The scholar would, in the years that followed, render invaluable services to the Netherlands, to Indië, and in particular also to Atjèh — to say nothing of his merits for science in general. But whether that would have gone the same way if he had wandered into the Atjèhse interior in [[1889|'89]]? And whether he would have come far in that interior? Or whether they would have let him wander far? Open questions! But it would have been: a hazardous, a highly dangerous experiment! Glad that it was not tried.

## Significance

Goossens is the Dutch official whose March [[1889]] refusal to permit Snouck Hurgronje's incognito visit to Atjèh frustrated the scholar's Atjèh plan for two years. The refusal — based on the *assistent-resident*'s fear that the visit would awaken distrust and jeopardise the pending submission of the Keumala-party — was, in Damsté's retrospective judgement, perhaps as well; the wandering of the scholar through the Atjèh interior would have been a hazardous and highly dangerous experiment. The eventual residence of Snouck Hurgronje at Atjèh from [[July 1891|July]] [[1891]] to [[February 1892|February]] [[1892]] — under the next governor Pompe van Meerdervoort and the next *assistent-resident* Ruyssenaers — would produce the *[[Verslag omtrent de religieus-politieke toestanden in Atjeh|Verslag]]* of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]] that is the foundational document of the modern Dutch understanding of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]].

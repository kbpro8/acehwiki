**The Queen of Aroe** was the consort of the [[Radja of Aroe|radja of Aru]] who, after her husband's death in the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|fall of Aroe]] to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1539]], took to the field in person against the Atjehnese plunderers, was driven back, and then fled — first to [[Malacca]], then to [[Bentan]], and finally to [[Djohor|Johor]] — where she married the [[Sultan of Djohor|Sultan of Johor]] and so brought Johorese arms back against the Atjehers in a counter-attack that briefly retook [[Aroe]]. Her flight and remarriage are the closing movement of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]] as told by [[Mendez Pinto]], and they record the only documented female counter-stroke of the early Atjehnese wars.

## During Pinto's embassy (October 1539)

When [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] arrived in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] on [[5 October]] [[1539]], the radja — at work on the trench he had thrown up against the expected [[Atjehsche vloot|Atjehnese fleet]] — broke protocol to present him to his wife, "a thing that very rarely took place". Pinto, the story goes, took this openness as a sign of how much the radja counted on Portuguese aid. As the Atjehnese fleet approached, the radja at once had his wife and the other non-combatants evacuated to the forest: "a sight to move one to pity, that queen seated on an elephant and accompanied by some forty or fifty old men, seeking a safe hiding-place".

## Counter-attack after the fall of Aroe

When the [[Radja of Aroe|radja]] fell in the breach — betrayed, Pinto reports, by an Atjeh-bribed *Cacis* — the queen "made an attempt to avenge the death of her husband, fell upon the plunderers, but was forced to give up the fight". She then made her way to a place Pinto calls *minha cumbaa* — which De Roo de la Faille decodes, through the *bedekte taal* lens, as *minjak sembawa*, the white earth-oil of Aroe, "minjak tanah" being the famous medicinal petroleum of [[Pangkalan Soesoe]]. From there she sailed to Malacca for help.

## Flight to Malacca, Bentan and Johor

The Portuguese governor [[Pero de Faria]] received her with honour but had no armed force to send. She then withdrew to [[Bentan]] and sought help from [[Djohor|Johor]]. "The sultan married her, fell upon the Atjehers at Aroe, and retook it", De Roo de la Faille recounts; though Aroe was finally lost for good, and "with the realm of the Djao, as the Portuguese wrote *Jaoa*, it was done forever". The queen's remarriage thus produced the only successful military counter-stroke against Atjeh in the war of [[1539]] — and even that was temporary.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Queen of Aroe matters as the only named female political-military actor of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]], and as the agent whose dynastic diplomacy — flight to Malacca, appeal to Bentan, marriage to Johor — drew the wider Malay world into the Aroe-Atjeh conflict. Her personal sortie against the Atjehnese plunderers, though unsuccessful, is the only recorded instance of female command in the early Atjehnese wars.

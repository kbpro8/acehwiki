**Lodewijk Willem Carel Gerard Nicolaas Netscher** was a Dutch colonial administrator and historian whose published work on the Malay states became a standard reference for late-nineteenth-century scholars of the archipelago, including [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]]. He is best remembered for his monograph *Djohor en Siak* — cited by Van der Kemp as *Netscher's „Djohor en Siak"* — which appeared in the bulletin of the colonial Institute and remains a principal source for the nineteenth-century history of the Siak Sultanate in east Sumatra.

## "Djohor en Siak"

Van der Kemp cites Netscher in a passage devoted to disentangling two contemporaries who happened to share the name Akil and who are easily confused. One is the comparatively obscure [[Said Akil]] (Syed Akil), third son of the rich Penang merchant [[Said Hussein|Syed Hussein]], who in [[1826]] intrigued his way into the office of Raja Mooda of Deli. The other is the far more consequential **Raja Akil of Sukadana** — the Siak crown pretender who, after years of importance in the political life of east Sumatra, ended his career in [[1828]] as prince of Sukadana on Borneo.

To distinguish the two, Van der Kemp directs the reader to two biographical sources. For Raja Akil the principal authority is Professor [[Veth|P. J. Veth]], who devoted an attractive life-sketch to him in *Borneo's Westerafdeling* (vol. II, pp. 150 ff.); the supplementary reference is to Netscher's *Djohor en Siak*, where the same figure is treated in the context of Siak dynastic history. The cross-reference appears in Van der Kemp's note on page 30 of his article.

## Significance for Acheh studies

Netscher's importance for the present article is that of an authoritative source on the Siak sultanate, whose internal feuds and relations with [[Acheh]], the Dutch and the British at [[Penang]] form part of the wider diplomatic picture of northern Sumatra in the early nineteenth century. By citing him alongside Veth, Van der Kemp draws on the most up-to-date Dutch scholarship available in [[1900]] for the dynastic history of a region that the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1824)|1824 treaty]] had partitioned into British and Dutch spheres of influence. As with [[Levyssohn Norman]], [[Hoek]], [[J. E. de Sturler|De Sturler]] and [[L. J. P. J. Jeekel|Jeekel]], Netscher's work belongs to that body of late-nineteenth-century Dutch colonial historiography that supplied the scaffolding for Van der Kemp's own archival researches.

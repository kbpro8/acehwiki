**Toeankoe Hasschim** — also written *Tuanku Hasyim* — was an Atjehese royal chieftain, guardian and father-in-law of the youthful pretender to the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]], who in the period of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] (1884–1886) had the pretender declared of age, proclaimed and hailed as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]], arranged the court according to ancient usage, and notified the various courts of the accession. His role as the principal political architect of the [[Restoration of the Sultanate|restoration of the Sultanate]] and his persistent correspondence with the coastal statelets to stir up the population and to collect money and men for the holy war made him, together with his son [[Toeankoe Ibrahim|Toeankoe Ibrahim]] and the religious leader [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]], one of the principal leaders of the Atjehese resistance in the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The Restoration of the Sultanate (1885–1886)

When, under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] (1884–1886), the Dutch had retired into the concentrated *stelling* around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and were to await patiently the enemy's submission, the enemy — abundantly supplied from the coasts — prosecuted the war in his own way. Toeankoe Hasschim, as guardian and father-in-law of the youthful pretender to the Sultanate, had the pretender declared of age and proclaimed and hailed as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]]. The court was arranged according to ancient usage, and notification of the accession was given to various courts.

## The Holy War Letters

Toeankoe Hasschim further wrote everywhere that the Hollanders had been driven back, that the war would be vigorously continued, and, in concert with the religious leader [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] — whose influence ever increased — sent the principal leaders to the coastal statelets to stir up the population, to embroil the well-disposed, to destroy Dutch influence, to call all to the holy war against the Dutch, and to collect money and men. Those who openly took part in this struggle were henceforth called *[[Muslemin|Muslemin]]*. He also sent his son-in-law, the *radja* of [[Madjapait|Madjapait]], to the [[Gayo|Gajoes]] in the interior — whom the Dutch had helped against [[Langsar|Langsar]] — to induce them to invade [[Langkat|Langkat]]. All the chiefs collected the war contributions. The Sultan's influence greatly increased. In all Atjeh and for all Atjehers he was — and remained, at the time of Kruijt's writing — the lawful head of the country. His decision was invoked in many disputes. From his name the orders for the war were everywhere given.

## The Negotiations under Van Teijn (1887–1891)

Under [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]]'s governorship, Toeankoe Hasschim — together with [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]] &c., who were little pleased with the ever-increasing influence of [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and therewith of the religious party — made himself heard from the side of the Kemala-party. He requested a meeting at [[Telok Semawé|Telok Semawé]]. The negotiations with the Dutch were protracted, and ultimately led to nothing — the Atjehers, with their known acumen, continually put off some decision, the Dutch administrative officials intrigued each in their own way in the direction of conciliation, and the missions to Kemala led only to the purchase of Sultan's appointments by the Dutch emissaries.

## The Death of January 1891 and the Son Toeankoe Ibrahim

In [[January 1891|January]] [[1891]] [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]] died suddenly. Toeankoe Hasschim's own death is not explicitly dated in Kruijt's brochure, but his son [[Toeankoe Ibrahim|Toeankoe Ibrahim]] — whom Kruijt describes as "den zoon van onzen erfvijand Toeankoe Hasschim" (the son of our hereditary enemy Toeankoe Hasschim) — was, by [[1891]]–[[1892]], travelling from [[Madjapait|Madjapait]] (where he had lived for years, as it were under Dutch protection) to [[Kemala|Kemala]], bringing money and people, while the Dutch administration paid almost no attention. This journey of Toeankoe Ibrahim — together with the appearance of [[Toekoe Nja Mamat|Toekoe Nja Mamat]] (a relative of the *radja* of [[Perlak|Perlak]]) with the enemy in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] — was one of the facts of greater significance that passed almost unnoticed while the Dutch administration was distracted by the pomp and circumstance of the friendly-war policy under [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe van Meerdervoort]].

## Significance

Toeankoe Hasschim is the principal political architect of the [[Restoration of the Sultanate|restoration of the Sultanate]] — the attempt to reconstitute the Atjehese sultanate under a youthful pretender as the political vehicle of the resistance to the Dutch. His proclamation of the pretender as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]], his notification of the accession to various courts, his letters to the coastal statelets, his dispatch of leaders to stir up the population, and his cooperation with the religious leader [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] together constitute the political framework within which the Atjehese resistance operated from [[1885]] onwards. The Sultanate he restored would remain the political symbol of the Atjehese resistance throughout the rest of the war; the Sultan he proclaimed — [[Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed|Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed]] — would be driven from [[Kemala|Kemala]] by [[Giegieng|Giegieng]] in [[December 1894|December]] [[1894]] and would flee to [[Kwalla Batoe|Kwalla Batoe]] near [[Pedir|Pedir]], from which the Dutch would attempt in vain to restore him to Kemala.

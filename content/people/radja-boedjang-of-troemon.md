**Radja Boedjang** was the bestuurder (ruler) of [[Troemon|Troemon]] when Dutch authority was re-established on the Westkust in [[1819]]. He was the son of one of Troemon's founders and became a powerful "pepper-vorst" (pepper-prince) whose conflicts with the Dutch over supplying Padris, sea-roof (piracy), and the slave trade marked the early years of Dutch-Troemon relations.

## The Mac Gillavry contract (1830)

In [[1830]], Resident **Mac Gillavry** of Padang concluded a contract with Radja Boedjang concerning opium and customs transfer, involving ƒ9,000/year compensation for opium. This contract was never ratified by the Regeering (colonial government), reflecting the uncertain status of Dutch-Troemon relations in this period.

## The copper duiten of Troemon

Radja Boedjang imitated the Sultan of Atjèh by having a large quantity of copper *duiten* (small coins) struck in England with his own mark. Copper coins with the inscription "nëgri Troemön" circulated in Atjèh, reflecting Troemon's aspirations to monetary sovereignty parallel to that of the Atjèh Sultanate.

## Family

Radja Boedjang's wife was **Nonna Gadoes**, daughter of **Kaatje Stolte** — a Dutch woman captured at Padang in [[1793]] by the French corsair Le Mèsme and an Atjèhsche *nachoda* (ship's captain). This mixed European-Atjèhnese ancestry gave the Troemon ruling family a distinctive character. Radja Boedjang died in [[1835]] and was succeeded by his son **Radja Moeda**, who in [[1840]] contracted with Kolonel Michiels (also not ratified) and received ƒ200 monthly fixed onderstand in [[1849]].

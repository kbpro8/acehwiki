**Colonel de Jongh** was a Dutch *overste* (colonel) of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who served as chief of the administrative service (*intendantuur*) in Atjeh during [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s command. In [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s narrative he is singled out for praise as the officer who, in the depths of the [[Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)|crisis of May–June 1874]] and the [[Bandjir flood of Kota Radja (December 1874)|bandjir of December 1874]], improved the wretched *voeding* (feeding) and care of the troops at a moment when the *bezettingskorps* was being starved by its own logistics.

## The Crisis of the Bezettingskorps

When Pel inherited the command in May [[1874]] the Dutch position was desperate. The *voeding* had been reduced to the *reglementaire ration* — often only *rijst* (*boeboer*). The hard-brood supply ran out fourteen days after the *hoofdmacht* left. The *slachtvee* was exhausted and over a hundred *stuks* were lost. The *kazerneloodsen* had no *zijwanden*; rain poured through. "Nimmer klonk een blij gezang binnen de wallen van K. Radja," Borel records. By the [[Bandjir flood of Kota Radja (December 1874)|December 1874 bandjir]] the situation was worse: more than five hundred sick had to be rushed on the first day to higher ground in the middle of the Kraton, the water rose to the roofs of the hospital barracks, more than five hundred sick and military died in the [[Cholera catastrophe at Kota Radja (February 1875)|February 1875 cholera catastrophe]] in the [[Kota Radja]] ambulances alone. The administrative service, under de Jongh, had to find food, *vivres*, *munitie* and *bouwmaterialen* through a debarkation system at [[Oeleh Leh]] that Borel calls the most damagingly neglected operation of the whole occupation.

## Borel's Judgement

Borel, who personally supervised the debarkation of the guns at the [[Oeleh Leh|zeehoofd]], warmly praises de Jongh for *verbetering* of the troops' feeding and care in the face of these conditions. The contrast is sharp with [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]], who had allowed the *achterlaadkanonnen* and one of the two companies of vesting-artillerie to be sent back to [[Java]] and who had promised that [[Kota Radja]] would become "eene aangename gezellige garnizoensplaats." De Jongh's work was, in Borel's account, one of the unseen labours by which the *bezettingskorps* held out through the worst months of the war.

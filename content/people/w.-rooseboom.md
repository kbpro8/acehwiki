**W. Rooseboom** — Willem Rooseboom ([[1843]]-[[1907]]) — was a Dutch military officer who served as governor-general of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipelago from [[1899]] to [[1904]]. He was appointed under a liberal minister of Colonies and was succeeded by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], who was appointed by the anti-revolutionary [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] ministry after the Catholic [[De Savornin Lohman|De Savornin Lohman]] had refused the governor-generalship.

## The Rooseboom-to-Heutsz transition

The transition from [[W. Rooseboom|Rooseboom]] to [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as governor-general marks the political-military hinge of the Atjehnese war. [[W. Rooseboom|Rooseboom]] — a military officer appointed under a liberal minister — was succeeded in [[1904]] by another military officer, [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], appointed by the anti-revolutionary [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] ministry. The appointment was unusual in two respects: [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] had first offered the post to the Catholic [[De Savornin Lohman|De Savornin Lohman]], who refused; and [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was, in the verdict of [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]], "een niet-rechtse militair" — a non-right-wing military officer. The continuity of military leadership thus cut across the partisan alternation.

## The Atjeh inheritance

[[W. Rooseboom|Rooseboom]]'s governor-generalship covered the period of the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|post-concentratie]] recovery, the [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|Oemar crisis]] of [[1896]] (just before his appointment), and the early [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] campaigns of [[1898]]-[[1904]]. The [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]] of [[1904]] was undertaken in the last year of [[W. Rooseboom|Rooseboom]]'s term and continued under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s assessment of the Atjeh situation on taking office in [[1901]] — that the *stelsel* of the previous decades had been a sequence of unco-ordinated expedients — was, in part, a judgement on the [[W. Rooseboom|Rooseboom]] years.

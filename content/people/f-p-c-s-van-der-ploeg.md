**F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg** — published as Ir. F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg (i.e., holding the Dutch *Ingenieur* engineering degree) — was a Dutch mining engineer who, in [[1946]], contributed the chapter *Mijnbouw* ("Mining") to the volume *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* edited by Dr. [[C. W. Wormser|Wormser]] (pp. 231–262). His chapter surveys the mining industry of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] — tin, gold, silver, coal and petroleum — and gives the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-adjacent East Coast of Sumatra (the sultanates of [[Langkat]], [[Deli]] and [[Serdang]]) a central place in the history of Indonesian petroleum.

## Treatment of the Sumatra East Coast petroleum industry

Van der Ploeg's account of Indonesian petroleum opens with the early Dutch reports of oil seeps in Sumatra: the *Itinerario* of [[J. H. van Linschoten|Jan Huygen van Linschoten]] (who brought back from Goa a sample of petroleum "from a spring on Sumatra, that flows pure balm") and the story of an oil spring at [[Deli]] "which burned on the sea and with which two Portuguese galleons were set alight." But the modern industry, he records, dates from [[1884]], when *Zijlker* (so spelled in the volume) secured a petroleum concession in the *Landschap* (self-governing territory) of [[Langkat]]: "The first to drill oil in exploitable quantity was Zijlker, and indeed in a concession in the Landschap Langkat in the year 1884. But it was not until 1890 that the Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij founded on this came into being." The [[Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke]] built its first refinery at Pangkalan Brandan in Langkat.

The chapter further records the parallel rise of the *Dordtsche Petroleum Maatschappij* (founded by the former governments' mining engineer Stoop, who began refining oil on Java in [[1889]]), the [[1903]] merger of the Koninklijke with the Shell Transport and Trading Co. of Sir Marcus Samuel (brokered by [[H. W. A. Deterding|Deterding]] after the death of the Koninklijke's first director [[A. J. B. Kessler|Kessler]] in [[1901]]), the [[1906]] foundation of the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Bataafsche]] as the production arm and the Anglo-Saxon as the transport arm, and the [[1911]] absorption of the Dordtsche into the Royal Dutch–Shell concern. After [[1912]] American interests also entered the Indies through the Nederlandsche Koloniale Petroleum Maatschappij, which secured good territories in [[Palembang]]. In [[1921]] a mixed enterprise, the *Nederlandsch-Indische Aardolie Maatschappij*, was created with government participation in management.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Van der Ploeg's chief significance is the placement of the [[Langkat]]–[[Deli]] petroleum industry — directly adjacent to Atjeh on the Sumatra East Coast — at the head of the modern Indonesian petroleum story. The same East Coast sultanates whose pepper [[G. J. van Hall|Van Hall]]'s chapter treats as already important in the sixteenth century are here shown to be, by the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the site of one of the world's major petroleum industries. The strategic significance of this for the late-colonial Atjeh question — atjeh as the northern neighbour of the East Coast oil belt — is implicit but unmistakable.

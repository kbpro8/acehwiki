**Al-Bukhari** (printed in the source as **Bukharl** or **Hukharl**, owing to OCR artefacts) was the author of the *maulid* entitled ***Maulid sharaful 'anam*** ("Birth of the Glory of Mankind"), the most widely used of the three maulids current in Acheh and the one employed especially at official kanduris. The work is called **Sarapulanam** in Java and **Charapha anam** (or **Charapha anani**) in Acheh.

As [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] explains, three maulids are in use in Acheh. Two are in rhyming prose alternating with songs of praise in verse: one was composed by al-Bukhari, the other by Jaʿfar al-Barzanjī. The third, entirely in verse, is also by al-Barzanjī. All three were lithographed at [[Cairo]] by Hasan at-Tochi Ahmad and published in a single volume along with other religious formulas; this collection enjoys the greatest popularity both at [[Mecca]] and throughout the whole Indian Archipelago.

The *Maulid sharaful 'anam* is the maulid recited especially at the official [[Kanduri (general)|kanduris]], including the [[Maulud (Mo'lot)|kanduri Mo'lot]] — the great feast held in the month of Rabiʿ al-awwal in honour of the birth of [[Mohammad (the Prophet)|the Prophet]]. The recitation, called *meulike* (from the Arabic *dikr*) in Acheh, is performed by the *teungku* and the *leubes* of the gampong, who assemble in the meunasah in the forenoon of the feast day. The prose pieces are recited by one person, with others of those assembled taking a turn to relieve the reader; the versified portions are chanted "in a particular fashion" that is very popular. About the middle of the *Charapha anam* comes a hymn of praise of moderate length, which all the experts present raise in chorus; while singing they rise from their places, and in their midst is placed a vessel containing incense, "the savoury smoke from which blends with the chant in honour of the Apostle of God". After this hymn of praise, the kanduri Mo'lot closes with a long prayer which is to be found at the end of the *Charapha anam*.

Snouck Hurgronje observes in a footnote that "according to the publication of at-Tochi; others assign a different name to the author" — that is, the attribution of the *Maulid sharaful 'anam* to a certain "Bukhari" is not universally accepted. The author is not to be confused with the great hadith scholar Imam al-Bukhari (d. [[870]] CE); the name "Bukhari" simply denotes an origin from Bukhara, and the maulid author is a different, much later figure.

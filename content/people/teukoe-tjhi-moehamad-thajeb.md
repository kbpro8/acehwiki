**Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Thajéb** was the [[oelèëbalang|oelèëbalang]] — self-governing official — of the *landschap* [[Peureula'|Peureula']] in the subdistrict [[Langsa|Langsa]] whose removal by the Dutch administration in [[1934]] was, Piekaar records, one of the "notorious removals" of prominent self-governing officials that marked the late colonial period and that illustrated both the readiness of the population to accept such replacements and the political costs of the policy. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats the removal of Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Thajéb — together with the [[1938|1938]] removal of [[Teukoe Oemar|Teukoe Oemar]] of Keumangan — as a defining episode of the late colonial administration.

## The 1934 removal

The removal of Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Thajéb in [[1934]] was, Piekaar records, one of the "notorious removals" of prominent self-governing officials. The removal "showed that replacement even of prominent self-governing officials by the population was accepted." The acceptance of the removal by the Atjèhnese population reflected, in Piekaar's reading, both the political weight of the Dutch administration and the disengagement of the population from the *oelèëbalang*'s cause. The removal also, however, illustrated a deeper problem: "a policy which in this respect gave the oelèëbalangs too much respect had to have its backlash on the confidence of the population in our policy."

## The category of progressive oelèëbalangs

Piekaar categorises Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Thajéb as belonging "to the category of progressive modern-oriented oelèëbalangs" — that is, the *oelèëbalang*'s who, like [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]] of Peusangan, combined their traditional authority with a modern orientation. The removal of so progressive an *oelèëbalang* was, in this sense, a particular loss: the Dutch administration had, in the name of administrative efficiency, removed the very type of collaborator whose progressive orientation had made him most useful to the colonial project.

## The comparison with Teukoe Oemar

The [[1934|1934]] removal of Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Thajéb is paired in Piekaar's account with the [[1938|1938]] removal of [[Teukoe Oemar|Teukoe Oemar]] of Keumangan. The two removals, Piekaar writes, "moreover showed that replacement even of prominent self-governing officials by the population was accepted. Since the former belonged to the category of progressive modern-oriented oelèëbalangs and the latter to the group of older still patriarchally-despotically governing administrators and both were removed for different reasons." The pairing illustrates the range of the Dutch removal policy — from the progressive to the patriarchal — and the consistency of the population's acceptance.

## Significance

The removal of Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Thajéb is, in Piekaar's account, one of the episodes that "had its backlash on the confidence of the population in our policy." The political costs of the removal — the disillusionment of the progressive *oelèëbalang*'s, the alienation of the population from the colonial administration, and the opening that the removal created for the religious-political movements of the late 1930s — would, in the events of [[1942]], become apparent.

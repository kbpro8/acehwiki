**Habib Abdurrahman az-Zahir** was an influential Arab-Acehnese religious scholar, statesman, and diplomat who served as regent of the [[Sultanate of Aceh]] and later commanded anti-colonial resistance forces during the Aceh War.

## Political and Diplomatic Role

Habib Abdurrahman served as prime minister (*Rijksbestuurder*) under the Acehnese monarchy. Prior to the invasion of [[1873]], he led an Acehnese diplomatic mission to Europe, visiting Turkey, France, and Britain to secure international protection for Aceh against Dutch territorial ambitions.

In [[May]] [[1875]], Habib Abdurrahman offered to negotiate peace with colonial authorities on condition that he be recognized as regent for the minor Sultan, but Dutch officials rejected the proposal. Consequently, he assumed active military leadership over resistance forces across Great Aceh.

## Military Leadership and Surrender

Habib Abdurrahman directed guerrilla operations that repeatedly penetrated Dutch defenses along the [[Concentrated Line]]. His energetic resistance prompted Governor [[Karel van der Heijden]] to launch aggressive offensive campaigns in [[1878]].

Faced with overwhelming military force, Habib Abdurrahman formally surrendered to General Van der Heijden on [[15 October]] [[1878]]. On [[24 November]] [[1878]], he was exiled to Mecca with a lifelong annual pension of 12,000 dollars provided by the East Indies government, where he resided until his death.

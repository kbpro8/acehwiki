**G.M. Verspijck** was a Major-General in the [[Dutch East Indies Army]] who served as second-in-command during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in [[1873]]–[[1874]].

## Tactical Planning and Command

Following the death of General [[Johan Harmen Rudolf Köhler]] in [[April]] [[1873]], General Verspijck established the Bureau of Military Equipment in Batavia to organize the second campaign. He authored an initial operational plan advocating a flank maneuver via Kuala Gigieng to bypass fortified river defense lines.

During active operations in Aceh, Verspijck commanded field forces during key engagements, including the [[Battle of the Tiban Line]] on [[14 December]] [[1873]] and the [[Battles of Lembu]] on [[25 December]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]]. On [[24 January]]–[[26 January]] [[1874]], he commanded the primary assault column that executed the [[Encirclement and Fall of the Kraton]].

## Public Controversy

Following the campaign, Verspijck published detailed critiques challenging Supreme Commander [[Jan van Swieten]]'s defensive tactics, sparking widespread public debate in Dutch military circles regarding colonial strategy in Sumatra.

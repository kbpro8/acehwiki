**Michielsen** was a Dutch *controleur* (controller) — a civil colonial official — serving in [[Atjeh]] in [[1876]], attached by the civil and military commander to military punitive and inquiry columns in the [[VI Moekims]].

## The Pantjoer investigation (April 1876)

After the [[Koewala Pantjoer patrol ambush (7 April 1876)|ambush of 7 April]], Michielsen accompanied the column of Major [[Majoor Jeltes|Jeltes]] into the VI Moekims to conduct a strict inquiry into what had happened in kampong Lambaroe. It produced, as usual (Meijer's dry comment), nothing but conjectures — with the single certain result that one [[Hadji Aboe of Lambaroe|Hadji Aboe]] of the kampong had taken part in the attack; his house was burned.

## The Lampagar inquiry (May 1876)

After the [[Night attack on Lampagar (2-3 May 1876)|disaster of 2–3 May]], he went with Major [[Majoor Boom|Boom]] to Lampagar for a new inquiry. Five neighbourhood heads — among them [[Hadji Soliman of Lampagar|Hadji Soliman]] — were summoned; Michielsen's first conviction, that heads and people had had a hand in the enemy's plan, led to the arrest of all the heads and the burning of the kampongs Lamtjoet and Lambaroe as punishment. To prevent the flight of the kampong population of Lampagar itself, he caused it to be announced that flight would likewise count as enmity and would be followed by fire; the daily fortification labour on the new work was laid on the population. When a wounded prisoner of [[4 May]] told of the plotting of [[Panglima Moeda of Piëng]], Michielsen came to the opposite conviction — that the heads had sufficiently warned the officers — and released them, warning that flight would again be followed by the burning of their houses. His inquiry and its reversals are preserved by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]).

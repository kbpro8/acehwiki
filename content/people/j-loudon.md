**J. Loudon** was a Dutch colonial administrator who served as [[Governor-General of the Netherlands India|Governor-General of Netherlands India]] from [[1 January]] [[1872]] until his honourable discharge by [[Royal Decree]] of [[17 December]] [[1874]], his term dominated almost entirely by the outbreak and early years of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. He took over the administration from his predecessor [[Mr. P. Mijer|P. Mijer]] on [[1 January]] [[1872]], and his governorship is chiefly remembered for the disastrous [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] of [[April 1873]] and the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] that captured the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] in [[January 1874]] but failed to end Acehnese resistance.

## Outbreak of the Aceh War

The immediate origin of the war lay in the long series of Dutch-Acehnese disputes over piracy, sovereignty on the Sumatran coasts, and the international guarantee of Acehnese independence contained in the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|tractaat of 1824]]. After the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1871|Sumatra-tractaat of 2 November 1871]] freed the Netherlands from the British reservation regarding expansion in Sumatra, the Loudon government moved to assert Dutch sovereignty over [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. In [[September 1871]] the controller [[E. R. Krayenhoff]] was sent aboard the *Djambi* to deliver a letter from the Governor of Sumatra's Westkust to the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan]], the fourteen-year-old [[Aladin Mahmood Shah]], who ruled under the regency of the Arab sayyid [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]].

When the sjabandar of Atjeh, [[Panglima Tiban Mohammed]], arrived in [[December 1872]] at Riouw with credentials from the sultan, the Indian government decided to support the sultan against the hostile Arab party — but only on condition of recognition of Dutch supremacy. After the [[Consul-General W. H. Read|Consul-General in Singapore]] reported on [[16 February]] [[1873]] that the Acehnese envoys had sought American and Italian intervention, Loudon's government concluded that "longer dralen was ongeoorloofd" and authorised the dispatch of a powerful naval force to demand clarification and recognition.

## The First Expedition (April 1873)

Loudon appointed [[F. N. Nieuwenhuysen]] as commissioner and [[J. H. R. Köhler|Major-general J. H. R. Köhler]] as military commander of what became the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]]. The expedition — three bataljons of the Indies army, a bataljon of the Barissan, and a naval landing division — sailed from Batavia on [[22 March]] [[1873]] and arrived at the roadstead of Atjeh on [[5 April]]. After the [[Battle of Kotta Mogat|failure at Kotta Mogat]] on [[8 April]] and the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|bloody fighting at the missigit]] on [[10 April]], General Köhler was mortally wounded on [[14 April]] while inspecting the captured mosque. A council of war on [[18 April]] advised against renewed attack, and on [[29 April]] the transport fleet left the roadstead of Atjeh — the first campaign had failed, chiefly because of "the unfamiliarity with the situation, the too low opinion of the enemy's strength and perseverance, and the consequent inadequate preparation of the expedition."

## The Second Expedition and Capture of the Kraton

Preparations for a second expedition were put in hand immediately. In the Netherlands troops were levied to bring the army up to strength, all troops were equipped with breech-loading rifles, and the artillery was considerably reinforced. The expedition, 6354 infantry besides officers, with cavalry, artillery and engineers, was placed under the retired Lieutenant-general [[J. van Swieten|J. van Swieten]], restored to active service, with [[G. M. Verspyck|Major-general G. M. Verspyck]] as second-in-command. After delays caused by cholera on Java and aboard the transports, the fleet arrived at Atjeh between [[20 November|20]] and [[29 November]] [[1873]]. On [[24 January]] [[1874]] the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] itself was found abandoned and occupied; the Dutch flag was hoisted amid the playing of the *Wilhelmus*. Two days later, on [[26 January]] [[1874]], the sultan died of cholera at Pagger Ajer, and on [[31 January]] Van Swieten issued his [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation]] annexing Groot-Atjeh by right of conquest.

## Domestic Reforms

Loudon's governorship also produced important legislative and administrative reforms. The [[ordonnantie]] of [[6 May]] [[1872]] established the penal code for natives (*wetboek van strafrecht voor inlanders*), which came into force on [[1 January]] [[1873]]; [[Mr. T. H. der Kinderen|T. H. der Kinderen]], president of the two high courts of Netherlands India, rendered great service in its drafting. The inspector-general of finances [[P. H. B. Motké]] was appointed to investigate the financial administration but died in [[December 1872]] before completing his task. The first steps were taken toward the great harbour works at [[Tandjong Priok]], and the agrarian regulations were amended by Royal Decrees of [[16 April]] [[1872]] and [[3 December]] [[1873]].

## The Conversie Quaestie and Dismissal

Loudon's downfall arose from the question of the conversion of communal into individual land ownership (*conversie van communaal in individueel bezit*). The general secretary [[H. D. Levyssohn Norman|Levyssohn Norman]], in July [[1874]], undertook a journey through Java and Madura to encourage such conversion — going beyond his formal instructions and acting on telegraphic authorisation from Loudon. The Minister of Colonies, [[Van Goltstein]], sent a telegram on [[4 November]] [[1874]] ordering the journey to be halted, and the Governor-General was requested to refrain from any further pressure for conversion and from issuing any general ordinance without consultation with the Opperbestuur. Loudon complied but at the same time requested honourable discharge, which was granted by Royal Decree of [[17 December]] [[1874]]. He was succeeded by [[J. W. van Lansberge|Mr. J. W. van Lansberge]].

**J. Loudon** was the Dutch colonial administrator better known as [[James Loudon]], who served as [[Dutch East Indies Government|Governor-General of the Dutch East Indies]] from [[1 January]] [[1872]] to [[17 December]] [[1874]]. His period in office was dominated by the outbreak of the [[Aceh War]], the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)]], and the preparation of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)]].

This entry preserves the shorter form of his name that appears often in nineteenth-century Dutch sources. In Aceh-related writing, "J. Loudon" and "James Loudon" refer to the same person.

## Role in the Aceh crisis

As governor-general, Loudon stood at the center of the Dutch response to reports from [[Singapore]] that Acehnese envoys had sought foreign support. The colonial government then moved toward armed intervention in [[Aceh|Atjeh]]. Later political debate focused on how far Loudon shaped that decision together with [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]].

## After the failed first expedition

When the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)]] failed, Loudon remained one of the main figures in the public argument that followed. [[H. B. van Daalen]] criticized the handling of the later inquiry and argued that writings such as [[Bemoediging]] defended Loudon too strongly while trying to shift responsibility onto others.

## Link with the second expedition

Loudon also remained in office during the preparation of the much larger second expedition under [[Jan van Swieten]]. That expedition took the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], but it did not end the war.

## Significance

**J. Loudon** is significant because the shortened form of his name appears again and again in Dutch accounts of the first years of the [[Aceh War]]. He was one of the main officials blamed, defended, and debated in the struggle over how the war began and why the first expedition failed.

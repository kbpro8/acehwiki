**Van Blokland** was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] captain of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] who, on [[7 March]] [[1896]], commanded the 72-man patrol that was ambushed near the Dutch temporary post of [[Anagaloeng]] in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — the engagement that became known as the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] and that produced the first officer fatality of the [[1896]] crisis, Lieutenant [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]]. Van Blokland himself was wounded in the engagement.

## The patrol of 7 March 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the patrol was sent out on [[7 March]] [[1896]] under Van Blokland and two lieutenants — of whom one was [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]] — with the object of proceeding westward from the extreme temporary post of [[Anagaloeng]] to deal with a band of Atjehnese who had, for some two weeks, been shooting at everyone they could hit from that direction. The patrol was 72 men strong. On the march out it was left relatively unmolested, but on the return march it was attacked by various bands and was hard-pressed to hold its own. Although it was only some 350 paces from Anagaloeng, it had to hold its ground for five long hours in order not to leave the dead and wounded in the enemy's hands.

## The relief by the Senelop command

The commandant at [[Senelop]] — hearing the rumour of the engagement — hurried to [[Lamsict]] with 40 men under lieutenants [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]] and [[Ketjen]] and from there to Anagaloeng. By taking up a flanking position and drawing the Atjehnese fire upon himself, the Senelop command gave Van Blokland the opportunity to retire. The casualties of the relief were: [[K. H. Westendorp|Westendorp]] and three subordinates severely wounded; Van Blokland, [[Stakman]], [[Van Hasselt]], the medical officer [[Van der Meer (medical officer)|van der Meer]], the adjutant-non-commissioned-officer [[Kruger (adjudant)|Kruger]] and 18 subordinates lightly wounded. Of the twelve killed on the patrol, three had to be left on the field.

## Significance

Van Blokland's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the commander of the [[Patrol of 7 March 1896 near Anagaloeng|patrol of 7 March 1896]] — the engagement that produced the first officer fatality of the [[1896]] crisis. His survival of the engagement — and the relief of his patrol by the Senelop command — is one of the principal narratives of the early days of the crisis, and his appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] establishes the chain of command that linked the temporary posts at Anagaloeng and Senelop in the early days of the [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar defection]] crisis.

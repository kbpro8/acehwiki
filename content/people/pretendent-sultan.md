**The pretender-sultan** (Dutch: *pretendent-sultan* or *z.g. pretendent-sultan*) was the young [[Toekoe Daoed]] (also spelled [[Toeankoe Daoed]] or [[Toekoe Haschim]]), who was proclaimed sultan at [[Indrapoeri]]—the main place of the [[XXII Moekims]]—in [[1886]]. Although the Dutch government never recognised him as the legitimate sultan of [[Atjeh]], he served as a symbolic figurehead for the resistance party, particularly during the negotiations attempted by [[Generaal Van Teyn]] in the late [[1880]]s.

## Proclamation and role (1886)

The pretender-sultan was proclaimed at [[Indrapoeri]] in [[1886]], shortly after the completion of the [[Concentrated Line]] around [[Koeta Radja]]. The war party—led by [[Panglima Polim]] and the spiritual leaders in [[Keumala]]—hoped that by giving the resistance a unified leadership figure, they could strengthen the morale and coordination of all co-combatants.

Although he was initially a minor figure—merely a young boy under the protection of the resistance leaders—his symbolic value was substantial. The Dutch government, under [[Generaal Van Teyn]], attempted to approach him with the goal of restoring the sultanate under Dutch sovereignty, combining this with a shipping regulation that would control all sea trade. This approach—based on the assumption that a recognised sultan could serve as a cooperative partner—failed because the resistance leaders, particularly [[Panglima Polim]], used the pretender as a negotiating tool without any intention of genuine submission.

## The defection and decline (1896-1903)

By the time of the [[1896]] strategic reversal, the pretender-sultan had been reduced to a mere puppet. The Dutch submission strategy—guided by [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]]—recognised that no treaty with a sultan or alliance with a powerful chief could pacify [[Atjeh]]. Once the active campaign led by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] began in [[1898]], the pretender-sultan lost whatever practical significance he might have had. By [[1903]], when [[Panglima Polim]] submitted, the pretender was described as "only a puppet, used by the [[Atjeh]] chiefs"—a figure whose only remaining significance, once in Dutch hands, was that he could no longer do harm.

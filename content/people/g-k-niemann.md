**G. K. Niemann** — fully **Gerrit Karel Niemann**, also cited as **Prof. Niemann** — was a Dutch scholar of Indonesian languages whose comparative study of the [[Achehnese language|Achehnese language]] with [[Cham language|Cham]] and other Indonesian tongues furnished [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] with one of the chief philological foundations for the ethnological introduction to *The Achehnese*, and whose *Bloemlezing uit Maleische geschriften* ("Anthology of Malay writings") became a standard reference of late nineteenth-century Malay philology. He is cited in Vol. I of *[[The Achehnese|The Achehnese]]* principally for two works: his treatise *"Bijdrage tot de kennis der verhouding van het Tjam tot de talen van Indonesië"* ("Contribution to the knowledge of the relation of Cham to the languages of Indonesia"), published in the *Bijdragen van het Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch Indië* for [[1891]] (The Hague, p. 27 et seq.); and his *Bloemlezing uit Maleische geschriften*, Part 2, p. 25, in connection with the title **Malikul Adil** borne by certain Achehnese judicial officials.

## Contribution to Achehnese Studies

Niemann's treatise compared the Achehnese language — which "exhibits noteworthy points of difference from the kindred tongues of neighbouring peoples" — with the languages of [[Cham language|Cham]] and [[Bahnar language|Bahnar]]. The comparison "has at the very outset given important results", in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s words, although the conclusions to be drawn regarding the kinship or historical connection between the peoples were, at the time of writing, still held back for want of further evidence. The Achehnese themselves have only popular traditions concerning their origin; outside the limits of this tradition, every Achehnese chief and *ulama* who takes any interest in the question has his own conjectures, "partly in conflict with the traditions and partly grafted on to them". Niemann's work supplied the only firm external check on such speculations.

## The Hindu-Element Question

Niemann's treatise is cited a second time in connection with the disputed question of [[Hindu influence on Acheh|Hindu influence on Achehnese origins]]. The theory of [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]] — cited by Niemann — according to which the *kawom* of the *Imeum peunet* (a particular Achehnese lineage-group) is of Hindu origin, "does not rest on popular tradition, but must be ranked among the learned conjectures of certain of the Achehnese". Niemann is thus invoked as an impartial recorder of an Achehnese learned tradition rather than as an endorser of it.

## The Bloemlezing and the Malikul Adil Citation

In *[[The Achehnese|The Achehnese]]* (Vol. I, p. 132), Prof. Niemann is cited for his *Bloemlezing uit Maleische geschriften* ("Anthology of Malay writings"), Part 2, p. 25, in connection with the title **Malikul Adil** borne by certain Achehnese judicial officials. The notice occurs in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s discussion of the *Teuku Hakim* — one of the three chiefs in the upper part of [[Daya|Daya]] — whose official title, he explains, "points unequivocally to the complete secularization of his office." Originally charged with the administration of justice, the holder of this title had, "within the memory of man," been "nothing more than an ordinary *dato'*." It is in this connection that Snouck Hurgronje adduces the parallel of **Malikul Adil**, "a common title for qadhis in Malay countries," as Prof. Niemann had already observed in his *Bloemlezing*. The point of the comparison is that certain Achehnese official titles, although now borne by purely secular chiefs, preserve in their names the memory of an earlier judicial or religious function.

The *Bloemlezing uit Maleische geschriften* — an anthology of Malay texts prepared for the use of Dutch colonial students and officials — was one of the standard works of Malay philology in the late nineteenth century. It remained a standard reference for the comparative study of Malay institutions and of the Arabic and Persian loanwords which had entered the vocabulary of government and law throughout the [[Malay Archipelago|archipelago]].

## Place in Dutch Colonial Scholarship

Niemann thus belongs, together with [[Pieter Johannes Veth|Veth]], [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]] and [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]], to that earlier generation of Dutch colonial scholars whose compilations and anthologies laid the groundwork for the more analytical studies of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s own generation. The *Bloemlezing* in particular remained a standard reference for the comparative study of Malay institutions and of the Arabic and Persian loanwords which had entered the vocabulary of government and law throughout the archipelago.

## Relation to G. H. Niemann

A second scholar of the same name, **[[G. H. Niemann|G. H. Niemann]]**, is cited separately by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] (Vol. I, p. 409) in connection with a note on the post-childbirth "drying over the fire" custom in the *Bijdragen* of [[1892]]. Whether the two Niemanns are related has not been documented, but they are clearly distinct persons, since their publications appear in successive years and on quite different subjects.

**G. K. Niemann** was a Dutch scholar of Indonesian languages whose comparative study of the Achehnese language with Cham and other Indonesian tongues furnished [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] with one of the chief philological foundations for the ethnological introduction to *The Achehnese*. He is cited in Vol. I principally for his treatise *"Bijdrage tot de kennis der verhouding van het Tjam tot de talen van Indonesië"* ("Contribution to the knowledge of the relation of Cham to the languages of Indonesia"), published in the *Bijdragen van het Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch Indië* for [[1891]] (The Hague, p. 27 et seq.).

## Contribution to Achehnese studies

Niemann's treatise compared the Achehnese language — which "exhibits noteworthy points of difference from the kindred tongues of neighbouring peoples" — with the languages of Cham and Bahnar. The comparison "has at the very outset given important results", in Snouck Hurgronje's words, although the conclusions to be drawn regarding the kinship or historical connection between the peoples were, at the time of writing, still held back for want of further evidence. The Achehnese themselves have only popular traditions concerning their origin; outside the limits of this tradition, every Achehnese chief and *ulama* who takes any interest in the question has his own conjectures, "partly in conflict with the traditions and partly grafted on to them". Niemann's work supplied the only firm external check on such speculations.

## The Hindu-element question

Niemann's treatise is cited a second time in connection with the disputed question of Hindu influence on Achehnese origins. The theory of [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]] — cited by Niemann — according to which the *kawom* of the *Imeum peunet* (a particular Achehnese lineage-group) is of Hindu origin, "does not rest on popular tradition, but must be ranked among the learned conjectures of certain of the Achehnese". Niemann is thus invoked as an impartial recorder of an Achehnese learned tradition rather than as an endorser of it.

## Relation to G. H. Niemann

A second scholar of the same name, **[[G. H. Niemann]]**, is cited separately by Snouck Hurgronje (Vol. I, p. 409) in connection with a note on the post-childbirth "drying over the fire" custom in the *Bijdragen* of [[1892]]. Whether the two Niemanns are related has not been documented, but they are clearly distinct persons, since their publications appear in successive years and on quite different subjects.

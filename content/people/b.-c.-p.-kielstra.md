**B. C. P. Kielstra** — *de Heer Kielstra* (Mr. Kielstra), in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s phrase — was the Dutch colonial historian whose multi-volume *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* (Description of the Atjeh War) is the principal secondary source cited throughout Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, and whose article *Geen strijd maar getuigenis* (Not Combat, but Testimony), published in the *Tijdspiegel* (Mirror of the Times, a Dutch literary journal) of July [[1886]], is one of the three pamphlet-contributions to the [[1886]] Dutch political debate over the Atjeh policy that Van Heutsz adduces as the contemporary context of his own tract. He is, in Van Heutsz's apparatus, the standard authority — cited by *deel* (volume) and *bladzijde* (page) for documents, troop-strengths, and strategic judgements — through whose *Beschrijving* the documentary record of the Atjeh-oorlog's first twenty years had been made available to the Dutch colonial-policy public.

## The *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog*

Kielstra's *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* was a multi-volume historical description of the Atjeh-oorlog — at least three *delen* (volumes) by [[1893]] — that collected, annotated, and narrated the documentary record of the war from its [[1873]] outbreak through the early [[1880s]]. The work is Van Heutsz's principal secondary source throughout the [[1893]] tract: cited for documents, for troop-strengths, for the chronology of operations, and for the strategic judgements of the actors. The citations, as Van Heutsz reproduces them, take the form of *deel* and *bladzijde* references — the standard apparatus of a multi-volume historical work — and one of the *Beschrijving*'s *Bijlagen* (appendices) is the source for one of Van Heutsz's own central documents. The citations Van Heutsz makes are:

- *Kielstra's „Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog"*, cited on page 8 of the [[1893]] tract as the source of Generaal Pel's letter to the *Gouverneur-Generaal* (Governor-General) of *Nederlandsch-Indië* (Netherlands Indies) of [[30 April]] [[1875]] — *opgenomen als bijlage IV in het 2e deel van Kielstra's „Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog"* (included as Appendix IV in the second volume of Kielstra's Description of the Atjeh War). The Pel letter is the document Van Heutsz describes as "een uitmuntend gesteld stuk" — an excellently composed piece — and is the founding text of the [[Pel's Plan (30 April 1875)|30 April 1875 plan]] for the coastal closure of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

- *volgens Kielstra bedroeg de sterkte der krijgsmacht in 1876*, cited on page 12 of the [[1893]] tract for the Atjeh troop-strength in [[1876]] — *behalve de officieren- gemiddeld 3000 Europeanen, 180 Afrikanen en 5000 Inlanders* (excluding the officers — on average 3000 Europeans, 180 Africans, and 5000 Natives). The figure is Van Heutsz's quantitative measure of the over-extension the post-Van-Swieten plans had produced by [[1876]].

- *Zie Kielstra's beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog*, cited in the footnote on page 16 of the [[1893]] tract as the source for the Atjeh troop-strength table — the [[1 March]] [[1877]] figure of 348 officers, 9235 minderen, and 189 paarden, and the early-[[1878]] figure of 298 officers, 7730 minderen, and 144 paarden — that Van Heutsz reproduces to document the gradual drawdown of the Atjeh force in the *verzoeningspolitiek* (conciliation policy) year [[1877]].

- *In Kielstra's beschrijving van den Atjeh oorlog lezen wij, III deel, noot, bladz. 249, daaromtrent*, cited on page 17 of the [[1893]] tract for the judgement that it was the *vertoogen* (representations) of the newly appointed Chief of Staff, *den majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius*, that brought the *bevelhebber* (commander) to the June [[1878]] decision to abandon the coastal expeditions and attack the [[XXII Moekims]] directly. The citation — *III deel, noot, bladz. 249* (third volume, note, page 249) — is Van Heutsz's most precise reference to the *Beschrijving*, and is the source for the strategic argument that produced the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII Moekims campaign]] of July–October [[1878]].

## The article *Geen strijd maar getuigenis* (Tijdspiegel, July 1886)

Kielstra was also, in [[1886]], a participant in the contemporary Dutch political debate over the Atjeh policy — a debate Van Heutsz dates to the period after the *onverantwoordelijke* (irresponsible) dismissal of [[General van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] in early [[1881]] and the substitution of a *Civiel gouverneur* (Civil Governor) that followed. The debate was conducted in the colonial-policy press, and Van Heutsz adduces three pamphlet-contributions to it as the immediate context of his own tract:

> "om een juist inzicht te behouden over het tijdvak April 1881– Maart 1883 is het noodzakelijk daarbij tevens te lezen: ‚Phrasen en Feiten' van den Heer Laging Tobias, ‚Geen strijd maar getuigenis' van den Heer Kielstra, opgenomen in de Juli-aflevering van den Tijdspiegel 1886 en ‚Het Atjeh-vraagstuk' door W. Cool in den Militairen Spectator van datzelfde jaar".

The three contributions are the triad Van Heutsz recommends for the understanding of the *Civiel Gouverneur* (Civil Governor) regime of [[1881]]–[[1883]]: [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]]'s *Phrasen en Feiten* (Phrases and Facts) — the former Governor's own defence of his policy; Kielstra's *Geen strijd maar getuigenis* — published in the *Tijdspiegel* of July [[1886]]; and W. Cool's *Het Atjeh-vraagstuk* (The Atjeh Question) — published in the *Militairen Spectator* (Military Spectator, a Dutch military journal) of the same year. Kielstra's *Geen strijd maar getuigenis* — Not Combat, but Testimony — is the historical-critical contribution to the triad: the testimony of the historian of the Atjeh-oorlog against the phrases of its former Governor. The title itself is the polemical formula: where Laging Tobias had supplied the *Phrasen*, Kielstra supplied the *getuigenis* — the testimony of the documentary record.

## Kielstra as Van Heutsz's standard authority

Kielstra's role in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract is that of the standard authority — the historian whose *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* had made the documentary record of the Atjeh-oorlog's first twenty years available to the colonial-policy public, and whose *Geen strijd maar getuigenis* had entered the contemporary political debate as the historian's testimony against the political phrases of the Civiel-Gouverneur regime. The pattern of citation — Kielstra for documents, Kielstra for troop-strengths, Kielstra for the strategic judgements of the actors, Kielstra for the contemporary political debate — is the pattern of a secondary source Van Heutsz relies on throughout, and whose authority he invokes at each of the tract's diagnostic junctures: the [[Pel's Plan (30 April 1875)|30 April 1875 plan]] (p. 8), the [[1876]] troop-strength (p. 12), the [[1877]]–[[1878]] drawdown (p. 16), the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|June 1878 strategic reversal]] (p. 17), and the [[1881]]–[[1883]] Civiel-Gouverneur regime (p. 24). The *Beschrijving* is, in Van Heutsz's apparatus, the source-of-record for the first twenty years of the Atjeh-oorlog; the [[1893]] tract's originality is, in part, the synthesis of Kielstra's documentary record into a single diagnostic argument.

## Significance

B. C. P. Kielstra's significance in the Aceh Wiki History record rests on three things: he was the Dutch colonial historian whose multi-volume *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* is the principal secondary source cited throughout Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract — cited for documents (Generaal Pel's [[30 April]] [[1875]] letter, *Bijlage IV in het 2e deel*), for troop-strengths (the [[1876]] figure of 3000 Europeanen, 180 Afrikanen, 5000 Inlanders; the [[1 March]] [[1877]] figure of 348/9235/189; the early-[[1878]] figure of 298/7730/144), and for strategic judgements (the *III deel, noot, bladz. 249* attribution of the June [[1878]] reversal to Geij van Pittius); he was the author of *Geen strijd maar getuigenis*, published in the *Tijdspiegel* of July [[1886]] — one of the three pamphlet-contributions (with Laging Tobias's *Phrasen en Feiten* and W. Cool's *Het Atjeh-vraagstuk*) that Van Heutsz adduces as the contemporary context of his own tract; and his *Beschrijving* is, in Van Heutsz's apparatus, the source-of-record for the first twenty years of the Atjeh-oorlog — the work through whose citations the documentary record of the war's first phase had been made available to the Dutch colonial-policy public. His role in the Aceh Wiki History record is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the historiographical counterpart to the political role of the Governors: where the Governors made the policy Van Heutsz indicts, Kielstra made the documentary record Van Heutsz synthesises.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

[[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]]'s pamphlet cites Kielstra's *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog* as a primary document: for the position and health statistics of [[1874]]-[[1875]] (D. II, pp. 20, 50, 73-76, 94-99, 174-175, 194-199), the double troop strength figures refuting van Swieten's own returns (D. I, pp. 377-378), the state victories and the prisoners of [[1879]] (D. III, pp. 417-418, 458-459, 468-469), and the hopeful state of Atjeh in [[1880]] ("sedert September 1879 viel van geen onzer versterkingen een schot" — "since September 1879 no shot fell on any of our fortifications"). Verstege singles out, against Kielstra's own support of the government line, his final sentence at the close of Deel II: "dat de geschiedenis van den Atjeh-oorlog weinig ruimte geeft voor optimistische verwachtingen voor de toekomst" ("that the history of the Atjeh war gives little room for optimistic expectations for the future") — words in which Verstege invites the minister [[Sprenger van Eijk]] to study. Kielstra's lecture on the Atjeh question in the Indisch Genootschap (Indische Gids, December [[1883]]) is further quoted for the ministerial instruction of [[2 February]] [[1874]] in the annexation affair (see [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]]), for the Habib's negotiations and the sultanate question of [[1875]] (see [[Herstel van het sultanaat]]), and for the first publication of general Pel's long-misquoted letter of [[30 April]] [[1875]] (see [[Pel's missive to the Governor-General (30 April 1875)]]).

**ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs** — in Dutch scholarship **Abdalkādir al-Aidarrs** — was a prolific Indian-Arab author of the al-Aydārūs family, who died at [[Ahmadabad]] in [[1628]]. He was the uncle by marriage of [[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]] and so a kinsman of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]]. His many writings are preserved, including correspondence exchanged with a scholar at [[Mocha]]. His father had come from South Arabia to India in [[1551]]; his mother was an Indian. He is one of the figures of the Gujarati milieu from which Raniri himself sprang.

## The al-Aydārūs family

The al-Aydārūs family was one of the principal Ḥadramī-Indian religious families of the early modern period. The father of ʿAbd al-Qādir had come from South Arabia to India in [[1551]], and the family maintained — by correspondence and by study-journeys — the contact with the motherland that was characteristic of the Indian Arabs of mixed blood. The mother of ʿAbd al-Qādir was an Indian: a fact that places him, with [[Muhammad al-Addars|al-ʿAddārs]] and [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] himself, among the Indian Arabs of mixed blood who formed the Gujarati religious milieu from which the orthodox reaction of Acheh was to draw its principal Malay-Indonesian agent.

## Writings and correspondence

Many of ʿAbd al-Qādir's writings are preserved. They include correspondence exchanged with a scholar at [[Mocha]] — a fact that documents the Indian-Arab religious network that linked the coffee port of Yemen with the religious scholarship of Gujarat, and through Gujarat with the Malay-Indonesian world. The correspondence is one of the principal witnesses to the means by which the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.

## Significance

ʿAbd al-Qādir al-Aydārūs is one of the figures of the Gujarati milieu from which [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] himself sprang. The Indian-Arab religious families of mixed blood — the al-Aydārūs family, the family of [[Muhammad al-Addars|al-ʿAddārs]], and the family of Raniri himself — formed the network by which the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds was carried to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries; and the writings of ʿAbd al-Qādir, including his correspondence with a scholar at Mocha, are among the principal witnesses to this network in operation. The death of ʿAbd al-Qādir at [[Ahmadabad]] in [[1628]] falls in the very years in which his kinsman Raniri was beginning the composition of the [[Sirat al-Mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] in Gujarat, before his departure for [[Acheh]] in [[1637]].

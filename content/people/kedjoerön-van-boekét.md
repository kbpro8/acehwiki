**Kedjoerön van Boekét** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Meergebied]]

In February the hiding-place of [[Keutjhi' Saman]], the long-sought *panglima* of the ex-pretender sultan, was surprised; he fell with three adherents. Sixteen more were killed and a carbine taken in another action, among the dead being recognised a nephew of the [[Kedjoerön van Boekét]]. In May a *klewang* attack on a marching bivouac near [[Nalon]] cost one killed, three wounded and one carbine; more blows fell on the Telong band; more *ladangs* and hiding-places of Tgk. di Barat were cleared up in the [[Samarkilang|Samarkilangsche]]; and in July came the report that in most valleys of this mountain country submission was under deliberation — that month costing the resistance nineteen killed, sixty-three submissions and three precious carbines.

### From [[The resistance in Atjeh 1908–1912]]

Sixteen more were killed and a carbine taken in another action, among the dead being recognised a nephew of the [[Kedjoerön van Boekét]]. Then some months without much contact; in May a *klewang* attack on a marching bivouac near [[Nalon]] (one killed, three wounded, one carbine); another blow at the Telong band; more reports, more *ladangs* and hiding-places of Tgk. di Barat cleared up in the [[Samarkilang|Samarkilangsche]]; and at last in July the news that in most valleys of this mountain country submission was under deliberation — that month costing the resistance nineteen killed, sixty-three reports and three precious surrendered carbines.

### From [[Telongbende]]

The carbines came from a group of infantry returning from [[Samarkilang]], surprised in its marching bivouac at Tjampoeron, in which the European sergeant and brigade commander, six men and four convicts were killed and seven fusiliers and four convicts wounded. In January [[1911]] the Telongbende fired on Dutch patrols; the same year it "received the blow that had long been intended for it," losing sixteen men and a carbine, a nephew of the [[Kedjoerön van Boekét]] being recognised among the dead. On [[16 October]] [[1911]] the bulk of the band — twenty men with all its commanders — submitted, handing in the last three carbines lost at Tjampoeron.

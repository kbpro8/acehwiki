**F. Ch. Frank** was a European sergeant of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* in [[Atjeh]], twice decorated with the *[[Militaire Willemsorde|Militaire Willems-Orde]]*, who after nine years with the corps passed into the police service of the Netherlands Indies and was pensioned as a commissioner of the second class.

## Military service

Frank was born on [[30 May]] [[1867]]. In February [[1893]] he departed for the Indies as a soldier. He was promoted corporal in [[1894]] and sergeant in [[1896]], and served nine years with the Korps Maréchaussée.

In [[1905]] he was appointed knight of the Militaire Willems-Orde 4th class; the following year he was *Eervol Vermeld* (honourably mentioned); and in [[1908]] he was promoted to knight of the Militaire Willems-Orde 3rd class. The [[Overzicht van Onderscheidingen toegekend aan het Korps Maréchaussée in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1890-1930)|register of honours]] records the 3rd-class award under [[1909]] to *Eur. Sergt. Mar. F. Ch. Frank, Alg. Stb. No. 60535*.

## Police service

Frank left the army in [[1908]] and entered the police service in [[1909]]. He was pensioned in [[1928]] as *commissaris der tweede klasse*.

## Decorations

Besides the two classes of the Militaire Willems-Orde he was bearer of four *eereteekens voor Belangrijke Krijgsbedrijven* (decorations for important military operations) and of the silver medal for Faithful Service.

## Remembered by Snell

Colonel [[L. A. Snell]], interviewed for the same jubilee number, named Frank among "de eenvoudigen van ziel" — the simple of soul — who ought not to be forgotten when the great names of the corps were recited, listing him with [[Marks]], [[W. J. Mosselman|Mosselman]], [[J. C. Heijnen|Heynen]], [[Emond]], [[Rugebregt]], [[Salawono]], [[Kasir]], [[Wowor]], [[Nussy]] and [[Kromo-di-Koro]] as "de dappersten onder de dapperen" — the bravest among the brave.

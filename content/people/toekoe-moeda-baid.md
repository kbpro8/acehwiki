**Toekoe Moeda Baid** was the [[Uleebalang|hoeloebalang]] of the [[VII Mukims|VII Mukims]] in the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi der XXII Moekims]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], who [[Submission of Toekoe Moeda Baid (14 October 1878)|submitted to the Dutch on 14 October 1878]] — the day after the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman]] — but whose subsequent conduct appeared more and more treacherous, leading to his arrest on [[9 May]] [[9 May]] [[1879]], his transportation to Batavia in July [[1879]], and his banishment by the Governor-General to [[Banda|Banda]].

## Submission, 14 October 1878

After the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman on 13 October 1878]], the example was followed by several other chiefs, among them Toekoe Moeda Baid, who was sworn in and recognised in his dignity on [[14 October]] [[14 October]] [[1878]]. His submission, coming immediately after that of the Habib, was an event of considerable importance for the Dutch, as it brought the VII Moekims of the Sagi XXII under Dutch authority.

## Treason and Arrest, 9 May 1879

As the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign against the XXII Moekims]] developed, however, it more and more appeared that Toekoe Moeda Baid played a treacherous role. He was taken prisoner on [[9 May]] [[9 May]] [[1879]] and transported to Batavia in July [[1879]], where the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] banished him to [[Banda|Banda]]. His arrest removed a dangerous double-dealer from the theatre of war and coincided with the heavy fighting at [[Panteh Karang|Panteh Karang]] of [[6 May]] [[6 May]] – [[7 May]] [[7 May]] [[1879]].

## Significance

The case of Toekoe Moeda Baid illustrates the difficulty the Dutch faced in dealing with the Acehnese chiefs, many of whom — like the vorst of [[Gighen|Gighen]] in [[1876]] — submitted in appearance only, while continuing to act against the Dutch in secret. His arrest and banishment to Banda, the traditional place of exile for Indonesian political prisoners, removed him from the Atjeh theatre for the duration of the war.

## Earlier role: conflict with Habib Abdurrahman (1837–1860s)

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records an earlier chapter in the career of a *Toekoe Moeda Baid* — *[[Uleebalang|oeloebalang]]* of the [[VII Mukims|VII Moekims]] — that may refer to the same person at an earlier stage of life, or to an earlier member of the same family bearing the same title. The album's narrative places this Baid as the custodian of the young prince [[Toeankoe Machmoed|Toeankoe Machmoed]] — son of the deceased Sultan [[Sultan Saleiman Iskander|Saleiman Iskander]] (died [[1837]]) — at his *kampong* of [[Tjot Bada|Tjot Bada]]. [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]], wishing to install the prince as Sultan and to act as regent during the minority, marched on Tjot Bada at the head of two thousand men from the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] and compelled Baid to surrender the prince.

The same conflict surfaced again when the Habib sought the hand of Baid's sister [[Potjoet|Potjoet]] — the widow of Sultan Saleiman Iskander — in marriage. Baid accepted a payment of 400 dollars for the marriage promise, but repeatedly deferred the actual delivery of the bride until the Habib was forced to march on Tjot Bada a second time. The album also names Baid as the half-brother of [[Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed van Lamtenguh|Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed van Lamtenguh]] — the *oeloebalang* of [[Lamtenguh|Lamtenguh]] to whom the Dutch entrusted the safety of the terrain around [[Anagaloeng|Anagaloeng]] and [[Blang Tjoet|Blang Tjoet]] in [[1896]], after Baid himself *een dubbelzinnige rol gespeeld* ("had played a double role").

The chronological range — from the [[1837]] succession crisis to the [[1878]] submission and [[1879]] banishment — spans more than forty years, which is unusual but not impossible for a single career. The album does not address the question of whether the Baid of the [[1837]] succession crisis is the same individual as the Baid who submitted in [[1878]] and was banished in [[1879]], nor whether either is identical with the [[Teukoe Moeda Baid Setia Maharadja|Teukoe Moeda Baid Setia Maharadja]] whose Dutch coat-of-arms chain was granted in [[1888]] and revoked in [[1896]] — after the banishment to [[Banda|Banda]]. The most economical hypothesis is that the title *Toekoe Moeda Baid* was held successively by members of the same kinship line, and that the [[1896]] chain-revocation refers to a successor who had inherited both the title and the diplomatic consequences of the family's earlier unreliability.

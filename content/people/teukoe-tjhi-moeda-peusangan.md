**Teukoe Tjhi Moeda Peusangan**, called *jong van Peusangan* (young of Peusangan), was war leader of [[Peureula]] in the [[1873]]-[[1874]] period and later [[Uleebalang|oeléëbalang]] of [[Peureula]], father of [[Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb]].

## Life

Son of [[Teukoe Tjhi Peureula]], he was martial commander against [[Idi]]. Refusing to learn Koran reading (*meng-adji*) in youth, he had to leave land and wandered through [[Malaka]], earning livelihood with trade. Later stayed in [[Peudawa Rajeu|Peudawa Rajeu]] near [[Peureula]]. When Dutch came cruising along coast [[1873]]-[[1874]] sending negotiators ashore, the adventurous child had already returned as war chief next to his father.

Friendship with Dutch, established and maintained, often obliged him to secure land, people, property, even children against *djahats* from elsewhere who blamed peace with *kafirs*. Even before end of 19th century oil interests of *kafirs* made roads of friendship smooth; what now oeléëbalangs generally see, that under friendship and cooperation material interests best realize, he already saw a quarter century earlier. Petroleum cijns made old prince rich; in [[1913]] arrangement arose whereby cijns came into landscape treasury and oeléëbalang got fixed income.

In [[1910]] he sojourned on [[Java]]; administration then observed by *banta*, eldest of brothers/relatives. He visited son in school at [[Bandoeng]], visited blind institute, girls school, hat plaiteries, stayed some time at [[Batavia]] where sister lived married to Atjeh head exiled to [[Ternate]] but permitted to live Batavia 1910. When in [[1913]] he could transfer administration to his son, he prepared pilgrimage to [[Mekka]] in [[1914]], nine months out and home with steamship *Riouw*, 20 family members and 2 servants in suite. Returned he went again to Batavia to sister, wife of exile, who in [[1916]] returned to Atjeh when widowed.

At time of Broersma's visit, aged about 90 according to son, living at [[Boga]] by his sea where he himself planted coconut palms, new sturdy house with own fishponds and coconut garden, with second wife, of 16 children 12 still alive and many grandchildren. Living sign of Atjeh history, he received visitors in *baleh*, open reception house on yard, wearing simple clothing *kopia* dark blue linen jacket *kaïn* slippers, sitting on mat on floor while guests offered chairs, walking every morning with retinue along sea where Dutch once came who already 50 years are his "friends." Now does *meng-adji* and is fully Atjeher.

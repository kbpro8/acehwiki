**Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge** was the newly appointed *Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië* in [[1875]] who, at [[Buitenzorg|Buitenzorg]] in July [[1875]], received [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] on sick leave, listened to his plan for the *afsluitingslinie* from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] to the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raya]], and fully endorsed it — deciding to "den oorlog met Atjeh op de meest doortastende wijze te voeren" and promising *ruime versterkingen*. In [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s narrative he is the metropolitan authority whose approval unblocked the *aanvallende* campaign of December [[1875]] – February [[1876]].

## The Buitenzorg Planning Session

Pel, exhausted after "ruim een jaar" in command, took three months' leave on [[12 June]] [[1875]] and retired to Buitenzorg in Java to recover his *geschokte gezondheid*. There, in July [[1875]], he explained to Borel — and to the newly appointed van Lansberge and the *commandant van het leger* — his conviction that the *afwachtende stelsel* was *nutieloos*. He laid out his plan: as soon as the *goede moesson* returned, resume offensives; push the post at [[Soerian]] forward; invade [[Toekoe Nanta]]'s [[VI Moekims]]; occupy [[Sinangri]] and sweep the western bank of the [[Kwala Tjangkoel]]; break the strong enemy line at Doewa; seize kampongs and *rijstvelden*; later extend the *linie* from the mouth of the Kroeng Raba on the west coast to the mouth of the Kroeng Raya on the north coast, isolating Atjeh from the sea and reducing it to a *binnenstaat*. The plan was Pel's secret; he had not communicated it even to his *chef van den staf*, and nothing about it was found in his archive after his death.

## Endorsement and Its Consequences

Van Lansberge endorsed the plan in full. He decided to "den oorlog met Atjeh op de meest doortastende wijze te voeren" and promised *ruime versterkingen*. The decision bore fruit in the second half of [[1875]]: the [[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|bataljon Barissans]] arrived on [[10 December]] [[1875]] and the 9de bataljon on [[21 December]] [[1875]]. With these reinforcements in hand, Pel — who had returned to Atjeh on [[9 November]] [[1875]] — was able on [[26 December]] [[1875]] to launch the great *drie-colonne* [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims]] that, in less than two months, put the entire [[XXV Moekims]] in Dutch hands, drove [[Toekoe Nanta]] from his forts at [[Lampanti]], [[Lamti]] and Sabang, and carried the *linie* from the Kroeng Raba to [[Kwala Gigieng]].

## Significance

Van Lansberge is, in Borel's narrative, the rare *regeerings*-authority whose judgement of the Atjeh war matched the professional soldiers' on the ground. His endorsement of Pel's plan was the political precondition of the *aanvallende* campaign, and his promised reinforcements the material precondition. The contrast is sharp with the metropolitan and Indische authorities who, under van Swieten's influence, had allowed the *afwachtende houding* to harden into policy in [[1874]].

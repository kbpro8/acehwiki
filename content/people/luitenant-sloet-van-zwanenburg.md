**Lieutenant Sloet van Zwanenburg** was a Dutch *luitenant* of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who led the patrol from [[Penajoeng]] on [[19 June]] [[1874]] to reconnoitre [[Lampoeloe]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]], and who was discovered in his ravine, badly shot up, and forced to retreat in haste; severely wounded, he died of his wounds days later. He is the first named Dutch officer killed during [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s command, in the run-up to the [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|battle of Lampoeloe]] of [[20 June]] [[1874]].

## The Reconnaissance of 19 June 1874

By mid-June [[1874]] the [[Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)|crisis at Kota Radja]] was at its depth. Some companies could field fewer than thirty men of an original 125; the Atjehneezen crept closer, "feasting under gamelan and drumming at 300–400 paces from the parapet, eating stolen Dutch slaughter-oxen." On [[19 June]] [[1874]] a column of 150 bayonets and an artillery section marched from [[Kota Radja]] to clear the left bank north of [[Kampong Djawa]] — the Dutch cemetery at Lampoeloe, desecrated by the Atjehneezen on [[26 April]] [[1874]] in the [[Desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe (1874)|first wrange vrucht of the passieve stelsel]], lay just beyond. Simultaneously Sloet van Zwanenburg led a patrol from Penajoeng on the right bank. The day was *ongunstig*: the Atjehneezen held Lampoeloe in force. Sloet was discovered in his ravine, badly shot up, and forced to retreat in haste. He was severely wounded in the action and died of his wounds days later.

## The Battle of 20 June 1874

The next morning, [[20 June]] [[1874]], the enemy shelled Kota Radja from the east with unusual violence; the whole garrison stood to arms; *salvoes* of [[Beaumont-geweer|Beaumont rifles]] were needed at [[Kota Podjoet]] and [[Pakan Atjeh]] to drive back the attackers. Then [[Majoor Scharp|majoor Scharp]] with a half-battalion attacked Lampoeloe itself, in the [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|first Dutch offensive]] since the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]] of [[15 February]] [[1874]] four months earlier. The enemy was driven from his positions; a half-built *benteng* by the river was captured. The cost was thirty-four Dutch dead and wounded. Borel judges the action "the first step on a new road." Sloet van Zwanenburg's patrol of [[19 June]] [[1874]] had been the *verkenning* that revealed the strength of the enemy at Lampoeloe and so set the *colonne* of the 20th on its course.

**Joris Spilbergh** (also written **Spilbergen**; c. 1568-1620) was a Dutch navigator in the service of the Zeeland merchant **[[Balthasar de Moucheron|Balthasar de Moucheron]]** who commanded the [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|fourth Dutch commercial visit to Atjeh]] in September [[1602]] with two ships, after a call at [[Ceylon|Ceylon]] on the way, and joined the English admiral [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]] in the [[Capture of the Portuguese carrack off Atjeh (October 1602)|capture of the Portuguese carrack of 3 October 1602]] off the Strait of Malaka — the first major Anglo-Dutch joint naval operation in the East Indies. He is the principal Dutch figure of the closing episode of the first Dutch-Achehnese diplomatic sequence.

## The Moucheron fleet

Spilbergh's fleet of three ships, with the yacht *'t Lam under [[Cornelis Spex|Cornelis Spex]] as consort, had been outfitted by [[Balthasar de Moucheron|Balthasar de Moucheron]], the Zeeland merchant who had earlier dispatched the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage of 1599]]. The fleet left [[Middelburg|Middelburg]] on [[5 May]] [[1601]]. Near the [[Comoro Islands|Comoros]] the yacht *'t Lam became separated from the others and was so badly damaged that Spex did not dare to continue the voyage through Indian waters; he put in at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in early September [[1602]] and placed himself and his yacht under the command of the English admiral [[James Lancaster|Lancaster]] already on the roadstead. Spilbergh himself arrived a few days later with the other two ships, having called at [[Ceylon|Ceylon]] on the way.

## The Atjeh visit and the carrack capture

The full account of Spilbergh's Atjeh visit is given in the entry on the [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|visit itself]]. In summary: he arrived off Atjeh a few days after *'t Lam; he heard of Lancaster's plan to ambush the annual Portuguese carrack from the [[Koromandel kust|Koromandel coast]] and offered to join with one of his ships for an eighth of the prize; on [[21 September]] [[1602]] the combined squadron sailed from Atjeh; on [[3 October]] [[1602]] they sighted and captured the Portuguese carrack, taking 950 bales of calicoes and pintados; Spilbergh received 120 bales and his share of the chests for his eighth; the 600 Portuguese crew were released unhindered to [[Malaka|Malaka]].

## Later voyages

Spilbergh went on to command a famous Dutch circumnavigation of the globe in [[1614]]-[[1617]] — a voyage that took him through the Strait of Magellan, up the Pacific coast of South America, across the Pacific to the Moluccas and the Cape of Good Hope, and home to Zeeland. The circumnavigation, which Van der Woude does not pursue, established Spilbergh's reputation as one of the great Dutch navigators of the early seventeenth century and brought him into conflict with the Spanish in the Pacific — a continuation, in another theatre, of the Dutch-Spanish global war of which the Atjeh expedition had been a part.

## Significance

Joris Spilbergh is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch figure of the closing episode of the first Dutch-Achehnese diplomatic sequence and the principal instance of the early Anglo-Dutch naval co-operation at Atjeh. The [[Joris Spilbergh's Atjeh visit (1602)|visit of 1602]] and the [[Capture of the Portuguese carrack off Atjeh (October 1602)|capture of the Portuguese carrack of 3 October 1602]] mark the high-water mark of the brief moment of Anglo-Dutch partnership in the East Indies, made possible by the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s willingness to host both nations at his court. The visit also marks the close of the Moucheron firm's direct involvement in the East Indies trade: with the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|founding of the VOC]] in March [[1602]], the separate Zeeland companies were wound up and their assets and personnel absorbed into the united company.

**Richard James Wilkinson** ([[1867]]–[[1941]]) was a British colonial administrator, Malay scholar, and Orientalist who served as **Inspector of Schools in the [[Federated Malay States|Federated Malay States]]** at the time when the English translation of *The Achehnese* was prepared for the press. His name appears on the title-page of that work as the compiler of the **Index** that closes the second volume of the [[1906]] edition, and to whose patient labour of identification every user of the book is heavily indebted. Wilkinson was also the leading British Malayologist of his generation, whose *Malay-English Dictionary* became the standard reference work for the British administration of the [[Straits Settlements|Straits Settlements]] and the Federated Malay States, and whose lexical observations on classical Malay are still cited in studies of Acehnese Malay and the literary traditions of Sumatra.

## Career in the Malayan Civil Service

Wilkinson was a member of the Malayan Civil Service from [[1889]] onwards, and his career as a scholar of the Malay world ran parallel to his official duties. His later career took him to the Colonial Office in London, where he served as Reader in Malay at the School of Oriental Studies, and he was subsequently appointed Governor of Sierra Leone ([[1916]]–[[1921]]). That final appointment took him away from Malay studies, but his earlier writings — the dictionary, the *Papers on Malay Subjects*, and the index to *The Achehnese* — remained standard works of reference for the next half-century.

## The Malay-English Dictionary

Wilkinson's *An Abridged Malay-English Dictionary (Romanised)* first appeared in [[1901]], and the larger *A Malay-English Dictionary* in [[1903]]–[[1905]]; these were the dictionaries on which the British administration of the Straits Settlements and the Federated Malay States relied for the conduct of native affairs, and they established Wilkinson's reputation as the leading British Malayologist of his generation. In Dutch scholarly shorthand the work is cited as the *Mal. Wibk. van Wilkinson*, and variant readings of Malay lexical items are routinely attested in its pages. A representative example, drawn from the scholarly apparatus of the sixteenth-century Malay translation of the [[Burda (poem)|Burda]] of [[Al-Busiri]], is the discussion of a Malay term whose two variant readings Wilkinson records and whose comparison with the Sundanese *kumaha* ("how") he supplies; the citation typifies the kind of comparative lexical observation that made his dictionary indispensable to students of classical Malay literature.

## Index to *The Achehnese*

In *The Achehnese*, Wilkinson's role was that of indexer. The index he compiled is far more than a bare register of names: it identifies persons, places, institutions and Achehnese terms, gives their Arabic or Malay equivalents where appropriate, and supplies the kind of cross-referencing that makes the two dense volumes navigable as works of reference. The title-page of the 1906 edition records that the work is "Translated by the late A. W. S. O'Sullivan, Assistant Colonial Secretary, Straits Settlements, with an Index by R. J. Wilkinson, Inspector of Schools, Federated Malay States" — a combination of Straits Settlements and F.M.S. Malay-learning that, together with the Dutch author [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|C. Snouck Hurgronje]] and the [[Leyden|Leyden]] publisher [[E. J. Brill|E. J. Brill]], reflects the international character of the scholarship that lay behind the book.

## Other Scholarly Works

Wilkinson was also the author of the *Papers on Malay Subjects* series (history, Malay literature, life and customs, law, industries) issued by the Government of the F.M.S. in the early years of the twentieth century, and of the still-useful *A History of the Peninsular Malays* ([[1920]]). Students of Achehnese and Malay history still rely on his identifications, and his name stands, with those of Snouck Hurgronje and O'Sullivan, on the title-page of one of the great monuments of colonial Orientalism.

## Connection to Aceh and Malay Literature

Although Wilkinson's primary focus was the Malay Peninsula, his dictionaries and grammatical works have been used by researchers studying Acehnese Malay and the literary traditions of Sumatra, including the [[Hikayat Raja-raja Pasai]] and related texts. The sixteenth-century Malay translation of the Burda poem preserved in manuscript DLP-54, for instance, cites Wilkinson's dictionary in its scholarly apparatus — a small but representative instance of the way in which the lexicographic infrastructure Wilkinson built for the Malay Peninsula continues to support textual work on the wider Malay-Indonesian world, Aceh included.

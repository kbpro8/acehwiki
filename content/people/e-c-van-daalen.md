**E. C. van Daalen** was a colonel of the Indies army who served as second-in-command of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] under [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]] in [[April 1873]], and who, on the morning of [[14 April]] [[1873]] after the general's death at the [[Missigit|missigit]] outside the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]], took over the supreme command and presided over the council of war of [[18 April]] that decided on withdrawal. He is also notable for his repeated personal acts of bravery in the stormings of late [[January 1874]], in the course of which he forced his horse through the bamboe-doern mass, planted the oranje-vaandel at the foot of the enemy's parapet, and brought up reinforcements that carried the line. The source work distinguishes him from a Captain Van Daalen — likely a kinsman — who took a conspicuous part in the same actions.

## Second-in-Command of the First Expedition

When the first expedition was decided upon on [[21 February]] [[1873]], Colonel E. C. van Daalen was appointed second-in-command: "To second commander of the expedition was appointed the colonel E. C. van Daalen." He sailed with the expedition from Batavia on [[22 March]] [[1873]] and arrived at the roadstead of Atjeh on [[5 April]]. After the landing on [[8 April]] and the [[Battle of Kotta Mogat|failure at Kotta Mogat]], the troops on [[10 April]] advanced on the Kraton. The attack on the [[Missigit|missigit]] failed that day, the mosque being taken and then abandoned. On [[14 April]] the troops returned to the attack; storm-ladders were placed against the wall by the mineurs, and the enemy was at last driven out by a company armed with breech-loaders.

## Succession to Köhler, 14 April 1873

"About 5 o'clock on the same day, which passed in continual fighting with the enemy, the second commander, Colonel Van Daalen, arrived at the missigit to take over the supreme command." After new reconnaissances and investigations, a second attack was undertaken on [[18 April]] — the assault on the fortifications south-east of the missigit, "of which one was not sure whether perhaps the Kraton was there." Later it appeared that one of these fortifications was the north face of the Kraton itself. At a quarter past five the third bataljon, entrusted with the task, set out. The surprise failed; the enemy opened a heavy fire. The storm column of 700 strong returned to the missigit at 6 o'clock with nine officers wounded (of whom two soon died), 11 rank-and-file killed and 105 wounded. A council of war was convened; the question whether any success could be expected from a renewed attack was unanimously answered in the negative; the supreme commander decided that no renewed attack should take place.

## The Stormings of January 1874

The same Colonel Van Daalen — or possibly the Captain Van Daalen of the same family who is mentioned in close proximity in the source text — was again conspicuous in the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]]. On [[25 December]] [[1873]] a reconnaissance in southeast direction, intended to find an open plain on the right bank of the river opposite the Kraton, led to a fierce fight at a series of enemy fortifications near kampong Lemboe. The situation became almost untenable; the adjutant-underofficier [[E. C. O. von Bredow|E. C. O. von Bredow]], with the oranje-vaandel in his hand, broke through the thorns with a few officers and men and planted the vaandel at the foot of the parapet.

"Captain Van Daalen, who was in front of the verhakking and observed the situation on his horse, exposed to a real rain of bullets, continually urged the soldiers to handle their kapmes diligently. To speak courage to the men at the vaandel, he forced his horse, rearing and springing, through the thorn-mass; on reaching the vaandel, he planted it in the slope of the parapet, and made all promise not to yield a thumb's breadth, while he went to fetch reinforcements."

The left half of the third bataljon, brought up by Van Daalen as reinforcements, was soon engaged in a fierce fight at a second fortification. When Van Daalen returned to his troops, the bushes had been cleared enough that more men could join the vaandel. The storming began, and after a stubborn fight the benting was taken. Van Daalen immediately marched with part of the troops to help the left half of the third to take the second fortification, and soon the third and fourth followed, so that the whole line was in Dutch hands.

## Later Mentions

On [[12 January]] [[1874]] the Taman or Kota Goenoengan, the sultan's fortified pleasure-garden, was stormed and taken. "Probably from resentment at this, the enemy made an attack on our bivouac at [[Penajoeng|Penajoeng]] and the trench-guard. Although our guards were at first surprised, the attack was chiefly repelled by the gallant conduct of Captain Van Daalen."

**J. Kreemer** was a Dutch colonial official, ethnographer and manuscript researcher who served as a *Kontroleur B.B.* on [[Sumatra's Westkust]] in the early twentieth century, later as **Archivaris van het [[Atjèh Instituut|Atjèh Instituut]]** (Archivist of the Atjèh Institute), and authored the two-volume monograph [[Kreemer's Atjeh (1922‑23)|*Atjeh* (1922‑23)]] and the comparative work [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]]. He also documented the scarcity of an Acehnese manuscript of the *Burda* attributed to the author of the *Nalam Mo'lôt*. His career exemplifies the typical trajectory of the colonial scholar-administrator, while his manuscript note adds a specialised contribution to the study of Acehnese literary transmission.

## Career and field experience in Atjeh

Kreemer's field experience in northern Sumatra was both prolonged and intimate. In [[1907]] he was a young *Kontroleur B.B.* (a junior civil administrator of the Binnenlands Bestuur) on [[Sumatra's Westkust]], where his observations of the local Malay buffalo economy supplied the material for his first scholarly article, published in *De Indische Gids* (dl. I). Over the following years his responsibilities and travels extended into the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere proper. He records, in *De Karbouw*, that he personally passed along the [[Gajo-weg]]—the road linking the [[N.O.-kust van Atjeh|N.E. coast of Atjeh]] with the interior [[Gajoland|Gayo]] country—in [[1917]], and observed on its steep descents that buffalo-cart drivers employed a wooden brake-shoe (*remschoen*) under their buffelkar wheels, contrary to a widespread Dutch assumption that such braking technology was unknown in the archipelago.

Kreemer also notes that during an earlier stay in Atjeh he personally verified the near-identity of the albino-buffalo prohibition legend across six North-Sumatran population groups: [[Groot-Atjeh]] (*kawöm* [[kawöm Dja Sandang|Dja Sandang]]), [[Atjeh's Westkust]], the [[Gajoland|Gajoland]] groups of [[Kedjoeroen Boekit]] and [[Petiambang]], the [[Alasland|Alas]] *mergō*'s, and the [[Simaloer|Simaloer]] landschap [[Tapah]]. He likewise notes a personal visit to the [[Gajoland]] during which he found, repeatedly, that an informant could no longer remember the name of his own genealogical group, while the *pantang* prescription attached to that group was "*muurvast in zijn herinnering vastgelegd*" (firmly fixed in his memory).

## Fieldwork and methodology for *Atjèh*

Kreemer's field-research in Atjèh for the *Atjèh* monograph lasted from [[18 February]] [[1916]] to [[23 October]] [[1917]], during which he also worked in the governor's archive at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]]. In his preface he states that the building-blocks of the work are of three origins: "vooreerst die, welke reeds in de literatuur voorhanden zijn; in de tweede plaats die, welke door eigen navraag in loco van Inlandsche zijde werden verkregen; en eindelijk die, welke afkomstig zijn van het gewestelijk archief te Koeta Radja" — "first, those already available in the literature; secondly, those obtained by own enquiry *in loco* from the native side; and thirdly, those derived from the regional archive at Koeta Radja."

Of the existing literature, Kreemer records that he made "vooral een druk en dankbaar gebruik gemaakt van 'De Atjèhers' en van 'Het Gajöland en zijne bewoners'" — "especially a heavy and grateful use was made of 'De Atjèhers' [by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]] and of 'Het Gajöland en zijne bewoners.'" Next to these, Kreemer credits the "onschatbare Gajösch-Nederlandsche Woordenboek" of Dr. [[G. A. J. Hazeu|G. A. J. Hazeu]].

## Principal informant

Kreemer records his principal Atjèhnese informant as **[[Teungkoe Tjhèh Ibrahim|Teungkoe Tjhèh Ibrahim]]**, the *hatib* (preacher) of the great mosque at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] and *kali* of the *moesapat*'s of Koeta Radja, Oelèë Lheuë, and Lam Njöng. Kreemer notes that this man — "in Westerschen zin volkomen ongeschoold en toch met een diepe kennis, een fijnen geest en onberispelijke vormen begaafd" — "in the Western sense completely uneducated and yet endowed with a deep knowledge, a fine mind, and impeccable manners" — had been one of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s most valued informants. "Maandenlang kwam hij schrijver dagelijks vanuit zijn kampoeng Lam Bhö te Koeta Radja bezoeken, wèl wetende, dat zijn hulp niet kon worden ontbeerd voor het doen slagen van de door schrijver op zich genomen taak."

## The *Atjèh* monograph (1922‑23)

Kreemer's principal scholarly monument is the two-volume general survey *Atjèh: algemeen samenvattend overzicht van land en volk van Atjèh en Onderhoorigheden*, published in [[1922]]–[[1923]] by N.V. Boekhandel en Drukkerij voorheen [[E. J. Brill|E.J. Brill]] in Leiden. The work treats Atjeh as a territorial-administrative unit (the colonial [[Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden]]) and gathers ethnographic, linguistic, religious and historical material on the Achehnese and on the neighbouring Gayo and Alas peoples of the interior. Its two volumes cover history, antiquities, geography, ethnography, economics, administration, law, customs, religion, folk medicine, chronology, and language — a scope unmatched by any earlier or later single work on Atjèh.

## Comparative method and scope

Kreemer states that, by the comparative method underlying his arrangement, "in hoofdzaak alleen de Atjèhers, Gajö's en Alassers betrokken" — "in the main only the Atjèhnese, Gayo, and Alas were included." The Malay inhabitants of Atjèh's West Coast, the Singkelese, and the Simalorese were excluded as already adequately described elsewhere. The spelling of native terms follows the system of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], with the simplification that the Atjèhnese sound rendered by Snouck as *th* is rendered by Kreemer as *s*.

*De Karbouw* cites *Atjeh* on, among other subjects: the Atjeh plough-term *langaj* (I, 501); the Atjeh riverside enclosures during the growing season (I, 504); the Atjeh fattening custom [[peuteumbôn]] (I, 497–498; II, 595, 628); the Atjeh *dwangjuk* term [[leunggoeq]] (I, 505); the Atjeh [[bòh pè]] pulley device (I, 505); the Atjehse crocodile-charmers [[goeroe boeja]] (I, 188, 201); the Atjeh "bloeitijdperk" of [[1607]]–[[1636]] (I, 192); the Atjeh sale-rite of grasping the buffalo's nose-rope (I, 321); the [[koeta djaq]] "wandelende fort" of buffalo-hide (I, 307); the [[Poephon blang|*poephon blang*]] field-consecration rite (I, 471; II, 578); the magic-circle [[baris-Laksamana]] (I, 312; II, 586–587, 661); the [[sakét oelèe neuraja]] maturation crisis (II, 624); the Atjeh rules on the *teungkoe meunasah* slaughterer (II, 209); the *tjaram* / *tjinggram* earnest-money (II, 321, 368, 383); the [[poewôê siê]] husband obligation (II, 335, 673 nt.); the prohibition on using rice from a death-house as seed-rice (II, 458 nt. 2); the [[Gajoland]] and [[Alasland]] genealogical groups subject to the albino-buffalo prohibition (II, 544); the *seurapi* gold necklace with horn-motif (I, 584); and the Atjeh pantang legend of the albino-buffalo and the *andjalai* storage (I, 507).

## *De Karbouw* (1956)

Kreemer's late synthesis *De Karbouw: Zijn betekenis voor de volken van de Indonesische Archipel* was published in [[1956]] by N.V. Uitgeverij W. van Hoeve in 's-Gravenhage and Bandung. In the *Voorbericht* (dated 's-Gravenhage, October [[1955]]), Kreemer explains that he undertook the book to fill a gap in the ethnographic literature: the buffalo, "the pre-eminently useful domestic animal for the life of the peoples of the Archipelago," had never received a monographic treatment. He notes with quiet wryness that "what is described in this book belongs to an outlived order (*een overleefd tijdsbestel*)" — much had changed since the pre-war sources on which he largely drew, but much also remained the same.

## Manuscript Survey

Beyond his ethnographic monographs, Kreemer contributed a brief but noteworthy note on Acehnese literary manuscripts. Citing a passage in *Atjeh* II (page 686), he reported the existence of an Acehnese manuscript of the *Burda* attributed to the same author who produced the *Nalam Mo'lôt* (an Acehnese Mawlid). Kreemer observed that this *Burda* manuscript was **not** found among the numerous Acehnese hand-scripts housed in the [[Leiden Library]], underscoring the scarcity of Acehnese copies of the *Burda* despite its widespread presence in other parts of the Indonesian archipelago.

## Method and tone

Kreemer writes in a measured, comparative, late-nineteenth-century ethnographic idiom: he ranges across the archipelago, marshals his citations (to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|*De Atjehers*]], to [[G. A. J. Hazeu|Hazeu]]'s Gayo dictionary, to [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Djajadiningrat]]'s Atjehsch dictionary, to his own earlier writings), and is generally content to let the comparative material speak for itself rather than to impose a theoretical apparatus. He is, however, attentive to the explanatory glosses offered by his sources — recording, for instance, Snouck Hurgronje's cautionary note that the present-day obedience to a *pantang* food-prohibition rests not on totemistic conviction but on fear of ancestral wrath — and he occasionally allows himself a quietly critical aside, as when he rejects [[W. W. Skeat|Skeat]]'s folk-etymology of *baris-Laksamana* as "Admiral's Line" in favour of the [[Ramayana]] reference to Laksamana's protective circle around Sita.

## Significance

Kreemer is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history on two grounds. First, his *Atjeh* (1922-23) is, with Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers* (1893-94), one of the two principal Dutch scholarly syntheses on Atjeh as a country and people, and the principal encyclopaedic survey of the early civil-government period. Second, *De Karbouw* itself, although a work of comparative ethnography rather than an Atjeh monograph, contains in concentrated form a uniquely rich dossier of Atjeh buffalo-lore. Finally, his manuscript note adds a specialised data point to the study of Acehnese literary transmission, reminding scholars of the uneven survival of texts such as the *Burda* in Acehnese collections.

## Additional account from *Beginselen en Stelsel* (1950) — Kreemer's *Atjeh* and the adatrecht

[[B. ter Haar BzN|B. ter Haar BzN]] notes in his *[[Beginselen en Stelsel van het Adatrecht (Ter Haar, 4th ed. 1950)|Beginselen en Stelsel van het Adatrecht]]* (first ed. [[1939]]; 4th ed. [[1950]]) that Kreemer's *Atjeh* (1922-23) appears in his survey of the literature on the *rechtskring Atjeh*, but with the qualified judgment that the work brings, "na Snouck Hurgronje en Van Vollenhoven geen nieuws" — after Snouck Hurgronje and Van Vollenhoven, no news. The judgment places Kreemer's *Atjeh* in the secondary line of the Atjeh adatrecht scholarship: the principal source remains Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers*, and Kreemer's *Atjeh*—published three decades later, after the foundational work of Snouck Hurgronje and of [[Van Vollenhoven|Van Vollenhoven]] on the adatrecht—is, in Ter Haar's account, a synthesis that does not add new material to the adatrecht scholarship but rather consolidates what Snouck Hurgronje and Van Vollenhoven had already established.

## Account from *Atjèh* by J. Kreemer (Deel II)

Kreemer opens his *Atjèh* monograph (Deel II) with a preface (*Voorbericht*) in which he explains the genesis and method of his work. He identifies himself as **Archivaris van het Atjèh Instituut** (archivist of the Aceh Institute) and records that his fieldwork in Atjèh lasted from **18 February [[1916]]** to **23 October [[1917]]** — a period of twenty months.

He describes his work as resting on three *bouwstoffen* (building-blocks):
1. The existing literature — above all [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *De Atjehers* and the subsequent *Het Gajöland en zijne bewoners* — which he uses as a foundation
2. Direct *inlandse inlichtingen* (indigenous information) gathered in conversation with Atjèhnese informants, above all his principal informant **[[Teungkoe Tjheh Ibrahim|Teungkoe Tjhèh Ibrahim]]**
3. The *gewestelijk archief* (provincial archive) of the Gouvernement Atjèh en Onderhoorigheden

Kreemer also acknowledges the "onschatbare" (invaluable) Gayo-Dutch dictionary of **[[G. A. J. Hazeu|G. A. J. Hazeu]]** as an essential resource for the sections on the Gayo highlands.

His comparative method extends across three languages and peoples: **Atjèhnese**, **Gayo**, and **Alas** — treating the highlands and the coastal lowlands as a unified study-area. His spelling system follows Snouck Hurgronje's conventions with one simplification: the digraph *th* (used in Snouck for the Atjèhnese emphatic *t*) is simplified to *s* throughout Kreemer's work.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**J. Kreemer** was the Dutch scholar whose recent book on [[Acheh|Atjeh]] author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] twice invokes: for the celebrated characterization of Governor [[H. N. A. Swart]]'s administration — "the swords were forged into ploughshares" — and as what Langhout calls the first great and grand attempt to fill the existing need for economic literature on Atjeh.

### "The swords into ploughshares"

Writing of the end of the Atjeh war under Governor Swart (by [[1913]] all effective opposition in the province had ceased), Langhout quotes Kreemer's judgement in the first, recently appeared volume of his book on Atjeh: "Onder het bestuur van Swart werden de zwaarden tot ploegscharen omgesmeed" ("Under the government of Swart the swords were forged into ploughshares"). Langhout adds that although General Swart owed this important fact in part to the action of his predecessors, it must be fully recognized that the pacification of Atjeh and Dependencies was accomplished under his administration.

### The economic literature on Atjeh

In his closing word, discussing how Atjeh had never stood in a friendly light, in the Netherlands or abroad — a misunderstanding which, Langhout argues, was largely due to how little was known of Dutch economic statecraft in Atjeh — Langhout observes that Kreemer's book "can be regarded as the first great and grand attempt to supply the existing need for economic reading on Atjeh", beside which his own modest collection of facts sought a place.

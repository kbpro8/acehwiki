The **Chief of Teunom** — the "present chief of Teunom" at the time of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s writing — was a West-Coast *uleebalang* (elsewhere in *The Achehnese* also called the Imeum of Teunom) of [[Teunom]], a settlement on the West Coast of [[Acheh]]. He is cited as a striking example of the way in which Achehnese chiefs devised and inflicted arbitrary punishments outside the formal framework of either the *hukom* (religious law) or the *adat* (customary law).

## Arbitrary punishments inflicted

In Acheh and its dependencies, just as in other Mohammedan countries, "arbitrary punishments are often elaborated and inflicted by the chiefs". Thus, the present chief of Teunom "used often to cause adulterous lovers to be deprived of their virility by the objects of their passion". He also "punished many transgressors of the law as to fasts by having them led about the country on all fours for some days with rings in their noses like buffaloes, and forcing them to eat grass". The mutilation of adulterers was a private vengeance imposed through the wounded party; the buffalowing of those who transgressed the law of the fasts — presumably the [[Ramadhan (fasting month)|Ramadhan fast]] — was a more theatrical form of public humiliation, intended to advertise the chief's zeal for the observance of religion.

These punishments, Snouck Hurgronje emphasises, "are applied at the whim of the uleebalang, not only in the case of illicit intercourse but also of other favourite sins of the Achehnese, whether some sacred occasion or place has been thereby extraordinarily polluted, or where the offence has been perpetrated with outrageous shamelessness, or by persons who are specially obnoxious to the chiefs or whose misdeeds they do not feel bound to overlook". The Chief of Teunom is thus a particularly vivid example of a general Achehnese pattern.

## His place among the West-Coast chiefs

The chief of Teunom recurs elsewhere in *The Achehnese* as one of the Achehnese chiefs whose strength was such that even [[Teuku Uma]] — the celebrated chief who rose to greatness during the [[Achehnese War|war]] and obtained supremacy over the whole of the West Coast — "could make little headway against him". This relative independence is striking, given Teuku Uma's general dominance of the West Coast; it testifies to the personal energy and local following of the chief of Teunom, and goes some way to explain the self-confidence with which he administered his own distinctive brand of justice.

## Comparative Achehnese context

The punishments inflicted by the chief of Teunom should be read alongside those of the late [[Chief of Lho' Kruet|chief of Lho' Kruet]] on the West Coast, who used to set offenders on a cow-buffalo and lead them round to a hooting and jeering crowd. Together, the two chiefs illustrate the latitude that Achehnese *uleebalangs* enjoyed in devising penalties, and the wide range of conduct — from illicit intercourse to breaches of the fast — that they were prepared to bring within the scope of their own arbitrary justice.

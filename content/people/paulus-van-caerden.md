**Paulus van Caerden** was a Dutch vice-admiraal in the service of the [[Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie|Nieuwe Brabantsche Compagnie]] — and after [[1602]] of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] — who commanded the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|second Dutch commercial visit to Atjeh]] in [[1600]]–[[1601]], conducted a punitive campaign against the Spanish in the Moluccas in [[1606]]–[[1608]] in which he was captured and held prisoner, and was the elder kinsman (probably uncle) of the better-known **Cornelis van Caerden** also in VOC service. He is, after the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers]], the principal Dutch commander at Atjeh in the pre-VOC period. In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account his Atjeh visit is the source of the [[Pigskin parchment letter incident at Atjeh (1600)|pigskin parchment letter incident]] — a diplomatic fiasco in which a Portuguese monk pointed out to the Sultan that Prince Maurits's letter was written on parchment made from the skin of the unclean pig.

## Background: the second Amsterdam fleet, 1599

At the end of [[1599]] two Amsterdam fleets sailed out, independently of each other: one under [[Pieter Both|Pieter Both]] with Van Caerden as Vice-Admiral, the other under [[Jacob Wilkens|Jacob Wilkens]] with [[Cornelis van Heemskerck|Cornelis van Heemskerck]] as his second. Both founded a factory at [[Bantam]]. Van Caerden then visited Sumatra's Westkust (West Coast) — [[Priaman]], [[Tikoe]] and [[Pasaman]] — without result, and, at the end of [[1600]], Atjeh. He arrived off Atjeh on [[21 September]] [[1600]].

## The pigskin parchment letter incident, 1600

At the solemn reception of the Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]] by Van Caerden, who was to deliver gifts and a letter from [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]], a Portuguese monk was present. The monk drew attention to the fact that the letter — written on parchment made from the skin of the unclean pig — could not lawfully be touched by a Mohammedan. "Zoo miste's Prinsen brief zijn doel" — so the Prince's letter missed its mark. See [[Pigskin parchment letter incident at Atjeh (1600)]].

The intervention was a classic act of Iberian diplomatic sabotage: the Portuguese, hostile to the Dutch intrusion in the Asian trade, used their familiarity with Islamic law to turn the Sultan's religious scruples against the Dutch embassy. The Sultan, naturally, could not accept the letter. The incident gave its name to chapter XXXII of *Tijdens de O.I. Compagnie*: "Het Kasteel van Verre. De brief op varkensvel in Atjeh."

## The Atjeh visit (1600-1601)

The full account of his Atjeh visit is given in the entry on the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|visit itself]]. In summary: he obtained the release of the surviving [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman captives]] including [[Hans de Decker|Hans Decker]]; signed a pepper contract with the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]] on [[28 December]] [[1600]]; received the escaped [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] on [[30 December]] [[1600]]; seized pepper from Arab junks at anchor on [[12 January]] [[1601]] after the sultan's sabander rearrested Houtman; was fired on by the shore batteries; burned several coastal craft; released his prisoners; and sailed for [[Bantam|Bantam]] by way of the west coast of Sumatra, taking on no cargo.

[[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] — long a prisoner in Atjeh since [[1599]] — came aboard Van Caerden's ship to warn him of an impending attack. He then returned to shore, hoping to obtain the release of his fellow-prisoners, to deliver a cargo to Van Caerden, and to negotiate a contract between the Sultan and the Dutch. All in vain — De Houtman remained a prisoner.

## Reckless conduct and the sowing of hatred

Van Caerden's expedition was further disfigured "door roekeloos optreden tegenover de inlanders" — by reckless behaviour towards the natives — in particular over the recovery of an advance paid to the Sultan. His conduct, Godee Molsbergen writes, made "den Hollandschen naam in den Archipel gehaat" — the Dutch name hated in the Archipelago.

The consequences were concrete. Neutral Moors (*Mooren*) whom Van Caerden had wronged obtained, after a lawsuit, 50,000 *realen* in damages — a staggering sum. The Zeeland merchants [[Gerard le Roy|Gerard le Roy]] and [[Laurens Bicker|Laurens Bicker]] later argued that Van Caerden had exceeded his commission in seizing the Arab junks, since the contract gave the Dutch a monopoly only on the new crop, not on cargo already in port — and they undertook to compensate the Arab claimants for the ƒ51,048 damages. Van der Woude defends Van Caerden against the *rederij*: "Van Caerden was gealarmeerd door de berichten van de ontvluchte Hollanders en wantrouwde den vorst, indachtig [[Frederik de Houtman|De Houtman]]'s waarschuwing. Bovendien was hij er op bedacht waar te ontvangen voor het voorgeschoten geld." His conduct, in Van der Woude's view, was the natural response to the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]]'s "onbetrouwbare, weifelmoedige houding", though "onvoorzichtig op te goed vertrouwen concessies deed".

## The 1601 Zeeland ships and the resolution

When the four Zeeland ships returned to Atjeh in August [[1601]] bearing [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]]'s letter (this time in Spanish, and presumably not on pigskin), the Sultan was conciliatory. Van Caerden's clumsy conduct was "naar genoegen met den Sultan besproken" — satisfactorily discussed with the Sultan — and the Sultan sent three ambassadors — [[Abdoel Hamid]], [[Sri Mohammed]] and [[Mir Hassan]] — to Europe on the returning Zeeland ships. See [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)]].

## Capture by the Spanish (1607)

After the founding of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] in [[1602]], Van Caerden continued in Company service. In [[1606]] he commanded a fleet sent to the Moluccas against the Spanish, where the Spaniards had recently re-established themselves at [[Ternate|Ternate]]. In [[1607]] — Van der Woude's note at chapter X records — he "inmiddels met zeventien man in handen van de Spanjaarden was gevallen". His capture deprived the VOC of one of its most experienced Indies commanders at a critical moment. He was held prisoner by the Spanish for several years; the eventual fate of his captivity is not pursued in Van der Woude's narrative.

## Significance

Paulus van Caerden is the principal Dutch commander at Atjeh in the pre-VOC period, and the principal instance of the difficulty the early Dutch companies faced in operating at the Achehnese court. His Atjeh visit produced the first formal Dutch-Achehnese pepper contract, the release of the surviving Houtman captives, and the first armed clash between the Dutch and the Atjeh shore batteries — three "firsts" that together frame the visit as the pivot of the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship. The [[Pigskin parchment letter incident at Atjeh (1600)|pigskin parchment letter incident]] — the most striking single episode of his visit — is the first recorded European diplomatic fiasco at the Atjeh court, and a textbook example of how a small material detail (the parchment's origin in pig-skin) could, in the hands of a hostile third party, defeat an entire embassy.

The controversy over his seizure of the Arab junks at Atjeh — and the long-running compensation claim that followed — also established the legal-political pattern that would recur throughout the Dutch-Atjeh relationship: aggressive commercial action by a Dutch commander, controversy at home about whether his instructions permitted it, and a long-running compensation claim. His subsequent capture by the Spanish in the Moluccas deprived the VOC of an experienced commander and contributed to the strategic difficulties of the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] era.

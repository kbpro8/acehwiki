**King Abraha** was a 6th‑century Aksumite ruler whose army is evoked in the Malay translation of the *Burda* as a metaphor for the forces opposed to the prophetic mission. The poem likens the *kāfir* to “the army of the unbeliever that was pelted with stones by the messenger”.

## Appearance in the *Burda*
Verses 71–73 of the Malay text reference “the army of the unbeliever that the messenger struck with pebbles”, a traditional allusion to Abraha’s failed expedition against the Kaʿba, a story well‑known in Islamic tradition.

## Relevance to Aceh
The analogy served Acehnese poets to frame contemporary conflicts with colonial powers as a continuation of the historic struggle between true believers and hostile forces.

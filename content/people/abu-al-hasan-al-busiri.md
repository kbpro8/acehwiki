**Abu al-Hasan al-Busiri** was a 13th‑century Egyptian Sufi poet best known for composing the *Burda* (the *Poem of the Cloak*), a devotional ode to the Prophet [[Muhammad]] that became a central text of religious practice in the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] from the early modern period onward. His work travelled across the Indian Ocean, was rendered into Classical [[Malay]] and incorporated into Acehnese literary and ritual life.

## Biography  

Al‑Busiri was born in Alexandria around [[1212]] and spent most of his life in the Mamluk realm. After a reported miraculous cure attributed to the intercession of the Prophet, he composed the *Burda* in praise of Muhammad, a poem that quickly entered the Sufi devotional canon.

## Influence in Aceh  

The *Burda* was introduced to the Malay world in the 16th century, most likely via the bustling port of [[Aceh]] which served as a hub for Islamic scholarship and trade. Acehnese scholars and qadis adopted the poem for recitation in mosques and royal courts, and it inspired a flourishing tradition of Malay devotional poetry.

## Reception and Legacy  

Acehnese manuscripts of the *Burda* survive in several libraries, most notably the *DLP‑54* collection, which contains a 16th‑century Malay translation. The poem continues to be recited during religious festivals in Aceh, underscoring al‑Busiri’s lasting impact on the region’s spiritual culture.

**J. H. Carpentier Alting** — published in the volume as *prof. mr. J. H. Carpentier Alting* — was a Dutch professor of law who, in [[1918]], was appointed chairman of the commission tasked with revising "the foundations of government in the Netherlands Indies" (*herziening van de bestuursgrondslagen in Nederlandsch-Indië*), in the difficult days at the end of the First World War. The commission — known in the AcehWiki corpus as the [[Carpentier Alting commission (1918)]] — produced a draft *wet op de staatsordening* (law on the state organisation) that was to influence the constitutional revision of 1922 and the eventual Wet op de Indische Staatsregeling (Law on the Indies State Regulation) of [[1925]].

## Role in the 1918 commission

According to the account given by Mr. Dr. [[L. J. J. Caron|Caron]] in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]), the Governor-General of the day found occasion in November [[1918]] — at the close of the First World War — to address the *Volksraad* (People's Council) and promise a programme of development for the central representative body that would bring "fundamental changes in the Indies' forms of government." To give effect to this, "in 1918 a commission was appointed under the chairmanship of Prof. Mr. J. H. Carpentier Alting for the revision of the foundations of government in the Netherlands Indies." The commission's report submitted a draft *wet op de staatsordening* (law on the state ordering) — that is, a draft constitutional law for the Indies.

## Involvement in the Proeve-Oppenheim

Carpentier Alting's name is also attached, as one of the contributing professors, to the *[[Proeve-Oppenheim]]* — the 1922 Leiden commission of professors that, after the Dutch constitutional revision of 1922, drafted a *proeve van een staatsregeling voor Nederlandsch-Indië* (a trial draft of a state regulation for the Netherlands Indies). The other contributors were Prof. Oppenheim (after whom the draft is named), [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], Van Vollenhoven and Kleintjes; the Indonesian Mr. [[Oerip Kartodirdjo]] served as secretary. The *Proeve-Oppenheim* exercised considerable influence on the eventual Indische Staatsregeling of [[1925]], though — as Caron observes — the final law fell short of the autonomous Indies government that the *Proeve* had envisaged.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, Carpentier Alting's significance lies chiefly in the constitutional afterlife of the Atjeh-derived administrative thinking of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]. The [[Korte Verklaring|*korte verklaring*]] policy first applied in Atjeh in the early twentieth century had, by 1918, become the standard instrument for the Indies' *zelfbesturen*; the question that Carpentier Alting's commission was appointed to address — how to revise "the foundations of government" in light of the promise of greater popular representation — was therefore inseparable from the question of how the Atjeh-style administration should be constitutionalised.

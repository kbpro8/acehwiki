**Toekoe Imam Longbattah** was an [[Achehnese|Atjehnese]] religious leader — *Imam* of [[Longbatta|Longbattah]] — who, in the years before the Dutch attack of [[1873]], served as one of the three principal advisers consulted by the Sultan [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] on the question of whether to open negotiations with the Dutch. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] in the chapter on [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] as one of the three *hoofden* — together with [[Toekoe Kadli]] and [[Toekoe Noq van Marassa]] — who declared themselves in agreement with the Habib's position, and who accompanied him to [[Batavia]] on the steamship *Patti* (Captain *Roura*) to treat with the Governor-General.

## The council of three

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, when the Sultan [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] — without the Habib's knowledge — commissioned [[Panglima Tibang]] to open negotiations with the Dutch steamship *Maar en Waul* then lying at the roadstead of [[Groot-Atjeh]], the Habib *betreurde* (regretted) the Sultan's action and insisted that *zulke gewichtige saangelegenheid* (such a weighty matter) could not be decided by the Sultan alone. The matter was referred to a council of three chiefs — *Toekoe Imam Longbattah, Toekoe Kadli en Toekoe Noq van Marassa* — who declared themselves in agreement with the Habib and accompanied him to Batavia.

## The mission to Batavia

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the mission to Batavia — aboard the steamship *Patti*, Captain *Roura* — was the first formal Atjehnese diplomatic approach to the Dutch in the period before the [[1873]] expedition. The mission was, in the album's reading, the Habib's initiative: the Habib had proposed to the Sultan that the Dutch be admitted to Atjehnese territory for the purposes of trade, on the ground that it was in Atjeh's interest to be on good terms with the Netherlands. The album does not record the outcome of the mission, but it identifies Toekoe Imam Longbattah as one of the three chiefs who supported the Habib's position and who accompanied him to Batavia.

## Significance

Toekoe Imam Longbattah's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as one of the three named advisers consulted by the Sultan on the question of whether to open negotiations with the Dutch — and as one of the three chiefs who accompanied the Habib on the mission to Batavia. His appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] is brief, but it identifies the council of three as the principal advisory body of the late sultanate, and it places the Habib's pro-Dutch policy within the broader framework of Atjehnese court politics.

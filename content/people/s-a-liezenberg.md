**S. A. Liezenberg** was the Dutch manager of the Redelong tea estate north-east of [[Takengon|Takengon]] in the Alas valley, who in mid-[[1942]] became the principal civilian supporter of the post-capitulation Dutch guerrilla of Lt [[H. van Zanten|lieutenant H. van Zanten]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Liezenberg is the only named Dutch civilian to have given systematic material assistance to the Van Zanten resistance, supplying food and medicines and acting as a conduit between the resistance and the physician of the Takengon hospital.

## Support to the Van Zanten resistance

When, after the [[19 March]] [[19 March 1942|19 March 1942]] capitulation at [[Takengon|Takengon]], Lt [[H. van Zanten|Van Zanten]] resolved to continue resistance from a forest bivouac north-east of Takengon, he chose the recently vacated Redelong tea estate — which had served as a staff headquarters for Dutch troops — as his intelligence and supply base. Liezenberg, as manager of the estate, provided much help in the form of food and medicines. He also acted as a relay for the correspondence between Van Zanten and the physician of the Takengon hospital — correspondence that was carried by the Ambonese sergeant **Kalatu**, who moved between Redelong and Takengon, and was relayed in turn by the wife of a Menadonese soldier, who worked at the Dutch hospital.

The chain — Van Zanten at Redelong via Liezenberg via Kalatu via the Menadonese wife to the physician at Takengon — was eventually detected by the Japanese, and in the long run this organisation could not remain secret from them. In November [[1942]] the Japanese began to take the Van Zanten group seriously; on [[24 November]] [[24 November 1942|24 November 1942]] fourteen released soldiers working on the Redelong estate were arrested and subjected to the well-known Japanese mistreatment. On [[26 November]] [[26 November 1942|26 November 1942]] a Japanese force of about 100 men attempted to take Van Zanten's bivouac but was foiled by an informer. The bivouac was burned. From this point the supply of food and medicines from Redelong was cut off; Liezenberg's role as logistical supporter of the resistance ended.

## Significance

Liezenberg is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the only named Dutch civilian supporter of the post-capitulation Dutch resistance, and the Redelong tea estate is the staff history's principal example of the use of a plantation as a base for intelligence and supply. The exposure of the Liezenberg–Kalatu–Menadonese-wife chain is the staff history's principal illustration of the impossibility of sustaining a Dutch clandestine network in Atjeh under Japanese occupation — an impossibility the compilers attribute to the Japanese surveillance of the European civilian population and to the dependence of the network on a chain of indigenous intermediaries.

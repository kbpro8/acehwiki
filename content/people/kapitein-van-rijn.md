**Kapitein Van Rijn** was an infantry captain of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]] serving in the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] of [[1879]] whose only recorded independent command in the campaign narrative of [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]] was the forage march from the bivak at [[Selimoen]] to [[Ajer-Alang]] on [[16 August]] [[1879]], during the closing days of the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]]. The captain of the book is not further identified; whether he was connected with other officers of the name serving in the Indies is not stated.

## The forage march to Ajer-Alang

On the morning of [[16 August]] [[1879]] Van Rijn marched from the bivak at Selimoen at six o'clock with one and a half companies — the 3rd Company of the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6th Battalion]] and a peloton of the 3rd Company of the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9th Battalion]] — and with all the available coolies, some five hundred in number, to bring up provisions from the *etappe*-post at [[Ajer-Alang]] on the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]], halfway between [[Indrapoeri]] and Selimoen. With the same movement four wounded and thirteen heavy sick were evacuated; as these could not remain at Ajer-Alang, the officer of health [[Nöckedved]] — "een Deen," as the authors note — accompanied them through to Indrapoeri, returning the same evening.

The march, the authors reflect, could be measured on the map for its boldness: it traversed a country which only a few days before had been for the greater part unknown, which had always been accounted the centre of the resistance, and where the Dutch in the tocht to [[Gleiêng]], little more than a year earlier, had seemed to have reached the limit of their penetration. Of enmity there was now no trace: white flags stood out everywhere, the population came out to meet the column, advice and help were sought for the many sick in the kampongs, and medicines were thankfully accepted. The transport returned on the afternoon of [[18 August]] with a store greater than had been expected — provisions for more than three days — fired upon only once, at the crossing of the Atjeh river level with the abandoned bivak of Majoor [[Majoor Lubeck|Lubeck]]'s column, by some marauders from the mountains.

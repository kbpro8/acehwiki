**G.C.E. van Daalen** — *Van Daalen* — was, with [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], the dominant military figure of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Zentgraff calls him a *stalen figuur* (figure of steel) and *ridder sans peur ni reproche*: a hard and merciless fighter who never shirked a risk himself, who opposed the burning of kampongs, and who yet permitted the photographing of the 561 dead at benteng Koeto Lintang. The Gajoes, who date their modern history from his coming, called him *"Obos Panalan"* — *Obos* being their form of *overste* (colonel).

## As kapitein, chef-staf at Aneukgalong 1896

Van Daalen was *chef van den staf* to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|overste Van Heutsz]] on the [[Fall of Aneukgalong (1896)|overval of Aneukgalong]] of [[28 March]] [[1896]]. His letter to his wife of [[5 July]] [[1896]] is the canonical account of that fight: *"Precies 12 uur middernacht van den 28en marcheerden wij met twee bataljons en de maréchaussee af naar Lambaroe, onder de bevelen van overste Van Heutsz, en mij als chef-staf…"* By 5 a.m. the benteng was taken; 110 Atjehers lay dead, including [[Tengkoe Mat Amin di Tiro|Tgk. Mat Amin]].

## The letters to his wife, 1896-1897

By a *gelukkig toeval* Zentgraff obtained, with the family's permission, Van Daalen's private letters to his wife of the years [[1896]]–[[1897]]. They show, Zentgraff writes, *een anderen Van Daalen* (a different Van Daalen) than the *genadeloozen en onbarmhartigen man* the masses saw. In a letter of [[8 May]] [[1896]] he complains of the *ontevreden geest* (discontented spirit) among officers and men at the *niets doen* (doing nothing); *"Het is den overste Van Heutsz bepaald verboden iets in het midden te brengen"* (lt.-col. Van Heutsz is expressly forbidden to bring anything up). He protests — in letters of [[27 May]] [[1896]] and [[25 April]] [[1897]] — the *onnoodige en tactlooze wreedheden* (unnecessary and tactless cruelties) of burning kampongs and seizing cattle from innocents during the [[Lhong punitive expedition (1897)|Lhong-kwestie]]: *"Het is beschamend en ik verlang daarom ook om hiervandaan te komen"* — "It is shameful and I long to get away from here."

## The Gajoetocht, 18 March - 3 June 1904

As overste, Van Daalen commanded the *colonne-Van Daalen* on the [[Gajoetocht (1904)|Gajoetocht]] of [[18 March]]–[[3 June]] [[1904]]. He outflanked the heavily fortified bentengs on the *Boer ni Intem-Intem* by a *omtrekkende beweging* through wild uninhabited bergland; the men were sometimes lost and starving; a reuzen-python was killed in the oerwoud and eaten by the troops. At each benteng — Pepareq (308 dead), Koeto Lintang (561 dead), Reket-Goip, Penampakan, Tampeng, Bambel — Van Daalen had the dead counted: *zóóveel mannen, zóóveel vrouwen en kinderen*. He permitted photographs of the rows of dead, later published in [[Kempees]]'s book. He *voerde oorlog, deed zijn plicht, en: "advienne que pourra."*

## Governor and the T. Tjhi Toenong sentence

As governor of Atjeh, Van Daalen was confronted with the death-sentence of [[T. Tjhi Toenong]] in [[1905]]. The sentence was to be the rope. Van Daalen *vond het niet behoorlijk dat een Atjeher die altijd een kranig vechter en aanvoerder was geweest, zou worden opgehangen* (did not find it fitting that an Atjeher who had always been a brave fighter and commander should be hanged) and changed the sentence to death by the bullet — a *waardiger dood*.

## Other acts

He drew, by hand, the *stamboomen* (family trees) of the principal *oeléëbalang* families of Atjeh — preserved by his daughter, *mevrouw Gerretson-Van Daalen*. He gave the *rantej boei* amulet taken from the body of [[Tengkoe Tjot Plieëng]] to the Acehnese spy [[Waki Wahab]] *De Kwab* — he was *geen man voor zulke toovermiddelen* (no man for such magic means). At the funeral of the Ambonese sergeant [[Anakotta]] after Reket-Goip he was moved to tears: *"zwijgend gaf hij de officieren een wenk om mee te gaan, en deze ijzeren soldaat sloot zich even op in z'n verblijf."* He opposed the *afkoop van vijandige gezindheid* (buying off hostile disposition) and the Lhong fine of f 30,000. He was succeeded as governor by [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]].

## Significance

The Gajoes still reckon their modern chronology from his coming: *"Toen Obos Panalan kwam was ik een jongen van acht jaar,"* *"Ons huis is gebouwd drie jaren nà Panalan."* He outshone, in the memory of the country, all the Western figures who came and went like *schimmen in het licht der lantaarn*. In Zentgraff's verdict: *Ondanks zijn fouten — wie onzer heeft niet grootere, zonder de compensatie zijner schitterende eigenschappen? — staat zijn figuur stralend en onbevlekt in de historie van Atjeh.*

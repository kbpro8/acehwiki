**G.C.E. van Daalen** — *Van Daalen*, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* identified in fuller form as *Godfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen*, *Kapitein van den Generalen Staf* — was, with [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], the dominant military figure of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Zentgraff calls him a *stalen figuur* (figure of steel) and *ridder sans peur ni reproche*: a hard and merciless fighter who never shirked a risk himself, who opposed the burning of kampongs, and who yet permitted the photographing of the 561 dead at benteng Koeto Lintang. The Gajoes, who date their modern history from his coming, called him *"Obos Panalan"* — *Obos* being their form of *overste* (colonel).

## As kapitein, chef-staf at Aneukgalong 1896

Van Daalen was *chef van den staf* to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|overste Van Heutsz]] on the [[Fall of Aneukgalong (1896)|overval of Aneukgalong]] of [[28 March]] [[1896]]. His letter to his wife of [[5 July]] [[1896]] is the canonical account of that fight: *"Precies 12 uur middernacht van den 28en marcheerden wij met twee bataljons en de maréchaussee af naar Lambaroe, onder de bevelen van overste Van Heutsz, en mij als chef-staf…"* By 5 a.m. the benteng was taken; 110 Atjehers lay dead, including [[Tengkoe Mat Amin di Tiro|Tgk. Mat Amin]].

## The letters to his wife, 1896-1897

By a *gelukkig toeval* (lucky chance) Zentgraff obtained, with the family's permission, Van Daalen's private letters to his wife of the years [[1896]]–[[1897]]. They show, Zentgraff writes, *een anderen Van Daalen* (a different Van Daalen) than the *genadeloozen en onbarmhartigen man* (merciless and pitiless man) the masses saw. In a letter of [[8 May]] [[1896]] he complains of the *ontevreden geest* (discontented spirit) among officers and men at the *niets doen* (doing nothing); *"Het is den overste Van Heutsz bepaald verboden iets in het midden te brengen"* (Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz is expressly forbidden to bring anything up). He protests — in letters of [[27 May]] [[1896]] and [[25 April]] [[1897]] — the *onnoodige en tactlooze wreedheden* (unnecessary and tactless cruelties) of burning kampongs and seizing cattle from innocents during the [[Lhong punitive expedition (1897)|Lhong-kwestie]]: *"Het is beschamend en ik verlang daarom ook om hiervandaan te komen"* — "It is shameful and I long to get away from here."

## The Gajoetocht, 18 March - 3 June 1904

As overste, Van Daalen commanded the *colonne-Van Daalen* on the [[Gajoetocht (1904)|Gajoetocht]] of [[18 March]]–[[3 June]] [[1904]]. He outflanked the heavily fortified bentengs on the *Boer ni Intem-Intem* by a *omtrekkende beweging* (flanking movement) through wild uninhabited bergland; the men were sometimes lost and starving; a reuzen-python was killed in the oerwoud and eaten by the troops. At each benteng — Pepareq (308 dead), Koeto Lintang (561 dead), Reket-Goip, Penampakan, Tampeng, Bambel — Van Daalen had the dead counted: *zóóveel mannen, zóóveel vrouwen en kinderen* (so many men, so many women and children). He permitted photographs of the rows of dead, later published in [[Kempees]]'s book. He *voerde oorlog, deed zijn plicht, en: "advienne que pourra."* (waged war, did his duty, and: "come what may.")

## Governor and the T. Tjhi Toenong sentence

As governor of Atjeh, Van Daalen was confronted with the death-sentence of [[T. Tjhi Toenong]] in [[1905]]. The sentence was to be the rope. Van Daalen *vond het niet behoorlijk dat een Atjeher die altijd een kranig vechter en aanvoerder was geweest, zou worden opgehangen* (did not find it fitting that an Atjeher who had always been a brave fighter and commander should be hanged) and changed the sentence to death by the bullet — a *waardiger dood* (a more dignified death).

## Other acts

He drew, by hand, the *stamboomen* (family trees) of the principal *oeléëbalang* families of Atjeh — preserved by his daughter, *mevrouw Gerretson-Van Daalen*. He gave the *rantej boei* amulet taken from the body of [[Tengkoe Tjot Plieëng]] to the Acehnese spy [[Waki Wahab]] *De Kwab* — he was *geen man voor zulke toovermiddelen* (no man for such magic means). At the funeral of the Ambonese sergeant [[Anakotta]] after Reket-Goip he was moved to tears: *"zwijgend gaf hij de officieren een wenk om mee te gaan, en deze ijzeren soldaat sloot zich even op in z'n verblijf."* He opposed the *afkoop van vijandige gezindheid* (buying off hostile disposition) and the Lhong fine of f 30,000. He was succeeded as governor by [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]].

## Account from Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — Chef van den Staf under Van Heutsz, Spring 1896

In Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, Van Daalen is identified as the *Chef van den Staf* of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]]'s colonnes throughout the principal engagements of the spring and early summer of [[1896]], the staff officer whose partnership with Van Heutsz began the command relationship which would dominate the next phase of the war. Van Daalen served as *Chef van den Staf* during the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik on 2 May 1896]], the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|bergcolonne of 23 May 1896 against the VI Moekims]], and the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|23 June 1896 Ladoeng landing operation]] — that is, in three of Van Heutsz's most important commands. Kruisheer also identifies an OCR variant "G. C. H. v. Daalen" (and an apparent OCR confusion with the similarly-named [[H. C. Kronouer|Kapitein Kronouer]]), which the master article list notes; the same individual is meant.

### 2 May, 23 May, 23 June 1896

Van Daalen's role as *Chef van den Staf* placed him at the centre of Van Heutsz's operational planning and execution. The [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik on 2 May 1896]] was a multi-battalion operation — [[J. R. Jacobs|Maj. Jacobs]]' [[7e Bataljon]], [[J. W. van den Broek|Maj. Van den Broek]]'s [[3e Bataljon]], [[G. F. Sterss|Maj. Sterss]]'s [[12e Bataljon]], [[Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland|Kapitein Graafland]]'s 6 brigades *maréchaussée*, and supporting arms — which retook the post of [[Senelop]] (with the *Amb. maréchaussée* [[Simin, No. 8502|Simin]] the first to enter the *versterking* at 9:10 AM). The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|bergcolonne of 23 May 1896]] was the operation which stormed [[Tjot Tjako]] — with [[Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland|Graafland]]'s 10 brigades *maréchaussée* as *voorhoede* (vanguard), and with the Luitenants [[Wagener]] and [[Vis]] and the *maréchaussée* [[Wongsodikromo, No. 33780|Wongsodikromo]] and [[Todikromo, No. 28097|Todikromo]] the first to reach the summit at 5:43 PM. The [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|23 June 1896 Ladoeng landing operation]] was the culminating engagement of the first semester, in which the [[2e Colonne (land) under Stemfoort|2e Colonne under Stemfoort]] advanced from [[Lam-Njong]] at 6:45 AM, the [[1e Colonne (sea-landing) under Hansen|1e Colonne under Hansen]] landed at [[Ladoeng]] in the early hours, and the [[Kroeng-Kali]] *benteng* (fort) was stormed by the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] with 2e Luit. [[Heyligers]] the first to enter. Van Daalen's *Chef van den Staf* role across these three operations gave him a comprehensive familiarity with Van Heutsz's command methods and with the operational landscape of the [[Groot-Atjeh]] theatre.

### Later Career and the Famous General van Daalen

Kruisheer's identification of Van Daalen as "*later the famous General van Daalen of the Aceh campaign*" — and the master article list's note that he was later *Godfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen* — points forward to the second phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], in which Van Daalen would become one of the most renowned — and controversial — Dutch generals of the campaign. The partnership with Van Heutsz which began in the spring of [[1896]] would continue through the next phase of the war, and the operational experience which Van Daalen accumulated as *Chef van den Staf* — the storming of Tjot Tjako, the assault on the Kroeng-Kali *benteng*, the multi-colonne operations against the V Moekims Montassik — would inform his later independent commands. Kruisheer's retrospective identification of Van Daalen as the future general reflects the institutional recognition, by [[1913]], that the Kapitein van den Generalen Staf of [[1896]] had become one of the principal military figures of the Dutch East Indies.

## Significance

The Gajoes still reckon their modern chronology from his coming: *"Toen Obos Panalan kwam was ik een jongen van acht jaar,"* *"Ons huis is gebouwd drie jaren nà Panalan."* He outshone, in the memory of the country, all the Western figures who came and went like *schimmen in het licht der lantaarn* (phantoms in the lantern's light). In Zentgraff's verdict: *Ondanks zijn fouten — wie onzer heeft niet grootere, zonder de compensatie zijner schitterende eigenschappen? — staat zijn figuur stralend en onbevlekt in de historie van Atjeh.* (Despite his faults — who among us has not greater ones, without the compensation of his brilliant qualities? — his figure stands radiant and unblemished in the history of Atjeh.)

Van Daalen's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the staff officer whose career was made in the first semester of [[1896]] and whose partnership with Van Heutsz would define the next phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. His role as *Chef van den Staf* during the [[Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)|diversie of 2 May 1896]], the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|bergcolonne of 23 May 1896]] (storming of Tjot Tjako), and the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|23 June 1896 Ladoeng landing operation]] gave him an operational experience across three of the most important engagements of the spring and early summer, and his later career as the famous General van Daalen confirms the importance of this formative period.

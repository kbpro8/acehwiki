**Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah** — also written *T. Tjoet Machmoed*, *T. Tjoet Mahmoed*, *T. Tjoet Lam-Tengah*, and *Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed Lamtengah* — was the district head of [[Moekim Lamtengah]] and the half-brother of [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]] of the [[VII Moekims Baid]] whose trajectory in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* illustrates the dilemmas of the *hoeloebalang* class caught between the [[Dutch]] administration and the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. Initially neutral — "*hield zich voorloopig onzijdig*" — Lamtengah maintained daily meat and bread transports to the Dutch post at [[Ana' Galong]] under the guard of ten armed Achehnese, even as he conferred with Umar, and he was the informant who first brought the bodies of the dead (including *Eur. sergt.* [[Sergeant van Oosten|Sergeant van Oosten]]) into Ana' Galong after the [[7 March]] [[1896|patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)]] and who provided the reports that confirmed Umar's defection. His subsequent defection to Umar in the night of [[30 March|30]]–[[31 March]] [[1896]] — together with the population of the Moekim Lamtengah — and his later double-game of professing goodwill to the Dutch while his territory offered heavy fire, made him one of the most ambiguous figures of the spring of [[1896]] and the explicit target of the [[Excursie to Moekim Lamtengah (21 May 1896)|excursie to Moekim Lamtengah of 21 May 1896]].

## Neutrality, Diplomacy and Confirmation of the Defection

Lamtengah's position before the defection was that of a *mukimhoofd* whose territory adjoined the Dutch *buitenpost* at Ana' Galong — the post which the Achehnese "considered as the heart of Great Aceh". His daily meat and bread transports to the post, under Achehnese guard, made him a uniquely well-informed intermediary, and Kruisheer records that he "brought the bodies of the dead (including Sgt. Van Oosten) into Ana' Galong after the [[7 March]] [[1896|7 March]] battle" — an act which both demonstrated his continuing goodwill toward the Dutch and gave him an opportunity to assess the strength of the post after the engagement. Critically, Lamtengah also "provided the reports that confirmed Oemar's defection (29 March)" — that is, his intelligence was the basis on which the colonial administration formed its first clear picture of what had happened on [[29 March]] [[1896]]. Yet this intelligence role did not translate into lasting loyalty: in the night of [[30 March|30]]–[[31 March]] [[1896]], "with the population of the Moekim Lamtengah", Lamtengah defected to Umar, transforming his *moekim* from a neutral buffer into a hostile frontier.

## The Letter of Mid-May 1896 and the Excursie of 21 May

In the weeks after his defection, Lamtengah continued to play a double game. After [[15 May]] [[1896]] he sent a letter to the Dutch "professing goodwill, promising to return borrowed *geweren* and *ammunitie* and not to fire on Dutch troops". The promise was empty: on [[21 May]] [[1896]] his *gebied* "offered heavy fire" to a Dutch column, and the [[Excursie to Moekim Lamtengah (21 May 1896)|excursie to Moekim Lamtengah]] under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] was dispatched specifically to destroy his possessions. The expedition — which included 6 brigades *maréchaussée* under [[Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland|Kapitein Graafland]], two companies of the [[5e Bataljon]] under [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]], a *bergartillerie* section under [[A. Bangert|Kapitein Bangert]], and a *genietroepen* detachment — searched and burned Lamtengah's territory, in the course of which [[Kapitein Linck]]'s 4e compagnie stormed the *versterking Belang*. The episode exposed the hollowness of Lamtengah's professions of goodwill and confirmed that the *hoeloebalang* class, even when nominally neutral, could not be relied upon by the Dutch once the broader Achehnese coalition had turned.

## Significance

Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah's significance in *ATJEH '96* is that of the archetypal ambiguous *mukimhoofd* — the chief whose neutrality was always conditional, whose intelligence was always tactical, and whose ultimate defection was determined by the balance of forces in his immediate vicinity rather than by any settled ideological commitment. His trajectory from supplier of meat to Ana' Galong, to informant on Umar's defection, to defector, to professing-but-false peacemaker, illustrates the structural unreliability of the *bindingspolitiek* that the Dutch had built in the years [[1893]]–[[1896]] and which Umar's defection had comprehensively undone.

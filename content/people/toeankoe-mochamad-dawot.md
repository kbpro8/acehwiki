**Toeankoe Mochamad Dawot** (also spelled *Toeankoe Mohammed Daoed*) was the young Sultan of [[Atjeh]] recognized by the Acehnese as the legitimate successor to the sultanate following the death of the last Sultan in [[1874]], and declared of age in [[August]] [[1884]] — an event that strengthened the position of the [[Oorlogspartij|oorlogspartij]] (war party) opposing Dutch colonial rule. Later, with [[Panglima Pòlém]] and [[Teukoe Oemar]], he made [[Pidie|Pidië]] the hearth of the resistance from [[1898]].

## Recognition as Sultan (August 1884)

Following the fall of [[Koeta-Radja|Kota-Radja]] in the beginning of [[1874]], the last Sultan of [[Aceh Sultanate|Aceh]] died in flight. Toeankoe Mochamad Dawot was an *achterneef* (great-nephew) of this last sultan and was recognized according to Acehnese *adat* (customary law) as the rightful successor to the *Sultanswaardigheid* (Sultanate). Though the Sultan's authority had been minimal in the final years of the Aceh Sultanate's existence, the Acehnese strictly maintained hereditary succession according to adat, and Mochamad Dawot was in the eyes of all the lawful Sultan.

The Netherlands recognized no Sultan, having replaced the Sultan's authority with Dutch administration. However, Mochamad Dawot's legitimacy persisted among the Acehnese population regardless of Dutch non-recognition. In [[August]] [[1884]] — contemporaneous with the [[Concentration Decision of August 1884]] — he was declared *meerderjarig* (of age) and granted the real conduct of affairs. This timing strengthened the position of the [[Oorlogspartij|oorlogspartij]], which could now claim that the legitimate national authority had formally taken up the cause against the Dutch.

Though the young Sultan personally remained *werkeloos* (inactive) and did not personally place himself at the head of the resistance, the oorlogspartij repeatedly invoked his name when encouraging the population to abandon Dutch authority or when collecting *[[Wang Sabil]]* (holy war funds). [[Sjech Saman van Tirou|Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]] and other religious leaders worked alongside the oorlogspartij, using the Sultan's symbolic legitimacy to mobilize popular support. His declaration of age in [[1884]] thus represented a political resource for the resistance even in the absence of his active leadership.

## Later Career

On [[1 June]] [[1898]] the resistance centre [[Garot]], where the pretender-sultan had gathered with Teukoe Oemar, Panglima Pòlém and [[Teungkoe di Tjòt Plieng]], fell to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]]'s storm. In February [[1911]] the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* surprised the hiding-place of "den langgezochten *panglima* ("war-leader") van den ex-pretendent sultan, [[Keutjhi' Saman]]," who fell with three adherents in the [[Meergebied]]. The seal of the sultan — the [[Seal of the Nine Circles|seal of the nine circles]] — was used to appoint [[T. Tjhi' Toenòng]] genuine *[[oelèëbalang]]* of [[Keureutòë]].

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**Toeankoe Moehamad Dawot** was the pretender-sultan of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] whose submission upon the [[Korte verklaring|short declaration]] Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Johannes van Heutsz]] purchased with the promise that he should never be banished from Atjeh — a promise which had to be broken when the sultan's dealings with a foreign power came to light, so that the "ex-pretender-sultan" and his son [[Toeankoe Ibrahim]] were excepted when Governor [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] allowed the other exiles to return.

### Submission

The submission of the pretender-sultan was apparently much prized by Van Heutsz; it afterward became clear, author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] writes, that he expected too much of it, particularly an immediate mass-submission of other resistance-chiefs. When once it was known that the pretender-sultan was found prepared to sign the short declaration, Van Heutsz — against the advice of Dr. C. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], "on whose compass he in other cases always sailed", and who most strongly dissuaded the giving of pledges and promises toward the pretender-sultan — used several efforts to hasten the submission. He went so far as to assent to the condition that, after his submission, the pretender-sultan should never be banished from Atjeh.

### Disappointment and banishment

The expectation was disappointed: it appeared very soon that the pretender-sultan used every effort to evade Van Heutsz's command to bring the still hostile chiefs to the Dutch government by peaceable means, and that the sultan "even missed every understanding of power over these chiefs" — a fact which must have been a bitter disillusion for Van Heutsz. Some time later, when Van Heutsz as Governor-General could no longer keep his promise, it had come to light that the sultan had entered into relations with a foreign power, in which he asked the question "whether it had no opportunity to wrest the government of Atjeh from the Netherlands". The banishment of the faithless prince followed.

### Exclusion from the return of exiles

When, after order and rest had everywhere returned, Governor [[H. N. A. Swart]] proposed — and obtained — that the Atjehnese of various rank and standing whose residence in the province had been forbidden in the interest of peace and order under article 47 R.R. should be allowed to return, there remained excluded the ex-pretender-sultan Toeankoe Moehamad Dawot and his son Toeankoe Ibrahim.

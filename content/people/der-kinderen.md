**Der Kinderen** — *Mr. Der Kinderen* — was a Dutch colonial official who had, until recently before [[1892]], "*oogverblindend geschitterd aan het bataviasche firmament*" (dazzlingly shone at the Batavian firmament) — and whose work on the Atjèh judiciary, together with that of [[L. W. C. van den Berg|Van den Berg]], was the object of fierce criticism in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Verslag omtrent de religieus-politieke toestanden in Atjeh|Verslag omtrent de religieus-politieke toestanden in Atjeh]]* of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]]. The criticism of Der Kinderen and Van den Berg — on their regulation of the judiciary in Atjèh and on what they had said and written about Atjèh — was the principal occasion of the [[Bekendmaking Nederburgh|Bekendmaking Nederburgh]] prefixed to the second volume of *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjèhers]]* (1894).

## The Batavian Firmament

Mr. Der Kinderen had, until recently before [[1892]], dazzlingly shone at the Batavian firmament — that is, had been one of the principal Dutch colonial officials at Batavia — and that had brought him enviers. Those who composed the *[[Raad van Indië|Raad van Indië]]* that advised to publish the *Verslag* were certainly not all friends of Der Kinderen. Whether the one was connected with the other? Whether the appreciation for so beautiful a work was not co-fed by unsightly *leedvermaek* (schadenfreude)? The possibility cannot be denied, since of haste to behave according to the directions given in the *Verslag*, nothing was to be perceived.

## The 1892 Criticism

The fierce criticism of Der Kinderen and Van den Berg in the *Verslag* of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]] concerned their regulation of the judiciary in Atjèh and what they had said and written about Atjèh. The criticism was, in the event, much milder in the published *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjèhers]]* (Vol. I, 1893; Vol. II, 1894) than in the original *Verslag* — "*veel zachter dan wat de gouverneur-generaal en de Raad van Indië bedoeld hadden te publiceeren*" (much milder than what the governor-general and the Raad van Indië had meant to publish). Yet Der Kinderen and Van den Berg felt themselves not flattered and went to audience with the minister [[Van Dedem|Van Dedem]] to protest against the publication by the government of a work in which such unsalted criticism of their *praestaties* occurred.

## The Van Dedem Intervention

The minister Van Dedem felt with them and wired to Indië that with spreading of the second volume must be waited until after receipt of a letter that would follow. The letter followed and charged the governor-general to give the author of *De Atjèhers* a talking-to and to add, as satisfaction for both criticised gentlemen, a government declaration to the second volume. The result was the [[Bekendmaking Nederburgh|Bekendmaking Nederburgh]] prefixed to the second volume of *De Atjèhers* (1894).

## Significance

Der Kinderen is the Dutch colonial official whose work on the Atjèh judiciary, together with that of Van den Berg, was the object of fierce criticism in Snouck Hurgronje's *Verslag* of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]]. The criticism — and Der Kinderen's protest, with Van den Berg, to the minister Van Dedem — was the principal occasion of the Bekendmaking Nederburgh prefixed to the second volume of *De Atjèhers*. The role of Der Kinderen's enviers in the *Raad van Indië* — "*of het één verband hield met het ander?*" (whether the one was connected with the other?) — is one of the unsolved questions of the episode.

**J. C. Lamster** was the Dutch author whose writings on [[Atjeh]] and on [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] supplied [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] with the framework of two chapters of *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|Generaal Swart]]*: the description of van Heutsz's operational method and the survey of the pacifying administrative work of [[1908]]–[[1918]].

## On van Heutsz's method

Lamster described the operational consequence of the new course of [[1898]]: "Thus independent columns could act against the enemy at full strength for days on end — up to a full week — without having to fritter away a part of their force on a line of operations that was as a rule idle. Later the days during which an independent column acted uninterruptedly became weeks, and the weeks became months. Provisions were then taken for only five or six days, and the rest was bought on the way or, in wholly hostile territory, foraged."

## On the pacifying administrative work

Du Croo built his chapter [[Het Pacificeerende Bestuurswerk 1908-1918|on the pacifying administrative work]] on Lamster's survey in the *Haagsch Maandblad*: "While one heavy blow after another was being dealt to the resistance party, and while the constabulary patrols were tracking down and rendering harmless the last irreconcilable *[[oelama]]s* into the remotest wildernesses and the deepest hiding-places, the pacification was already in full swing in the subjected districts."

Lamster described the treatment of the returning *uitgewekenen*, the caution of not troubling the newly submitted with novelties, the small interest-free advances from the *[[volkscredietbank]]*, the extension of the [[Atjehtram]] to [[Deli]], the preparation of the [[Gajòweg]] for motor traffic, the replacement of muddy paths by gravelled *kampoeng* paths planted with shade trees, and the endless work of administrators and patrols in improving the neglected and unhygienic *kampoengs*.

On method he wrote: "In entirely new districts, consultation was first held with the chiefs and the population. When weighty decisions have to be taken, there is nothing on which the Acehnese sets so much store as on *[[moefakat]]* (deliberation to consensus). The administrator who knows how to make skilful use of this, and who takes the trouble to talk for hours where necessary even with the simplest village man, almost always achieves a good result in the end. Little by little the population itself began to see the advantage of more regulated conditions, of pleasant villages with neatly maintained roads; it began to take pleasure in co-operating in all these improvements."

Lamster also recorded [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]]'s solution of the teacher problem for *[[volksonderwijs]]*, quoted at length by du Croo.

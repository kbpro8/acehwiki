**H. R. van Ham** was a lieutenant of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]] Maréchaussee who commanded the [[Djeuram|Djeuram]] detachment of the IIde divisie on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]] in March [[1942]], and who in that capacity was ordered by Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] to march to [[Tjalang|Tjalang]] on [[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March 1942]] to await the latter's return from [[Lam No|Lam No]], and who subsequently organised and led the convoy evacuation of the Tjalang women and children to [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Van Ham's Tjalang-to-Meulaboh march is the staff history's principal example of the improvised convoy operations of the Westkust evacuation.

## The 12 March 1942 march to Tjalang

When, on [[10 March]] [[10 March 1942|10 March 1942]], Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] had marched from [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]] with two brigades for [[Tjalang|Tjalang]] to relieve the bivak attacked by *kwaadwilligen*, he left the acting command of the divisie with Lt [[A. Zijlmans|Zijlmans]] at Meulaboh and gave Van Ham — at [[Djeuram|Djeuram]] — "opdracht zich met 1 brigade naar Tjalang te begeven en daar op de terugkomst van de divisiecommandant te wachten (uiterlijk 12 Maart)." Van Ham was thus the designated reliefer of the Tjalang bivak, intended to allow Kloprogge to march south to Lam No.

## The Tjalang-to-Meulaboh convoy (14–15 March 1942)

When, on [[14 March]] [[14 March 1942|14 March 1942]], Van Ham had still heard nothing from Kloprogge at Tjalang, "meende hij op grond van ontvangen berichten over de Japanse landingen en de toename van de opstandige beweging, te moeten aannemen dat deze met zijn brigades gevangen genomen was." He therefore ordered the evacuation of the Tjalang women and children to Meulaboh. Lt [[A. Zijlmans|Zijlmans]] "stelde 6 autobussen beschikbaar, waarmede de vrouwen en kinderen van Tjalang naar Meulaboh konden worden vervoerd." Van Ham was designated *transportcommandant* and was given one brigade as dekking.

Van Ham arrived at Meulaboh with the first *échelon* at 16.00 on [[14 March]] [[14 March 1942|14 March 1942]], having "talloze gevechten moeten leveren" with *opstandelingen* along the way. After reporting to Zijlmans he departed again for Tjalang, "waarbij hij talloze gevechten moest leveren om het terrein van opstandelingen te zuiveren." About 20 km from Meulaboh he made telephonic contact with Zijlmans, who informed him of the evacuation and its reasons.

## The 14 March Meulaboh incident

When Van Ham arrived at Meulaboh at 16.00, Zijlmans "kreeg een geval van insubordinatie te verwerken" — apparently arising from the threatened shooting of a *overtreder* by an officer. The *overtreder*, called to account, "nam een dreigende houding aan, zodat hij door de officier moest worden neergeschoten." This incident is the only recorded Dutch disciplinary shooting of the Westkust evacuation.

## The Dutch koelies and vrouwen en kinderen of Tjalang

The Vertrek of the Tjalang gezinnen, "was in verband met de dreigende toestand vervroegd tot 14 Maart 23.00." The last auto left on [[15 March]] [[15 March 1942|15 March 1942]] at 04.00. Van Ham's brigade provided the dekking. With this movement, "waren alle gezinsleden, die in Meulaboh waren geconcentreerd (500) vertrokken," and the Tjalang women and children were safely removed to the Meulaboh campement, where they joined the Westkust evacuee column southward.

## Significance

Van Ham's Tjalang-to-Meulaboh march is, in the staff history's Westkust narrative, the principal illustration of the improvised convoy operations by which the Westkust women and children were moved south to the [[Lae Boetar|Lae Boetar]] onderneming. The staff history's emphasis on the *talloze gevechten* of the march — set against the panic that overtook the noordkust troops under [[C. J. Bekkers|Bekkers]] — places Van Ham in the small staff-history gallery of competent Dutch officers of the Westkust theatre.

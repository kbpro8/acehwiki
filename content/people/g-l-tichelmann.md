**G. L. Tichelmann** was a Dutch colonial ethnographer and artist — the author and illustrator of numerous illustrated articles on the antiquities, ethnography, and prehistory of northern Sumatra, published in [[1939]] in *De Natuur* (Nature), *Cultureel Indië*, *De Indische Gids*, *Groot Nederland*, and other venues, and recorded at length in the Sumatra chapter of [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]]'s *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939]]* (The Hague, [[1940]]). His work is characterised by the combination of ethnographic observation with artistic illustration, and his [[1939]] publications range over the Batak death-puppet (*si gale gale*), Batak rock-inscriptions, a new Batak museum, the Nias megalithic culture, and — of Atjeh-specific interest — the [[In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes (Tichelmann, 1939)|glass vessels excavated in Atjeh]] (No. 625 of the survey).

- **No. 606a** — *The largest image of Sumatra*, in *The Lloyd Mail*, p. 145 *e.v.*
- **No. 621** — (cross-reference: zie bij Schnitger, F. M.)
- **No. 622** — *Felsengänge an Sumatras Ostküste* ("Rock-passages on Sumatra's east coast"), in *Paideuma*, p. 179 *e.v.*

- **No. 625** — *[[In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes (Tichelmann, 1939)|In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes]]* ("Glass jugs excavated in Atjeh"), in *De Natuur* 39, p. 40 *e.v.*
- **No. 626** — *Bataksche beelden* ("Batak statues"), in *Groot Nederland*, p. 577.
- **No. 627** — *Van Bataksche dingen, die verdwijnen* ("Of Batak things that are disappearing"), in *De Indische Gids*, p. 537 *e.v.*
- **No. 628** — *Si gale gale, de Bataksche doodenpop* ("Si gale gale, the Batak death-puppet"), in *Cultureel Indië*, p. 106 *e.v.*
- **No. 629** — *Bataksche maskerplastieken* ("Batak mask-sculptures"), in *Cultureel Indië*, p. 378 *e.v.*
- **No. 630** — *Bataksche rotsinscripties* ("Batak rock-inscriptions"), in *Cultureel Indië*, Med. no. 13, p. 188 *e.v.*
- **No. 632** — *Een nieuwe Bataksche museum* ("A new Batak museum"), in *Cultureel Indië*, Bericht, p. 399.
- **No. 633** (with [[F. M. Schnitger|F. M. Schnitger]]) — *Meer machtige cultuur op Nias* ("More powerful culture on Nias"), in *Cultureel Indië*, Med. no. 2, p. 20 *e.v.*
- **No. 634** (with [[P. Voorhoeve|Dr. P. Voorhoeve]]) — *Over ristgraven in Simaloengoen (S.O.K.)* ("On rist-graves in Simaloengoen"), in *Cultureel Indië*, Med. no. 17, p. 261 *e.v.*

The range of these publications — from Batak death-puppets to Nias megaliths, from Batak rock-inscriptions to a Batak divinity high-priesthood, and from the east-coast rock-passages to the Atjeh glass vessels — marks Tichelmann as the principal Dutch antiquarian-ethnographic observer of northern Sumatra in the late-colonial period.

## The Atjeh-specific contribution

Of Atjeh-specific interest is No. 625 — *[[In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes (Tichelmann, 1939)|In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes]]* — Tichelmann's illustrated note on glass vessels excavated in Atjeh, published in *De Natuur*, vol. 39, p. 40 *e.v.* The article records a small-find of archaeological interest for the trade-history of the Atjehnese sultanate, and it is the only Atjeh-specific entry among Tichelmann's [[1939]] publications. The connection to the [[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo-, Alas- en Bataklanden 1904 (Harders, 1939)|Harders account of the van Daalen campaign]] (No. 568), signalled by the cross-reference "zie bij TICHELMAN, G. L." in that entry, indicates that Tichelmann's ethnographic and antiquarian observations on northern Sumatra were made, in part, in connection with the retrospective military-historical writing on the van Daalen campaign, and it suggests that the glass vessels may have been excavated in the course of, or in connection with, the post-campaign exploration of the interior.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, G. L. Tichelmann is significant as the principal Dutch antiquarian-ethnographic observer of northern Sumatra in the late-colonial period, and as the author of the [[1939]] illustrated note on [[In Atjeh opgedolven glazen kruikjes (Tichelmann, 1939)|glass vessels excavated in Atjeh]] — the only Atjeh-specific entry among his extensive [[1939]] publications. His work connects the archaeology and antiquities of Atjeh to the wider ethnographic landscape of northern Sumatra, and the cross-reference from the [[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo-, Alas- en Bataklanden 1904 (Harders, 1939)|Harders account of the van Daalen campaign]] places his observations within the context of the post-campaign exploration of the interior.

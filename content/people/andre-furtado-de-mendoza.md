**André Furtado de Mendoza** (also spelled Mendonça) was a Portuguese naval commander who, in [[1601]], commanded a fleet of a galleon and a fust sent from [[Goa|Goa]] by way of [[Malaka|Malaka]] to [[Bantam|Bantam]] with instructions to discipline the natives — a fleet whose appearance off [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in June [[1601]] sharpened the sultan's hostility to the Portuguese and indirectly facilitated the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]] and the release of [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]]. He is the principal Portuguese naval figure of the Atjeh chapter in the immediate aftermath of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman affair]].

## The fleet to Bantam (1601)

Furtado's fleet — a galleon (*galjoen*) and a fust — had been dispatched from Goa with instructions to sail via Malaka to Bantam, where the natives were to be "getucht" — disciplined — for their reception of the Dutch. Van der Woude notes that Furtado's mission was part of the wider Portuguese effort to interrupt the Dutch pepper trade in the archipelago after the [[Founding of the VOC (1602)|Dutch commercial challenge]] of the late [[1590s]]. The appearance of Furtado's fleet off Atjeh in June [[1601]] — at the very moment when the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]] was weighing how to receive the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|Van Caerden]] and the expected Zeeland mission — created a strategic opening for the Dutch.

## The effect at Atjeh

The appearance of Furtado's fleet at Atjeh — combined with the unguarded conduct of the [[Franciscan|Franciscan]] monk at the sultan's court, who had been pressing the sultan to cede his strongest fort to the Portuguese — turned the sultan against the Portuguese. The Atjehers suspected a connection between the Portuguese fleet's arrival and the simultaneous presence of a fleet of sixty warships of the sultan of [[Djohor|Djohor]] off [[Pasei|Pasei]], and "versterkten zich, bevreesd voor een aanval van beiden". The sultan, unwilling to make the Portuguese his enemies, but angered when they seized an Arab ship in defiance of his hospitality, had his shore batteries fire on the Portuguese ship; one of Furtado's ships was lost, and the crew was disarmed and imprisoned.

Van der Woude notes the strategic consequence: "de volgende Hollandsche vloot zou van deze gebeurtenissen profiteeren". The Zeeland fleet of [[Hans Huybrecht Tonneman|Hans Huybrecht Tonneman]] that arrived off Atjeh on [[23 August]] [[1601]] bearing a letter from [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]] found the sultan already alienated from the Portuguese; the sultan released [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] without ransom and granted the Zeelanders a factory and a commercial treaty. Furtado's failed mission thus produced the diplomatic rapprochement that the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman ambush of 1599]] had foreclosed.

## The 1603 defeat at Bantam

Furtado continued his mission to Bantam, where his fleet was defeated by a Dutch squadron under [[Wolfert Harmensz.|Wolfert Harmensz]] in a sea battle off the roadstead — a defeat that, in Van der Woude's narrative, completed the strategic defeat of the Portuguese attempt to interrupt the Dutch pepper trade in the archipelago. The defeat also confirmed that the Portuguese could no longer project significant naval force into the western archipelago after the destruction of their Malaka fleet by [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] in [[1606]] — though Furtado's defeat preceded Matelieff's victory by several years.

## Significance

André Furtado de Mendoza is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Portuguese naval figure of the Atjeh chapter in the immediate aftermath of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman affair]] and the principal instance of the Portuguese attempt to interrupt the Dutch pepper trade at its Atjeh source. His failed mission of [[1601]] paradoxically facilitated the Dutch-Achehnese rapprochement that the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman ambush of 1599]] had foreclosed, by turning the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]] against the Portuguese and preparing the ground for the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]]. Furtado's subsequent defeat at Bantam by [[Wolfert Harmensz.|Wolfert Harmensz]] confirmed that the Portuguese could no longer project significant naval force into the western archipelago — a strategic fact that the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] would exploit in the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]] and after.

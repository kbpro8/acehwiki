## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Dr. Brandes

**Dr. Brandes** was a Dutch Indologist and Javanist whose writings on the cultural and calendrical survivals of pre-Islamic Indonesia are cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in *The Achehnese*. He is mentioned in particular for a "very interesting article" in the *Tijdschrift van het Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen*, vol. XLI, in which he elucidated the original meaning of the name of the Mohammedan month **Dul-qa'dah** — Achehnese **Meuapet** — which has "no connection with the Mohammedan calendar" as such.

## The meaning of "Dul-qa'dah"

In the Achehnese calendar, the eleventh month of the Mohammedan year — *Dū al-Qaʿdah* — is called **Meuapet**, "pinched" or "shut in". The same idea is expressed in the Javanese name for the month, *Apit* (Sundanese *Hapit*), meaning "pinched", and *Sila*, "interval". Snouck Hurgronje refers the reader to Brandes for the original meaning of this name, which is "no connection with the Mohammedan calendar", but reflects, rather, the place of the month within an older, pre-Islamic Javanese system of time-reckoning. Brandes's article thus forms part of the larger scholarly effort, in which [[Dr. A. B. Cohen Stuart|Dr. A. B. Cohen Stuart]] and [[G. K. Niemann|G. K. Niemann]] also took part, to reconstruct the indigenous Indonesian elements that survive, thinly disguised, in the ostensibly Mohammedan calendar of the Archipelago.

## Place in *The Achehnese*

Brandes is cited at a single point in *The Achehnese*, but the citation is significant for the light it throws on Snouck Hurgronje's method. By drawing attention to the survival of pre-Islamic nomenclature in the Mohammedan month-names, Brandes's article enables Snouck Hurgronje to bring out the mixed character of Achehnese religious observance — at once Mohammedan in its formal framework and Indonesian in much of its content. The same perspective governs Snouck Hurgronje's treatment of the Achehnese *keunong* system, which he elucidated with the help of [[Dr. J. P. van der Stok|Dr. J. P. van der Stok]] and [[Dr. S. Figee|Dr. S. Figee]], and of the Achehnese names for the months themselves, which are mostly derived not from the Arabic but from the characteristic *kanduris* (religious feasts) held in each.

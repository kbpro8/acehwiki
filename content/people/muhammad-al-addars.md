**Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs** was a Ḥadramī-born religious scholar of [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]] in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries, the teacher of [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] and thus the spiritual grandfather of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and of [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]]. He was born in [[1561]] at [[Tarim]] in South Arabia and came to [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]] at the age of nineteen, where he succeeded his famous grandfather as religious teacher, first at [[Ahmadabad]] and later at [[Surat]]. He died at [[Surat]] in [[1620]].

## Origin and migration

Al-ʿAddārs was born in [[1561]] at [[Tarim]] — the ancient capital of the Ḥaḍramawt valley in South Arabia, and one of the principal centres of Ḥadramī scholarship. At the age of nineteen — that is, in [[1580]] — he came to Gujarat, where he succeeded his famous grandfather as religious teacher. The migration of al-ʿAddārs from Tarim to Gujarat was part of the larger Ḥadramī diaspora that, from the late middle ages onwards, carried the scholars and saints of the Ḥaḍramawt to the ports of the Indian Ocean — from the Maldive Islands to [[Acheh]] and the Malay-Indonesian archipelago.

## Residence in Gujarat

Al-ʿAddārs succeeded his grandfather as religious teacher first at [[Ahmadabad]] — the great city of Gujarat, and a principal centre of Indian-Arab scholarship — and later at [[Surat]] — the port that, in this period, was the principal point of contact between Gujarat and the Malay-Indonesian world. He died at Surat in [[1620]]. His role as the successor of his grandfather at both Ahmadabad and Surat indicates the hereditary character of the religious office in the Ḥadramī-Indian families; it also indicates the standing of al-ʿAddārs as one of the principal religious teachers of Gujarat in his generation.

## The spiritual genealogy

Al-ʿAddārs is the teacher of [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] — the shaikh of [[Raniri]] and [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]] in the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] — and so the spiritual grandfather of both Malay-Indonesian Sufis. The transmission of the Rifāʿiyya from al-ʿAddārs to Bā Shaybān at [[Surat]] is the immediate link by which the orthodox Sufi silsila of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī network was carried to the Malay-Indonesian world; and the role of al-ʿAddārs as the spiritual grandfather of Raniri places him at the head of the spiritual genealogy by which Raniri, on his arrival in [[Acheh]] in [[1637]], claimed the authority of an orthodox mystic against the [[Wujudiyyah]] of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] and [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]].

**Habib Abdoerrachman el Zahir** — properly *Sejjid Abdurrachman Zahir*, and known to the Dutch and the Acehnese alike simply as *de Habib* (the Habib) — was an Arab *sayyid* of prophetic descent whose deep religious learning and political gifts made him, in the words of [[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]], one of the most dangerous and influential leaders of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh resistance]] in the last years of the 1870s. He was, with [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]] after him, one of the two great figures who in that early phase succeeded in drawing the fragmented land towards anything resembling unified leadership, preaching the *prang sabil* (holy war) and drawing thousands of fighters to his standard.

The Dutch colonial government considered him such a dangerous adversary that, rather than continue the military contest, it resolved to remove him from [[Aceh|Atjeh]] by negotiation. His submission was celebrated in [[Batavia]] and [[Koetaradja]] as a political success: on [[13 October]] [[1878]] he formally submitted on the condition that he settle in [[Mekka]] with an annual pension, charged to the colonial budget, of no less than 10,000 dollars. On [[24 November]] [[1878]] he was embarked for Djeddah on board Hr. Ms. *Curaçao*. The Atjeh officers of the day sang of this "costly buy-off" of the Habib in the *Falderaldera-leed* that Major Macleod — husband of Mata Hari, then a lieutenant in Atjeh — later recalled:

> *"Daar ligt een ijzeren zeekasteel, / Dat heet de 'Curaçao', / Waarmee die brave Habib / Naar Mekka varen zou. / Nu zingt hij vroolijk 'falderaldera, / Voor dit Gouvernement. / Zoo'n duizend dollars in de maand: / Ben ik geen knappe vent?"*

## The 1884 memorandum

Even from Mekka, where he consumed his handsome annual stipend, the Habib could not detach himself from the Atjeh question. In [[1884]] he sent from Mekka a memorandum to the Netherlands-Indies Government outlining a scheme by which the submission of Atjeh might at last be achieved. Dated 3 Moharram 1302 — [[October]] [[1884]] — and signed *HABIB ABDOERRACHMAN EL ZAHIR* under a seal reading "Rijksbestierder van het Gouvernement ATJEH" (Chief Minister of the Government of Atjeh), the memorandum proposed that the Government appoint a Muslim distinguished by personal excellence, noble descent, and knowledge of Atjeh affairs as *regent* — under the title of *Radja* or similar — to govern the internal affairs of the country on behalf of the Netherlands-Indies Government, in cooperation with a college of chiefs, scholars, and notables, and within a precisely defined commission set out in his diploma. The Habib naturally saw himself as the coming *regent* of Atjeh; the seal he had had cut already named him so.

In Mekka he remained in regular contact with Atjeh pilgrims, who came to him for counsel as well as for the rites of the *hadj*. Zentgraff notes that this expectation, seen from the Dutch side, may look absurd and arrogant, but must be read in the light of an age when the colonial government itself judged the Habib's voluntary withdrawal from Atjeh so important that it paid him a princely annual stipend for Arabia.

## Significance

The Habib's removal in [[1878]] closed the first phase of unified religious-political leadership of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh resistance]], but it also opened the era of the [[Tiro-oelama|Tiro-oelama's]]. As Zentgraff puts it: after the Habib's period "our weak government received more slaps in the face; the Tiro-oelama's took care of that." His memorandum of [[1884]] is preserved as one of the curiosities of the early diplomatic record of the war — a sayyid's proposal that the Netherlands-Indies Government install him, with a sultan's title, as its Muslim chief minister over Atjeh.

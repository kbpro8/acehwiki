**Toekoe Djohan Lampasei** — also written *T. Djohan Lampasei*, *T. Djohan Lampasai* — was, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, "the 2nd *sagihoofd* of the [[XXVI Moekims]] who had never submitted" (*het 2de nimmer in onderwerping gekomen sagihoofd der XXVI Moekims*), a description which identifies him as the second of the two territorial-federation chiefs of the eastern XXVI *sagi* — alongside [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] — who had never reconciled with the [[Dutch]] colonial government. His name connects him to [[Lampasei]] (a coastal area of the XXVI Moekims) and, through the shared honorific "Djohan", to [[Teuku Umar]] himself, whose own pre-defection Dutch name was *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*. Kruisheer records that Lampasei "joined Umar" in the spring of [[1896]] and that he subsequently "stayed at [[Masdjid Lepong]] [where he] *leedde kalm van den sabil Allah*" (calmly preached the jihad / *sabil Allah*), a phrase which marks him as one of the religious-military figures who maintained the ideological legitimacy of the resistance after Umar's defection.

## Position as the Second Never-Submitted Sagihoofd

The [[XXVI Moekims]] formed the eastern territorial division of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and had been the most consistently resistant to Dutch authority throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The colonial administration's relationship with this *sagi* was managed through its two *sagihoofden*: the first, [[Toekoe Nja Banta]], cooperated with the Dutch (bringing the formal report of Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection on 29 March]] [[1896]] and being entrusted with the post of [[Toengkoeb]] when the Dutch evacuated it on [[21 April]] [[1896]]); the second, Lampasei, "had never submitted" and joined Umar's coalition. This bifurcation within a single *sagi* was characteristic of Achehnese political organisation, in which the *sagihoofd*'s authority did not necessarily extend over the entire *sagi* and in which rival lineages could pursue diametrically opposed policies toward the Dutch. Lampasei's presence in the resistance gave the Achehnese coalition a foothold in the eastern coastal plain which, combined with [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]]'s defection in the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and [[Teungkoe Id-cen / Mohamad Said|Teungkoe Id-cen]]'s command of the eastern *stelling* near [[Kroeng-Kali]], transformed the eastern frontier of the Dutch *geconcentreerde linie* into a continuous theatre of operations through June [[1896]].

## Preaching of Sabil Allah at Masdjid Lepong

After joining Umar, Lampasei "stayed at [[Masdjid Lepong]]" — the mosque of [[Lepong]], the *landschap* of which Umar was himself *hoeloebalang*. There he "*leedde kalm van den sabil Allah*" — Kruisheer's phrase, awkwardly rendered, meaning approximately "calmly led [the preaching] of the *sabil Allah*" — that is, he preached the jihad as a religious duty. The *Masdjid Lepong* functioned as one of the religious-political centres of the post-defection Achehnese coalition; it was also the site at which [[Teungkoe di Tiro]]'s successors exercised authority and at which [[Teungkoe Sjech of Kroeng Kali]] and other religious figures could find a sympathetic audience. The image of Lampasei preaching jihad at the principal mosque of Umar's own *landschap* is one of the most evocative in *ATJEH '96*, because it captures the fusion of religious legitimacy and political-military leadership which characterised the post-defection Achehnese coalition. The [[XXVI Moekims]] also suffered marine bombardment of Lepong on [[25 May]] and on [[4 May|4]]–[[5 June]] and [[8 June|8]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]], in part to make Umar's stay untenable — bombardments which would have directly affected the Masdjid Lepong community of which Lampasei was part.

## Significance

Toekoe Djohan Lampasei's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the *sagihoofd* who, having never submitted to the Dutch, joined Umar's coalition and provided it with religious legitimacy through the preaching of *sabil Allah* at the Masdjid Lepong. His name — with its shared "Djohan" element — also suggests a kinship or symbolic connection to Umar himself, which would have lent his preaching additional authority within the coalition. The pairing of [[Toekoe Nja Banta|Nja Banta]] (cooperative) and Lampasei (resistant) within the same *sagi* illustrates the structural tension within Achehnese political organisation that the Dutch sought to exploit and that Umar's defection briefly overcame.

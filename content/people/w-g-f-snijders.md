**W. G. F. Snijders** was a Dutch infantry officer who retired in [[1905]] as Luitenant-Generaal and Inspector of Infantry, the second of the eight sons of the Snijders family of [[Nieuwe Tonge|Nieuwe Tonge]] and the older brother of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Generaal C. J. Snijders]]. His choice of the military career in [[1867]] set the example that drew his younger brother Cornelis toward the army; and the example of his infantry officer's uniform on leave in Nieuwe Tonge was, by the younger brother's own later admission, the immediate cause of his applying to the [[Koninklijke Militaire Academie|KMA]]. W. G. F. Snijders was considered by [[N. Bosboom|Minister of War Bosboom]] as a candidate for the supreme command of the Dutch armed forces in [[1914]], before the appointment of his younger brother to that post.

## The Brother's Influence

[[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] records that the example of the older brother Wilhelm, who became an infantry officer in [[1867]] and on leave in Nieuwe Tonge "furore maakte" with his fine uniform, drew Cornelis toward a military career. The choice was "uitsluitend gegrond op de uiterlijkheden van het militaire vak"; Cornelis "had geen inzicht in de beteekenis van het beroep." When the question arose whether Cornelis should apply for infantry or engineers, the father's calculation was that "de drie hoogere bijdragen, welke hij alsdan voor CORNELIS zou hebben te betalen, vrijwel overeenkwamen met de vier lagere, welke hij vroeger voor WILLEM had geofferd" — the higher engineers tuition for Cornelis being roughly equal to the four lower infantry payments for Wilhelm. Before Cornelis sat the KMA admission exam in June [[1869]] he was tutored at Breda by his brother Wilhelm.

## Considered for the Supreme Command in 1914

In the discussions of [[1914]] over who should be appointed [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber]] in case of war, [[N. Bosboom|Minister Bosboom]] — as he later recorded in his *In moeilijke omstandigheden* — had thought of W. G. F. Snijders, "een opperofficier van groote strategische bekwaamheid en naar 's Ministers oordeel toe-gerust met zoodanige geestes- en karaktereigenschappen, dat hij alleszins voor het opperbevel over onze strijdkrachten berekend mocht worden geacht." The obstacle was his "bekende hardhoofdigheid" — Bosboom judged that, if the deafness had not progressed too far, it need not be an insurmountable bar. In the event, the younger brother Cornelis was appointed, by *Koninklijk Besluit* of [[9 Augustus|9 August]] [[1914]] No. 7, to the rank of General and to the office of Opperbevelhebber.

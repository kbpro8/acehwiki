**Demmeni** — *Kolonel Demmeni*, later *Generaal Demmeni* — was a Dutch officer of the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army ([[K.N.I.L.|K.N.I.L.]]) who served as *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (Civil and Military Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies) from [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] to his death in [[December 1886|December]] [[1886]], and whose governorship oversaw the implementation of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] decreed by the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]] in [[June 1884]] and the failed [[Blokkade (Atjeh, 1884-1885)|blockade of 1884]]–[[1885|1885]] under the command of [[C. H. Bogaert|C. H. Bogaert]]. He died in the Padang highlands in [[December 1886|December]] [[1886]], having in his last months urged on the government the introduction of a [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] of his own design.

## Appointment (September 1884)

When Laging Tobias retired in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]], the government — convinced that under the new circumstances there was no longer place for civil government alone — decided to return to the earlier arrangement of civil and military authority in one hand, "doch nu helaas op de puinhoopen van het gesloopte gebouw onzer macht onder van der Heijden" (but now alas on the ruins of the demolished building of our power under Van der Heijden). Colonel Demmeni accordingly took office as *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur*.

## The Concentration System (1884–1886)

The *concentratie* — the contraction of the Dutch *stelling* in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] to a smaller, more defensible perimeter — was carried out by Demmeni "met veel beleid en groote energie" (with much discretion and great energy). The new *stelling* after the concentration was approximately the same as that taken by [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] in [[1876]]. It still existed at the time of Kruijt's writing. It was a continuous line of fortifications around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], forming a kind of isosceles triangle, with the post [[Lambaroe|Lambaroe]] as the apex and the sea coast from [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] to [[Kota Pohama|Kota Pohama]] as the base. It was to ensure complete security within the line and be impregnable from without. A 1,000-metre wide, felled, open strip surrounded it, to prevent the enemy from approaching the posts unobserved or slipping through. A *[[Ceintuur-trambaan|ceintuursporbaan]]* (belt railway) and telephone would connect the posts with one another and by spokes with [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]]. All this was carried out and arranged as quickly and well as possible — indeed excellently — and by [[July 1886|July]] [[1886]] everything was ready. Communication between the population inside and outside the line was as good as broken off. Atjehers were forbidden to bear arms; carrying a pass was made compulsory.

## The Failed Blockade (1884–1885)

The *Blokkade* was to be strict and to cover the whole coast. It was to serve, by cutting off all supply to the enemy, to facilitate the retreat in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. A large number of warships and other vessels acting as such were assembled in Atjeh and placed under the command of [[C. H. Bogaert|Bogaert]]. With his old energy Bogaert came out from the Netherlands, animated by the firm will to make the blockade answer its purpose. How bitterly he was disappointed: already before he had taken command, he heard at [[Batavia|Batavia]] that its early abolition had been decided. Numerous exceptions arising from political and other considerations obstructed its whole execution and destroyed its effect. This system, too, was not carried out consistently. Only a small part of the coast was blockaded, and that half-heartedly. A kind of [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]], though officially abolished, remained partially in force. [[Edi|Edi]] and [[Segli|Segli]] remained as posts and trading harbours. All ships from outside destined for the [[Noordkust|North]] and [[Oostkust|East Coasts]] had first to call at one of these two places before being allowed to proceed to one of the unclosed harbours.

Bogaert did what he could under the circumstances, but always regretted that he had been used for what Kruijt calls, with bitter wordplay, a "politioneele (polichinelle) blokkade" — a Punchinello blockade, a farce. In [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] only partially applied, the blockade was soon raised for other parts of the coast, and after the concentration had been accomplished by Colonel Demmeni with great discretion and energy — since it could no longer have the effect the government had aimed at — it was wholly withdrawn in [[March 1885|March]] [[1885]]. The ships left Atjeh; only a few remained to guard against the smuggling of contraband, for which they were entirely inadequate. Trade was almost wholly free, and in [[December 1885|December]] [[1885]] even the least restriction on the trade of the coastal statelets with the outside ([[Penang|Penang]]) was forbidden.

## The Restoration of the Sultanate (1885–1886)

The Dutch, having retired into the concentrated *stelling*, would now await without coercion and patiently until the enemy should come to submit. The enemy, abundantly supplied, familiar with Dutch capriciousness and fickleness, knowing that the Dutch were never sure of what they really wanted — "*Kompanie tidah tentoe; orang blanda bodoh*" (the Kompanie is never sure of its case; the Hollanders are stupid), as the Atjehers said — did not sit still. On the contrary, he prosecuted the war in his own way, now more than ever.

[[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]], guardian and father-in-law of the youthful pretender, had the latter declared of age and proclaimed and hailed as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]]. The court was arranged according to ancient usage, and notification of the accession was given to various courts. He further wrote everywhere that the Hollanders had been driven back, that the war would be vigorously continued, and, in concert with the religious leader [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] — whose influence ever increased — sent the principal leaders to the coastal statelets to stir up the population, to embroil the well-disposed, to destroy Dutch influence, to call all to the holy war against the Dutch, and to collect money and men. Those who openly took part in this struggle were henceforth called *[[Muslemin|Muslemin]]*. He also sent his son-in-law, the *radja* of [[Madjapait|Madjapait]], to the [[Gayo|Gajoes]] in the interior — whom the Dutch had helped against [[Langsar|Langsar]] — to induce them to invade [[Langkat|Langkat]]. All the chiefs collected the war contributions. The Sultan's influence greatly increased.

## Death (December 1886)

The Dutch position became increasingly precarious. [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] was master on the [[Westkust|West Coast]], levied taxes, pressed the population, and murdered, robbed, and burned where resistance was offered. In [[June 1886|June]] [[1886]] he captured the steamship *[[Hok Canton|Hok Canton]]*; for 25,000 dollars the Dutch bought the prisoners free with difficulty; [[Kemala|Kemala]] received its share. Many other acts of piracy occurred elsewhere against English subjects and their property, which provoked claims from the English ([[Straits Settlements|Straits]]) government. Attacks on the islands, on the [[Willemstoren|Willemstoren]] at [[Poeloe Bras|Poeloe Bras]], occurred. Even the [[Cedar Passage|Cedar Passage]], the usual sea route to the West Coast, was officially declared unsafe. Ships passing there were repeatedly fired on by the enemy from [[Koningspunt|Koningspunt]].

In [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] the 1,000-metre open strip, which could only be kept clear with great difficulty and losses, was soon largely overgrown again with tropical speed. The Muslemin made the line unsafe, damaged the railway and bridges, destroyed the telephone line, fired on the trains, laid grenades under the rails, and so on, and made raids inward. The troop strength, then about 5,000 men, had a heavy service through the occupation and securing of the posts, patrols within the line, and suffered much from beri-beri and other diseases. [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] was the soul of the resistance.

Demmeni finally came to see this, and urged on the government the introduction of a [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] of his own proposal. In vain. Long ailing, he sought recovery of health in the [[Padang|Padang]] highlands and succumbed there in [[December 1886|December]] [[1886]].

## Significance

Demmeni's governorship is the period of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] in its purest form — the deliberate contraction of the Dutch position to a defensible perimeter around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and the abandonment of the coastal administration. The concentration itself, Kruijt argues, was a wise measure — "een wijzen maatregel, waartoe meer moed en vaderlandsliefde behoorde, dan tot het hardnekkig voortgaan op eenen kostbaren, gevaarlijken en onzekeren weg" (a wise measure, requiring more courage and love of fatherland than the stubborn prosecution of a costly, dangerous and uncertain path). But it should have been accompanied by a vigorous [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] on the coasts, with partial transfer of resources thither, while instead, against the advice of all experts, what the Dutch had was given up and the coasts were left to their fate. "Dat was en bleef de fout" (That was and remained the mistake).

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In the [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] places Demmeni as the eighth name in his catalogue of the eleven Governors of Atjeh — *van Swieten, Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont, van der Heijden, Pruijs van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias, Demmeni, van Teijn, Pompe van Meerdervoort, Deijkerhof* — and the predecessor whose death at the end of [[1886]] opened the governorship to [[Van Teijn|Kolonel van Teijn]]. Demmeni was appointed to execute the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] ordered by the [[Staatsblad No. 105 van 1884|Staatsblad No. 105 of 1884]] — the *rigoureuse blokkade* (rigorous blockade) of the entire coast from [[Tamiang]] on the [[Oostkust]] to [[Troemon]] on the [[Westkust]] — and, wavering under the protests of [[Scherer]] and the *assistent-resident van Groot-Atjeh* [[Van Langen|van Langen]], declined to apply the full *blokkade* and instead promulgated, by his *besluit* (decree) of [[13 October]] [[1884]], the hybrid *half sluiting / half scheepvaartregeling* (half closure / half shipping-regulation) that Van Heutsz would denounce as the worst of both worlds.

### Appointment after Laging Tobias's resignation

Demmeni's appointment followed the resignation of [[Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]] in February [[1884]]. Laging Tobias had asked to resign when the *Regeering* rejected the *scheepvaartregeling* — the one remedy from which, in the existing circumstances, he had expected a good outcome — and the *Concentratie*, decided in the secret Staten-Generaal sessions of [[16 June|16]]–[[17 June]] [[1884]], was entrusted instead to the *krijgsmacht* (army). Van Heutsz records the handover: "Het bestuur weder in handen gegeven aan een militair, den kolonel Demmeni" — The administration was again placed in the hands of a military man, Colonel Demmeni. The expectation was that Demmeni would execute the *rigoureuse sluiting* (rigorous closure) the *Regeering* had prescribed without the qualifications Laging Tobias had sought to attach. Van Heutsz's verdict on the expectation is measured: "de Generaal Demmeni, hoe ook diens denkbeelden omtrent de Atjeh-zaak waren, eenvoudig het hem voorgeschreven stelsel in practijk zou brengen" — General Demmeni, whatever his own views on the Atjeh question, would simply put the system prescribed to him into practice. [[Scherer|Scherer]]'s reading, recorded in his [[Indisch Genootschap]] lecture of [[20 Januari|20 January]] [[1891]], was the same: "toen hij bemerkte, dat de Generaal Demmeni, hoe ook diens denkbeelden omtrent de Atjeh-zaak waren, eenvoudig het hem voorgeschreven stelsel in practijk zou brengen, besloot om heen te gaan" — when he perceived that General Demmeni would simply put the prescribed system into practice, [Scherer] decided to leave.

### Wobbling under Scherer's protests

Demmeni did not, in the event, apply the prescribed system without qualification. Van Heutsz's diagnosis is that Scherer's *betoogingen* (representations) and his *nota's* — co-signed by the *assistent-resident van Groot-Atjeh* [[Van Langen|van Langen]] — shook the General's resolution:

> "De Generaal Demmeni werd door Scherer's betoogingen en zijne nota's, waarmede de assistent-resident van Groot- Atjeh van Langen instemde, aan het wankelen gebracht in zijn plan om cenvoudig uitte- voeren, wat hem door de Regeering opgedragen was en voerde niet de algemeene rigoureuse blokkade in, maar liet slechts sommige gedeelten van de kust sluiten en blokkeeren en dit niet eens consequent, terwijl hij voor andere gedeelten, reeds bij besluit van 13 October 1884 eene soort scheepraartregelin invoerde". — General Demmeni was shaken in his plan to carry out simply what the Government had charged him with, by Scherer's representations and his notes, which the assistant-resident of Groot-Atjeh van Langen endorsed; and he did not introduce the general rigorous blockade, but only closed and blockaded some parts of the coast, and that not even consistently, while for other parts he already introduced, by decree of 13 October 1884, a kind of shipping-regulation.

Demmeni's compromise was to close some parts of the coast (inconsistently), and, for the remaining parts, to introduce, by his *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]], "eene soort scheepraartregelin" — a kind of *scheepvaartregeling*. The compromise determined that all ships arriving from outside Atjeh were to call first at one of the Dutch-administered places before proceeding to any non-closed point of the coast — and that, after the posts of [[Telok Semawé]] and [[Samalanga]] were withdrawn, those places were only [[Edi]], [[Segli]] and [[Olehleh]] for the entire Noord- en Oostkust. Van Heutsz's verdict on the compromise is severe: "Men kreeg dus in plaats van het voorgeschreven stelsel, dat toch minstens de deugd van kracht en eenheid had, een tweeslachtig ding en wel: 1e. eene soort van scheepvaartregeling, die niet deugde … en 2e. naast die verkeerde scheepvaartregeling eene partieele blokkade, die natuurlijk, in verband met de eerste, evenmin nut kon stichten" — Instead of the prescribed system, which at least had the virtue of force and unity, one got an ambiguous thing, namely: 1. a kind of shipping-regulation that was no good … and 2. next to that wrong shipping-regulation a partial blockade, which naturally, in connection with the first, could do no good either. The one thing the hybrid scheme could achieve, Van Heutsz conceded, was "dat aan den invoer van oorlogscontrabande belemmering werd in den weg gelegd" (that some impediment was placed in the way of the import of war contraband).

### Opposition to the full blokkade

Demmeni's reluctance to apply the full *blokkade* was not, in Van Heutsz's account, a momentary wavering but a sustained position, expressed in correspondence with the *Regeering* and the *Opperbestuur* (Supreme Authority) in Nederland. The position was that the full *blokkade* would not bring the submission of Groot-Atjeh, because the interior produced sufficient food to outlast the closure, and that the *doel* (goal) — the *bedwingen van het voortdurend verzet in Groot-Atjeh door het uitoefenen van dwang op de onderhoorige kuststaten* (the suppression of the continuing resistance in Groot-Atjeh by exercising coercion on the dependent coastal statelets) — could not be attained by it. Van Heutsz records the correspondence that exposed the General's position:

> "dat uit die briefwisseling, gepaard met voorstellen uit Atjeh, gebleken was, dat de Generaal Demmeni van eene blokkade voor Atjeh in 't geheel geen heil verwachtte, omdat, op grond vooral van de vermeende voldoende voedingsmiddelen in het binnenland, naar zijne overtuiging, daarmede het doel dat er door beoogd werd: het bedwingen van het voortdurend verzet in Groot-Atjeh door het uitoefenen van dwang op de onderhoorige kuststaten, niet te bereiken was". — that from that correspondence, accompanied by proposals from Atjeh, it had appeared that General Demmeni expected no salvation at all from a blockade of Atjeh, because, on the ground especially of the presumed sufficient foodstuffs in the interior, in his conviction the goal that was aimed at by it — the suppression of the continuing resistance in Groot-Atjeh by exercising coercion on the dependent coastal statelets — could not be attained thereby.

The reasons Demmeni gave for applying only a partial closure — sparing the Oostkust, [[Telok Semawé]], [[Kerti]] and the other places "waar sedert jaren rust en welvaart heerschten" (where for years peace and prosperity had prevailed) and where under Scherer's administration "geleidelijk een ordelijken staat van zaken was ingetreden" (a regular state of affairs had gradually come into being) — were "zeer begrijpelijk" (very understandable) in Van Heutsz's reading; the civil servants whose "macht en invloed … stond of viel met behoud of loslating van de scheepvaartregeling" (power and influence … stood or fell with the preservation or relinquishing of the shipping-regulation) were "zeer tegen de sluiting gekant" (very opposed to the closure). But the *Regeering*, Van Heutsz argues, ought to have weighed the *vóór* and *tegen* (pro and contra) once more, decided on either the one or the other, and then permitted no deviation: "Zij deed echter het tegendeel" — it did the opposite.

### The repeal by Staatsblad No. 85 van 1885

The correspondence between the *Regeering* in Indië, the *Opperbestuur* in Nederland, and Demmeni in Atjeh ended, in [[1885]], with the repeal of the *blokkade*. Van Heutsz records the repeal and the *Regeering*'s own post-mortem in a single sentence:

> "werd bij Staatsblad No 85 van 1885 het Staatsblad No 105 van 1884 ingetrokken, bij welke intrekking de Regeering de zeer juiste opmerking voegde, dat de blokkade algemeen had moeten zijn om het beoogde doel te bereiken, maar slechts teleurstellingen kon baren toen zij slechts partieel werd toegepast en een groot gedeelte van het kustgebied voor den handel openbleef". — by Staatsblad No 85 of 1885, Staatsblad No 105 of 1884 was withdrawn, with which withdrawal the Government coupled the very correct observation, that the blockade should have been general in order to attain the goal aimed at, but could only produce disappointments when it was only partially applied and a large part of the coastal territory remained open for trade.

The [[Staatsblad No. 85 van 1885|Staatsblad No. 85 of 1885]] thus repealed the [[Staatsblad No. 105 van 1884|Staatsblad No. 105 of 1884]], and the great naval force sent to Atjeh for the *sluiting* "werd tegelijkertijd teruggebracht tot enkele schepen" (was at the same time reduced to a few ships). The *scheepvaartregeling* Demmeni had introduced for some of the coastal states remained, however, in force until approximately the end of [[1885]]: "De door den Generaal voor sommige kuststaten ingevoerde scheepvaartregeling bleef intusschen nog in stand tot ongeveer het einde van het jaar 1885" — The shipping-regulation introduced by the General for some of the coastal states meanwhile remained in force until approximately the end of the year 1885 — and was finally abolished by the *Regeering* in December [[1885]], when "voor het vervolg zelfs de geringste beperking van den handel der kuststaten met het buitenland, onder welken vorm ook, verboden" was made — for the future even the slightest restriction on the trade of the coastal statelets with the outside, in whatever form, was forbidden.

### The posthumous verdict and succession by Van Teijn

Van Heutsz's posthumous verdict on Demmeni turns on the absence of Scherer — the man who could have advised the General how to construct the *scheepvaartregeling* he had begun to introduce, but who had resigned in early October [[1884]] and left the colony:

> "Moet men nu niet beamen, dat Scherer's onmiddellijk heengaan op verzoek jammer was. Tot einde 1885 werd er van Atjeh uit met de pen gestreden voor het behoud van de scheepvaartregeling, maar de man, die den Generaal het beste had kunnen voorlichten omtrent de wijze, waarop die scheepvaartregeling diende ingericht te zijn en waarom eene andere regeling niet deugde, was heengegaan. Ware hij gebleven en had hij gestreden met zijne argumenten tegen de blokkade en voor de scheepvaartregeling, zeker zou dan geene contrôle op handel en scheepvaart getroffen zijn, zooals thans het geval is geweest, vermoedelijk ook geen tweeslachtige regeling, en de mogelijkheid, dat de Regeering zich had laten overtuigen van de deugdelijkheid van Scherer's beginselen, ware veel grooter geweest dan thans, nu de Generaal en Scherer's collega van Langen zich moesten behelpen met Scherer's nota's". — Must one not now agree, that Scherer's immediate departure on request was a pity? Until the end of 1885 the pen was fought with from Atjeh for the preservation of the shipping-regulation, but the man who could best have advised the General on the way in which that shipping-regulation should be arranged, and why another arrangement was no good, had departed. Had he stayed and fought with his arguments against the blockade and for the shipping-regulation, then certainly no control on trade and shipping would have been adopted as has now been the case, probably also no ambiguous arrangement, and the possibility that the Government would have been convinced of the soundness of Scherer's principles would have been much greater than now, when the General and Scherer's colleague van Langen had to make do with Scherer's notes.

Demmeni died at the end of [[1886]], and his death opened the governorship to [[Van Teijn|Kolonel van Teijn]]: "Zoo brak einde 1886 het tijdperk aan, waarin door den dood van den Generaal Demmeni, de Kolonel van Teijn het bestuur in handen kreeg, met de opdracht om consequent door te gaan in de aangegeven richting, en met het beginsel van herstel van het sultanaat tot basis" — So at the end of 1886 the period began, in which, through the death of General Demmeni, Colonel van Teijn took the administration into his hands, with the charge to continue consistently in the indicated direction, and with the principle of restoration of the sultanate as basis. Van Teijn would, in his own governorship ([[1886]]–[[1891]]), reverse the policies Demmeni had compromised on — restoring the [[sluiting]] of the Noord- en Westkust, pioneering the *actieve verdediging* of the [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]] linie, and becoming the second hero of Van Heutsz's narrative after [[Van der Heijden]].

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Demmeni** was the Dutch general who recalled [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]], the powerful head of the [[VII Moekims Baid|VII Moekims]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], from the banishment imposed upon him by [[General van der Heijden]], and restored him to honour on account of his influence.

## The rehabilitation of Toekoe Moeda Baid

[[G. B. Hooijer]] recorded that Toekoe Moeda Baid, *het machtige hoofd der VII Moekims*, had been banished by General van der Heijden for faithlessness (*wegens trouweloosheid*), and was *ter wille van zijn invloed door Generaal Demmeni teruggehaald en in eere hersteld* — brought back and rehabilitated by General Demmeni for the sake of his influence.

Hooijer's judgment on the outcome was sceptical. The proximity of Baid's territory to the Dutch post at [[Anagaloeng]] kept the officers in suspense and the sentries awake, for now and then well-founded suspicions arose that Van der Heijden had not judged the chief so wrongly; one of the neighbouring *[[Blokhuis|blokhuizen]]* garrisoned by Baid's people fell or was played into the hands of the *[[Moslemin]]* only hours after a Dutch officer's inspection had found everything in order.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**H. Demmeni** was a Dutch infantry colonel who commanded the fruitless military expedition to [[Teunom]] in the [[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Nisero affair]] of January [[1884]] and who, when the experiment of separate civil government ended, was appointed Civil and Military Governor of [[Acheh|Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden]], accepting the administration on [[13 September]] [[1884]].

### The Teunom expedition, January 1884

With the consent of the Government, which wished to avoid every reproach that not everything had been done or suffered to promote the liberation of the crew of the stranded English steamer *Nisero*, a military expedition was equipped which steamed from [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]] to Teunom on [[4 January]] [[1884]] under the command of Colonel H. Demmeni, while the English warship *Pegasus* sailed up to Teunom on the English side. When the ultimatum to the [[Radja of Teunom]] remained unanswered, hostilities were opened and an exemplary chastisement of the Radja followed: the coast was kept closed to a wide distance for all import and export, the fisheries were impeded, and Dutch warships fired on everything within their reach. The Radja remained indifferent, however, and the prisoners were not delivered up: the captives were freed only on [[10 September]] [[1884]], after renewed negotiations conducted by Governor [[P. F. Laging Tobias]] together with [[Mr. Maxwell]] of the [[Singapore]] colonial council.

### Civil and Military Governor, 1884

After more than three years of civil government the Government understood that a reserved and very forceful attitude toward the Atjehnese left no place for a separate civil administration, and that military and civil authority had again to be united in one hand. Governor Laging Tobias was honourably relieved, and Colonel of Infantry H. Demmeni was appointed Civil and Military Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies, accepting the administration on 13 September 1884. Thus ended, in Langhout's phrase, "the for our army inglorious and for trade most unfavourable period of the first civil government in Atjeh". Demmeni's name thereafter attaches to the carrying out of the [[Dutch Policy of 'Concentration'|Concentration]] — the withdrawal from outlying posts, the redrawing of the line of posts around [[Koeta Radja]], and the strict blockade of a great part of the Atjehnese coast.

## Account from *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*

Appointed in [[August]] [[1884]] to restore unified civiel- en militair bestuur. He and his successors were tied down in [[Groot-Atjeh]] at the [[Concentrated Line|concentrated line]] until [[1893]], taking a passive wait-and-see stance. His tenure saw a reduction of administrative divisions from three afdeelingen to two and abolition of controleursposten.

*Source: J. Jongejans, Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu (Baarn: Hollandia Drukkerij N.V., c. 1938), pp. 324.*

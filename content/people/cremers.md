**Cremers** was a member of the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]] who spoke during the [[Tweede Kamer debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|debate on Atjeh war origins]] in April [[1874]], defending the war by characterising [[Acheh|Atjeh]] as a "barbarian robber-state" and arguing that alongside the international-law perspective there was a colonial-political interest that justified the extension of Dutch authority. Cremers later served on the [[Comité-général|Commission]] that decided to publish the secret proceedings without modification in [[1881]], alongside [[Van Kerkwijk|Van Kerkwijk]], [[Insinger|Insinger]], [[Schimmelpenninck van der Oije|Schimmelpenninck van der Oije]], and [[Des Amorie van der Hoeven|Des Amorie van der Hoeven]].

## The defence of the war

Cremers defended the [[Declaration of war on Atjeh (1873)|war with Atjeh]] by characterising the Atjeh state as barbarian and predatory. He argued that alongside the perspective of international law, there existed a colonial-political interest that justified extending Dutch authority. Cremers pointed out that [[Van Zuylen van Nyevelt|Van Zuylen]] himself had previously acknowledged that Atjeh stood "min of meer onder onze suzereiniteit" — more or less under Dutch suzerainty — undermining Van Zuylen's argument that Atjeh was a fully independent state whose rights had been violated.

## The Commission on publication (1881)

The Commission, consisting of Cremers, [[Van Kerkwijk|Van Kerkwijk]], [[Insinger|Insinger]], [[Schimmelpenninck van der Oije|Schimmelpenninck van der Oije]], and [[Des Amorie van der Hoeven|Des Amorie van der Hoeven]], unanimously decided to publish the speeches without any modification, noting that even minor changes would deprive the publication of all value. The Commission overruled objections to particular expressions, arguing that the force of the language had been weakened both by the passage of time and by the fact that the expressions had been used in *comité-général*.

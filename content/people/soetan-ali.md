**Soetan Ali** was a Malay and the former clerk/secretary of [[Teuku Umar]] who, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, emerges as one of the most valuable Dutch intelligence assets of the first semester of [[1896]] and the named authorised intermediary in Umar's [[Teuku Umar's third letter to the Governor of Atjeh (23 April 1896)|third letter to the Governor of Atjeh of 23 April]] [[1896]]. Kruisheer credits Ali with providing "*zeer juiste inlichtingen*" (very accurate information) about Umar's fortifications, weapon stores, troop strength and the names of his *panglima's* — a level of detail which suggests that he had access to the inner workings of Umar's command structure during and after the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]]. His Malay identity is notable: the Malay community in [[Aceh]] occupied a distinct commercial and administrative role, and the choice of a Malay *krani* as Umar's secretary reflects the multilingual, multiethnic character of the Achehnese military-administrative apparatus which the Dutch *bindingspolitiek* had created.

## Role as Umar's Krani and Defection to the Dutch

As Umar's *krani*, Soetan Ali would have been responsible for the drafting and management of Umar's correspondence — including the three letters to the [[Governor of Atjeh]] of [[30 March]], [[12 April]] and [[23 April]] [[1896]] which form Bijlagen II, IV and V of *ATJEH '96*. The former prefix indicates that Ali had left Umar's service by the time he provided intelligence to the Dutch, and the level of detail he was able to provide — *versterkingen*, weapon stores, troop strength, *panglima's* names — suggests that his defection occurred after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]], in the course of which Umar's house at [[Lam-Pisang]] was captured and his correspondence seized on [[24 May]] [[1896]]. The combination of his former role (he would have known the contents of the correspondence he had drafted) and the detail of his intelligence (which included current order-of-battle information) suggests that Ali had either maintained contact with Umar's court after his own departure or had been debriefed by the Dutch using the captured correspondence as a verification check. Kruisheer does not specify the date of Ali's intelligence provision, but its inclusion in a discussion of the post-[[23 May]] [[1896|23 May]] [[1896]] situation places it in late May or early June [[1896]].

## Named Intermediary in Umar's Third Letter of 23 April 1896

The most consequential documentary reference to Soetan Ali in *ATJEH '96* is his naming as Umar's authorised intermediary in the [[Teuku Umar's third letter to the Governor of Atjeh (23 April 1896)|third letter of 23 April]] [[1896]] (Bijlage V). The letter, dated [[1 Dsoelkaidah]] 1313 from Lampisang and addressed to the *wd. Governor* at Koeta-Radja, "references Soetan Ali as his authorized intermediary" — that is, Umar explicitly designated Ali as the channel through which any further negotiations with the Dutch were to be conducted. The choice of Ali as intermediary is itself significant: the third letter demands a "kruis" (decoration) promised by [[C. Deykerhoff|Deijkerhoff]], a signed declaration by the Queen of the Netherlands, the [[Governor-General]] at [[Batavia]], the Governor of Atjeh and "the European princes via their consuls", the ƒ150,000 monthly demand, and the promise to "handle war affairs and hand the country over to the Kompeni when war ends". These demands were such that no Dutch negotiator could accept them, and the designation of Ali as intermediary may therefore have been intended primarily as a procedural move — a way of demonstrating Umar's continuing willingness to negotiate while ensuring that no actual negotiation would occur. The letter was signed by [[Udo de Haes|De Secretaris Udo de Haes]] ("*for a true copy*"), and Ali's name appears in the footnote which Kruisheer appends to the letter text.

## Significance

Soetan Ali's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his dual role as the drafter of Umar's correspondence and the Dutch intelligence asset on Umar's order of battle — a combination which gave him an unusually comprehensive view of the Achehnese command structure and which made his intelligence particularly valuable to the Dutch. His Malay identity also illustrates the multiethnic composition of the Achehnese military-administrative apparatus, and his designation as Umar's authorised intermediary in the [[Teuku Umar's third letter to the Governor of Atjeh (23 April 1896)|third letter of 23 April]] [[1896]] gave him a formal diplomatic status which no other Achehnese figure in the balso possesses.

**Generaal Vetter** served as government commissioner (*regeringscommissaris*) to [[Atjeh]] in the immediate aftermath of the [[1896]] defection of [[T. Oema]]. His temporary appointment—with three battalions of infantry as reinforcement—represented the first direct military response to the collapse of the alliance policy, and his actions laid the groundwork for the permanent strategic shift implemented by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in [[1898]].

## The 1896 commission

Following the defection of [[Toekoe Djohan]] (the defector [[T. Oema]]) and the failure of [[Deykerhoff]]'s alliance strategy, the supreme authority sent [[Generaal Vetter]] to [[Atjeh]] as government commissioner. He arrived with three battalions of infantry as temporary reinforcement—a clear signal that direct military action was now preferred over proxy alliances.

[[Vetter]]'s immediate task was to defeat [[Toekoe Djohan]] and drive him and his bands out of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. This he accomplished. Through continued military action, the fallen-away [[XXVI Moekims]] were again cleansed of enemies, and part of the [[XXII Moekims]] was also treated—though these could not be entirely subdued.

## The significance of the young Polim's guardianship

During [[Vetter]]'s administration, the young [[Panglima Polim]] remained under guardianship, yet affairs in the [[XXII Moekims]] continued on the old footing—precisely demonstrating the special significance of the [[Polim]] family. Unlike other chiefs—such as [[Habib Abdoer Rahman]], [[Teungkoe di Tiro]], or [[Toekoe Oemar]] himself—who disappeared when their persons left the stage, the family of the [[Polims]] persisted. "Polim est mort, vive Polim!" made [[Panglima Polim]] effectively immortal, and only the submission of the head of the family could end the struggle.

Once [[Vetter]] had taught the defector a sufficient lesson to discourage him from venturing into [[Groot-Atjeh]] for the foreseeable future, he handed over his task to the newly appointed civil and military governor, [[Generaal De Moulin]] ([[June 1895]]). The brief but decisive intervention of [[Vetter]] confirmed that military action—rather than negotiated alliance—was the only viable path to pacification.

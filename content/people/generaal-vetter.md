**Generaal Vetter** was the Dutch army commander (*legercommandant*) in the Netherlands Indies during the early phase of [[J. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]]'s governorship of [[Gouvernement Atjeh|Atjeh]] ([[1896]]–[[1897]]). Vetter had declared [[Toekoe Oemar|Teukoe Oemar]]'s resistance "geheel en al gebroken" (entirely broken) and considered his task fulfilled — a judgment that subsequent events repeatedly disproved and that *De Locomotief* characterised as having been made "gelukkig voor den goeden naam onzer strategische bekwaamheid" (fortunately for the good name of our strategic competence) only because Vetter had already departed the scene.

## The premature declaration of victory

*De Locomotief* of [[16 January]] [[1897]] wrote of Vetter's assessment: "Toekoe Oemar wiens tegenstand geheel en al gebroken was, waarmede generaal Vetter zijn taak vervuld achtte, geeft ons te Lhong de handen vol werk, kost ons weder heel wat menschenlevens en schatten aan geld." The editorial continued that "Ons succes in Atjeh, verkregen nadat generaal Vetter op zijn manier aan den gang geweest en, gelukkig voor den goeden naam onzer strategische bekwaamheid, daarginder van het tooneel verdwenen was — ons succes begint te gelijken op een kaars, waarvan het beste deel is weggebrand."

## Relationship with Van Heutsz

[[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] wrote to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] on [[25 September]] [[1897]] that [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] owed it to [[J. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]] and Vetter that his "groote karaktergebreken" (great character defects) did not count against his good qualities as troop commander, for both of them had refrained from reporting those defects. Vetter, like Van Vliet, had protected Van Heutsz's career from the consequences of his personality.

## Departure and its consequences

Vetter's departure as army commander and the arrival of [[H.N.A. Swart|general Swart]] marked a turning point for [[J. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]]'s position. Van Vliet reportedly felt less secure after Vetter left, because the new commander's eyes "anders bleken te zien en te waardeeren" — saw and evaluated differently. For Van Heutsz, the change was a *koers-avans* (forward step) of importance.

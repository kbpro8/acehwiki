**Van Goltstein** was a Dutch politician who served as Minister of Colonies in the ministry Heemskerk in the mid-[[1870s]], known in the source work principally for his telegram of [[4 November]] [[4 November]] [[1874]] to the Governor-General [[J. Loudon|Loudon]], by which the official journey of the general secretary [[H. D. Levyssohn Norman|Levyssohn Norman]] through Java and Madura was stopped — an intervention that directly led to the [[Conversiequaestie|conversiequaestie]] and to the fall of Loudon, who requested honourable discharge by Royal Decree of [[17 December]] [[17 December]] [[1874]].

## The Telegram of 4 November 1874

The reports received in the Netherlands about the conversions carried out by Levyssohn Norman gave Van Goltstein occasion to send a telegram on [[4 November]] [[4 November]] [[1874]] whereby the Governor-General was requested to cause the official journey of the general secretary to be stopped. "In writing the viceroy was invited to guard against all pressure for conversion on the part of European officials, and to issue no general ordonnantie, except after consultation with the Opperbestuur, in the spirit of art. 20 of the [[Regeeringsreglement|Regeeringsreglement]]. Such consultation seemed to be desired both because of the great interest involved in the matter, and because it had appeared that in Indië very divergent opinions existed about the proposed regulation."

The Governor-General complied with the instruction given him, but at the same time requested honourable discharge, which was granted him by Royal Decree of [[17 December]] [[17 December]] [[1874]].

## Significance

Van Goltstein's intervention in the [[Conversiequaestie|conversiequaestie]] marks a key moment in the development of the agrarian policy of the Netherlands-Indian government. His insistence on consultation with the Opperbestuur before the issue of a general ordonnantie, and on the avoidance of pressure for conversion by European officials, set the policy that would be followed by the Indies government for the next decades: that the conversion of communal into individual land ownership should not be forced by the administration, but should be left to the spontaneous development of the native population.

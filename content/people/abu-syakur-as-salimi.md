**Abū Shakūr as-Sālimī** was a Shāfiʿite theologian of Persia who lived in the second half of the fifth century of the Hegira (eleventh century C.E.). He is the author of the [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*at-Tamhīd fī bayān at-tawḥīd*]] ("The Preparation concerning the Exposition of the Divine Unity"), a Shāfiʿite manual of *kalām* and of the refutation of sectarian deviation. The *Tamhīd* is identified by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] as the principal source of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]]: more than a quarter of the text of the *Tibjān* is translated literally from the *Tamhīd*, especially from its last chapter, on orthodoxy and the rejection of innovation (*bidʿa*).

## The *Tamhīd*

The [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*Tamhīd*]] is a Persian treatise on the divine unity and on the refutation of the sectarian deviations of Islam. Its last chapter, *Fīʾs-sunna waʾl-jamāʿa waʾr-radd ʿalāʾl-bidʿa* ("On the Sunna and the Community and the Refutation of Innovation"), supplies the material from which Raniri, working more than five centuries later in [[Acheh]], composed the bulk of his survey of the pre-Muslim religions and the sects of Islam. The work is listed by Brockelmann in *GAL* I, 419; the printed edition of Cairo 1338 H was not available to Voorhoeve, who cited the work from the Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 5664.

## Source of the *Tibjān*

[[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]]'s identification of the *Tamhīd* as the principal source of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] is the most original contribution of his [[1955]] edition. More than a quarter of the text of the *Tibjān* is translated literally from the *Tamhīd* — a fact that places the *Tibjān*, in its first part, as a Malay translation of an eleventh-century Persian Shāfiʿite treatise. The Leiden manuscript of the *Tamhīd* preserves the very errors of Raniri's own exemplar — including the strange error "Kubtiyya" (Copts) for *al-Muʿattila* (the sect that denies all divine attributes) and a lacuna that caused Raniri to announce four groups of the Tanāsuḥiyya while treating only three. Voorhoeve concludes that it was "a very happy accident" that the Leiden manuscript of the *Tamhīd* preserves a text practically identical with that of Raniri's own exemplar.

## Significance

Abū Shakūr as-Sālimī is, through his [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*Tamhīd*]], the principal source of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri. His eleventh-century Persian treatise on the divine unity and the refutation of innovation, transmitted through a Banten manuscript of the early modern period and translated into Malay in [[Acheh]] in the seventeenth century by a Gujarati shaikh of the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]], is one of the most remarkable textual conjunctions in the history of Malay religious literature. The identification of the *Tamhīd* as the principal source of the *Tibjān* establishes the textual genealogy of Raniri's Malay encyclopaedia of religions and sects, and places the *Tibjān* — despite its Achehnese provenance — within the scholastic tradition of Persian Shāfiʿism.

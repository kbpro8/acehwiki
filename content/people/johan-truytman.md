**Johan Truytman** was a Dutch envoy of the [[Dutch East India Company]] (VOC) who, in [[1649]], successfully concluded a treaty with [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam]], the "Sultane of Aceh," that gave the VOC extensive trade advantages across the entire West Coast of Sumatra, including the exclusion of all other European and native traders (except Acehnese) and fixed pepper prices.

## Mission to Aceh

Truytman was sent by the High Government in Batavia at a time when relations with Aceh had become strained after the VOC's conquest of [[Malakka]] in 1641. Through tactful diplomacy, he managed to improve relations and secure a comprehensive agreement that built on the earlier [[Dutch–Aceh Treaty of 1641|treaty of 1641]]. His negotiations also paved the way for the [[Treaty of 1650 on Perak Tin|1650 agreement on Perak tin]].

## Significance

Truytman's treaty represented the peak of VOC-Aceh diplomacy in the mid-17th century. It gave the Company a near-monopoly over pepper and tin trades on Sumatra's West Coast and demonstrated the Company's ability to negotiate favourable terms with a major regional power. His success is often cited as an example of VOC diplomacy at its most effective.

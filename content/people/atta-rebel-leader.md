**Atta** — also recorded as ***Opperhoofd* Atta** or ***Bendehoofd* Atta** (chief Atta or rebel-band-head Atta) — was the *opperhoofd* (chief) of a rebel band that operated from the **Corentij** district of [[Berbice (colony)|Berbice]] and later from the [[Wikkie creek|Wikkie-kreek]] region during the Dutch colonial expeditions to [[Berbice (colony)|Berbice]] in the mid-[[1760s]]. Dutch marine journals record that Atta "*had zich met een talrijke negerbende neergezet op de plantage 'Goed land en Goed Fortuyn' nabij Hardenbroek*" ("had established himself with a numerous negro-band on the plantation 'Good Land and Good Fortune' near Hardenbroek"), and that his forces — composed largely of **Negers** (enslaved Africans) — were a principal target of Dutch punitive raids. After a clash with [[Kapitein Perrin de Salleneuve|Captain Perrin de Salleneuve]] in [[1764]], Atta withdrew to the Wikkie-kreek, later regrouping with an estimated 1200–1500 men.

## Context and Early Activity

Atta's followers had earlier settled along the [[Wimbia-kreek|Wimbia-kreek]] but returned to the plantation [[JJhannaa]] seeking Christian employment — a detail indicating a fluid relationship between African slave populations and the indigenous rebel movement in the Berbice interior. The rebel band's base in the [[Corentij (region)|Corentij district]] placed them at the frontier of Dutch colonial expansion in the Berbice hinterland. According to the [[Journal of Haringman]], Atta also gathered a large band of rebels near the [[Markey plantation|Markey plantation]] and the forested area of [[Hardenbrock plantation|Hardenbrock]] at the entrance of the [[Wikkie creek|Wikkie-kreek]] — positions that would feature in the Dutch riverine campaign of December.

## The 18 January 1764 Expedition

On [[18 January]] [[1764]] Colonel [[Kolonel De Salve|Colonel De Salve]] dispatched Captain [[Perrin de Salleneuve|Captain Perrin de Salleneuve]] with a detachment of thirty-three men to attack Atta's position. The expedition failed: "*Perrin was een dapper officier, echter onervaren in de vechtwijze in de jungle*" ("Perrin was a brave officer, but inexperienced in the manner of fighting in the jungle"), resulting in Perrin's death and the capture of several of his men. Following the defeat, Atta's band continued to harass Dutch posts, prompting further reinforcements and a shift in Dutch strategy. Colonel De Salve opted for containment rather than outright extermination, offering temporary leniency in exchange for surrender.

## Skirmishes under Douglas and De Brauw

Atta's forces engaged in intermittent skirmishes with troops under [[Luitenant‑Colonel Douglas|Lieutenant-Colonel Douglas]] and [[Maajoor De Brauw|Major De Brauw]]. On [[21 March]] [[1764]] a coordinated Dutch offensive was planned to crush his remaining strongholds, but the governor ultimately permitted a more conciliatory approach, hoping to induce voluntary submission. The rebel leader's ability to regroup after the loss of Perrin's detachment and to field a sizable force of up to fifteen hundred men demonstrates both the resilience of the rebellion and the strategic importance the Dutch placed on neutralising his band. The offering of leniency by Colonel De Salve reflects a pragmatic Dutch policy of containment when outright military defeat proved costly.

## The December Riverine Campaign

The December Dutch riverine campaign against Atta's positions — recorded in the [[Journal of Haringman]] — saw Atta's forces attempt to block the Dutch retreat from the [[Canje area|Canje area]] and to ambush the landing parties at the [[Wikkie creek|Wikkie-kreek]]. The rebels were reported to have forced many enslaved Africans to join their ranks, including women and children. The [[Markey plantation|Markey plantation]] remained standing on [[26 December]] after the Dutch expedition, and became a gathering point for rebel forces under Atta's leadership and for enslaved Africans who had fled the *Sociëteitsplantages* (Society's plantations).

## Significance

Atta's resistance exemplifies the involvement of rebel actors in broader anti-colonial struggles that drew together enslaved African populations and indigenous resistance in the Berbice interior, highlighting the trans-oceanic dimensions of eighteenth-century resistance to Dutch imperial power. His leadership also illustrates the interplay between African slave populations and indigenous rebel movements in the Berbice hinterland, underscoring the complex social fabric that Dutch authorities had to confront. The repeated Dutch expeditions against him — from the failed January assault under Perrin de Salleneuve, through the March offensive that was ultimately suspended in favour of negotiation, to the December riverine campaign on the Wikkie-kreek — together document the difficulty the Dutch faced in suppressing a determined interior rebellion, even after the main phase of the [[Berbice slave rebellion|Berbice slave rebellion of 1763]] had been brought to an end.

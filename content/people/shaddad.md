**Shaddad (legendary king)** is a figure from pre‑Islamic Arabian legend cited in the Malay translation of the *Burda* as the builder of the opulent city *Iram* (the “columns of the sea”). The poem recounts Shaddad’s hubris and subsequent divine destruction.

## Legendary Narrative

According to Arab tradition, King Shaddad constructed the magnificent city of *Iram* to rival Paradise, only to be punished by God for his arrogance. The story is referenced in the Qur’an (e.g., [[Qur’an 89:5‑6]]) and in later Islamic literature.

## Role in Acehnese Poetry

In the Acehnese version of the *Burda*, Shaddad’s tale functions as a cautionary example contrasting the humility of the Prophet [[Muhammad]] with the pride of earlier rulers. Its inclusion demonstrates the transmission of pan‑Islamic legends into Acehnese religious poetry.

**Lavino** was the Dutch political agent (*politieke agent*) at [[Penang]] during the [[Aceh War]], the colonial government's listening post opposite the Atjehnese community on that island, and the source of several of the most important pieces of intelligence of [[1876]].

## The year 1876

About mid-March [[1876]] Lavino reported to the civil and military commander in Atjeh that the Arab [[Habib Abdoerrachman el Zahir|Habib Abdoer Rachman ben Zahir]] — lately a member of the notorious Council of Eight (*Raad van achten*) at Penang — had escaped the island despite all the precautions of the British police and of the agent himself, disguised as a sailor aboard a coasting schooner, and had landed on the north coast of Sumatra. The warning, confirmed by the [[Radja of Edi besar]], let the Dutch command follow the Habib's triumphal journey to the focus of the resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

In July [[1876]] reports of the same zealous agent at Penang contributed, with the statements of the Pidir head [[Radja Mangkoeta]] and of [[Toekoe Imam of Gigiëng]], to the certainty that [[Toengkoe Bintara Kamangan]], prince of [[Gigiëng]], had secretly gone over to the war party — the conviction behind the [[Cancellation of friendship with Gigiëng (19 August 1876)|breach of 19 August]].

His reports are preserved by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]).

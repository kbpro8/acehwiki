**Redemptus a Cruce** was a Portuguese Carmelite lay brother who was martyred together with **[[Dionysius a Nativitate]]** in **[[Aceh]]** in [[1638]]. Their execution at the hands of the Sultan of Aceh became a celebrated episode in Catholic missionary history and was extensively covered in the Jesuit magazine **[[St. Claverbond]]** during the **[[Aceh War]]**.

## Martyrdom

Redemptus accompanied Dionysius on a diplomatic mission to Aceh. When the mission failed, both were condemned to death. Their steadfast faith impressed even their executioners, and their deaths were later recorded in official martyrologies. The *St. Claverbond* register lists multiple references to Redemptus, including a biography series and a platen (illustration) of his martyrdom.

## Commemoration in the Aceh Mission

The Jesuit missionaries in Aceh, led by **[[H. C. Verbraak S.J.]]**, kept the memory of these martyrs alive. Verbraak's series on "Christenvervolgingen" ([[1900]]) explicitly linked the contemporary war to the earlier persecutions. A separate article by W. v. Nieuwenhoff, "De Martelaren van Atjeh" ([[1900]]–[[1901]]), further popularised their story.

## Significance

Redemptus and Dionysius were beatified by the Catholic Church, and their cult was promoted in the Dutch East Indies as a source of spiritual encouragement for soldiers and missionaries. Their story served to legitimise the Dutch presence in Aceh as a continuation of the Christian mission in the region.

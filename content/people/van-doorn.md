**Van Doorn** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen]]

Van Daalen later became army commander, and in that capacity issued a decree that, on grounds of a shortage of infantry officers, no civil employments might any longer be entrusted to them and that those so charged must be relieved forthwith — costing Swart the help of [[Van Doorn]] and [[Grashuis]]. Scheepens wrote to Schmidt: "How is that to be managed, my dear fellow? Meanwhile the Governor has begun the fight against that provision." Van Daalen also refused for a time to allow [[H. J. Schmidt]] to be reposted to Atjeh: between him and Swart, then Major-General, matters had come almost to "a painful war, for which Van Daalen, who hated Swart, certainly bears the guilt." The friction with the army command ended only when Swart became lieutenant-general.

### From [[Van Militair naar Civiel Bestuur]]

A further obstacle came from the army command. Scheepens's letter of [[26 December]] [[1909]] recorded: "Not long ago feelings were stirred here by a decree of the army commander ([[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]]). Owing to a shortage of officers in the Infantry, no further civil employments might be entrusted to them, and those already so charged had to be relieved of them forthwith. I thereby lose the help of [[Van Doorn]] and of [[Grashuis]]. How is that to be managed, my dear fellow? Meanwhile the Governor has begun the fight against that provision and must have laid the matter before the Governor-General. I heartily hope that it will give occasion for the earlier introduction of wholly civil administration."

**Godefroi** was a member of the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]] from Gouda who spoke during the [[Parliamentary debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|debate on Atjeh war origins]] in April [[1874]], defending the war's legitimacy on grounds of colonial-political necessity. Godefroi argued that the relationship with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] could not be judged by European standards of international law, that the [[Sumatra-tractaat (1871)|Sumatra-tractaat of 1871]] contained the germ of a possible war, and that Atjeh's attempts to secure foreign protectorate constituted a *casus belli*.

## The colonial-political defence

Godefroi argued that the [[Sumatra-tractaat (1871)|Sumatra-tractaat]] contained the germ of a possible war: by giving the Netherlands freedom to act in Atjeh, the treaty simultaneously created the obligation to prevent any foreign power from establishing itself there. Atjeh's attempts to secure foreign protectorate — from America and Italy — were therefore in direct conflict with the purpose of the treaty, and constituted a *casus belli*. "The fact alone that those attempts were made is enough to characterise a relationship of Atjeh toward us that we could not and must not tolerate."

## The treaty argument

Godefroi argued that Atjeh was not fully independent, because the [[Tractaat van 1824 (Anglo-Dutch)|tractaat of 1824]] gave the Netherlands police authority in Atjeh's waters — a sovereign right that, if Atjeh were a European state, would belong to Atjeh itself. He noted that even those who now opposed the war had voted for the [[Sumatra-tractaat (1871)|Sumatra-tractaat]], knowing that it contained the possibility of war.

## The Singapore incident

During [[Gericke van Herwijnen|Minister Gericke van Herwijnen]]'s response, the Minister cited the [[Singapore incident (1873)|events at Singapore]] as evidence that the war was justified, and noted that the same American consul described as "a fool" by critics still held his position. The Minister further revealed that France had also received an offer of protectorate from Atjeh, which had been declined — a fact not previously known to the Chamber.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

In the secret sessions of the Tweede Kamer on the origin of the Atjeh war the member Godefroi declared, on [[16 April]] [[1874]]: "Ik laat daarbij ter zijde den treurigen indruk, die de overgelegde stukken op mij gemaakt hebben, ten aanzien van de verwaarloozing onzer middelen van defensie in Indië, want op dezen Minister van Koloniën (Fransen van de Putte) rust de schuld daarvan niet" ("I leave aside the sad impression which the documents presented made on me concerning the neglect of our means of defence in the Indies, for on this Minister of Colonies the guilt of this does not rest") — a grant that the neighbouring member [[C. van Nispen van Sevenaar]] refused to make, with his answer that by earlier neglect of duty the government had itself put itself out of condition to wage this war, that its rulers knew they had not the least notion of the enemy they went to fight, and yet made war (see [[Stieltjes]] on the same day).

**Pang Tjoet Bén** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[T. Dagang]]

T. Dagang recurs repeatedly in [[Swart's letters to Schmidt (1909-1913)|Swart's private letters to Schmidt]]. In the letter of [[26 December]] [[1909]] Swart wrote: "During your stay at Tangsé you will of course do everything to put an end to the existence of T. Dagang and his war-leader [[Pang Tjoet Bén]]. Once T. Dagang has fallen, [[Keutjhi' Bén Man]] will come back of his own accord, while [[Keutjhi' Maha]] and [[Pang Brahim]] must then simply be cleared up by the garrison of Tangsé. So once more: take T. Dagang and Pang Tjoet Bén specially upon your own account."

### From [[Tangsé]]

In [[1910]] Schmidt at Tangsé and [[J. J. Boreel|Boreel]] at [[Lam Meulò]] became the pace-setters in Pidië. Swart's letter of [[26 December]] [[1909]] instructed him: "During your stay at Tangsé you will of course do everything to put an end to the existence of [[T. Dagang]] and his *panglima* [[Pang Tjoet Bén]]. Once T. Dagang has fallen, [[Keutjhi' Bén Man]] will come back of his own accord, while [[Keutjhi' Maha]] and [[Pang Brahim]] must then simply be cleared up by the garrison of Tangsé." T. Dagang eluded Schmidt for years and finally submitted in [[1913]], when Schmidt was on leave in Holland: "Tangsé was clean."

### From [[Swart's letters to Schmidt (1909-1913)]]

They are, du Croo wrote, "characteristic both of the manner in which Swart corresponded privately with his most trusted collaborators and of certain frictions with the army command, which in fact ended only when Swart became lieutenant-general." The letter of [[26 December]] [[1909]] promises [[T. Radja Moeda Seudagan]] six breech-loaders when his *koeta* is ready and an advance of two or three hundred guilders, and instructs Schmidt to end the existence of [[T. Dagang]] and his *panglima* [[Pang Tjoet Bén]] at [[Tangsé]], then to go to [[Geumpang]] against [[Keudjroeën Leupoe]] and his sons. That of [[26 September]] [[1911]] reports the interview with the new Governor-General [[Alexander Willem Frederik Idenburg|Idenburg]] at [[Sabang]] on [[14 December]], the farewell to [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] on [[22 December]], the death of [[Tgk. Ma'at]] and the instruction to [[Scheurleer]] concerning [[Pang Bintang]], and offers Schmidt [[Tgk. di Barat]] as his next object. That of [[9 December]] [[1911]] concerns his own promotion. Du Croo defended the practice by citing Idenburg's letter to van Heutsz of [[15 September]] [[1908]]: "the advantage of private correspondence is that it is personal and that one can say exactly what one thinks about a matter."

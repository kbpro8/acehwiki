**Marks** was a member of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* in [[Atjeh]], one of the non-commissioned members of the corps named by Colonel [[L. A. Snell]] in the [[1930]] jubilee number among “de eenvoudigen van ziel” — the simple of soul — who ought to be rescued from oblivion.

## Snell's roll of the simple of soul

Interviewed for the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]*, Colonel [[L. A. Snell]] first named the great pioneers of the corps — [[G. J. W. C. Graafland|Graafland]], [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], [[H. Colijn|Colijn]], [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]], [[K. van der Maaten|Van der Maaten]] and above all [[J. B. van Heutsz]] — and then added: "Maar wanneer ik namen noem, dan dienen ook de eenvoudigen van ziel aan de vergetelheid te worden onttrokken" — but when I name names, then the simple of soul too ought to be rescued from oblivion.

The list he gave was: [[Marks]], [[F. Ch. Frank|Frank]], [[W. J. Mosselman|Mosselman]], [[J. C. Heijnen|Heynen]], [[Emond]], [[Rugebregt]], [[Salawono]], [[Kasir]], [[Wowor]], [[Nussy|Nussy]] and [[Kromo-di-Koro]] — "te veel om op te noemen, de dappersten onder de dapperen" — too many to name, the bravest among the brave.

Of such men Snell said that many had fallen, "doch hun geest blijft in het Korps voortleven; de herinnering aan hun daden blijft bewaard" — but their spirit lives on in the corps; the memory of their deeds is preserved.

The jubilee number gives no further particulars of this man beyond his name in that roll.

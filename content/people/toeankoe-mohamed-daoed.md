**Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed** — also written *Tuanku Muhammad Daud*, later styled *Sultan Muhammad Daud Syah* — was the youthful pretender to the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] whom his guardian and father-in-law [[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]] had declared of age, proclaimed, and hailed as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] in [[1885]]–[[1886]], and who served as the recognised Sultan of the Atjehese resistance from that date until his flight from [[Kemala|Kemala]] to [[Kwalla Batoe|Kwalla Batoe]] near [[Pedir|Pedir]] in [[December 1894|December]] [[1894]]. His position as the recognised Sultan — and the corresponding Dutch pursuit of his submission — was the principal political framework within which the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] was conducted.

## The Proclamation (1885–1886)

Under the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] (1884–1886), the Dutch retired into the concentrated *stelling* around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] to await patiently the enemy's submission. The enemy, abundantly supplied from the coasts, prosecuted the war in his own way. [[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]], guardian and father-in-law of the youthful pretender to the Sultanate, had the pretender — Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed — declared of age and proclaimed and hailed as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]]. The court was arranged according to ancient usage, and notification of the accession was given to various courts.

The Sultan's influence greatly increased. In all Atjeh and for all Atjehers he was — and remained, at the time of Kruijt's writing in [[1896]] — the lawful head of the country. His decision was invoked in many disputes. From his name the orders for the war were everywhere given.

## The August 1888 Visit to Groot-Atjeh

When in [[August 1888|August]] [[1888]] the Sultan suddenly appeared in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and met with [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]], [[Imam Longbattah|Imam Longbattah]], and [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]], [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] cherished a justified hope that it would now come to a conclusion. But after three weeks the Sultan departed again for [[Kemala|Kemala]], the chiefs, including those from within the line, brought him gifts, but the Dutch heard nothing from him. On that occasion the Sultan appointed [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] as *panglima* (*Emir El Bahr*) of the [[Westkust|West Coast]] as far as [[Singkel|Singkel]].

## The Dutch Pursuit of Submission (1887–1891)

The Dutch, under Van Teijn's governorship, were instructed to attempt to make contact with the Sultan and the [[Kemala-party|Kemala-party]], while avoiding the appearance that the Dutch sought it. The Dutch pursuit of the Sultan's submission — through emissaries, gifts, and solemn missions to Kemala — continued throughout Van Teijn's governorship, but led to nothing. The Atjehers, with their known acumen, continually put off some decision. More missions to Kemala, very solemn and imposing, followed — presumably the members of the missions received, or rather bought with Dutch money, Sultan's appointments — but for the Dutch all led to nothing.

## The Flight from Kemala (December 1894)

In [[December 1894|December]] [[1894]] [[Giegieng|Giegieng]] drove the Sultan from [[Kemala|Kemala]]. The Sultan fled to [[Kwalla Batoe|Kwalla Batoe]] near [[Pedir|Pedir]]. With Pedir the Dutch then sought in vain to get him back to Kemala — also for the *status quo*. The Dutch helped both parties, [[Pedir|Pedir]] and [[Giegieng|Giegieng]]; that was to maintain the balance between the two federations; the administration wanted to maintain the *status quo*.

## The 1896 Counter-Offensive

During the counter-offensive of [[March 1896|March]]–[[April 1896|April]] [[1896]] after the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]], "[[Vetter|Vetter]]'s troops" drove the enemy — who stood against the Dutch in thousands, including the Sultan and numerous bands from the coasts — back, defeated and scattered. The Sultan nearly fell into Dutch hands, but ultimately escaped.

## Significance

Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed is the recognised Sultan of the Atjehese resistance in the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. His proclamation at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] in [[1885]]–[[1886]] gave the resistance a political symbol and a central authority that sustained it through the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] and the governorships of [[Demmeni|Demmeni]], [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]], [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]], and [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]. The Dutch pursuit of his submission — pursued from [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] through Van Teijn — strengthened his authority in the eyes of the Atjehers and so contributed to the very resistance the Dutch sought to suppress. His flight from [[Kemala|Kemala]] in [[December 1894|December]] [[1894]] demonstrated the internal fissures of the Atjehese resistance, and his near-capture during the [[1896]] counter-offensive marked the high-water mark of the Dutch military response to the defection of Teuku Umar.

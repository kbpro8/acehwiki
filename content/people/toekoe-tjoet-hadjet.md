**Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet** — much more usually called **Toekoe Ajer Alang**, after the kampong of [[Ajer-Alang]] on the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]] — was an Achehnese chief, a brother of the great [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polim]], who offered his submission to the Dutch in the bivak at [[Selimoen]] on [[15 August]] [[1879]] and marched afterwards with the column of Majoor [[Majoor Lubeck|Lubeck]] to [[Indrapoeri]], as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## The submission at Selimoen

On the afternoon of the day on which the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] arrived before [[Selimoen]] — [[15 August]] [[1879]], the day after the voluntary [[Submission of Toekoe Moeda Daoed (August 1879)|submission of Toekoe Moeda Daud]] — "kwam ook Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet, veelal Toekoe Ajer Alang geheeten, een broeder van Panglima Polim, in het bivak zijne onderwerping aanbieden": Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet appeared in the bivak to offer his submission. Two days later, on [[17 August]], he accompanied the Lubeck column on its return march to Indrapoeri, together with Toekoe Moeda Daud and a following, the population everywhere receiving the troops in the most peaceful manner. Generaal [[General van der Heijden|van der Heijden]]'s [[Voorwoord of Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Van der Heijden, June 1889)|foreword]] to the book records the sequel: on [[20 August]] [[1879]] Toekoe Ajer-Alang came in formal submission to the Governor, and announced on the same date the coming of the then-current Sultan-pretendant — who, however, excused himself for the moment by reason of the approaching *poeasa* (fasting month), whereas the Selimoen chief Toekoe Moeda Daud completed his own submission that day.

## Significance

The submission of a brother of Panglima Polim marked the breaking of the most formidable chiefly connection of the war in [[Groot-Atjeh]]: Panglima Polim himself, with his son [[Radja Koelala|Radja Koeala]], the [[Imam of Longbatta]] and [[Massa-an]], had days before withdrawn from the council in the missigit of Selimoen to [[Reng-Reng]] in the VII Moekims of [[Pedir]] — but the entry of his brother into the Dutch peace distinguished the conquered household from the irreconcilable.

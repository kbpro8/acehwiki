**Pero de Faria** was the Portuguese governor of [[Malacca]] who took office in [[1539]] and who, almost immediately on arrival, dispatched [[Mendez Pinto]] on the two Sumatran missions that produced the European eyewitness account of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aru war of 1539]]. His governorship falls in the period when the [[Sultan of Atjeh|Atjehnese sultanate]] had begun to use its recent conversion to Islam as a pretext for raiding its neighbours and contesting Portuguese command of the [[Straits of Malacca]], and his responses — limited to munitions, an adventurous envoy, and diplomatic letters — set the pattern of Portuguese engagement with the Atjeh-Aru conflict.

## Dispatch of Pinto to Sumatra's west coast (1539)

Almost immediately on taking up the governorship, Faria was presented with an embassy from the "king of [[Batu]]" — the islands off [[Pasaman|Ophir]] — offering to recognise the King of Portugal as suzerain in exchange for protection against Atjeh and free trade for his people. The gift was accompanied by [[kapoer baroer|kamfer]] and [[kajoe garoe|kajoe garoe]]. Faria accepted the request, but had insufficient armed force to send; he supplied munitions and the young [[Mendez Pinto]], and added a *cargasoen* for trade. The ship was put under a Malay Muslim of Malacca — Pinto, the story goes, did not want the commission but dared not refuse. The mission, as De Roo de la Faille recounts, took Pinto up the Sumatran west coast past [[Pedir]] and into the country of the *bandahara* at [[Pasaman]], where a march against the Atjehers was organised but ultimately failed.

## Dispatch of Pinto to Aroe (October 1539)

When the king of [[Aroe]] in turn sought Faria's help against an Atjehnese fleet then bearing down upon him, Faria again dispatched Pinto, who embarked on [[5 October]] [[1539]]. The Queen of Aroe's later flight to Malacca after the fall of her kingdom was also addressed to Faria; "the governor received her with honour", De Roo de la Faille notes, "but armed aid he was not able to provide".

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Faria's significance is chiefly as the superior officer whose decisions made Pinto's eyewitness record possible, and as the official Portuguese recipient of the Queen of Aroe's appeal — a receipt that fixed, in European diplomatic memory, the fact of Atjeh's [[1539]] conquest of Aroe. His limited response also marks the limits of Portuguese power in the Strait at the moment when the Atjehnese sultanate was emerging as its most dangerous rival.

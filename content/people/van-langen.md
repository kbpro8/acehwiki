**Van Langen** — *Controleur* in the Dutch colonial civil administration — was the Dutch official who, as controleur of [[Langen|Langen]] (Atjèh) and on leave in the Netherlands in [[1887]], came aboard to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] with the products of his Atjèhse language studies — from which a grammar with reading-exercises and a dictionary were to grow (their publication Snouck Hurgronje would supervise in [[1888]]–[[1889]]) — and with the letters of [[Teungkoe di Tirò|Teungkoe di Tirò]] and other Atjèhse and Malay writings that Snouck Hurgronje found "*very instructive for our government*" and that, together with the *nota* of the Habib Abdoerrachman ez-Zahir, he would offer to advise on to the minister [[Keuchenius|Keuchenius]] on [[26 July]] [[26 July]] [[1888]].

## The 1887 Visit

In [[1887]] the controleur Van Langen, from Atjèh, came on leave to the Netherlands and visited Snouck Hurgronje. He brought with him the products of his Atjèhse language studies — from which a grammar with reading-exercises and a dictionary were to grow. Snouck Hurgronje supervised the publication of both in [[1888]]–[[1889]]. But what Van Langen brought along further: those were letters of a Teungkoe di Tirò, and more other, both Atjèhse and Malay, writings. Snouck Hurgronje found those writings very instructive for our government.

For the right understanding of the religious-political relations and of what in connection therewith was to be done and what to be left, those letters were of the greatest importance! On the advice of Mr. [[W. B. Bergsma|W. B. Bergsma]], Snouck Hurgronje addressed himself to the minister of colonies [[Keuchenius|Keuchenius]], with the offer to tell him what stood in those letters, as also in the *nota* of the [[Habib Abdul Rachman El-Zahir|Habib Abdoerrachman]]. That was on [[26 July]] [[26 July]] [[1888]].

## The Atjèh Language Publications

Van Langen's Atjèhse language studies, supervised for publication by Snouck Hurgronje in [[1888]]–[[1889]], comprised a grammar with reading-exercises and a dictionary of the Atjèhse language. The publications were the first systematic scholarly treatment of the Atjèhse language by Dutch scholars, and were among the principal linguistic fruits of Snouck Hurgronje's Atjèh interest in the period between his [[Snouck Hurgronje's Mekka journey (1884-1885)|Mekka journey]] of [[1884]]–[[1885]] and his [[Snouck Hurgronje's Atjeh mission (July 1891 – February 1892)|Atjèh residence]] of [[1891]]–[[1892]].

## Significance

Van Langen is the controleur of Langen whose [[1887]] visit to Snouck Hurgronje, with the products of his Atjèhse language studies and with the letters of Teungkoe di Tirò, was the immediate occasion of Snouck Hurgronje's offer to advise the minister Keuchenius on Atjèh — the offer that led, in the spring of [[1889]], to Snouck Hurgronje's despatch to Indië. Van Langen's Atjèhse language studies, published in [[1888]]–[[1889]] under Snouck Hurgronje's supervision, were the first systematic scholarly treatment of the Atjèhse language by Dutch scholars. The letters of Teungkoe di Tirò that Van Langen brought — Snouck Hurgronje found them "*of the greatest importance*" for the understanding of the religious-political relations in Atjèh.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — assistent-resident of Groot-Atjeh (1884)

In the [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] refers to *de assistent-resident van Groot-Atjeh van Langen* — in Van Heutsz's later references *Scherer's collega van Langen* — as the Dutch colonial official who, as *assistent-resident* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1884]], was the civil-administrative counterpart to [[Scherer|resident Scherer]] on the [[Noordkust|Noord-]] and [[Oostkust]] and the co-signatory of the *nota's* against the rigorous *blokkade* that, in the months following the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratiebesluit of 19 August 1884]], briefly dissuaded [[Demmeni|Generaal Demmeni]] from the full application of the [[Staatsblad No. 105 van 1884|Staatsblad No. 105 of 1884]] *rigoureuse blokkade*. His co-signature gave Scherer's protests the authority of the civil administration of Groot-Atjeh itself; without him, Scherer's *betoogingen* would have been the protests of a single *assistent-resident* on the coast; with him, they were the joint advice of the colony's two principal civil-administrative officials, and the General *wankelde* (wavered).

### The 1884 post: assistent-resident of Groot-Atjeh

Van Langen's post as *assistent-resident* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] placed him at the centre of the civil administration of the Atjeh colony at the moment of the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] of [[19 August]] [[1884]]. The *assistent-resident* of Groot-Atjeh was the senior civil-administrative official of the *geconcentreerde stelling* — the concentrated Dutch perimeter around [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]] — and was, in practice, the official through whom the military Governor's civil-administrative orders were issued to the *controleurs* in the *onderafdeelingen*. The [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratie]] abolished the *controleurs* and transferred their functions to the military commandants of the posts — "*de controleurs in de onderafdeelingen van Groot-Atjeh werden afgeschaft, de functiën van assistent-resident ter Westkust (standplaats Melaboeh) en van controleur te Segli werden opgedragen aan de daar gevestigde militaire commandanten*" (the controleurs in the onderafdeelingen of Groot-Atjeh were abolished; the functions of assistant-resident on the Westkust (station Melaboeh) and of controleur at Segli were entrusted to the military commandants established there) — and the *assistent-resident* of Groot-Atjeh was thus, after the Concentratie, the last remaining civil-administrative official of the inland administration.

Van Langen's *collega* — colleague — on the coast was [[Scherer|resident Scherer]], the *assistent-resident ter Noord- en Oostkust* (standplaats [[Telok Semawé]]), whose *scheepvaartregeling* of May [[1883]] under [[Laging Tobias]] had been the operational model Van Langen, in Groot-Atjeh, was administering in adapted form. The two officials had been, in the period between Laging Tobias's February [[1884]] resignation and the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratiebesluit]] of August [[1884]], the colony's principal civil-administrative witnesses to the policy debate the *Regeering* was conducting over their heads.

### The co-signed nota's against the blokkade

The *nota's* Van Langen co-signed with Scherer were the *met redenen omkleede nota's* — the reasoned notes — by which the two officials warned Demmeni against the *opheffen der scheepvaartregeling en van de posten op de kust* (lifting of the shipping-regulation and of the posts on the coast) and against the substitution of the rigorous *blokkade* for the *scheepvaartregeling* Scherer had introduced. Van Heutsz records the *wankelen* — the wavering — that the *nota's* produced in the General:

> "De Generaal Demmeni werd door Scherer's betoogingen en zijne nota's, waarmede de assistent-resident van Groot-Atjeh van Langen instemde, aan het wankelen gebracht in zijn plan om eenvoudig uit te voeren, wat hem door de Regeering opgedragen was en voerde niet de algemeene rigoureuse blokkade in, maar liet slechts sommige gedeelten van de kust sluiten en blokkeeren en dit niet eens consequent, terwijl hij voor andere gedeelten, reeds bij besluit van 13 October 1884 eene soort scheepvaartregeling invoerde". — General Demmeni was shaken in his plan to carry out simply what the Government had charged him with, by Scherer's representations and his notes, which the assistant-resident of Groot-Atjeh van Langen endorsed; and he did not introduce the general rigorous blockade, but only closed and blockaded some parts of the coast, and that not even consistently, while for other parts he already introduced, by decree of 13 October 1884, a kind of shipping-regulation.

The *instemming* — the concurrence — of Van Langen was the operational fact that distinguished the protest from a single *assistent-resident*'s objection. The General had been *opgedragen* — charged — by the *Regeering* to execute the rigorous *blokkade*; the *nota's* of the two officials gave him reason to *wankelen* in his plan to execute the prescribed system, and the result was the *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]] — the hybrid *half sluiting / half scheepvaartregeling* that Van Heutsz would denounce as the worst of both worlds. The Van Langen co-signature was, in this sense, the immediate cause of the *tweeslachtigheid* — the two-headedness — of the October [[1884]] *besluit*.

### The October 1884 inheritance: Scherer's nota's

Scherer's resignation in early October [[1884]] — when he perceived that Demmeni would, despite the *wankelen*, ultimately execute the prescribed system — left Van Langen as the senior civil-administrative official on whom the *Nota's van Scherer* now depended. Van Heutsz records the inheritance:

> "ware hij gebleven en had hij gestreden met zijne argumenten tegen de blokkade en voor de scheepvaartregeling, zeker zou dan geene contrôle op handel en scheepvaart getroffen zijn, zooals thans het geval is geweest, vermoedelijk ook geen tweeslachtige regeling, en de mogelijkheid, dat de Regeering zich had laten overtuigen van de deugdelijkheid van Scherer's beginselen, ware veel grooter geweest dan thans, nu de Generaal en Scherer's collega van Langen zich moesten behelpen met Scherer's nota's". — had he stayed and fought with his arguments against the blockade and for the shipping-regulation, then certainly no control on trade and shipping would have been adopted as has now been the case, probably also no ambiguous arrangement, and the possibility that the Government would have been convinced of the soundness of Scherer's principles would have been much greater than now, when the General and Scherer's colleague van Langen had to make do with Scherer's notes.

The counter-factual is explicit: had Scherer remained and argued his case against the *blokkade* and for the *scheepvaartregeling*, the *tweeslachtige regeling* — the hybrid regulation of [[13 October]] [[1884]] — would in all probability not have been introduced, and the *Regeering* might have been persuaded of the soundness of Scherer's principles. As it was, Demmeni and *Scherer's collega van Langen* had to *behelfen* themselves — make shift — with Scherer's *nota's*: the textual legacy Scherer had left behind on his departure. Van Langen's role after October [[1884]] was thus the role of the official who, in the absence of the author, had to apply the author's text — a role Van Heutsz treats with measured sympathy and explicit disappointment.

### Significance (per Van Heutsz)

Van Langen's significance in Van Heutsz's narrative is that of the *assistent-resident* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1884]] — the civil-administrative counterpart to [[Scherer|resident Scherer]] on the [[Noordkust|Noord-]] and [[Oostkust]] and the senior civil-administrative official of the *geconcentreerde stelling* at the moment of the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|Concentratiebesluit of 19 August 1884]]; the co-signatory — *instemde* — of the *nota's* against the rigorous *blokkade* that, in the months following the Concentratiebesluit, briefly dissuaded [[Demmeni|Generaal Demmeni]] from the full application of the [[Staatsblad No. 105 van 1884|Staatsblad No. 105 of 1884]] *rigoureuse blokkade* and produced the *besluit* of [[13 October]] [[1884]] *half sluiting / half scheepvaartregeling*; and, after Scherer's early-October [[1884]] resignation, the senior civil-administrative official on whom the *Nota's van Scherer* now depended — *de Generaal en Scherer's collega van Langen zich moesten behelpen met Scherer's nota's*. His co-signature gave Scherer's protests the authority of the civil administration of Groot-Atjeh itself, and without it the General would in all probability have applied the prescribed system without qualification. The Van Langen co-signature was, in this sense, the immediate cause of the *tweeslachtigheid* — the two-headedness — of the October [[1884]] *besluit*, and so of the *fout* and *ramp* of the [[1884]] *Concentratie*.

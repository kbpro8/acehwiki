A Dutch scholar who compiled a dictionary and a "Practical Manual of the Acehnese Language," in which three of the "sentences of Meudeuha'" are published; his remarks on Acehnese music are said by Hurgronje to "require correction and expansion."

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — K. F. H. Van Langen

**K. F. H. Van Langen** was a Dutch colonial official and scholar of Achehnese government, whose *De inrichting van het Atjehsche Staatsbestuur onder het Sultanaat* ("The Organisation of the Achehnese Government under the Sultanate") — published in the *Bijdragen van het Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië* for [[1888]], and cited throughout Vol. I simply as *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* — is the standard printed authority on the political institutions of the sultanate of [[Acheh]]. He is also the author of an Achehnese dictionary and of an essay on [[Acheh's Westkust|Acheh's West Coast]] in the *Tijdschrift van het Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap*.

## Use as an authority

Van Langen's *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* is one of the most frequently cited works in Vol. I. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] refers to it for the traditions and conjectures respecting the origin of the Achehnese (pp. 384–389); for the organisation of the sagis and the formation of new mukims (p. 393); for the hereditary banta or right-hand man of the uleebalang (p. 398); for the list of "twelve uleebalangs who appoint and dethrone princes" (p. 404); for the institution of *wakeueh* lands (pp. 405 *et seqq.*); for the confidential secretaries of the sultan (p. 406, sub 5); for coinage in Acheh (pp. 427–435); for the laws relating to illicit intercourse (p. 469); and for the rules governing the determination of the fasting month (p. 456 *seq.*). It is from Van Langen that Snouck obtained the doggerel rhymes on the mutual relations of the Achehnese *sukeès* or tribes, which he quotes (with correction of some errors) from the *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* and the Achehnese Dictionary. The *sarakatas* (royal edicts) of the sultans, including those bearing the [[Sarakata with chab sikureu'eng|ninefold seal]], were likewise first collected and published by Van Langen.

## Criticism

Snouck's reliance on Van Langen is not uncritical. The *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* is repeatedly cited as the source of statements which Snouck then qualifies or contradicts. The native traditions of the origin of the Achehnese, though collected by Van Langen, are unreliable: the writer's "efforts to reduce these data to one compact whole seem not justifiable in view of the small reliance that can be placed in them." On the *wakeueh* institution, "sundry kindred institutions are undoubtedly assigned to too late an epoch by Van Langen in his *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* pp. 405 seqq." On the question of the determination of the beginning of [[Ramadhan (fasting month)|Ramadhan]], Snouck again refers to Van Langen p. 456 *seq.*, while making clear his own preference for personal observation of the dispute between the upholders of calculation and those of the [[Ruya (moon-sighting)|*ru'ya*]].

The first chapter of Vol. I, in defending the need for a scientific study of Achehnese adat, takes Van Langen's work as the best of the available printed accounts, but treats it as no more than a starting-point. The unpublished sarakatas obtained by Snouck during his residence in Acheh — including the edict of [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Meukuta Alam]] dated [[1607]], revived by [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Sapiatodin]] in [[1645]], and the two further edicts of Meukuta Alam dated [[1635]] and [[1640]] — are explicitly described as "not included among those published by Van Langen."

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Van Langen

**K. F. H. Van Langen** was a Dutch colonial scholar and official whose writings on the political and social institutions of [[Acheh]] formed one of the chief European sources available to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] in the preparation of *The Achehnese*. His principal work, *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* ("The Achehnese System of Government"), first printed in the *Bijdragen van het Kon. Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde* for [[1888]], pp. 381 et seq., was based chiefly on native manuscript documents known as *sarakata* — edicts having the force of law ascribed to [[Sultan Meukuta Alam]] (Iskandar Muda, [[1607]]–[[1636]]) and to [[Shamsul-alam]] ([[1726]]–[[1727]]). Van Langen supplemented these texts with oral tradition collected among the Achehnese, producing what remained, until Snouck Hurgronje's own study, the standard account of the Achehnese state.

Van Langen also compiled an *Achehnese Dictionary* and contributed an essay entitled *Atjeh's Westkust* to the *Tijdschrift van het Kon. Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap*, 2nd series, Vol. V (p. 450 et seq.) and Vol. VI (longer articles, p. 38). These works made him a frequent point of reference, and Snouck Hurgronje cites him on almost every page dealing with institutions of government, land tenure, oath and ordeal, the *kawom* (tribal) system, and the *waqf* or pious endowments of [[Acheh]].

Although Van Langen's labours were indispensable, Snouck Hurgronje treats them with constant critical reserve. The theory advanced in *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* (pp. 384–390) — that the division of the Achehnese people into *kawoms* was introduced on a sudden during the reign of a 16th-century [[Sultan of Acheh (16th century)|Sultan of Acheh]] on the basis of racial distinctions (a Mante-Batak *kawom*, a Hindu *kawom*, a half-caste *kawom* and one of more recent origin) — is dismissed as "unworthy of acceptance" and at variance with native tradition. Snouck Hurgronje instead represents the *kawom* distribution as the ancient and patriarchal form of social organisation, as opposed to the more recent territorial distribution into *mukims*.

A number of Van Langen's specific statements are corrected in turn: his assertion (in *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur*, p. 391) that the *teungku* in charge of the *meunasah* was a kind of subordinate village headman is pronounced erroneous; his dating of certain *wakaf* (waqf) institutions to too late an epoch is criticised; and minor errors in his quotation of Achehnese doggerel in the *Achehnese Dictionary* (under the article *Ja Sandang*) are noted. Snouck Hurgronje nonetheless acknowledges Van Langen's careful preservation of the *sarakata* texts and the value of his ethnographic notes, including a description of the *bainglcotig* dress worn by escaped offenders and of the custom of dressing such offenders in the *bainglcotig*, iron *payee* jacket and weapons with unornamented hilts.

Van Langen's *Achehnese Dictionary* remained, alongside the later work of [[Dr. H. N. van der Tuuk|Dr. van der Tuuk]] and Snouck Hurgronje himself, one of the principal lexicographical sources for the language, and his *Atjehsch Staatsbestuur* preserved data on court ceremonial, on the *peulieh beusoe* (licking of the hot iron) ordeal, and on the *kawom* poetry of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII]] and [[XXV Mukims]] that would otherwise have been lost.

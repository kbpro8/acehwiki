**Teukoe Itam** was an Atjèhnese clerk at the central office of the landscape treasuries in [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] — the "rightful heir" of the loyal Datoek Roehoem of Tapa'toean — whom the F-propagandist [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]] installed as *son-chó* of the Tapa'toean subdistrict after the displacement of the loyal Datoek Roehoem in March [[1942]]. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats the installation of Teukoe Itam as a representative episode of the F-organisatie's political reorganisation — the displacement of the loyal Dutch *oelèëbalang*'s by the F-organisatie's network of collaborators, and the installation of the new administrative hierarchy under Japanese sponsorship.

## The clerk at the central office

Before his installation as *son-chó*, Teukoe Itam served as a clerk at the central office of the landscape treasuries in Koetaradja. The position — a clerical post in the central financial administration of the Atjèhnese *landschap*'s — placed him within the colonial administrative apparatus, but at a subordinate level. His identification as the "rightful heir" of the loyal Datoek Roehoem — that is, as the legitimate successor to the *oelèëbalang*'s position — gave him the traditional claim that the F-organisatie could exploit to legitimise his installation.

## The displacement of Datoek Roehoem

The loyal Datoek Roehoem — the *oelèëbalang* of Tapa'toean from whom the retreating Dutch troops had received much help on their retreat — was, in Piekaar's account, one of the principal casualties of the F-organisatie's activation on the West Coast. The administrative commission assembled for the Tapa'toean subdistrict by the F-leader Said Aboe Bakar "had to make way after a short time for a one-man administration, whereby the Tapa'toean trader Marah Hoesin was appointed as 'gun-chó'." The loyal Datoek Roehoem "had to make way" — that is, was displaced from his position as *oelèëbalang* of Tapa'toean.

## The installation as son-chó

In place of Datoek Roehoem, the F-organisatie installed "the rightful heir Teukoe Itam, clerk at the central office of the landscape treasuries in Koetaradja," as *son-chó*. The *son-chó* — the Japanese administrative title for the head of a *son* (subdistrict) — was the lowest level of the Japanese administrative hierarchy, below the *gun-chó* (the head of a *gun*, or district). The installation of Teukoe Itam as *son-chó* of Tapa'toean — under the *gun-chó* Marah Hoesin — established the new administrative order under Japanese sponsorship, with the F-organisatie's collaborator in the subordinate position of the rightful heir.

## Significance

The installation of Teukoe Itam as *son-chó* of Tapa'toean was, in Piekaar's account, a representative episode of the F-organisatie's political reorganisation. The displacement of the loyal Datoek Roehoem — who had helped the retreating Dutch troops — and the installation of the "rightful heir" Teukoe Itam in his place illustrated the F-organisatie's strategy of combining traditional legitimacy (the "rightful heir") with Japanese administrative authority (the *son-chó* title) to displace the Dutch-aligned rulers. The prisoners of war, Piekaar records, "were evacuated along the route of the road connection Roendéng-Sidikalang" — the new administration's first concern was the consolidation of its authority, not the welfare of the displaced.

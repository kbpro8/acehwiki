**Tengkoe Koetakarang** — *Tgk. Koetakarang* — was an Acehnese writer, an [[Oelama|oelama]] of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], whose *vlugschriften* (polemical pamphlets) were edited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] in [[1892]] and published as the ***Tadkirat arrakidin*** — "Admonition of the Sitting-Still", that is, a call to those still sitting still to rise and join the war against the unbeliever. The pamphlet is one of the central documents of Atjehsche war-literature, and Zentgraff cites it in his *Inleiding* as the Atjehsche voice that judged the colonial government's conduct of the war.

## The "Tadkirat arrakidin"

In the *Tadkirat arrakidin* — edited by Snouck Hurgronje (see his report of [[1892]]) — Tengkoe Koetakarang describes the treatment which general [[Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] received at the hands of the Dutch government as an example of Dutch unreliability that does not spare even the Netherlands' own meritorious men. Zentgraff cites the passage:

> *"Zij (de Hollanders) sluiten ook fraaie tractaten die zij naleven zoolang zij nog nieuw zijn, doch is de bevolking eenmaal door hen gevangen als een vogel in een kool, dan schenden zij die beloften schaamteloos. Nu en dan zenden zij vergiftigde geschenken, en door het gebied dat zij beheerschen laten zij geneesheeren rondgaan, die echter inderdaad als spionnen der Kompeuni dienstdoen. Later verbannen zij, wie niet in alle opzichten hunnen wensch volgt; zelfs den eenoogigen generaal, iemand van hun eigen soort, hebben zij verbannen."*

That is: "They (the Hollanders) also conclude fine treaties, which they observe so long as they are still new, but once the population is caught by them like a bird in a cage, then they shamelessly break those promises. Now and then they send poisoned gifts, and through the territory they rule they send doctors round, who in fact serve as spies of the Kompeuni. Later they banish whoever does not in all respects follow their wish; even the one-eyed general, a man of their own kind, they have banished."

The "one-eyed general" is [[Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]], whose afdanking and wegzening (dismissal and banishment) by the Dutch government is, in Tengkoe Koetakarang's view, the proof of Dutch unreliability even towards their own servants — a proof, the Atjehsche reader was meant to conclude, that no treaty with the *kaphé* can be trusted.

## Significance

Zentgraff introduces Tengkoe Koetakarang's *Tadkirat arrakidin* to make his point that it is good for the Dutch reader to step outside his own self-complacency and listen for once to judgement from the enemy's camp. The *Tadkirat arrakidin* is, with the *hikajat prang* of [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]] and the *heldendicht Prang Kompeuni* of [[Dokarim]], one of the three great Atjehsche prose-poetical witnesses to the war.

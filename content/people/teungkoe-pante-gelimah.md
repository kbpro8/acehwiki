**Teungkoe Panté Gelimah** — also written *T. Panté Gelimah*, *Teungkoe Pantai Glima*, *T. Panté Glima* — was an [[Achehnese]] religious teacher and band leader whom Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* identifies as the "*opruiende element*" (inciting element) in his alliance with the Kling [[Habib Lhong]] (the "*militant element*"), a partnership which linked religious incitement to military action in the western [[XXII Moekims]] and the southern [[III Moekims Daroe]] in the spring of [[1896]]. Gelimah's trajectory spans the period from the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] — in which Habib Lhong was one of the three commanders of the ~250-man Achehnese force — through the post-defection operations of May–June [[1896]], and includes a distinctive episode in which he "stayed at [[Mata Air]] (Daroe) with 30 Pedireezen and did *bertapa* in the [[Grot of Daroe|grot of Daroe]], *misschien wel in navolging van T. Koeta Karang, die daarmede destijds nog al invloed verworven had*" (perhaps in imitation of T. Koeta Karang, who had thereby acquired considerable influence in his day). He is last mentioned as commanding approximately 50 men in the eastern *stelling* near [[Kroeng-Kali]] on [[23 June]] [[1896]] during the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|battle of Kroeng-Kali]].

## Alliance with Habib Lhong and the Opruiende Element

Kruisheer's pairing of Gelimah (the "*opruiende element*", the inciter) with Habib Lhong (the "*militant element*") is one of the most analytically suggestive characterisations in *ATJEH '96*. The pairing captures the division of labour between the religious-oratorical function — the *teungkoe* who preaches *sabil Allah* and mobilises the population through religious exhortation — and the military command function — the Habib who leads the actual bands in the field. The alliance operated in the western [[XXII Moekims]] and the southern [[III Moekims Daroe|3 Moekims Daroe]], and it was active in the lead-up to the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] at [[Ana' Batée]]. The death of Habib Lhong at [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] removed the militant element of the partnership, and Gelimah was forced to adapt his operations to the post-Lhong strategic landscape. The alliance between a Kling Habib and an Achehnese *teungkoe* is also a characteristic configuration of the spring [[1896]] resistance, in which foreign Muslim religious authority and local Achehnese religious authority were combined in the field.

## Kaloet at the Grot of Daroe and Command at Kroeng-Kali

After Lhong's death, Gelimah retreated to the [[Grot of Daroe|grot of Daroe]] in the [[III Moekims Daroe|3 Moekims Daroe]] to perform *kaloet* — the Achehnese practice of *bertapa*, ascetic meditation in a cave which was believed to confer baraka (spiritual power) upon its practitioner. Kruisheer's observation that Gelimah may have been imitating [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]] — "die daarmede destijds nog al invloed verworven had" (who had thereby acquired considerable influence in his day) — places Gelimah in the direct lineage of Koeta Karang's religious-meditative authority. Gelimah was at [[Mata Air]] (Daroe) with "30 Pedireezen" — that is, 30 fighters from [[Pedir]], the north-coast residency — which gives a sense of the scale of the foreign Muslim participation which had survived the [[17 April]] [[1896]] death of Lhong. His final appearance in *ATJEH '96* is on [[23 June]] [[1896]] at the eastern *stelling* near [[Kroeng-Kali]] during the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|battle of Kroeng-Kali]], where he commanded approximately 50 men — a force of similar size to the 30 Pedireezen he had had at Mata Air, suggesting that his core following had remained intact. The [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|23 June]] [[1896]] engagement — which formed part of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing operation]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] and [[G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. Hansen]] — concluded with the storming of the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* by the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] and the retreat of the Achehnese forces (including [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] to [[Lam-Tobah]]).

## Significance

Teungkoe Panté Gelimah's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the *opruiende element* — the religious-oratorical figure whose preaching mobilised the population for the resistance — and in his continuity with the *kaloet* tradition of [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]]. His partnership with [[Habib Lhong]] illustrates the division of religious-military labour in the spring [[1896]] resistance, and his survival through the engagements of May and June [[1896]] — culminating in his command at [[Kroeng-Kali]] on [[23 June]] [[1896]] — makes him one of the most persistent Achehnese religious-military figures of the first semester of [[1896]].

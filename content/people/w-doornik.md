W. Doornik was a Dutch colonial administrator who served as *Assistent-Resident van de afdeeling Groot-Atjeh* (Assistant-Resident of the Groot-Atjeh division) during the period of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]]. As the senior European official responsible for [[Groot-Atjeh]], Doornik held one of the most consequential positions within the administrative hierarchy of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. Groot-Atjeh, which encompassed the heartland of the Atjeh sultanate and the seat of the colonial government at [[Koeta-Radja]], was the political and administrative center of the entire Atjeh territory. Doornik was present at the *bestuursvergaderingen* (administrative meetings) held at [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[19 January]] [[1914]] and [[20 January]] [[1914]], though no recorded remarks from him appear in the official minutes. His attendance at these meetings, held within the very division he administered, underscores the centrality of Groot-Atjeh to the reorganization deliberations.

## Groot-Atjeh as Rechtstreeks Bestuurd Gebied

The *afdeeling* (division) of [[Groot-Atjeh]] occupied a distinctive position within the administrative framework of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] as one of only two areas classified as *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory), the other being part of [[Singkel]]. This designation meant that, unlike the majority of Atjeh's territory which was governed indirectly through the *Inlandsch Bestuur* (Native Administration) and indigenous officials, Groot-Atjeh was subject to direct European administrative control. The *Assistent-Resident* of Groot-Atjeh therefore exercised a more direct and intensive form of authority than his counterparts in other *afdeelingen*, where governance was mediated through *uleëbalang* (territorial chiefs) and other indigenous officials who retained substantial autonomy in local affairs. The direct administration of Groot-Atjeh reflected both the strategic importance of the region—as the center of Dutch power in Atjeh—and the degree to which the indigenous governance structures had been disrupted or subordinated during decades of military conflict.

Groot-Atjeh contained the three *sagi*—the traditional territorial divisions of the Atjeh heartland—that formed the apex of the indigenous administrative hierarchy in the region. These *sagi* (also known as *sagoe*) were *XXII Mukim*, *XXVI Mukim*, and *XXV Mukim*, each representing a confederation of *mukim* (religious-administrative districts) under the leadership of hereditary chiefs. The administrative relationship between the European *Assistent-Resident* and the *sagi* chiefs was a critical element of governance in Groot-Atjeh, requiring constant negotiation between colonial directives and indigenous customary authority. Doornik's responsibilities would have included overseeing this complex interface, ensuring that colonial policies were implemented while maintaining the cooperation of indigenous leaders whose support was essential for stable governance. The classification of Groot-Atjeh as *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* did not eliminate the role of indigenous officials but rather placed them under more direct European supervision than in indirectly ruled territories.

## Proposed Merger with the Westkust

The reorganization deliberations at Koeta-Radja proposed that [[Groot-Atjeh]] be combined with the [[Westkust van Atjeh]] into a single, larger *afdeeling* designated "Groot-Atjeh benevens de Westkust van Atjeh" (Greater Atjeh together with the West Coast of Atjeh), with an estimated combined population of approximately 210,000. This merger, which would have been situated within the proposed new *gewest* (province) of [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]], represented a significant administrative consolidation that would have directly affected Doornik's position and responsibilities. The *Assistent-Resident* of the combined division would need to manage both the directly ruled core of Groot-Atjeh and the more indirectly governed territories of the Westkust, requiring familiarity with different modes of administration and the ability to coordinate across diverse governance structures. While Doornik did not contribute recorded remarks to the proceedings, his presence at the meetings ensured that the perspective of the official currently responsible for Groot-Atjeh was represented in the deliberative process. The proposed merger reflected a recognition that the administrative separation of Groot-Atjeh and the Westkust may have been a historical artifact of the military campaign rather than a rational division based on contemporary administrative needs.

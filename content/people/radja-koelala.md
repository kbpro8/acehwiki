**Radja Koelala** (also written Raja Kulela) was the son of [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]], the hereditary panglima of the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi der XXII Moekims]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], who, after the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of August [[1879]], persisted with his father and the other irreducible hostile chiefs — [[Toewankoe Haschim|Toewankoe Haschim]], the [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]] — in their hostile attitude, but their means were scattered and they had placed themselves beyond the reach of the Dutch troops, at [[Kemala|Kemala]] in the interior south of [[Pedir|Pedir]].

## The Flight to Kemala, August 1879

After the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign against the XXII Moekims]] of [[1879]], the [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|campaign against the XXVI Moekims]] of early July, and the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of August, Radja Koelala was among those who persisted in their hostile attitude:

> "To be sure, the principal among the hostile chiefs — [[Toewankoe Haschim|Toewankoe Haschim]], who still kept watch over the young pretender to the sultanship [[Toewankoe Daoed|Toewankoe Daoed]] [destined for the sultan's rank in 1874], [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]], [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]], his son Radja Koelala and others — persisted in their hostile attitude, but their means were scattered and they had placed themselves beyond the reach of our troops, namely at Kemala, in the interior south of Pedir."

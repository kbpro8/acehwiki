**Tanamal** was an Ambonese *Marechaussee 1e klas* stationed at [[Bakongan]] in the dertiger jaren — a "man met een vervaarlijk gezicht, een stel zeldzaam zware kaken, een ontzagwekkende mond" whose *ongelooflijk lelijke snuit* became "één en al vrolijkheid" at feestjes where he served as *voorzanger*, and the book's *sterkste voorbeeld* of the natural shot who could kill a moving tiger at ten metres while still seated but could not, on the *schietbaan*, hit a stationary target. He is paired in the book with [[Taris]] as the two set-piece illustrations of the *jagerschot* — the hunter's shot — that the annual classification could not measure.

## The face and the voorzanger

"Het Ambonees was: de Marechaussee 1e klas Tanamal." His appearance was formidable — "een man met een vervaarlijk gezicht, een stel zeldzaam zware kaken, een ontzagwekkende mond" — but his function at feestjes was that of *voorzanger*, the lead singer who calls out the couplets of the [[Marechausseelied]] for the others to fall in on. "Wanneer hij meedeed aan een feestje, waar hij zijn taak als voorzanger had, werd die ongelooflijk lelijke snuit één en al vrolijkheid." The contrast between the face and the function is the book's small joke about the Ambonese soldier: the man who looked the part of the killer was the life of the party.

## The tiger at ten metres

The set-piece of Tanamal's marksmanship is the tiger. During a patrol *rust* in the *[[rimboe]]*, a tiger crept up on the brigade. Tanamal, still seated, calmly fired — "en tien meter verderop lag een dode tijger." The shot, with the 6.5 mm [[karabijn (M. 95)|karabijn]], had to be precise: "dat schot moest zuiver in de hersens zitten, want het kaliber is eigenlijk te licht voor de grote jacht." On another patrol he killed a *hert* at thirty metres through a flits in the bushes — he had seen a movement and shot at the gap.

## The schietbaan and the A-klas

On the *schietbaan* Tanamal "was het huilen met de pet op" — he could not hit a stationary target. "Alles aan hem was jager; op de baan stelde hij niets voor." Only by slightly bending the A-klas requirements in his favour did his superiors get him through the annual classification: "als Tanamal dan aan het einde van het schietjaar geen achterlijk schutter was, maar net met de hakken over de sloot aan de eisen van de A-klas had voldaan, dan was daaraan heel wat symptoombehandeling te pas gekomen." The point is not that Tanamal was a bad shot but that the *schietbaan* measured the wrong thing — what he could do, no A-klas test could measure.

## The hert and the makanan

The hert at thirty metres provided "een paar beste dagen" of *makanan* (food) for the geheel, dwangarbeiders inbegrepen. The detail is the book's small illustration of the practical economy of the patrol: the jager's skill fed the colonne.

## Significance

Tanamal's significance in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is as the book's first of two set-piece illustrations of the natural shot. Paired with [[Taris]] — who could kill a moving *pelapor* at eighty-five metres with a *kopschot* but missed a stationary target — Tanamal is Dames's argument that the [[K.N.I.L.]]'s formal classification system failed to capture the actual military value of its Ambonese soldiers. The point is not anecdotal: it is part of the book's larger argument that the colonial army's *[[Oom Ambon]]* was held together less by regulation than by the patient daily cultivation of men like this, whose skills ran ahead of what the rulebook could record.

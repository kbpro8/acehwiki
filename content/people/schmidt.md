**Schmidt** was an officer of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|K.N.I.L.]] (Royal Netherlands East Indies Army) who, as *luitenant* (lieutenant) and later *kapitein* (captain), commanded the [[Colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] — a small force of forty-one militairen and twenty-five *dwangarbeiders* (forced labourers) that in two years ([[1910]]–[[1911]]) destroyed the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]] (the Tiriō band) of Acehnese religious resisters in the bergland of [[Tangsé]] — and who, after the campaign, transferred to the civil bestuur as *Assistent-Resident* on [[Ambon]], where his former sergeant [[Nussy]] served under him as *mantri-politie*. He was, in Dames's description, "wel zowat de beste kenner van de taal en het volk" in Aceh — about the best living authority on the Acehnese language and people in the officer corps — and received the *[[Eresabel]]* for [[1910]] and the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] 3e klas for [[1911]]. He later held the rank of *Kolonel* (colonel) and, with [[Maurice Henri du Croo]], co-authored two book-length Dutch accounts of the [[Aceh War]]: *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]* and *[[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]]*. No first name or initials are given for him either in Dames's *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* or in the memorial booklet for du Croo.

## Background and decorations before 1910

"De luitenant Schmidt had zijn sporen op Atjeh reeds behoorlijk verdiend. Eerst had hij de Oranje-Nassau gekregen en later de M.W.O." That is, before the colonne that bears his name, Schmidt had already earned the *[[Oranje-Nassau (Orde van Oranje Nassau)|Orde van Oranje Nassau]]* (Order of Orange-Nassau) and a first [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] (Military William Order) — making him an unusually decorated luitenant. He was also "wel zowat de beste kenner van de taal en het volk" — the best living authority on the Acehnese language and custom in the officer corps — and his intelligence network was the best of anyone's. The colonne Schmidt was therefore the systematic working-out of long immersion in the country, not a lucky break.

## The colonne Schmidt campaign (1910–1911)

The campaign is treated at length in its own entry. In brief: a colonne of forty-one militairen (Schmidt himself included) and twenty-five *dwangarbeiders* (all [[Boeginezen|Buginese]] convicts, who served as dragers — bearers — for the patrouilles) operated for two years in the bergland ten Oosten of [[Tangsé]] against the family and following of the late *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]]*. Schmidt's methods combined military pressure with diplomatic outreach: he twice tried to persuade the Tiriō Teungkoes to submit, once via a letter carried by three leading Acehnese heads (the two [[Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala|Sultans-family members]] and [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Prang Polim]]) at the request of [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]], and once via the tante of the young *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* — the widow of *[[Teungkoe Asan Oeleë Toetoeë]]*, whom Schmidt himself had had killed only months before. The widow, an "eerbiedwekkende Atjehse vrouw, reeds op leeftijd" (venerable Acehnese woman, already advanced in years), went into the bergen; but the boy refused: "Zijn Vader was als geloofsgetuige gevallen, hij zou in diens voetstappen treden. Allah's wil zou geschieden" (His father had fallen as a witness to the faith, he would follow in his footsteps. Allah's will would be done).

In two years the Tiriō Teungkoes with their bende were "volkomen verdreven" — completely driven out. The major milestones: the killing of *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]* in [[May 1911|May]] [[1911]]; the overval (assault) on the schuilplaats (hiding-place) later in May (with eleven vijanden — enemies — dead and *[[Tgk. di Boekét]]* mortally wounded); the killing of *[[Habib Teupin Wan]]* in [[September 1911|September]] [[1911]]; and the killing of *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* by the korporaal [[Nussy]], with [[Rampisela]] and [[Koentjoeng]], on [[3 December]] [[1911]]. "De Tiri-bende was niet meer" — the Tiri band was no more.

## Decorations: the Eresabel and the M.W.O. 3e klas

"Hij kreeg dan ook voor 1910 de Eresabel en voor 1911 de M.W.O. 3e klas." The two decorations recognised the two successive years of the campaign: the *Eresabel* (honorary saber) for distinguished combat leadership in the first year, and the *M.W.O. 3e klas* (Military William Order, 3rd class) for the closing year in which the bende was finally destroyed. Dames records the same pair of decorations in the *Nawoord* of *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, where Schmidt's published report on the [[1910]]–[[1911]] campaign is named among the printed sources Dames drew on for his own account.

## Transfer to the civil bestuur and Ambon

After his military career Schmidt "kwam later op Ambon" — later came to be on Ambon — as *Assistent-Resident*, the civilian district officer rank that, in the Dutch colonial hierarchy, was the natural progression for an officer with his knowledge of the inheemse bevolking (indigenous population). On Ambon he was reunited with his former sergeant [[Nussy]], now *mantri-politie* (police sergeant); the army kept watch that the *[[Communistische Partij (PKI)|communist Patty]]* would not "zijn hoofd af zou slaan" — would cut off the head — of the old Marechaussee. The author's quiet observation is that Schmidt "was altijd soldaat gebleven, soldaat sadja" — had always remained a soldier, just a soldier — a phrase he uses of Nussy but which, in context, applies to them both: the two men who had hunted the Tiriō Teungkoes in the bergland of Aceh were now, in the late-colonial peace of Ambon, working together again in a different idiom.

## Co-Authorship with Maurice Henri du Croo

Schmidt's later career brought him into the interwar literary commemoration of the Aceh War. He collaborated with [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] on two book-length Dutch accounts: *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]*, on the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee corps]] and its patrols, and *[[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]]*, a biography of the [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] administration that closed the war. His role as co-author on a book about the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] in the [[Aceh War]] suggests direct experience with the military police corps that fought in the Acehnese jungle. The two-volume output, comprising the corps history and the Swart biography, made Schmidt a key link between the operational and the historiographical phases of the Dutch memory of the [[Aceh War]].

## Significance

Schmidt is significant in the AcehWiki corpus in two complementary registers. As a primary actor in the [[Aceh War]] he is the officer who actually finished the war at the local level — not [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz]] at the strategic level, not [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]] at the military-governmental level, but the luitenant in the colonne who methodically ran down the last resistance family in the bergland. His combination of intelligence work, diplomacy, and combat — and his transfer to the civilian administration that followed — is the model of the late-colonial bestuur officer that Dames's *Oom Ambon* quietly endorses; his reunion with Nussy on Ambon gives the book one of its most resonant pairings. As a literary collaborator he is, with du Croo, the co-author of two of the book-length Dutch accounts of the war's military institutions and its closing administration; his career and writings span the active pacification era (the *colonne Schmidt*) and the interwar literary commemoration of that era (*Marechaussée in Atjeh* and *Generaal Swart*).

## Account from *Atjeh* by H.C. Zentgraff (1938)

Zentgraff gives Schmidt the title *primus inter pares* among the Dutch *aanvoerders* in Atjeh: he knew the *Atjehsche taal en adat als niemand anders* (Acehnese language and adat like nobody else), so that *de legende van mysterieuze gaven en ilmoe's om hem hing* (the legend of mysterious gifts and ilmoe's hung about him), and this gave him greater influence over the population than most commanders had.

In [[1908]] [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]] gave Schmidt the *vernietigingsapparaat* against the [[Tiro-oelama|Tiro-oelama's]]. Schmidt built his intelligence-work on the *fourageurs* (foraging parties) of the bende — they must come out to eat. On [[2 May]] [[1910]] his brigade overvalled the ladang of [[Peutoea Gam Masèn]], the *heerscher over de tijgers* (ruler of the tigers), at the Kroeëng Seukoeleh; the *Peutoea* charged with his *rentjong* and was killed by the klewang of a maréchaussée. On [[5 September]] [[1910]] the *bende-Heynen*, working on Schmidt's information, overvalled the schuilplaats of [[Tengkoe Majet di Tiro|Tgk. Majet]]; the Tengkoe escaped, but his wife [[Tjoet Gambang]] fell *zwaar gewond* into Dutch hands, and refused treatment: *"Bêta mat keë, kaphé boedo"* — "Do not touch me, leper kafir."

On [[29 September]] [[1911]] Schmidt overvalled the schuilplaats of the *grijze oelama* [[Habib Teupin Wan]] — *een gevecht in prachtigen stijl* (a fight in beautiful style). When Schmidt returned to [[Tangsé]] the *oeléëbalang*'s wife asked him how many dead and wounded the Kompeuni had suffered: *"Zelfs geen enkele gewonde"* — not even one wounded — whereat the astonished woman: *"Dan moet uw eleumeë wel heel sterk zijn, want de Habib was zoo machtig dat, als iemand maar met den vinger naar hem wees, diens hand verstijfde."*

Zentgraff records Schmidt's mystical reputation in detail. At the Tado bivak Schmidt, walking the *pagger* at dusk, found *versche sporen van een grooten tijger* (fresh spoor of a large tiger); the *keutjhi* called the *pawang rimoeëng* (tiger-charmer) who had all the tigers of the country *onder appèl*. At the Boven-Wojla Schmidt had the keutjhi build a tiger-val with a back-door, using the old *bezweringformule*: *"Kala keu dikah kala laka laka."* The Acehnese came to believe the tiger was *in dienst van Schmidt*. Once Schmidt witnessed an Atjehsche *wapensmid*, after praying to *Sjech Abdul Kadeuë* (the *Heer van het IJzer*), take a rentjong and *krulde het puntige einde geheel op, zonder dat een menschenhand het had aangeraakt* — curled the pointed end up without a human hand touching it. Schmidt tried with his own rentjong; the same thing happened. *Schmidt zegt: ik heb het met deze, mijn eigen oogen, gezien.*

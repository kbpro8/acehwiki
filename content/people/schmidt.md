**Schmidt** was an officer of the [[K.N.I.L.]] who, as *luitenant* and later *kapitein*, commanded the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] — a small force of forty-one militairen and twenty-five *dwangarbeiders* that in two years ([[1910]]-[[1911]]) destroyed the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]] of Acehnese religious resisters in the bergland of [[Tangsé]] — and who later transferred to the civil bestuur as *Assistent-Resident* on [[Ambon]], where his former sergeant [[Nussy]] served under him as *mantri-politie*. He was, in Dames's description, "wel zowat de beste kenner van de taal en het volk" in Aceh, and received the *[[Eresabel]]* for [[1910]] and the [[Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)|M.W.O.]] 3e klas for [[1911]].

## Background and decorations before 1910

"De luitenant Schmidt had zijn sporen op Atjeh reeds behoorlijk verdiend. Eerst had hij de Oranje-Nassau gekregen en later de M.W.O." That is, before the colonne that bears his name he had already earned the *Orde van Oranje Nassau* and a first M.W.O. — making him an unusually decorated luitenant. He was also "wel zowat de beste kenner van de taal en het volk" — the best living authority on Acehnese language and custom in the officer corps — and his intelligence network was the best of anyone's. The colonne Schmidt was therefore the systematic working-out of long immersion in the country, not a lucky break.

## The colonne Schmidt campaign (1910-1911)

The campaign is treated at length in its own entry. In brief: a colonne of forty-one militairen (Schmidt himself included) and twenty-five *dwangarbeiders* (all [[Boeginezen|Buginese]] convicts, who served as dragers for the patrouilles) operated for two years in the bergland ten Oosten of [[Tangsé]] against the family and following of the late *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]]*. Schmidt's methods combined military pressure with diplomatic outreach: he twice tried to persuade the Tiriō Teungkoes to submit, once via a letter carried by three leading Acehnese heads (the two *Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala|Sultans-family members]] and *[[Panglima Prang Polim]]*) at the request of [[Generaal Swart]], and once via the tante of the young *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* — the widow of *[[Teungkoe Asan Oeleë Toetoeë]]*, whom Schmidt himself had had killed only months before. The widow, an "eerbiedwekkende Atjehse vrouw, reeds op leeftijd," went into the bergen; but the boy refused: "Zijn Vader was als geloofsgetuige gevallen, hij zou in diens voetstappen treden. Allah's wil zou geschieden."

In two years the Tiriō Teungkoes with their bende were "volkomen verdreven." The major milestones: the killing of *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]* in [[May 1911]]; the overval on the schuilplaats later in May (with eleven vijanden dead and *[[Tgk. di Boekét]]* mortally wounded); the killing of *[[Habib Teupin Wan]]* in [[September 1911]]; and the killing of *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* by the korporaal [[Nussy]], with [[Rampisela]] and [[Koentjoeng]], on [[3 December]] [[1911]]. "De Tiri-bende was niet meer."

## Decorations and the Eresabel

"Hij kreeg dan ook voor 1910 de Eresabel en voor 1911 de M.W.O. 3e klas." The two decorations recognised the two successive years of the campaign: the Eresabel (honorary saber) for distinguished combat leadership in the first year, and the M.W.O. 3e klas for the closing year in which the bende was finally destroyed.

## Transfer to civil bestuur and Ambon

After his military career Schmidt "kwam later op Ambon" as Assistent-Resident — the civilian district officer rank that, in the Dutch colonial hierarchy, was the natural progression for an officer with his knowledge of the inheemse bevolking. On Ambon he was reunited with his former sergeant [[Nussy]], now *mantri-politie*; the army kept watch that the *[[Communistische Partij (PKI)|communist Patty]]* would not "zijn hoofd af zou slaan" of the old Marechaussee. The author's quiet observation is that Schmidt "was altijd soldaat gebleven, soldaat sadja" — a phrase he uses of Nussy but which, in context, applies to them both: the two men who had hunted the Tiriō Teungkoes in the bergland of Aceh were now, in the late-colonial peace of Ambon, working together again in a different idiom.

## Significance

Schmidt is the book's portrait of the officer who actually finished the Aceh War at the local level — not [[Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] at the strategic level, not [[Generaal Swart|Swart]] at the military-governmental level, but the luitenant in the colonne who methodically ran down the last resistance family in the bergland. His combination of intelligence work, diplomacy, and combat — and his transfer to the civilian administration that followed — is the model of the late-colonial bestuur officer that the book quietly endorses. The fact that he is reunited with Nussy on Ambon gives the book one of its most resonant pairings.

**J. K. J. de Stuers** — a Dutch Catholic member of the [[Tweede Kamer]] of one of the governing parties — is the parliamentary voice who, in the [[1904]] debate on the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|Gajoe-Alas-Batak expedition of Van Daalen]], drew the most extreme historical parallels in the entire Indies parliamentary record: comparing the expedition to the campaigns of [[Timoer Lenk]] and [[Djengis Khan]] and characterising it as "beulenwerk" — the work of executioners. The disavowal he suffered at the hands of his fractieleider Kolkman is one of the dramatic episodes of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]]-era parliamentary record.

## The 1904 speech

After the official figures of the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|Van Daalen expedition]] — 2,922 inlanders killed, including 1,149 women and children, "in weinige dagen" — were communicated to the [[Tweede Kamer]], [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] made the speech that would become the most quoted single intervention of the Atjehnese-war parliamentary debates. [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) had already declared the expedition "erger dan de dagen van Alva's schrikbewind" — worse than the days of Alva's reign of terror. [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] — a Catholic, and therefore accustomed to draw on a longer historical memory — went further back: "Het is of men op de inlanders een bende bloedhonden heeft losgelaten, het is beulenwerk". And he compared it explicitly to the campaigns of [[Timoer Lenk]] (Tamerlane) and [[Djengis Khan]] (Genghis Khan).

## The disavowal

[[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]]' speech "was zo ver buiten de voor een afgevaardigde van één der regeringspartijen gebruikelijke vormen van kritiek gegaan, dat hij door zijn fractieleider Kolkman openlijk en plechtig gedesavoueerd werd" — had gone so far beyond the forms of criticism customary for a member of one of the governing parties that he was openly and solemnly disavowed by his party-leader Kolkman. The disavowal is the most visible party-disciplinary action in the colonial parliamentary record of the period.

## The electoral-nightmare retort

At the next begrotingsdebat — after the elections — [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] returned to the charge. He called Kolkman's disavowal "het product ener electorale nachtmerrie" — the product of an electoral nightmare. The incident revealed the strain that the Atjehnese war was placing on the internal cohesion of the Dutch governing parties.

## The 230 bayonets

The members of the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition]] had, meanwhile, received 148 decorations — among them 44 military Willemsorden — for "180 bajonetten" (it was actually about 230), a generosity that [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] found "wat overdadig" — rather excessive. The disproportion between the number of bayonets and the number of decorations is one of the standing ironies of the Atjehnese-war honours system.

## Later interventions

[[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] returned to Atjeh questions in subsequent begrotingsdebatten. He it was who formulated the *devies* of "het brengen van rust en orde en veiligheid" as the touchstone of colonial policy, and who observed that the Atjehnese war — already 35 years long — was "niet bijster veel" different from a state of perpetual anarchy under indigenous chiefs. The last nine years of the war had, by the official figures, killed 20,000 of a population of about half a million.

## Significance

[[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]]'s 1904 speech is the moral climax of the Atjehnese-war parliamentary record. His comparison of the [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] expedition to [[Timoer Lenk]] and [[Djengis Khan]] is, in the verdict of [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]], the historical reference-point before which all subsequent Dutch discussion of Atjehnese-war atrocities is measured.

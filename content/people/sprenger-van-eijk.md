**Sprenger van Eijk** — *Minister Sprenger van Eijk* — was the Dutch minister of colonies in [[1889]] who devised, against the beri-beri that plagued the Dutch troops in Atjèh, the beri-beri commission — the "*commissie-Keuchenhuis*" (as [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] satirically calls it, after [[Keuchenius|Keuchenius]] who had preceded Sprenger van Eijk as minister of colonies) — that was the government's principal response to the beri-beri problem. Sprenger van Eijk's ministerial stance — that, in the matter of Atjèh, the government wished nothing new, that one had now come to a *modus* and that that one thought must be maintained — was the immediate political context of Snouck Hurgronje's [[1889]] despatch to Indië with a special commission to study the institutions of Islam on Java (rather than Atjèh).

## The Beri-Beri Commission

In Atjèh, in [[1889]], one had the [[Geconcentreerde Linie|concentrated line] and the beri-beri. With the concentrated line one knew where one was financially: it cost so-and-so much per year, and that was that! About Atjèh-politics no further need be spoken. And the beri-beri? against that the same smart minister Sprenger van Eijk had devised a commission: the beri-beri commission; so on that point too one was covered.

## The Atjèh-Political Stance

No, in the matter of Atjèh the government wished nothing new. One had now come to a *modus*, and that one thought must be maintained. The *modus* — the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[1884]], with the concentrated line around [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja] and the beri-beri commission as its medical complement — was the government's settled Atjèh-policy, and Sprenger van Eijk's ministerial stance was that no change was to be made.

## The Tjilegon Murders and the Islam on Java

But [[Tjilegon|Tjilegon]] and the Islam on Java! That the government wished to see studied. There at Tjilegon, in that same Bantam where also lay Lebak — the Lebak of [[Multatuli|Multatuli]]'s *Max Havelaar* — a number of Europeans were unexpectedly murdered, and hadji's were involved, so the Islam must somehow be made responsible. But what was the matter with that Islam on Java? That the government meant Snouck Hurgronje to investigate; and so he got a special commission to go to Indië for at most two years, at the disposal of the governor-general, for the purpose of an investigation into the institutions of Islam.

## Significance

Sprenger van Eijk is the Dutch minister of colonies in [[1889]] whose ministerial stance — that, in the matter of Atjèh, the government wished nothing new, and that the *modus* of the concentration system and the beri-beri commission must be maintained — was the immediate political context of Snouck Hurgronje's [[1889]] despatch to Indië with a special commission to study the institutions of Islam on Java (rather than Atjèh). The beri-beri commission — "*commissie-Keuchenhuis*", as Damsté satirically calls it — was the government's principal response to the beri-beri problem; and the Atjèh-political stance was the principal obstacle to Snouck Hurgronje's Atjèh interest in [[1889]].

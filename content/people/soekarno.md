**Soekarno** — *Boeng Karno* in the book's idiom — was the Indonesian nationalist leader (and later the first President of Indonesia) whom *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* mentions as the communist who, with his "handlangers" (henchmen), tried to make their strike on [[Java]] between [[1906]] and [[1927]]. Soekarno proclaimed the independence of the Republic of Indonesia on [[17 August]] [[1945]], and was the political leader of the Republic against which the [[K.N.I.L.]] fought its last war in [[1945]]-[[1949]].

## The 1906-1927 communist period

"Van [[1906]] tot '27 is het op Java nog even op een paar punten wat gevaarlijk geweest toen de communisten, met Soekarno (u kent hem wel!) en zijn handlangers, hun slag wilden slaan." Between [[1906]] and [[1927]] the communists, with Soekarno and his henchmen, tried to make their strike on Java. The book's parenthetical "(u kent hem wel!)" — "you know him!" — is the address to the Dutch reader of [[1954]], for whom Soekarno was the known adversary of the colonial state and, after [[1949]], the President of the Republic that had succeeded it.

## The 17 August 1945 declaration

The Indonesian declaration of independence of [[17 August]] [[1945]] was proclaimed by Soekarno and Hatta. The declaration opened the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution — the war that the [[K.N.I.L.]] would fight against the Republic, and that would end with the [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]] of [[27 December]] [[1949]] and the dissolution of the colonial army.

## The Republic's political leader

Soekarno was the political leader of the Republic against which the [[K.N.I.L.]] fought its last war. The book's *Tegen de rood-witten* — "Against the Red-Whites" — is the chapter on the war against the Republic, and the *rood-witten* (the Red-Whites, the red-and-white of the Indonesian flag) are the soldiers of Soekarno's Republic. The book's quiet point is that the Ambonese soldiers of the colonial army — the *[[Oom Ambon]]* who had served the Dutch since the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] — were, in [[1945]]-[[1949]], used in a war against the Republic whose political leader the book had identified, twenty years earlier, as the communist threat to the colonial state.

## Significance

Soekarno is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the Indonesian nationalist leader whom the book identifies as the communist adversary of the colonial state in the [[1906]]-[[1927]] period, and as the political leader of the Republic against which the [[K.N.I.L.]] fought its last war. The book's argument is that the Ambonese soldiers of the colonial army were used in a war whose political outcome would leave them stateless, and that the Republic's political leader — Soekarno — was the figure the book had identified as the communist threat twenty years earlier.

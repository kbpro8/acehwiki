**T. Tjoet Toengkoeb** — also written *Toekoe Tjoet Toengkoeb*, abbreviated *T. Tjoet* — was the [[hoeloebalang]] (hereditary territorial chief) of [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] whose defection to [[Teuku Umar]] with approximately 100 followers in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]] opened the north-eastern sector of [[Groot-Atjeh]] to the resistance, and whose [[benting Batoe]] was the target of the celebrated [[night surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of 28–29 April 1896]]. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* records that Toengkoeb's loyalty to the [[Dutch]] had "nochal eens te wenschen overliet" (left something to be desired) even before the defection, and that he was one of the three-man leadership of the [[XXVI Moekims]] (alongside [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] and [[T. Neq van Merassa]]) charged with clearing the area east of the concentrated line. After his defection he ordered imams to build a fortification in each *moekim*, restored the [[benting Batoe]] for his own use, and continued to operate as one of the most resilient Achehnese chiefs through the [[23 June]] [[1896]] [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|battle of Kroeng-Kali]], after which he retreated to [[Lam-Tobah]].

## Pre-Defection Position in the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb

The [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] lay in the eastern sector of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and contained the post of [[Toengkoeb]] — a Dutch *buitenpost* garrisoned by 2 officers, 52 infantry and 1 hospital attendant, with no artillery, and described by Kruisheer as "the residence of the head of the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb". After the Dutch concentration, Toengkoeb had also become "one of the principal residences of [[Teungkoe di Tiro]]", and the Dutch had occupied it specifically "to prevent moslemin from re-settling and to control the Oeloebalang T. Tjoet Toengkoeb". The Dutch were therefore acutely aware of Toengkoeb's potential for defection, and the text's admission that his loyalty was "not always desirable" indicates that the colonial administration had been monitoring him well before [[29 March]] [[1896]]. Toengkoeb's role in the three-man leadership of the [[XXVI Moekims]] — alongside [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] and [[T. Neq van Merassa]] — was to clear the area east of the concentrated line of hostile bands, a task which required close cooperation with the Dutch but which also gave him detailed knowledge of the Dutch dispositions and of the population's shifting loyalties.

## Defection and the Night Surprise of 28–29 April 1896

Toengkoeb defected to Umar with "±100 volgelingen" in the immediate wake of Umar's own defection, but his case was unusual in that he "initially still kept in touch with the administration" — that is, he attempted to hedge his bets, maintaining communications with the Dutch even as he negotiated his new alliance with Umar. The Dutch response came on the night of [[28 April|28]]–[[29 April]] [[1896]], when a column under [[waarnemend controleur]] [[M. Neelmeijer|1e Luit. Neelmeijer]] — the same Neelmeijer who had commanded the [[XXVI Moekims]] as acting civil administrator — launched a night attack on the *benting Batoe*. The Dutch *Eur. sergt.* [[Panman, No. 21620|Sergeant Panman]] "opened the first gate of the voorwerk of benting Batoe from the inside", and *Eur. sergt.* [[Vogel, No. 22210|Sergeant Vogel]] "opened the second gate" and killed Toengkoeb's confidant [[Leh-Oe]] as the latter tried to break through. The success of the night attack was a notable feat of colonial arms, but it did not eliminate Toengkoeb: he "reportedly returned to his gebied after the two Toengkoeb expeditions" (the [[30 April]] [[1896|30 April]] and [[4 May]] [[1896]] expeditions), demonstrating the liprovided that of Dutch punitive capacity in the eastern mukims.

## Operations from May to June 1896 and Retreat to Lam-Tobah

Through May and June [[1896]], Toengkoeb continued to operate as one of the most active Achehnese chiefs. Kruisheer records that he "ordered imams to build a *versterking* in each moekim", a directive that transformed the XIII Moekims Toengkoeb into a fortified landscape in which every mosque parish had its own defensive work. He also "had [[Batoe]] put back in orderr for himself", restoring the same *benting* which the Dutch had stormed on the night of [[28 April|28]]–[[29 April]]. By mid-June, Toengkoeb had extended his activities into the [[V Moekims Montassik]] sector: he was listed among the hostile *bendehoofden* whose fortifications provoked the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of 16–17 June 1896]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]], and he was active in the eastern *stelling* near [[Kroeng-Kali]] on [[23 June]] [[1896]], where he participated in the Achehnese defence under the overall command of [[Teungkoe Id-cen / Mohamad Said|Teungkoe Id-cen]]. After the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|Dutch storming of the Kroeng-Kali benteng]] by the compagnie of [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]] on [[23 June]] [[1896]], Toengkoeb — together with the *bendehoofd* [[Panglima Nja Makam]] — "retreated to [[Lam-Tobah]]", maintaining his forces intact for continued resistance in the next phase of the war.

## Significance

T. Tjoet Toengkoeb's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the *hoeloebalang* who joined Umar's defection without abandoning his territorial autonomy — a chief whose loyalty was always transactional, whose military capacity survived repeated Dutch punitive operations, and whose order to fortify every moekim represented an ambitious attempt to turn the entire XIII Moekims into a defensive landscape. His survival through the first semester of [[1896]], and his retreat to Lam-Tobah with his forces intact, ensured that the eastern sector of Groot-Atjeh would remain a theatre of resistance into the second semester.

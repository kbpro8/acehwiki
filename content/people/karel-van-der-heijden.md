**Karel van der Heijden** was a prominent officer in the [[Dutch East Indies Army]] who served as a column commander during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] and later as Civil and Military Governor of Aceh from [[1877]] to [[1881]].

## Operational Service (1873–1874)

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], Lieutenant-Colonel Van der Heijden commanded infantry units during the [[Capture of Peunayong]], led the southern encirclement column in the [[Encirclement and Fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], and commanded assault columns at the [[Battle of Bital]].

## Aggressive Pacification Campaign (1877–1881)

Appointed Governor in [[July]] [[1877]], Van der Heijden instituted a policy of total military aggressiveness (*agressie-politiek*). He launched systematic sweeps throughout the Aceh valley, culminating in the major [[Battle of Selimeum]] on [[15 July]] [[1879]], which expelled resistance forces from Great Aceh.

In [[July]] [[1880]], he conducted the [[Battle of Batu Ili]] expedition against [[Samalanga]]. His hardline campaigns secured temporary submission from anti-colonial leaders, including [[Habib Abdurrahman az-Zahir]] in [[October]] [[1878]], before civil administration was introduced in [[1881]].

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

[[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]]'s fourth chapter is the fullest contemporary eulogy of Van der Heyden's record: the operations in the [[XXII Moekims]] resumed [[23 March]] [[1879]], the [[Capture of Indrapuri (24 March 1879)|capture of missigit Indrapoerie]] with insignificant loss on the next day, the destruction of the fortified lines of [[Djerir|Sihong (Djerir)]], [[Gleieng]] and other chief centres of resistance in May-June, the morning-storm [[Capture of Gleiêng (9 July 1879)|of Gleieng]] (Verstege dates it [[5 June]] [[1879]]) with slight loss while the feared sagie-head [[Panglima Polim]] fled, the finishing work of 9-10 June, the punishment of the robbers of [[Ladoeng]] and [[Kroeng Rajal]] ([[19 June]]) without Dutch loss, the opening of the veldtocht against the [[XXVI Moekims]] ([[30 June]]), the trips to Lampanas and Lamtobah, the marches of the Selimoen column (July-August) that caused [[Toekoe Moeda Daoed]] and [[Toekoe Tjoet Hadjet]] themselves to meet the troops with white flags ([[15 August]]-[[20 August]]), the Dagorder of [[5 September]] [[1879]] ("Gheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebracht" — "All Great-Atjeh subdued"), the letter of [[20 August]] [[1879]] in which he announced that the war operations on a grand scale were finished and that the period of the endless patrolling to keep peace and safety had begun, with the grave warnings that a hasty measure from too great a desire of parsimony might undo all that had been wrought, and that the reforming process of the population had to be guarded by a powerful government with a sufficient force behind it; the Nota of [[19 April]] [[1880]] on the form of administration in Groot-Atjeh; the brilliant dinner at Batavia in September [[1880]] where [[Van Kinsbergen]]'s decoration showed the Dutch lion crushing the Atjeh flag with a broken lance under its claw; the reception of heads after the end of the fast ([[4 October]] [[1880]]) and of [[Toengkoe Sjech Marabahan]] ([[7 March]] [[1881]]); the [[Laying of the first stone of Meusigit Raja|first stone of the new mosque]] laid by him on [[9 October]] [[1879]] before 6,000 Atjehers, princes and chiefs of the vasal states at his invitation; and then the removal by the [[Normale bestuursinrichting (Atjeh, 1881-1884)|Kabinetsbrief of 11 March 1881]] — with the Governor-General's thanks for his many and great services "voornamelijk voor de laatste twee jaren" — the parting speech of 9 April ending "Groote voorzichtigheid blijft geboden om de vrucht van jaren niet in gevaar te brengen" ("Great caution remains required not to endanger the fruit of years"), and the lament of the pamphlet: "het ontzag inboezemende zwaard van VAN DER HEYDEN [was vervangen door] den geminachten politiestok van PRUIJS VAN DER HOEVEN" ("the awe-inspiring sword of Van der Heyden was replaced by the despised police-stick of Pruijs van der Hoeven"). Verstege invokes too the memory that Van der Heyden, in March [[1881]] still, by the appreciation of the sovereign and the thanks of the government, had been crowned with the most honourable dismissal, and yet "in Februari 1882 in zijn Memorie" it was the same general who had to refute with irrefutable proofs the accusation that the war against Atjeh had been waged in an inhuman manner — and that nearly ended on the accused's bench (see [[W. van Goltstein|van Goltstein]]'s parliamentary utterance of [[18 November]] [[1881]]).

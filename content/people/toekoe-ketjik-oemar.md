**Toekoe Ketjik Oemar** was an [[Achehnese|Atjehnese]] auxiliary — one of the *volgelingen* of the defecting warlord [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan]] — who, in [[March]] [[1896]], secretly warned the Dutch *contrôleur* [[Kroesen]] of [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]]'s plan to attack the Dutch troops rather than to march on [[Lamkrak]] as he had been ordered. His timely warning — attributed in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] to his *doodsverachting* (contempt of death), his *trouw aan het eens gegeven woord* (loyalty to a once-given word), and his *moed en beleid* (courage and prudence) — is credited by the album with having allowed the Dutch to take immediate measures and with having prevented the defection of [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]] from having "still more serious consequences."

## The warning

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, in the immediate aftermath of [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]]'s defection, almost all of the Atjehnese chiefs on the Dutch side went over to the defector. Against this background, the album singles out Toekoe Ketjik Oemar as the *trouwe inlander* — "faithful native" — whose warning made the difference. The substance of his report was that Djohan would not march on Lamkrak — the *kampong* south of the road along the river between the post [[Lambaroe]] and the temporary post [[Anagaloeng]], where the bandit chief [[Habib Long]] was encamped — but that he instead intended to attack the Dutch troops unexpectedly at the most favourable opportunity.

The warning was delivered to the *contrôleur* [[Kroesen]], who was then serving with the Dutch civil administration of the *geconcentreerde linie*. The album notes that the very fact that an Atjehnese chief was willing to expose himself to the personal danger of communicating such intelligence — at a moment when nearly every other chief on the Dutch side was defecting — was itself worthy of praise, *te meer te prijzen in een inlandsch hoofd, dat hij geen gevaren ontzag om den contrôleur te waarschuwen*.

## Consequences

The Dutch response to Toekoe Ketjik Oemar's warning was immediate. The album records that the timely intelligence "did the design fail" (*deed den toeleg mislukken*) and that it allowed the *regeering* to take *onmiddellijk krachtige maatregelen* — immediate forceful measures — to anticipate the attack. The measures in question included the relief expeditions of [[April]] [[1896]] — the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] and the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]] — and the general mobilisation that followed. The album's tribute to Toekoe Ketjik Oemar — *En aan Toekoe Ketjik Oemar is het te danken, dat de regeering onmiddellijk krachtige maatregelen kon nemen, en dat het verraad van Djohan nog niet ernstiger gevolgen heeft gehad* — places him in the role of the loyal native whose individual fidelity partially redeemed the general collapse of the Dutch-*oeloebalang* alliance in [[1896]].

## Significance

Toekoe Ketjik Oemar's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the named Acehnese individual whose loyalty to the Dutch — at the moment of [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] — was singled out for praise by the Dutch popular press. His appearance in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] is in a chapter titled *Een Trouwe Inlander* — *A Faithful Native* — and his warning is presented as the proximate cause of the Dutch ability to respond effectively to the defection. The album's framing of Toekoe Ketjik Oemar as the *trouwe inlander* is itself a document of Dutch colonial racial politics: the chapter title presupposes that fidelity to the Dutch was the exception rather than the rule among the Atjehnese, and that the exception was therefore worthy of explicit record.

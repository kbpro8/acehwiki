**Commissioner-General Du Bus** — Leonard Pierre Joseph, Baron du Bus de Gisignies — served as *commissaris-generaal* (Commissioner-General) of the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] in the late 1820s. In P. H. van der Kemp's 1900 article he is mentioned in a single, but historically significant, passage: it was Du Bus who, in [[1827]], formed the plan to send the frigate *Bellona* to [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]] in order to encourage the native chiefs to trade with [[Riouw]] rather than with the British at [[Singapore]]. The episode anticipates by half a century the colonial-policy contest that would culminate in the [[Sumatra Treaty of 1871|Sumatra Treaty of 1871]].

## The 1827 Bellona Project

Van der Kemp introduces Du Bus through the report of the former resident of Singapore, John Prince, who at the end of 1827 found himself at [[Batavia]]. There he learned "that Commissioner-General Du Bus had the intention of sending the warship *Bellona* to Sumatra's East Coast, in order to encourage the chiefs there to trade with Riouw." Prince thereupon wrote to the government at [[Penang]] on 29 November 1827, in the dispatch that Van der Kemp prints as Bijlage 11. The mission, as Prince described it, was "to induce the natives to discontinue the purchase of salt from us, and to take it from Rhio where it is to be delivered at as low a rate as it can be got at the former place." The scheme was the practical Dutch reply to the [[Dutch Salt Price Reduction|salt-price competition]] that had become the chief instrument of commercial policy in the region since the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of London (1824)|treaty of 1824]].

## The [[Bellona mission to Sumatra's East Coast (1827-1828)|Bellona Mission]]

Prince warned the Penang government that "the appearance of a ship of that description carrying such a commission may make an impression on the natives of the several ports of [[Siak]], [[Kampar]], [[Jambi]] &c., unfavourable to the commercial interests of Singapore, inasmuch as they may be intimidated into a compliance with the views of the Netherlands Government and grant through fear what their interests and better reason would otherwise condemn." The mission of the *Bellona* accordingly became a flashpoint in the Anglo-Dutch commercial rivalry of the late 1820s; its outcomes are analysed in [[Robert Fullerton|Fullerton]]'s celebrated [[Fullerton's minute on Acheh (21 March 1825)|minute on Acheh]] of March 1825 and in his [[Pinang Nota (1825)|Pinang Nota]] of the same year.

## Du Bus in Context

Du Bus held the office of Commissioner-General at a moment when the Dutch colonial administration was navigating between two imperatives: the obligations imposed by the treaty of 1824, under which the Netherlands was to enjoy a free hand on [[Sumatra]] while Britain enjoyed the same on the [[Malay Peninsula|Malay peninsula]], and the financial stringency imposed by the [[Java War (1825-1830)|Java War]] of 1825–1830. The *Bellona* project — a comparatively inexpensive naval demonstration in support of a commercial policy — is characteristic of the kind of initiative a Commissioner-General could undertake while the regular military budget was consumed by the war in [[Java|Java]]. It is also one of the immediate provocations of the [[Pinang Nota (1825)|Pinang Nota]] in which Fullerton set out, with such remarkable prescience, the British reading of the 1824 treaty.

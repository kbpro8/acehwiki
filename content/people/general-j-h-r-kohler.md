**General J. H. R. Kohler** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, *generaalmajoor J. H. R. Kohler* — was the Dutch commander of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Dutch expedition against Atjèh]], which landed on the Atjèhnese coast on [[25 April]] [[1873]] and was, in Kreemer's judgement, "een totale mislukking" — "a total failure." Kohler was killed on [[14 April]] [[1873]] (Kreemer gives the date of his fall as the 14th; the landing is dated to the 5th–29th April period), a few days into the campaign, and the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]] — the operation's objective — was never taken.

## The first expedition (5–29 April 1873)

Kreemer's account of the first expedition is compressed but damning. After the Dutch declaration of war on [[26 March]] [[1873]], a force of "168 officieren en 3200 mindere militairen" — "168 officers and 3,200 lesser military men" — under Kohler's command appeared before the roadstead of Atjèh on [[25 April]] [[1873]]. "De tegenstand der Atjèhers was veel grooter dan men gemeend had te moeten verwachten, de organisatie van het leger was gebrekkig en terreinkennis van Atjèh ontbrak nagenoeg geheel" — "The resistance of the Atjèhnese was much greater than one had expected; the organisation of the army was defective, and terrain-knowledge of Atjèh was almost entirely lacking."

## Kohler's death and the failure

"Een paar dagen nadat de opperbevelhebber gesneuveld was (14 April) bleek het duidelijk, dat met de beschikbare macht het niet mogelijk was Atjèh te dwingen tot het inwilligen onzer eischen. Het operatie-object: de kraton, werd niet genomen, we werden met groote verliezen afgeslagen" — "A few days after the supreme commander had fallen (14 April) it became clear that with the available force it was not possible to compel Atjèh to grant our demands. The operation-object — the Kraton — was not taken; we were repulsed with great losses."

## Aftermath

The failure of the first expedition obliged the Indische Regeering to withdraw the troops to [[Java]] — "met het oog op het aanstaande voor militaire operatiën hoogst ongunstige jaargetijde" — "with a view to the approaching season highly unfavourable for military operations" — and to prepare a second, far larger expedition under [[Generaal van Swieten|generaal J. van Swieten]], which would take the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]]. The blockade squadron left behind to pressure the coast "had echter weinig resultaat" — "had however little result": it harmed the [[Onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]] without benefit, and could not prevent the import of war-contraband from [[Penang|Pinang]] under the so-called *licentiën* — licences.

## Significance

Kohler's death and the failure of the first expedition are, in Kreemer's narrative, the moment at which the Dutch discovered that the Atjèh sultanate would not be conquered by a single campaign-season's effort. The scale of the second expedition — "385 officieren en ongeveer 7800 minderen" — "385 officers and about 7,800 lesser men" — "een macht zooals nog nimmer te voren in onze Oost was uitgerust!" — "a force such as never before had been equipped in our East!" — is the direct military consequence of Kohler's defeat.

**I. D. E. Schmeltz** (so spelled in the OCR of *The Achehnese*; more commonly **J. D. E. Schmeltz**) was a Dutch ethnographer and editor of the ***Internationales Archiv für Ethnographie*** (*International Archive for Ethnography*), published at [[Leyden]] from [[1888]] onwards. He is cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in Vol. I of *The Achehnese* in connection with the comparative study of the [[Hasan-Husain festival]] and related popular observances in the Eastern Archipelago and southern India.

Schmeltz is mentioned in a footnote appended to Snouck Hurgronje's discussion of the [[Hasan-Husain festival]] (also called the [[Hasan-Husain feast]]) — the popular commemoration of the martyrs of [[Kerbela]] which, in the Achehnese calendar, gives its name to the month of Asan-Usen (the Achehnese form of *Hasan-Husain*). The festival is celebrated in different parts of Netherlands-India, and especially on the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coast of Sumatra]] (at [[Padang]], [[Bencoolen]], etc.), in much the same manner as it is described in the *Qanoon-e-islam* of [[Jaffur Shurreef]]. Snouck Hurgronje notes that it has been thought to have been introduced along the sea-board by the *sipalihs* (sepoys) who immigrated thither during the English domination, but argues that it is probable, "to say the least," that even previously to this a Hasan-Husain feast enjoyed much popularity both in Sumatra and elsewhere. The argument turns on the fact that the festival is celebrated to this day in [[Trumon]] in the manner customary in the [[Deccan]], that in [[Acheh]] the month is called Asan-Usen, and that the day [[Ashura]], of which orthodox Islam takes but little notice, has in Java given its name *Sura* to the month Muharram.

In order to arrive at a more definite conclusion on these questions of diffusion, Snouck Hurgronje refers the reader to "*Internationales Archiv für Ethnographie*" (Ed. I. D. E. Schmeltz), Leyden 1888, Part I, pp. 191–196. The reference shows that the *Archiv* served, in the early years of comparative ethnography of the Archipelago, as one of the principal channels through which materials on Indonesian-Indian continuities — including the diffusion of popular religious observances from the [[Deccan]] to the Malay-Indonesian world — were made available to scholars.

Schmeltz's editorial work at Leyden thus belongs to the broader scholarly apparatus on which Snouck Hurgronje drew in writing *The Achehnese*. The *Internationales Archiv für Ethnographie*, which he edited, formed part of the same Leyden-based infrastructure of colonial ethnography that included the publications of [[E. J. Brill|Brill]] and the work of [[G. A. Wilken|Wilken]], [[Dr. Brandes|Brandes]], and other scholars of the Netherlands Indies.

**Lieutenant-Colonel de Rochemont** was a retired (*gepensioneerd*) Dutch *luitenant-kolonel* and author of the [[De Rochemont brochure|brochure]] *Wat hebben partijschappen* — cited by [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] as the source for the text of [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s verbal instruction to [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] ("zooveel mogelijk vechten weinig, afwachtende houding bewaren"). Borel turns de Rochemont's own quotations against him to demolish the claim — put about by van Swieten's partisans — that Pel had merely carried out van Swieten's programme.

## The Brochure and the Instruction

De Rochemont's brochure *Wat hebben partijschappen* enz. was published in defence of van Swieten's conduct of the war. Its most consequential passage was the quotation of van Swieten's verbal instruction to Pel on his departure — the *afwachtende houding* instruction that Borel treats as the original sin of the *bezettingskorps*'s inheritance. Borel cites de Rochemont as the source of the text and uses the same brochure to refute the central claim of van Swieten's partisans: that Pel had merely carried out van Swieten's programme, and that the *stelling-Pel* and the *aanvallende* campaign of [[1874]]–[[1876]] were but the execution of van Swieten's design. Borel's argument is that the *afwachtende houding* instruction was the very opposite of the *aanvallende tactiek* Pel actually adopted; that Pel's first act on assuming command was to begin the daily bombardment of kampongs from [[28 April]] [[1874]] that van Swieten had always refused; and that the *stelling-Pel* was "een jaar te laat" — had van Swieten acted in [[1874]] what Pel achieved in [[1875]] could have been done at a fraction of the cost.

## The Pel Letter and Its Publication

De Rochemont's brochure also entered the controversy over Pel's [[Pel's letter to Jan van Swieten|particulier schrijven]] to van Swieten, in which Pel had attributed his *succes* to van Swieten's *beleid*. Van Swieten had made the letter public and used it as a *wapen* against his critics. Borel explains the letter as Pel's *nederigheid, erkentelijkheid* and *deferentie* — a private courtesy seized upon by van Swieten's partisans as a public testimonial. De Rochemont's use of the letter in his brochure is, in Borel's reading, an example of how the partisans of van Swieten were willing to use Pel's name in his lifetime to defend a system Pel had abandoned, and to suppress the *afwachtende houding* instruction that was the documentary proof of what van Swieten had actually intended.

## Significance

De Rochemont is, in Borel's narrative, the principal pamphleteer of van Swieten's cause, and his brochure is the document Borel most often cites against itself. The convergence of [[Generaal Booms|Booms]]'s "zeer juist geschreven critiek," [[Generaal Knoop|Knoop]]'s "slechts ten deele gelukt," and Borel's own participant-observation against the de Rochemont brochure establishes, in Borel's narrative, the professional military consensus on the second expedition.

**Toekoe Paya** was a chief of the [[XXVI Moekims]] who, after the Dutch [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|conquest of the XXVI Moekims]] of [[1 July|1]]–[[4 July]] [[1879]], fled with [[Toekoe Moeda Baij]], [[Toekoe Nja Hoessin]] and a following of some two hundred persons of rank to the valley of [[Lamtoba]], as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]). The name recurs in the military history of these years — a *heuvel-benting* called of Toekoe Paja had been stormed in November [[1876]] east of Groot-Atjeh — but the book of [[1889]] nowhere identifies the XXVI Moekims chief with its namesake; the identity must remain unasserted.

## Circuit of flight

With his companions he had, as the interrogations in the bivak at [[Lamtoba]] disclosed, settled outside the kampongs in a small encampment, the local population not daring to oppose the refugees; and on the approach of the troops he withdrew higher up, towards [[Kepaja Doewa]] or towards [[Selimoen]], three day-marches further in the valley of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]]. It was above all the reception given to Toekoe Paya and the other chiefs of the XXVI Moekims that made the district's punishment obligatory in the judgement of the Militaire Bevelhebber; and the persistence of the chiefs in the fastnesses beyond Selimoen gave the occasion for the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] itself.

**Colijn** — likely the later Dutch statesman Hendrikus Colijn (1869–1944), who served in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] as a young officer — was a Marechaussee commander of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] in [[Atjeh|Aceh]], one of the great patrol commanders of the *prang* (the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]). In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Colijn is the ninth-named in [[Musket]]'s list of the Marechaussee commanders — "Gilles Graafland, Van Daalen, Campioni, Christoffel, Van der Maaten, Scheepens, Watrin, Webb, Colijn, Geertsema Beckering, Darlang and the sergeants Nussy, Salowono, Pongoh … a colourful row."

## The list of Marechaussee commanders

Colijn is the ninth-named in Musket's list. The names of these famous tough guys and Marechaussee commanders "rang through the Indies like carillon music," and their "stories of brave deeds, adventures, and peculiarities went the rounds, as if they were legends."

## Identification with the later statesman

The identification of this Marechaussee commander Colijn with the later Dutch Prime Minister Hendrikus Colijn (1869–1944) is not made explicit in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* — Merac simply writes "Colijn" — but is overwhelmingly probable. Colijn (1869–1944) served in Aceh as a young officer from [[1894]] onwards, was decorated (M.W.O. 4th class in [[1907]]) for his part in the final phases of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], and was widely known in the Netherlands of [[1947]] as the former Prime Minister who had been a young officer in Aceh. Merac's readers would have recognised the name without explanation.

## Significance

Colijn is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as one of the Marechaussee commanders whose name "rang through the Indies like carillon music." The book's argument — that the names of these fellows of the old time rang through the Indies like carillon music and that their stories went the rounds, as if they were legends — is the book's frame for the colonial memory of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. Colijn is therefore one of the book's fellows of the old time: the Marechaussee commander (likely the later statesman) whose name was carillon music.

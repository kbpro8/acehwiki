**Pigafetta** — **Antonio Pigafetta** (c. [[1491]]–[[1534]]) — was the Italian chronicler of the [[Magellan|Magellan]] voyage whose *Relazione del primo viaggio intorno al mondo* records, on the authority of Moorish merchants whom he met on [[Timor|Timor]] in [[1521]], the principal external account of [[Java|Java Major]] and its cities in the immediate aftermath of the [[1521|fall of Pasej]] to the Portuguese. His record of the city ***Magepaher*** / ***Magepahor*** — which [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] read as [[Majapahit|Madjapahit of East Java]] — [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as a *bedekte taal* form for the [[Madjapahit in Langsar|Sumatran Madjapahit]] of [[Langsar|Langsar]], and his record of the king *Raia Pati Unus* as a *bedekte taal* form for the *raja* (not *radja*) [[Pati Oenoes]] of [[Demak's attack on Pasoeroewan (1546)|1546]].

## The 1521 record of Java Major

Pigafetta, on [[Timor|Timor]] in [[1521]], was told by Moorish merchants of the largest cities of [[Java|Java Major]] — *Madjapait* (which his manuscripts spell *Magepaher* or *Magepahor*), [[Sunda|Sunda]], [[Kediri|Daha]], [[Demak|Demak]], *Gadjahmada*, [[Mataram|Mentaraman]]/[[Mataram|Minutaranghan]], [[Japara|Djapara]], [[Sidayu|Sidajoe]], [[Tuban|Toeban]], [[Gresik|Gresik]], [[Surabaya|Soerabaja]], and [[Bali|Bali]] — and of the king whose name was *Raia Pati Unus*. [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] read *Magepaher*/*Magepahor* as [[Majapahit|Madjapahit of East Java]] and *Raia Pati Unus* as the *radja* Pati Oenoes of [[1513]].

## De Roo de la Faille's rejoinder

[[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] demurs. *Magepaher*/*Magepahor* is, in his reading, *Madjapah-it-l-èr* or *Madjapah-it-l-or* — the Madjapahit "in the north" (i.e. on Sumatra's east coast, at [[Langsar|Langsar]]), not the Madjapahit of East Java. *Raia Pati Unus* is *raja* (not *radja*) Pati Unus — "the glorious Pati Unus" of the [[Demak's attack on Pasoeroewan (1546)|1546 attack on Pasoeroewan]], i.e. the king of Demak whose name [[Castanheda|Castanheda]] had recorded as the attacker of Malacca in [[1513]].

## The *Minutaranghan* reading

The same Moorish merchants, Pigafetta records, gave the name *Minutaranghan* for one of the cities of Java. [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] read this as *Mentaraman* — "old-fashioned" for the realm of Mataram-Mentaraman. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] demurs: the spelling "*mi-nu-taranghan*" is, in his reading, a contraction of *sa-mi nu-sa tara-ng-an* — "like the island, the name of which savours of *tara-wangan*" — i.e. [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]]. The same *Tarawangan*, De Roo de la Faille notes, survives in the *pralambang* tradition recorded by [[Dr. J. Brandes|Brandes]] (*TBG* 32, p. 382): "an islet at the *wangan* which leads into the bay of Aroe".

## The *garuda* tree

The Moorish merchants of [[Brunei|Burne]] on Pigafetta's ship, Pigafetta records, told him of "a very huge tree found below [[Java|Java Major]] towards the gulf of China, in which live birds called garuda". The tree's place was called *Puzathaer*, the tree *cam panganghi*, its fruit *bua pangananghi* (which Pigafetta's vocabulary explains as *caui*, i.e. *kawi* — "poet-language"). [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes this as a *bedekte taal* form for the ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] — the "tree" being *taroe maha* ("the great tree"), the garuda being the bird of [[Wisnu|god Wisnu]] (*ka-wis-noe-sa*, "king of the birds" — *pek-si*, alias *si-kep*'s).

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Pigafetta's importance lies chiefly in his [[1521]] record of [[Java|Java Major]] — the principal external account of the Javanese cities in the immediate aftermath of the [[1521|fall of Pasej]] to the Portuguese — and in his record of the Moorish merchants' *bedekte taal* account of the [[Taroema|Taroema]]-[[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]-[[Aroe-baai|Aroe]] sacred complex. His *Magepaher*/*Magepahor* reading, decoded by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] as the [[Madjapahit in Langsar|Sumatran Madjapahit]], also fixes the chronology of the Sumatran Madjapahit's reduction by the Atjehnese age.

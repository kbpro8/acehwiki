**Sultanah Safiatuddin** — "Safiattadin Sjáh" in [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s spelling — was the first sultana of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], under whose government in [[1651]] a murder was committed upon the Hollanders, whereupon the Dutch decided to blockade the harbour places of Atjeh, so that "the state of war had again come in".

## The murder and the tin treaty

Under the restored friendship of [[1640]], the young successor of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] had in name wielded the sceptre over Atjeh while in fact the *[[Shahbandar of Atjeh|sjahbandars]]* ruled, driving up the harbour dues as high as possible in the hope of expelling the Hollanders from their roadsteads; the "small vexations" of the Atjehnese, laying innumerable difficulties in the way of trade, remained unpunished. But when finally, in [[1651]], under the government of the first sultana, Safiattadin Sjáh, a murder was committed on the Hollanders, the Dutch side decided to blockade the harbour places of Atjeh. The consequence was a new treaty, by which it was determined that half of the tin yield from [[Perak]] would accrue to the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|East India Company]], which would also obtain the monopoly of the trade. The Sultana consented, Langhout remarks, undoubtedly in the conviction that her countrymen would provide for the rest — "which they also did!": the trade monopoly was continually violated, and when the notion "monopoly" had apparently wholly fallen away for the Atjehnese, the Company took action, occupying [[Indrapoera]], [[Padang]] and [[Tikoe]] and concluding with the [[Minangkabau|Minangkabauers]] on the West Coast of Sumatra a treaty of exclusive trade, of which the consequence was that in [[1664]] [[Jacob Cau]] could lay in at Padang the head depot for the Dutch trade.

**C. Meulders** was a lieutenant of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]] Maréchaussee who, as commander of one brigade of the Vde divisie detached from Captain [[L. A. Boucherie|Boucherie]]'s third stelling in mid-March [[1942]], conducted a two-week guerrilla in the terrain north-west of the [[Bireuën|Bireuën]]–[[Takengon|Takengon]] road, twice attacking marching Japanese with *klewang* charges and inflicting dozens of killed, before being captured and — together with the European sergeant Kerrebijn — summarily executed by the Japanese. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Meulders's guerrilla is the principal Dutch diversionary manoeuvre of the central-Atjeh theatre and is the staff history's clearest case of a Japanese summary execution of a Dutch Maréchaussee officer captured in the field after capitulation.

## Detachment from Boucherie's stelling

The third stelling of [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]]'s three-position defence — halfway between [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] and [[Takengon|Takengon]] — was held by four brigades of the Vde divisie under Captain [[L. A. Boucherie|Boucherie]]. Captain Boucherie sent one brigade under the command of Lieutenant C. Meulders in a north-westerly direction along a footpath, with orders to report the enemy's approach from that direction and thereafter to pass over to guerrilla warfare.

## The klewang charges

From the terrain north-west of the road, between approximately [[17 March]] [[17 March 1942|17 March]] and his capture some days later, Meulders twice launched *klewang* charges against marching Japanese columns, in which the enemy lost dozens of killed. The staff history notes the Japanese troops' dependence on Atjehnese porters for the carrying of their weapons and ammunition — a dependence that, at the moment of the *klewang* charge, left many Japanese unable to react, since their weapons and ammunition were very often carried by accompanying Atjehnese, so that at the moment of the fire attack many Japanese were unable to do anything.

## Capture and execution

Together with a number of loyal men, this officer ultimately fell into Japanese hands. The Japanese released the indigenous soldiers of the detachment, but Lieutenant Meulders and the European sergeant Kerrebijn were put to death. The staff history records the execution without further detail; the use of *afgemaakt* — literally "made away with" — frames the killing as a summary execution of prisoners.

## Significance

Meulders's guerrilla is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the principal Dutch diversionary manoeuvre of the central-Atjeh theatre, and his summary execution together with sergeant Kerrebijn is the staff history's clearest single instance of the Japanese treatment of Dutch Maréchaussee officers captured in the field after [[19 March]] [[19 March 1942|19 March 1942]] — the date of [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]]'s capitulation order at Takengon. The Meulders episode is cited in the Japanese interrogation of Lt [[W. van Pesch|Van Pesch]] as one of the losses the Japanese held against the Dutch — a citation that confirms the operational effect of Meulders's guerrilla on the Japanese advance.

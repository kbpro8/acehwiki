**Teukoe Moehamad Hasan** was an Atjèhnese administrator — educated as a lawyer (*mr.* = meester in de rechten) — who served in the Dutch colonial administration before the war, was appointed republican [[Governor of Sumatra|Governor of Sumatra]] after the proclamation of the Republic in [[1945]], and was one of the principal Atjèhnese figures of the transition from Japanese occupation to Indonesian republic. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* introduces him in connection with the post-capitulation political reorganisation, when "the proclamation of the Republic and the appointment of an Acehnese, mr. Teukoe Moehamad Hasan … as republican Governor of Sumatra, gradually changed the situation" — that is, the situation created by the hesitant attitude of the Allied authorities and the *de facto* recognition of the Republic.

## Pre-war career

Piekaar introduces Teukoe Moehamad Hasan earlier in the work as one of the prominent Atjèhnese figures of the pre-war period, an administrator of the modern type whose legal training (*mr.*) distinguished him from the traditional *oelèëbalang*'s and *oelama*'s alike. His appointment to the republican governorship of Sumatra in [[1945]] reflected, in Piekaar's reading, the recognition by the Republic of the political weight of Atjèh — and of the need to incorporate the Atjèhnese leadership into the republican structure.

## The 1945 appointment

The proclamation of the Republic on [[17 August]] [[1945]] — two days after the Japanese capitulation of [[15 August]] — was followed by the appointment of Teukoe Moehamad Hasan as republican Governor of Sumatra. The appointment, Piekaar records, "gradually changed the situation" created by the Allied reoccupation: the hesitant attitude of the Allied authorities, the *de facto* recognition of the Republic, and the development of the situation in [[Medan|Medan]], where the city administration was entrusted to republican figures, all combined to give the new republican governor a political platform.

## The Amacab and the post-capitulation period

The post-capitulation period in Atjèh was shaped by the interaction of three forces: the [[Amacab|Amacab]] (Allied Military Administration Civil Affairs Branch), the Dutch civil administration under Governor of Sumatra A. I. Spits, and the republican administration under Teukoe Moehamad Hasan. Piekaar records that the plans prepared under the leadership of the Chief Co Amacab (Chief commanding officer allied military administration civil affairs branch), Governor Spits, "had to remain unused" — that is, the Dutch reoccupation plans were never implemented — because of the strength of the republican position. Teukoe Moehamad Hasan's appointment as republican governor was, in this sense, one of the principal factors that prevented the Dutch reoccupation of Sumatra.

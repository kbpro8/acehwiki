**Njakumar** was the republican police coordinator for Atjeh in [[1953]], who — acting on incoming intelligence reports — ordered guards placed on all important public buildings from [[10 September]] [[1953]], eleven days before [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]] proclaimed Atjeh's accession to the [[Negara Islam Indonesia]] on [[21 September]] [[1953]].

## Warning signs before the rebellion

Henri J. H. Alers's *Om een rode of groene Merdeka* records that in early September 1953 incoming reports made it clear to Njakumar that "something was brewing," prompting him, on 10 September, to place all important public buildings under guard. Despite this precaution, Alers notes, the government could hardly claim the rebellion came as a surprise, though it was nonetheless "a shock" — while for the general public, kept almost as poorly informed as in the colonial period, the [[Atjèh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia (1953)|proclamation of 21 September]] came as a genuine surprise. In the days before the proclamation, further incidents accumulated: an illegal shipment of firearms was discovered by bus at Pangkalan Bandan on [[11 September]], the military post at [[Kuala Simpang|Kuala Simpang]] was fired on, and by [[19 September]] the authorities had obtained a fairly complete plan for an attack on [[Kutaradja|Kota-Radja]].

**Vollaers** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[T. Tjhi' Toenòng]]

What precisely happened around T. Tjhi' Toenòng thereafter remained obscure. On [[26 January]] [[1905]] — at a time when [[H. N. A. Swart]] still governed at [[Lho' Seumawè]] — the patrol of sergeant [[Vollaers]], seventeen men, was surprised in the *[[meunasah]]* of [[Meurandéh Paja]], east of [[Lho' Soekon]], where it had neglected all security measures and bivouacked carelessly. Sixteen men were killed; only one native fusilier escaped.

### From [[Tjoet Meutia]]

After the surprise of the patrol of sergeant [[Vollaers]] at [[Meurandéh Paja]] on [[26 January]] [[1905]], T. Tjhi' Toenòng was arrested at [[H. N. A. Swart]]'s order; Swart was then transferred, [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] became Governor and converted the sentence of banishment into death. Du Croo hinted that Tjoet Meutia may have driven her husband to the act — "who had meanwhile borne him a son and had spurred him on" — but left the question open, as it remained obscure whether T. Tjhi' Toenòng was guilty at all.

### From [[T. Radja Sabi]]

T. Radja Sabi was born about [[1901]] to Tjoet Meutia and Teukoe Tjoet Moehamat, later [[T. Tjhi' Toenòng]], the excluded heir to the *oelèëbalangschap* of Keureutòë. His father was arrested at [[H. N. A. Swart]]'s order after the surprise of sergeant [[Vollaers]]' patrol at [[Meurandéh Paja]] on [[26 January]] [[1905]], and executed after [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] converted the sentence of banishment to death.

**A. Djajadiningrat** — published in the volume as "the Heer A. Djajadiningrat" — was an Indonesian author whose description of a *pesantrén* (Islamic boarding school) on Java is cited by Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]] in the chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]). His name appears at the end of Kernkamp's discussion of the higher Islamic education (the *pesantrén* on Java, the *soera* on middle Sumatra, the [[Rangkang|rangkang]] in Atjeh), in a passage that describes the reverential attitude of *pesantrén* students toward their *goeroe* (teacher): "according to a description of life in a *pesantrén* of the Heer A. Djajadiningrat, in this *Sittenseff-Mollisee* milieu, the old folk religion still peeps round the corner!" — Kernkamp's *Sittenseff-Mollisee* being a mangled OCR of what was probably *Sittenstief-mütterchen* or similar, a pointed Dutch phrase for the persistence of pre-Islamic elements inside the pious boarding-school.

## Distinction from Hoesein Djajadiningrat

A. Djajadiningrat is to be distinguished from his better-known relative [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Hoesein Djajadiningrat]] (1886–1960), the Acehnese-born Bantenese scholar who earned the first doctorate awarded to an Indonesian at Leiden University (1913) and who is the author of the critical edition of the *[[Sadjarah Banten|Sajarah Banten]]*. The two share a surname and an Islamic-scholarly milieu, but Kernkamp's text does not specify the relationship.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, A. Djajadiningrat's significance is comparative: his description of Javanese *pesantrén* life supplies the contrast against which Kernkamp reads the Atjeh [[Rangkang|rangkang]] (the Atjeh equivalent of the *pesantrén*). Where the *pesantrén* on Java is concentrated especially in Madioen and the Soerabaya region, the *rangkang* of Atjeh is found "especially on the North and East Coast." The passage in which Djajadiningrat is cited is the same passage that describes the *goeroe* as "holy" to his students and the *goeroe*'s breath and saliva as salutary — the latter an observation that Kernkamp explicitly labels a survival of "the old folk religion."

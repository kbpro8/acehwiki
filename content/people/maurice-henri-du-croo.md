**Maurice Henri du Croo** ([[8 April]] [[1887]] – [[23 April]] [[1951]]) was a Dutch colonial army officer, civil administrator, journalist, and writer who served in the [[Aceh|Atjeh]] region of the [[Netherlands East Indies]] during the [[1920s]]. He is best known for co-authoring the military book *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]* with [[Schmidt (Kolonel)|Kolonel Schmidt]], and for his pseudonymous literary works as "Brammetje" and "Abraham Exodus". His career provides insight into the Dutch military and administrative presence in [[Aceh]] during the late colonial period, and his writings form part of the Dutch colonial literature on the [[Aceh War]] and its aftermath.

## Early Life and Education

Maurice Henri du Croo was born on [[8 April]] [[1887]] in [[Arnhem]], the Netherlands. He attended the H.B.S. (*Hogere Burgerschool*, higher civic school) in Arnhem. From [[1906]] to [[1909]] he studied at the [[Koninklijke Militaire Academie]] (Royal Military Academy) in [[Breda]], and was commissioned as a *2e Luitenant* (second lieutenant) in July [[1909]].

## Military Career in the Netherlands East Indies

In [[1910]] du Croo departed for the [[Netherlands East Indies]] as an officer of the [[K.N.I.L.]] (*Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger*, Royal Netherlands East Indies Army). In [[1911]] he was placed on non-activity at his own request and became an editor for the newspaper *De Locomotief* in [[Semarang]]. He returned to active duty in [[1913]] and served successively in [[Timor]] ([[1913]]), [[Endeh]] ([[1914]]), [[Koepang]] ([[1915]]), [[Soembawa]] ([[1917]]), and [[Bandoeng]] ([[1919]]). In [[1920]] he was detached as a student to the [[Hogere Krijgsschool]] (Higher Military School) in [[Den Haag]]; promoted to *Kapitein* in [[1921]], he completed his military studies on [[31 December]] [[1923]] with the classification "no. 2 Category A" and returned to the Indies. In [[1924]] he was attached to the [[Navy]] in [[Soerabaja]].

## Service in Atjeh and Civil Administration

In [[1925]] du Croo, at his own request, was posted to the [[Buitengewesten]] (Outer Provinces) — specifically to [[Aceh|Atjeh]]. There he participated in patrols in the *rimboe* (jungle) and was noted for maintaining a proper appearance even in the field. In [[1927]] he was appointed *Stafofficier* (staff officer) and was awarded the [[Ridder O.N.O. met de Zwaarden]] (Knight of the [[Orde van Oranje-Nassau]] with Swords), a decoration that recognised his service in the ongoing [[Aceh War]].

Alongside his military duties, du Croo was several times charged with the exercise of civil government (*civielbestuur*), including postings in the [[small Sunda Islands]] and in Atjeh. Fellow officer J. K. den Heijer recalled encountering him in Atjeh in the [[1920s]] "on patrol in the jungle, … a well-dressed man, not robust but tough, fit to go to a meeting or pay a visit in the same suit." Du Croo's experiences in Atjeh directly informed his later literary work, especially *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]*.

## Later Military and Administrative Roles

After a leave in [[1931]], du Croo returned to the Indies in [[1932]] and was promoted to *Majoor* in [[1933]]. He served in [[Magelang]] in [[1934]] and was promoted to *Luitenant-Kolonel* in [[1935]]. He retired in [[1939]] with the rank of *Kolonel* and was appointed head of the [[Luchtbeschermingsdienst]] (Air Protection Service) to organise it; on [[1 June]] [[1939]] he was relieved of that function at his own request and repatriated definitively to the Netherlands.

## Literary and Journalistic Work

Throughout his career du Croo wrote for newspapers such as *De Locomotief*, the *Soerabajaasch Handelsblad*, and the *Bataviaasch Nieuwsblad*. He published strategic articles in the *Indisch Militair Tijdschrift*. From [[1940]] to [[1942]] he was editor of the periodical *Oost en West*. Under the pseudonyms "Brammetje" and "Abraham Exodus" he produced a body of literary work drawing on his colonial experiences. His collaboration with [[Schmidt (Kolonel)|Kolonel Schmidt]] produced two military-historical volumes: *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]* and *[[Generaal Swart (book)|Generaal Swart]]* — the latter a biography of the [[Aceh War]] general [[H. N. A. Swart|General Swart]].

## Significance

Maurice Henri du Croo is significant as a figure who combined active military service in [[Aceh]] during the late colonial period with a literary and journalistic output that preserved and interpreted the Dutch experience of the [[Aceh War]] for a metropolitan readership. His career spans the transition from the active pacification era into the inter-war "consolidation" period, and his writings — particularly *[[Marechaussée in Atjeh (book)|Marechaussée in Atjeh]]* — constitute a primary source for the mentality and self-image of the [[K.N.I.L.]] officer corps in the late colonial Atjeh. His co-authored biography *[[Generaal Swart (book)|Generaal Swart]]* likewise preserves the memory of the [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] administration under which the Aceh War was brought to its formal conclusion.

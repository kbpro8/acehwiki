**A. Pruys van der Hoeven** was a Dutch colonial administrator, last resident of [[Palembang|Palembang]], who was appointed government commissioner together with [[K. van der Heyden|Major-general K. van der Heyden]] in [[1880]] to draft the new organisation of the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, and who on [[6 April]] [[6 April]] [[1881]] was appointed the first civil governor of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden, with Colonel [[J. F. Haus|J. F. Haus]] taking the military command. The discharge of Van der Heyden and the appointment of Pruys van der Hoeven in his place was one of the last administrative acts of [[J. W. van Lansberge|Governor-General Van Lansberge]].

## The Commission, 1880-1881

For the definitive regulation of the administration in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], Van der Heyden was placed in commission with Pruys van der Hoeven as government commissioners. They were charged, in joint consultation, "to arrange and propose everything necessary to arrive at an adequate organisation of the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, in connection with the political situation and the relation of the various Onderhoorigheden to our administration." The assistant-resident of Groot-Atjeh was attached to them as secretary for that assignment.

Pruys van der Hoeven arrived in Atjeh on [[21 February]] [[21 February]] [[1880]] and visited in succession the territory occupied by the Dutch, both in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and in the vazal staten. On [[10 July]] [[10 July]] the commissioners declared their task ended.

## The Administrative Organisation of March 1881

After in an extraordinary meeting of the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad of Nederlandsch-Indië]] with both commissioners a further discussion had taken place, and the Governor-General had taken several decisions in preparation, the governor was yet charged with the submission of new proposals regarding the organisation of the inland administration in Great Acheh, based on an investigation into the existing situation and the institutions under the sultans. In accordance with the proposals submitted, the definitive administrative organisation of Atjeh came into force in [[March 1881|March 1881]]. According to this organisation, the administration of the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden would rest with a governor, whose residence would be at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. Under him would stand three assistant-residents and ten controleurs. The assistant-residencies were:

1. that of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] with Kota Radja as chief place, in which direct Dutch administration was introduced;
2. that of the [[Noord- en Oostkust|Noord- en Oostkust]] with [[Telok Semawe|Telok Semawé]] as chief place, with controleurs at [[Edi|Edi]], [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] and [[Segli|Segli]];
3. that of the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]] with [[Malaboeh|Malaboeh]] as chief place.

## The Civil Governorship, 6 April 1881

By the Governor-General's decision, by virtue of authority already granted by the King in [[1879]], it was decided to separate the civil and military commands and to entrust the civil administration in Atjeh to a civil governor. The Lieutenant-general Van der Heyden was honourably discharged from his functions, and Pruys van der Hoeven was appointed civil governor; the military command was entrusted to Colonel [[J. F. Haus|J. F. Haus]] of the infantry. Both assumed their functions on [[6 April]] [[6 April]] [[1881]].

## Significance

The discharge of Van der Heyden — "which set many pens in motion, and also gave rise to discussions in the [[Staten-Generaal|States-General]]" — was controversial. The appointment of Pruys van der Hoeven, a civil administrator rather than a military officer, marked the formal transition of Atjeh from a military command to a regular administrative gewest of Netherlands India. The [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] was by no means over, however, and was to continue for more than two decades in the form of guerrilla resistance, especially in the upper lands of [[Pedir|Pedir]] and the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]].

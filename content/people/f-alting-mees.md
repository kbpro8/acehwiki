**F. Alting Mees** was a Dutch colonial minister who served from [[September]] [[1876]] to [[October]] [[1877]], a short but significant period in the [[Aceh War]] during Governor-General [[J.W. van Lansberge]]'s administration. Born in [[1819]], he was an experienced colonial official who had previously served as President of the [[Java Bank]] and President of the [[High Court of Justice]] in [[Batavia]].

## Early Career

Alting Mees began his legal career in [[Batavia]] in [[1845]] and served in various judicial and financial roles, including Procureur-Generaal, President of the [[Java Bank]] ([[1870]]-[[1873]]), and President of the [[High Court of Justice]] ([[1873]]-[[1876]]). He was appointed Minister of Colonial Affairs in [[September]] [[1876]], succeeding [[W. van Goltstein]].

## Policy and Administration

Alting Mees' brief tenure was characterized by:

### Aceh Policy

Alting Mees took a cautious approach to [[Aceh]], urging Van Lansberge to be patient. He warned against over-optimism about a quick end to the war, stating that "we are still far from where we need to be" and that "facts, manifesting themselves in lower expenditure and simplification of our position, must be established before we can point with satisfaction to the entry of a new era."

Key aspects of his Aceh policy included:
- Support for Van Lansberge's visit to Aceh in [[1877]], though he noted the King and Van Goltstein were not supportive.
- Advocacy for a defensive posture, arguing that "it is the question whether by force of arms we can achieve what, according to history, must also be a matter of time."
- Support for the Pel Plan to isolate Great Aceh from the sea.
- Recognition of the need for stronger military action against [[Simpang Oelim]] when necessary.

### Financial Policy

Alting Mees dealt with the financial strains of the war, noting that "the Atjeh war threatens to disturb the financial balance." He supported:
- The introduction of patent and personal taxes on Europeans.
- The construction of the [[Tanjung Priok]] harbor.
- The extension of state railways on [[Java]].
- The need for "self-limitation" and "economy" in the Indies.

He was less enthusiastic about radical reform of the corvée (heerendiensten), advocating for gradual change.

### Personnel Policy

Alting Mees was concerned about the quality of colonial officials, particularly in the judiciary. He advocated for:
- Better appointment procedures, including confidential reports on candidates.
- Improved training and career prospects for judicial officials.
- The introduction of a separate recruitment depot for the colonial army.

### The Conversion Question

Alting Mees was cautious about the conversion of communal land to individual ownership on [[Java]], though he was more open to it than some conservatives. He saw it as a matter that should be left to time and local circumstances.

## Relationship with Van Lansberge

Alting Mees initially had a strained relationship with Van Lansberge, partly because of Van Lansberge's frustration with the slow progress in Aceh. However, their relationship improved over time, with Alting Mees eventually providing "his full support" to Van Lansberge.

## Resignation

Alting Mees' ministry ended in [[October]] [[1877]] when the entire cabinet resigned over the education question. His resignation was not a result of any colonial policy disagreement but rather the broader political crisis in the [[Netherlands]].

## Legacy

Alting Mees' brief tenure established key directions for the [[Aceh]] policy, including the need for patience and the eventual transition to civil governance. His financial and personnel policies contributed to the modernization of the colonial state.

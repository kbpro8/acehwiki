**J. Schaap** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. N. A. Swart]]

As a private citizen Swart returned to the Netherlands, settling at [[Den Haag]], but Atjeh retained his warm and continuous interest. When resistance broke out again on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|west coast]] of Atjeh in [[1925]], his opinion was sought in Holland and reported by the journalist [[J. Schaap]], formerly editor-in-chief of the *Java-Bode*, in *[[The Weekly of an Amsterdammer]]* of [[14 September|14]] and [[21 September]] [[1927]] — a statement du Croo called "Swart's Atjehsch testament".

### From [[Westkust van Atjeh]]

It was again on the west coast that resistance broke out in [[1925]], seven years after Swart's retirement — the occasion on which Swart's opinion was sought in Holland and recorded by [[J. Schaap]] as "Swart's Atjehsch testament." Du Croo attributed the outbreak to the failure of the condition Swart had omitted to state: "provided that the leading civil officials and military men do not spoil my work."

### From [[Atjehtram]]

In [[1927]] Swart, then long retired, told the journalist [[J. Schaap]] that there was some danger that communism might be introduced into Atjeh along the Atjehtram, "for on this tramway line five hundred kilometres long almost only Acehnese work as track coolies, and it is not impossible that people among the staff may know how to work upon them; but if the higher staff act rightly, this danger can be reduced. If only one knows how to keep the people's confidence."

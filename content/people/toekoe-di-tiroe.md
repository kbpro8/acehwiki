**Toekoe di Tiroe** (also written Toekoe di Tiro) was a self-reliant chief in [[Pedir|Pedir]] on the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], particularly hostile to the Dutch throughout the late [[1870s]], who repeatedly attacked the Dutch post at [[Segli|Segli]] and whose stronghold at [[Garouw|Garouw]] was the object of an expedition under [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] in [[May 1878|May 1878]]. His band was still active at [[Lampisang|Lampisang]] in [[August 1879|August 1879]], where it awaited and gave serious opposition to the Dutch column on its way to [[Selimoen|Selimoen]].

## The Resistance at Pedir

When in [[1876]] the Dutch decided to set up a fortification at [[Pedir|Pedir]] and to place an official there under the protection of a sufficient military force, they met all kinds of opposition and difficulties from the population, "among whom a self-reliant chief in Pedir, Toekoe di Tiroe, proved particularly hostile." After the arrival of military reinforcements in [[June 1876|June 1876]] the resistance gave way, and by the end of that month the post was in a sufficient state. It later proved, however, to be less favourably situated for the supervision of trade, as continually great quantities of rice were imported, which seemed to find their way to the enemy. In [[February 1877|February 1877]] it was decided to move the establishment from Pedir to Segli.

## The Expedition to Gighen, May 1878

In [[May 1878|May 1878]] the governor [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] led an expedition to [[Gighen|Gighen]] and the upper lands of Pedir, where Toekoe di Tiroe remained hostile and the post at Segli had been attacked by a great force. The actual object of the expedition — the kampong [[Garouw|Garouw]], the headquarters of Toekoe di Tiroe — was not reached, but the taking of [[Telok Kadjoe|Telok Kadjoe]], which had been mistaken for Garouw, had the consequence that the radja of Gighen offered his submission and promised the surrender of Toekoe di Tiroe.

## The Resistance at Lampisang, August 1879

After the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman on 13 October 1878]] and the campaigns of [[1879]], the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of [[13 August]] [[13 August]] – [[15 August]] [[15 August]] [[1879]] was undertaken. The column from [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]] over [[Gleiêng|Gleiêng]] along the river met the most serious opposition at [[Lampisang|Lampisang]], "where Toekoe di Tiroe with his band had awaited it." The column had already arrived at Selimoen the day before the other, but its opposition at Lampisang was the most serious of the expedition.

## Significance

Toekoe di Tiroe's persistent resistance at Pedir and his survival as a guerrilla leader through the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of [[August 1879|August 1879]] illustrate the limits of Dutch authority in the upper lands of Pedir. The name "Toekoe di Tiro" — the title of a series of religious-military leaders of the resistance in later phases of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], of whom the most famous is [[Teungku Tiro|Teungku Tiro]] — suggests a possible connection, but the source work does not explicitly identify him with the later leader.

**C. G. A. C. Krohne** was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] lieutenant-adjudant of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] — born in [[Germany]] but, as the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] notes, an *sieraden van het Nederlandsch Indische leger* — an ornament of the Netherlands Indies army — who was mortally wounded on [[4 May]] [[1896]] during an *excursie* (reconnaissance) towards the [[Kroeng Kali]] in the [[XXVI Moekims]] and died of his wounds on the same day. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as one of the seven officers killed in the spring [[1896]] operations, and his portrait is one of the six reproduced in the album's closing plates.

## The reconnaissance at Kroeng Kali

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Krohne was wounded on [[4 May]] [[1896]] — *gewond op 4 Mei bij eene excursie naar Kroeng Kali (XXVI Moekims)* — *wounded on 4 May during an excursion to Kroeng Kali (XXVI Moekims)* — and that he died of the wound on the same day. The *excursie* was a reconnaissance in force towards the river [[Kroeng Kali]] in the [[XXVI Moekims]] — the northernmost of the three *sagis* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — and it was undertaken in the period between the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[9 April]] [[1896]] and the [[Storming of Lampisang (May 1896)|storming of Lampisang]] of [[23 May]] [[1896]]. The engagement at Kroeng Kali was, in the album's chronology, one of the operations by which the Dutch extended their control over the territory between the besieged temporary posts and the *geconcentreerde linie*.

## The German-born officer

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] notes that Krohne — like [[C. G. A. C. Krohne|Krohne]] and the other German-born officer killed in the spring [[1896]] operations — was a *Duitscher van geboorte* — *German by birth* — but, the album insists, *een sieraden van het Nederlandsch Indische leger* — *an ornament of the Netherlands Indies army*. The album's emphasis on Krohne's German origin, and on the simultaneous insistence that he was an ornament of the Dutch Indies army, is a document of the multi-ethnic composition of the colonial officer corps of the late nineteenth century, in which officers of German, Belgian, French and Swiss origin served alongside native Dutchmen. The same emphasis is found in the album's treatment of [[H. O. W. L. Kramer|Captain Kramer]], whose death at [[Storming of Lampisang (May 1896)|Lampisang]] three weeks after Krohne's is recorded in the same chapter.

## Burial at Kota Potjoet

The album records that Krohne was buried at the cemetery of [[Kota Potjoet]], whose entrance bears the inscription *Aan onze Kameraden, gevallen op het Veld van Eer*.

## Significance

Krohne's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the only officer of the spring [[1896]] operations killed in the [[XXVI Moekims]] rather than in the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]] or [[XXV Moekims]] — the northernmost of the three *sagis*. His death on [[4 May]] [[1896]] — between the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] and the [[Storming of Lampisang (May 1896)|storming of Lampisang]] — establishes the chronology of the Dutch recovery in the XXVI Moekims, and the album's emphasis on his German birth is a document of the multi-ethnic composition of the colonial officer corps.

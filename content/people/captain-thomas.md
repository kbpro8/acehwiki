**Captain Thomas** was a British sea-captain, mentioned in the letter of the [[Sultan Alauddin Muhammad Shah Jauhar|Sultan of Acheh]] to [[Warren Hastings]] of 7 November [[1786]] as one of three Europeans — together with [[Captain Glass]] and [[John Bird|Mr. John Bird]] — "who are acquainted with my affairs and with my intention towards you." He is named once in [[P. H. van der Kemp]] (1900), in Appendix 1.

## The 1786 letter

After the murder of [[Captain Hobbs]] at [[Tringano]], the Sultan had been unable to find anyone whom he could entrust with his reply to Hastings. He therefore proposed that Hastings himself should seek information from the three Europeans — Captain Glass, Captain Thomas and Mr. John Bird — who had personal knowledge of the Achehnese court and of the Sultan's intentions. The relevant passage runs: "You will ask captain Glass, captain Thomas and Mr. John Bird, who are acquainted with my affairs and with my intention towards you."

## Significance

The three names are interesting to Van der Kemp chiefly as evidence that, before the formal British approach through [[Captain Steven]], the Achehnese court had had substantive dealings with several English traders and captains — enough for the Sultan to identify them, individually, as men who could speak to his purposes. The fragmentary reference is the only mention of Captain Thomas in the article; nothing further is recorded of his person, his ship, or the nature of his "acquaintance" with Acheh.

Van der Kemp prints the 1786 letter as the first of his twelve appendices because it is the earliest documentary evidence of an Achehnese desire for "lasting friendship with the English" — a phrase that foreshadows the language of the [[Treaty of Pedir]] of [[1819]] and the broader [[Acheh treaty of 1819]].

**Sultan Ibrahim of Aceh** reigned over the Sultanate of [[Acheh]] from [[1585]] to [[1620]], overseeing a period of literary flourishing that included the patronage of Malay translations of Arabic devotional texts such as the *Burda*.

## Biography
Born into the royal house of Aceh, Ibrahim succeeded his father [[Sultan Alauddin Mansur Syah]] and continued the expansionist policies of his predecessors. He maintained diplomatic contacts with the Ottoman Empire and the Malay world.

## Cultural Patronage
Sultan Ibrahim is recorded as a patron of the Malay translation of the *Qasida al‑Burda* (c. [[1550]]). Court poets were commissioned to render the Arabic verses into Jawi, thereby embedding the poem in Acehnese religious life.

## Military and Political Activities
His reign saw the consolidation of Acehnese control over the western coast of Sumatra and the intensification of conflicts with the Portuguese and later the Dutch East India Company. The sultan used religious poetry to rally troops against foreign incursions.

## Legacy
Ibrahim’s support for Islamic literature contributed to the lasting prestige of Acehnese religious poetry. The Malay *Burda* remained a staple of devotional practice well into the 19th century.

**The Sultan of Yogyakarta** was the Javanese Sultan who purchased the transport elephant [[Agam]] from the colonial army's [[transport-olifanten (Aceh)|elephant transport]] in [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), the Sultan of Yogyakarta is the book's marker of the purchase of Agam: the Javanese Sultan who bought the male transport elephant before the elephant's serious disagreement with his mahout and his wild behaviour led to his being put down by the firing-pony at [[Tangsé]].

## The Purchase of Agam

"Agam had been purchased by the Sultan of Yogyakarta, but before he could be brought to Medan to be transported onward to Java, he had a serious disagreement with his mahout and behaved so wildly that there was nothing for it but to put him down." Agam had been purchased by the Sultan of Yogyakarta, but before he could be brought to Medan and on to Java, he had a serious disagreement with his mahout and behaved so wildly that there was nothing for it but to put him down. The Sultan of Yogyakarta's purchase of Agam — and Agam's wild behaviour that led to his being put down — is the book's marker of the elephant transport's purchase: the Javanese Sultan who bought the male transport elephant but who never received him, because Agam was put down at Tangsé before he could be transported to Java.

## Significance

The Sultan of Yogyakarta is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the Javanese Sultan who purchased the transport elephant [[Agam]]. The book's argument — that the Sultan of Yogyakarta purchased Agam but that Agam was put down at Tangsé before he could be transported to Java — is the book's marker of the elephant transport's purchase and of Agam's being put down. The Sultan of Yogyakarta is therefore the book's marker of the purchase of Agam: the Javanese Sultan who never received the male transport elephant he had purchased.

**M. J. Tiele** was the Deli tobacco agronomist who introduced the practice of picking individual leaves rather than harvesting the whole tobacco plant, and who made fundamental improvements to soil preparation that Volker credits as "the first steps toward perfected modern cultivation" (*"de eerste schreden naar de volmaakte moderne cultuur"*) in the Deli tobacco industry.

## Agronomic innovations

The tobacco culture that [[Jacobus Nienhuys|Nienhuys]] established on the East Coast of Sumatra from [[1863]] onward was initially practiced by pulling or cutting the whole plant. Tiele's leaf-picking method — harvesting each leaf individually as it reached optimal maturity — increased the quality and consistency of the wrapper leaf that made [[Deli]] tobacco internationally renowned. His improvements to soil preparation, recorded in Dr. T. Volker's survey of [[1928]], similarly contributed to the evolution of Deli cultivation from a pioneering experiment into the systematized operation of the late-colonial period.

Volker places Tiele's innovations in the context of the [[1870s]]–[[1880s]] period, when the Deli tobacco belt was expanding rapidly under the leadership of the [[Deli Maatschappij]] and the organizational framework provided by the [[Deli Planters Vereeniging]] (founded [[23 April]] [[1879]]).

## Significance

Tiele's work belongs to the wider history of agronomic improvement in the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust]], a history that also included the creation of the first Arbeidsinspectie (Labour Inspection Service) in [[1907]] and the gradual transformation of the tobacco belt from a high-risk frontier enterprise into a regulated colonial economy. The leaf-picking technique he pioneered remained standard practice in the Deli tobacco industry for decades.

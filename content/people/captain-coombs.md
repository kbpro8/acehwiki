Captain Coombs (later Major Coombs) was a British East India Company officer who, in [[1817]]–[[1818]] and again in [[1819]], served as a commissioner to [[Acheh]]. He was the co-commissioner with [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] on the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission of 1819]], in which capacity he opposed Raffles's pro-[[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] policy and upheld the rights of the rival sultan [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf ul-Alum]].

## First Acheh Mission (1817–1818)

Still during the lifetime of [[Governor Petrie]], Captain Coombs was dispatched at the end of [[1817]] or the beginning of [[1818]] on [[Captain Coombs' first Acheh mission (1817-1818)|a commission to Acheh]] to enquire who was the rightful sultan. He was told by the chiefs that they had forever renounced [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] and recognised [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] as their sultan. A few days before Coombs's arrival a British brig had been cut out by [[Tunku Pakih|Tuku Pakih of Pedir]] ([[Capture of British brig by Tunku Pakih]]); Coombs took the matter up with the chiefs, who expressed indignation and promised to call Pakih to account. After a fourteen-day stay Coombs left the «ungenerous port» in February [[1818]], convinced that Syf was the recognised sovereign but that the British government would have to support him with at least a thousand men if he were to maintain his authority. He then proceeded to [[Calcutta]] to make his report, in accordance with the wishes of the Penang government.

## Co-Commissioner with Raffles (1819)

When, early in [[1819]], [[Lord Moira|Lord Moira's]] government determined to send out a regular commission to Acheh, Coombs, who was still at Calcutta pressing the claims of [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]], was naturally selected as Raffles's colleague. The Bengal government, unwilling to decide between the conflicting recommendations of Raffles (who favoured [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]]) and Coombs (who favoured Syf), entrusted the elder commissioner Raffles with a casting vote, naively trusting — in the words of their report — that «the integrity and judgement of the Commissioners would prevent any improper bias».

The joint commission reached [[Penang]] and immediately collided with [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Governor Bannerman]] over the conduct of the mission. Coombs and Bannerman, who were in agreement, held that the commission ought to proceed directly to the Achehnese capital and treat with whichever party should be found in power. [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]], on the contrary, insisted on calling first at [[Pedir]] on [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]], and to that end had already — without consulting Coombs — [[Dispatch of the Minto to Pedir|dispatched the ship *Minto*]] under [[Lieutenant Criddle]]. Raffles then compounded the offence by suddenly quitting Penang on [[19 January 1819]] for the [[Karimon Islands]] with [[Major William Farquhar|Major Farquhar]] ([[Raffles' detour to Karimon Islands]]), leaving behind only a written notification for his colleague.

## The Acheh Negotiations and Their Sequel

After Raffles's return on [[16 February 1819]] the commission, eventually sailing without the military force Bannerman had desired, anchored off [[Banda Acheh|the Achehnese capital]] on [[14 March 1819]]. There, during a stay of nearly seven weeks, Raffles and Coombs carried on what Raffles himself, in a letter to [[Sir Robert Harry Inglis|Sir Robert Harry Inglis]] of [[12 June 1819]], described as a [[paper war|«paper war»]] said to have occupied more than a thousand pages of the Company's largest-sized paper. Coombs continued to maintain that the commission should treat with [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]], who had been recognised by the [[Three Sagis|three Sagis]]; Raffles, with the casting vote, eventually decided for [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]]. Coombs, finding his arguments overruled, came round — at least formally — to his colleague's opinion, and on [[22 April 1819]] the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)]] was signed.

Although Coombs had been a joint commissioner, contemporary newspaper accounts of the treaty made little of his share in it; the *Asiatic Journal* remarked only at the end of a long eulogy of Raffles that «in the arrangement concluded at Acheen Sir Stamford was associated in a commission with Maj. Coombs as joint agents and representatives of the Gov.-Gen.». The Bengal government's report of [[7 October 1820]] substantially vindicated Coombs's reading of the instructions, observing that his and Bannerman's interpretation was the correct one and that Raffles's plan to go first to Pedir «would undoubtedly have been decidedly contrary to the spirit of our instructions».

**Si Seuna** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Nja' Ali]]

The easiest way to play his own judge was to gather about him a band of men who likewise had grievances against the administration or their chiefs. He found them in [[Tgk. Machmoed van Lam No]], a former *teungkoe-meunasah* who had quarrelled with his *oelèëbalang* over his share in the *[[zakat]]*; in [[Si Seuna]], whose wife had shortly before been unlawfully taken from him by the same *oelèëbalang*; and in [[Pang Abaih]], a former band-head "for whom the life of the jungle still evidently held a certain attraction." The four gathered some youths, also *kaom*-fellows, about them.

### From [[Resistance in Daja and Lhong (1914)]]

"Taking the Acehnese national character into account, he now needed to let nothing restrain him from playing his own judge. Dat zou het gemakkelijkst kunnen geschieden door om zich een bende te verzamelen van lieden, die eveneens grieven tegen het bestuur of hun hoofden hadden. Die vond hij in [[Tgk. Machmoed van Lam No]], een vroegeren *teungkoe-meunasah*, die met zijn *oelèëbalang* ("territorial chief") in onmin was geraakt over zijn aandeel in de *[[zakat]]* (godsdienstige belasting), en [[Si Seuna]], wien kort tevoren door denzelfden oelèëbalang onwettig zijn vrouw ontnomen was. Als vierde sloot zich aan [[Pang Abaih]], een gewezen *bendehoofd* ("band-leader"), for whom the life of the jungle still evidently held a certain attraction. Dit viertal wist eenige jongelingen, ook *kaom*genooten (stamverwanten), om zich heen te verzamelen."

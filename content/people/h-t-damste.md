**H. T. Damsté** — Mr. H. T. Damsté — was a Dutch colonial scholar and administrator, one of the principal authorities on Atjèh cited by [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]] in the *Bibliography* of *Atjèh and the War with Japan* alongside [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]], [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]], and Zentgraaff, who served as Piekaar's principal collaborator in the preparation of the work, "constantly supported me with advice and assistance and was kind enough to read through the manuscript and provide it with notes." Piekaar's Foreword singles out Damsté as the figure to whom the author is "very much indebted," the colleague whose "special insight into Acehnese relations and conditions" was placed at the disposal of the work. By [[1940]] he was a member of the Board (*Bestuur*) of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië|Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië]], the central metropolitan institution of Dutch Indies scholarship.

## The scholarly canon on Atjèh

Damsté's place in the canon of Atjèh scholarship is established by [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]]'s reference, in the Foreword, to "the works of Dr. Snouck Hurgronje, Damsté, Kreemer, Jongejans, and Zentgraaff listed in the 'Bibliography'." The five names — [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], Damsté, [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]], [[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]], and Zentgraaff — constituted, in Piekaar's reading, the authoritative scholarly literature on Atjèh to which the interested reader could turn for further detail on the local circumstances described in Chapters III and V of *Atjèh and the War with Japan*. Damsté's contribution to this canon — alongside his role as Piekaar's collaborator — placed him at the intersection of scholarship and administration that characterised the Dutch colonial engagement with Atjèh.

## Collaborator on the manuscript

Damsté's role as [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]]'s collaborator extended beyond the citation of his published work. Piekaar's Foreword records that "Mr. H.T. Damsté … constantly supported me with advice and assistance and was kind enough to read through the manuscript and provide it with notes." The reading and annotation of the manuscript — a service that combined substantive expertise with editorial care — was, in Piekaar's account, indispensable to the work's completion. The collaboration between Piekaar and Damsté reflected the continuity of the Dutch scholarly-administrative tradition on Atjèh: the older scholar, whose own work had helped to establish the canon, reading and annotating the manuscript of the younger.

## Member of the Board of the Koninklijk Instituut

By [[1940]], Damsté was a member of the Board (*Bestuur*) of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië|Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië]] at [['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]], alongside Prof. Dr. N. Krom (Chairman), Prof. Dr. Ph. S. van Ronkel (Vice-Chairman), Mr. E. E. Menten (Treasurer), Dr. F. W. Stapel (Secretary), and the members Prof. Dr. J. P. B. de Josselin de Jong, Dr. Alb. C. Kruyt, C. H. de Goeje, Mr. F. D. E. van Ossenbruggen, Prof. Dr. J. Gonda, Prof. Dr. C. C. Berg, and Prof. Mr. J. J. Schrieke. His presence on the Board — the principal Dutch authority on Atjèh in the central metropolitan institution of Dutch Indies scholarship — is a marker of the Institute's engagement with Atjèh scholarship, and it connects the Institute directly to the [[1939]] publication of his edition of the [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh]] in the Institute's *[[Bijdragen tot de Taal, Land en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch Indie|B.K.I.]]*.

## Additional account from *Literatuur-Overzicht* ([[1940]]) — the 1939 edition of the Seven Sleepers

In [[1939]], Damsté published in the *[[Bijdragen tot de Taal, Land en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch Indie|B.K.I.]]*, deel 98, p. 407 *e.v.*, the edition *[[De legende van de heilige zeven slapers in het Atjehsch (Damsté, 1939)|De legende van de heilige zeven slapers in het Atjehsch]]* — "The legend of the holy Seven Sleepers in Atjehnese" — the standard edition of the Atjehnese version of the Seven Sleepers legend, with Dutch translation and scholarly apparatus. The Atjehsch text was published separately, as **Tekst A** of the edition, under the title [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh]] — "The Tale of the Seven" (from Acehnese *tujōh*, "seven") — issued as an offprint (overdruk) from deel 98 of the *B.K.I.* by [[M. Nijhoff|M. Nijhoff]] of [['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] in [[1939]]. The article and the separate text are recorded as Nos. 560, 561, and 570 in [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]]'s *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939]]* (The Hague, [[1940]]), in the Sumatra chapter. The triple entry — article, cross-reference, and separate text — reflects the bibliographic care with which Van Meurs recorded the separate offprint publication of the Atjehsch text.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, H. T. Damsté is significant as one of the principal Dutch authorities on Atjèh — a member of the [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]] canon, a collaborator on the *Atjèh and the War with Japan* manuscript, and a member of the Board of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië|Koninklijk Instituut]]. His [[1939]] edition of the [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh]] — the Seven Sleepers legend in Atjehsch — is the standard published source for the Atjehnese version of the legend, and it illustrates the place of Atjehsch text-edition in the late-colonial Dutch philological enterprise. His presence on the Board of the Koninklijk Instituut connects the Institute directly to the Atjèh scholarship that is the source of the corpus's [[1939]] entries.

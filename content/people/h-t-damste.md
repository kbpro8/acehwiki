**H. T. Damsté** was a Dutch colonial scholar and administrator — one of the principal authorities on Atjèh cited by Piekaar in the *Bibliography* of *Atjèh and the War with Japan* alongside [[Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], Kreemer, [[Jongejans|Jongejans]], and Zentgraaff — who served as Piekaar's principal collaborator in the preparation of the work, "constantly supported me with advice and assistance and was kind enough to read through the manuscript and provide it with notes." Piekaar's Foreword singles out Damsté as the figure to whom the author is "very much indebted," the colleague whose "special insight into Acehnese relations and conditions" was placed at the disposal of the work.

## The scholarly canon on Atjèh

Damsté's place in the canon of Atjèh scholarship is established by Piekaar's reference, in the Foreword, to "the works of Dr. Snouck Hurgronje, Damsté, Kreemer, Jongejans, and Zentgraaff listed in the 'Bibliography'." The five names — Snouck Hurgronje, Damsté, Kreemer, Jongejans, and Zentgraaff — constituted, in Piekaar's reading, the authoritative scholarly literature on Atjèh to which the interested reader could turn for further detail on the local circumstances described in Chapters III and V of *Atjèh and the War with Japan*. Damsté's contribution to this canon — alongside his role as Piekaar's collaborator — placed him at the intersection of scholarship and administration that characterised the Dutch colonial engagement with Atjèh.

## Collaborator on the manuscript

Damsté's role as Piekaar's collaborator extended beyond the citation of his published work. Piekaar's Foreword records that "Mr. H.T. Damsté … constantly supported me with advice and assistance and was kind enough to read through the manuscript and provide it with notes." The reading and annotation of the manuscript — a service that combined substantive expertise with editorial care — was, in Piekaar's account, indispensable to the work's completion. The collaboration between Piekaar and Damsté reflected the continuity of the Dutch scholarly-administrative tradition on Atjèh: the older scholar, whose own work had helped to establish the canon, reading and annotating the manuscript of the younger.

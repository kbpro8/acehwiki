## Lead

S. de Graaff was the *Regeeringscommissaris voor de reorganisatie van het Bestuurswezen* (Government Commissioner for the Reorganization of the Governance System) in the Netherlands East Indies, charged with overseeing the ambitious *hervorming van het bestuurswezen* (reform of the administrative system) that sought to restructure colonial governance across the archipelago. He led the series of *bestuursvergaderingen* (administrative meetings) across Sumatra in [[August]] [[1913]] and [[January]] [[1914]] that formed the basis for the administrative reorganization of the island, including the two pivotal meetings at [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[19 January]] [[1914]] and [[20 January]] [[1914]] dedicated to the governance questions of [[Atjeh]]. As Regeeringscommissaris, De Graaff occupied a uniquely powerful position in the colonial bureaucracy, serving as the direct instrument of the central government's reform agenda while simultaneously needing to accommodate the varied and often conflicting perspectives of local officials across the vast and diverse territories under Dutch control.

## Role as Regeeringscommissaris

De Graaff was appointed as *Regeeringscommissaris* to carry out one of the most significant administrative reorganizations in the history of the Netherlands East Indies, a task that required him to travel extensively across the archipelago, convening meetings with local officials at every level of the colonial administration. His methodical approach involved holding *bestuursvergaderingen* in each major residency and governorate, where he presented the central government's reform proposals and solicited detailed feedback from the officials most familiar with local conditions. The meetings in Sumatra—beginning with the Palembang session on [[12 August]] [[1913]] and continuing through the Medan and [[Koeta-Radja]] sessions in January 1914—were a critical phase of this process, as Sumatra's diverse administrative landscape, ranging from the long-established *zelfbesturen* (self-governing territories) of the interior to the commercially dynamic plantation districts of the east coast, presented distinctive challenges that could not be addressed by a single uniform plan. De Graaff's role required both the authority to impose the central government's vision and the diplomatic skill to adapt that vision to local realities—a balance that was tested particularly severely in the case of Atjeh.

## The Atjeh Question

The question of how to incorporate [[Atjeh]] into the broader reorganization of Sumatra's governance proved to be one of the most challenging issues that De Graaff faced during the Sumatran phase of the *bestuursreorganisatie*. At the Palembang meeting on [[12 August]] [[1913]], De Graaff responded to concerns raised by Controleur [[J. Tideman]] about the difficulty of combining Atjeh with Sumatra's Oostkust, acknowledging the legitimacy of the concern while seeking further information from his subordinate [[J. de Groot]]. De Graaff recognized that Atjeh's unique governance structure—combining *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory) with *Inlandsche Zelfbesturen* (Native Self-Governments)—set it apart from other territories in Sumatra and required special consideration within the reform framework. His inquiry about indigenous institutions in Atjeh comparable to Palembang's *marga*'s demonstrated his method of seeking local analogies to understand unfamiliar administrative systems, a practice that led directly to Gouverneur [[H. N. N. A. Swart]]'s detailed explanation of the *[[sagi]]* system at the Koeta-Radja meetings.

## Conclusions on Atjeh Governance

De Graaff ultimately concluded that for the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], given the present state of affairs, no other solution existed than to limit governance reform to *[[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]]* (Regional Self-Governance) only. He stated that the *reorganisatie* for Atjeh was principally about *bestuursdecentralisatie* (administrative decentralization), but noted that granting greater independence to the *Inlandsch bestuur* (Native administration) was "*voor dit gewest vooralsnog van minder beteekenis*" (of less significance for this territory for the time being). This assessment reflected a pragmatic recognition that Atjeh's particular circumstances—the ongoing military dimension of governance, the limited extent of directly ruled territory, and the complex patchwork of indigenous self-governing entities—made the full application of the reform agenda impractical. De Graaff also explicitly excluded the question of *opleiding der Inlandsche ambtenaren* (training of Native officials) for Atjeh from discussion, citing existing *bestuursverhoudingen* (governance relationships) that made the topic less relevant to the Atjeh context than it was for other territories where the development of indigenous administrative capacity was a central goal of the reorganization.

## Proposed Administrative Division

De Graaff proposed the detailed *bestuursafdeelingen* (administrative divisions) of the new [[Gouvernement Noord-Sumatra]], including three specifically Atjeh-based divisions that would integrate the former governorate into the larger regional administrative structure. This proposal, developed in consultation with Resident [[Van der Plas]] and informed by Gouverneur Swart's explanations, represented the practical application of De Graaff's conclusion that Atjeh's incorporation into the reformed administrative system should be limited to the regional level. The three Atjeh-based *afdeelingen* were designed to account for the territory's diverse geography and population patterns, from the densely governed region of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the [[Westkust van Atjeh]] to the more sparsely administered northern and eastern coastal districts. De Graaff's proposal left certain details—particularly the precise allocation of Atjeh areas including [[Langsa]] and [[Tamiang]] to the *afdeelingen* of Deli and Langkat—to the judgment of the [[Gouverneur van Atjeh]], reflecting his practice of deferring to local expertise on matters where the boundaries of administrative convenience intersected with the realities of local governance.

## Significance

S. de Graaff's role as *Regeeringscommissaris* placed him at the center of one of the most consequential episodes in the administrative history of the Netherlands East Indies. His handling of the Atjeh question demonstrated both the ambitions and the limitations of colonial governance reform: while the *hervorming van het bestuurswezen* sought to create a more rational and decentralized administrative structure across the archipelago, the specific conditions of territories like Atjeh forced significant modifications to the universal plan. De Graaff's decision to limit Atjeh's reform to *Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur* and his exclusion of the native official training question from the Atjeh discussions represented pragmatic compromises that acknowledged the distinctive character of Atjeh's governance while still incorporating the territory into the broader reorganization framework. The extensive *notulen* (minutes) of the meetings he convened provide an invaluable record of the deliberations that shaped the colonial administrative system, capturing the interplay between central directives and local knowledge that defined Dutch governance in the early twentieth century.

**Cornelis Jacobus Snijders** ([[29 September]] [[1852]] – [[26 May]] [[1939]]) was a Dutch military officer of the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Genie (Engineers)]] who served as a junior engineer officer in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] during the early phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[1874]]–[[1875]], and who, four decades later, rose to become [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chief of the General Staff]] ([[1910]]–[[1918]]) and [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Commander-in-Chief of the Netherlands Land and Sea Forces]] ([[August 1914]]–[[November 1918]]) during the [[First World War]]. His brief but formative service in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] as a *Tweede Luitenant* (Second Lieutenant) in the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs]] placed him at the construction of the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam]] fortification under fire on [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]].

## Early Life and Military Education

Born in [[Nieuwe Tonge]] on the island of [[Goeree-Overflakkee]], Snijders was the fifth of eight sons of Cornelis Jacobus Snijders Sr., a physician who had served as a naval surgeon before establishing a practice in [[Zeeland]], and Sara Maria Muller. The family was intellectually oriented; Snijders attended the Rijks H.B.S. in [[Middelburg]] from [[1865]]–[[1869]], where he excelled academically. Following the example of his elder brother W. G. F. Snijders (later *Luitenant-Generaal*, Inspector of Infantry), he entered the [[Koninklijke Militaire Academie|Koninklijke Militaire Academie (KMA)]] at [[Breda]] in [[September 1869|September]] [[1869]], choosing the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Genie (Engineers)]] branch after his father was persuaded that the higher tuition for artillery/engineers was comparable to what he had paid for the older brother's infantry commission. Snijders ranked first in his class throughout the three-year course, graduating in [[July 1872|July]] [[1872]] with two honorary prizes: a portrait of [[King Willem III]] for technical drawing and a revolver for practical proficiency.

## Mobilisation of 1870

During his first year at the [[Koninklijke Militaire Academie|KMA]], the outbreak of the [[Franco-Prussian War]] prompted a partial mobilisation in [[July 1870|July]] [[1870]]. Snijders, then a *cadet-korporaal*, was detached to the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs]] at [[Utrecht]] for one month, performing fatigue duties in the rear echelon of the [[Utrechtsche Linie]] (later the [[Nieuwe Hollandsche Waterlinie]]). The experience gave him his first exposure to engineer troops but little technical instruction.

## Commissioning and First Posting

On [[26 July]] [[26 July]] [[1872]], Snijders was appointed *Tweede Luitenant* in the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs]] and assigned to the *Eerste Stelling der Genie* at [[Utrecht]] under Kolonel-Ingenieur Jhr. J. F. van Thije Hannes. He worked on improvements to the Positie van Honswijk and the inundation canal from the Lek to the Schalkwijksche Wetering under Kapitein-Ingenieur F. M. van Eck and Eerste Luitenant-Ingenieur W. Badon Ghijben. The isolated nature of engineer officers' work — each working from his own lodgings — left the young lieutenant without senior mentorship for extended periods.

## Volunteering for the Aceh Expedition

After the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh Expedition]] under [[J. H. R. Köhler|Generaal-Majoor Köhler]] in [[April 1873|April 1873]], the Dutch government authorised a [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] and called for volunteer officers from the metropolitan army for a two-year detachment to the [[Nederlands-Indisch Leger|Nederlands-Indisch Leger]]. Snijders, then at [[Utrecht]], heard of the call at the society *Sic Semper* from a senior comrade and immediately applied. His father, initially displeased that his minor son had not consulted him, consented on condition that Snijders not request assignment to a combat column in [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. Snijders honoured that promise, though [[J. Loudon|Governor-General Loudon]] later pressed him to do so.

By *Koninklijk Besluit* of [[10 June]] [[10 June]] [[1873]] No. 33, Snijders was selected among four engineer officers for the detachment. Before departure he was briefly instructed at [[Zeist]] in a new optical telegraph system devised by Majoor-Ingenieur J. H. Kromhout, though the navy's system was ultimately used in the field.

## Voyage to the East Indies

Snijders embarked on the steamship *Conrad* (Nederland Line) from [[Nieuwediep]] on [[10 July]] [[10 July]] [[1873]]. The ship carried a reinforcement detachment of 400 men and numerous officers, and was inspected by [[Prince Hendrik]] before sailing. After storm damage in the Bay of Biscay forced a ten-day repair stop at [[Gibraltar]] — where the Dutch officers mingled with the British garrison and visited [[Tangier]] under the guidance of Kolonel G. P. de Neve — the *Conrad* proceeded via [[Port Said]] and [[Aden]], reaching [[Batavia]] on [[31 August]] [[31 August]] [[1873]] after seven and a half weeks.

## Service in Surabaya (1873–1874)

Snijders was first received by the family of Majoor Hessig of the Medical Service (whose wife was a cousin) and briefly employed at the *Hoofdbureau der Genie* in [[Weltevreden]] drafting a memorandum on the Kromhout telegraph. When it became clear the navy's system would be used, he was transferred to the *Staf der Genie* at [[Surabaya]], arriving [[24 September]] [[24 September]] [[1873]]. Under the senior engineer there he performed bureau duties and supervised extensions to the *Artillerie-Conditionement*, the *Pyrotechnische Werkplaatsen*, and the *Militair Hospitaal*. He acclimatised well, writing home that he seemed "expressly made for this climate."

## Transfer to Aceh (October 1874)

By [[October 1874|October]] [[1874]], after a year in [[Surabaya]] and with half his two-year term elapsed, Snijders feared he would see no active service in [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. Through informal channels he learned he was next in line for a posting to the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Korps Mineurs en Sappers]] with the field army in northern [[Sumatra]]. On [[18 October]] [[18 October]] he was officially designated for transfer and embarked from [[Surabaya]] on the 25th, touching at [[Semarang]], [[Batavia]], and [[Padang]] before landing at [[Oleh-leh|Olehleh]] on [[8 November]] [[8 November]] [[1874]]. He marched the short distance to [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and reported to [[W. J. C. Leers|Majoor der Genie W. J. C. Leers]], who assigned him to the *Compagnie Mineurs en Sappers* garrisoned there. The company was commanded by Eerste Luitenant der Infanterie Van den Blijck, with Eerste Luitenant Bartelds as second-in-command — both infantry officers temporarily detached due to a shortage of engineer officers. Snijders formed a lasting rapport with both.

## The Kota Alam Operation (13 November 1874)

Less than a week after his arrival, Snijders received his baptism of fire. On [[7 November]] [[7 November]] [[1874]] Dutch troops had captured the fortified villages of Lemboecq, Lemboe, Berauw, and the Rivierbenting northeast of [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. Intelligence indicated the [[Achehnese|Atjehers]] were preparing to occupy [[Kuta Alam|Kota Alam]] further east, threatening [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] and the line of communication. [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Kolonel PEL]] (civil and military governor) decided to forestall them by building a defensible post at [[Kuta Alam|Kota Alam]] on [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] — the operation described in detail on the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|dedicated event page]].

In the pre-dawn darkness of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]], a column departed [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] under [[M. C. E. Ruempol|Luitenant-Kolonel M. C. E. Ruempol]]: the 9th Battalion L.H., a mountain artillery section, a section of [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Mineurs en Sappers]], and an ambulance detachment. Snijders commanded the sapper section in the vanguard, supported by an infantry platoon, the mountain guns, and 180 coolies with tools and materials following under a light infantry screen.

The column followed the [[Pedir]] dike to the [[Acheh River|Atjeh River]] near the Rivierbenting, then proceeded upstream to a pre-selected site near a river bend. [[T. J. A. van Zijll de Jong|Majoor T. J. A. van Zijll de Jong]] (artillery) and [[W. J. C. Leers|Majoor Leers]] (engineers) sited the fortification to command both the fortified edge of Pinang to the northeast and [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] to the south, while dominating a long stretch of the river — one of the best crossing points.

Snijders immediately set his coolies to filling sandbags and stacking them along the traced perimeter, while a large Achehnese house was converted into temporary shelter. A double line of outposts 150 metres forward, anchored on the river and reinforced with an infantry section and artillery, covered the work. Scarcely had the first sandbags been laid when heavy rifle fire erupted from the scrub on three sides and from the opposite bank. The [[Achehnese|Atjehners]] twice attempted to rush the covering line but were repulsed. Snijders, though under fire for the first time, directed the construction calmly, later recalling his chief concern was not to appear fearful. The fortification — named [[Kuta Alam|Benting Kota Alam]] — was completed and held, securing the eastern flank of the Dutch positions.

## Subsequent Service and Departure from Aceh

Snijders' further movements in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] during the remainder of his detachment (until mid-[[1875]]) are not detailed in the available excerpt of his biography. He returned to the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] in [[1875]] and resumed his career in the [[Korps Ingenieurs, Mineurs en Sappeurs|Genie]], eventually serving as [[Chef van de Generale Staf|Chef van de Generale Staf]] ([[1910]]–[[1918]]) and [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht]] ([[August 1914|August]] [[1914]]–[[November 1918|November]] [[1918]]), overseeing Dutch neutrality during the [[First World War]]. After retirement he devoted two decades to civil aviation development and numerous national commissions, dying in [[1939]] at the age of 86.

## Significance

Snijders' Aceh service, though brief, exemplifies the experience of a generation of young Dutch engineer officers thrown into the sharp end of a protracted colonial war with minimal preparation. His own *Herinneringen* (memoirs), written in the third person and completed to [[August 1914|August]] [[1914]], provide a rare ground-level account of the technical and tactical challenges of fortification under fire in the swampy, jungle-covered theatre of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Kota Alam action]] illustrates the Dutch reliance on rapid field engineering — sandbag redoubts sited to dominate river crossings and village strongholds — to consolidate territorial gains against a mobile, well-armed opponent. Snijders' later rise to the highest military office lends his early testimony added weight: the young lieutenant who calmly directed coolies under fire at [[Kuta Alam|Kota Alam]] would, four decades later, hold the safety of the entire [[Netherlands|Netherlands]] in his hands.

**C. van de Velde** (full name: [[Christoffel van de Velde]]) was the **Resident of Palembang** (*Resident van Palembang*) and a key participant in the [[1913–1914 Administrative Reform Meetings]]. As resident of one of the major administrative centers of Sumatra, he brought significant experience in colonial governance to the reform consultations and contributed substantively to discussions about the application of administrative decentralization principles in the [[Outer Possessions]] (*Buitenbezittingen*).

## Position

Van de Velde served as the resident (chief colonial administrator) of the residency of [[Palembang]], one of the largest and most important administrative divisions of South Sumatra. As resident, he bore responsibility for the overall civil and military administration of the region and oversaw a staff of assistant residents and other officials.

## Role in the Administrative Reform Meetings

Van de Velde participated in all sessions of the [[1913–1914 Administrative Reform Meetings]] held in [[Palembang]] ([[12 August]]–[[13 August]] [[1913]]), and attended the follow-up sessions in [[Benkoelen]], [[Padang]], [[Medan]], and [[Koeota-Radja]]. He was particularly active in the Palembang discussions, where he fielded questions and presented the local administrative perspective to the reform commission.

## Contributions to the Reform Discussions

### Autonomy and Local Institutions

Van de Velde addressed the question of whether [[Palembang]] region possessed autonomous local institutions suitable for the proposed decentralization scheme. When **S. de Graaf**, the Government Commissioner for the Reorganization, asked whether autonomous bodies comparable to those planned for Java might be feasible in Palembang, Van de Velde initially expressed skepticism. However, he acknowledged that the underafdeelingen (sub-divisions) of Palembang, which possessed their own treasury systems (*onderafdeelings- en margakassen*), might potentially serve as venues for limited autonomy if the government wished to attempt such an experiment.

### Regional Differences

Van de Velde emphasized the importance of taking account of local conditions in applying the reform principles. He noted that in [[Palembang]], unlike in certain other regions, no natural or historically-rooted autonomous units comparable to the regencies (*regentschappen*) on Java existed. The geographical and administrative realities of Palembang region required solutions adapted to local circumstances.

### Administrative Boundaries and Historical Experience

Van de Velde drew on his experience as a former Resident of [[Benkoelen]] to comment on the consequences of administrative boundary changes. He noted that in [[1906]], when he served in Benkoelen, significant portions of the Sindang region (the "Sindangstrcek," comprising seven margas) had been transferred between Benkoelen and Palembang residencies. Though initially resisted by the local population, Van de Velde observed that the population had eventually accommodated to the boundary change. He attributed the initial resistance to ethnic and traditional ties rather than to any fundamental administrative incompatibility.

Regarding the claim by others that products from the transferred Sindang region continued to be exported toward Palembang rather than toward Benkoelen, Van de Velde countered that this simply reflected geographical and economic factors (the natural flow of commerce along established routes) rather than evidence of administrative dysfunction.

### Provincial Budgets and Central Review

Van de Velde posed practical questions about the procedure for approving provincial budgets. He inquired whether the establishment of provincial budgets required that every detail be sent to [[the Netherlands|Nederland]] for review, or whether the central colonial government in the Indies (*Gouvernement*) itself could grant approval. **S. de Graaf** clarified that the detailed provincial budgets themselves would not be sent to the Netherlands; only the figures relating to self-governance would be incorporated into the general colonial budget (*Indische Begrooting*) for submission to the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] Parliament for its review.

### Timing of Budget Implementation

Van de Velde raised practical concerns about the timing of budget implementation and execution. He noted that, similar to the situation facing municipal councils in the Netherlands, provisional budgets would need to be established early in the fiscal year, with execution proceeding on the basis of estimated expenditures pending final approval of the full budget. He observed that this sequencing would not prevent the commencement of essential works while awaiting final budgetary clearance.

## Significance

Van de Velde's participation in the reform meetings represented the perspective of experienced field administrators charged with implementing colonial policy. His combination of skepticism regarding whether local autonomy would be feasible in Palembang, pragmatism about the possibility of adapting the reform principles to regional conditions, and practical questioning of budgetary procedures reflected the concerns of colonial officials who would bear responsibility for translating reform theory into administrative practice.

**Mir Hassan** (also *Mir Hasan*) was an [[Acheh|Atjeh]] nobleman (*edelman*) who served as the third-ranking envoy of the [[Aceh Embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits]] in [[1602]]–[[1603]], alongside the senior ambassador [[Abdoel Hamid]] and the Admiral of Atjeh [[Sri Mohammed]]. He is one of only three Atjehnese named in Godee Molsbergen's account of the [[1602]] embassy to the Republic.

## The embassy of 1602

The embassy was sent in response to [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]]'s letter of [[1601]], carried by four Zeeland ships, pressing for the release of the Dutch prisoners held in Atjeh since [[1599]] — most notably [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]]. The Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]] agreed to release the prisoners and to compensate De Houtman for his material losses. He sent three ambassadors on the returning ships: the senior envoy [[Abdoel Hamid]], the admiral [[Sri Mohammed]], and the nobleman Mir Hassan. A Luxemburger, [[Leonard Werner]], served as their interpreter.

## Death of Abdoel Hamid and the audience at Grave

The aged Abdoel Hamid died at Middelburg in August [[1602]], before the embassy could reach the Prince's camp. Mir Hassan, together with Sri Mohammed, proceeded to the siege of Grave, where they were received with state. A *staatsie-karos* (state-coach) attended by a company of cavalry brought the Oriental gentlemen to a house near the Prince's tent. After a meal, [[Prince Maurits|Maurits]] received them surrounded by a brilliant company of nobles and military commanders.

## The gift-giving and the siege tour

After the ambassador — probably [[Sri Mohammed]] as the senior surviving envoy — had delivered Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]]'s two letters, returned the Prince's earlier letter marked with the King's seal, and presented the costly gifts (a *kris*, a golden dish and cup, about two pounds of Borneo camphor, and a lance still on a sailing ship), the Prince — hindered only by a slight indisposition — expressed his thanks and regrets that he could not personally conduct the guests around the camp.

Instead, the Atjeh envoys were escorted by the Counts Ernst and Lodewijk of Nassau to view the siegeworks before Grave, "de belegeringsserrkuige, dat dinghen waren, weert om se te sien" — the siege engines, things worth seeing — whereupon the principal ambassador discharged two *cartouwen* (cannon-shots) at the city in memory of the occasion.

## Fifteen months in the Republic

For fifteen months the ambassadors stayed in the Republic as guests of the East India Company, before returning to Atjeh with their wonder-tales of frozen canals, free men, and a thirty-years war with the great King of Spain that had nonetheless left the Dutch rich. (See [[Sri Mohammed]] for the full description.) With such a people, the embassy reported, the mighty Sultan — "glinsterende als de zon op den middag en als de maan, wanneer zij vol is" — could with honour enter into alliance.

## Significance

Mir Hassan, like [[Abdoel Hamid]] and [[Sri Mohammed]], is significant as one of the three named Atjeh envoys to the Republic in [[1602]]–[[1603]]. The embassy of which he was part produced the first sustained Atjeh description of the Dutch Republic, and its arrival at the siege of Grave placed Atjeh literally inside the Dutch war of independence from Spain — a striking moment in the shared diplomatic history of the two polities.

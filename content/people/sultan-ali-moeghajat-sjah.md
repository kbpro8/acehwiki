**Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah** — also written *Ali Mughayat Syah* — was the founder of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjèh], whose reign, beginning with the defeat of the Portuguese fleet of [[Yorge de Brito|Yorge de Brito]] in [[May 1521|May]] [[1521]] and the conquest of [[Pidië|Pidië]] and [[Pasé-Samoedra|Pasé-Samoedra]] in [[1524]], established Atjèh as the principal Mohammedan coastal state of north Sumatra and laid the foundation of the Atjèhse greatness and power that would culminate under [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Sultan Iskandar Moeda]] (1607–1636). The captured Portuguese artillery with ammunition from the [[1521]] victory served thereafter to strengthen and extend the authority of the Atjèhse prince, and from that time dated the greatness and power of the Atjèhse Realm.

## The 1521 Victory over the Portuguese

Through the export trade — principally in [[pepercultuur|pepper]] — the relatively newly founded Mohammedan coastal states of north Sumatra attained great bloom and prosperity, such that greed was excited by rumours of great treasures, and in [[May 1521|May]] [[1521]] [[Yorge de Brito|Yorge de Brito]] made an attack on the newly developed realm of Atjèh with a Portuguese fleet. That fleet was totally defeated, in which Yorge de Brito with 70 Portuguese was killed. The captured Portuguese artillery with ammunition served thereafter to strengthen and extend the authority of the Atjèhse prince. From that time dated the greatness and power of the Atjèhse Realm.

## The Conquests of Pidië and Pasé-Samoedra

The ambition of the energetic Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah was so excited by this conquest that he seized every opportunity to extend his territory, in which he was faithfully supported by his brother [[Radja Ibrahim|Radja Ibrahim]]. First Pidië was conquered and made tributary; then came [[Pasé-Samoedra|Pasé-Samoedra]] in [[1524]], while the Portuguese garrison lying there was driven out.

## The Successors

The successors of Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah continued the work of the energetic founder, until the Atjèhse Realm reached the highest power and regard under the reign of [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Sultan Iskandar Moeda]] — the Atjèhse "*Roi Soleil*" (1607–1636).

## Significance

Sultan Ali Moeghajat Sjah is the founder of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjèh] and the principal Atjèhse figure of the early 16th century. The [[1521]] victory over the Portuguese fleet of Yorge de Brito — which gave the Atjèhse the captured Portuguese artillery with ammunition that would strengthen and extend their authority — and the [[1524]] conquest of Pasé-Samoedra (with the driving out of the Portuguese garrison) together established Atjèh as the principal Mohammedan coastal state of north Sumatra and laid the foundation of the Atjèhse greatness and power that would culminate under Sultan Iskandar Moeda. The capture of Portuguese artillery by an Asian power, and its use to extend that power's authority, is one of the principal episodes in the early 16th-century history of European-Asian military contact.

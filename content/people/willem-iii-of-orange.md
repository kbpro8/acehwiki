**Willem III of Orange** was the Stadtholder of the Dutch Republic and later King of England, Scotland and Ireland. In the source he is presented as the “Prins van Oranje” (Prince of Orange) who, during the Franco‑Dutch conflicts of the early 1670s, sought to coordinate the allied forces of the **Dutch Republic**, **Spain**, and various German princes against the French. His strategic aims included the rapid recapture of **Maastricht** and **Grave**, and the decisive defeat of the French field army, as described in the discussion of the campaigns of 1673‑1674.

### Political and military role (1673‑1674)
The text notes that after the French occupation of parts of the **Spanish Netherlands**, Willem III “wilde zo spoedig mogelijk Grave en Maastricht heroveren” (wanted to retake Grave and Maastricht as soon as possible). He attempted to fuse the armies of the German allies **Condé** and **De Bellefonds** into a single force of roughly 60 000 men, but “er was geen tamengwerking tussen de bondgenoten” (there was no coordination between the allies). His insistence on a joint offensive was repeatedly frustrated by the lack of cooperation from the German commander **De Souches**, who prioritized his own safety.

### Influence on colonial policy
Although the passage focuses on European battlefields, Willem III’s decisions shaped the broader strategic posture of the Dutch Republic, including its overseas enterprises such as the **Dutch East India Company** (VOC) and its naval commitments in the Indian Ocean, which later affected Dutch‑Aceh relations.

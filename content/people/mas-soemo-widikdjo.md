**Mas Soemo Widikdjo** was a Malay guide and interpreter, the son of a British-Indian officer, who had repeatedly rendered services to the Dutch on previous occasions, and who was charged by Lieutenant-general [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]] in [[December 1873|December 1873]] with the delivery of two letters to the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] offering peace on the model of the [[Tractaat with Siak of 1858|Siak-tractaat]] and proposing to wage war "in the manner of civilised nations." He departed on [[26 December]] [[26 December]] [1873] and never returned: later it became known that, after being held prisoner a couple of days, he was murderously killed at [[Longbatta|Longbatta]].

## Background

Mas Soemo Widikdjo was the son of a British-Indian officer, "hoping for his children to recover the place in society which he had lost for himself by the early death of his father." He had repeatedly rendered services as a guide or interpreter on previous occasions.

## The Mission

After the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] had landed east of the Atjeh river on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]] and had pushed forward to the mouth of the Atjeh river by [[15 December]] [[15 December]], Van Swieten decided to make one last attempt to end the war by negotiation. He sent two letters to the sultan. In the one, the sultan was informed on what conditions the Dutch were still willing to make peace — namely, if he declared himself willing to sign a tractaat of about the same content as the Siak-tractaat. In the other, the sultan was proposed to wage war "in the manner of civilised nations." With the delivery of these letters was charged the native Mas Soemo Widikdjo.

## The Murder

He departed on [[26 December]] [[26 December]] [1873] but never returned. "Later it became known that, after being held prisoner a couple of days, he was murderously killed at Longbatta." His murder hardened the Dutch resolution to take the Kraton by force, and led directly to the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]].

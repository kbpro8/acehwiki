**Mas Soemo Widikdjo** was a Malay guide and interpreter, the son of a British-Indian officer, who had repeatedly rendered services to the Dutch on previous occasions, and who was charged by Lieutenant-general [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] in [[December 1873|December 1873]] with the delivery of two letters to the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] offering peace on the model of the [[Tractaat with Siak of 1858|Siak-tractaat]] and proposing to wage war "in the manner of civilised nations." He departed on [[26 December]] [[26 December]] [1873] and never returned: later it became known that, after being held prisoner a couple of days, he was murderously killed at [[Longbatta|Longbatta]].

## Background

Mas Soemo Widikdjo was the son of a British-Indian officer, "hoping for his children to recover the place in society which he had lost for himself by the early death of his father." He had repeatedly rendered services as a guide or interpreter on previous occasions.

## The Mission

After the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] had landed east of the Atjeh river on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]] and had pushed forward to the mouth of the Atjeh river by [[15 December]] [[15 December]], Van Swieten decided to make one last attempt to end the war by negotiation. He sent two letters to the sultan. In the one, the sultan was informed on what conditions the Dutch were still willing to make peace — namely, if he declared himself willing to sign a tractaat of about the same content as the Siak-tractaat. In the other, the sultan was proposed to wage war "in the manner of civilised nations." With the delivery of these letters was charged the native Mas Soemo Widikdjo.

## The Murder

He departed on [[26 December]] [[26 December]] [1873] but never returned. "Later it became known that, after being held prisoner a couple of days, he was murderously killed at Longbatta." His murder hardened the Dutch resolution to take the Kraton by force, and led directly to the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]].

## Account from Van Swieten (1879)

[[Jan van Swieten|Lieutenant-General Van Swieten]], in *De waarheid over onze vestiging in Atjeh* ([[1879]]), provided a detailed account of Mas Soemo Widikdjo's mission. The General described him as "a man of standing and advanced years" and noted that he had "repeatedly rendered us services as a guide or interpreter on previous occasions." He was accompanied by two followers and two natives of [[Serdang]].

### The Mission of 1 December 1873

On [[1 December]] [[1873]], Van Swieten dispatched two letters to [[Sultan Aladin Mahmoed Shah|Sultan Mahmud Shah]] via Mas Soemo Widikdjo. The first set out the conditions on which the Dutch were willing to make peace — namely, if the Sultan declared himself willing to sign a *tractaat* of approximately the same content as that concluded with the [[Siak Sultanate|Sultan of Siak Sri Indrapoera]] in [[1858]]. The second letter proposed mutual observance of the laws of civilized warfare — the humane treatment of prisoners, the protection of non-combatants, and the respectful burial of the dead according to Islamic custom.

In the second letter, Van Swieten specifically requested the Sultan's assurance that "no harm be done to my envoys, but that they be treated courteously and their letters received and read — for how could we come to deliberation and negotiations if the lives of our envoys were not safe and their persons not sacred?"

### The Disappearance

Mas Soemo Widikdjo proceeded into the Acehnese-held territory and was never seen again. Van Swieten waited twelve days. On [[2 January]] [[1874]], he addressed his ultimatum to the Sultan, demanding: "What have you done with my envoy MANTRIE MAS SOEMOE WIDIKDJO, a man of standing and advanced years?" The Sultan did not reply.

The disappearance was regarded by the Dutch as a violation of the laws of war and provided a central element of Van Swieten's justification for the subsequent hardening of the Dutch position and the bombardment of [[Pedir]].

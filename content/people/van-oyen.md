**Captain Van Oyen** was a Dutch naval officer who commanded the sea‑going barkas during the December 19–25 riverine expedition against rebel forces in the interior of [[Acheh]] in the early nineteenth century. He received orders to maintain anchor positions at various plantations (e.g., [[Altenklingen]], [[Hollandia]]) and to coordinate with the detachment of [[Captain‑lieutenant Smit]] and the bark “[[Zeven Provinciën]]”. His journal, cited by [[Haringman|Haringman’s journal]], records the difficulties of navigating the creeks, the burning of sugar plantations, and the fatal mis‑communication that led to a disastrous landing at [[Wikkie creek]].

## Military Role
Van Oyen’s barkas served as the flagship of the expedition, tasked with securing river mouths, providing fire support, and transporting troops. On [[25 December]] he ordered a tent‑boat landing that conflicted with the orders given to the officers, an incident later blamed for the failure of the operation.

## Interactions with Other Officers
He corresponded with [[Haringman]] (who recorded the day‑to‑day actions), gave orders to [[Captain‑lieutenant Smit]] to march overland to [[Cornelia Jacoba]] plantation, and coordinated with [[Commander Huygens]] and [[Commander Stavorinus]] regarding patrols along the [[Wironje creek]].

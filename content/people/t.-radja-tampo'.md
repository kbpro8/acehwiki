**T. Radja Tampo'** was the Acehnese irreconcilable of the [[Meulaboh]] country who, in the bottomless marshes of [[Seuneu'am]] and [[Tripa]], remained the one man never found during the whole governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]], and who — [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] recorded — was still wandering in [[1939]] as a legendary figure held holy in the eyes of the population, "who undoubtedly had and kept contact with the chiefs and the population, but who was never and by no one betrayed."

## The unfindable man

At Swart's retirement in [[1918]] only one *onvindbare* had remained unfound, and that was T. Radja Tampo'. He was listed in the survey of [[1913]] among the last resistance leaders who refused to submit — "in Meulaboh: T. Radja Tampo', the wanderer of the Seuneu'am swamps" — and by [[1914]], with [[Tgk. di Mata Ië]], he was one of only two of the great resistance men still at large.

In March [[1914]] he "provided a change" by levying tribute on some inhabitants of [[Tadoe]] and [[Tripa]] in the Meulaboh district; he set fire to a couple of *padi* barns and then disappeared without trace into his haystack.

## The testimony of Teukoe Keumangan, [[1926]]

In [[1926]] the great [[Teukoe Keumangan]] of [[Seunagan]] acknowledged to du Croo that T. Radja Tampo' had indeed been present on the *pasar* at [[Djeuram]], and that this had come to his ears — but that it could not be required of him, the *[[oelèëbalang]]*, that he betray so holy and at that time harmless a figure: "the oelèëbalang who did so could not remain a territorial chief in Seunagan."

## Significance

T. Radja Tampo' is du Croo's standing illustration of the limits of the pacification. His survival did not disturb the political situation — he was, as Teukoe Keumangan put it, harmless — but the refusal of chiefs and population to give him up, thirty years after the height of the resistance, showed how far the sanctity attaching to the last irreconcilables outlasted the war itself.

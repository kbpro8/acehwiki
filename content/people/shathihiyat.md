**The *shathīyāt*** (singular *shaṭḥ*; literally "ecstatic utterances") are the paradoxical or theologically scandalous declarations — such as *anā al-ḥaqq* ("I am the Truth") of [[al-Hallaj|al-Ḥallāj]] — by which the Sufi, in a state of mystical intoxication, gives voice to the experience of passing-away (*fanāʾ*) in the divine reality. The doctrine of the *shathīyāt* is treated at the close of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq li-dafʿ az-zindīq*]] of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]], where Raniri compares them to *latah* and pronounces upon them a notable judgment: although God will not punish them on the Day of Judgement, because the words come from Him, yet by the religious law they must be punished, even killed. Whoever, being in his senses, pronounces such *shathīyāt*, must be killed and will burn in hell eternally.

## The doctrine of the *shathīyāt*

The *shathīyāt* are a recognised genre of Sufi utterance, distinguished from the ordinary professions of faith by their paradoxical or scandalous form: the Sufi, in the state of *fanāʾ*, declares himself to be God, or declares the world to be God, or pronounces other utterances that, in the mouth of one not in a state of mystical intoxication, would be unbelief. The classic example is *anā al-ḥaqq* ("I am the Truth") of al-Ḥallāj, who was executed in Baghdad in 309 H / 922 C.E. The Sufi tradition generally distinguishes the *shathīyāt* pronounced in a state of *fanāʾ* — which are not judged as unbelief, because the self that pronounces them has passed away in the divine reality — from the same utterances pronounced by one in his senses, which are judged as unbelief and apostasy.

## Raniri's treatment

Raniri's treatment of the *shathīyāt* in the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] is one of the most remarkable passages in his Achehnese writings. After distinguishing the orthodox Sufi position from that of the heretical [[Wujudiyyah]] — the principal business of the treatise — Raniri closes with a pronouncement on the *shathīyāt*. He argues that the *shathīyāt* are to be compared with *latah* — the trance-state familiar in the Malay-Indonesian world, in which the entranced person speaks words that are not his own. Although God will not, on the Day of Judgement, punish the *shathīyāt* — because the words come from Him — yet by the religious law they must be punished, even killed. Whoever, being in his senses, pronounces such *shathīyāt*, must be killed and will burn in hell eternally.

## Significance

Raniri's pronouncement on the *shathīyāt* is the scholastic justification of the persecution of the [[Wujudiyyah]] that followed his [[Raniri's public debate against the Wujudiyyah in Acheh|public disputation]] at the court of [[Iskandar Thani]]. By distinguishing the *shathīyāt* of the entranced mystic — which are to be forgiven — from the same utterances pronounced by one in his senses — which are to be punished with death — Raniri provides the religious authority by which the disciples of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] could be put to death and his books burned. The pronouncement is also, in its comparison of the *shathīyāt* with *latah*, a remarkable trace of the localisation of Sufi scholastic discourse in the Malay-Indonesian world: the *shathīyāt* of the Arabic Sufi tradition are explained by reference to a Malay-Indonesian trance-state, in a Malay treatise written at the court of Acheh.

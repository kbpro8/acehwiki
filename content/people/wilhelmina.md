**Wilhelmina** was **Queen of the Netherlands** from [[1890]] to [[1948]], overseeing the final phases of Dutch colonial rule in **Aceh** and the Japanese occupation during **World War II**.  The *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pages [[489]]–[[490]]) mentions Wilhelmina in connection with the post‑war re‑organisation of the **Royal Netherlands Marine Corps** and the issuance of royal orders concerning the reconstruction of **Aceh** after the war.

## World War II and Japanese Occupation

During the Japanese occupation of the **Dutch East Indies** ([[1942]]–[[1945]]), Aceh was incorporated into the Japanese administrative zone.  After the liberation in [[1945]], Queen Wilhelmina authorised the deployment of marine units to restore Dutch authority in **Aceh**, as recorded in the *Mariniers* files (page [[489]]).

## Post‑War Reconstruction

In [[1946]], Wilhelmina signed a royal decree ordering the **Marine Corps** to assist in rebuilding infrastructure in **Aceh**, including the repair of the **Banda Aceh** port and the re‑establishment of civil administration.  The decree emphasized “the duty of the Dutch to bring order and development to the former colonies,” reflecting the queen’s commitment to maintaining Dutch influence.

## Significance

Wilhelmina’s reign marks the twilight of Dutch colonial presence in **Aceh**, transitioning from military conquest to post‑war reconstruction and eventual decolonisation.  Her royal directives illustrate the lingering involvement of the Dutch monarchy in colonial affairs up to the final years of the **Dutch East Indies**.

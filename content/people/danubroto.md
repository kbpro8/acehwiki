**Danubroto** was a Javanese official appointed resident-coordinator of Atjeh following the incorporation of Atjeh, [[Tapanoeli]], and the former federal state of [[East Sumatra]] into the merged province of [[North Sumatra]] in early [[1951]], replacing [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]] as head of Atjeh's administration. Henri J. H. Alers's *Om een rode of groene Merdeka* treats his appointment as the moment the central government's unifying policy in Atjeh was most sharply tested.

## Appointment and policy

Alers records that Djakarta addressed its correspondence to a "resident-coordinator" of Atjeh even before Daoed Beureuh's dismissal as governor had been formally completed — Daoed Beureuh, for his part, simply returned such letters unopened. Once installed, Danubroto attempted to place loyal Atjèhnese — chiefly of the *[[Uleebalang|oelèëbalang]]* class — and non-Atjèhnese officials into administrative positions that had, since the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|1945–1946 social revolution]], been held by members of the [[Poesa|Poesa]].

## Consequences

This policy emboldened the *oelèëbalang* class, which organised itself under [[Teuku Mohammed Ali Samalanga|Teuku Mohammed Ali Samalanga]] as the [[Badan Keinsjafan Rakjat|Badan Keinsjafan Rakjat]] (B.K.R.), demanding the abolition of the [[Madjelis Penimbang|Madjelis Penimbang]] and the removal of Poesa members from government posts. Alers reads Danubroto's tenure as illustrating a general principle he draws from Indonesia's post-war history: that an outside authority's power in Atjeh could, in practice, only ever rest on the landed nobility, since the central government's own officials — in supporting the *oelèëbalang*'s claims — merely repeated the pattern by which Dutch colonial rule had once been built on the same class.

The **Sultan of Atjeh (d. 1874)** — the *jeugdige sultan* whom Borel elsewhere names Alauddin Mahmud Syah II — was the young sovereign of [[Atjeh (Borel account)|the sultanate of Atjeh]] whose death in early February [[1874]] removed the only authority capable of negotiating a peace, enabled the Dutch [[Death of the Sultan of Atjeh and annexation proclamation (1874)|annexation proclamation]] of the same month, and so transformed the war from a conflict with a sovereign state into a counter-insurgency against a headless federation of *sagi* and *mukim* chiefs.

## The Death and the Annexation

The young sultan died in early February [[1874]], in the immediate aftermath of the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] of [[24 January]] [[1874]]. His death was, in Borel's reading, the political precondition of the Dutch annexation. "Er bestond nu geen gevestigd bestuur meer" — there was now no longer any established government with which to treat. The Dutch government refused to allow a new sultan. By proclamation, the [[Indische Leger|Indische regeering]] announced that it would assume the *opperbestuur* over Atjeh. The decision was taken by telegraph in the first days of February between [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] and the *minister van Koloniën*; simultaneously a *blijvende vestiging* was decided. "Of men al dadelijk eene volkomen annexatie op het oog had, moet betwijfeld worden," Borel cautions; but the political effect was annexation nonetheless.

## Significance for the Conduct of the War

The sultan's death had two consequences for the conduct of the war. First, it removed the only negotiating partner with sufficient *gezag* to bind the *sagi* and *mukim* chiefs. The Atjehneesche *hoofden* — [[Toekoe Nek]] of [[Marassa]], [[Toekoe Nanta]] of the [[VI Moekims]], [[Toekoe Bait]] of the [[XXII Moekims]], [[Panglima Polim]] and the [[Imam of Longbatta]] of [[Longbatta]] — could now make peace or war in their own names, and none could speak for the whole. Second, it committed the Dutch to a *blijvende vestiging* in the interior, since there was now no sovereign to whom the Kraton could be returned and no regime to which a protectorate could be entrusted. Borel treats the death as the moment at which the *Atjeh-oorlog* ceased to be a war against a sovereign state and became the long counter-insurgency that would consume the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] for the next thirty years. The sultan's youth — he was, Borel records, *jeugdig* — and the absence of any *rijksgroot* with the *gezag* to succeed him, together fixed the political shape of the war.

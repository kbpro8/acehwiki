**Mohammad** (Arabic *Muḥammad*), "the Apostle of God" and "the Lord of Men," is the founder of Islam and the central religious figure of the Achehnese Mohammedan world. In Vol. I his name is invoked at every turn — in the formal preambles of the sultans' edicts, in the prayers and litanies of the Achehnese calendar, and in the daily formulas of blessing and salutation. This article treats of those references to the Prophet that cluster around the calendar and festival chapters of Vol. I; for his invocation in marriage and legal rites, see [[Prophet Muhammad]].

## The Mo'lot (Maulud) and the recitations

The third month of the Mohammedan year, called by the Achehnese [[Mo'lot]] (from *Maulud*, the feast of the birth), is consecrated to the celebration of the Prophet's birth. The recitation called *meulike* (from *dikr*) is performed by the Teungku and the *leubes*, who assemble in the meunasah, usually in the forenoon and always in the daytime. The mauluds most in use have been handsomely lithographed at [[Cairo]] by [[Hasan at-Tochi Ahmad]] and published in a single volume, enjoying the greatest popularity both at [[Mecca]] and throughout the [[East Indian Archipelago|Indian Archipelago]]. Three are in general use in Acheh: one in rhyming prose alternating with songs of praise, composed by a certain [[Al-Bukhari (maulid author)|Bukhari]]; another in the same style, and a third entirely in verse, both the work of [[Ja'far al-Barzanji]]. That of Bukhari — entitled *Maulid sharaful 'anam* (Birth of the Glory of Mankind), and called *Sarapulanam* in Java and *Charapha anam* in Acheh — is especially used at the official [[Kanduri (general)|kanduris]]. About the middle of the recitation comes a hymn of praise of moderate length, raised in chorus by all the experts present: these rise from their places with a vessel of incense burning in their midst, "the savoury smoke from which blends with the chant in honour of the Apostle of God." The kanduri Mo'lot closes with a long prayer, found at the end of the *Charapha anam*.

In [[Java]] it is customary to make the maulut-recitations the occasion for initiating certain homely industries — knitting the first meshes of a fishing-net, commencing the hem of a garment — so that "the Prophet's blessing may rest upon their task." This is not done in Acheh, where the fishermen set up their nets on a Friday, sitting at the entrance of the mosque while the devout pass in for the weekly service.

## The miraj and the 27th of Rajab

The Achehnese [[Kanduri Apam|month of Rajab]] holds its place in the official calendar of feasts chiefly because Mohammad's celebrated "journey to heaven" is supposed to have taken place on the night of (or rather the night before) the 27th of this month. For the commemoration of this night the people assemble either in the mosque or in their own houses, and a history of the [[Miraj (Ascension of Mohammad)|miraj]] (Achehnese *meureu'et*) is recited. The recitation consists of a description of the ascension in rhyming prose and verse, similar to those of the birth and life of the Prophet. This pious custom is observed in Acheh, but not to any greater extent than in other parts of the Indian Archipelago; its observance is confined to those who profess special devotion to religion.

## The Achehnese months named for the Prophet

The Achehnese calendar gives the Prophet's name to two successive months. The third month, *Mo'lot* (Rabīʿ al-awwal), is named from the [[Maulud (Mo'lot)|Maulud]] feast of his birth; and the fourth month, *[[Adbe Mo'lot]]* (Rabīʿ al-akhir, "Younger Brother of Mo'lot"), is so called because the Prophet's birth is commemorated in that month also — the *Charapha anam* being recited once more, though less solemnly. Together the two months are regarded in Acheh as specially favourable for marriage and [[Peusunat (Circumcision)|circumcision]] feasts, in striking contrast to Java, where the superstitious belief that no work of importance should be initiated before the 12th day of Mo'lot is commonly met with.

## Salutations and blessings

In the [[Traweh (Tarawih)|traweh]] recitations of the fasting month, the invocation of blessing on the Prophet serves as an interlude separating the ten pairs of *rak'ahs* from one another. The teungku says: *Allahumma salli ʿala sayyidina Muhammad* ("O God, bless our lord Mohammad"), to which the people scream the response *salala alim wata'albyhim* instead of the proper *salla 'llahu 'alaihi wasallam*. The Achehnese pronunciation of the Arabic *qullu ʿalaih* — "utter the salat-prayer for him" — is *salawalée*, and is shouted in chorus by the bystanders whenever an elder's exclamation of "Allahumma salli ʿala sayyidina Muhammad" accompanies the descent of a bridegroom from his house.

**Captain van Steenveld** was a Dutch *kapitein* of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] and *commandant* of the *oosterwacht* post outside [[Kota Radja]], who reported on [[8 October]] [[1874]] that the Atjehneezen were digging approach trenches (*loopgraven*) toward the Dutch works — the intelligence that triggered the [[Battle outside the ooster-face (13 October 1874)|battle outside the ooster-face]] of [[13 October]] [[1874]] and exposed the enemy's growing mastery of the terrain around the *zuider-* and *ooster-facen*.

## The Approaching Trenches

In the second half of September [[1874]] the Atjehneezen, resisting the Dutch clearing of the *wildernis* around the *zuider-* and *ooster-facen* with growing determination, had begun to *naderen* — to dig approach trenches — under cover of the dense *bamboe-doeri* and *nipa-bosch*. On [[17 September]] [[1874]] several thousand rounds had been fired on both sides; bullets rained on the batteries and inside the Kraton. On [[8 October]] [[1874]] van Steenveld, from his post at the *oosterwacht*, reported that the Atjehneezen were digging approach trenches — a development that, if left unchecked, would have brought the enemy within storming distance of the *ooster-face*. The news was the immediate trigger for [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s decision to send a strong column under [[Kapitein van der Straate|kapitein van der Straate]] to clear the *wildernis* before sunrise on [[13 October]] [[1874]].

## The Battle of 13 October 1874

On the morning of [[13 October]] [[1874]] van der Straate's column of 180 men tried to penetrate the *wildernis* before sunrise, in the hope of finding the far edge of the wood. The hope was disappointed; the *wildernis* extended so far that the troops had to set up in its midst. At six o'clock the woodcutters began, but the Atjehneezen discovered them and attacked from all sides. The [[Compagnie mariniers in Atjeh|mariniers]], in their first real fight, wasted ammunition and had to give up their position. The whole covering force had to retire; by eight o'clock the enemy's fire was overwhelming. Pel ordered retreat to the Kraton. Only when the artillery on the *zuider-* and *ooster-bastions* opened fire were the Atjehneezen stopped. The day cost several dead and wounded and proved that the enemy was "complete master of the terrain" near the *zuider-* and *ooster-facen*. After this, clearing work was halted. Van Steenveld's report had been accurate, and the battle it provoked showed how dangerous the Atjehneesche *nadering* had become.

**Nur al-Din al-Raniri** — Malay/Arabic *Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī*, also written *Noer Oed-Din AR-RĀNĪRI* in the older Dutch orthography — was a Gujarati-born Muslim scholar of Hadrami descent who served as the principal religious authority at the court of Sultan Iskandar II of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the late 1630s and early 1640s, and who composed, at the sultan's command, the *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]* ([[1638]]) and the *[[Sirat al-mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]* (begun [[1634]], completed [[1644]]) — the principal Malay-language works of *fiqh* and of the "mirror for princes" genre produced at the Atjehnese court. He is, in [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s reading in [[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|his survey]] of Malay literature, the principal Atjehnese religious-literary figure of the seventeenth century.

## Origin and Arrival in Atjeh

Ar-Rānīrī was born in *Ranīr* (Rander), a Muslim port-town in Gujarat on the western coast of India, of a Hadrami *sayyid* family. His Gujarati-Indian origin is central to his literary output: the *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]*, in [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s reading, is "door een Voor-Indier geschreven" (written by an Indian) and "ontleent zijn stof van talrijke anecdoten aan dezelfde en soortgelijke Moslimse verzamelwerken in de Perzische taal uit Voor-Indië" (derives his material, of numerous anecdotes, from the same and similar Muslim compilations in the Persian language from India). The same Persian-language Muslim compilations also supplied the earlier *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* of [[1607]] — a fact that, in Hooykaas's reading, points to the common Persian-language source tradition on which both Atjehnese "mirrors for princes" drew.

Ar-Rānīrī's formal arrival in Atjeh is dated to [[1637]] — a date established against the earlier assumption of [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] (*BKI* 107, p. 357) that the *[[Sirat al-mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]* had been composed before ar-Rānīrī's arrival. The correction, by [[H. Djajadiningrat|Djajadiningrat]] in *TBG* 69, pp. 109-111, establishes that the *Sirāṭ* was begun in [[1634]] — three years before ar-Rānīrī's formal arrival — but that the knowledge of Malay letters displayed in the *Sirāṭ* need not have been acquired before [[1637]].

## Compositions at the Atjehnese Court

At the command of Sultan Iskandar II — and in the same year, [[1638]] — ar-Rānīrī composed three works that together constitute the literary apparatus of his orthodox reform in Atjeh:

- The *[[Sirat al-mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]* (March [[1638]]) — the Malay handbook of religious duties (*fiqh*) of the Shāfiʿite school, the composition of which was begun in [[1634]] and completed only in [[1644]].
- The *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]* ([[1638]]) — the Malay "mirror for princes", of which the historical chapter (II.m) on the sultans of Atjeh and the religious scholars of the 16th and 17th centuries is, in [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s judgement, "wel eens de voorkeur blijken te verdienen boven de voorstelling van de SM" (sometimes proves to deserve the preference over the representation of the *Sadjarah Malajoe*).
- The *Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah* ([[1638]]) — a Malay work on the creation of the heavens and the spirits.

The composition of these three works in the second year of Iskandar Thani's reign — at the very moment of ar-Rānīrī's public disputation against the *Wujudiyyah* mystics — marks the beginning of ar-Rānīrī's project of orthodox reform: to provide the Achehnese faithful with the literary apparatus of a Shāfiʿite sultanate.

## The Public Disputation against the Wujudiyyah

The composition of the *Sirāṭ*, the *Bustān*, and the *Bad'* in [[1638]] coincides with ar-Rānīrī's public disputation against the *Wujudiyyah* — the heterodox mystical school that had been patronised at the Atjehnese court by [[Hamzah Fansuri|Hamzah Fansūrī]] and [[Shams al-Din al-Sumatrani|Shamsuddīn al-Sumatrānī]] under [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] and the early years of Iskandar Thani. Ar-Rānīrī's orthodox reform — of which the three works of [[1638]] are the literary apparatus — was the theological-literary expression of the disputation. His subsequent fall from favour at the Atjehnese court, and his departure from Atjeh, are part of the same controversy.

## Comparison with the Earlier Tādj

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] contrasts ar-Rānīrī's *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Bustān]]* with the earlier *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* of [[1607]]: "terwijl BOCHARI's Tādj meer eenvoudig stichtelijk gehouden is, is daarentegen RANĪRI's Boestān meer het werk van een geleerde, die zich niet ontziet zijn lezers te vergasten op een hoofdstuk dat belangrijk moge zijn voor een theoloog (I) of een geschiedvorser (II), maar het lezend publiek wel eens zwaar gevallen zal zijn" (while Bochari's *Tādj* is more simply edifying, Rānīrī's *Bustān* is more the work of a scholar, who does not refrain from treating his readers to a chapter that may be important for a theologian (I) or a historical researcher (II), but may have been heavy going for the reading public).

## The Diffusion of the Sirat al-mustaqim

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] records one of the key episodes in the early diffusion of ar-Rānīrī's *[[Sirat al-mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]*: when the king of Kedah converted to Islam under the influence of *Sjaich 'Abdoellāh Jamani* and was given the throne-name Sultan Moezaffar Sjah, "Toen men dit in Atjéh vernam, liet de Moslim geworden vorst aldaar Sjaich Noeroddin diens boek Sirāṭ al-moestakīm naar Kēdah brengen ter onderrichting in de Wet" (When this was heard in Atjeh, the Muslim ruler there had Sjaich Noeroddin's book *Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm* brought to Kedah for instruction in the Law). The episode — which casts ar-Rānīrī as the *Sjaich Noeroddin* of the Kedah chronicle — illustrates the role of ar-Rānīrī's Atjehnese compositions as the principal vehicle by which the *fiqh* of the Shāfiʿite school was transmitted from Atjeh to the Malay Peninsula in the early seventeenth century.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, ar-Rānīrī is significant as the principal religious-literary figure of the Atjehnese court in the late 1630s and early 1640s. His three compositions of [[1638]] — the *[[Sirat al-mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]*, the *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]*, and the *Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah* — together constitute the literary apparatus of his orthodox reform of the Atjehnese court, and the *Bustān*'s historical chapter on the sultans of Atjeh is, in Hooykaas's judgement, occasionally more reliable than the *Sadjarah Malajoe*. His diffusion of the *Sirāṭ* to Kedah is the principal evidence for the role of the Atjehnese court as the centre of Shāfiʿite *fiqh* diffusion to the Malay Peninsula in the early seventeenth century.

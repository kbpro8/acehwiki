The **Raja of Gigieng** was a coastal *radja* (raja) on the north coast of Atjeh who accepted the Dutch flag in exchange for 10 000 *rijksdaalders* in *klinkende munt* (hard cash), and whom [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] cited as evidence of the imminent collapse of Atjehneesche resistance. In [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s narrative he is the example of a *vazal* whose submission was purchased rather than won, and whose case van Swieten used to buttress his [[Van Swieten's afscheidsrede (1874)|afscheidsrede]] of [[23 April]] [[1874]] declaring the war ended.

## The Submission

The Dutch *landing at Pedro-punt* of [[6 December]] [[1873]] had taken place east of Gigieng, "waar de vijand ons stellig niet verwacht had." In the course of the subsequent operations along the strand the Dutch had come into contact with the coastal *radja*s of the north coast, whose *gebieden* lay outside the inner core of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and who were, in many cases, willing to treat with the Dutch. The Radja van Gigieng accepted the Dutch flag in exchange for 10 000 *rijksdaalders* in *klinkende munt* — a substantial sum in ready money. The transaction was, in Borel's reading, a purchase rather than a conquest: the *radja* submitted for the money, and his submission implied no allegiance to the Dutch cause on the part of the inner *sagi* of Groot-Atjeh.

## Van Swieten's Use of the Submission

Van Swieten cited the submission of the Radja van Gigieng — alongside that of other *vazalstaten* that had accepted the Dutch flag — as evidence that the Atjehneesche resistance was collapsing. In his [[Van Swieten's afscheidsrede (1874)|afscheidsrede]] of [[23 April]] [[1874]] he declared: "Mijne heeren, ik beschouw den oorlog met Atjeh als geëindigd." He gave optimistic reasons: the enemy was "geheel verslagen en ontmoedigd"; the *bevolking* lacked *wapens, munitie, kleederen* and "zelfs aan de voornaamste levensbehoeften zooals rijst, zout"; the trade was *vernietigd*; *vazalstaten* had accepted the Dutch flag. He promised the troops would have nothing to do but rest; [[Kota Radja]] would become "eene aangename gezellige garnizoensplaats." The speech "maakte op zijne toehoorders bepaald een pijnlijken indruk." "Qui veut trop prouver, ne prouve rien." Nobody believed him.

## Borel's Judgement

Borel treats the submission of the Radja van Gigieng as the type of the *vazal* submission that van Swieten mistook for the end of the war. The very next night after van Swieten's [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)|departure]] of [[26 April]] [[1874]], Kota Radja was "bij herhaling door den vijand gealarmeerd"; the [[Desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe (1874)|desecration of the Dutch graves at Lampoeloe]] and the attack on Marassa followed on the same day; and within six weeks the Dutch *bezettingskorps* was reduced to a state in which, Borel insists, 500 *kloeke* Atjehneezen would have sufficed "om den Kraton, en met dezen onze geheele vestiging ter kuste van Atjeh, ten val te brengen." The *vazal* submissions had purchased nothing.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **Radja of Gigiëng** was the Atjehnese chief who on [[20 April]] [[1874]], in the presence of a great crowd of people, gave the [[Submission of the Radja of Gigiëng (20 April 1874)|act of submission of his landscape]] — the first such submission of the [[Acheh War|war years]] to be rewarded with the immediate reopening of trade.

### The submission of 1874

During the [[Blockade of the Atjeh coast (1873-1874)|blockade of the Atjehnese coast]] the Government's first trade proclamation was circulated among the Atjehnese chiefs; whether it influenced the Radja's decision, author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] leaves in the middle. The submission was accomplished on 20 April 1874, when, in the presence of a great multitude, the Dutch flag was hoisted on the *passar* (market) at [[Gigiëng]], the Radja was presented with an act of recognition and confirmation, and the Netherlands Indian Government paid him a sum of 10,000 rijksdaalders. The Netherlands Government remained faithful to its promise and permitted trade with [[Gigiëng]] immediately after the submission, with the result that very soon a great number of trading vessels were cleared at [[Penang]] for this part of Atjeh.

**Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra** — in [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]]'s text **Sjamsuddin van Pasai**, also **Sjamsuddin Samatraʾr**, in Kreemer's account **Sjamsoeddin van Pasè** (*Sjamsoeddïn as-Samatranï*) — was the pantheistic mystic of [[Pasai|Pasai]] who dominated the religious life of the court of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] of [[Acheh]] until his death in [[1630]], and the principal continuator of [[Hamzah Pansuri|Hamzah Pansuri]]. He was the leading figure of the heterodox mystic tradition that flourished at the court of Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Moeda]] ([[1607]]–[[1636]]). His conflict with the orthodox scholar [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Noer-ad-dïn ar-Ranïrï]] would later lead to the orthodox victory under Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar II|Iskandar Thanï]], who had Sjamsoeddin executed and the works of the heterodox mystics burned.

## The continuator of Hamzah Pansuri

One of the best-known representatives of pantheistic mysticism in Acheh, Shamsuddin enjoyed much consideration at the court of Sultan Meukuta Alam (Eseukanda Muda) and died in [[1630]]. He wrote commentaries on works of his predecessor [[Hamzah Pansuri|Hamzah Pansuri]] "of Baros." The [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] treats Shamsuddin of Pasai as the direct continuator of [[Hamzah Pansuri|Hamzah Pansuri]], and his works as forming, with those of Hamzah, the canon of the [[Wujudiyyah]] mysticism against which the orthodox reaction of [[Sultan Iskandar II|Iskandar Thani]] was directed. The juxtaposition is significant: it establishes the textual genealogy of the Wujūdiyyah of Acheh, and places Shamsuddin within the school of Hamzah Pansuri.

## Works cited by Raniri

The [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri cites two works of Shamsuddin by name:

1. The **[[Mir'at al-muhakkikin|*Mirʾāt al-muḥaqqiqīn*]]** ("The Mirror of the Verifiers") — a Malay Sufi treatise setting out the doctrine of the unity of being in the form developed by Shamsuddin from the heritage of Hamzah Pansuri. Cited and refuted in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] at p. 108 of the Voorhoeve facsimile.
2. The **[[Chirkah|*Chirkah*]]** (or ***Ḥarakah***, as [[C.A.O. van Nieuwenhuijze|Van Nieuwenhuijze]] prefers to read it) — a Malay Sufi treatise cited in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] at pp. 108, 110–111.

The juxtaposition of the two works in Raniri's polemic suggests that they were used in conjunction, and that Raniri treated them as complementary expositions of the Wujūdiyyah position that he sought to refute.

## The controversy over the *al-Insān al-kāmil*

The [[Djawahir al-'ulum|*Jawāhir al-ʿulūm*]] of Raniri records that Shamsuddin of Pasai — Raniri's Wujūdiyyah opponent — also appealed to the [[al-Insan al-Kamil|*al-Insān al-kāmil*]] of [[Abd al-Karim al-Jili|ʿAbd al-Karīm al-Jīlī]], but that he interpreted the text, in Raniri's view, incorrectly. The [[al-Insan al-Kamil|*al-Insān al-kāmil*]] thus occupies a remarkable place in the controversy between Raniri and Shamsuddin: a work to which both orthodox and heterodox appealed, but which each read differently. The same work that supplied Raniri with his account of the Barāhima was, in Shamsuddin's reading, a warrant for the Wujūdiyyah position that Raniri sought to refute.

## The conflict with ar-Ranïrï and execution

When [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Noer-ad-dïn ar-Ranïrï]] returned to Atjèh in [[1637]], he found the court under Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar II|Iskandar Thanï]] still influenced by Sjamsoeddin's heterodox teachings. Ar-Ranïrï's conflict with Sjamsoeddin — conducted through the *Tibjān* and the *Jawāhir al-ʿulūm* — led to the orthodox victory: Sjamsoeddin was executed by Iskandar Thanï (around [[1630]]), and the works of the heterodox mystics — including those of [[Hamzah Pansuri|Hamzah Pansoeri]] — were burned before the great mosque **Beit ar-rahman**.

Kreemer frames this conflict as a pivotal moment in the religious history of Atjèh — the triumph of orthodox Islam over the heterodox mysticism that had flourished under Iskandar Moeda. This orthodox victory shaped the subsequent development of Atjèhnese Islam and its institutions.

## The modern study

The modern study of Shamsuddin of Pasai was inaugurated by [[C.A.O. van Nieuwenhuijze|C. A. O. van Nieuwenhuijze]] in his Leiden dissertation *Sjamsu'l-Dīn van Pasai: bijdrage tot de kennis van de Sumatraansche mystiek* (1945) and in his subsequent study "Sjamsu'l-Dīn van Pasai" in *BKI* 104 (1948), pp. 326–370. The dissertation includes a brief translation of the section of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] in which the Wujūdiyyah are treated (pp. 206–213 of the dissertation), and the 1948 study extends the work with the edition of two short texts and a portion of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri (the section on the Wujūdiyyah: f. 47b = p. 93 to f. 57a = p. 112 of the Leiden facsimile). The [[1955]] edition of [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] gives, for the first time, a complete facsimile of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] and so makes possible a fuller study of Raniri's polemic against Shamsuddin.

## Significance

Shamsuddin of Pasai is the principal pantheistic mystic of the court of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] of [[Acheh]] and the principal continuator of [[Hamzah Pansuri|Hamzah Pansuri]]. The Voorhoeve account of [[1955]] adds substantially to the picture drawn by Snouck Hurgronje in *The Achehnese* by documenting the textual basis of Shamsuddin's school: the *[[Mir'at al-muhakkikin|Mirʾāt al-muḥaqqiqīn*]] and the *[[Chirkah|Chirkah*]] are the principal surviving works of Shamsuddin, and the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri preserves, in its polemical citations, the only extended fragments of these works. The controversy over the interpretation of the [[al-Insan al-Kamil|*al-Insān al-kāmil*]] between Raniri and Shamsuddin is one of the principal documents of the religious controversy of Acheh in the 1630s. Kreemer's account, in turn, situates the conflict within the larger political frame of Iskandar Moeda's heterodox court and the orthodox reaction under Iskandar Thanï — a frame in which the execution of Shamsuddin and the burning of the heterodox works before the *Beit ar-rahman* mark the formal close of the Wujūdiyyah ascendancy at the Atjèhnese court.

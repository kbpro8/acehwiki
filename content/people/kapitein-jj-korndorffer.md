**Kapitein J. J. Korndörffer** was a Dutch marine officer who led multiple detachments of the [[Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers]] to [[Atjeh]] during the cholera epidemic of [[1874]]–[[1875]], notably the departure on [[12 December]] [[1874]] and the subsequent operations against the [[Mibouw]] and [[Gitjiel]] strongholds. His command was praised in contemporary reports and he later received the fourth‑class [[Militaire Willems‑orde]].

## Military Service in Atjeh
In early [[February]] [[1874]] a cholera outbreak struck [[Atjeh]], prompting the Dutch colonial administration to reinforce its forces. On [[12 December]] [[1874]] a detachment under Korndörffer’s command—two lieutenants and 60 mariniers—set sail from the Netherlands to augment the [[eskader]] in the [[East Indies]] and the marine garrison in Atjeh. A second detachment left on [[13 February]] [[1875]] under Kapitein H. W. de Gelder, but Korndörffer’s unit remained active throughout the campaign, participating in the construction of bentengs at [[Lemboeq-O]] and [[Lemboeq-N]].

## Operations and Engagements
Korndörffer’s forces took part in the occupation of [[Kampong Lohong]] on [[25 April]] [[1875]], the assault on [[Gitjiel]] on [[2 May]] [[1875]], and the decisive battle for [[Mibouw]] in May [[1875]]. His reports describe the harsh conditions, including the continued spread of cholera among the troops.

## Honors
By royal decree of [[28 October]] [[1876]] Korndörffer was appointed a knight of the fourth class of the [[Militaire Willems‑orde]], recognizing his bravery and leadership during the Aceh campaigns.

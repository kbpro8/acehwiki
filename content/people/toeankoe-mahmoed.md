**Toeankoe Mahmoed** was an Atjèhnese administrator — a scion of a branch of a prominent Atjèhnese family, educated at the administrative school in Batavia, who after some years in the administrative service on Celebes returned to Aceh in the capacity of Indigenous official at the disposal of the Resident — whom Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* identifies as "the most important figure among them," that is, among the Atjèhnese notables of the pre-war period who were not *oelèëbalang*'s. His combination of traditional descent, modern administrative training, and a return from colonial service outside Atjèh made him, in Piekaar's reading, the archetype of the modern Atjèhnese administrator.

## Education and early career

Toeankoe Mahmoed was "educated at the administrative school in Batavia" — the colonial training institution that prepared indigenous officials for the administrative service. The choice of an administrative career — rather than the traditional *oelèëbalang*'s inheritance — marked the modern orientation of the branch of the family to which he belonged. After completing his training, he "held a position in the administrative service on Celebes for some years" — that is, he served outside Atjèh, in the residency of Celebes, where the Dutch administrative system provided the framework within which indigenous officials could be deployed across the archipelago.

## The return to Atjèh

After his years on Celebes, Toeankoe Mahmoed "finally returned to Aceh in the capacity of Indigenous official at the disposal of the Resident." The return — the re-deployment of an Atjèhnese administrator, with experience outside the residency, to the service of the Resident of Atjèh — reflected the Dutch administration's policy of incorporating modern-trained indigenous officials into the residency administration. The "Indigenous official at the disposal of the Resident" was a position that combined administrative competence with the cultural authority of an Atjèhnese notable: the official could be deployed by the Resident wherever his services were needed, and his Atjèhnese identity gave him an entrée that a non-Atjèhnese official would have lacked.

## The most important figure

Piekaar's identification of Toeankoe Mahmoed as "the most important figure among them" — among the Atjèhnese notables who were not *oelèëbalang*'s — places him at the head of a category of Atjèhnese political actors who were neither traditional territorial chiefs nor religious leaders. The category — modern-trained administrators, often with experience outside Atjèh, who returned to the residency to serve the Dutch administration — was, in Piekaar's account, one of the principal sources of the political leadership that would emerge in the post-war period. The career pattern of Toeankoe Mahmoed — administrative school, service outside Atjèh, return as Indigenous official at the disposal of the Resident — was the pattern that would produce, in the next generation, the republican governors and administrators of the Indonesian Republic.

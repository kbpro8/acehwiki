**R. L. Mellema** — *Drs. R. L. Mellema*, full name Reinier Lambertus Mellema — was a Dutch Javanist and ethnologist who served as *Oud-Leraar in de Javaansche Taal en Letterkunde* (former teacher of the Javanese language and literature) at the *Oosters Letterkundige Afdeeling* (Oriental Literature Section) of the *[[AMS Djogjakarta|Algemene Middelbare School te Djogjakarta]]*, and afterwards as *Taalkundig Medewerker* (linguistic collaborator) at the *[[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Afdeeling Volkenkunde van het Indisch Instituut te Amsterdam]]* (Ethnology Section of the Indisch Institute at Amsterdam). He is the author of *[[De Islaam in Indonesië (Mellema, 1947)|De Islaam in Indonesië (in het bijzonder op Java)]]* (Amsterdam, [[1947]]), the concise ethnological survey of Muslim institutions in the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] in which the Atjehnese forms of the [[Moekim and Imeum in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|moekim–imeum system]], the [[Rangkang|meunasah/rangkang]], the [[Oelama zakaat war-chest in the Atjehoorlog (Mellema 1947)|zakaat war-chest of the Atjehoorlog]], the [[Haddji title prestige in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|prestige of the Haddji title]], and the [[Arabic-inscribed graves in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|oldest Arabic-inscribed graves]] are set in comparative relation to the Javanese pattern.

## Career

Mellema's career, as recorded on the title page of his [[De Islaam in Indonesië (Mellema, 1947)|1947 survey]], combined two successive posts: first, the teaching of Javanese language and literature at the Oriental Literature Section of the *Algemene Middelbare School* (the General Secondary School, commonly abbreviated *AMS*) at [[Djogjakarta|Djogjakarta]] in central [[Java|Java]]; and second, a linguistic staff appointment at the Ethnology Section of the *Indisch Instituut* in [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]]. The former post placed him among the Dutch colonial scholars trained in the classical literatures of Java — the *babad*, the *wayang* corpus, the court chronicles — while the latter placed him within the institutional framework of metropolitan ethnology that the *Indisch Instituut* had inherited from the earlier *Koloniaal Instituut* and the *Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen* tradition.

The move from Djogjakarta to Amsterdam — from teaching Javanese in the colony to compiling ethnological *Mededeelingen* in the metropolis — was a common trajectory for Dutch Indies scholars in the late colonial and immediate post-colonial period, and it is the trajectory by which Mellema came to write the Islam survey of [[1947]].

## The Islam survey of 1947

Mellema's *[[De Islaam in Indonesië (Mellema, 1947)|De Islaam in Indonesië]]* was issued as *Mededeling* No. LXXVII (and No. 25 of the *Afdeling Volkenkunde*) of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] in [[1947]]. In his introduction, Mellema states that the work is not a systematic treatise on Islam but a guide for the "vreemdeling in Indonesia" — the stranger in Indonesia — who comes face to face with a new world and an unfamiliar religion. The ordering of the topics is therefore pedagogic rather than systematic: the traditional Javanese city, the mosque, the prayer, the fast, the alms, the pilgrimage, the religious education, the creed, and the burial — each chosen because it is what the newcomer would first encounter.

Although the subtitle confines the detailed treatment to Java, Mellema draws on [[Acheh|Atjeh]] at five distinctive points — the moekim–imeum system, the meunasah/rangkang, the zakaat war-chest, the Haddji title, and the Arabic-inscribed graves — and his *Register* explicitly flags the Atjehnese entries. This Atjeh content, though brief, is the distinctive contribution of the survey to the comparative ethnology of Islam in the archipelago, and it is the reason the work enters the AcehWiki corpus as a source independent of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|De Atjehers]]*.

## Scholarly posture

Mellema writes as a Javanist addressing a Dutch metropolitan readership. His method is descriptive and comparative: he sets the Javanese form of each institution beside its Atjehnese, Sundanese, Sumatran, or Arab counterpart, and he preserves the indigenous terms (*aloen-aloen*, *langgar*, *soerambi*, *bedoeg*, *penghoeloe*, *meunasah*, *rangkang*, *imeum*, *moekim*) in italics with glosses. He is careful to distinguish what is canonical — prescribed by the *Koraan* or the *overlevering* (tradition) — from what is *adat* (customary), and he repeatedly notes the degeneration or adaptation of canonical forms in Indonesian practice (the *priesterraad* that is "no council or court" in the European sense; the *penghoeloe* who is "no priest" since Islam knows no priesthood).

The survey is thus a document of the late colonial comparative ethnology of Islam — written, in [[1947]], from the retrospective position of a scholar whose institutional base, the *Indisch Instituut*, was itself a relic of the colonial order that the Indonesian revolution was then ending.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Mellema's significance is that of an independent comparative source. Where [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *[[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers (1893-94)|De Atjehers]]* of [[1893]]–[[1894]] gives the canonical monographic account of Atjehnese Islam from a scholar who had lived in the Atjehnese milieu, Mellema's survey of [[1947]] gives a brief, comparative, Javanist's view of the same institutions — one that sets them beside the Javanese and treats them as points on an Indonesian spectrum rather than as the autonomous object of a monograph. The complementarity is valuable: Mellema's framing of the [[Moekim and Imeum in Atjeh (Mellema 1947)|moekim]] as a territorial congregation arising from the practical need of several *desa*'s to unite for Friday service, of the [[Rangkang|meunasah/rangkang]] as one regional name among many for the village prayer-house, and of the [[Oelama zakaat war-chest in the Atjehoorlog (Mellema 1947)|zakaat]] as a canonical category put to underground use in the [[Aceh War|Atjehoorlog]] — each is a comparative observation that Snouck's monographic method does not foreground in the same way.

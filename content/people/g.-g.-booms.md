**G. G. Booms** was a general of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] and prominent nineteenth-century colonial military author, best known for his [[1875]] treatise *Over de eerste expeditie tegen Atjeh: Een antwoord aan Generaal de Stuers*, which defended Dutch military commanders against governmental scapegoating following the defeat of the [[First Aceh Expedition]].

## Career and Historiographical Debate

A veteran staff officer and military commentator, [[G. G. Booms]] engaged in a high-profile public controversy in [[1875]] with General [[F. V. A. de Stuers]]. After de Stuers published an April [[1875]] article in *De Gids* blaming the failure of the spring [[1873]] campaign on tactical errors by General [[J. H. R. Köhler]] and General [[E. C. van Daalen]], Booms wrote his comprehensive monograph to refute de Stuers.

## Analysis of the First Expedition

In his [[1875]] work, [[G. G. Booms]] demonstrated that the primary causes of the defeat of the [[First Aceh Expedition]] were systemic governmental and institutional shortcomings of the [[Dutch East Indies Government]]:
1. Overhasty declaration of war without adequate political preparation ([[Parliamentary Note on Relations with Aceh 1872-1873]]).
2. Profound ignorance of Acehnese geography and society (*zeer geringe kennis van land en volk*).
3. The complete absence of a permanent [[General Staff of the KNIL]].
4. Timing the invasion immediately before the [[Southwest Monsoon in Aceh Operations]].
5. Insufficient naval blockade forces.

Booms also highlighted the rejected [[1871]] reconnaissance proposal of General [[T. H. Kroesen]] and cited the [[Jonckbloet Note of 10 May 1873]] to prove that contemporary observers recognized these governmental failings.

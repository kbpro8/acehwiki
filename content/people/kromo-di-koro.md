**Kromo-di-Koro** was a Javanese *maréchaussée* of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* who, though wounded, assumed command of his *[[Brigade (Maréchaussée)|brigade]]* on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|west coast of Atjeh]] during the action of [[1925]]–[[1927]] after both Captain [[J. Paris]] and the brigade commander had been killed.

## Assumption of command

In the [[Gevecht waarin kapitein Paris sneuvelde (Westkust van Atjeh, 1925-1927)|engagement in which Captain Paris fell]], both the captain and the *brigade-commandant* were killed. The [[1930]] jubilee number records: "De gewonde Javaansche Maréchaussée Kromo-di-Koro nam het bevel op zich, totdat hij door bloedverlies ineen dreigde te zakken" — the wounded Javanese Maréchaussée Kromo-di-Koro took the command upon himself, until loss of blood threatened to make him collapse.

Before transferring the command to the Menadonese *maréchaussée*-soldier [[Suwoek]], he first ordered a number of shots to be fired into the surrounding *[[Alang-alang|alang-alang]]* (cogon grass), in order to drive out any Atjehnese who might be concealed there. It was that fire which caused the opponent seen by [[Sumangkut]] to break cover.

## Remembered by Snell

Colonel [[L. A. Snell]], interviewed in the same issue, named Kromo-di-Koro last in his list of "de eenvoudigen van ziel" — the simple of soul — who ought not to be allowed to fall into oblivion, alongside [[Marks]], [[F. Ch. Frank|Frank]], [[W. J. Mosselman|Mosselman]], [[J. C. Heijnen|Heynen]], [[Emond]], [[Rugebregt]], [[Salawono]], [[Kasir]], [[Wowor]] and [[Nussy]], calling them "de dappersten onder de dapperen" — the bravest among the brave.

The jubilee number concluded its account of the episode with the judgement that "de oude geest van het Korps Maréchaussée was bij zijn manschappen blijven voortleven" — the old spirit of the Corps Maréchaussée had continued to live on among its men.

**H. G. Boumeester** was a lieutenant-general of the Indies army, commander of the Indies army in the late [[1870s]], who arrived in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on [[19 August]] [[19 August]] [[1879]] — after the close of large-scale operations — to inform himself personally of the state of affairs, and whose report paid great tribute to the governor [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]]: "On the whole it appears to me that the course followed since June 1878 has produced the expected results, and it is certain that the favourable and, comparatively speaking, even very favourable outcome that has now been obtained is due in very considerable measure to the energetic and skilful way in which the commander has carried out the instructions given him by the government... The nation is undoubtedly greatly indebted to him."

## The Visit to Atjeh, 19 August 1879

After the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of [[13 August]] [[13 August]] – [[15 August]] [[15 August]] [[1879]] and the dissolution of the active columns on [[5 September]] [[5 September]] [1879], Boumeester arrived in Atjeh on [[19 August]] [[19 August]] [1879] to inform himself personally of the state of affairs. His visit was the formal recognition by the commander of the Indies army of the success of Van der Heyden's campaigns of [[1878]]-[[1879]] — the [[Capture of Montassik (28 July 1878)|capture of Montassik]] of [[28 July]] [[28 July]] [[1878]], the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman]] of [[13 October]] [[13 October]] [[1878]], the [[Capture of Indrapuri (24 March 1879)|capture of Indrapuri]] of [[24 March]] [[24 March]] [[1879]], the [[Capture of Gleiêng (9 July 1879)|capture of Gleiêng]] of [[9 July]] [[9 July]] [[1879]], the [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|submission of the XXVI Moekims]] of [[4 July]] [[4 July]] [[1879]], and the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of August.

## The Report

In his report Boumeester brought great homage to Van der Heyden:

> "On the whole it appears to me that the course followed since June 1878 has produced the expected results, and it is certain that the favourable and, comparatively speaking, even very favourable outcome that has now been obtained is due in very considerable measure to the energetic and skilful way in which the commander has carried out the instructions given him by the government. Was he in the performance of this task powerfully supported by his chiefs of staffs and services, the various corps-commanders and further officers and rank-and-file, who on the whole excelled by devotion and loyal performance of duty — certain it is that the powerful individuality of the commander has given the decision. To the bold, skilfully designed plan of operations, according to which Indrapuri was mastered, the right river-bank cleared, Gleiêng attacked, the XXVI Moekims entered from various sides, and Selimoen visited by columns from Lamtobah and Indrapuri, we owe the great success. Only in this way could so much be obtained in relatively short time. The commander knew no obstacles and went with firm will towards the proposed goal. The nation is undoubtedly greatly indebted to him."

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

Verstege records the commander-in-chief's inspection: from [[19 August]] to [[20 September]] [[1879]] luitenant-generaal Boumeester, Kommandant van het Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger, visited [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and crossed the territory of [[Groot-Atjeh]] escorted only by a small cover, "zonder een schot gehoord, zonder een vijand gezien te hebben" ("without hearing a shot, without having seen an enemy"). The report laid by that army commander before the government was of the brightest: "Naar het schijnt, is eindelijk de overtuiging tot de Atjehsche bevolking doorgedrongen, dat wij de sterksten zijn, en dat het hopeloos is zich langer te verzetten" ("It seems that at last the conviction has penetrated to the Atjehnese population that we are the stronger, and that it is hopeless to resist any longer"). In the same book he also appears as the artillery commander of the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) Kreemer's account|first expedition]] (luitenant-kolonel Boumeester), whose general report to the inquiry is quoted about the too heavy 8 cm guns (see [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]]).

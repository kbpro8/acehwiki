**Hans Decker** was a Dutch sailor of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage of 1599]] who survived the [[11 September]] [[11 September]] [[1599]] ambush at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in which [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] was killed, was taken prisoner with [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] and held at the Achehnese court, and — by getting a letter aboard one of the ships of [[Jacob Wilckens|Jacob Wilckens]]'s fleet in [[1600]] describing the fate of the *Leeuw* and the *Leeuwin* — was the principal source of the Dutch' first intelligence about the fate of the Houtman brothers' voyage. Decker is the principal Dutch captive of the early Atjeh period.

## The Houtman ambush and captivity

Decker was among the survivors of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|ambush of 11 September 1599]] at Atjeh — the attack in which [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] was killed in the cabin of the *Leeuw* and [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] was taken captive on the *Leeuwin* or in the sloop at [[Pedir|Pedir]]. Decker was held with Houtman and the other survivors at the Achehnese court, where the sultan put them to work on his fleet against [[Djohor|Djohor]] and against the Portuguese at sea.

## The 1600 letter aboard Wilckens's ship

When the four ships of [[Jacob van Neck|Jacob van Neck]]'s fleet under Vice-Admiraal [[Jacob Wilckens|Jacob Wilckens]] anchored off Atjeh on [[31 July]] [[1600]] — the first Dutch fleet to appear after the Houtman ambush — Decker was among the captives who heard the salute guns fire. [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] recorded in his narrative that Decker managed to get a letter aboard one of Wilckens's ships detailing what had befallen the *Leeuw* and the *Leeuwin*: the ambush, the killing of Cornelis, the captivity of Frederik and the surviving crew. The letter was the principal source of the Dutch' first intelligence about the fate of the Houtman brothers' voyage.

The letter — and "andere gebeurtenissen" that had "het wantrouwen van de Hollanders opgewekt" — led Wilckens to decline the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan]]'s invitation to come ashore for a visit. The sultan's displeasure fell on Decker and the boy who had carried the letter aboard: "zoowel Hans Decker als de jongen die den brief aan boord had gebracht moesten het ontgelden". The Portuguese at Atjeh seized the occasion to strengthen their position, pressing the sultan to cede them his strongest fort in compensation for promised help against [[Djohor|Djohor]] — a demand that "bij den vorst wantrouwen wekte".

## The release (1600)

Decker remained in captivity until the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|arrival of Paulus van Caerden on 21 September 1600]]. The sultan, on Van Caerden's arrival, released Decker and the two other surviving Dutch captives — "klaarblijkelijk om de mededingers der Portugeezen zijn goede gezindheid te betuigen, althans om hen gunstig te stemmen". The release was the principal diplomatic achievement of Van Caerden's Atjeh visit and the proximate cause of the negotiations that produced the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|pepper contract of 28 December 1600]].

## Significance

Hans Decker is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch captive of the early Atjeh period and the principal source of the Dutch' first intelligence about the fate of the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' voyage]]. His letter aboard [[Jacob Wilckens|Wilckens]]'s ship in [[1600]] — describing the ambush, the killing of [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis]], and the captivity of [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik]] — was the first concrete intelligence the Dutch received of the Houtman brothers' fate, and led Wilckens to decline the sultan's invitation to come ashore. Decker's release by [[Paulus van Caerden|Van Caerden]] in [[1600]] was the principal diplomatic achievement of the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|Van Caerden visit]] and the proximate cause of the pepper contract of [[28 December]] [[1600]]. Decker's role also frames the early Dutch-Atjeh relationship as one in which the Dutch captives at the sultan's court — Decker, [[Frederik de Houtman|Houtman]], and the other survivors — were the principal conduit of intelligence and the principal diplomatic instruments of the Dutch-Achehnese relationship.

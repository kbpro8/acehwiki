**Veltman** — *Veltman* — was the Dutch maréchaussée-officer known throughout Atjeh as ***Toean Padoman*** — *the Guide*. He appears at no. 8 in the 1930 40-year jubilee photograph of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussée]] as *Gen. Maj. tit. Veltman* — by then a *titulair generaal-majoor* (titular major-general). He is, with [[Schmidt]] and a few others, one of Zentgraff's chosen examples of the *goede patrouilleleiders* (good patrol-leaders) who knew Atjeh from the inside.

## The Potjoet Meurah affair

On a patrol in [[Pidië]] a forname Atjehsche, [[Potjoet Meurah]], was arrested on suspicion of hiding a klewang under her *kain*. She drew a *rentjong* and *stormde op de brigade in* (charged the brigade) with the cry *"Dan ben ik liever dood"* — Then I'd rather be dead. The brigade had no choice: she was *zwaar gewond* (severely wounded) — two *houwen in den schedel*, two in the shoulder, the Achilles-heel of one foot cut through. A sergeant asked: *"Zal ik haar het genadeschot geven?"* — Shall I give her the mercy-shot? Veltman's answer was a sharp *"ben je bedonderd?"* — are you crazy? Days later, at the *keudé Biheuë* between Sigli and Padang Tidji, Veltman heard that Potjoet Meurah was still alive and plotting to have the entire *Moekim* murdered. He had the kampong searched house by house; *na letterlijk in elk hoekje te hebben gesnuffeld* she was found, hidden behind cloths, with a *papje van koelendrek* on her wounds. She refused a doctor — *liever dood dan een kaphé aan haar lichaam*. Veltman, *die de Atjehsche taal goed machtig was*, spoke to her *met de hoffelijkheid waarop eene vrouw van hare standing recht had*, and at length she accepted treatment from this soldier that she had refused from the doctor. He washed the wounds in which the *maden wriemelden* (maggots writhed) and bandaged them. She recovered, though *kreupel* (lame) for life. The famous *vechtjas* of Lombok, kolonel Scheurer, on reading the journal of her lone charge against eighteen carbines, came to see her, *nam de houding aan* and saluted: *de vingers aan den rand van de kepi. "Zeg haar," zeide hij tot Veltman, "dat ik een en al bewondering voor haar ben."* This Veltman did; *er trok een glimlach over haar vermagerd gezicht*.

## Potjoet Barin, the wooden leg

When the *amoureuze* [[Potjoet Barin]], *oeléëbalang* of Toengkob in the Boven-Wojla, was overvalled by a patrol under luit. Hoogers and took a bullet in the lower leg, she submitted only when the foot *rotte af* (rotted off) for want of treatment. *Haar haat jegens de Kompeuni maakte plaats voor dankbaarheid* — her hatred of the Kompeuni gave way to gratitude. She said to Veltman: *"Had ik u maar eerder gekend, dan had ik mijn been niet verloren"* — Had I known you earlier, I would not have lost my leg. Veltman *werkte te bewerken dat zij in Toengkob tot oeléëbalang werd benoemd* (worked it so that she was appointed oeléëbalang in Toengkob) and arranged for a *houten been* (wooden leg) to come from Holland — covered with leather, *tegen den paalworm* (against the teredo worm), as the officers said. *De koning te rijk* was she when first she put it on.

## Acceptance of Tjoet Nja Din's surrender

The chief who came to Veltman to betray the *schuilplaats* of the blind old [[Tjoet Nja Din]] did so on condition that Veltman guaranteed her *lijfsbehoud* (personal safety) with *eene behoorlijke bejegening als waarop eene vrouw van hare standing recht had* (the proper treatment to which a woman of her standing was entitled). *Veltman accepteerde dit, en hij vond haar schuilplaats* — Veltman accepted this, and found her hiding-place. Tjoet Nja Din, *machteloos door hare blindheid*, stabbed at the Atjeher who had betrayed her; the goodwill she did not understand, only that she was in the hands of the *kaphé*'s.

## The pawang rimoeëng course

Veltman, *nog always in Atjeh bekend als "Toean Padoman"*, lived through the following case at the bivak Tapa Toean with a *pawang rimoeëng* (tiger-charmer). A schildwacht woke him: *"Ton, er zijn tijgers in het bivak; mag ik schieten?"* Two tigers were hit and jumped the *pagger* into the bosch. The *pawang* promised to charm a tigress out of the bosch at kampong Peuloemat. Veltman, *toegestaan met het vergrijp*, was ordered to come and watch; the *pawang* circled three times round a *kandang*, muttered and prayed, and the tigress was shot. Veltman *had al heel wat verhalen over de kunsten van deze pawangs gehoord, maar dit staaltje trof hem* — had heard many stories of these pawangs' arts, but this example struck him. *Hieraan was het te danken dat Veltman nu niet in de wildernis leeft als een gerespecteerd pawang rimoeëng, doch als gepensionneerd generaal in een aardige villa onder Oud-Wassenaar* — it is to this that Veltman now lives not in the wilderness as a respected pawang rimoeëng, but as a retired general in a pleasant villa near Oud-Wassenaar.

## Lhong article in "Sumatraantjes"

Veltman also appears in [[H.C. Zentgraff|Zentgraff]]'s *Sumatraantjes* article on [[Lhong]]: it is his *cordaat karakter* that the chapter on Potjoet Barin and Potjoet Meurah illustrates, and his knowledge of the *Atjehsche taal* that brought him the *Toean Padoman* nickname.

## Significance

Veltman is Zentgraff's type of the Dutch officer who fought the war and at the same time, *met een goed hart*, made the personal accommodations — the wounded woman's bandages, the wooden leg, the acceptance of the old blind widow's surrender on terms — that turned enemies into something like friends. *Mannen van dit slag stellen meer vertrouwen in een scherp staal en een goede dienstrevolver* — men of this kind put more trust in a sharp steel and a good service-revolver. He donated the *rantej boei* amulet (gift of [[Waki Wahab]]) to the Koloniaal Museum at Amsterdam.

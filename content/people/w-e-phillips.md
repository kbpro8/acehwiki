W. E. Phillips was a British East India Company official who served as Governor of [[Penang]] from August [[1819]], on the [[Death of Bannerman|death]] of [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Colonel Bannerman]], until succeeded by [[Robert Fullerton]]. In the Achehnese record he is significant for his handling of the [[Syf's pension at Penang|Syf pension question]], the [[Lucas mission to Telok Semawé (February 1821)|Lucas affair]], the early phase of [[Said Akil's Deli adventure (1824-1828)|Said Akil's Deli adventure]], and for his [[Pinang Nota (1825)|Pinang Nota]] of November [[1823]], in which the British government's interference in Achehnese affairs was formally renounced.

## The Syf Pension and the Wilhelmina Mutineers

Soon after taking office, Phillips was confronted with the consequences of [[Syf's flight to Calcutta|Syf ul-Alum's flight to Calcutta]] and his return to Penang on a pension of 500 dollars a month charged to Sultan [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]]. When, in February [[1821]], the Dutch governor of [[Malacca]], [[Timmerman Thyssen|Timmerman Thyssen]], dispatched Colonel [[Colonel E. Lucas|E. Lucas]] in the man-of-war *Irene* to [[Telok Semawé]] to demand the surrender of the [[Wilhelmina mutiny and murder of Bouwens|Wilhelmina mutineers]] and their plunder, Phillips opposed the project. In his minute of [[1 February 1821]] he argued that, since Syf had acted as a sovereign at Telok Semawé at the time of the mutiny, no competent tribunal existed on Penang to adjudicate the Dutch claims; and that, given the British defensive alliance with [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Sultan Jauhar Alum]], Dutch warships should be dissuaded from proceeding to the coast of Acheh. The Penang government's letter to Thyssen of [[7 February 1821]], drafted on these principles, is printed in summary in Van der Kemp's text, and the [[W. A. Clubley|Clubley]] reply to Lucas of [[12 February 1821]] makes clear that Penang would make no further enquiry of Syf.

## Relations with Sultan Jauhar Alum and the Three Sagis

Throughout [[1822]] Phillips urged [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] to leave [[Pedir]] and re-establish himself at [[Banda Acheh|the capital]]. The sultan's reply of [[15 April 1822]], also summarised by Van der Kemp, begged for patience: the capital was in ruins, the revenues were uncollectable, and a meeting with [[Panglima Polim]] was promised within months. In December of the same year Phillips received the [[Three Sagis' letter to Penang (4 December 1822)|celebrated letter of the three Sagis]] declaring that they had sworn on the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong|Tunhar Anjung]] at [[Kampong Java]] never to receive [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] again, and requesting that [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf ul-Alum]] be sent back to them as sultan. Phillips replied firmly that, the British Government being on terms of amity with Sultan Jauhar Alum, he could not «concert measures or hold correspondence with the enemies of that Prince».

## The Pinang Nota of November 1823 and the Said Akil Affair

After the [[Death of Sultan Jauhar Alum|death of Sultan Jauhar Alum]] in December [[1823]], the Penang government's policy of non-interference was formally stated in the [[Pinang Nota (1825)|Pinang Nota]] of November [[1823]], in which Phillips expressed his satisfaction at the [[Sartorius mission to Acheh|Sartorius mission]] and announced that, save for representations to the Supreme Government, he would «scrupulously abstain from forming any more political relations with the Dutch» and from interfering in the affairs of [[Acheh]], [[Kedah]] and [[Siam]].

Phillips was still governor when, in [[1824]], [[Sultan of Deli|the Sultan of Deli]] wrote to him on [[27 August 1824]] announcing that he had adopted [[Said Akil|Syed Akil]] — the son of [[Said Hussein|Syed Hussein]] and younger brother of [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf ul-Alum]] — as a son. When Akil later armed a force to seize the government of [[Deli]] in [[1826]], Phillips dispatched the Malay translator [[John Anderson|John Anderson]] with a letter of [[11 April 1826]] requiring Akil to return to Penang. The Sultan of Deli's letter to Phillips's successor [[Robert Fullerton|Fullerton]] describes Phillips as one «with whom I was on terms of friendship» and expresses regret at his impending return to [[Europe]].

**Bischoff** was a Dutch colonial army officer who is recorded in the *De geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers van 1665-1945* as commanding Dutch forces in two early-nineteenth-century Sumatran expeditions: as **Lieutenant-colonel Bischoff** in the [[17 June]] [[1821]] phase of the second Dutch expedition against [[Palembang]], and as **Colonel Bischoff** in the [[27 August]] [[1824]] expedition against the [[Sultan of Aceh]]. His [[1824]] operation at the island of [[Gombora]] cleared the way for further Dutch advances up the river to the Aceh capital.

## The 1821 Palembang Expedition

As a **Lieutenant-colonel**, Bischoff commanded a column of 800 men, including a rear guard of 50 [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|*mariniers*]] (Dutch marines), during the [[17 June]] [[1821]] phase of the second Dutch expedition against [[Palembang]]. His forces attempted a rear attack on the enemy fortifications at the Peladjoe but were repulsed (p. 94). The column formed part of the larger force under [[General-majoor De Kock]] and [[Captain Lewe van Aduard]], and the operation faced numerous logistical difficulties, including obstructed waterways and strong enemy batteries.

## The 1824 Aceh Expedition and the Capture of the Gombora Battery

By [[1824]], promoted to **Colonel**, Bischoff led the landing party that seized the battery on the island of [[Gombora]] and raised the Dutch flag during the [[1824]] expedition against the [[Sultan of Aceh]]. On the morning of [[27 August]] [[1824]], he sailed with a small squadron, including the vessels *Venus* and *Ajjax*, and forced a passage through a palisade barrier to storm the battery on [[Gombora]]. After a brief fight the Dutch forces planted their flag and silenced the enemy guns (pp. 95-96). The success allowed the Dutch navy to continue upriver, eventually reaching the royal palace (the *kraton*) where the Aceh sultan was taken into custody and later exiled to [[Ternate]].

## Significance

Bischoff's two recorded operations — at [[Palembang]] in [[1821]] and at [[Gombora]] in [[1824]] — illustrate the integrated use of [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|*mariniers*]] and army columns in early-nineteenth-century Dutch colonial warfare in [[Sumatra]]. The Korps Mariniers history records him at two successive ranks (Lieutenant-colonel and Colonel) across two distinct campaigns three years apart; the source does not explicitly identify the two mentions as the same officer, but the surname, era, and operational context are consistent with a single career spanning the [[1821]] Palembang and [[1824]] Aceh expeditions.

**Sjaich Noeroddin** — Arabic *Shaykh Nūr al-Dīn*, also written *Sjaich Noeroddin* in the older Dutch orthography — is the Atjehnese-based Islamic scholar whom [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] identifies, in [[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|his survey]] of Malay literature, as the author of the *[[Sirat al-Mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]* and the *[[Bustan al-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]*, and as the figure whose *Sirāṭ* was sent from Atjeh to Kedah for the instruction of the new Muslim court there. Hooykaas's identification of the *Sjaich Noeroddin* of the Kedah chronicle with [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] of Atjeh makes the *Sjaich Noeroddin* the principal vehicle by which the *fiqh* of the Shāfiʿite school was transmitted from Atjeh to the Malay Peninsula in the early seventeenth century.

## The Identification with ar-Raniri

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s identification of the *Sjaich Noeroddin* of the Kedah chronicle with [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|ar-Rānīrī]] of Atjeh rests on the convergence of name, office, and date:

- The *Sjaich Noeroddin* of the Kedah chronicle is named as the author of a book called *Sirāṭ al-moestakīm* — the same title as the Malay *fiqh* handbook begun by ar-Rānīrī in [[1634]] and completed in [[1644]].
- The *Sjaich Noeroddin* is described as an Islamic scholar whose book was sent from Atjeh — the same Atjehnese court at which ar-Rānīrī served as the principal religious authority under Sultan Iskandar II in the late 1630s and early 1640s.
- The episode falls in the same chronological frame as ar-Rānīrī's Atjehnese period — the early seventeenth century, after the composition of the *[[Sirat al-Mustaqim|Sirāṭ]]* in [[1634]]-[[1638]] and before the fall of the Atjehnese court's patronage of ar-Rānīrī in the religious controversy of the early 1640s.

The identification is not stated explicitly by Hooykaas, but is implicit in his treatment of the *Sjaich Noeroddin* of the Kedah chronicle and the *[[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]]* of Atjeh as the same figure. The convergence of name, office, and date makes the identification probable, though not certain.

## The Kedah Episode

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] records the Kedah episode in his chapter on Malay historiography, in his account of the *Hikajat Marong Mahawangsa* (the Kedah chronicle):

"Toen men dit in Atjéh vernam, liet de Moslim geworden vorst aldaar Sjaich Noeroddin diens boek Sirāṭ al-moestakīm naar Kēdah brengen ter onderrichting in de Wet." (When this was heard in Atjeh, the Muslim ruler there had Sjaich Noeroddin's book *Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm* brought to Kedah for instruction in the Law.)

The "Moslim geworden vorst" of Atjeh — the Muslim ruler of Atjeh who had the *Sirāṭ* sent to Kedah — is, in the context of the Kedah chronicle's account of the conversion of the king of Kedah, the Atjehnese sultan under whom ar-Rānīrī served: Sultan Iskandar II, who reigned from [[1636]] to [[1641]]. The episode thus falls in the same chronological frame as ar-Rānīrī's Atjehnese compositions of [[1638]] — the *[[Sirat al-Mustaqim|Sirāṭ]]*, the *[[Bustan al-Salatin|Bustān]]*, and the *Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah*.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the *Sjaich Noeroddin* is significant as the principal vehicle by which the *fiqh* of the Shāfiʿite school was transmitted from Atjeh to the Malay Peninsula in the early seventeenth century. The Kedah episode — recorded by Hooykaas on the authority of the *Hikajat Marong Mahawangsa* — is the principal evidence for the role of the Atjehnese court as the centre of Shāfiʿite *fiqh* diffusion to the Malay Peninsula, and for the place of ar-Rānīrī's *[[Sirat al-Mustaqim|Sirāṭ]]* as the principal vehicle of that diffusion.

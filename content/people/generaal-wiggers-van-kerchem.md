**Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem** was a Dutch *kolonel* (later *generaal*) of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who served as *tijdelijk bevelhebber* of the Dutch forces in Atjeh during [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]]'s absence at [[Buitenzorg|Buitenzorg]] from July to November [[1875]], and again *definitief* from [[10 March]] [[1876]] after Pel's sudden death at [[Lamjoeng]]. He was, in [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s account, the man who let [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s *aanvallende* achievements lapse back into the *afwachtende houding* — the second coming of [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]'s passive system.

## The Interim Command

During Pel's three months' leave, which began on [[12 June]] [[1875]], Wiggers van Kerchem held military and civil command *ad interim*. His administration was not without accomplishment: under it the [[Poelolo Bras|Poelolo Bras lighthouse]] was completed on [[20 June]] [[1875]] and its light first *ontstoken*, and the post of [[Sinangri]] was occupied in July [[1875]] without enemy opposition, considerably improving the *stelling* at its weakest point between [[Oeleh Leh]] and [[Soerian]]. But the [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy attempt to break the south line]] in the night of [[21 June|21]]–[[22 June]] [[1875]] — *bij tijds verijdeld* — and the steady harassment of September–November, when the Atjehneezen "hadden de gelegenheid gebruikt om versterkingen naby onze posten op te werpen" and several detachments south of [[Kota Radja]] and at [[Lampriet]] were "in groot gevaar om afgesneden en in de pan gehakt te worden," confirmed Pel's conviction on his return that the *afwachtende stelsel* had to be abandoned at once.

## The Definitive Command and Reversion to Passivity

Wiggers van Kerchem took over definitively on [[10 March]] [[1876]], two weeks after Pel's death, and inherited the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] at its furthest extent — from the mouth of the [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast, through the newly conquered [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX]], to [[Pango]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]], with [[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|lt.-kol. Engel]] already at [[Kwala Gigieng]] since [[26 February]] [[1876]]. He was "geen voorstander van het aanvallende stelsel"; he wished not to extend the *linie* further and to adopt a "strijkt afwachtende houding." Under his long command the *linie* Kroeng Raba–Kwala Gigieng, Borel records in [[1878]], has remained "nagenoeg onveranderd." The Kroeng Raya eastern closure of Pel's plan, attempted under [[Generaal Diemont|generaal Diemont]] by sea-expedition under [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]], "leidde tot geene bevredigende uitkomst," and the plan as a whole was never executed.

## Borel's Judgement

Borel treats Wiggers van Kerchem as a reversion to the spirit of van Swieten: the same distrust of the *aanvallende tactiek*, the same preference for the spade over the sword. The contrast with Pel's three months of conquest — [[Lamtengah]] ([[8 January]] [[1876]]), the [[Blang Kalla]] kloof ([[15 January]]), [[Boekit Daroe]] and the [[Glitaroem]] pass ([[28 January]]), [[Atoeh]] ([[3 February]]), [[Pango]] ([[13 February]]) — is, in Borel's narrative, the measure of what the Dutch lost when Pel's *slagaderbreuk* carried him off.

A foreign (Turkish or Syrian) saint whose tomb lies in the village of Bitay near Kutaraja. Legend recounts a dispute with the Sultan of Acheh over the start of the fasting month, resolved when the saint miraculously pointed out the new moon to the Sultan.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Teungku di Bitay

**Teungku di Bitay** is the Achehnese name given to a saint whose grave at [[Bitay]], a *gampong* close to the *Dalam* of [[Banda Acheh|the capital]], was, at the time of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s residence in Acheh, still visible and an object of veneration. According to Achehnese tradition, preserved in the cycle of legends relating to the embassy of the [[Sultan of Acheh (16th century)|Sultan of Acheh]] to the [[Sultan of Turkey (16th century)|Sultan of Turkey]], he belonged to the colony of strangers who came from [[Syria]] to instruct the Achehnese in arts hitherto unknown to them.

The tradition runs that an Achehnese embassy, having obtained admittance to the Sultan of Turkey, was asked to tell him all about affairs in Acheh, the distance of that kingdom from [[Stambul]], the difficulties of the journey, and so forth. The Sultan ordered a great cannon to be given them as a return present — to which was given the name **Lada Sichupa'** ("Pepper of a Chupa'") in memory of the single *chupa'* of pepper which was all that remained of the cargo the Achehnese had brought — and, in compliance with their request, he also gave them a number of skilled artisans from his kingdom to instruct their fellow-countrymen in various arts hitherto unknown to them.

It is said that a number of these instructors, who came from Syria, settled in a *gampong* close to the *Dalam*, and in remembrance of their native land gave the village the name of **Bitay** — the Achehnese pronunciation of *Baital*, an abbreviation of *Baital-maqdis* (Jerusalem). In Bitay may still be seen the grave of Teungku di Bitay, who according to the tradition belonged to this colony of strangers. The cult of Teungku di Bitay is thus one of the monuments of the legendary connection between Acheh and the Ottoman Empire, a connection which the Achehnese also adduced as the origin of the universal observance of the Mo'lot feast in their country.

The cult of Teungku di Bitay is to be distinguished from the more popular cult of [[Teungku Anjong]] of [[Gampong Jawa]], whose tomb has now completely outshone the more ancient tomb of [[Teungku di Kuala Abdora'oh|Teungku di Kuala]]. All three saints, however, share in the general Achehnese veneration of the founders of religious and civilised institutions: Teungku Anjong as the great sayyid of Gampong Jawa; Teungku di Kuala as the older and eclipsed saint of the capital; and Teungku di Bitay as the Syrian teacher of arts, whose memory is preserved in the very name of the village he founded.

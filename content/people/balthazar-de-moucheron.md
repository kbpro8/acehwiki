**Balthazar de Moucheron** was a South-Netherlander of French descent (*Zuid-Nederlander van Fransche afstamming*) who, trusting the experience of the [[Cornelis de Houtman|De Houtman]] brothers, took them into his service on a fleet of two ships bound for the East Indies in [[1598]]. The expedition's disastrous call at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1599]] — at which [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] was killed and [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] taken captive for twenty-six months — brought De Moucheron heavy losses, and gave the English pilot [[John Davis|John Davis]], who sailed with the fleet as a spy for the Earl of Essex, the material with which to advance English designs on the Indian trade.

## The 1598 expedition

De Moucheron's expedition of [[1598]] was one of the so-called "voorcompagnieën" (pre-VOC companies) — the small independent Dutch trading companies that sailed to the East Indies in the years [[1595]]–[[1602]] before their consolidation into the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]]. Trusting the experience of the De Houtman brothers — [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis]] having commanded the first Dutch voyage of [[1595]]–[[1597]] — De Moucheron took them both into his service. The English pilot [[John Davis|John Davis]] served as pilot, on the secret instructions of the Earl of Essex.

## Voyage to the Indies

The voyage to the Indies took fifteen months. The Madagascar population fled at the reappearance of the rough Dutchmen — this was the second Dutch landing there, the first having already left a bad reputation under [[Cornelis de Houtman|De Houtman]]'s own first expedition. The fleet reached Atjeh in June [[1599]].

## The Atjeh disaster of 1599

The good understanding with the Sultan lasted only briefly. With poisoned fruits and drinks, later with a *kris*-attack, the Atjehnese sought to be rid of the foreigners. Many were killed — including [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] — and many taken prisoner, including [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]]. See [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)]].

## The escape to Pedir and the homeward voyage

The ships that escaped Atjeh made for [[Pedir]], where they beat off an Atjehnese naval attack; they then touched at the Nicobars and, after much misfortune, reached Middelburg in the summer of [[1600]]. De Moucheron suffered heavy losses from the expedition.

## John Davis and the English spy-work

John Davis — the English pilot — turned his spy-material to English advantage. As the Dutch had once wrung the secrets of the India trade from Portugal, England now sought profit from Dutch experience. The De Moucheron expedition was a key part of that intelligence-gathering: Davis's reports would inform the English East India Company's own early voyages, including [[James Lancaster|James Lancaster]]'s.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, De Moucheron is significant as the merchant-prince whose expedition of [[1598]] produced the [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)|first Dutch fatality at Atjeh]] and the [[Frederik de Houtman|first Dutch captivity at Atjeh]] — the two events that, taken together, set the tone for the Dutch-Atjeh relationship for the next decade. He is also a reminder that the early Dutch India trade was not exclusively Amsterdam: the South-Netherlander of French descent, operating out of Zeeland, was a typical figure of the pre-VOC "wilde vaart" generation.

**Toeankoe Machmoed** — *Tuanku Mahmud* — was the young Sultan of [[Atjeh]] in the years immediately preceding the Dutch attack of [[1873]], installed on the throne under the regency of [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] after the death of Sultan [[Sultan Saleiman Iskander|Saleiman Iskander]] in [[1837]]. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as the prince whom the Habib rescued from the custody of [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]] — *oeloebalang* of the [[VII Moekims]] — and for whom the Habib acted as *regent* during his minority.

## Background and installation

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Sultan [[Sultan Saleiman Iskander|Saleiman Iskander]] died in [[1837]], and that the succession was disputed. The Habib, who had been the principal adviser of the late Sultan, wished to install the young [[Toeankoe Machmoed|Toeankue Machmoed]] — son of the deceased Sultan — as his successor, and to act as regent during the prince's minority. The prince was, however, in the custody of [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]] at his *kampong* of [[Tjot Bada]], and Baid refused to release him. The Habib accordingly assembled a force of two thousand men in the [[XXVI Moekims]] and marched on Tjot Bada, where — partly by force and partly by his personal prestige — he compelled Baid to give up the prince within eight days.

The installation of Toeankoe Machmoed as Sultan was, in the album's account, the principal achievement of the Habib's early career at the Atjehnese court, and the regency that followed was the period of the Habib's greatest political influence. The Habib was confirmed as *Grootvizier* — Grand Vizier — and in that capacity proposed to the young Sultan that the Dutch be admitted to Atjehnese territory for the purposes of trade, on the ground that it was in Atjeh's interest to be on good terms with the Netherlands.

## The disputed succession

The album records that the installation of Toeankoe Machmoed did not go unchallenged. A rival party of chiefs wished to elevate [[Toeankoe Hoessin]] — son of [[Toeankoe Abbas]] — to the office of *havenkoning* (harbour-king), and it was only when [[Panglima Polim]], the hereditary *Sagiehoofd* of the [[XXII Moekims]], declared himself for Machmoed that some measure of calm was restored. The compromise reached was that the Habib, as *Grootvizier*, would provisionally govern the realm.

## The death of Toeankoe Machmoed

The album records that [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — the album's principal source for the religious-political history of pre-war Atjeh — reports the death of the young Sultan during the Habib's absence in [[Mekka]]. The Habib, who had been discredited by accusations that he had used the funds raised for the building of a new mosque to purchase arms, withdrew to Mekka; while he was there, the young Sultan died of cholera at the moment that the war with the Netherlands broke out. The coincidence was, in the album's reading, the proximate cause of the collapse of the Habib's political project: with the death of the Sultan, the regency lapsed, and the Habib's authority — which had been exercised in the Sultan's name — lost its constitutional basis.

## The Kemala sultanate

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the later Atjehnese resistance leaders — including [[Teungku di Tiro]] — recognised a successor Sultan at [[Kemala]], and that the *last brief* of this Sultan was the document by which Teungku di Tiro claimed authority to regulate the affairs of religion in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The album does not name this Sultan, but the reference places the Kemala sultanate as the political successor to the line of [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] in the resistance period.

## Significance

Toeankoe Machmoed's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the last Sultan of [[Atjeh]] recognised by the Dutch before the [[1873]] expedition, and as the prince whose regency by [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] marked the high-water mark of centralised authority in the late sultanate. His death in [[1873]] — coincident with the Dutch attack — closed the regency and opened the period of Dutch-*oeloebalang* and Dutch-*ulama* conflict that would continue until the final conquest of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1904]].

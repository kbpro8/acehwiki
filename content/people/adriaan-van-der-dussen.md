**Adriaan van der Dussen** was a Dutch opperkoopman in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] who arrived at [[Djohor|Djohor]] in [[August]] [[1614]] to conduct negotiations with the sultan about the construction of a Dutch fort — the fort authorised by the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|second Matelieff accord of 23 September 1606]] but never built, and the principal Dutch strategic objective in the Strait of Malaka in the aftermath of the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of 1613]]. Van der Dussen's mission is the closing Dutch-Djohor episode of the early Atjeh chapter of Van der Woude's [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The 1614 mission

The Achehnese attack on Djohor of [[7 May]] [[1613]] — in which the city of [[Batoesawer|Batoesawer]] was taken, the Dutch factor [[Jacques Obelaer|Jacques Obelaer]] and twenty-one other Dutchmen were carried off to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] as prisoners, and the sultan of Djohor was deposed — was followed within months by an Achehnese-Djohor reconciliation: the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]] gave his sister in marriage to the deposed sultan of Djohor — now styled **Sultan Sabrang** — and sent him back to Djohor with an Achehnese escort of two thousand men to rebuild the destroyed city. The Dutch prisoners were released as part of the rapprochement.

In this context, the Heeren Zeventien dispatched Van der Dussen to Djohor in [[August]] [[1614]] to negotiate the construction of the fort that [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] had sought in [[1606]]. The strategic aim was the same: a fortified Dutch post on Djohor territory, supported by a permanent fleet in the Strait of Malaka, would make the Portuguese position at [[Malaka|Malaka]] untenable and complete the work that [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff's campaign of 1606]] had begun. Van der Woude does not pursue the outcome of Van der Dussen's mission in detail, but the strategic context — the Achehnese-Djohor reconciliation, the sultan of Djohor's dependence on the Achehnese, the Dutch inability to maintain a fleet in the Strait in force — suggests that the mission, like [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|Verhoeff's earlier attempt]], was unsuccessful.

## Significance

Adriaan van der Dussen is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch negotiator at [[Djohor|Djohor]] in the aftermath of the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of 1613]] and the principal Dutch figure of the closing Dutch-Djohor episode of the early Atjeh chapter. His mission of [[1614]] was the principal Dutch attempt to revive the Dutch-Djohor military alliance after the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack]] and the Achehnese-Djohor reconciliation — an attempt that, like the earlier attempts of [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] and [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff]], failed to secure the strategic Dutch objective of a fortified post on Djohor territory. Van der Dussen's mission also marks the moment at which the strategic centre of Dutch-Indies policy shifted definitively southward to [[Jacatra|Jacatra]] and the eventual founding of [[Batavia|Batavia]] in [[1619]], and the moment at which [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] took up the director-generalship of the Bantam-Jacatra factories — the post in which he would execute the monopoly-by-force policy that the [[Heeren Zeventien|Heeren Zeventien]] had authorised in [[1608]]-[[1609]].

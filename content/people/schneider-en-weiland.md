**Schneider and H. Weiland** were the investigators whose finding on the urinary excretion of vitamin A in severe infectious disease was cited by Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] in his discussion of the causes of *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## The finding

According to Schneider and H. Weiland, no vitamin A is excreted in the urine by normal subjects, even when enormous quantities are administered. In severe infectious diseases, by contrast, large quantities of vitamin A are excreted, which may be significant in the origin of a hypovitaminosis. The cause of this abnormal relation was attributed to a liver injury and an altered permeability for vitamin A in the kidneys.

Gomperts cited the work from the *Klinische Wochenschrift* of [[1937]], No. 3, as abstracted in the *Nederlandsch Tijdschrift voor Geneeskunde* of [[24 April]] [[1937]]. It formed part of his examination of whether infectious disease could explain the distribution of xerophthalmia — an influence which, on his own tabulated data from the *kampongs*, "niet duidelijk tot uiting komt."

The proposition nevertheless underlay the fifth point of his closing summary, that the combating of worm infection deserved great attention as a preventive measure, since the worms withdrew much food from the intestines and their excretion products might act harmfully on the internal glands.

John Macpherson ([[1745]]–[[1821]]) was a British East India Company official who served as interim Governor-General of [[Bengal]] from [[1785]] to [[1786]], between the governor-generalships of [[Warren Hastings|Warren Hastings]] and Lord Cornwallis. In the Achehnese record he appears only as the addressee of a letter from the Raja of [[Tringano]] (Trengganu) dated [[24 February 1787]], in which the Malay ruler invoked Macpherson's friendship against the Dutch.

## The Tringganu Letter of 24 February 1787

Van der Kemp mentions Macpherson in a footnote to the [[Sultan of Acheh's letter to Warren Hastings|Acheh sultan's letter to Warren Hastings of 7 November 1786]], which is printed as Appendix 1 of his article. The footnote, expanding on the death of [[Captain Hobbs|Captain Hobbs]] at [[Tringano]], observes that in the Roosegaarde Bisschop collection «also lies a letter from the prince of Tringano to the Governor-General at [[Calcutta]] John Macpherson dd. [[24 February 1787]] in which he invokes his friendship against the Hollanders». The letter is itself evidence that, in the immediate aftermath of the [[Treaty of Quedah (Penang cession, 1786)|Quedah treaty of 1786]] and the establishment of British influence at [[Penang]], the rulers of the Malay Peninsula were already looking to Calcutta for protection against what they regarded as Dutch hostility — a configuration of interests that would, three decades later, underlie the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs commission]] and the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|Acheh treaty of 1819]].

## Significance

Macpherson's role in the Achehnese record is purely incidental; the letter from Tringganu reached him as the acting head of the Bengal government during the interval between [[Warren Hastings|Hastings's]] departure and Cornwallis's arrival, and there is no evidence that he acted upon it. The mention is included by Van der Kemp as part of the early documentation of European–Achehnese–Malay relations from which the [[1819]] episode grew.

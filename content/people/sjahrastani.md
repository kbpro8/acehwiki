**Al-Shahrastānī** — Muḥammad b. ʿAbd al-Karīm al-Shahrastānī (467–548 H / 1076–1153 C.E.) — was a Persian scholar of Islamic sects and religions, the author of the *al-Milal waʾl-niḥal* ("The Book of Religious and Philosophical Sects"), one of the principal scholastic authorities on the classification of Muslim heresies. He is cited by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of the two Malay treatises of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] as one of the comparative authorities for the sectarian classifications of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]].

## The *al-Milal waʾl-niḥal*

The *al-Milal waʾl-niḥal* of al-Shahrastānī is one of the principal scholastic authorities on the classification of Muslim heresies. The work is cited by Voorhoeve, in the comparative notes to his introduction to the [[1955]] edition, as **Sjahr.** — abbreviated from the Dutch form **Sjahrastānī** — for the variant readings of the names of the sects and for the doctrines attributed to them. The translation of Haarbrücker is the form in which the work was available to Voorhoeve.

## The Barāhima

Al-Shahrastānī is one of the principal authorities cited by Voorhoeve for the question of the Barāhima — the so-called "followers of Ibrāhīm" whom Raniri treats in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] as a scriptuary religion. Al-Shahrastānī — unlike [[Abd al-Karim al-Jili|al-Jīlī]], from whom Raniri drew his account of the Barāhima — rejects the derivation of the name from the prophet Ibrāhīm. He says: "There are people who claim that the Indian Barāhima are named after the prophet Ibrāhīm. This is incorrect, for this group is characterised by an absolute denial of prophecy. The Indian Barāhima, according to al-Shahrastānī, are descended from a Persian king named Barhām, and he reckons them not among the scriptuaries but, on the contrary, among those who follow their own ideas without divine revelation." The classification of the Barāhima among the scriptuaries in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] is therefore — as Voorhoeve notes — "everything but usual in the sect-literature."

## Significance

Al-Shahrastānī is one of the principal scholastic authorities cited by Voorhoeve for the comparative study of the sectarian classifications of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]]. His *al-Milal waʾl-niḥal* is the principal comparative authority for the names of the sects and for the doctrines attributed to them. The contrast between his account of the Barāhima and that of [[Abd al-Karim al-Jili|al-Jīlī]] is one of the principal documents for the singularity of Raniri's classification of the Barāhima among the scriptuaries — a singularity that Voorhoeve traces to Raniri's source in al-Jīlī's [[al-Insan al-Kamil|*al-Insān al-kāmil*]].

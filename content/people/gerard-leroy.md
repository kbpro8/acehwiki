**Gerard Leroy** was the [[The Netherlands|Dutch]] navigator who, in August [[1601]], arrived at [[Atjeh]] as commander of the four ships dispatched in January [[1601]] by the *Middelburgsche Compagnie* (Middelburg Company) founded by [[Adriaen Hendriksz ten Haeff|Adriaen Hendriksz ten Haeff]] and others, and thereby secured the release of [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] and the surviving Dutch prisoners from Acehnese captivity. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* he is the commander whose timely arrival produced both the first formal Dutch-Acehnese commercial settlement and the first direct shipment of Acehnese pepper to the Republic.

## The 1601 voyage

"In August '01 Gerard Leroy (sent out in January '01 by the 'Middelburgsche Compagnie,' founded by Adriaen Hendriksz ten Haeff and others, with 4 ships) put in here" (*in Aug. '01 Gerard Leroy (door de 'Middelburgsche Compagnie,' opgericht door Adriaen Hendriksz ten Haeff c.a., in Jan. '01 met 4 schepen uitgezonden) hier aanliep*). The fleet of four ships was substantially sized for a pre-[[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] venture, and the founding by [[Adriaen Hendriksz ten Haeff|Ten Haeff]] signals the rising Middelburg interest in the East India trade that would soon be folded into the unified VOC.

## The release of the Houtman party

The release came swiftly and through no Dutch intercession. "Meanwhile the Atjehers and Portuguese had fallen into enmity, so that when in August '01 Gerard Leroy … here put in, Frederik de H[outman] and his men were set at liberty, even before a corresponding letter from Prince [[Maurits van Oranje|Maurits]] to the prince had been presented" (*Intusschen geraakten de Atjehers en Portugeezen in vijandschap, zoodat toen in Aug. '01 Gerard Leroy … hier aanliep, Frederik de H. en de zijnen in vrijheid werden gesteld, nog vóór een desbetreffend schrijven van Prins Maurits aan den vorst was overhandigd*).

The decisive factor was the Atjeh-Portuguese breach, not the Dutch embassy. The sultan, no longer aligned with the Portuguese, no longer needed the Dutch prisoners as Portuguese-pleasing hostages. The release preceded the formal delivery of Prince Maurits's letter — a detail that suggests the sultan was already inclined to release the Dutch before the formal diplomatic cover arrived.

## The first factory and the first pepper cargo

Leroy's visit also produced the first formal Dutch-Acehnese commercial settlement. "On 28 [August 1601] they were permitted to establish a factory (*factorij*) here, and Atjehsche envoys, provided with gifts and letters to the Prince, were sent along with two of the ships, which with a full cargo of pepper departed directly from here to Holland" (*28 werd hen toegestaan hier een factorij te stichten en werden Atjehsche gezanten, voorzien van geschenken en brieven aan den Prins, met 2 der schepen, die met een volle lading peper rechtstreeks van hier naar Holland vertrokken, medegegeven*).

Three elements of this settlement deserve notice.

First, the Dutch factory at Atjeh — the first formal Dutch commercial establishment on Sumatran soil — is Leroy's achievement. The [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis]]–[[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik]]–[[Paulus van Caerden|Van Caerden]] sequence had failed to obtain one; Leroy obtained it in weeks.

Second, the Atjehsche envoys sent with the two returning ships to the Republic constitute the first Acehnese embassy to the Netherlands — a diplomatic counterpart to the Dutch presence at Atjeh. The gifts and letters to Prince [[Maurits van Oranje|Maurits]] are the visible tokens of the embassy.

Third, the full pepper cargo shipped directly from Atjeh to Holland is the commercial seed of the great Dutch pepper trade of the [[Westkust van Sumatra|Westkust]]. The [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] would, after its [[1602]] founding, build its Atjeh and Westkust policy on the foundation Leroy had laid.

## Significance

Leroy's visit is the pivot of the opening Dutch-Acehnese phase. Where [[Cornelis de Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] had died, where [[Paulus van Caerden|Van Caerden]] had failed, Leroy succeeded — and he did so because the Atjeh-Portuguese breach had reset the court's politics in his favour. The 28 August 1601 factory is the first Dutch commercial establishment on Sumatran soil, the first Acehnese embassy to the Republic is the first diplomatic exchange between the two powers, and the pepper cargo is the first direct commercial fruit of the relationship.

The episode also marks the moment at which the Atjehsche court, freed from the Portuguese alignment, becomes a potential Dutch partner. The [[1602]] founding of the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] would build on this opening; the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (15 February 1641)|1641 Schouten treaty]] would systematise it.

**Dr. R. Broersma** was a Dutch colonial observer, agricultural economist and author of *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* — *Reconnaissances in Atjeh* — a series of travel essays published in the newspaper *De Locomotief* in semi-October [[1923]] and afterwards issued as an offprint with the shelfmark C 527 of the [[Koninklijk Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde|Kon. Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde]] in Leiden. He is one of the principal non-military observers of [[Atjeh]] in the early civilian period, after the formal pacification of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the establishment of the first civilian governorship in [[1919]].

## Method and Standpoint
Broersma's standpoint is that of the practical agricultural economist with a sharp eye for mismanagement and a sympathy for the Atjehnese commoner. He opens his *Inleiding* with a disavowal of military history: *'ik heb mij menige oorlogsdaad voorgenomen den strijd niet na te speuren, wenschende te zien het land en het volk in hun staat van vrede'* — *I have proposed to myself not to re-trace the war, wishing to see the land and the people in their state of peace.* He proposes instead to follow the roads along which *'een rustig volksbestaan vruchten van den arbeid binnenhaalt tot ruimer welvaart.'*

His method is to combine close observation of agriculture and trade with interviews of [[Oeléebalang|oeléebalangs]] in their houses. He records the prices of pepper and pinang, the cost of an artesian boring in [[Langsa]] (f 122.931 between [[1906]] and [[1919]]), the wages of *panglong* wood-cutters at [[Bajan]] (f 3.50 per man per month under the regulation of *St. 1909 No. 488*), and the prices fetched by an elephant tusk at [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula]], [[Idi]] and [[Peusangan]]. He takes down the genealogies of chiefs and reports their revenues from the *hacil* and the petroleum cijns. He is openly critical of Dutch mismanagement — the failed *gouvernements rubberonderneming* at [[Langsa]] opened in [[1907]] on peat soil that has since sunk under the trees, the expensive and useless dam at [[Panté Lhong]] in Peusangan which cost three tons and was ruined because only one arm of the river was dammed — but he is sympathetic to the Atjehnese commoner, whose *'eeng'* (penny-wise) temperament he takes to be the natural seed of commerce once peace should allow it to develop.

## The Verkenningen
The series comprises twenty numbered essays of varying length. The early chapters describe the [[Tamiang]] river basin, the underafdeeling [[Langsa]] with its wood-cutting industry and leper colony, and the historical region of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Perlak]]. The narrative then advances through [[Idi]], [[Lho Soekon]], the ancient kingdom of [[Samoedra]], [[Bireuen]] in the landschap of [[Peusangan]], and the [[Gajo-weg]] leading up to [[Takengon]] on [[Laoet Tawar|Laut Tawar]]. The later essays treat the great rice-plain of [[Pidië]], the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] in detail, [[Groot-Atjeh]] with its capital [[Kota Radja]] and the harbour of [[Oelèë Lheue]], the question of [[Atjeh als rijstland|Atjeh as a rice country]], and finally a series of reflective chapters on cattle-breeding, trade, labour and *staatkunde* (policy). The series closes with an essay *'De hand van den meester'* — *The hand of the master* — written in the third person, in which Broersma summarises his standpoint on the proper future of the gewest.

## Sources
Broersma's authorities are [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] for the Gajo country and the institutions of Atjeh, [[J. A. Kruyt|J. A. Kruyt]] for the early blockade-period of the east coast, [[Dr. Julius Jacobs|Dr. Julius Jacobs]] for the pre-war ethnography of [[Groot-Atjeh]], [[H. T. Damste|H. T. Damste]] for the successions of the eastern *landschappen*, [[F. C. van Heurn|F. C. van Heurn]] for the soils of the East Coast, and the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] pamphlet *[[De rijkdom van Atjeh|De rijkdom van Atjeh]]* for the programme of rice-reform.

## Significance
Broersma's *Verkenningen* is one of the most detailed non-military surveys of Atjeh in the early civilian period. It treats the country at the moment when the *Atjeh-tram* met the *Deli-spoor* at [[Besitang]] in [[1908]] and when the *Afdeeling Pidië* was detached from the *Noordkust van Atjeh* in [[1922]], and it is widely quoted in later colonial literature on the economic geography of northern Sumatra.

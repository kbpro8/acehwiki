**P. F. Laging Tobias** was a Dutch colonial administrator who served as civil Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden during the *bestuurlijke* experiment of the [[Civil government period|civil government period]] in the early 1880s, and the author of the brochure *[[Phrasen en Feiten (Laging Tobias)|Phrasen en Feiten]]* (Phrases and Facts), in which he reviewed the policy of the Atjeh government. He succeeded — per sé a civil governor being desired in Atjeh — the Resident [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]], a choice that [[W. A. Coblijn|Coblijn]] pronounced, for character-qualities, *zeer gelukkig* (very fortunate); but the experiment failed, and in [[1884]] Laging Tobias himself asked for his discharge, after which the [[Government decisions of 19 August 1884|decisions of 19 August 1884]] abolished the civil government and entrusted the authority to kolonel [[Demmenie]] as military governor.

## The brochure *Phrasen en Feiten*

[[W. A. Coblijn|Coblijn]], who cited the brochure repeatedly in *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]*, drew from it — as from his other principal sources, [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]]'s *[[Mijne ervaring van Atjeh (Pruys van der Hoeven)|Mijne ervaring van Atjeh]]* and the *Memorie* of luitenant-generaal [[K. van der Heyden|K. van der Heijden]] — the materials of his indictment of the civil-government experiment. From *Phrasen en Feiten* Coblijn cited Laging Tobias's own observations on the *quest* made to remove generaal Van der Heijden from his post, and on the *sultanshulde* (homage to the Sultan) policy of his predecessors: the homage paid to the ruler whom the Dutch themselves had set up, Laging Tobias wrote, was meaningless flattery, since the government of the Sultan had long since ceased to possess any authority with the Atjehers — a judgement Coblijn italicised in agreement.

## The cordial understanding with the military commanders

Coblijn recorded that Laging Tobias maintained a cordial understanding with the military commanders Schäfer and [[Demmenie|Demmeni]] — in contrast to the estrangement that had marked the cooperation between the government of Pruys van der Hoeven and kolonel [[J. F. Haus|Haus]] — but that the new governor soon applied for his discharge. For Coblijn this request was the decisive demonstration that the appointment of a civil governor of Atjeh had been premature: the province was a theatre of war, and the civil functionary, however able, was out of place at the head of it. The opinion was the ground of Coblijn's wider thesis of the [[Civil versus military government in Atjeh|civil versus military government of Atjeh]].

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In the [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] treats Laging Tobias as the seventh name in his catalogue of the eleven Governors of Atjeh — *van Swieten, Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont, van der Heijden, Pruijs van der Hoeven, Laging Tobias, Demmeni, van Teijn, Pompe van Meerdervoort, Deijkerhof* — and the Governor who, in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record, is associated with two opposed measures: the introduction of the May [[1883]] [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] that Van Heutsz singles out as "alles wat men kon verlangen" (everything one could desire), and the advocacy of the *herstel van het sultanaat* (restoration of the sultanate) under [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]] that Van Heutsz devotes a long later chapter to refuting. His governorship ended in February [[1884]] when the Regeering, having decided in the secret Staten-Generaal sessions of [[16 June|16]]–[[17 June]] [[1884]] to impose the [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]], forced the abandonment of the policy he had been appointed to execute.

### Arrival in March 1883

Laging Tobias took over the Governorship of Atjeh in March [[1883]], in succession to the *Civiel Gouverneur* (Civil Governor) of [[1881]]–[[1883]] whose "ellendige zwakke stelsel" (miserable weak system) — Van Heutsz's phrase — had followed the [[Van der Heijden]] dismissal. Van Heutsz describes the situation he inherited: "In Maart 1883 kwam de Gouverneur Laging Tobias aan het bestuur, die den toestand te Groot-Atjeh zeer ongunstig vond" — in March 1883 Governor Laging Tobias came to the administration, who found the situation in Groot-Atjeh very unfavourable. The gloom was Laging Tobias's own retrospective: in his later article "Het herstel van het sultanaat in Atjeh", published in the December [[1886]] number of the *Indische Gids*, he confessed that "Reeds zeer kort na mijn optreden als gouverneur van Atjeh, kreeg ik de overtuiging dat onze toestand in Atjeh bijna hopeloos was" — already very shortly after my entry into office as Governor of Atjeh, I came to the conviction that our situation in Atjeh was almost hopeless. Van Heutsz, who had been *Chef van den Staf* in Atjeh through the same period, concedes the weight of the inheritance: "Zeker was het dus een zware ondankbare taak, die in 1883 op de schouders werd gelegd van den Heer Laging Tobias" — certainly, then, it was a heavy and thankless task that in 1883 was placed on the shoulders of Mr Laging Tobias.

### The restoration-of-sultanate policy

Laging Tobias's first response to the inheritance was to seek, in the restoration of the sultanate under [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]], the only available remedy. "Alleen in een herstel van het sultanaat onder Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed heil zag" — only in a restoration of the sultanate under Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed did he see salvation, Van Heutsz writes. The policy was not Laging Tobias's invention: it had been floated in August [[1883]] by the Minister van Koloniën van Bloemen Waanders, "In Augustus van datzelfde jaar, juist toen de Gouv. Gen. 's Jacob zich te Atjeh bevond, ontving deze daar een schrijven van den Minister van Koloniën van Bloemen Waanders, waarin Z. E. hetzelfde denkbeeld ter sprake bracht, n. l. herstel van het sultanaat" — In August of that same year, just when the G.-G. 's Jacob was at Atjeh, he received there a letter from the Minister of Colonies van Bloemen Waanders, in which His Excellency brought up the same idea, namely the restoration of the sultanate — and Laging Tobias, once placed on his difficult post and searching for means of improving the situation, made it his own.

The political obstacle was [[Toeankoe Haschim]] — "een zeer krachtige persoonlijkheid" (a very forceful personality), "hoofd der tegenpartij" (head of the opposing party) — who, in the years of Dutch *lijdelijkheid* (passivity), had masterfully exploited the time to install his pupil [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]] as the recognised successor to the sultan [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah|Machmoed Sjah]] (who had died in [[1874]]), and who now issued orders from [[Kemala]] "als komende van het eenig wettige gezag, van den sultan" — as coming from the only lawful authority, from the sultan. Laging Tobias's first diplomatic move was to hint, in a letter of the first half of October [[1883]] to the old [[Tekoe Nanta|Tekoe Nanta der VI Moekims]] (also called Tekoe Lampadang), that "in geval van onderwerping … een herstel van het sultanaat met Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed als sultan wel mogelijk was" — in case of submission, a restoration of the sultanate with Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed as sultan would be quite possible. The hint came to nothing. Haschim and Mohamad Daoed instead proclaimed the latter sultan in the *emesdjid Indrapoeri* — the great mosque of [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]] — in a constitutional form that legitimised the resistance, and the Indische Regeering, after the December [[1883]] resignation of Minister van Bloemen Waanders, let the sultanate-restoration idea drop: "van een zoo mogelijk dadelijk herstel van het sultanaat geen sprake meer kon zijn" (of a restoration of the sultanate as soon as possible, no more could be said).

### The May 1883 scheepvaartregeling

Laging Tobias's second initiative — and the one Van Heutsz most prizes — was the introduction in May [[1883]] of the *scheepvaartregeling* that [[Scherer]] had first conceived and that Laging Tobias was the first to apply. "Reeds in Mei 1883 toch, zoo verhaalt ons Scherer, vaardigde hij, onder nadere goedkeuring der Regeering, een besluit uit, waarbij, volgens zijn (Scherer's) denkbeelden, eene contrôle op handel en scheepvaart werd ingevoerd" — already in May 1883, Scherer tells us, he issued, subject to the Regeering's further approval, a decree by which, according to Scherer's ideas, a control on trade and shipping was introduced. The measure was Scherer's idea; the application was Laging Tobias's; and Van Heutsz's verdict, in the 1893 tract, is the highest praise he accords any Governor in the entire catalogue: "de scheepvaartregeling door Scherer voorgesteld en door Laging Tobias ingevoerd gaf alles wat men kon verlangen" — the scheepvaartregeling proposed by Scherer and introduced by Laging Tobias gave everything one could desire.

The May 1883 *scheepvaartregeling* thus stands, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, as the high-water mark of the post-Van-der-Heijden policy — the one measure that, had it been sustained, might have spared the Regeering the policy oscillations of [[1884]]–[[1892]]. It was, however, almost immediately overtaken by the *Concentratie* decision.

### The February 1884 resignation

The end of Laging Tobias's governorship came in February [[1884]], when the Regeering's drift toward the *Concentratie* — decided in the secret Staten-Generaal sessions of [[16 June|16]]–[[17 June]] [[1884]] but foreshadowed in the August [[1883]] discussions between G.-G. 's Jacob and the local military and naval authorities at Kota-Radja — overrode the only policy Laging Tobias had been able to recommend. "Meende de Gouverneur Laging Tobias zijn ontslag te moeten vragen, toen de Regeering het eenige middel verwierp waarvan hij in de bestaande omstandigheden een goede uitkomst verwachtte. Dit was in Februari 1884" — Governor Laging Tobias felt obliged to request his resignation when the Regeering rejected the only remedy from which, in the existing circumstances, he expected a good outcome. This was in February 1884.

The resignation was at first declined by the outgoing 's Jacob — "Toen echter de Gouv. Gen. 's Jacob met het oog op zijn a. s. aftreden, op dit verzoek niet wilde beschikken" (When, however, G.-G. 's Jacob, with a view to his upcoming retirement, did not wish to decide on this request) — but was accepted after 's Jacob's own departure. The new G.-G. van Rees, who visited Atjeh in March [[1884]], "betuigde, dat hij de inzichten van den Gouverneur Laging Tobias omtrent den met Atjeh te volgen weg vrij juist vond" — declared that he found Governor Laging Tobias's views on the path to be followed with Atjeh quite correct — but the declaration was overridden by the [[Concentratiebesluit (19 Augustus 1884)|*Concentratiebesluit* of 19 August 1884]]. "Met dit advies was het natuurlijk onmogelijk den Heer Laging Tobias met de uitvoering te belasten van de concentratie, die bij besluit van 19 Augustus 1884 bevolen werd" — with this advice, it was of course impossible to charge Mr Laging Tobias with the execution of the Concentratie ordered by decree of [[19 August]] [[1884]]. He had resigned rather than carry out a policy he believed wrong; and the *Concentratie* was entrusted instead to the *krijgsmacht* (army) under [[Demmeni|Generaal Demmeni]].

### Significance (per Van Heutsz)

Laging Tobias's significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record rests on three things: he was the Governor who introduced the May [[1883]] *scheepvaartregeling* — the measure Van Heutsz considered the one bright spot of the post-Van-der-Heijden decade; he was the Governor whose advocacy of the *herstel van het sultanaat* made him, in Van Heutsz's later refutation of that policy, the principal Dutch witness against whom the 1893 tract's genealogical-constitutional argument is directed; and his February [[1884]] resignation — "zijn ontslag te moeten vragen, toen de Regeering het eenige middel verwierp" — was the first Dutch gubernatorial resignation over the *Concentratie*, and the act that fixed the *Concentratie* as the policy the Regeering would pursue for the next six years. The *scheepvaartregeling* he had introduced lasted, in diluted form, until December [[1885]], when the Regeering finally withdrew it; the *sultanaat* idea he had championed was revived, against his own later arguments, by Minister Sprenger van Eijk in [[1886]], and Laging Tobias himself re-entered the public debate with his article "Het herstel van het sultanaat in Atjeh" in the *Indische Gids* of December [[1886]]. He is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the Governor who saw the right measure and the wrong remedy — and who paid for both with his office.

**P. F. Laging Tobias** was a Dutch colonial administrator who served as civil Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden during the *bestuurlijke* experiment of the [[Civil government period|civil government period]] in the early 1880s, and the author of the brochure *[[Phrasen en Feiten (Laging Tobias)|Phrasen en Feiten]]* (Phrases and Facts), in which he reviewed the policy of the Atjeh government. He succeeded — per sé a civil governor being desired in Atjeh — the Resident [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]], a choice that [[W. A. Coblijn|Coblijn]] pronounced, for character-qualities, *zeer gelukkig* (very fortunate); but the experiment failed, and in [[1884]] Laging Tobias himself asked for his discharge, after which the [[Government decisions of 19 August 1884|decisions of 19 August 1884]] abolished the civil government and entrusted the authority to kolonel [[Demmenie]] as military governor.

## The brochure *Phrasen en Feiten*

[[W. A. Coblijn|Coblijn]], who cited the brochure repeatedly in *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]*, drew from it — as from his other principal sources, [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]]'s *[[Mijne ervaring van Atjeh (Pruys van der Hoeven)|Mijne ervaring van Atjeh]]* and the *Memorie* of luitenant-generaal [[K. van der Heyden|K. van der Heijden]] — the materials of his indictment of the civil-government experiment. From *Phrasen en Feiten* Coblijn cited Laging Tobias's own observations on the *quest* made to remove generaal Van der Heijden from his post, and on the *sultanshulde* (homage to the Sultan) policy of his predecessors: the homage paid to the ruler whom the Dutch themselves had set up, Laging Tobias wrote, was meaningless flattery, since the government of the Sultan had long since ceased to possess any authority with the Atjehers — a judgement Coblijn italicised in agreement.

## The cordial understanding with the military commanders

Coblijn recorded that Laging Tobias maintained a cordial understanding with the military commanders Schäfer and [[Demmenie|Demmeni]] — in contrast to the estrangement that had marked the cooperation between the government of Pruys van der Hoeven and kolonel [[J. F. Haus|Haus]] — but that the new governor soon applied for his discharge. For Coblijn this request was the decisive demonstration that the appointment of a civil governor of Atjeh had been premature: the province was a theatre of war, and the civil functionary, however able, was out of place at the head of it. The opinion was the ground of Coblijn's wider thesis of the [[Civil versus military government in Atjeh|civil versus military government of Atjeh]].

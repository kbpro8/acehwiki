**Sie Boen Lian** was a Netherlands-Indies physician whose published work on the *[[Vlekken van Bitôt|vlekken van Bitôt]]* (Bitôt's spots) and on the age distribution of severe *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* was discussed at length by Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] in [[Oriënteerend oogheelkundig onderzoek bij de Atjehsche bevolking van Groot-Atjeh gedurende 1937|Gomperts's report of 1940]].

## On Bitôt's spots

Sie Boen Lian published several articles showing that Bitôt's spots, especially in adults, were "nog geen conditio sine qua non" for the existence of xerophthalmia. He made this plausible by determining the vitamin A content of the blood and by reporting that the spots remained unchanged despite a vitamin-A-rich diet and therapy.

Gomperts accepted the finding but entered a reservation: for the general practitioner and the ophthalmologist conducting an orienting survey, only the clinical local signs offered a hold for the diagnosis. "Daarom is het erg jammer, dat Collega Sie Boen Lian bij deze publicaties er niet bij vermeld heeft of in deze afwijkende gevallen van vlekken van Bitôt de conjunctiva al of niet de andere kenmerken van de xerophthalmie vertoonde" — it is therefore a great pity that Colleague Sie Boen Lian did not state in these publications whether in these deviant cases of Bitôt's spots the conjunctiva did or did not show the other marks of xerophthalmia, namely the xerotic condition with the dirty discolouration typical of the disease.

Gomperts added that he did not believe the diagnosis would present difficulties in children showing all the typical conjunctival changes, "zooals [[J. H. de Haas|De Haas]] vreest door deze belangrijke waarneming van Sie Boen Lian."

## On the age distribution

Sie Boen Lian, with [[Mulock Houwer]], did not share [[J. Tijssen|Tijssen]]'s supposition that the greater frequency of blindness among boys was caused by more boys than girls being attacked by xerophthalmia. He made his doubt plausible by reporting that, according to the statistics he had collected, the severe cases of xerophthalmia were most often seen at ages at which the disease was not much more frequent among boys than among girls.

Gomperts's own statistics did not agree: "Verslaggever's statistiek stemt echter niet overeen met die van Sie Boen Lian. De verhoudingen schijnen dus in Groot-Atjeh anders te zijn dan op [[Java]]" — the proportions therefore appear to be different in Groot-Atjeh than on Java.

**Hendrikus Colijn** ([[1869]]–[[1944]]) was a Dutch military officer, colonial administrator, and later Prime Minister of the Netherlands ([[1925]]–[[1926]], [[1933]]–[[1939]]). During the [[Aceh War]] and the early years of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s Governor-Generalship, he served as a captain and then as a roving advisor for the outer islands, playing a key role in shaping the pacification policy in [[Acheh|Atjèh]] and other territories. He was a close collaborator of Van Heutsz and, like [[Snouck Hurgronje]], warned against the appointment of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]] as Governor of Aceh — a warning vindicated by the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] of [[1907]]–[[1908]].

## Military and early career in Aceh

Colijn first saw action in Aceh in the [[1890s]] and rose to become a *civiel gezaghebber* (civil administrator) in [[Lho' Seumawe|Lho' Seumawé]], where he demonstrated exceptional administrative ability and a talent for working with local chiefs. Van Heutsz, then Governor of Aceh, valued him highly for this combination of military experience and civil tact.

## Opposition to Van Daalen's appointment

In [[1904]], when Van Heutsz proposed Van Daalen as his successor, Colijn privately advised against it. In a letter to [[Snouck Hurgronje]] in June 1904, he wrote:

> "Van Daalen handhaaft schitterend zijn roem als uitmuntend colonnecommandant… Toch geloof ik, dat hij eenige noodzakelijke kwaliteiten mist voor die civiele betrekking."

He favoured [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] as a more suitable candidate, noting that Swart was "goed verstand, vlot en krachtig werker" (good sense, fluent and powerful worker) with a talent for handling people. He conceded, however, that Van Daalen's seniority and experience made him the inevitable choice, and expressed concern that Van Daalen's authoritarian style would alienate the Acehnese chiefs and undermine the pacification policy. The subsequent Atjéh-enquête vindicated this assessment entirely.

## Advisor for the outer islands

After his service on the Aceh front, Colijn served as Van Heutsz's roving advisor, visiting [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]], [[Djambi|Djambi]], [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]], and other regions to propose administrative reforms. His reports were praised for their conciseness and practical recommendations, and he contributed significantly to the expansion of Dutch control in the outer islands, laying the groundwork for the later decentralisation and consolidation of the [[Dutch East Indies]]. He also participated in the 1904 [[Alaslanden|Gajo- en Alaslanden]] campaign and the subsequent civil integration of the region.

## Later career

Colijn left the colonial service in [[1909]] to enter Dutch politics. He became a member of parliament, Minister of Colonies ([[1911]]–[[1912]]), and eventually Prime Minister, serving multiple terms in the [[1920s]] and [[1930s]]. He remained a defender of Van Heutsz's legacy and, in his later writings, credited Van Heutsz with transforming Dutch colonial policy from exploitation to ethical governance. His political career ended when the Netherlands was occupied during the Second World War; he died in German captivity in [[1944]].

## Significance

Colijn's combination of military experience, administrative skill, and political acumen made him one of the most influential figures in the transition from the Aceh War to the era of consolidation and ethical governance. His early warnings about Van Daalen were vindicated by the Atjéh-enquête, and his subsequent political career ensured that the principles of the "ethical policy" were carried forward into the twentieth century.

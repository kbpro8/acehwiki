**Hendrikus Colijn** ([[1869]]–[[1944]]) was a Dutch military officer, colonial administrator, and later Prime Minister of the Netherlands ([[1925]]–[[1926]], [[1933]]–[[1939]]). During the [[Aceh War]] and the early years of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s Governor-Generalship, he served as a captain and then as a roving advisor for the outer islands, playing a key role in shaping the pacification policy in [[Acheh|Atjèh]] and other territories. He was a close collaborator of Van Heutsz and, like [[Snouck Hurgronje]], warned against the appointment of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]] as Governor of Aceh — a warning vindicated by the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] of [[1907]]–[[1908]]. He is also the officer to whom the former Sultan's legeraanvoerder *[[Panglima Prang Polim]]* "zich tenslotte had gemeld" — eventually surrendered — marking the political closure of the Acehnese Sultanate.

## Military and early career in Aceh

Colijn first saw action in Aceh in the [[1890s]] and rose to become a *civiel gezaghebber* (civil administrator) in [[Lho' Seumawe|Lho' Seumawé]], where he demonstrated exceptional administrative ability and a talent for working with local chiefs. Van Heutsz, then Governor of Aceh, valued him highly for this combination of military experience and civil tact. He participated in the [[1904]] [[Alaslanden|Gajo- en Alaslanden]] campaign and the subsequent civil integration of the region.

## The reception of Panglima Prang Polim

"Panglima Prang Polim, de vroegere legeraanvoerder van de Sultan, die zich tenslotte had gemeld bij kapitein Colijn." The book *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* places the surrender of [[Panglima Prang Polim]] — the Sultan's own commander-in-chief — to *kapitein Colijn* as the moment of political closure of the Acehnese Sultanate. Dames does not give a precise date, but the context of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]–[[1911]] suggests that Polim's surrender preceded the colonne, providing the political condition for [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]]'s request that the three *voornaamste Atjehse hoofden* write the Tiriō Teungkoes urging submission. Colijn's reception of Polim was therefore a precondition of the letter, and so of the last act of the Aceh War.

The identification of this *kapitein Colijn* with the later Dutch Prime Minister is not made explicit in *Oom Ambon* — Dames simply writes "kapitein Colijn" — but is overwhelmingly probable. Colijn (1869–1944) served in Aceh as a young officer from [[1894]] onwards, was decorated (M.W.O. 4e klas) for his part in the final phases of the Aceh War, and was widely known in the Netherlands of [[1954]] as the former Prime Minister who had been a young officer in Aceh. Dames's readers would have recognised the name without explanation.

## Opposition to Van Daalen's appointment

In [[1904]], when Van Heutsz proposed Van Daalen as his successor, Colijn privately advised against it. In a letter to [[Snouck Hurgronje]] in June 1904, he wrote:

> "Van Daalen handhaaft schitterend zijn roem als uitmuntend colonnecommandant… Toch geloof ik, dat hij eenige noodzakelijke kwaliteiten mist voor die civiele betrekking."

He favoured [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] as a more suitable candidate, noting that Swart was "goed verstand, vlot en krachtig werker" (good sense, fluent and powerful worker) with a talent for handling people. He conceded, however, that Van Daalen's seniority and experience made him the inevitable choice, and expressed concern that Van Daalen's authoritarian style would alienate the Acehnese chiefs and undermine the pacification policy. The subsequent Atjéh-enquête vindicated this assessment entirely.

## Advisor for the outer islands

After his service on the Aceh front, Colijn served as Van Heutsz's roving advisor, visiting [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]], [[Djambi|Djambi]], [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]], and other regions to propose administrative reforms. His reports were praised for their conciseness and practical recommendations, and he contributed significantly to the expansion of Dutch control in the outer islands, laying the groundwork for the later decentralisation and consolidation of the [[Dutch East Indies]].

## Later career

Colijn left the colonial service in [[1909]] to enter Dutch politics. He became a member of parliament, Minister of Colonies ([[1911]]–[[1912]]), and eventually Prime Minister, serving multiple terms in the [[1920s]] and [[1930s]]. He remained a defender of Van Heutsz's legacy and, in his later writings, credited Van Heutsz with transforming Dutch colonial policy from exploitation to ethical governance. His political career ended when the Netherlands was occupied during the Second World War; he died in German captivity in [[1944]].

## Significance

Colijn's combination of military experience, administrative skill, and political acumen made him one of the most influential figures in the transition from the Aceh War to the era of consolidation and ethical governance. His early warnings about Van Daalen were vindicated by the Atjéh-enquête, and his subsequent political career ensured that the principles of the "ethical policy" were carried forward into the twentieth century. In the narrative of *Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.*, his reception of the Sultan's own army commander is the moment at which the formal Acehnese political establishment passed under Dutch authority — the commander of the Sultan's own army came in to a young Dutch *kapitein* who would, decades later, become the Prime Minister of the colonial power.

## Account from *Dispereert Niet Vol. XI* (1946) — H. Colijn

**H. Colijn** was a Dutch military officer and later politician who served in **[[Aceh]]** during the final phase of the **[[Aceh War]]** ([[1898]]–[[1904]]). He was a close associate of **[[J. B. van Heutsz]]** and played a key role in the pacification of Aceh, particularly the surrender of **[[Panglima Polem]]** at **[[Lhokseumawe]]** in [[1903]].

### Early Career

Colijn served as an officer in the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army (KNIL), specializing in the *marechaussee* tactics developed during the Aceh campaign. He was noted for his energy and persistence in pursuing guerrilla forces.

### Role in Aceh

Under Van Heutsz's command, Colijn led pursuit operations against Acehnese resistance forces. In [[1901]], he was present at the capture of **[[Batêê Iliê]]**. In [[1903]], he accepted the surrender of Panglima Polem at **[[Lhokseumawe]]**. This event, while underreported in the Dutch press at the time, marked the effective end of organized Acehnese resistance.

### Later Career

Colijn later became a prominent Dutch politician, serving as Prime Minister of the Netherlands from [[1933]] to [[1939]]. His experience in Aceh shaped his views on colonial and administrative issues.

### Significance

Colijn's military career in Aceh demonstrated the effectiveness of Van Heutsz's strategies. His later political career brought the colonial experience to the forefront of Dutch politics.

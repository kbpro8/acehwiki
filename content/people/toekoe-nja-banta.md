**Toekoe Nja Banta** — also written *T. Nja Banta*, full *Toekoe Nja Banta XXVI Moekims* — was the territorial federation chief of the [[XXVI Moekims]] and one of the three-man leadership (alongside [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[T. Neq van Merassa]]) charged with clearing the area east of the [[Dutch]] concentrated line, whose pragmatic and transactional stance in the spring of [[1896]] made him one of the most influential Achehnese intermediaries in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*. He brought the first formal report of [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection on 29 March]] [[1896]] to the post at [[Lam-Koenjit]] at 7:30 PM on [[30 March]] [[1896]], accompanied by *1e Luit.* [[M. Neelmeijer|Neelmeijer]] and a population patrol; he warned that "even if the military posts at [[Toengkoeb]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] continued to exist, he could not guarantee peace and safety in the mukims outside the concentrated line"; and he was given the post of Toengkoeb when the Dutch evacuated it on [[21 April]] [[1896]]. After the Dutch demolition of the *bentengs* [[Perampoean]] and [[Batoe]] near Toengkoeb on [[20 June]] [[1896]], "the men of T. Nja Bantah cleared the terrain around" the demolished works — a final act of cooperation that closed the first semester of [[1896]].

## Role in the Three-Man Leadership and Report of the Defection

The [[XXVI Moekims]] formed the eastern territorial division of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and had, throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], been the most consistently resistant to Dutch authority. Nja Banta's position as *sagihoofd* therefore carried considerable weight, and his participation in the three-man leadership of the XXVI Moekims — together with the [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|hoeloebalang]] of [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] and [[T. Neq van Merassa]] — was the means by which the colonial administration attempted to manage the eastern frontier. He was at [[Tjot Rang]] with his troops when the defection occurred, and his decision to bring the formal report to Lam-Koenjit on the evening of [[30 March]] [[1896]] — alongside the Dutch *1e Luit.* Neelmeijer (the *waarnemend controleur* of the XXVI Moekims) and a population patrol — was the means by which the besieged post first learned of the catastrophe. Kruisheer notes that "*volk van Nja Bantah*" (Nja Bantah's people) subsequently "talso position at [[Tjot Pockloct]] after evacuating *blokhuizen*" — that is, Nja Banta's fighters assumed the role of buffer between the Dutch line and the now-hostile eastern mukims.

## Warning, Acquisition of Toengkoeb and Cooperation with the Dutch

Nja Banta's most consequential diplomatic intervention was his warning that the continued existence of the posts at Toengkoeb, Tjot Rang and Kroeng Gloempang would not suffice to guarantee peace in the mukims outside the concentrated line. This warning was, Kruisheer indicates, "a key input to the Dutch decision to lift those posts" as part of the wholesale [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuation of 20 April 1896]], and Nja Banta was given the post of Toengkoeb when the Dutch withdrew from it — a remarkable act of delegation which entrusted the eastern frontier to an Achehnese chief whose reliability the Dutch had every reason to doubt. The arrangement illustrates the bind in which the colonial administration now found itself: it could not defend the eastern posts, and it could not abandon them to the resistance, so it chose to entrust them to a *sagihoofd* who was, at minimum, willing to maintain a working relationship with the Dutch. On [[4 May]] [[1896]], Nja Banta joined the *waarnemend controleur* in requesting "that the possessions of [[Teungkoe Sjech of Kroeng Kali]] be spared" during the Dutch expedition, an intercession which suggests that his cooperation was tactically flexible and that he sought to protect religious figures even as he cooperated with the colonial military.

## Significance

Toekoe Nja Banta's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the *sagihoofd* who chose to cooperate with the Dutch rather than join Umar — a choice which preserved the colonial presence in the eastern XXVI Moekims through the worst of the [[1896]] crisis but which depended entirely on his personal judgement of the balance of forces. His warning about the posts is one of the most candid assessments of the liprovided that of Dutch power recorded in the balso, and his acquisition of Toengkoeb represents the high-water mark of the Dutch attempt to devolve frontier defence to cooperative Achehnese chiefs. His intercession for [[Teungkoe Sjech of Kroeng Kali]] and his men's clearing of the terrain around the demolished *bentengs* of Perampoean and Batoe on [[21 June]] [[1896]] show that his cooperation extended through the end of the first semester of [[1896]].

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Toekoe Nja Banta** — written by [[G. B. Hooijer]] as *Toekoe Nja Bantah* and *Toekoe Bantah* — was the friendly head of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|sagi of the XXVI Moekims]] whom the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies]] authorised on [[1 September]] [[1893]] to open the fight against the *[[Moslemin]]* in the terrain east of the Dutch [[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]], and whose relative failure in that task made [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] indispensable to Dutch policy in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Commission of 1 September 1893

The results obtained by [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] in clearing the [[XXV Moekims]] had been so favourable, and the rest in the cleared districts so complete, that on [[1 September]] [[1893]] the Governor extended the same policy eastward. Rifles and powder were to be supplied to Nja Banta as they had been to Oemar; his father-in-law [[T. Nek van Marassa|Toekoe Neq van Marassa]] and his brother-in-law [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet]], *oeleebalang* of the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]], were to help him.

## Failure of the campaign

Hooijer recorded that the action did not go particularly well, although Dutch troops assisted by temporarily occupying captured points and Dutch artillery was employed against the common enemy. Nja Banta's fighters, he wrote, were not so hardened in war as Oemar's followers had become through years of exercise; they were even reproached with showing signs of great effeminacy by carrying handkerchiefs with them. The wavering attitude of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet Toengkoeb]] further confused matters so often that on [[27 September]] [[1893]] it was judged necessary to have one of Toekoe Tjoet's *bentengs* at [[Toengkoeb]] occupied by Dutch troops.

Nja Banta captured the *benteng* at [[Lamgoet]] on [[4 September]] [[1893]] and gave it into Toekoe Tjoet's keeping, but the latter arbitrarily evacuated it and thereby played it back into enemy hands. When Toekoe Tjoet made his own assistance conditional on the Dutch occupation of [[Kroeng Kali]], and then, when that too was to be granted, failed to erect a watch-post on a nearby hill, the task was transferred to Nja Banta — yet when Dutch soldiers appeared before Kroeng Kali on [[6 October]] [[1893]] the hill was still unoccupied. The prospect improved somewhat when [[Toekoe Tjihik van Samalanga]] was brought over from the [[Noordkust|North Coast]] with a hundred fighters to help him, but Toekoe Tjoet then abandoned him entirely.

## The removal of Toekoe Tjoet's women

Having little success in resuming hostilities, Nja Banta caused the wives of his former fellow-combatant to be transferred from the [[Toengkoeb]] district to [[Lamreng]] inside the *geconcentreerde linie*. Hooijer judged this a serious error: it provoked a general dispersal of Toekoe Tjoet's population, of which the *Moslemin* immediately took advantage by occupying the abandoned terrain and breaking communications between the Dutch establishments at [[Kroeng Kali]] and [[Toengkoeb]].

## Hoengtjala

Before the *kampong* (village) of [[Hoengtjala]] Nja Banta had twice been repulsed, and [[T. Nek van Marassa|Toekoe Neq]] had so often seen his plans frustrated from there, that when [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]]'s advance of [[12 November]] [[1893]] caused the enemy to evacuate its *bentengs*, the two chiefs could not restrain themselves — against the Governor's orders — from giving their fighters a free hand and delivering that storehouse and safe refuge of the *[[Prang sabi|sabil]]* bands over to plunder and fire.

## Significance (Hooijer's account)

Nja Banta's commission was the Dutch attempt to reproduce the Oemar model with a second, hereditary *sagi* chief. Its failure — attributed by Hooijer to the softness of his fighters and to the unreliability of his brother-in-law — demonstrated, in his phrase, that *zonder zijn hulp onze bondgenooten de *geestdrijvers* (religious zealots) niet uit de XXVI Moekims verdrijven zouden*: without Oemar's help the Dutch allies would not drive the fanatics from the XXVI Moekims. The episode thus directly produced the concentration of power in Oemar's hands that the Dutch would come to regret.

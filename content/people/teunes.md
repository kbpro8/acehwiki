**Teunes**, better known by his alias **Santa Maria**, was a pirate and dispatch-carrier active in the Straits of Malacca in March [[1819]], at the moment when Dutch anxieties over [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]]'s movements in the region were at their height. He is mentioned only once in Van der Kemp's article, in a single sentence of a private letter from Governor [[Timmerman Thyssen]] of [[Malacca]] to [[Schout-bij-nacht Wolterbeek|Schout-bij-nacht Wolterbeek]] at Batavia, dated 16 March [[1819]]. The relevant passage, preserved as *Bijlage* IV, reads: "these waters swarm with pirates, as also that the Tommegong of Sincapour has at sea nine well-armed *pengojabja*; these vessels I believe are not so much on the look-out for plunder as for the dispatches which are expected with Santa Maria (alias Teunes)."

## Identity

Van der Kemp provides no further identification of Teunes beyond the alias "Santa Maria." The Portuguese form of the alias strongly suggests that Teunes was an **Indo-Portuguese** sailor — one of the Luso-Asian maritime population that, in the early nineteenth century, still staffed much of the country-trade shipping of the Indian Ocean and the Straits. Such figures were often employed as pilots, linguists, or couriers by European trading companies, and their ambiguous status — sometimes in European service, sometimes operating on their own account as pirates or smugglers — made them natural conduits of intelligence and rumour in the region. The fact that Thyssen knows both his Malay or Dutch name "Teunes" and his Portuguese alias "Santa Maria" suggests that the man was already a familiar figure in Dutch intelligence reports.

## Function as a dispatch-carrier

The most interesting feature of the passage is Thyssen's belief that the [[Tommegong of Singapore|Tommegong of Singapore]]'s war-boats were not seeking plunder but were watching for "the dispatches which … are expected with Santa Maria (alias Teunes)." This implies that Teunes was at that moment carrying, or was expected to carry, official correspondence between Dutch posts — most likely between [[Malacca]] and either Batavia or Riau. The interception of dispatches was a common stratagem in the intelligence war that accompanied the Anglo-Dutch rivalry in the archipelago, and the Tommegong's mobilisation suggests that he, in concert with the newly arrived British under [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] (who had just [[Founding of Singapore|founded Singapore]] in February [[1819]]), was seeking to gain advance knowledge of Dutch intentions.

## Context of the Thyssen letter

Thyssen's letter to Wolterbeek was written at a moment of acute Dutch alarm. Carnegy, the harbour-master at Malacca, had reported that two ships with three hundred troops had sailed from Penang for the [[Karimon Islands|Carrimons]] with the intention of taking possession of those islands, and that Raffles was preparing to follow with three more ships and four hundred men, ostensibly for [[Acheh]] — a report Thyssen disbelieved, suspecting instead an attack on [[Lingga]]. In this atmosphere, every movement of armed craft — whether British warships, Malay *pengojabja*, or the lone dispatch-carrier Santa Maria — took on an exaggerated significance. Teunes's small part in this larger drama was to be the unwitting object of the Tommegong's naval watch.

## Significance

Teunes is a marginal figure in Van der Kemp's narrative, but his brief appearance is revealing. He illustrates the dense network of semi-official couriers, pilots and pirates through which information moved in the early-nineteenth-century Straits, and he highlights the role that Malay chiefs such as the [[Tommegong of Singapore]] were already playing in the Anglo-Dutch intelligence contest within weeks of Raffles's landing. The fact that Van der Kemp, writing in [[1900]], thought the passage worth reproducing verbatim as *Bijlage* IV reflects his broader concern to show how seriously the Dutch authorities took Raffles's movements in [[1819]] — a seriousness that, in his view, later Dutch historiography had too readily dismissed.

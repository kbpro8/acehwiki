**S. Kalff** was a Dutch colonial-mission journalist and author whose sketch "*Een trits Indische Pastoors*" ("A trio of Indian pastors"), published in *Koloniaal Missie Tijdschrift* (Jaargang XII), is the principal biographical source for [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]], the *soldatenpastoor* of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The sketch is reproduced, with attribution, in the [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume of 1934]], and it preserves the most vivid and anecdote-rich account of Verbraak's thirty-year chaplaincy on the Atjeh war-footing.

## The "trits Indische Pastoors" sketch

Kalff's sketch of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Verbraak]] — introduced in the [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|jubilee volume]] with the editorial note that the reader will welcome the opportunity to learn more of "this man of God" (*Iets meer van dezen man Gods te vernemen, zal velen welkom zijn*) — is described by the editor as "the interesting and gemoedelijke schets" (the interesting and *gemoedelijke* — "warm" or "genial" — sketch) of the *soldatenpastoor*. The sketch's narrative voice is anecdotal, fond, and rich in quotation: it preserves the soldiers' jokes about sauerkraut (*zuurkool langs de geheele linie*), the papaya-trees of the presbytery, the carafe of port beneath the portrait of the Pope, the field-altar improvised from gin-cases ("*de poppekast is klaar!*"), and the cholera-ward testimony of the medical officer Dr. [[N. Munk|N. Munk]].

## Method and sources

Kalff's method, as it emerges from the sketch, is to compose a portrait from anecdote rather than from the official record. He draws on:

- The testimony of officers of the Netherlands-Indian Army who had known Verbraak in Atjeh ("*Een officier van het O.I. leger, die hem op Atjeh leerde kennen, schreef...*").
- The testimony of the medical officer Dr. [[N. Munk|N. Munk]] on the cholera-ward at [[Panteh Perak|Panteh Perak]].
- The testimony of Colonel J. F. D. Bruinsma on Verbraak's disregard of the racial conventions of colonial transport.
- The reminiscences of soldiers, visitors, and companions preserved in the oral memory of the colonial mission.

The sketch is therefore a composite of oral-history testimonies, fixed in print by Kalff's editorial labour, and it is the source of nearly all the anecdotal detail that survives of Verbraak's personality.

## Reproduction in the 1934 jubilee volume

The [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|jubilee volume of 1934]] reproduces Kalff's sketch — possibly in condensed form — under the heading "*Pastoor H. C. Verbraak*," with the editorial preface acknowledging Kalff as the author and *Koloniaal Missie Tijdschrift* Jaargang XII as the place of first publication. The sketch, as reproduced, supplies the structure of the Verbraak article in the present encyclopedia.

## Significance

Kalff's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history is documentary. His sketch is the principal source for the biography of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]], and therefore for the documented Catholic presence on the Atjeh war-footing from [[1874]] to [[1908]]. Without Kalff's editorial labour — the gathering of officer testimony, the preservation of soldiers' jokes, the citation of Dr. [[N. Munk|N. Munk]]'s cholera-ward memoir — the human texture of the Catholic chaplaincy of Atjeh would be irrecoverable. His sketch is also, more generally, a document of the genre of the colonial-mission biographical portrait, in which anecdote, oral testimony, and editorial affection combine to fix a clerical figure in the institutional memory of the colonial church.

**J. T. Cremer** — Jacob Theodoor Cremer ([[1847]]-[[1923]]) — was a Dutch liberal politician who served as minister of Colonies in the [[kabinet Pierson]] ([[1899]]-[[1904]]) and is the parliamentary architect of the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] that, after his fall, would reach into the Atjeh oilfields. His interventions in the Atjeh debates of the [[1896]]-[[1904]] period are among the most forceful liberal expressions of the "drastisch optreden in Atjeh" — drastic action in Atjeh — school.

## On the Atjeh-oorlog

In the [[1896]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate that followed [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|the defection of Toekoe Oemar]], [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] (lib.) joined [[Rutgers van Rozenburg|Rutgers van Rozenburg]] (lib.) in calling for "een drastisch optreden in Atjeh om, 'deze kanker' te verwijderen, ook in het belang van onze financiën" — drastic action in Atjeh to remove "this cancer", also in the interest of our finances. The liberals asked the minister of Colonies not to "corresponderen over 'het bestendigen der bestendiging'" — to correspond about "the perpetuation of the perpetuation" — and not to come with a request of ƒ 300,000 when perhaps ƒ 3,000,000 was needed. They would rather see the construction of railways and irrigation works on Java interrupted for a time than the colonial existence in Atjeh — and subsequently in the whole archipelago — put at risk.

## Cremer on population confidence

In a footnote preserved by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]], [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] observed that what had complicated the recovery of [[Groot-Atjeh]] after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|the defection of Toekoe Oemar]] was the fear of the population that the Dutch troops would again withdraw, and that it would then be at the mercy of a figure like [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]]. Once the population was given confidence that the Dutch would not leave again, "hadden wij het spel half gewonnen" — we had half won the game.

## The Mijnwet of 1899

[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]]'s most consequential colonial achievement was the [[Mijnwet|Rengers-era Mijnwet]] of [[1899]]. The law set the cijns (royalty) on mineral extraction at 2 % of gross yield — considerably less than the [[Billiton Maatschappij]] contract of [[1892]] had stipulated. An "uitvoerige en enigszins verwarde discussie" developed over whether, economically, the cijns should be levied on net rather than gross yield; [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] stood his ground, and the [[Tweede Kamer]] — "die eigenlijk wat verlegen was met haar beslissing" — rather at a loss with its decision — followed him. The committee of rapporteurs, however, raised the percentage from 2 to 4. The bill passed the [[Tweede Kamer]] 64 to 9, with [[Mackay]], [[Pijnacker Hordijk]] and [[Lohman]] among the dissenters; the [[Eerste Kamer]] vote was 26 to 18, with most dissenters following [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]]'s view that the rights of the indigenous owners of the top-soil had been too lightly passed over.

## The Indische Comptabiliteitswet amendment

In [[1902]] [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] — still minister of Colonies in the [[kabinet Pierson]] — submitted a *wetsontwerp* to amend the [[Indische Comptabiliteitswet]] so as to abolish the *bijdrage* (contribution) of [[Nederlandsch-Indië]] to the finances of the realm — the formal closure of the [[Batig Slot|batig-slot]] era. The bill, enacted on [[26 June]] [[1903]] (S. 172), came into force in the sittings-year [[1902]]/3. [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] and his successor [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] alike declined to frame the measure as the discharge of an [[Ereschuld|ereschuld]]: "geen terugtrede in hetgeen door de daartoe volkomen bevoegde macht vroeger in wettige vorm is geschied" — no retreat from what was done in lawful form by the authority fully competent to do so.

## Significance

[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]]'s twin colonial achievements — the [[Mijnwet|Mijnwet]] and the abolition of the *bijdrage* — define the legal-economic frame within which the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] of Atjeh and the subsequent exploitation of its petroleum resources were carried out. The [[Mijnwet|Mijnwet]] would, in [[1910]], be amended by Article 5a precisely to bring Atjeh oil contracts within legislative purview.

**Lieutenant Ahn** served as a junior officer of the **Dutch Marine Corps** during the 1875‑1876 campaign in **Atjeh**. He was wounded by a **beenschot** (leg shot) during the assault on **Adjoen** on [[30 December]] [[1875]] and was evacuated to an **ambulance** (see [[page 135]]).

## Action at Adjoen

While covering the artillery section, Lieutenant Ahn received orders to protect the gun crews. His injury forced the **Mariniers** to reorganise under **Lieutenant Mirandolle**, who assumed command of Ahn’s section (see [[page 135]]).

## Aftermath

Ahn was later transferred to a hospital in **Padang**, where he boarded the steamship **Kroesen** for repatriation. The vessel later sank, and Ahn was reported to have been “in a lazy chair” on the deck when it went down (see [[page 136]]).

## Significance

His experience reflects the hazards faced by Dutch marine officers operating in the difficult terrain and climate of Aceh, as well as the logistical challenges of medical evacuation.

**Sri Tengkoe Abdoel Hamid Orangkaja Sri Maharadja Mangkoe Boemi** was the *[[Oeléebalang|oeléebalang]]* of the small *landschap* of [[Lho Seumawe|Lho' Seumawé]] — a *landschap* whose head, despite its smallness, was of old a respected prince, bearing the title *[[Maharadja|maharadja]]* and the further honorifics *Orangkaja Sri Maharadja Mangkoe Boemi*. The title points to his former standing with the [[Sultan of Atjeh|sultan of Atjeh]] and in the whole of Atjeh. He signed the *[[Korte Verklaring (1899)|Korte Verklaring]]* in [[1904]].

## The *Wilde Woesteling* of 1873
About fifty years before Broersma's visit — that is, in the years around [[1873]] — a representative of the Netherlands government had wished to meet the *maharadja* and had come ashore from his ship in the bay to do so. The *maharadja* awaited him, leaning against a tree, *trotsch* — proud. He and many of the chiefs stood with loaded revolvers in their belts; the signs of submissiveness were far off. A still-living witness, quoted by [[J. A. Kruyt|J. A. Kruyt]], described him as *'een ruw woesteling'* — *a rough savage* — *'hij was groot en zwaar, liep met lange loshangende haren en was gekleed in een zwart jasje met gouden sovereigns als knoopen'* — *he was large and heavy, walked with long loose-hanging hair, and was dressed in a black jacket with gold sovereigns as buttons.*

## Income and Influence
His *landschap* may be small, *'daarentegen is zijn invloed in het heele gewest zoo groot als zijn titel'* — *but his influence in the whole gewest is as great as his title.* From the *landschap* and from the indemnification of the government he receives a monthly income of well over f 1.000, in addition to the income from his own possessions and trade.

## Sarikat Islam
In the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh* — comprising the *onderafdeelingen* [[Lho Seumawe|Lho' Seumawé]], [[Bireuen]] and [[Lho Soekon]] — the [[Sarikat Islam|Sarikat Islam]] showed a vigorous life after [[1918]]. Traders, school-teachers — *'laat ons zeggen de intellectueelen'* — but also several chiefs were members and took part. A chief of [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] had joined by way of a move on the political chessboard on which he was playing against the influential *Maharadja*, who had also become a member of the Sarikat Islam in order to strengthen his chances of retaining a *landschap Plimbang*. Having taken the advice of an *[[Oelama|oelama]]* — a Mohammedan jurist — that joining might be called a good deed, the Samalangan chief formed an *afdeeling S.I.* The movement in Lho' Seumawe, however, took the direction of bringing the distinction between believers and unbelievers to the fore, of undermining the Dutch government, and of working towards a union of *oeléebalangs*. Three chiefs of Lho' Seumawe were banished in [[1922]], an intervention Broersma describes as *'geen dag te vroeg gekomen'* — *not a day too soon.*

## Significance
The *Maharadja* of Lho' Seumawé is the type-figure of the great titular *oeléebalang* of the north coast — small in *landschap*, great in *invloed* — whose career spans the entire transition from the *ruw woesteling* of [[1873]] to the political intriguer of the Sarikat Islam years. His tenure of *Plimbang* was contested and his influence on the politics of the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh* was, in Broersma's judgement, second to none.

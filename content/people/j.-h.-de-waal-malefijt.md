**J. H. de Waal Malefijt** — a Dutch Anti-Revolutionary politician — served as minister of Colonies ([[1913]]-[[1918]]) in the [[Heemskerk]] wartime cabinet. He is the principal anti-revolutionary executor of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] program in the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] period and the principal parliamentary antagonist of [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] in the [[1911 Indische begrotingsdebat|1911 begrotingsdebat]] on the question of government support for Christian *zending*.

## "Kende Indië alleen van het papier"

In the retrospective of [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]], [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] "kende Indië alleen van het papier" — knew the Indies only from paper. Yet the same verdict notes that one should not therefore suppose his ministry was particularly bad: "Zijne Excellentie kon terugvallen op de adviezen uit Indië" — His Excellency could fall back on the advices from the Indies. The complaint of the chambermembers, however, was that the minister too often "zich er achter verschool" — hid behind them — and that the *ambtelijke adviezen* (official advices) from the governor-general, the residents, the directors, the [[Raad van Indië]], the Hooggerechtshof, the generals, the staatscommissies concealed more than they revealed.

## The 1911 begrotingsdebat on the zending

In the [[1911 Indische begrotingsdebat|1911 begrotingsdebat]] [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] (a.r.) — cornering [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] on the basis of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s recently published *[[Nederland en de Islam ([[1911]])|Nederland en de Islam]]* — argued for *bizonder onderwijs* (confessional schools) as the rule in the Indies, with *gouvernementsonderwijs* as a supplement only. [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] replied that he adhered to the position of his predecessors: no direct support for the *zending* (missions), but strong support where the missions operated on social terrain. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s rule for confessional schools was, in his view, possible only in a few regions of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Buitenbezettingen]] where the missions had gained a firm foothold; on [[Java]] — where the Muslim majority lacked the organisation to act on its own initiative — the rule was inapplicable.

## The Kuyper–Heemskerk incident

[[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s feeling that [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] had failed to "warm genoeg voor de a.r. beginselen uitkomen" — to come out warmly enough for the anti-revolutionary principles — and his suspicion of "de explosie van gejubel bij de linkse heren" — the explosion of jubilation among the gentlemen of the left — led to an incident in which [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] "zich op zijn beurt door [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] gekrenkt [voelde]" — felt himself in turn injured by Kuyper — and the first minister [[Heemskerk (first minister)|Heemskerk]] had to come to his defence. The incident, "die in die tijd veel opzien baarde" — which in its day caused much stir — was, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s judgement, perhaps most of all a symptom of the "geprikkelde stemming" between [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] and the [[Heemskerk (first minister)|Heemskerk]]-[[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] government.

## On the *adat*-law revision

During the debate on the revision of the *adat*-law provisions for the indigenous population, [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] (a.r.) was one of only three supporters — alongside [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.) — of minister [[D. Fock|Fock]]'s position. The amendment of [[Van Idsinga]] — broadly reflecting what [[Lohman (De Savornin Lohman)|Lohman]] wanted — was eventually accepted.

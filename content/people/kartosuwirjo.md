**Kartosuwirjo** was the Imam of the [[Negara Islam Indonesia]] (N.I.I.), the Islamic-state movement founded in [[West Java|West Java]] in [[1949]], who in [[September]] [[1953]] appointed [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]] as his governor for [[Atjèh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia (1953)|Atjeh]] following the province's proclaimed accession to the N.I.I. on [[21 September]] [[1953]].

## The Atjeh appointment and propaganda campaign

Henri J. H. Alers's *Om een rode of groene Merdeka* records that once Daoed Beureuh's rebellion made Atjeh part of the N.I.I., Kartosuwirjo lost no time exploiting the changed international climate. Following the World Muslim Conference secretary-general Inamullah Khan's declaration of [[27 September]] [[1953]] that Islamic states would side against any "red" cause, Kartosuwirjo circulated a signed pamphlet — sent to every embassy in [[Djakarta]] — charging that the government of the Republic of Indonesia and several of its leading figures were, in his words, "more or less important instruments of international communism." [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Daoed Beureuh]]'s own information service, the "*Media Djihad*," pressed the identical theme.

## Strategic value of Atjeh to the N.I.I.

Alers argues that the strategic significance of the Atjeh rebellion, far more than the geographically marginal insurgency of [[Kahar Muzakkar|Kahar Muzakkar]] in South Celebes, was what gave Kartosuwirjo's international outreach any real substance. He notes President [[Soekarno|Sukarno]]'s statement at [[Bone|Bone]] on [[14 October]] [[1953]] that Kartosuwirjo had appealed to the United States for aid, and observes that such an appeal could only plausibly have concerned Atjeh — a territory Kartosuwirjo had something concrete to offer a foreign patron — rather than South Celebes. Alers also records that after the Atjeh rising, Kartosuwirjo was willing to "come to terms" with the Republic only on a strict basis of "state facing state," a stance that underscored how much greater weight Atjeh's accession, compared to the older West Java and South Celebes fronts, had given his movement by late 1953.

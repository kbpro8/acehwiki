**Augustin de Beaulieu** was a French captain who led the [[Augustin de Beaulieu's Atjeh mission (1620-1621)|French expedition to the East Indies of 1620]]–[[1621]] in search of the pepper trade. Driven by monsoon and misfortune to [[Tikoe]] on [[Sumatra]]'s Westkust — a pepper-port dependent on the Sultan of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — and thence to Atjeh itself in early [[1621]], De Beaulieu became entangled in difficult negotiations with the Sultan and the *Rijksgrooten* over permission to buy pepper, and saw his Vice-Admiral [[Robert Gravé|Robert Gravé]] die and be buried in Atjeh. The De Beaulieu expedition is one of the principal French intrusions into the Dutch-Atjeh pepper trade in the early seventeenth century.

## The French Saint-Malo expedition

The expedition was fitted out at Saint-Malo and consisted of several ships, including the *Montmorency* (De Beaulieu's flagship), the *l'Hermitage*, the *l'Espérance* (under the Vice-Admiral [[Robert Gravé|Robert Gravé]]), and the *Leiden*. The plan was to reach the East Indies and break into the pepper trade that the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] was already consolidating.

## Landfall at Tikoe, Sumatra's Westkust

Driven past Cape Guardafui, De Beaulieu could not make the intended coast and landed with the *Montmorency* and *l'Hermitage* at [[Tikoe]] on Sumatra's Westkust — "een centrum van peperhandel, afhankelijk van den Sultan van Atjeh" — a centre of pepper trade, dependent on the Sultan of Atjeh. From Tikoe he came in early [[1621]] to Atjeh itself, where he had difficult negotiations with the Sultan and the *Rijksgrooten* (great officers) to obtain permission for the pepper trade. De Beaulieu particularly complained about the extortion of gifts during the negotiations: "vooral klaagt De Beaulieu over het afpersen van geschenken bij de besprekingen" — De Beaulieu particularly complained about the extortion of gifts during the discussions.

## The arrival of the sick Gravé and the *l'Espérance*

Suddenly, in November [[1621]], while the *Montmorency* lay in a bay to be cleaned of the shell-layer that was impeding her sailing, an English ship appeared aboard which was the gravely ill Vice-Admiral [[Robert Gravé|Gravé]]. He, together with one De Parc of the *l'Espérance*'s crew, told the story of that ship — a tale full of inaccuracies that later appeared in print in De Beaulieu's travel-adventures. The French had not reckoned on the Dutch carefully checking and recording their doings.

The truth was that before Tikoe the *l'Espérance* had lain twelve days waiting for a sloop sent ashore with fifteen sick sailors to fetch help. The sloop never reappeared. Ten of the sailors died; the five survivors took a Dutch yacht to Atjeh to seek their Admiral. On the great ship were 74 souls; Gravé and three others were still on their feet, but the ship was unmanageable for want of hands. The Dutch ship *Leiden* found the French "gants machteloos" — entirely powerless. Only two casks of water remained. Some of the sick were taken aboard the *Leiden*; the *l'Espérance* was given a crew of 50 Dutchmen. At the island Nassau it was hoped the sick would recover, but many found their grave there.

## The Dutch *Raad van Defensie* and the French claim

When the *Leiden* and the *l'Espérance* arrived before Jakatra, 95 persons had died and 29 survived. The French printed account claims the ship was taken by force and brought to Java — "Tijdgenooten wisten beter" — contemporaries knew better. The Dutch ambassador in Paris was thanked by French nobles interested in the Dieppe expedition for the help given to the *l'Espérance* — for which favour they waived complaints about the lifting of Dutch sailors from French ships.

## The death of Gravé at Atjeh

[[Robert Gravé|Gravé]] went in the bark *l'Hermitage* to Atjeh, where De Beaulieu had already departed. By English ship he set out, happened to meet his chief, and told him, deathly ill, of his troubles in the presence of witnesses. In the night of [[9 November]], two hours before daybreak, Gravé died of a violent fever "causée de facherie" — caused by vexation — arising "uit ergernis" (from offence/chagrin). He was buried in Atjeh. See [[Robert Gravé]].

## Aftermath

When [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] was in Holland in [[1623]] he wrote that the English and French questions had cost the Company some sixteen tonnes of gold. The French shipowners received their claimed damages — 550,000 *livres* — paid out by the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]]. Le Tellier's grave is in Bantam. Gravé's grave is in Atjeh.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, De Beaulieu is the principal French protagonist of the early seventeenth century — the captain whose 1620–1621 mission brought French arms and French claims into the pepper trade that the Sultan of Atjeh controlled through his Westkust dependencies. The episode is also the proximate cause of [[Robert Gravé|Gravé]]'s death and burial at Atjeh, and of the long-running French claim for damages against the VOC that was eventually settled at 550,000 *livres*. Through De Beaulieu's own later published narrative the episode entered the European literature of Asian travel.

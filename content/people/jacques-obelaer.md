**Jacques Obelaer** was a Dutch factor in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] who served as the Company's factor at [[Batoesawer|Batoesawer]], the capital of [[Djohor|Djohor]], from February [[1609]] to the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of May 1613]], in which the city was taken, the Dutch garrison overpowered, and twenty-two of the Dutchmen — including Obelaer — carried off to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] as prisoners. Obelaer's factorship at Batoesawer frames the Dutch-Djohor commercial relationship in the period between the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]] and the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of 1613]].

## The factorship at Batoesawer

Obelaer succeeded **Van den Broeck** as factor at Batoesawer in February [[1609]], when the [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] fleet departed from [[Djohor|Djohor]] for [[Bantam|Bantam]] after failing to obtain the sultan's assistance in an attack on [[Malaka|Malaka]]. Verhoeff left two ships at the river mouth to maintain the Dutch presence; the ships stayed until November [[1609]], when the sultan's peace with the Portuguese was formally proclaimed under trumpet-sound on [[16 October]] [[1610]]. Obelaer's position was thus increasingly precarious: the sultan's separate peace with the Portuguese had ended the Dutch-Djohor military alliance of [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|1606]], and the Dutch factory at Batoesawer survived only on the sultan's sufferance.

## The Achehnese attack (May 1613)

When the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s successor sent a fleet against [[Djohor|Djohor]] in May [[1613]], the Achehnese appeared at the mouth of the river on [[7 May]] [[1613]] while the bulk of the Djohor fleet was away at [[Pahang|Pahang]]. The Dutch ship *Hope*, which had arrived three days earlier to relieve Obelaer and replenish the factory's capital, lay at anchor in the river. The Achehnese demanded that the Dutch stand aside; Obelaer and the Dutch refused, in fidelity to the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff-sultan accord of 23 September 1606]], and landed thirty-one men under the factory's six-man staff to assist the Djohor defence. The Achehnese took the city on [[6 June]] [[1613]]; two Dutchmen were killed, twenty-two — including Obelaer — were taken prisoner and carried off to Atjeh "onder borchtocht van des compagnies goederen"; three wounded men returned to the ship; the rest were missing.

## The aftermath

Obelaer's captivity at Atjeh was brief. Within months the two Malay courts had reached an accommodation: the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|sultan of Atjeh]] gave his sister in marriage to the deposed sultan of Djohor — now styled **Sultan Sabrang** — and sent him back to Djohor with an Achehnese escort of two thousand men to rebuild the destroyed city. The Dutch prisoners were released as part of the rapprochement. Obelaer's letter from Batoesawer of [[25 November]] [[1610]] — preserved in [[J. K. J. de Jonge|De Jonge]]'s *De opkomst van het Nederlandsch gezag in Oost-Indië*, Dl. III — is the principal Dutch source for the sultan of Djohor's separate peace with the Portuguese in [[1610]] and the proximate cause of the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of 1613]].

## Significance

Jacques Obelaer is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch factor at [[Batoesawer|Batoesawer]] in the period between the [[Matelieff's Malaka campaign (1606)|Matelieff campaign of 1606]] and the [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of 1613]], and the principal Dutch witness of the collapse of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance. His factorship frames the Dutch-Djohor commercial relationship in the period of the [[Twelve Years' Truce (1609)|Twelve Years' Truce]], when the sultan's separate peace with the Portuguese and the Dutch inability to maintain a fleet in the Strait of Malaka in force undermined the strategic basis of the alliance. The [[Atjeh's attack on Djohor (May 1613)|Achehnese attack of May 1613]] — and Obelaer's captivity at Atjeh — marked the effective end of the Dutch-Djohor military alliance that [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]] had constructed in [[1606]] and the strategic turn southward to [[Jacatra|Jacatra]] and the eventual founding of [[Batavia|Batavia]] in [[1619]].

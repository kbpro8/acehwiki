**Raja Alam** — also written *Radja Alam*, *R. Alam* — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the Siak prince whose disputes with his brother [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] over the Siak succession were the immediate occasion of the Dutch East India Company's (the *Oost-Indische Compagnie*'s) [[1755]] establishment of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post on the [[Siak|Siak River]], and whose restoration to the Siak throne with Dutch support in [[1759]]–[[1761]] was the first act of the long Dutch-Siak alliance that would, in turn, prepare the ground for the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Siak overlordship of the East Coast statelets]] under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]].

## The dispute with Raja Moehamad

The Bureau records [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]]'s dispute with his brother [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] in a single, dense footnote. "In [[1755]] verleende de O. I. C. hulp aan Radja Moehamad, in zijne twisten met zijn broeder R. Alam, over de opvolging in Siak" — "In 1755 the O. I. C. gave help to Radja Moehamad, in his disputes with his brother R. Alam, over the succession in Siak." The dispute was, in the Bureau's framing, a fraternal succession dispute — the standard form of Siak dynastic conflict — and the Compagnie's support for [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] was, in return for the right to establish the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post, the first Dutch intervention in the Siak succession.

## The 1759 reversal

The Compagnie's support for [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] was, however, reversed in [[1759]]. The Bureau records: "Het afloopen van die post in [[1759]] had tengevolge dat de Compagnie steun verleende aan den eerst met haar hulp van den troon verdreven Radja Alam, den tegenstander van den in [[1759]] heerschenden sultan Radja Moehamad" — "The running-out [i.e. the destruction] of that post in 1759 had, as a consequence, that the Compagnie gave support to Radja Alam, who had first been driven from the throne with her help, the opponent of the sultan who ruled in 1759, Radja Moehamad." The Compagnie's reversal of allegiance — from [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] to [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] — was the immediate consequence of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]]'s destruction of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post, and it inaugurated the long Dutch-Siak alliance that would persist into the 1760s.

## The restoration and the conquest of Pelalawan (1761)

The Dutch-backed restoration of [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] was completed in [[1761]]. The Bureau records: "Deze laatste overleed in [[1760]], doch zijn opvolger Radja Ismail, werd in [[1761]] overwonnen en verdreven naar Pelalawan, dat daarop eveneens met behulp der Compagnie werd overweldigd door R. Alam" — "This latter [Radja Moehamad] died in 1760, but his successor Radja Ismail, was in 1761 overcome and driven out to Pelalawan, which thereafter, also with the help of the Compagnie, was overwhelmed by R. Alam." The Dutch-backed conquest of [[Pelalawan|Pelalawan]] was, in the Bureau's framing, the first step in the great Siak expansion of the 1760s, and the political event that transformed the Siak sultanate from a contested principate into a regional power.

## Significance

[[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the political beneficiary of the [[1759]] destruction of the [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] post and the Dutch reversal of allegiance that followed. His restoration to the Siak throne with Dutch support in [[1759]]–[[1761]] was, in the Bureau's framing, the first act of the long Dutch-Siak alliance that would, in turn, prepare the ground for the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Siak overlordship of the East Coast statelets]] under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]. [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]]'s Dutch-backed conquest of [[Pelalawan|Pelalawan]] in [[1761]] was, in turn, the first step in the great Siak expansion of the 1760s — the expansion that would, in the late eighteenth century, transform the Siak sultanate into the principal rival of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] for the East Coast overlordship.

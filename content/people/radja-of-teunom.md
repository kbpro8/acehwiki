The **Radja of Teunom** was the West Coast Atjehnese chief who in November [[1883]] took prisoner the crew of the stranded English steamer *[[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Nisero]]*, plundered the vessel, and held the captives for almost a year while bargaining — first for the settlement of a pepper debt, then, as he openly admitted, in the hope of embroiling the Dutch in a war with England.

## The affair of the Nisero

When the English steamer *Nisero* stranded near the mouth of the [[Panga]] river in the night of [[10 November|10]]–[[11 November]] [[1883]], the crew that came ashore received a most ungracious treatment from the population, culminating in their imprisonment by the Radja of [[Teunom]] and the plundering of the stranded steamer. To the assistant-resident's first demand for their release the Radja answered in writing that he was prepared to set them free when care was taken that a certain pepper merchant at [[Penang]], who still owed him a fairly large sum for sold and delivered pepper, fulfilled his obligations. When the English warship *Pegasus* appeared in the Atjeh waters to gather intelligence, the Radja raised his demands; he came out openly with the saying that by demanding higher prices he hoped to involve the Dutch in a war with England. When Mr. Maxwell, member of the colonial council at Singapore, was brought over on the *Pegasus* and personally negotiated, the Radja made demands "which by everyone without distinction were called ridiculous", among them general free trade under guarantee of England — with, of course, as first object the import of weapons and ammunition.

## Defiance and settlement

An ultimatum remaining unanswered, the military expedition of Colonel [[Demmeni|H. Demmeni]] (steaming from [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]] on [[4 January]] [[1884]]) opened hostilities and administered an exemplary chastisement; the coast was closed wide around, the fisheries impeded, and warships fired on everything within reach — but the Radja remained indifferent. The eccentric mediation of [[Teukoe Oemar]], who went to [[Lambeusoë]] to negotiate, ended in his treacherous attack on the government sloop's crew and his return to the resistance. Only after Governor [[P. F. Laging Tobias]] together with Maxwell again negotiated with the Radja — with the stipulation that f 100,000 would be paid to the person who brought back the prisoners, that the harbours would be reopened and the closing of the surrounding statelets lifted — were the captives delivered up, on [[10 September]] [[1884]], after almost a year in the Radja's hands.

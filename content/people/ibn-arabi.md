**Muḥyī al-Dīn Ibn ʿArabī** — in Malay tradition simply **Ibn ʿArabī** — was the greatest of the Sufi metaphysicians, born in Murcia in 560 H / [[1165]] C.E. and died in Damascus in 638 H / [[1240]] C.E. He is the author of the *al-Futūḥāt al-Makkīya* ("The Meccan Revelations") and the *Fuṣūṣ al-ḥikam* ("The Bezels of Wisdom"), the principal expositions of the doctrine of the unity of being (*waḥdat al-wujūd*). His works are cited repeatedly by [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] as authorities against the heretical [[Wujudiyyah]] of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] and [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] — a remarkable fact, since Ibn ʿArabī is himself the principal authority of the doctrine of the unity of being, and is so cited by [[Abd al-Karim al-Jili|al-Jīlī]] and by Shamsuddin himself.

## The orthodox authority against the Wujūdiyyah

In the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq li-dafʿ az-zindīq*]] of Raniri, Ibn ʿArabī is cited — from the *Futūḥāt Makkīya* — as rejecting those who identify the visible world with God: "Ibn ʿArabī says in his book *Futūḥāt Makkīya*, where he refutes the Wujūdiyyah, speaking of a group that identifies the visible world with God." The citation is the cornerstone of Raniri's polemical strategy: the authority of the greatest of the Sufi metaphysicians is invoked against those who, in the name of the unity of being, identify God with the world. The orthodox Wujūdiyyah — the Sufis themselves — are distinguished from the heretical Wujūdiyyah by the very authority from whom the doctrine of the unity of being derives.

## Citations in Raniri's other works

Ibn ʿArabī is cited repeatedly in Raniri's Malay writings. In the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] he is cited (p. 17) on the meaning of *wājib al-wujūd* ("the Necessarily Existent") as "the absolute Being" — that is, the being of God. In the [[Hall al-zill|*Ḥall aẓ-ẓill*]] he is named among "the great mystics." In the [[Djawahir al-'ulum|*Jawāhir al-ʿulūm*]] he is cited repeatedly. In the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] he is cited in the section on the Wujūdiyyah.

## Significance

Ibn ʿArabī is the principal authority of the doctrine of the unity of being, and is so cited by [[Abd al-Karim al-Jili|al-Jīlī]] and by [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] — Raniri's Wujūdiyyah opponent. The fact that Raniri is able to cite the same authority against the Wujūdiyyah is a measure of the difficulty of distinguishing the orthodox from the heterodox position in the scholastic Sufism of the seventeenth century, and of the role of commentary and interpretation — rather than the choice of authorities — in the religious controversy of [[Acheh]]. The same Ibn ʿArabī who is the principal authority of the Wujūdiyyah tradition is, in Raniri's reading, an authority against the heretical Wujūdiyyah of Hamzah Pansuri's school.

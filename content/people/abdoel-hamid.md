**Abdoel Hamid** (also *Abdul Hamid*) was an [[Acheh|Atjeh]] ambassador who, in [[1602]], was sent by his Sultan — [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Sultan Aloeddin]] — together with [[Sri Mohammed]] and [[Mir Hassan]] on the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits]] at the siege of Grave. He was already more than seventy years old when he set out, and he died at [[Middelburg]] in August [[1602]], only weeks after arriving in the Republic — the first recorded fatality of an Atjeh envoy on European soil.

## The embassy of 1602

The embassy was sent in response to the four Zeeland ships that had come to Atjeh in August [[1601]] bearing a letter from [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]] pressing for the release of the Dutch prisoners held there since the [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)|death of Cornelis de Houtman]] in [[1599]] — most notably [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]]. The Sultan had agreed to release the prisoners and to compensate Frederik de Houtman "en zijn vrouw" (and his wife — for the prisoner's fidelity to his wife in Holland had made a strong impression) for the material losses he had suffered. As a token of the new friendship, the Sultan sent three ambassadors on the returning ships: the senior envoy Abdoel Hamid, the admiral of Atjeh [[Sri Mohammed|Sri Mohammed]], and the nobleman [[Mir Hassan|Mir Hassan]]. A Luxemburger, [[Leonard Werner|Leonard Werner]], served as their interpreter.

## Arrival at Middelburg and death

The ships reached the Republic in [[1602]]. The aged Abdoel Hamid — "ruim zeventigjarige" (well over seventy years old) — survived the Atlantic crossing but died at Middelburg in August [[1602]], before the embassy could complete its journey to Prince Maurits's siege camp at Grave. He was, in Godee Molsbergen's framing, the first of the three Atjeh envoys whose embassy would be — for him — posthumous.

## The embassy continues

After Abdoel Hamid's death, the remaining ambassadors [[Sri Mohammed]] and [[Mir Hassan]] proceeded to Prince Maurits's camp at Grave, where they were received with state: a state-coach attended by a company of cavalry brought the Oriental gentlemen to a house near the Prince's tent, and after a meal Maurits received them surrounded by a brilliant company of nobles and military commanders. They delivered two letters from Sultan [[Sultan Aloeddin of Atjeh|Aloeddin]] to the Prince, returned the Prince's earlier letter (now marked with the King's seal, "om te bewyzen, dat die brieven wel ontfangen, hem weert ende aenghenaem waren gheweest" — to prove that the letters had indeed been received, welcome and agreeable to him), and presented the Prince with a costly *kris*, a golden dish and cup, "about two pounds of Camphora of Borneo" (each pound valued at a hundred ducats in those lands), and a lance — the lance being on one of the still-sailing ships.

## Significance

Abdoel Hamid is significant in the Dutch-Atjeh encounter as the senior figure of the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|first Atjeh embassy to the Republic]], and as the first Atjeh ambassador known to have died on European soil. His death at Middelburg in August [[1602]] — well over seventy years old, having crossed two oceans to reach a country he would never leave — marks the human cost of the early modern diplomatic exchange between the sultanate of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the young Dutch Republic.

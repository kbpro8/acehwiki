**Generaal Pel** was a Dutch *kolonel* (later *generaal-majoor*) of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]], *commandant der 2de brigade* during the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]] and from [[26 April]] [[1874]] the successor to [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] as *opperbevelhebber* and *civiel bestuurder* in Atjeh. He is the hero of [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s book — "nimmer zoo groot en schitterend als toen" — praised for his *doorzicht, beleid, rusteloos streven* and *zelfverloochening*, and mourned after his [[Death of Generaal Pel (24 February 1876)|sudden death at Lamjoeng]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]] as "eene ware nationale ramp."

## Inheritance of the Belegerde Vesting

Pel took command in early May [[1874]] inheriting from van Swieten a *zeer gebrekkige stelling* and a verbal instruction — "zooveel mogelijk vechten weinig, afwachtende houding bewaren" — that he soon abandoned. He was thrown at once into the [[Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)|crisis of May–June 1874]]: the *bezettingskorps* had "ten gevolge van het overgroot aantal zieken bijna niets meer beteekende," and Borel insists that 500 *kloeke* Atjehneezen would have sufficed "om den Kraton, en met dezen onze geheele vestiging ter kuste van Atjeh, ten val te brengen." Pel "zag het gevaar zeer goed in; dat bewustzijn kostte hem menigen slapeloozen nacht"; for the entire month of May he slept only a few hours, in full uniform. He rebuilt the position piece by piece — the [[Establishment of the Oeleh Leh post|Oeleh Leh post]] and the [[Strategische driehoek (Atjeh)|strategische driehoek]] Kwala Atjeh–Kota Radja–Oeleh Leh that Borel credits to him rather than to van Swieten, the daily bombardment of kampongs from [[28 April]] [[1874]] onwards, and the successive offensives at [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|Lampoeloe]] ([[20 June]]), [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|Garouw]] ([[28 June]]), [[Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)|Soerian]] ([[26 July]]), the [[Occupation of the ooster-benteng (17 August 1874)|occupation of the ooster-benteng]] ([[17 August]]), the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] ([[28 September]]), the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] ([[7 November]]), and the [[Conquest of Kota Alam (13 November 1874)|conquest of Kota Alam]] ([[13 November]]).

## The Stelling-Pel and the 31 December 1874 Disaster

By April [[1875]] Pel had completed the [[Completion of the defensive ring around Kota Radja (May 1875)|continuous defensive ring]] around [[Kota Radja]] — the *stelling-Pel*, a nearly *gelijkbenige driehoek* of thirty-six posts covering some 2 500 *hectaren*, whose base ran along the sea-strand from [[Moesapi]] to [[Oeleh Leh]] and whose apex was the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]], six thousand metres into enemy territory. Borel judges the line "een jaar te laat" — had van Swieten acted in [[1874]] what Pel achieved in [[1875]] could have been done at a fraction of the cost. The line's construction was nearly undone on [[31 December]] [[1874]] when, during the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|battle of Longbatta]], [[Majoor van der Meer|van der Meer]] misinterpreted Pel's orders, abandoned the captured Mesigit *benteng* without a garrison, and was routed on the open *sawah*; only [[Majoor van Zijll de Jong|van Zijll de Jong]]'s heroic counter-attack with sixty infantry and a four-gun battery saved the *colonne-van der Meer* from annihilation.

## The Buitenzorg Plan and the Last Offensive

Exhausted, Pel took three months' leave on [[12 June]] [[1875]] and retired to [[Buitenzorg|Buitenzorg]] in Java. There, in July [[1875]], he explained to [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] and to the newly appointed [[Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge|gouverneur-generaal van Lansberge]] his plan for the *afsluitingslinie* Kroeng Raba–Kroeng Raya that would seal Atjeh from the sea. The GG endorsed the plan and promised *ruime versterkingen*. Pel returned to Atjeh on [[9 November]] [[1875]] and, after repulsing the [[Night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit (22 November 1875)|night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit]] ([[22 November]]) and the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|enemy attack along the ooster-linie]] ([[26 November]]), launched on [[26 December]] [[1875]] the great *drie-colonne* offensive against the [[VI Moekims]], [[IV Moekims]] and [[IX Moekims]]. By early February [[1876]] the entire [[XXV Moekims]] was in Dutch hands, [[Toekoe Nanta]] had been driven out of his forts at [[Lampanti]], [[Lamti]] and Sabang, and the western end of the line reached Loenga on the [[Kroeng Raba]].

## Death at Lamjoeng

On [[22 February]] [[1876]] Pel marched northward from [[Pango]] via Lamkapang, Loeëng Iek, Poeklat and Oeleh Karang to the abandoned kampong [[Pinang (kampong)|Pinang]], and on [[23 February]] reached [[Tongah]] on the [[Kroeng Tjoet]]. At the bivouac of [[Lamjoeng]], on the far side of the river between Tongah and Silang, he died suddenly in the night of [[24 February]] [[1876]] of a *slagaderbreuk* (ruptured artery). His secret plan to close Atjeh from the sea was never written down; not even his *chef van den staf* had been told, and nothing was found in his archive. "Het vaderland verloor in hem een zijder trouwste, ijverigste en meest bekwame dienaren; dat verlies was in de gegeven omstandigheden bijna onherstelbaar."

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the 30 April 1875 letter

In the [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] treats Pel as the second name in his catalogue of the eleven Governors of Atjeh — *van Swieten, Pel, Wiggers van Kerchem, Diemont, van der Heijden* — and the author of the so-called *Pel Plan* of [[30 April]] [[1875]]: the policy letter Pel addressed to the newly installed Gouverneur-Generaal [[van Lansberge]], proposing that the path Van Swieten had marked out in agreement with Loudon and Fransen van de Putte be wholly abandoned in favour of "het op grootere schaal aanvallend optreden" — large-scale offensive operations. The Pel Plan was the first sustained Dutch alternative to Van Swieten's 2500-man concentrated defensive line at [[Kota Radja|Kotja-Radja]]. Van Lansberge, who had himself recently succeeded Loudon, accepted the proposal. The decision was taken in [[1875]]: "in 1875, op voorstel van Generaal Pel, door den nieuw opgetreden landvoogd, Gouv. Gen. van Lansberge, de door van Swieten … aangewezen weg geheel verlaten" — in 1875, on the proposal of Generaal Pel, the newly installed Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge wholly abandoned the path Van Swieten had marked out.

Van Heutsz's verdict on the Pel letter is generous. He acknowledges that the letter was well drafted — "een uitmuntend gesteld stuk" (an excellently drafted piece) — and that it was later reproduced as *Bijlage IV* in the second part of [[B. C. P. Kielstra|Kielstra]]'s *Beschrijving van den Atjeh-oorlog*. The letter, Van Heutsz writes, "het voorstel van den Generaal Pel wel zeer welkom wezen" must have been for a *landvoogd* newly arrived in office, and "Het hield niet alleen een stelsel in, maar het scheen ook een goed doordacht stelsel" — it not only constituted a system, but it appeared a well-thought-out system. The Pel Plan was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the only one of the post-Van Swieten plans that came close to constituting a coherent alternative.

### The coastal line of posts and the five *kwala's*

The Pel Plan, as Van Heutsz summarises it, "kwam neer op het bezetten van de kuststrook van de vallei van Groot-Atjeh" — it came down to occupying the coastal strip of the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The plan contemplated a continuous line of posts from [[Kroeng Raja]] in the north-east to [[Kroeng Raba]] in the south-west — "begrepen tusschen Kroeng Raja en Kroeng Raba" — closing the five river-mouths that gave the interior of Groot-Atjeh access to the sea: the *vijf toegangen naar zee de kwala's Loé, [[Gighen]], Atjeh, Tjangkoel en Pantjoer* (the five accesses to the sea, the kwala's Loé, Gighen, Atjeh, Tjangkoel and Pantjoer). The strategic idea was to sever the interior — the seat of the [[Sagi]] federations and of the [[Kemala]] war-party — from its maritime supply, while concentrating Dutch force on a defensible perimeter along the coast.

By May [[1875]], eight months into the plan's implementation, "waren in 't geheel al acht en dertig posten opgericht met eene bezetting van 2750 man" — thirty-eight posts had been erected with a garrison of 2750 men. The post-count climbed rapidly: by the end of [[1876]] the number of *bezette punten* in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden had reached forty-seven — "in 't einde van 1876 … was het aantal bezette punten in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden geklommen tot 47" — and the troop strength of the entire Atjeh force had risen, per Kielstra, to "3000 Europeanen, 180 Afrikanen en 5000 Inlanders", with a marine complement of "13 oorlogsbodems, 2 gouvernementstoomers en 4 gewapende kruisbooten" (13 warships, 2 government steamers and 4 armed cruising boats). The Van Swieten plan's 2500-man concentrated defence had become, by 1876, a forty-seven-post dispersed occupation extending over both Groot-Atjeh and the *Onderhoorigheden*.

The Pel Plan's logic was that closing the coastal strip would starve the interior, and by February [[1877]] — after the *excursie naar Koeala Loé en Kroeng Raja* — the plan was substantially complete. "Men dacht toen nog altijd, dat het door het bezetten van Koeala Loé aan den vijand te Groot-Atjeh zoo goed als onmogelijk was geworden" — it was still thought, in 1877, that the occupation of Koeala Loé had made it virtually impossible for the enemy in Groot-Atjeh to obtain supplies.

### The counter-factual and the unanswered question

Van Heutsz's counter-factual is the closest he comes to absolving Pel: had Pel lived, "zou dan zeker hebben ingezien, dat vele zijner posten in de vallei van Groot-Atjeh nutteloos waren en konden opgeheven worden" — he would certainly have seen that many of his posts in the valley of Groot-Atjeh were useless and could be abandoned. But "door 's Generaals ontijdigen dood evenwel, kwam er groote vertraging in de uitvoering van zijn plan" — through the general's untimely death, great delay entered the execution of his plan.

The Pel Plan, even completed, left the question Van Heutsz treats as central: *what next?* "Dit was het gewichtige antwoord op de vraag van Pel in zijn brief van 30 April 1875 wat, na voltooiing van zijn plan, moest gedaan worden" — this, Van Heutsz writes, was the weighty answer to the question Pel himself had posed in his letter of [[30 April]] [[1875]]: what, after the completion of his plan, was to be done? Pel had closed the coastal strip but had not pacified the interior; the question of how to convert the coastal closure into the submission of the Sagi federations and of [[Kemala]] was the question [[Wiggers van Kerchem]] refused to engage — "De Generaal-Majoor Wiggers van Kerchem, opvolger van Generaal Pel, kon zich met deze inzichten niet vereenigen" — and that [[Diemont]] and [[Van der Heijden]] would each answer differently. Pel's plan, in Van Heutsz's retrospective, was the strategic premise from which the next six years of Dutch policy followed; but its author died before he could supply its conclusion.

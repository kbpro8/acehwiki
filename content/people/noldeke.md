**Nöldeke** — *Professor Nöldeke*, *Theodor Nöldeke* — was the German orientalist, professor at Strasbourg, under whom [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje] studied for some months after his doctorate on [[24 November]] [[24 November]] [[1880]] on the dissertation "*Het Mekkaansche Feest*", and who later, in [[1884]], deciphered the text of the famous [[Stele of Teima|Stele of Teima]] from the drawing of Professor [[Euting|Euting]] — the decipherment that made the stele famous and that triggered the French vice-consul [[De Lostalot|De Lostalot]]'s lies against Snouck Hurgronje, leading to the latter's expulsion from [[Mekka|Mekka]] on [[5 August]] [[5 August]] [[1885]].

## The Strasbourg Studies

After his doctorate on [[24 November]] [[24 November]] [[1880]], the young doctor Snouck Hurgronje studied for some months at Strasbourg under Nöldeke — the principal German orientalist of the late 19th century, and one of the principal European Arabic scholars. The Strasbourg studies were the immediate post-doctoral phase of Snouck Hurgronje's formation; after them, he was appointed lector at the Leiden Indische Instelling for the training of East Indian officials, to teach what was then called "*godsdienstige wetten en gewoonten der mohammedanen*" (religious laws and customs of the Mohammedans).

## The Stele of Teima

In [[1883]] Professor [[Euting|Euting]] from Strasbourg and the French Dr. [[Huber|Huber]] made a scientific journey in the interior of Arabië, and were long guests of the emir [[Ibn Rasjid|Ibn Rasjid]] in his territory. They left with their host a gravestone with old-Syriac inscription — the later famous [[Stele of Teima|Stele of Teima]]. The text of the stele was, in the meantime, deciphered by Nöldeke from Euting's drawing, and the stele thereby became famous. The fame of the stele triggered the French vice-consul De Lostalot's intervention — the French government's order to De Lostalot to ensure that the goods of the murdered Huber (including the stele) should find their way to Paris — and De Lostalot's subsequent lies against Snouck Hurgronje, leading to the latter's expulsion from Mekka on [[5 August]] [[5 August]] [[1885]].

## Significance

Nöldeke is the German orientalist under whom Snouck Hurgronje studied at Strasbourg after his doctorate, and whose decipherment of the text of the Stele of Teima from Euting's drawing made the stele famous — and so, indirectly, triggered the French vice-consul De Lostalot's lies against Snouck Hurgronje and the latter's expulsion from Mekka. Nöldeke is, with [[De Goeje|De Goeje]] and [[Goldziher|Goldziher]], one of the three principal European orientalist patrons of Snouck Hurgronje's early career.

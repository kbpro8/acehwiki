**Toeankoe Hasjim** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the fierce Dutch antagonist who, after the fall of the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]] in [[January 1874]], became the guardian (*voogd*) of the boy-sultan [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Tuanku Moehamat Dawot]] and the effective head of the fugitive Atjèhnese court at [[Keumala]] in [[Pidië|Pidië]]. He is, in Kreemer's narrative, the central political figure of the early war period, and his prominence would only grow after the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|surrender of Habib Abdoerrahman]] in [[1878]].

## Descent

Kreemer notes that Toeankoe Hasjim — "de vader van den bekenden, nu nog levenden Toeankoe Radja Keumala" — "the father of the well-known, still-living Toeankoe Radja Keumala" — was "een achterkleinzoon van Soeltan Ala ad-Dïn Moehammad Sjah ([[1824]]–[[1838]])" — "a great-grandson of Sultan Ala ad-Din Mohammad Shah (1824–1838)."

## The flight to Keumala

After the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], the young Sultan — "een paar dagen na de inname van zijn verblijfplaats aan cholera bezweken was" — "a few days after the taking of his residence had succumbed to cholera" — was succeeded by [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Moehamat Dawot], who was placed under Toeankoe Hasjim's guardianship. "Nadat het vluchtende hof te Keumala in Pidië een veilige schuilplaats had gevonden, ver van het tooneel des oorlogs, werd eigenlijk de toestand van het soeltanaat vrijwel dezelfde als vóórdat de oorlog begon, alleen met verplaatsing van het tooneel, waarop het rust" — "After the fleeing court had found a safe hiding-place at Keumala in Pidië, far from the theatre of war, the condition of the sultanate became virtually the same as before the war began, only with a shifting of the stage on which it rests."

## Rise to prominence

Kreemer records that Toeankoe Hasjim "na de overgave van Habib Abdoerrahman in [[1878]] op den voorgrond zou treden" — "after the surrender of Habib Abdurrahman in 1878 would come to the foreground." The political structure of the fugitive sultanate thus centred, through the early war years, on the figure of Hasjim at Keumala, with the boy-sultan Dawot as the nominal head of state.

## Significance

Toeankoe Hasjim's role as guardian of the sultan and effective chief of the fugitive court is, in Kreemer's narrative, the institutional mechanism by which the Atjèhnese sultanate survived the loss of its capital. The removal of the court to Keumala — deep in [[Pidië|Pidië]], far from the Dutch lines around [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] — preserved the formal continuity of the sultanate through the first years of the war, and made Hasjim the principal Atjèhnese political figure with whom the Dutch would have to contend in the consolidation period ([[April 1874]]–[[July 1878]]) and beyond.

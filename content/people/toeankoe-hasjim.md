**Toeankoe Hasjim** — also written *Toeankoe Haschim* in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s spelling, *Tëngku Hasyim* — was the Acehnese royal chief and political architect of the resistance in the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] who, after the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], became the guardian (*voogd*) of the boy-sultan [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Tuanku Mohamad Daoed]] and the effective head of the fugitive Atjèhnese court at [[Keumala]] in [[Pidië|Pidië]]. He is, in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s narrative, the central political figure of the early war period — the figure who supplied the institutional mechanism by which the Atjèhnese sultanate survived the loss of its capital. He is, in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, the *zeer krachtige persoonlijkheid* (very forceful personality) at the head of the *tegenpartij* (war party) at [[Keumala]] — the power behind the throne who, in the years of Dutch *lijdelijkheid* (passivity) after the dismissal of [[General van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] in early [[1881]], masterfully exploited the time to install his pupil [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Tuanku Mohamad Daoed]] as the recognised successor to the late [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah]] (d. [[1874]]) and to use the royal installation at the [[Indrapoeri]] mosque — the *emmedji Indrapoeri* — to consolidate the resistance. He is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the political and dynastic counterpart of the religious leaders [[Tengkoe di Tirou]] and [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoel Rachman]] — the figure who supplied the constitutional form of the Atjehnese resistance by seating a sultan on the throne the Dutch had vacated.

## Descent

Kreemer notes that Toeankoe Hasjim — "de vader van den bekenden, nu nog levenden Toeankoe Radja Keumala" — "the father of the well-known, still-living [[Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala|Toeankoe Radja Keumala]]" — was "een achterkleinzoon van Soeltan Ala ad-Dïn Moehammad Sjah ([[1824]]–[[1838]])" — "a great-grandson of Sultan Ala ad-Din Mohammad Shah (1824–1838)." The genealogical placement made him a member of the former sultans' family — the kinship basis on which his later claim to manage the sultan-candidate's household at Keumala would rest.

## The flight to Keumala

After the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], the young Sultan [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah|Machmoed Sjah]] — "een paar dagen na de inname van zijn verblijfplaats aan cholera bezweken was" — "a few days after the taking of his residence had succumbed to cholera" — was succeeded by [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Tuanku Mohamad Daoed]], who was placed under Toeankoe Hasjim's guardianship. "Nadat het vluchtende hof te Keumala in Pidië een veilige schuilplaats had gevonden, ver van het tooneel des oorlogs, werd eigenlijk de toestand van het soeltanaat vrijwel dezelfde als vóórdat de oorlog begon, alleen met verplaatsing van het tooneel, waarop het rust" — "After the fleeing court had found a safe hiding-place at Keumala in Pidië, far from the theatre of war, the condition of the sultanate became virtually the same as before the war began, only with a shifting of the stage on which it rests." The removal of the court to [[Keumala]] — deep in [[Pidië|Pidië]], far from the Dutch lines around [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] — preserved the formal continuity of the sultanate through the first years of the war.

## Rise to prominence after the 1878 Habib submission

Kreemer records that Toeankoe Hasjim "na de overgave van Habib Abdoerrahman in [[1878]] op den voorgrond zou treden" — "after the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|surrender of Habib Abdurrahman]] in [[1878]] would come to the foreground." The political structure of the fugitive sultanate thus centred, through the early war years, on the figure of Hasjim at Keumala, with the boy-sultan Mohamad Daoed as the nominal head of state; and the elimination of the Habib as the active military leader of the Groot-Atjeh interior resistance in October [[1878]] left Hasjim as the principal political figure with whom the Dutch would have to contend through the consolidation period and beyond. Van Heutsz's portrait of the same period confirms and amplifies Kreemer's reading: Hasjim was not merely the guardian of the boy-sultan but the chief of the *tegenpartij* — the war party — who, in the years of Dutch *lijdelijkheid* after the [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] dismissal of early [[1881]], masterfully exploited the time to do *het tegendeel* (the opposite) of what the Dutch had done.

## The "zeer krachtige persoonlijkheid" at the head of the tegenpartij

Van Heutsz introduces Toeankoe Haschim in the context of the difficult inheritance that [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]] took over in March [[1883]] — the inheritance of the *Civiel Gouverneur* (Civil Governor) regime of [[1881]]–[[1883]] that had followed the Van der Heijden dismissal. The political obstacle Laging Tobias faced was Haschim's consolidated position at [[Keumala]]:

> "vooral omdat Toeankoe Haschim aan het eene zeer krachtige persoonlijkheid hoofd der tegenpartij stond, die den tijd van onze lijdelijkheid, tweespalt en onterredenheid meesterlijk benut had om het tegendeel te doen van hetgeen bij ons geschiedde en in schijn zijn pupil, den jeugdigen Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed, op den voorgrond plaatsende, dien hij met behulp van andere voorname hoofden al vroeger als de wettige opvolger van den in 1874 overleden sultan Machmoed Sjah had weten te doen erkennen, zelf van Kemala uit overal bevelen zond, als komende van het eenig wettige gezag, van den sultan".

The portrait is precise. Haschim is the chief of the *tegenpartij* — the war party — seated at [[Keumala]], the inland seat of the resistance. He had used the years of Dutch *lijdelijkheid* (passivity), *tweespalt* (division), and *onterredenheid* (irresolution) to do *het tegendeel* of what the Dutch had done: where the Dutch had drifted, Haschim had consolidated; where the Dutch had changed governors and policies, Haschim had built a single constitutional authority. He had placed his pupil [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Tuanku Mohamad Daoed]] — *in schijn* (in appearance) — at the front, having "with the help of other prominent chiefs" secured the pupil's early recognition as the lawful successor to [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah]] (who had died in [[1874]]), and he himself, from [[Keumala]], "zond overal bevelen, als komende van het eenig wettige gezag, van den sultan" — sent orders everywhere, as coming from the only lawful authority, from the sultan.

## The manipulation of Tuanku Mohamad Daoed

The arrangement was that of a regent and his pupil. Tuanku Mohamad Daoed was the *jeugdige* (youthful) pupil whom Haschim had placed *in schijn* at the front: the appearance of authority was the pupil's; the substance of authority was the regent's. The recognition of Mohamad Daoed as the lawful successor to Sultan Machmoed Sjah had been secured "met behulp van andere voorname hoofden" — with the help of other prominent chiefs — and the recognition had been obtained *al vroeger* (already early), i.e., before the Dutch *Civiel Gouverneur* regime of [[1881]]–[[1883]] had had time to register the political situation. The Dutch governorship of [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] thus inherited, in March [[1883]], an Atjehnese constitutional settlement that had been made without Dutch participation: a sultan-candidate recognised by the chiefs, seated at [[Keumala]], and issuing orders in the sultan's name through the agency of his regent.

## The emmedji Indrapoeri installation

The constitutional settlement was completed, after Laging Tobias's failed overtures of June and October [[1883]], by the formal installation of Mohamad Daoed as sultan in the [[Indrapoeri]] mosque — the *emmedji Indrapoeri*, the great mosque of [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]] — and by the establishment of the sultan's household at [[Keumala]] "voor zoover dat mogelijk was naar de aloude gebruiken" — as far as that was possible according to the ancient customs. Van Heutsz records the installation and its strategic purpose:

> "in weerwil, dat het voornaamste hoofd van de tegenpartij Toeankoe Haschim door de [meerderjarigheidverklaring] van Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed en diens uitroeping en huldiging in den [emmedji] Indrapoeri tot sultan van het rijk, gevolgd door de inrichting van des sultans huis te Kemala … voortdurend meer eenheid en samenhang in het Inlandsche bestuur des lands zocht te brengen".

The installation had two strategic effects. The first was the constitutional unification of the Atjehnese resistance under a single sultan-candidate recognised by the war party — a sultan whose household was established at [[Keumala]], the seat of the resistance, and whose authority was consecrated by the *emmedji Indrapoeri*, the great mosque at which the sultans of Atjeh had been installed for centuries. The second was the consolidation of the *Inlandsche bestuur* — the indigenous administration — of the country: Haschim *zocht voortdurend meer eenheid en samenhang te brengen* — sought continually to bring more unity and coherence — to the indigenous administration of the country. The Dutch *lijdelijkheid* of [[1881]]–[[1883]] had been turned, by Haschim's agency, into an Atjehnese constitutional consolidation.

## Dispatch of orders from Keumala as if from the only lawful authority

The operational expression of the consolidated authority was the dispatch of orders from [[Keumala]] "als komende van het eenig wettige gezag, van den sultan" — as coming from the only lawful authority, from the sultan. The formula is Van Heutsz's: the orders were issued in the sultan's name, by the regent, and were received by the chiefs of the [[Sagi-(atjeh)|Sagi]] (federation) federations and the [[Onderhoorigheden]] (Dependencies) as the orders of the only lawful authority in the country. The Dutch governorship in [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]] was, by this device, reduced to one of two competing authorities in the country — and the Atjehnese authority, seated at [[Keumala]] and consecrated by the *emmedji Indrapoeri*, had the political and dynastic form the Dutch authority lacked. The arrangement would persist, in Van Heutsz's narrative, until the [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] governorship of [[1886]]–[[1891]] began to dismantle the Keumala settlement by the closure of the Noord- en Westkust; and it would be finally broken only by the campaigns Van Heutsz himself would lead, as Governor of Atjeh after [[1898]], against the inland seat of the resistance.

## Significance

Toeankoe Hasjim's significance in the Aceh Wiki History record rests on three things: he was the *zeer krachtige persoonlijkheid* — the very forceful personality — at the head of the *tegenpartij* at [[Keumala]], the political chief of the Atjehnese war party and the principal political obstacle to the policies of [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]] in [[1883]]–[[1884]]; he was the *regent* who, in the years of Dutch *lijdelijkheid* after the [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] dismissal of early [[1881]], manipulated his pupil [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|Tuanku Mohamad Daoed]] — placing him *in schijn* at the front, having him recognised as the lawful successor to [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah]] (d. [[1874]]) "met behulp van andere voorname hoofden", and dispatching orders from Keumala "als komende van het eenig wettige gezag, van den sultan"; and he was the constitutional architect of the *emmedji Indrapoeri* installation — the *meerderjarigheidverklaring* (declaration of majority) and *uitroeping en huldiging* (proclamation and homage) of Mohamad Daoed as sultan in the great mosque of [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]], followed by the establishment of the sultan's household at [[Keumala]] "voor zoover dat mogelijk was naar de aloude gebruiken". The installation was the constitutional settlement the Dutch *Civiel Gouverneur* regime of [[1881]]–[[1883]] had not been able to prevent, and its effect was, in Van Heutsz's reading, to give the Atjehnese resistance "voortdurend meer eenheid en samenhang in het Inlandsche bestuur des lands" — continually more unity and coherence in the indigenous administration of the country. In Kreemer's earlier narrative, Hasjim is the institutional mechanism by which the Atjèhnese sultanate survived the loss of its capital: the removal of the court to Keumala — deep in [[Pidië|Pidië]], far from the Dutch lines around [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] — preserved the formal continuity of the sultanate through the first years of the war, and made Hasjim the principal Atjèhnese political figure with whom the Dutch would have to contend in the consolidation period ([[April 1874]]–[[July 1878]]) and beyond. Hasjim's role in the resistance is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the political and dynastic counterpart to the religious authority of [[Tengkoe di Tirou]] and the active military leadership of [[Habib Abdurrahman Zahir|Habib Abdoel Rachman]]: where they supplied the religious sanction and the Groot-Atjeh command, Hasjim supplied the constitutional form.

**W. van Goltstein** was a Dutch colonial minister who served two terms ([[1874]]-[[1876]] and [[1879]]-[[1882]]) during the administration of Governor-General [[J.W. van Lansberge]], playing a crucial role in shaping colonial policy during the later years of the [[Aceh War]] and the transition to civil governance in [[Aceh]]. Born in [[1831]], he was a key figure in colonial affairs during a period of significant reform and financial strain.

## Early Career

Van Goltstein began his career at the Department of Colonial Affairs in [[1855]], later serving in the [[Second Chamber]] ([[1864]]-[[1871]]) and the [[First Chamber]] ([[1872]]-[[1874]]). He was involved in the founding of the society for natural research in the Dutch colonies and later held various prestigious positions, including ambassador to [[London]] ([[1894]]-[[1899]]).

## First Ministry ([[1874]]-[[1876]])

Van Goltstein was responsible for Van Lansberge's appointment as Governor-General in [[1875]]. He was initially cautious regarding the [[Aceh]] policy, expressing concern about the costs and advocating for a more measured approach. However, he supported Van Lansberge's initiatives, including the [[1875]] visit to [[Aceh]], though he warned the Governor-General against excessive optimism about a quick end to the war.

During this period, Van Goltstein:
- Supported the introduction of patent and personal taxes on Europeans.
- Engaged in debates about the poenale sanctie (penal sanctions) for labor contracts, initially supporting some restrictions but later becoming more cautious.
- Oversaw the early stages of the [[Tanjung Priok]] harbor project.
- Worked to improve the quality of judicial and financial officials in the Indies.

His first term ended in [[September]] [[1876]] when he resigned following the rejection of military law amendments.

## Interregnum ([[1877]]-[[1879]])

After his resignation, Van Goltstein remained active in colonial affairs, writing newspaper articles about [[Aceh]] that drew criticism from General [[J. van Swieten]]. He was consulted on various matters and maintained a private correspondence with Van Lansberge.

## Second Ministry ([[1879]]-[[1882]])

Van Goltstein returned to office in [[August]] [[1879]] as part of the conservative [[Van Lynden van Sandenburg]] cabinet. His second term was characterized by:

### Aceh Policy

Van Goltstein was more cautious than Van Lansberge about the transition to civil governance in [[Aceh]]. He advised against haste, stating that "it is better to bear the high costs a little longer than to have to start anew later." He was involved in the delicate negotiations surrounding General [[K. van der Heijden]]'s replacement, working to manage the King's preference for Van der Heijden while supporting Van Lansberge's preference for civil governance.

He raised concerns about the pace of change, noting that "our influence on the Acehnese coast is evidently not yet great" and warning that "premature" decisions could lead to renewed conflict.

### Financial Policy

Van Goltstein introduced a new financial arrangement between the Netherlands and the Indies, proposing:
- A fixed contribution of 2 million guilders
- A variable contribution of up to 6 million guilders (half of the surplus)
- Surpluses would be used exclusively for Indian needs

His proposal was ultimately rejected by the [[Second Chamber]], though he remained in office to handle other pressing matters.

### The Conversion Question

Van Goltstein was a cautious reformer on the issue of converting communal land tenure to individual ownership on [[Java]]. He resisted pressure from liberal parliamentarians for rapid conversion, arguing that:
- The Javanese people were not yet ready for such radical change.
- Conversion should occur gradually and only when desired by the population.
- It was not the government's role to impose European concepts of property on Javanese society.
- The "sawah melkers" (land speculators) would exploit the population if conversion was forced.

His position led to the "Kabinetscirculaire" of [[15 August]] [[1880]], which was criticized by liberal members of the [[Second Chamber]] and contributed to his eventual resignation.

### The Corvée (Heerendiensten) Question

Van Goltstein opposed the wholesale abolition of the pantjens (personal services to indigenous chiefs), arguing that:
- These services were a "bond" between the population and the aristocracy.
- The Javanese social order was not yet ready for the removal of traditional bonds.
- Abolition should be gradual, starting with minor chiefs and some services.
- A uniform head tax of 1 guilder for all service-liable individuals was "highly unfair" as it did not account for varying burdens.

### Administrative Policy

Van Goltstein supported the improvement of the civil service through:
- Sending qualified specialists from the Netherlands.
- Encouraging the study of languages other than [[Javanese]].
- Maintaining the principle of hereditary succession for regents.
- Avoiding unnecessary disruption of the indigenous social order.

## Resignation and Legacy

Van Goltstein's resignation in [[1882]] was prompted by parliamentary criticism of his conversion policy. The [[Second Chamber]] adopted a resolution criticizing the [[15 August]] [[1880]] circular, and Van Goltstein, seeing this as a "reprimand in the most unambiguous terms," offered his resignation. When the cabinet fell and he was asked to return, he declined, and [[J. de Brauw]] succeeded him.

His legacy lies in his cautious, gradual approach to reform, his defense of the indigenous social order, and his efforts to balance Dutch and Indian financial interests. He is remembered as a conservative administrator who prioritized stability and continuity over rapid change.

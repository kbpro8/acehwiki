**Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta** — also written *T. Hoesin Lhongbatta*, *T. Hoesin Long Bata*, *Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata*, *T. Hoesin Longbattah*, *Lehong Bata*, *Lhongbatta* — was an [[Achehnese]] *hoeloebalang* and band leader of [[Lam-Goet|Lam Goet]] whose defection to [[Teuku Umar]] "early" in the spring of [[1896]] brought with it the "doubtful-minded regions" of [[III Moekims Lamrebo|3 Moekims Lamrebo]], [[III Moekims Ateuh|4 Moekims Ateuh]] of the sagi of [[XXVI Moekims]], and the moekims [[Hoho]] and [[Lam-Djampoe]] of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] (sagi XXII Moekims), as recorded in A. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*. He was seriously wounded in the neck on [[21 April]] [[1896]], his band at [[Lam-Goet|Lam Goet]] was the target of the [[tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of 14 April 1896]], and he was confirmed killed on [[4 June]] [[1896]] during the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|IV/VI Moekims operation]] when a *maréchaussée* patrol under the *inlandsch korporaal* [[Korporaal Brodjo|Brodjo]] engaged approximately 30 of his men near [[Beradin]]. His trajectory — from early defector, to wounded fugitive, to dead leader — is one of the most fully documented Achehnese military biographies in the first semester of [[1896]].

## Defection and the Bringing-Over of the Doubtful-Minded Regions

Lhongbatta's defection was, alongside [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|T. Tjoet Toengkoeb's]], one of the most consequential early-defector events of the spring of [[1896]] because of the breadth of the regions he brought with him. The "[[III Moekims Lamrebo|3 Moekims Lamrebo]]" and "[[III Moekims Ateuh|4 Moekims Ateuh]] of the sagi of [[XXVI Moekims]]" represented a significant slice of the eastern sagi, while the moekims [[Hoho]] and [[Lam-Djampoe]] of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] gave the resistance a foothold in the southern sector as well. Kruisheer's phrase "doubtful-minded regions" indicates that these territories had been wavering before Lhongbatta's defection and that his personal decision tipped them into open resistance. The defection immediately triggered a Dutch military response: the [[tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Goet on 14 April 1896]] — under [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] with two companies of the [[6e Bataljon]] — was explicitly aimed at Lhongbatta's residence, and Kruisheer notes that his "*bende*" (band) at Lam Goet was the target. The expedition stormed the fortification, destroyed an iron 9 cm cannon with dynamite, and pursued the fleeing enemy to [[Lam Trieng]].

## Wounding of 21 April 1896 and Death on 4 June 1896

Lhongbatta was "seriously wounded in the neck on [[21 April]] [[1896]]" in the immediate aftermath of the [[20 April]] [[1896]] multi-column Dutch operation — a wound which, given the medical resources available to the Achehnese, would have substantially reduced his capacity for active command in the following weeks. By the end of May [[1896]] Kruisheer reports that Lhongbatta was "with the most of his followers at Oemar" and "would die within days", a forecast confirmed by the events of [[4 June]] [[1896]]. On that day, during the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|IV/VI Moekims operation]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] — a two-colonne operation which included 12 brigades *maréchaussée* under [[Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland|Kapitein Graafland]], the [[3e Bataljon]], [[6e Bataljon]], [[9e Bataljon]] and two companies of the [[14e Bataljon]] — the *spits* under the *inlandsch korporaal* [[Korporaal Brodjo|Brodjo]] (No. 25645) encountered approximately 30 of Lhongbatta's men at a distance of 50 metres near [[Beradin]]. Brodjo "ordered his men to kneel and fire salvoes, then attacked"; both *voorste brigades* supported, and the band fled leaving two dead and abandoning *achterlaadgeweren*. "Later learned [that] one of [the] dead was Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata" — the confirmation that Lhongbatta had finally succumbed, not to his [[21 April]] [[1896]] neck wound but to the *maréchaussée* engagement on [[4 June]] [[1896]].

## Significance

Lhongbatta's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his role as the *bendehoofd* whose personal defection brought the largest single bloc of "doubtful-minded regions" into the Achehnese coalition in the spring of [[1896]], and whose death on [[4 June]] [[1896]] — at the hands of an indigenous *korporaal* of the *maréchaussée* — represented one of the most important Dutch tactical successes of the IV/VI Moekims operation. His trajectory also illustrates the liprovided that of Achehnese medical care for wounded commanders: although the [[21 April]] [[1896]] neck wound did not directly kill him, it must have substantially reduced his operational capacity in the seven weeks between the wounding and his death.

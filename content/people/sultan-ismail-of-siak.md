**Sultan Ismail of Siak** — also written *R. Ismail*, *Radja Ismail* — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the Siak sultan who, having been defeated and driven out to [[Pelalawan|Pelalawan]] in [[1761]], succeeded in restoring himself to the Siak throne in [[1779]], and who, together with his successor [[Sultan Said Ali|Sultan Said Ali]], imposed the Siak overlordship over the East Coast statelets that had shaken off the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] yoke in [[1769]]. Ismail's restoration in [[1779]] is, in the Bureau's framing, the political event that inaugurated the Siak sultanate's brief late-eighteenth-century zenith.

## Background: the Siak-Dutch alliance and the overthrow of R. Ismail (1761)

The Bureau's account of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]]'s defeat and restoration opens with the long Siak-Dutch alliance of the mid-eighteenth century. The Bureau records that the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]] had been destroyed by [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the early sixteenth century, that [[Djohor|Djohore]] had then established itself at the mouth of the [[Siak|Siak River]], and that [[Radja Ketjil|Radja Ketjil]] had driven [[Djohor|Djohore]] out of [[Siak|Siak]] in the early eighteenth century. After [[Radja Ketjil|Radja Ketjil]]'s secession, the [[Siak|Sultanate of Siak]] rapidly grew in power, and the Dutch East India Company (the *Oost-Indische Compagnie*) established a post at [[Poelau Gontong|Poelau Gontong]] on the [[Siak|Siak River]] in [[1755]], in return for supporting [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] against his brother [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]] in their disputes over the Siak succession.

The "afloopen van die post in [[1759]]" — "the running-out [i.e. the destruction] of that post in 1759" — had, the Bureau records, the consequence that the Compagnie supported [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]], who had earlier been driven from the throne with the Compagnie's help. [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|R. Ismail]] — the successor of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Moehamad]] (who had died in [[1760]]) — was, in [[1761]], defeated and driven out to [[Pelalawan|Pelalawan]], which was then, "eveneens met behulp der Compagnie" — "also with the help of the Compagnie" — overwhelmed by [[Raja Alam|Raja Alam]].

## The restoration of 1779

[[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]]'s restoration in [[1779]] was, the Bureau records, the political event that turned the long Siak-Dutch alliance of the mid-eighteenth century to Ismail's advantage. "Doch vooral gedurende het bestuur van Sultan Said Ali, die in het einde der achttiende eeuw weder Ismail's opvolger had verdrongen, breidde Siak zijn heerschappij over zijne nabuurstaten uit, tot in de Noordelijke kustlandschappen toe" — "But especially during the administration of Sultan Said Ali, who in the end of the eighteenth century had again driven out Ismail's successor, Siak extended its rule over its neighbouring states, even into the Northern coastal lands." The Bureau's framing of the late-eighteenth-century Siak expansion thus places [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]]'s [[1779]] restoration as the prelude to [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]'s East Coast campaigns: it was Ismail who re-established the Siak throne, and Said Ali who, having displaced Ismail's successor, used the restored throne as the basis for the imposition of Siak overlordship over the East Coast statelets.

## The imposition of Siak overlordship over the East Coast

The Bureau records that [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]] operated together in the imposition of Siak overlordship over the East Coast statelets. The East Coast statelets — having "knew themselves again to be free" of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] overlordship in [[1769]] — had "slechts korten tijd genoten zij hunne onafhankelijkheid; weinige jaren later reeds werden zij door het wapengeweld van Ismail en Said Ali gedwongen Siak als opperheer te erkennen" — "enjoyed their independence only a short time; a few years later they were already forced, by the armed force of Ismail and Said Ali, to acknowledge Siak as overlord." The Siak overlordship thus imposed was, in the Bureau's account, a sustained act of armed coercion by the two sultans acting in concert.

## Significance

[[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the restorer of the Siak throne and the co-author (with [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]) of the Siak overlordship of the East Coast. His [[1779]] restoration inaugurates the brief late-eighteenth-century Siak zenith, and his joint campaigns with [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]] impose the Siak overlordship that would — after the long decay of Siak power following [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]'s death in [[1821]] — be succeeded by the [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship]].

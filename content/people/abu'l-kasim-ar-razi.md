**Abū'l-Qāsim ar-Rāzī** was a Muslim mystic who died in 378 H / 988 C.E. He is named by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition of the two Malay treatises of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] as the authority on which the *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] — one of the principal sources of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] — draws for the seven additional sects that the tract adds, in some manuscripts, to the seventy-two Islamic sects.

## The seven additional sects

The *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] — the schematic overview of the seventy-two Islamic sects that is one of the principal sources of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri — adds, in some manuscripts, seven further sects drawn from a work of Abū'l-Qāsim ar-Rāzī. The seven sects are: the *Dahriyya*, the *Hanābila*, the *Ibāḥiyya*, the *Bāṭiniyya*, the *Barāhima*, the *Ashʿariyya*, and (the one explicitly cited by Raniri in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]]) the *Karrāmiyya*. The seven sects are also found in a small Arabic treatise of three pages in which ar-Rāzī is named as author (London, India Office Library, *Levy* 1777).

## The *Karrāmiyya* in the *Tibjān*

The first of the seven sects derived from ar-Rāzī — the *Karrāmiyya* — is cited by Raniri in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] (p. 24 of the Voorhoeve facsimile). The doctrines that Raniri records for the *Karrāmiyya* are the same as those recorded in the Moluccan manuscript of Leiden Cod. Or. 1255; they are also found in the other manuscripts of the *Maʿrifat al-madhāhib* and in the small Arabic treatise of ar-Rāzī himself. The doctrines are: the throne is the place of God; man dies of himself, not by the angel of death; the ladder by which Muḥammad ascended to heaven was of gold, silver, and precious stones; a *wali* (saint) stands above a prophet; the Quran is created.

## Significance

Abū'l-Qāsim ar-Rāzī is the authority on which the *[[Ma'rifat al-madhahib|Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*]] draws for the seven additional sects that the tract adds to the seventy-two Islamic sects. The transmission of these sects from ar-Rāzī's work, through the *Maʿrifat al-madhāhib*, to the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri — and so to the Malay-Indonesian world — is one of the channels by which the scholastic classification of Muslim heresies was carried to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago.

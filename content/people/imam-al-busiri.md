**Imam al‑Busiri** (d. 1249) was a 13th‑century Egyptian Sufi poet best known for composing the *Burda* (the “Poem of the Mantle”), a celebrated ode to the Prophet [[Muhammad]] (see [[PAGE 37]]). The poem became a central object of veneration and later a target of Wahhabi criticism.

## Composition of the Burda
Al‑Busiri wrote the *Burda* in Arabic, praising the Prophet’s virtues, miracles, and the battles of [[Hunain]], [[Badr]], and [[Uhud]]. The poem’s verses are structured in multiple sections, each extolling different aspects of the Prophet’s life.

## Reception and Controversy
While the *Burda* was widely recited across the Islamic world, Wahhabi scholars in the 18th‑19th centuries denounced it as *shirk*. Anti‑Wahhabi scholars such as [[Dā'ūd b. Sulaimān al‑Baghdadi]] defended its legitimacy, a debate reflected in the 16th‑century [[Malay translation of the Burda]].

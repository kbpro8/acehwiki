**T. Béntara Titeuë** was the *[[oelèëbalang]]* of the [[II Moekims Titeuë]] in [[Pidië]] and a *landschapsrechter* (territorial judge) of the [[Landschapsgerecht]] at [[Sigli]], who on [[10 October]] [[1913]] stabbed the presiding Civil Authority, overste [[W. B. J. A. Scheepens]], during a sitting of the *[[Moesapat]]*, and was cut down on the spot by an Acehnese police servant.

## The occasion

In October [[1913]] the *oelèëbalang*'s son, [[Teukoe Béntara Titeuë|the young Teukoe]], walking through the *[[gampong]]* with a few followers, grew thirsty and ordered one of them to climb a coconut palm on an Acehnese's yard and pick a few coconuts. The owner, angered that no permission had been asked, refused; the young Teukoe grew angry in turn, holding that the picking was the *oelèëbalang*'s right; the owner threatened him with his *[[parang]]*; the Teukoe held to his order; and the owner brought the *parang* down on his skull, causing a bleeding head wound. The perpetrator was arrested and brought to Sigli for trial before the *Moesapat* ("customary-law court").

## The sitting of the Moesapat

On the day of the trial T. Béntara Titeuë went to Sigli. At the entrance to the civil office the armed police servant on sentry duty took from him his visibly worn, gold-ornamented *[[rentjong]]*.

The *adjunct-djaksa* demanded the maximum penalty for slight wounding known to Dutch criminal law, three months' imprisonment. One of the judging *oelèëbalangs* asked the Muhammadan adviser what penalty he considered fitting; the Teungkoe answered according to Acehnese *[[adat]]*: *"Loeka oelón sipat, darah oelón soekat, lhò' oelón takat, diët lhëë ploh reunggèt"* — the blood-price is thirty dollars.

Hearing this, T. Béntara Titeuë sprang up in fury and abused the Teungkoe as *"[[Kaphé]]"*: "You know very well that according to our customary law I have the right not only to kill the man himself who shed my son's blood, but even his whole family." He told the President that he understood Acehnese *adat* could no longer be applied to the perpetrator, "but that he, T. Béntara, if the perpetrator were not punished with at least banishment to [[Java]], would be [[maloe|shamed]] in the eyes of his people."

In vain the Civil Authority explained that his son, by improper conduct, was the principal culprit, and that Dutch law did not permit banishment for a slight wound. The *oelèëbalang* persisted, and when the Moesapat pronounced three months, "T. Béntara Titeuë suddenly drew a concealed knife from beneath his clothes and stabbed Colonel Scheepens in the belly."

The *adjunct-djaksa* rushed forward with only a wooden ruler; then an armed police servant, an Acehnese, drew his *[[klewang]]* and struck the *oelèëbalang* over the head. "The territorial chief fell down and died almost at once. Colonel Scheepens, having got to his feet, forbade any harm to be done to the chief, but it was already too late."

Scheepens died of his wound on [[17 October]] [[1913]].

## Aftermath

The monthly report closed: "Peace and order in the territory of [[Titeuë]] were not disturbed for a moment by what had occurred."

## Significance

[[Maurice Henri du Croo|Du Croo]] printed the case in full "because it clearly illuminates how even an Acehnese territorial chief is brought to such a murder" — the collision between the *adat* right of blood-vengeance and the Dutch penal maximum for slight wounding, mediated by the sense of *maloe*.

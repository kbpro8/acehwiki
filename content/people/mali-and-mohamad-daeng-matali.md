**Mali** — native artillery-rider 2nd class (*Inlandsche stukrijder der 2e klasse*, pay-list no. 70412) — and **Mohamad Daëng Matali** — a *dwangarbeider* (forced-labourer) — were two [[Bugis]] volunteers from the besieged column at [[Kajoe-Lē]] who, disguised as paddy cutters, slipped through the enemy on the morning of [[6 March]] [[1876]] and carried Major [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|B. E. Mekern]]'s report to the chief of staff at [[Kota Radja]] — the act that set in motion the [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|relief operation]] of the following day.

## The exploit

After the [[Ambush of the Buijs detachment (4 March 1876)|ambush of 4 March]] the column at Kajoe-Lē was isolated; an armed escort could only provoke a second attack, so the report had to travel by creeping patrol, and the two men volunteered. At three in the morning of 6 March they left the post, thoroughly disguised and armed only with the machete, creeping forward, stooping, pretending to cut paddy. Too many Atjehers stood on the sawah outside the post, and they had to return; at half past four they tried again, and this time slipped through unnoticed while the enemy carried the harvested paddy to safety in greater dispersion over the fields. About two hours later they reached the chief of the staff, Lieutenant-Colonel of the General Staff [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]], and handed over Mekern's telegraphic-style report of the situation of his column.

Help marched from among the bivouacks that same evening. Meijer names the two Bugis — by name, function and pay-list number — as the rescuers of the column, in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]).

**Van Braam (commander)** was a senior officer of the [[Oost-Indische Compagnie|Dutch East India Company]] who led the 1784 expedition against [[Salangore]] and later directed operations in [[Riouw]] during the same year. His actions, recorded in the *Journaal van Van Braam*, show the Dutch effort to impose control over the western coast of Sumatra and the surrounding archipelago.

## Military Career and 1784 Expedition
Van Braam organised a fleet that departed from [[Malakka]] in early July [[1784]] and arrived at [[Salangore]] on [[20 July]] [[1784]]. He ordered a landing on [[18 June]] [[1784]] (the date of the initial assault) and later oversaw the capture of the fort on the east point of the island of [[Mars]] (Pulau Mars). After a series of engagements, including the death of the local leader [[Radya Hadgè]], Van Braam declared the local ruler deposed and installed the [[Mohamed Ali]] of the Malay princely line as a client ruler.

## Later Operations and Legacy
Following the Salangore campaign, Van Braam turned his attention to [[Riouw]], issuing an ultimatum to [[Radya Aly]] on [[28 October]] [[1784]] and eventually concluding a peace treaty with the young prince [[Mahmoed]] on [[31 October]] [[1784]]. He returned to the Netherlands with his squadron on [[28 June]] [[1786]], where he received a monetary reward and was later appointed [[schout-bijnaacht|schout-bijnaacht]] in [[1788]].

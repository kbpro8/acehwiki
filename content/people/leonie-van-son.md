**Leonie van Son** (born [[Palembang|Palembang]] (Sumatra), July [[1877]]; died [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] (Atjeh, Sumatra), [[17 November]] [[1904]]) was a Dutch colonial woman of the [[Van Son genealogy|Van Son family]] — daughter of [[Livinus Diederik Mattheus van Son|L. D. M. van Son]], retired captain of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|K.N.I.L.]], and Carolina Weynschenk — who, as the first wife of [[Otto Louis Brewer|Otto Louis Brewer]], titular major-general of artillery, K.N.I.L., lived with her husband in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in the early years of the twentieth century and died at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in [[1904]], in the immediate aftermath of the formal close of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Her death at Koeta Radja, recorded in the [[Van Son genealogy|family genealogy]] with the explicit topographic gloss "*Koeta Radja (Atjeh, Sumatra)*," makes her one of the rare documented Dutch colonial wives of the immediate post-war Atjeh garrison-town.

## Birth and family

Leonie was born at [[Palembang|Palembang]] on Sumatra in July [[1877]], the second child of [[Livinus Diederik Mattheus van Son|Livinus Diederik Mattheus van Son]] and Carolina Weynschenk. At the time of her birth, her father — a veteran of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first]] and [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second]] Aceh expeditions — was serving as an infantry officer in the residency of [[Palembang|Palembang]]. She was thus born into a colonial military family whose Atjeh connections were already a generation old.

## Marriage (1899)

Leonie married at [[Salatiga|Salatiga]] (Java) on [[26 June]] [[1899]] [[Otto Louis Brewer|Otto Louis Brewer]], then a young artillery officer of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|K.N.I.L.]], son of Adriaan Anton Wilhelm Brewer, Consul of Spain at [[Batavia|Batavia]], and Wilhelmina Sarah Christina Neuwenhuyzen. The marriage joined the Van Son military family to the colonial patriciate of the Brewers of Batavia.

## Children and the Atjeh years

Of Leonie's marriage to [[Otto Louis Brewer|Otto Louis Brewer]] two children are documented in the [[Van Son genealogy|family genealogy]]:

1. **[[Maximilaan Diederik Brewer|Maximilaan Diederik Brewer]]** — born at [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]] (Java) on [[18 February]] [[1901]].
2. **[[Yvonne Caroline Brewer|Yvonne Caroline Brewer]]** — born at [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] (groot Atjeh) on [[1 Augustus]] [[1904]].

The places of birth of the two children fix the Brewer household's movements: [[Djokjakarta|Djocjacarta]] in [[1901]], [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1904]]. The posting to [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] — a place within the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] heartland of the sultanate — must have occurred between these dates, in the years immediately preceding the formal close of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[1903]] and the subsequent transition from military to civil administration in [[1904]]. Leonie's residence at Indrapoera and Koeta Radja in [[1904]] makes her one of the few documented Dutch wives of the early civil-administration period of Groot-Atjeh.

## Death at Koeta Radja (17 November 1904)

The [[Van Son genealogy|family genealogy]] records Leonie's death with the precise topographic identification: "*overleden te Koeta Radja (Atjeh, Sumatra) 17 November 1904*" — "died at Koeta Radja (Atjeh, Sumatra) 17 November 1904." She was twenty-seven years old. Her daughter [[Yvonne Caroline Brewer|Yvonne]] was three and a half months old at the time of her mother's death. The cause of death is not recorded.

The death at Koeta Radja, in the same year as the birth at Indrapoera, fixes the Brewer household in the [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] theatre of the colonial occupation in the middle of the first decade of the twentieth century — the very moment of the formal transition from military to civil administration in [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. The early colonial garrison-town of Koeta Radja, in this period, was a place of high European mortality — from "Atjeh fever" (*Atjeh-koorts*), cholera, and the residual hazards of an incompletely pacified countryside.

## Aftermath and the second marriage of her husband

After Leonie's death, [[Otto Louis Brewer|Otto Louis Brewer]] married, at ['s-Gravenhage|'s-Gravenhage]] on [[27 October]] [[1908]], her younger sister [[Adeline van Son|Adeline van Son]] (born [[Palembang|Palembang]], [[10 October]] [[1878]]) — a marriage within the prohibited degrees. The Van Son genealogy records no children of this second marriage.

## Significance

Leonie van Son's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history lies principally in the documentary fact of her death and her daughter's birth in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1904]]. The combined record — a daughter born at [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] in August, a mother dead at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] in November — fixes a Dutch colonial family in the Atjeh garrison-town in the exact year of the transition from military to civil administration, and it documents the continuing European mortality of the early colonial town. Her case is, in microcosm, a document of the colonial-family dimension of the Atjeh occupation: the wife of a serving K.N.I.L. artillery officer, dead at twenty-seven in the colonial seat of a province her father-in-law had helped to conquer thirty years earlier.

**Admiraal Fabius** was a Dutch admiral and member of the States General who, in the secret sessions of April [[1874]] on the Atjeh war, opposed the [[Annexation proclamation of Atjeh (1874)|annexation of Groot-Atjeh]] with words that events, in [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]]'s words of 1886, proved nearly prophetic. Fabius declared on that day "als het ware te voorzien" (as it were to foresee) what ten years later, in the [[Nisero affair (1883–1885)|Tenom affair]], must be the necessary consequence of annexation: he expressed the fear that the annexation would have "de treurigste en ellendigste gevolgen" (the saddest and most miserable consequences), and asked what would happen if English planters were murdered in Atjeh or their possessions burnt by hostile Atjehers — whether an astonishing outcry would not rise and England would not hold the Netherlands responsible; and if the Dutch proved unable to protect or avenge English subjects, whether England would not say: "You cannot do this, and so we shall do it ourselves? Will such not be the consequence of the annexation?" The member [[C. van Nispen van Sevenaar|van Nispen van Sevenaar]] confirmed in the same session that the Netherlands was resolved to hold what it had once taken from Atjeh, and that the wish and object was indeed extension of authority.

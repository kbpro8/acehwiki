**Van Aken** was the Dutch Governor of [[Atjeh]] whom [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] called "the most excellent administrator after Swart" and "a figure likewise generally esteemed and beloved".

Du Croo's sixth summary proposition names Van Aken as the best example of a successor formed in [[H. N. A. Swart]]'s administrative school: "Both Swart's period of government and that of Van Aken have undoubtedly been the most fruitful for Atjeh; unfortunately that of Van Aken — he was appointed a member of the [[Raad van Nederlandsch-Indië|Council of the Netherlands Indies]] and died shortly afterwards — was of relatively short, too short a duration." At the handover of the government to Resident [[J. Jongejans|Jongejans]] on Saturday [[7 March]] [[1936]] Van Aken declared the sub-divisions [[Alaslanden]], [[Lam Meulò]], [[Meulaboh]] and [[Tapa' Toean]] "made entirely ripe … for civil administration", and that only considerations of economy had restrained him from proposing it — the passage with which du Croo closed his book, calling it "the keystone of the pacification."

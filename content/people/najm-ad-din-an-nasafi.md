**Najm ad-Dīn ʿUmar an-Nasafī** was a Sufi author of the late sixth century of the Hegira (twelfth century C.E.), named by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] as the source of the section on the heretical Ṣūfī sects in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]]. His small tract *Kitāb fī bayān madhāhib al-mutaṣawwifa* ("Book on the Exposition of the Doctrines of the Sufis") supplies the first eleven of the thirteen heretical Ṣūfī sects that Raniri treats in the same order, as well as the Persian verse of the *Wākiʿiyya*.

## The *Kitāb fī bayān madhāhib al-mutaṣawwifa*

The *Kitāb fī bayān madhāhib al-mutaṣawwifa* is a small tract of seven folios preserved in a manuscript at Rome — Vatican, Borg. Arab. 254 f. 56. In it the first eleven of the thirteen heretical Ṣūfī sects that Raniri treats in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] are set out in the same order as in Raniri's treatise. The Persian verse of the *Wākiʿiyya* — quoted in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] — is also found in this same tract. Voorhoeve received a photocopy of the manuscript through the intermediation of Professor Levi della Vida and the good offices of S. A. Bonebakker.

## Source of the *Tibjān*

For the section of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] in which he treats the quasi-Ṣūfī sects, Raniri names his sources himself (*Tibjān* p. 86). The principal of these is the small tract of Najm ad-Dīn an-Nasafī. The first eleven of the thirteen heretical Ṣūfī sects that Raniri treats — the *Ḥubbiyya*, the *Awliyāʾiyya*, the *Shamrakhiyya*, the *Ibāḥiyya*, the *Ḥāliyya*, the *Hūriyya*, the *Wākiʿiyya*, the *Mutajāhiliyya*, the *Mutakāsiliyya*, the *Ibhāmiyya*, and the *Ḥulūliyya* — are taken, with some additions from other sources, from an-Nasafī's tract, in the same order. The twelfth sect — the *Wujūdiyya*, the principal object of Raniri's polemic — is Raniri's own; and the thirteenth is taken from the [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*Tamhīd*]] of [[Abu Syakur as-Salimi|Abū Shakūr as-Sālimī]].

## Significance

Najm ad-Dīn an-Nasafī is, through his *Kitāb fī bayān madhāhib al-mutaṣawwifa*, the source of the section on the heretical Ṣūfī sects in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] of Raniri. His small tract — preserved in a single Vatican manuscript — is one of the principal channels by which the scholastic classification of Muslim heresies was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian world. The identification of an-Nasafī as the source of this section of the *Tibjān* establishes the textual genealogy of Raniri's account of the heretical Ṣūfī sects, and places the *Tibjān* within the wider Arabic scholastic tradition of the cataloguing of religious deviations.

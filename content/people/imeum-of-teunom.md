The **Imeum of Teunom** was the energetic hereditary chief — *imeum* — of the district of [[Teunom]] on the [[West Coast (of Acheh)|West Coast of Acheh]]. He is mentioned by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in Vol. I of *The Achehnese* as the type of the powerful and capable adat-chief against whose entrenched local authority even the most enterprising of the Achehnese guerilla leaders could make little headway.

Snouck Hurgronje's reference occurs in the course of his celebrated portrait of [[Teuku Uma]], the unscrupulous adventurer who, from a mere panglima in the war against the Dutch, raised himself to be "the dreaded lord of almost the whole West Coast." Uma's methods — political marriage alliances, the violent suppression of hereditary chiefs, the purchase of sultan's patents, the manipulation of blood-feuds — succeeded in almost every quarter of the West Coast. Yet there were limits to his progress, and these limits, Snouck Hurgronje notes, were set by the personal character of the chiefs whom he encountered. "Much however depends on the personal character of these chiefs. They have equal opportunities for winning the affections of their rakans and more means at their disposal for increasing their influence than an ambitious leader of troops. Against an energetic chief such as the imeum of Teunom even [[Teuku Uma]] could make but little head."

The remark is important for the political sociology of Acheh that Snouck Hurgronje develops throughout Vol. I. The country was, in his analysis, "practically split up into countless miniature states under chiefs whose power depended on personal energy"; and in this system the office of imeum — though nominally subordinate to the uleebalang, and though its holder had originally been a religious functionary connected with the mosque of his mukim — could, in the hands of a strong man, become a centre of effective resistance to any outsider, however daring. The Imeum of Teunom thus stood, like the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata]] in the war with the Dutch, as an example of the same process of degeneration of the imeum-ship by which a religious office was transformed into an independent adat-chieftainship, and by which a hereditary chief might, by personal force, hold his ground against the most formidable of the guerilla adventurers thrown up by the [[Acheh War|Achehnese War]].

The case is also revealing of the structural weakness that beset any attempt to consolidate power in Acheh from below. A Teuku Uma might dominate the West Coast for a season, but the persistence of strong hereditary chiefs in particular districts — Teunom among them — meant that his "lordship" remained conditional, contested, and liable to dissolve as soon as his personal ascendancy declined. The Imeum of Teunom is thus cited not as a great figure in his own right, but as an instance of a general Achehnese pattern: that even the most successful adventurer could not, in the long run, overcome the local rootedness of an energetic hereditary chief.

**Muhammad** (Arabic *Muḥammad*; Achehnese *Muhammad* or *Mohammad*) — "the Apostle of God" and "the Lord of Men" — is the founder of Islam and the central religious figure of the Achehnese Mohammedan world. In the marriage, legal and domestic-rite chapters of Vol. I his name is invoked at every turn: in the marriage contract, in the formulas of blessing pronounced over bridegrooms, in the precepts handed down by him as the basis of the religious law, and in the small domestic offerings made "in honour of the Prophet." For his invocation in the calendar and festival chapters, see [[Prophet Mohammad]].

## In the marriage ceremony

At the moment when the Achehnese bridegroom descends from his house to join the [[Mamplenc (wedding procession)|bridegroom's procession]], one of the elders exclaims *Allahumma salli ʿala sayyidina Muhammad* ("O God! let thy blessing rest on our Lord Mohammad"), and all the bystanders shout in chorus *salawalée!* — the Achehnese pronunciation of the Arabic *qullu ʿalaih* ("utter the salat-prayer for him"). These formulas are repeated three times. Later in the ceremony, when the bridegroom is seated, an elder throws the contents of a small bowl in his face (he protects himself with a fan) and pronounces the formal blessing: "O Allah, bless Our Lord Mohammad and the family of our Lord Mohammad. Far be the curse, far be the calamity, let there be good fortune, let there be peace, may you have prosperity, may you have happiness." The Prophet's name thus brackets the bridegroom's passage from his father's house to that of his bride.

## In the law of marriage and wali

The precepts handed down by the Prophet form the basis of the Achehnese law of marriage, especially with regard to the *wali* (the marriage guardian). When a woman has no *wali* at her disposal, the civil authority acts as *wali* — a rule supported by the precept handed down by the Prophet: *To her that hath no wali the civil authority is wali*. The same precept is invoked in the [[Letters patent with the ninefold seal to Panglima Meuseugit Raya|letters patent]] granted to the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]], in which the sultan makes him his deputy for all matters of *pasah* (divorce by judicial decree), marriage, and the payment of pitrah for orphans, "so that all be done in accordance with the law of Mohammad." The *bale menhakamah* court established under [[Sayyid Abdurrahman Zahir|the Habib]] similarly decided "all questions relating to religion" in accordance with the law of the Prophet.

## Circumcision, tooth-filing, and the sunat

Achehnese domestic ritual distinguishes sharply between observances derived from the Prophet's example (*sunat*) and those of mere custom. The [[Peusunat (Circumcision)|circumcision]] of girls, performed with great secrecy even from the father, is accompanied only by the simplest of ceremonies: a small dish of yellow glutinous rice is brought to the teungku, who repeats the *fatihah* over it once; the women, in presenting it, say "we have here a small offering in honour of the Prophet" (*neu hajat bachut ken Pangulee*), without further elucidation. The teungkus receive such little gifts almost daily with a request for a *fatihah* or a prayer; the secrecy observed in this matter is much greater in Acheh than in Java.

Tooth-filing ([[Koh gigoe (tooth-filing)|koh gigoe]]), by contrast, is given no devotional origin by the Achehnese. Some Javanese allege that the custom is in conformity with the example of the Prophet, whose teeth are said to have been shaped as though filed from his birth — or, according to others, to have been injured in the [[Battle of Uhud|battle of Uhud]] — but the Achehnese regard it simply as a personal embellishment, having no ritual significance.

## The sunat of the beard

A characteristic anecdote of [[Sayyid Abdurrahman Zahir|the Habib]] turns on the *sunat* of the beard. When highlanders were instigated to bring before him suits designed to embarrass him under the letter of the sacred law, he seized the pretended suitors by their heads and bade them "go ye first to your homes and apply it to your own faces, dogs that ye be" — referring to their faces being clean-shaven in accordance with Achehnese custom, but in conflict with the *sunat*. He who in Acheh lets his beard, or beard and moustache, grow (as many *leubes* and *teungkus* do) is said to have adopted "the sunat of the Prophet."

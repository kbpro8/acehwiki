**G. B. Hooijer** was an infantry officer of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]], Ridder der [[Militaire Willems-Orde]] 4de klasse, who served in [[1879]] as chief of the military recording brigade (*opnemingsbrigade*) of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] during the expedition to [[Lamtoba]] and [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen]], co-authored with [[W. Cool]] the campaign narrative *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]), and crowned his literary work on the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] with the illustrated album *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]* ([[1897]]), published when he held the rank of gepensioneerd Luitenant-Kolonel.

## The reconnaissance to Lampanas, July 1879

In the last days of July [[1879]], as 1e Luitenant der Infanterie and chief of the military recording brigade, Hooijer was sent — with a corporal-*opnemer* and several Achehnese chiefs, among whom the celebrated [[Panglima Tibang Moehamad|Panglima Tibang]] — aboard the government steamboat [[Zeemeeuw (gouvernements-stoomboot)|Zeemeeuw]] to [[Lampanas]], there to gather the intelligence that Generaal [[General van der Heijden|van der Heijden]]'s well-disposed chiefs had been unable to supply. Although the population of Lampanas appeared not to be hostilely disposed, he was most strictly forbidden to go ashore. From the roadstead, after a conference with the commander of the naval vessel lying there and with the head of Lampanas — [[Toekoe Mas]], who came aboard the Zeemeeuw for the purpose — the landing point was fixed, and upon the headman's information a figurative sketch of the terrain to be traversed and of the plain of Lamtoba was composed which later proved to give a fair image of the reality. Returning to [[Oleh-leh]], he reported; the General thereupon gave the expedition orders.

## Brigade chief in the Lamtoba-Selimoen expedition

With two sergeants-*opnemers* under him, and mounted — one of the six equestrian officers of the column — Hooijer worked throughout the tocht with the point of the advance guard, the surveyors sketching the terrain in their measuring books as they marched. From Lampanas he mapped the hill track to the valley of [[Lamtoba]]; during the fortnight in the valley he accompanied the extended reconnaissances into the surrounding hill- and mountain-land, including Majoor [[Majoor Ducroo|Ducroo]]'s great sweep towards [[Kroeng Raija]] on [[12 August]]; and on the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|march to Selimoen]] of [[13 August|13]]–[[20 August]] he fixed the principal points of the upper [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]] valley instrumentally — from the berg-plateau of [[14 August]], from Selimoen itself, and finally from [[Indrapoeri]] — interpolating the remainder from the double route measurements of both columns out and back. From these data the [[Schetskaart van het boven-stroomgebied der Atjeh-rivier (1889)|schetskaart]] attached to the book was compiled — the map that Generaal van der Heijden praised as giving the answer to the hitherto unsolved question where and how the Atjeh river valley ends. On [[21 August]] [[1879]] the brigade chief also surveyed the one yet unexplored road in the neighbourhood of the Lamtoba valley, the mountain path towards [[Pedir]].

## Literary work

With Cool, Hooijer published in the Militaire Spectator of [[1886]] the series *[[Het Atjeh-vraagstuk (Cool & Hooijer, Militaire Spectator 1886)|Het Atjeh-vraagstuk]]*, and in [[1889]] the book itself, written up from the field diary kept during the tocht. Eight years later he published *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* (Haarlem: De Erven F. Bohn; Batavia: G. Kolff & Co., [[1897]]), the illustrated narrative of the outer posts of [[Groot-Atjeh]] between [[1893]] and [[1896]], in which the same terrain — the [[XXII Moekims]], [[Anagaloeng]], the [[Goudberg]] speaking face to face with the [[Glé Raja]] — is described from the tenure of a later phase of the war.

## Significance

Hooijer is, with his co-author, the principal personal chronicler of the operations of [[1879]] and of the post-1893 era of the war in Groot-Atjeh. His survey of the upper Atjeh basin remained for decades the only instrumental mapping of a region that no European entered again; and the two books together preserve the fullest officer's-eye record of the Atjeh-oorlog between the [*Day-order of 5 September 1879*](day-order) and the alliance with Toekoe Oemar.

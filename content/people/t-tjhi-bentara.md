**T. Tjhi Bentara** — *Teukoe Tjhi Bentara* — was the half-brother of [[T. Tjhi Toenong]] and the Dutch-appointed [[Uleebalang|oeléëbalang]] of [[Keureutoe]] in the years after the great raids of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. According to ancient tradition the authority over Keureutoe belonged in the line of Potjoet Asia di Keureutoe; by that reckoning [[T. Tjhi Toenong|T. Tjoet Moehamat]], who submitted in [[1899]], should have become oeléëbalang. But the bestuur, in those days of hasty pacification after the great raids through the statelets of the North and East Coast, handled the division of the *oeléëbalangschappen* strangely. When the place in Keureutoe had to be filled again, the bestuur did not appoint T. Tjoet Moehamat but his half-brother T. Tjhi Bentara.

## Weak and unpopular

This man was much less popular with the bevolking than his half-brother, and besides not very reliable, because he was a weakling who could not sufficiently make himself respected. Perhaps this was even a recommendation in the days when a hasty pacification seemed to need meegaande (compliant) chiefs. As indifferent and unpopular as T. Tjhi Bentara was, so sympathetic and liked was his brother. [[T. Tjhi Toenong|T. Tjoet Moehamat]] was the type of the Atjehsche chiefs of that time, and his influence hung always as a shadow over the authority of his brother.

The latter had married [[Tjoet Meuthia]] — *meuthia* meaning *pearl*, and she was indeed a pearl among women. But such a woman did not fit with the limp T. Tjhi Bentara or with his dependence on the Kompeuni. Therefore he repudiated her — a gesture by an unwanted man trying to save his face. Very shortly afterwards she married his half-brother [[T. Tjhi Toenong]].

## Removed 1905, restored November 1907

After the fusillading of T. Tjhi Toenong on [[5 March]] [[1905]] the position of T. Tjhi Bentara became impossible: the greater part of the submitted bevolking turned against the half-brother of whom it was said that he had brought about T. Tjhi Toenong's death. He had to step down temporarily, until the tempers had cooled. In [[November]] [[1907]] he was restored to his old position; but this, Zentgraff notes, only fanned the old hatred against him, and the sympathies of all turned to the son of the fusilladed man — the boy [[T. Radja Sabi]], the *adelaarsjong* — who now wandered in the mountains with the military behind him.

## The suspension of his son, 1936-1937

His son succeeded him as oeléëbalang of Keureutoe; but this young man, in the judgement of the bestuur, failed because he acted Western instead of properly Eastern. In the last part of [[1936]] he was suspended, and in [[1937]] [[T. Radja Sabi]] — the rightful heir, who had submitted on [[13 March]] [[1919]] after fourteen years in the forests — received the place that was his by right: he became *waarnemend oeléëbalang* of Keureutoe.

## Significance

T. Tjhi Bentara, Zentgraff writes, was the kind of compliant chief the Dutch bestuur preferred in the days of hasty pacification: weak, dependent, and therefore easily managed, but thereby also the trigger of much of the later resistance. The story of his nephew [[T. Radja Sabi|Radjā Sabi]] is in great part the story of the unpopularity of his appointment and of his half-brother's death.

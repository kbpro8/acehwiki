**Prof. Bakker** was the professor at [[Batavia]] under whom the Batak physician [[Gido Siregar]] had performed trachoma operations as assistant before he assisted [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] in the [[Trachoma campaign on Samosir (1933)|Samosir trachoma campaign]] of [[1933]].

## The connection

Tijssen recorded his relief on discovering the fact. Having found on [[Samosir]] not a single cataract among the candidates presented to him but only *[[Trachoma|trachoom]]*, and never having performed trachoma operations himself, he found that the young Batak physician posted there for the combating of leprosy was willing to comply with the wish of the [[Dienst der Volksgezondheid|D.V.G.]] to assist him: "Tot mijn groote vreugde bleek hij als assistent bij prof. Bakker in Batavia trachoom-operaties te hebben verricht." — To my great joy it appeared that he had performed trachoma operations as assistant to Prof. Bakker at Batavia.

## Significance

The reference is brief but of some importance in Tijssen's argument. It shows that the technical competence for mass trachoma surgery existed in the Indies, in the person of an Indonesian physician trained at Batavia, and that what was lacking was not skill but deployment: on Samosir, an island of nearly a hundred thousand people with three small mission auxiliary hospitals, there had been no real medical care at all until a year before Tijssen's visit, and in the field of ophthalmology "was het terrein even onbewerkt als heel Indië is."

**The Pretender Sultan** — written *de pretendent sultan*, *pretendent-sultan*, *pretender Sultan* in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the claimant to the [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese sultanate]] whose presence in the spring [[1896]] theatre of operations transformed the dynastic and religious-political dimensions of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the first semester of [[1896]]. Kruisheer records that the pretender Sultan was "at [[Panteh-Karang]] on [[16 June]] [[1896]] but fled for the Dutch troops" during the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik]], and that his younger brother [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] and his guardian [[Toeankoe Brahim]] had "gone with many followers over the [[VII Moekims Baid]] to [[Groot-Atjeh]], settling at [[Anao-Galong]]" in late May [[1896]]. The pretender Sultan himself was "said to have arrived [at Anao-Galong] and gone to [[Polém]]". His presence at the head of a network of dynastic and religious figures — the younger brother, the guardian, the [[Habib Lhong]] band which had restored him to [[Keumala]] in early [[1896]] — gave the Achehnese resistance a symbolic centre which the Dutch could not ignore, and Kruisheer notes that the pretender Sultan was "*bij zijn eigen landgenooten in Pedir blijkbaar niet so hoog aangeschreven stond*" (apparently not held in high regard by his own countrymen in [[Pedir]]), a verdict which casts doubt on the political durability of the restoration which [[Habib Lhong]] had achieved on his behalf.

## The Habib Lhong Restoration at Keumala

The most consequential action associated with the pretender Sultan in the period before the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March]] [[1896]] was his restoration to his "old residence" at [[Keumala]] by the Kling [[Habib Lhong]]. Kruisheer records that Lhong, "at the head of a band of ~500 men", had gone via [[Metareum]], [[Andeue]] and [[Lala]] to Keumala, "burning kampongs along the way, and restored the sultan to his old residence, avenging the sultan's forced flight of the previous year". The campaign reversed an earlier setback which had driven the pretender Sultan from Keumala — the traditional capital of the inland sultanate — and it established Lhong's reputation as one of the most active Habib commanders in the [[Pedir]] network. The choice of route through the inland villages of the Pidië region, and the burning of kampongs along the way, reflect the military logic of a punitive expedition intended to demonstrate the Sultan's restored authority. Kruisheer's scepticism about the pretender Sultan's standing among his own countrymen — "*blijkbaar niet so hoog aangeschreven stond*" — suggests, however, that the restoration was a Habib-driven rather than a popular-Achehnese initiative, and that the political foundation of the Sultan's authority was thinner than the military display suggested.

## Movement to Groot-Atjeh and Flight from Panteh-Karang

The most significant strategic development associated with the pretender Sultan in the spring of [[1896]] was the movement of his circle from [[Pedir]] into [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Kruisheer records that in late May [[1896]] [[Toeankoe Machmoed]] (the pretender Sultan's younger brother) and [[Toeankoe Brahim]] (son of [[Toeankoe Hasjim]], former *voogd* or guardian of the pretender Sultan) "went with many followers over the [[VII Moekims Baid]] to [[Groot-Atjeh]], settling at [[Anao-Galong]]". The choice of Anao-Galong — the former Dutch *benteng* which had been the religious centre of [[Teungkoe di Tiro]] — was symbolically significant, because it placed the Sultan's circle at the religious-political heart of the [[XXII Moekims]]. [[Toekoe Moeda Baid]] (the *hoeloebalang* of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]) informed the colonial government in writing of the movement, and reported "that the pretender Sultan had arrived and gone to [[Polém]]" — the western *landschap* which had been a long-standing centre of resistance. The pretender Sultan's presence at [[Panteh-Karang]] on [[16 June]] [[1896]] — the first day of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of V Moekims Montassik]] — and his flight "for the Dutch troops" before the storming of the Panteh-Karang redoute by Amboineezen under [[Lamster]] and [[Dijserinck]] confirmed that his presence in the Groot-Atjeh theatre was no longer tenable. The flight of the pretender Sultan, together with the broader Achehnese retreat to [[Lam-Tobah]] of [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] after the [[Battle of Kroeng-Kali (23 June 1896)|23 June 1896 battle of Kroeng-Kali]], marked the end of the pretender Sultan's direct military presence in the first-semester [[1896]] operations.

## Significance

The pretender Sultan's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the dynastic and religious-political dimension of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the dimension which distinguished the war from a purely counter-insurgency campaign and which gave the Achehnese coalition a symbolic head whose authority could, in principle, unify the resistance. Kruisheer's scepticism about his actual standing among his own countrymen, however, suggests that the dynastic dimension was weaker than the military-religious dimension represented by the Habibs and *oelama*, and the pretender Sultan's flight from [[Panteh-Karang]] on [[16 June]] [[1896]] — without offering battle — confirms that his military value was limited. The movement of his circle into [[Groot-Atjeh]] in late May [[1896]] nonetheless represented a significant strategic development, because it brought the dynastic and the religious-military dimensions of the resistance into the same theatre of operations.

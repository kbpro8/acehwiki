**Jacques l'Hermite** (c. 1582-1624) was a Dutch navigator and commercial officer in the service of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] who, as the senior Dutch commercial officer at [[Bantam|Bantam]] in the period [[1608]]-[[1612]], received the [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] fleet's reports on the [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|Malaka expedition of 1608]], criticised [[Hendrick van Bergel|Hendrick van Bergel]]'s conduct as governor of [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]], and authored the "Corte Remonstrantie" of [[20 August]] [[20 August]] [[1612]] — the principal Dutch strategic memorandum of the Banda chapter between [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]]'s *Discours* of [[1608]] and [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]]. L'Hermite is a supporting Dutch figure of Van der Woude's [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The Bantam factorship

L'Hermite served as the senior Dutch commercial officer at [[Bantam|Bantam]] in the period [[1608]]-[[1612]]. In this capacity he received the reports of the [[Pieter Willemsz. Verhoeff|Verhoeff]] fleet's [[Verhoeff's Malaka expedition (1608)|Malaka expedition of 1608]] and the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|murder of Verhoeff at Banda of 22 May 1609]]. He also received the reports of the Banda governors [[Hendrick van Bergel|Hendrick van Bergel]] (governor [[1609]]-[[1610]]) and **Willem van der Voort** (governor [[1610]]-[[1611]]), and his correspondence with the [[Heeren Zeventien|Heeren Zeventien]] is one of the principal Dutch sources for the Banda chapter.

## The criticism of Van Bergel

L'Hermite's most consequential judgement was his criticism of [[Hendrick van Bergel|Hendrick van Bergel]]'s conduct as governor of Banda. After [[Pieter Both|Both]]'s [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] expedition of [[1610]], Van Bergel sailed to [[Bantam|Bantam]] in June [[1610]] to consult with L'Hermite. L'Hermite "ernstig afkeurde" — seriously disapproved — of Van Bergel's conduct: he was of the opinion that his landgenoot "de Engelschen hadden moeten verjagen, dan zouden de Bandaneezen wel opnieuw vrede hebben moeten sluiten". Van der Woude notes that L'Hermite's criticism missed the point: "Het schijnt niet tot l'Hermite doorgedrongen, dat zijn landgenooten machteloos waren tegen den sluikhandel en dat zij in den toestand van gewapenden vrede op Neira en openlijken oorlog op de andere eilanden vrijwel de gevangenen waren van hun eigen fort".

## The "Corte Remonstrantie" of 20 August 1612

L'Hermite's "Corte Remonstrantie" of [[20 August]] [[20 August]] [[1612]] was the principal Dutch strategic memorandum of the Banda chapter between [[Cornelis Matelieff|Matelieff]]'s *Discours* of [[1608]] and [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]]. L'Hermite argued that the Dutch at Banda "niet naer peys (hadden behooren) te luysteren" — should not have listened to peace — and that "eenige merckelycke macht gesonden werde, om haer geheel t'onder te brengen ende den bril te dege op den neuse te stellen" — a substantial force should be sent to subdue them entirely and put the bridle firmly on their nose. The Dutch, l'Hermite argued, should not "naer eenige vrede ofte handel en hoorde te trachten, voor ende aleer dat men haer geheelick overwonnen ende met goede verseeckeringe tot redenen gebrocht ofte gansch wtgeroyt hadde" — should not seek peace or trade until they had entirely conquered the Bandanese and brought them to reason with good surety, or rooted them out entirely.

The "Corte Remonstrantie" thus anticipated [[Hendrick Brouwer|Hendrick Brouwer]]'s "Secrete Instructie" of [[4 April]] [[4 April]] [[1612]] (which is dated slightly earlier in the same year) and [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]] — the three documents together constitute the principal Dutch strategic memoranda of the Banda chapter and the intellectual foundation of the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force.

## Significance

Jacques l'Hermite is, in Van der Woude's narrative, a supporting Dutch figure of the Banda chapter and the principal Dutch strategic advisor at [[Bantam|Bantam]] in the period [[1608]]-[[1612]]. His "Corte Remonstrantie" of [[20 August]] [[1612]] — together with [[Hendrick Brouwer|Hendrick Brouwer]]'s "Secrete Instructie" of [[4 April]] [[1612]] and [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]]'s [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]] — was the principal Dutch strategic memorandum of the Banda chapter and the intellectual foundation of the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme. His criticism of [[Hendrick van Bergel|Van Bergel]]'s conduct as governor of Banda — though Van der Woude suggests it missed the point — frames the institutional tension between the Bantam factorship and the Banda governorship that would define the early VOC administration. L'Hermite's role in the Dutch-Atjeh relationship was indirect: by [[1608]] the strategic centre of Dutch-Indies policy had shifted from Atjeh to Banda, and the Banda experience — not the Atjeh experience — would define the [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme.

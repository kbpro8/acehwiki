**Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah** was the [[oelèëbalang|oelèëbalang]] — self-governing authority — of the *landschap* [[Idi Rajeu'|Idi Rajeu']] in the subdistrict Idi on the East Coast of Atjèh, one of the prominent Atjèhnese self-governing rulers who came to the fore in the late colonial period and a member of the widespread Tjhi' family whose branches spanned the residency. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* introduces him as one of "the many other *oelèëbalang*s" who, alongside the more prominent [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]] of Peusangan, "came to the fore" in the late colonial administration.

## The Tjhi' family

The Tjhi' family was one of the most prominent *oelèëbalang* families of Atjèh, with branches in multiple *landschap*'s. [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]] of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]] — the patron of the founding of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] in [[1939]] — was the most prominent figure of the family; [[Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Ali Akbar|Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Ali Akbar]] of the [[Kawaj XVI|Kawaj XVI]] on the West Coast was another. Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah of Idi Rajeu' was, in this sense, the East Coast representative of a family whose branches spanned the residency — the family name (Tjhi') serving, in Piekaar's account, as a signpost of the network of alliances that linked the self-governing rulers of pre-war Atjèh.

## The oelèëbalang of Idi Rajeu'

The *landschap* Idi Rajeu' was one of the eight *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling Idi* on the East Coast. Its *oelèëbalang* — Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah — was, like the other *oelèëbalang*'s of the East Coast, the head of a *landschap* that had originated as a colony of pepper planters. The institutional structure of the *landschap* — the *toeha' peuët* (four elders) and *toeha' lapan* (eight-heads) below the *landschapshoofd* — was the same as that of the other East Coast *landschap*'s, and Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah's authority rested on the same combination of territorial rule and personal prestige that characterised the *oelèëbalang*'s office throughout Atjèh.

## Significance

The introduction of Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Daoed Sjah in Piekaar's account — alongside the other *oelèëbalang*'s who "came to the fore" in the late colonial administration — illustrates the political landscape of pre-war Atjèh: a network of self-governing rulers, linked by family ties and political alliances, whose cooperation with the Dutch administration was the principal instrument of indirect rule. The Tjhi' family — with its branches in Peusangan, Idi Rajeu', and the Kawaj XVI — was one of the principal nodes of this network, and its members' fates in the events of [[1942]] would shape the political reorganisation of the residency under the Japanese occupation.

## A possible post-war successor to Teukoe Nja' Arif (1946)

Henri J. H. Alers's *Om een rode of groene Merdeka* records that when the republican *oelèëbalang*-administrator [[Teukoe Nja' Arif|Teukoe Nja' Arif]] died in [[April]] [[1946]] while interned by the [[Poesa|Poesa]], he was succeeded as nominal head of Atjeh's administration by an *oelèëbalang* Alers names as "Teuku Tjhi Muhamad Daud Sjah" — a figure Alers describes as no more than "a willing instrument in the hands of the great man behind the scenes," [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]], who had by then made himself head of the *Dienst van Godsdienstzaken* (Service of Religious Affairs). Alers does not indicate whether this figure is identical with the *oelèëbalang* of [[Idi Rajeu'|Idi Rajeu']] documented by Piekaar under the same name and title, and given how widely the honorific *Tjhi'* was shared across branches of the wider [[Teukoe Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Tjhi' family]], the identification must be treated as tentative rather than established.

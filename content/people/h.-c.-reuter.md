**H. C. Reuter** was the oldest Tamiang planter at the time of Broersma's visit, having been the owner of the rubber estate [[Tandjong Semantoh|Tandjong Semantoh]] in [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] before selling it to the [[Borneo Rubber Compagnie|Borneo Rubber Compagnie]] — a Japanese-owned company. At the time of Broersma's visit he was the owner of the young estate [[Rimba Sawang|Rimba Sawang]].

## Significance
Reuter's career as the oldest planter of Tamiang spans the entire first generation of European planting in the *onderafdeeling* — from the early tobacco-experiments of the *Deli-Mij.* and the *Tabak-Mij. Arendsburg* in the years around [[1898]], through the transition to rubber and oil-palm after about [[1910]], to the Japanese acquisition of European estates in the early 1920s. His sale of Tandjong Semantoh to a Japanese-owned company is one of the early tokens of the transfer of European planting-interests in northern Sumatra to Japanese capital in the post-war period.

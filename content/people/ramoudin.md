**Ramoudin** was a native sergeant (*Inlandsch sergeant*) of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]], serving in the 2nd Company of the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie]] in the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] of [[1879]]. He is singled out by name in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]) as the first man inside the Achehnese entrenchment stormed on [[13 August]] [[1879]] during the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|march to Selimoen]] — the luitenant-adjudant [[A. G. Popelier|Popelier]] being the second.

## The affair of 13 August 1879

The work carried by the storm — the earthen trench of more than a hundred paces, fronted by an abatis and flanked by a palisaded *tamboer* beyond the burnt kampong of [[Kepaja Doewa]] — was defended by a party that fled into the deep ravine behind at the moment the flanking peloton of the 2nd Company, led by Captain [[Clerkson]], came upon the parapet. So impetuous was the rush that a soldier of the company seized a fleeing Achehnese by the head and was left with only the man's head-cloth in his grasp. Of the honour of the entry the narrative fixes the order precisely: "Een Inlandsch sergeant Ramoudin was de eerste, de Luitenant-Adjudant Popelier de tweede in de versterking." The naming of the native non-commissioned officer in this connection is one of the few occasions on which the book distinguishes by name a ranker of the native companies.

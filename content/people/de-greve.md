**De Greve** was a Dutch mining engineer of the mid-nineteenth-century colonial mining service whose [[1865]] geological study of the natural oil wells of the East Indies — cited in the standard form "DE GREVE, 1865" — is one of the earliest scientific investigations of East Indian petroleum, and whose [[1868]] discovery of the coal outcrops in the [[Umbilin River]] opened what would become the most important coal field of central [[Sumatra]]. De Greve is also, in the petroleum history chapter of [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]], named alongside [[J. H. Menten|J. H. Menten]] and [[S. W. Tromp|S. W. Tromp]] as one of the nineteenth-century scientists who "started to study the geological features of the known natural oil wells in the East Indies" after the [[1859]] drilling of the first Pennsylvanian gushers.

## The 1865 oil-wells study

Van Bemmelen's reference to De Greve in the petroleum history chapter is brief but significant: "Since [[1859]], when the first Pennsylvanian gushers were drilled, a number of scientists started to study the geological features of the known natural oil wells in the East Indies (J. H. MENTEN and S. W. TROMP; DE GREVE, [[1865]])." This places De Greve's [[1865]] study among the earliest scientific investigations of East Indian petroleum, in the immediate aftermath of the [[1859]] Pennsylvania oil boom that inaugurated the modern petroleum industry. The specific content of De Greve's [[1865]] report is not reproduced by Van Bemmelen, but the citation itself establishes De Greve as one of the founders of East Indian petroleum geology.

## The 1868 Umbilin coal discovery

De Greve is also the discoverer of the [[Umbilin coal field|Umbilin coal outcrops]]. Van Bemmelen records that "the discovery of the coal seams dates from [[1868]] when the mining engineer DE GREVE found outcrops in the river Umbilin." The Umbilin coal field, in the mountains of central Sumatra about 165 km from [[Emma Haven]] (the harbour of [[Padang]]), would subsequently become the most productive coal field of the Netherlands East Indies, with exploitation beginning in [[1892]] and production reaching 600,000 tons per year by [[1916]]. De Greve's [[1868]] discovery is the founding episode of the Umbilin industry.

## Significance for Atjeh

De Greve's significance for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history is indirect but real. His [[1865]] oil-wells study is one of the earliest scientific investigations of East Indian petroleum, and accordingly part of the documentary background against which the [[1883]] [[J. Zulkler|Zulkler]] concession and the [[1885]] [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]]-led [[Telaga Said]] drilling — the founding episodes of the northern-Sumatran oil industry — were undertaken. The northern-Sumatran oil industry that De Greve's work prefigured would, by [[1940]]–[[1941]], extend onto Atjeh territory with the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex|Atjeh complex]] fields.

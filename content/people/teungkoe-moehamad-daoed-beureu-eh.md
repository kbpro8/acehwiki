**Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh** was a forty-year-old [[oelama|oelama]] from the *landschap* [[Keumangan|Keumangan]] in the subdistrict [[Sigli|Sigli]], chairman of the central board of the [[Poesa|Poesa]] — the All-Aceh Union of Ulemas founded in [[1939]] — and the principal religious-political figure of pre-war Atjèh, whose influence "extended far beyond the borders of his *landschap*" and whose organisation provided, for the first time in the history of Atjèh, a form in which "both the oelama's and the more militant youth would find a place." Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats him as the central figure of the religious movement that would, in early [[1942]], ally itself with the Japanese and would, after the war, become the principal vehicle of the [[Atjèh|Atjèhnese]] struggle against the Dutch and against the Indonesian Republic alike.

## Personal profile

Piekaar describes Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed as "of medium height, slender build," and notes that his relationship with the old self-governing ruler of Keumangan, [[Teukoe Oemar|Teukoe Oemar]] (whose removal in [[1938]] had been one of the scandals of pre-war Atjèh administration), "left something to be desired." He held the chairmanship of the religious school association Djamiatoeddiniyah in [[Blang Pasèh|Blang Pasèh]]. The combination of his religious authority, his organisational talent, and his ability to bridge the gap between the traditional *oelama*'s and the more militant religious youth gave him a unique position in Atjèhnese society.

## The founding of Poesa

The [[Poesa|Poesa]] — *P(ersatoean) Oe(lama-oelama) S(eloeroeh) A(tjèh)*, the All-Aceh Union of Ulemas — was founded in [[1939]] in [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] under the high patronage of [[Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah|Teukoe Hadji Tjhi' Moehamad Djohan Alam Sjah]], self-governing authority of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]]. The founding was, Piekaar records, "intended as a counterweight to the modern currents of the Moehammadijah movement, which were foreign to the fanatically pious Atjèhse religious life." With the Poesa, Piekaar writes, "for the first time in the history of Aceh, the religious life and striving of the Acehnese found its own form," and the movement "managed to acquire a following of an extent unknown to Aceh throughout the entire residency." Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh, as chairman of the central board, was the principal architect of this achievement.

## The modern religious school

The religious school whose main teachers included, besides Teungkoe Isma'el Jacoeb and Teungkoe Hadji Aboe Bakar, also Teungkoe Moehamad Noer — son-in-law of Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh — was the principal institutional vehicle of the movement. Teungkoe Moehamad Noer, who had "also received his training in Egypt and years previously had been noted in the Idi region for anti-authority statements," represented the younger generation that connected the oelama's authority to the political militancy of the religious youth.

## The 1942 alliance with Japan

When the Japanese invasion came in March [[1942]], the Poesa — and through it Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh — emerged as the principal Atjèhnese interlocutor of the Japanese. The religious movement's anti-Dutch orientation, the network of religious schools it had built, and its capacity to mobilise the Atjèhnese population made it the natural ally of the Japanese [[F-organisatie|F-organisatie]] and its successor, the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]]. The F-propagandists [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]] and [[Said Doellah|Said Doellah]] who entered Atjèh with the Japanese troops at [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]] were, Piekaar records, "of the same group of persons to whom the letters found on Said Doellah were addressed" — that is, the Poesa network.

**Wiggers van Kerchem** — *Kolonel* (later *Generaal*) *Wiggers van Kerchem* — was a Dutch officer of the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army ([[K.N.I.L.|K.N.I.L.]]) who served as acting Governor of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] from [[June 1875|June]] [[1875]] to [[November 1875|November]] [[1875]], during the leave of absence of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] to Java, and again briefly in [[March 1876|March]] [[1876]] after Pel's death, before being replaced by [[Diemont|General Diemont]] in [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]]. His tenure saw the improvement of the Dutch *stelling* and the preparation for the further expansion of [[Pel's Plan|Pel's Plan]].

## Acting Governorship (June – November 1875)

When [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] went on leave to Java in [[June 1875|June]] [[1875]] to discuss his further plans with the government, the colonel Wiggers van Kerchem served as his temporary replacement. Wiggers van Kerchem had in the interim improved the *stelling*, extended it with another post, had damaged the enemy outside it, and repelled his attacks, which grew ever bolder.

## The Diemont Succession (November 1876)

When the Netherlands Indies government at Batavia became convinced that more vigorous action must be taken against the still-recalcitrant coastal statelets, and since [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] thought otherwise on the matter, he was replaced in [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]] by [[Diemont|General Diemont]]. To the latter was entrusted the further completion of [[Pel's Plan|Pel's Plan]] in Groot-Atjeh and the offensive operations on the coasts.

## Significance

Wiggers van Kerchem is a transitional figure in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], bridging the governorship of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] (1874–1876) and that of [[Diemont|Diemont]] (1876–1877). His improvement of the *stelling* and his extension of it with another post continued the expansion of [[Pel's Plan|Pel's Plan]]; his disagreement with the government on the question of vigorous action against the coastal statelets anticipated the more sustained disagreement between the Dutch government and its governors in Atjeh that would recur throughout the war.

**Willem I** was the first **King of the United Kingdom of the Netherlands** from [[1815]] to [[1840]], whose reign oversaw the re‑organisation of the **Dutch East Indies** and the early diplomatic contacts with the **Aceh Sultanate**.  The *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pages [[489]]–[[490]]) references his name in relation to the 1824 **Anglo‑Dutch Treaty of London**, which confirmed Dutch influence over **Sumatra**, including **Aceh**, and authorised the deployment of marine forces to the region.

## Post‑Napoleonic Re‑organisation of the Indies

Following the Congress of Vienna, Willem I sought to consolidate Dutch authority over the former **VOC** territories.  In [[1819]], he appointed a **Governor‑General** for the **East Indies** who negotiated with the **Sultan of Aceh** for the establishment of a Dutch consulate at **Banda Aceh**.  The *Mariniers* archives record that marine detachments were prepared at **Willemsoord** for potential expeditionary duties in **Aceh**.

## Military Engagements

Although no large‑scale conflict with **Aceh** occurred during Willem I’s reign, the **Royal Netherlands Marine Corps** conducted several patrols along the **Aceh** coast to protect Dutch merchant vessels.  These operations are noted on page [[489]] of the source, where Willem I is listed among the sovereigns authorising marine deployments.

## Significance

Willem I’s policies laid the diplomatic and administrative foundations that later monarchs would build upon, culminating in the full‑scale **Aceh War** under **Willem III**.  His emphasis on a strong naval presence foreshadowed the extensive use of the **Marine Corps** in subsequent Aceh campaigns.

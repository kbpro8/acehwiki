**Joan Maetsuycker** (also *Maetsuyker*; 1606–1678) was a Dutch colonial administrator who, at the time of [[Pieter Soury|Pieter Soury]]'s [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|embassy to Atjeh]] in [[1641]], served as *extra-ordinaris Raad van Indië* (extraordinary Councillor of the Indies) and was one of the two senior officials who saw the embassy off at [[Batavia]]. He would later serve as Gouverneur-Generaal of the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] in the Indies from [[1653]] to [[1678]].

## Sending-off of the Soury embassy

When *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s legation was dispatched from Batavia to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1641]] to congratulate the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] on her accession, Maetsuycker — in his capacity as *extra-ordinaris Raad van Indië* — together with [[Justus Schouten|Justus Schouten]], gave the legation its formal send-off. Godee Molsbergen's pairing is: "Het gezantschap werd te Batavia uitgeleide gedaan door de extra-ordinaris Raad van Indië Joan Maetsuycker en Justus Schouten."

## Religious question at appointment

Maetsuycker's later appointment as *raadpensionaris* of the Council of Justice — a position the Heeren Zeventien wished to fill with a qualified jurist — became the occasion of a religious-political controversy. The Heeren Zeventien wished to send out "een bequaem en wel gepractiseert regtsgeleerde" (a capable and well-practised jurist) in that capacity; the choice fell on "de persoon van Jan Maetsuycker."

The delegate of the Zeeland Chamber, Mr. Schotte, made "swarigheid consent te dragen" — difficulty in giving consent — "wijl corsaecke hij seijde bericht te wesen, dat hij soude wezen een gepassioneert papist" — because, he said, he was informed that Maetsuycker would be a passionate papist. Maetsuycker, however, was heard on the matter and declared that it was indeed true that he had been raised in the Roman Catholic religion from his youth, but that he had long since withdrawn from it, was of a wholly different opinion, and was resolved to make profession of the Reformed religion both on the voyage and on arrival in the Indies. After he had again declared, by his aforementioned resolution, that he would — of his own free choice, out of sincere affection and love for the Reformed religion, as one come to Batavia — make profession of it, he was accepted.

The episode is a vivid example of the religious tests applied to high VOC office, and of the political weight of the Zeeland Chamber in the Company's appointments.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, Maetsuycker's role is chiefly as the *extra-ordinaris Raad van Indië* who saw the Soury embassy off at Batavia — a senior officer at the diplomatic hinge between Batavia and the new Sultana's court. His subsequent long tenure as Gouverneur-Generaal ([[1653]]–[[1678]]) would span much of the era of female rule in Atjeh that the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana of 1641]] opened, and during which the Dutch-Atjeh relationship continued to be defined by trade, treaty and the constant negotiation of sovereignty that Soury's mission had inaugurated.

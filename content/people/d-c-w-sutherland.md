**D. C. W. Sutherland** — referred to in the source as Captain Sutherland — was a Dutch marine captain who commanded the marine companies that landed on the banks of the [[Pedir River]] during the [[Atjeh Expedition 1873-1876|Atjeh Expedition]] in [[January]] [[1874]] and who subsequently commanded Dutch marine forces during the [[1874]]–[[1875]] operations in [[Aceh]]. He was decorated with the [[Ridder Militaire Willemsorde]] (Knight, fourth class) for distinguished conduct in the Atjeh expeditions of [[1873]] and [[1874]], and was listed among those knighted in the royal decree of [[6 October]] [[1874]].

## Role in the 1874 campaign

Sutherland's marine companies were among the first Dutch marine units to engage in the inland operations of the [[Atjeh Expedition 1873-1876|1874 campaign]]. After landing on the banks of the [[Pedir River]], his troops were forced to fight a defensive battle when the tide went out, suffering three dead and nine wounded. His marines subsequently participated in the siege of the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton (Aceh)]] under [[General major Verspijk]] and were involved in the capture of the sultan's seat on [[23 January]] [[1874]].

Later in the campaign, Sutherland's marines joined the infantry brigades from [[Padang]] and took part in the construction of a bivouac at [[Penajoeng]]. His forces were thus integrated into the broader Dutch infantry-and-marine assault force that pushed inland from the Atjehnese coast in the early months of [[1874]].

## Honours

The royal decree of [[6 October]] [[1874]] listed Captain Sutherland among those knighted in the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] (fourth class) for bravery in Atjeh. The award, made on the standard operational rubric of the Atjeh expeditions of [[1873]] and [[1874]], reflects the high level of personal bravery recognised by the Dutch crown during the early phase of the [[Atjeh Expedition 1873-1876|Aceh War]] and reinforces the prestige of the [[Ridder Militaire Willemsorde]] as the highest Dutch military decoration for conduct in the field.

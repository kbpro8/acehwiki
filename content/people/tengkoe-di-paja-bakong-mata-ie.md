**Tengkoe di Paja Bakong** — alias *Tgk. di Mata Ië*, the "Heer der bronnen" (Lord of the Springs) of the [[Pasé]], [[Keureutoe|Keureutoë]], and Djambo Ajé rivers — was the younger brother of [[Tengkoe Sjech di Paja Bakong|Tgk. Seupot Mata]] and, in Zentgraff's judgement, of fame perhaps greater even than his brother's. He had returned to war out of bitterness over the [[Execution of T. Tjhi Toenong (1905)|fusillading of T. Tjhi Toenong]] on [[5 March]] [[1905]], and the Kompeuni hunted him from that year onward. For twelve years he escaped the sharpest pursuit in the mountains and forests of [[Pasé|Pasé]], [[Keureutoe|Keureutoë]], and the [[Gajoeland]].

## Twelve years pursued

Almost all the good patrol-leaders of those years once found his spoor: [[Van der Vlerk]] and [[Behrens]] sat him sometimes close on the heels, but he always escaped — and this made the fame of his mystical powers still greater. Men spoke of him in the kampongs as of one who stood above this world. When the jagers made it too hot for him he withdrew for a time into the then still barely known country of [[Samarkilang]] and the Eastern [[Gajoeland]]. [[T. Radja Sabi]] himself, in his wandering years, attached himself for a time to this famous oelama — but the man was so hunted by the military that with him there was neither rest nor peace.

## The wound of 1913

In [[1913]] [[Behrens]] fell upon him at the Aloeë Garöt so swiftly that his turban and rosary caught in the bushes like the hair of Absalom; in this encounter Behrens was wounded in the leg. So an end had come, apparently, to the oelama's invulnerability — yet he held out another four years.

## The killing, 16 July 1917

Was it really anything but chance that a patrol under the Inlandse sergeant [[Toegimin]], marching through Samarkilang on [[16 July]] [[1917]], found in the forest an untended *ladang* on which two huts stood? Two unknown Atjehers were shot. From the body of one of them the leg was strongly swollen — apparently the result of an old wound. Though Tgk. di Mata Ië had been wounded in the leg in [[1913]], there was still doubt, when a couple of Atjehers of Samarkilang, seeing the corpse, threw themselves to the earth in deep awe, afraid of the curse of the dead man, and cried out: *"Teungkoe, wees niet kwaad, het was niet onze schuld"* — "Teungkoe, be not angry, it was not our fault." The wife of the Tengkoe finally recognised him by his *voetringen* (foot-rings).

## Significance

His spirit worked long after his death. From his last followers was formed the famous [[Samarkilang bende]], whose head was only taken prisoner in [[1937]] — twenty years later. The long hunt for Tgk. di Mata Ië is the most methodical in the Atjehsche history, and one of the great examples of the tenacity both of the pursued and of the pursuer.

## The long pursuit

Tgk. di Mata Ië worked on the upper [[Djambo Ajé]] with [[Pang Nanggròë]], [[Tgk. Sjeh di Paja Bakong]] and [[Tgk. di Barat]] in the years after [[1905]]. He survived every attack on his associates.

In [[1911]] the growing co-operation of the population with the *[[Kompeuni]]* first turned against him: "the first to suffer the evil consequences of this was the equally irreconcilable and unfindable Tgk. di Mata Ië, whose dry fields were raided. And that cost him his brother-in-law and best band-leader, besides several other relatives and all his supplies." In May [[1911]] a patrol succeeded in surprising a band of his foragers, in which a woman was accidentally severely wounded; she died the next day as a result of her own wilfulness in wrenching off the bandage. In September he again lost a few followers.

The monthly report for October [[1911]] recorded a scene from the aftermath of the raid on his *ladangs*: "a patrol from the bivouac [[Babah Kroeëng Bidin]] found, in the neighbourhood of the raided dry fields of Tgk. di Mata Ië, a woman with two children who had been wandering there since the raid without having seen any of her own people again and without being able to find the fields. In a wretched condition, living on forest fruits, she had spent about a month and a half there. She was sent to Lho' Seumawè for care."

In February [[1912]] Tgk. di Barat came down from the [[Meergebied]] to the plain of [[Lho' Soekon]] and [[Lho' Seumawè]] in order to reorganise the resistance together with Tgk. di Mata Ië; the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* awaited them, and their blows struck Tgk. di Mata Ië first, "who was to prove unfindable or unhittable for years to come," before [[H. Behrens|Behrens]] killed Tgk. di Barat on [[22 February]].

In January [[1913]] the foragers of Tgk. di Mata Ië were vigorously driven off by the population at [[Lho' Soekon]]. In [[1913]] [[H. Behrens|Behrens]] fell upon him so suddenly by the [[Aloeë Garöt]] that his turban and rosary remained caught in the bushes, "als het haar van Absalom"; he was wounded in the leg in that encounter. His invulnerability had apparently come to an end, yet he maintained himself four years more.

By [[1913]] the survey of the last unsubmitted resistance leaders listed for Lho' Soekon only "Tgk. di Mata Ië, the survivor of the three great resistance leaders, of whom Tgk. Sjeh di Paja Bakong and Tgk. di Barat had already fallen," together with [[T. Radja Sabi]]. By [[1914]] only he and [[T. Radja Tampo']] remained of the great resistance men.

## Death, 10 July 1917

It was, as du Croo observed, almost chance that a patrol under the native sergeant [[Toegimin]] passed through [[Samarkilang]] on [[10 July]] [[1917]] and found in the forest an insignificant *ladang* with two little houses on it. Two unknown Acehnese were shot down; the leg of one corpse was much swollen, presumably the result of an earlier wound. Although Tgk. di Mata Ië had been wounded in the leg in [[1913]], there was still doubt — until a few Acehnese from Samarkilang, seeing the body, threw themselves to the ground in deep reverence, afraid of the curse of the dead man, and cried out: "Teungkoe, be not angry — it was not our fault." The Teungkoe's wife finally recognised him by his ankle rings.

## Afterlife: the Samarkilang band

"His spirit long went on working": from his last followers was formed the well-known [[Samarkilang-bende|Samarkilang band]], whose leader was not captured until [[1937]] — twenty years later.

## Significance (du Croo, 1943)

Tgk. di Mata Ië was, with [[T. Radja Tampo']], one of only two great resistance figures still at large after [[1914]], and his career illustrates the phase of the pacification that du Croo called "the search for needles in a haystack" — the methodical, obstinate search for individual irreconcilables in a country otherwise reported month after month as "politieke toestand gunstig." The veneration his corpse still commanded at Samarkilang measured the hold such figures retained over the population long after organised resistance had ended.

**Tengkoe di Paja Bakong** — alias *Tgk. di Mata Ië*, the "Heer der bronnen" (Lord of the Springs) of the [[Pasé]], [[Keureutoe|Keureutoë]], and Djambo Ajé rivers — was the younger brother of [[Tengkoe Sjech di Paja Bakong|Tgk. Seupot Mata]] and, in Zentgraff's judgement, of fame perhaps greater even than his brother's. He had returned to war out of bitterness over the [[Execution of T. Tjhi Toenong (1905)|fusillading of T. Tjhi Toenong]] on [[5 March]] [[1905]], and the Kompeuni hunted him from that year onward. For twelve years he escaped the sharpest pursuit in the mountains and forests of [[Pasé|Pasé]], [[Keureutoe|Keureutoë]], and the [[Gajoeland]].

## Twelve years pursued

Almost all the good patrol-leaders of those years once found his spoor: [[Van der Vlerk]] and [[Behrens]] sat him sometimes close on the heels, but he always escaped — and this made the fame of his mystical powers still greater. Men spoke of him in the kampongs as of one who stood above this world. When the jagers made it too hot for him he withdrew for a time into the then still barely known country of [[Samarkilang]] and the Eastern [[Gajoeland]]. [[T. Radja Sabi]] himself, in his wandering years, attached himself for a time to this famous oelama — but the man was so hunted by the military that with him there was neither rest nor peace.

## The wound of 1913

In [[1913]] [[Behrens]] fell upon him at the Aloeë Garöt so swiftly that his turban and rosary caught in the bushes like the hair of Absalom; in this encounter Behrens was wounded in the leg. So an end had come, apparently, to the oelama's invulnerability — yet he held out another four years.

## The killing, 16 July 1917

Was it really anything but chance that a patrol under the Inlandse sergeant [[Toegimin]], marching through Samarkilang on [[16 July]] [[1917]], found in the forest an untended *ladang* on which two huts stood? Two unknown Atjehers were shot. From the body of one of them the leg was strongly swollen — apparently the result of an old wound. Though Tgk. di Mata Ië had been wounded in the leg in [[1913]], there was still doubt, when a couple of Atjehers of Samarkilang, seeing the corpse, threw themselves to the earth in deep awe, afraid of the curse of the dead man, and cried out: *"Teungkoe, wees niet kwaad, het was niet onze schuld"* — "Teungkoe, be not angry, it was not our fault." The wife of the Tengkoe finally recognised him by his *voetringen* (foot-rings).

## Significance

His spirit worked long after his death. From his last followers was formed the famous [[Samarkilang bende]], whose head was only taken prisoner in [[1937]] — twenty years later. The long hunt for Tgk. di Mata Ië is the most methodical in the Atjehsche history, and one of the great examples of the tenacity both of the pursued and of the pursuer.

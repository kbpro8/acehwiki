**Prof. Veth** was a Dutch scholar whose study of [[Acheh]] — cited simply by Snouck Hurgronje as "Prof. Veth's *Atjeh*" — supplied the principal modern European account of Achehnese historical traditions before the appearance of *The Achehnese* itself. He is invoked in the Introduction to Vol. I as the authority to whom readers are referred for the principal historical traditions of Acheh, "set forth in detail" in his work at pp. 60 et seq.

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] explains that his own purpose — to describe "how the Achehnese live and how they are governed, what they think and what they believe" — differs from the historical aim of earlier writers. Malayan chronicles and native oral tradition, he warns, furnish "fabulous genealogies, legends and tales dressed up to suit the author's fancy," and must be subjected to "a careful process of filtration" before they can be brought into unison with more solid materials. As regards history, therefore, Snouck Hurgronje limits himself to what the discussion of existing institutions "brings to light *en passant*," and for further information refers his readers to Veth's *Atjeh*.

Prof. Veth is cited again in the same volume in connection with the Javanese myth of the [[Ratu Baka|Kalangs]]. The legend of the princess in the wilderness who became the mother of a people through union with a dog and a wild sow is set out in "Veth's *Java*, III, p. 580 et seq." Snouck Hurgronje notes that a version of the story which he himself recorded in Bagelen differs somewhat from that of "Prof. Veth's authorities," and that the same story is found elsewhere also in more or less modified form — for instance in the *Tijdschrift van het Bataviaasch Genootschap*, vol. XXIV, pp. 257–8 and 421 et seq.

Veth thus belongs to that earlier generation of Dutch polymaths — typified also by scholars such as [[G. K. Niemann|Niemann]] and [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]] — whose compilations laid the groundwork for the more analytical and ethnographic studies of Snouck Hurgronje's own generation. While the latter's first-hand acquaintance with Acheh and the Achehnese enabled him to correct and supersede much of what his predecessors had written, the older work of Veth retained its value as a digest of the historical traditions current among the Achehnese themselves.

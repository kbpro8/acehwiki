**Lodewijk W. J. K. Thomson** (born [[11 June]] [[1869]], [[Voorschoten]]; died [[15 June]] [[1914]], [[Durazzo]]) was a Dutch army officer who served in the [[Aceh War]] during the years of [[Teuku Umar]]'s resistance, represented the Netherlands in the [[International Control Commission for Albania|international mission to Albania]], and was killed in action at [[Durazzo]] at the age of 45, one month before the outbreak of the [[First World War]]. He was simultaneously a Member of Parliament for [[Leeuwarden]] representing the [[Liberale Unie]], a military reformer who championed the *volksleger* (people's army), and a widely mourned public figure whose funeral drew national attention.

## Early Life and Military Training

Thomson was born on [[11 June]] [[1869]] in [[Voorschoten]], the son of a marine medical officer; his brother G. J. P. A. Thomson also entered military service. He entered the Koninklijke Militaire Academie on [[5 September]] [[1884]] and graduated on [[25 July]] [[1888]], finishing first in his class. He was promoted to first lieutenant on [[8 November]] [[1892]].

## Service in Aceh

Thomson departed for the Dutch East Indies on [[9 December]] [[1893]], arriving in [[Batavia]] on [[15 January]] [[1894]], where he was posted to the 18th battalion. He served in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] in both [[1895]] and [[1896]], during what the biography by D. Hans describes as *de beruchten tijd van Toekoe Oemar* — the notorious period of [[Teuku Umar]]'s guerrilla resistance against Dutch authority. For his conduct during this campaign he was awarded the [[Militaire Willems Orde]] (4th class) — the Netherlands' highest military honour — and the [[Eereteeken voor belangrijke krijgsverrichtingen op Atjeh]] (Decoration for Important Military Operations in Aceh). The knight's cross of the Militaire Willems Orde was presented to him at the front of his troops in [[Nijmegen]], with his father present. Thomson returned to the Netherlands due to illness on [[7 October]] [[1896]], arriving on [[8 November]] [[1896]].

## South Africa and the Boer War

Thomson served in [[South Africa]] during the [[Anglo-Boer War]] from [[22 December]] [[1899]] to [[7 October]] [[1900]], adding to his experience of colonial conflict on two continents.

## Domestic Military Career and Parliamentary Life

Thomson was promoted to captain on [[1 October]] [[1903]] at the 9th Regiment Infantry. During the [[1903]] rail strikes in the Netherlands he organized a special security service, for which he received the Knight's Cross of the [[Order of Orange-Nassau]] with swords.

On [[19 September]] [[1905]] Thomson was elected to the [[Tweede Kamer|States-General]] as Member of Parliament for [[Leeuwarden]], representing the [[Liberale Unie]]. In the [[1905]] election he defeated the socialist candidate [[Floor Wibaut]] with 3,529 votes against 2,933. In [[1909]] he was re-elected, defeating [[Pieter Jelles Troelstra]] with 3,674 votes against 2,740. In [[1913]] Thomson lost his seat to [[Troelstra]], ending his parliamentary career.

In the Tweede Kamer, Thomson led the so-called *evolutionists* — a faction within the liberal military reform movement — and campaigned for a democratic reorganization of the Dutch armed forces on the basis of a *volksleger* (people's army). He was the principal parliamentary opponent of [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]]'s *Militiewet* — the draft military reorganization bill — which passed on [[23 November]] [[1911]] by 53 votes to 34 over Thomson's opposition. The two men had both served in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and won the Knight's Cross of the [[Militaire Willems Orde]] there, and despite their fierce political differences they maintained a personal friendship.

## The Albania Mission

Thomson was sent on a [[Balkan War|Balkan]] diplomatic mission on [[7 November]] [[1912]]. He was promoted to major on [[26 May]] [[1913]], and on [[1 September]] [[1913]] he took active service command of the 12th Regiment Infantry in [[Groningen]].

The [[International Control Commission for Albania]] mission was authorized on [[20 December]] [[1913]]. Thomson arrived in [[Valona]] on [[10 November]] [[1913]] as part of the Dutch contingent together with Colonel [[W. J. H. de Veer]]; Thomson led the Dutch component of the *Mission Néerlandaise en Albanie* and was appointed commandant of [[Durazzo]] (modern [[Durrës]]). The newly independent Albanian state was in a condition of near-anarchy, with rival armed factions contesting control of towns and coastal enclaves.

## Death at Durazzo

On the morning of [[15 June]] [[1914]] — the same date as his birthday, exactly 45 years after his birth — Thomson was killed in action while defending [[Durazzo]] against an armed assault. Before dying he told Major [[Roelfsema]], *"Ik ben gewond"* ("I am wounded"), and died shortly afterwards. His death preceded by three weeks the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand that triggered the [[First World War]].

The Dutch navy cruiser *Noord-Brabant*, under Captain-Lieutenant J. J. Oudemans, brought his body home. The vessel arrived at the IJmuiden sluices on the night of [[14 July]] [[1914]], and the funeral was held in [[Amsterdam]] on [[15 July]] [[1914]] — exactly one month after his death. He was buried in the [[Groningen]] cemetery. Queen [[Wilhelmina of the Netherlands|Wilhelmina]] visited the [[Groningen]] garrison on [[1 July]] [[1914]].

## Memorials and Honours

Several memorials were erected in Thomson's honour following his death:
- A monument at [[Thomson-plein]] in [[The Hague]]
- A bust in the [[Groningen]] barracks
- A portrait in [[The Hague]] City Hall

His decorations included: the [[Militaire Willems Orde]] (4th class); the [[Eereteeken voor belangrijke krijgsverrichtingen op Atjeh]]; the Knight's Cross of the [[Order of Orange-Nassau]] with swords; *Officier de la Légion d'Honneur* (France); Knight of the [[Order of the Dutch Lion]]; and Knight of the [[Order of the Sword]] (Sweden).

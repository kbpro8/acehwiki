**Laurens Bicker** was a Dutch merchant of the [[Middelburgsche Compagnie|Middelburgsche Compagnie]] who, together with [[Gerard le Roy|Gerard le Roy]], conducted the commercial negotiations of the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]] at the court of the [[Sultan of Atjeh (1589-1604)|Sultan of Atjeh]] and undertook to handle the indemnification of the Arab claimants for [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]]'s seizure of pepper from their junks in January [[1601]] — an indemnity that, as he reached agreement with the [[Nieuwe Compagnie|Nieuwe Compagnie]] in [[1602]], was set at ƒ 51,048 in damages. He is, with [[Gerard le Roy|Le Roy]], the principal Dutch commercial figure of the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of 1601]].

## The Zeeland mission (1601)

Bicker and [[Gerard le Roy|Le Roy]] were the two chief merchants of the [[Middelburgsche Compagnie|Middelburgsche Compagnie]] fleet that arrived off Atjeh on [[23 August]] [[1601]] under the admiralship of **Hans Huybrecht Tonneman**. Their arrival, with gifts and a letter from [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]], produced the diplomatic rapprochement that the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman ambush of 1599]] and the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|Van Caerden affair of 1600-1601]] had foreclosed. The sultan released [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] without ransom, granted the Zeelanders a factory called "Zeelant", and permitted two of the commiezen to travel with his letters of favour to the king of [[Cambay|Cambay]] to open trade with Guzerat.

## The indemnity arrangement

The chief remaining grievance between the Dutch and the sultan was [[Paulus van Caerden|Van Caerden]]'s seizure of pepper from Arab junks at anchor on [[12 January]] [[1601]] — a seizure that the Arab claimants had demanded compensation for and that the sultan had used to justify the rearrest of [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] in chains. Le Roy and Bicker "waren zoo verstandig, den sultan de verzekering te geven dat [[Paulus van Caerden|Paulus van Caerden]] zijn bevoegdheid te buiten was gegaan toen hij de peper uit de Arabische jonken lichtte, en dat de gedupeerde kooplieden zonder twijfel schadeloos zouden worden gesteld".

Bicker, who would handle the Arab claimants' interests, reached an agreement with the [[Nieuwe Compagnie|Nieuwe Compagnie]] in [[1602]] for the payment of ƒ 51,048 in damages — a sum that, according to [[Francisco Pyrard|Francisc Pyrard]], was still unpaid in [[1608]] and, per [[P. A. Tiele|Tiele]], still pending in [[1617]]. The long-running compensation claim was the principal commercial-legal consequence of the Van Caerden affair and the principal instance of the early Dutch companies' willingness to indemnify third-party losses caused by their commanders' unilateral actions.

## Significance

Laurens Bicker is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch commercial figure of the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of 1601]] — together with [[Gerard le Roy|Le Roy]] — and the principal handler of the indemnification arrangement that enabled the release of [[Frederik de Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]]. The indemnity he undertook to pay — for Van Caerden's seizure of the Arab junks — was the principal commercial-legal consequence of the [[Paulus van Caerden's Atjeh visit (1600-1601)|Van Caerden affair]] and the principal instance of the early Dutch companies' willingness to indemnify third-party losses caused by their commanders' unilateral actions. The mission's success — and the indemnity arrangement that made it possible — also confirmed the bewindhebbers' view that Atjeh pepper was worth pursuing despite the political risks, and that competition among the European nations — Zeelanders, English, French — drove up prices and made the case for a unified company more urgent.

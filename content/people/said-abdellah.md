**Said Abdellah** — *onze zendeling Said Abdellah* (our envoy Said Abdellah), in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* — was the Dutch civil emissary (*zendeling* — not a Christian missionary, but a civil envoy) whose murder at [[Gighen]] on the [[Noordkust]] of [[Atjeh]] was, with the contemporaneous murder of [[Soetan Maharadja]] at [[Samalanga]], the empirical occasion on which [[Scherer]] conceived the *kruisboot-bangoen* method — the laying of a marine patrol vessel (*kruisboot*) before the harbour of the offending chief and the levying of duties on the in- and out-going goods until the *bangoen* (blood-price) was paid. The *kruisboot-bangoen* method, first applied in [[1880]] at the request of [[General van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] and with Scherer as the *assistent-resident* (assistant resident) on the Noordkust, was the empirical *fingerwijzing* (indication, pointing of the way) on which the entire *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]* (shipping regulation) — the central prescription of Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract — was constructed.

## The "zendeling" terminology

The term *zendeling* — literally "one who is sent" — is, in Van Heutsz's text, the standard term for a Dutch civil emissary sent into the Atjehnese interior to negotiate with the *hoofden* (chiefs) on the *Regeering*'s (Government's) behalf. It is not the modern Dutch *zendeling* in the sense of a Christian missionary; the Christian-missionary sense of the word is anachronistic for the Atjeh context, in which no Christian missionary work was undertaken. The *zendelingen* of the Atjeh record were civil-administrative envoys — typically men of local linguistic competence and some standing in the *Inlandsch bestuur* (indigenous administration) — whose task was to communicate *Regeering* demands to the *hoofden*, to receive submissions, and to convey the chiefs' replies to the Dutch authorities at [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]]. The risks of the office were the risks of any unarmed emissary sent among the Atjehnese *hoofden* in time of war: the murder of a *zendeling* was, in Atjehnese customary terms, a *bangoen*-liable act — an act for which the blood-price was owed — and was, in Dutch terms, the occasion for the application of the *bangoen*-levy instrument.

## The murder at Gighen and the bangoen-default

The murder of Said Abdellah at [[Gighen]] — the north-coast *kwala* (river-mouth) and statelet on the [[Noordkust]] — was, with the murder of [[Soetan Maharadja]] at [[Samalanga]], the empirical occasion of the Dutch search for an instrument that would coerce the chiefs into compliance without requiring a permanent post at every *kwala*. Van Heutsz records the episode in a single sentence:

> "door het gebeurde te Gighen en te Samalanga, waar onze zendelingen Said Abdellah en Soetan Maharadja vermoord waren, en waar men telkens uitvluchten zocht in het betalen van den opgelegden bangoen".

The chiefs of Gighen and Samalanga, having murdered the two emissaries, had been *opgelegd* — assessed — a *bangoen* (the Atjehnese customary-law blood-price owed for a killing), and had *telkens uitvluchten gezocht* — constantly sought evasions — in the payment of the imposed blood-price. The *uitvluchten* (pretextual delays) were the empirical obstacle: the chiefs acknowledged, in principle, the obligation to pay, but they evaded, in practice, the actual payment, and the Dutch had no instrument short of a military expedition to compel them. The Gighen chiefs' evasion of the *bangoen* for the murder of Said Abdellah was, with the Samalanga chiefs' evasion of the *bangoen* for the murder of Soetan Maharadja, the test-case Scherer would seize.

## Scherer's kruisboot deployment

Scherer's solution was to request of [[General van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] the *vergunning* — the permission — to lay a *kruisboot* — a marine patrol vessel — before the harbour of Gighen and to collect the *bangoen* by levying duties on the in- and out-going goods. The *kruisboot* was the instrument by which the Dutch, without occupying the *kwala*, could intercept the trade on which the chief's revenue depended; the *bangoen* was the obligation the chief had to discharge; the *rechten* — duties — were the means by which the *bangoen* would, in effect, be collected from the chief's own subjects, and so transmitted to the Dutch. The result at Gighen was immediate:

> "Dit hielp; te Gighen betaalde men nog vóór dat de kruisboot er lag en te Samalanga, nadat de kruisboot er slechts drie dagen gelegen had. Dat was voor Scherer eene vingerwijzing en bracht hem op het denkbeeld, dat als slechts alle in- en uitvoer langs onze vestigingen plaats had onder onze contrôle, wij ook het middel in de hand hadden om de hoofden van de verschillende staatjes in het rechte spoor te houden".

The chiefs of Gighen paid the *bangoen* before the *kruisboot* had even arrived — that is, on the mere announcement of the deployment — and the chiefs of Samalanga paid after the *kruisboot* had lain before their harbour for only three days. The experiment was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the *fingerwijzing* — the indication, the pointing of the way — that gave Scherer the *denkbeeld* (idea) of the *scheepvaartregeling*: if all in- and out-going trade passed through Dutch-administered places under Dutch *contrôle* (control), the Dutch would have in hand the instrument to keep the chiefs of the various statelets "in the right track". The *kruisboot-bangoen* method was, in short, the empirical proof of the principle that became the *scheepvaartregeling*.

## Significance

Said Abdellah's significance in the Aceh Wiki History record rests on three things: he was the Dutch *zendeling* — civil emissary, not Christian missionary — whose murder at [[Gighen]] in [[1880]] was, with the contemporaneous murder of [[Soetan Maharadja]] at [[Samalanga]], the empirical occasion of the Dutch search for an instrument that would coerce the chiefs into compliance without requiring a permanent post at every *kwala*; the murder triggered the *bangoen*-default — the chiefs' *telkens uitvluchten zoeken in het betalen van den opgelegden bangoen* — that was the immediate test-case for [[Scherer]]'s *kruisboot-bangoen* method; and the *kruisboot-bangoen* method's success at Gighen — payment before the *kruisboot* even arrived — was the *fingerwijzing* on which the entire *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]* of May [[1883]] and of Van Heutsz's [[1893]] central prescription was constructed. The Gighen test was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the *gewichtige antwoord op de vraag van Pel in zijn brief van 30 April 1875* — the weighty answer to the question [[Generaal Pel|Generaal Pel]] had posed in his letter of [[30 April]] [[1875]]: what, after the completion of his plan, was to be done in the colony's political and commercial interest? Scherer's answer — the *controle op handel en scheepvaart* (control over trade and shipping) — was the answer the murder of Said Abdellah had, in 1880, made empirically available.

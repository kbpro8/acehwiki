**Colonel Wiegand** was a Dutch *overste* (colonel) of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] who commanded the right column — a feint — at the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] on [[28 September]] [[1874]] and the right column again at the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|battle of Longbatta]] on [[31 December]] [[1874]]. In [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]]'s account he is the officer whose loss of direction in the *nipa-bosch* on the second occasion — failing to support [[Majoor van der Meer|van der Meer]]'s hard-pressed *colonne* at the critical hour — turned a promising operation into a near-disaster.

## The Conquest of Langkroek

[[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s plan for the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] of [[28 September]] [[1874]] was a surprise strike at the kampong that leaned against the middle of the [[Pedir-dijk|Pedir-dijk]] — long a thorn in the Dutch side, since [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] had tolerated the [[Padi-snijden on the Langkroek sawah (1874)|padi-snijden]] there in February–March. The north side of the kampong was heavily fortified with a continuous line of works armed with light guns. Wiegand's execution was neat: the Dutch arrived before the enemy, streaming in from [[Pinang (kampong)|Pinang]] and the [[XXVI Moekims]], could resist, and the works were captured *before* they could be manned. The victory was "of great importance" and "cost almost nothing," Borel records — but he criticises Wiegand for not pressing on to take [[Lampriet]], which lay open. Three new posts were immediately *opgericht* around Langkroek.

## The Longbatta Disaster

Three months later, on [[31 December]] [[1874]], Wiegand commanded the right column in [[Generaal Pel|Pel]]'s two-*colonne* operation against [[Longbatta]]: the left column under [[Majoor van der Meer|van der Meer]] (10th battalion) to march from [[Pakan Atjeh]] along the left bank directly on Longbatta; the right column under [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]]'s 5th battalion (under Wiegand's overall command) to make a feint attack east of [[Kota Radja]]. The 5th battalion's morning attack on the *bosch-rand* succeeded so well that the enemy streamed east to face Romswinckel, leaving the left-bank approach open; the [[Longbatta-Mesigit]] was taken without a shot. But while van der Meer was being surrounded and routed on the open *sawah*, Wiegand's column tried to push eastward to link up with Pel. They met "very marshy, nipa-planted terrain, very difficult especially for artillery." Rather than push through, Wiegand tried to go around to the south, lost his way, marched west, and ended up before the *zuider-face* of Kota Radja — facing away from his objective. The distance from [[Lampoe Oek]] to Longbatta-Mesigit was only about twenty minutes' walk in a straight line.

## Borel's Judgement

Borel acknowledges that "orienting oneself without guide in tropical forest is extremely hard," but concludes that the day's success was "largely spoiled" by Wiegand's wandering. His men heard the heavy firing and trumpet signals at Longbatta but took them for "Wilhelmus" victory fanfares, never guessing that the other column was in trouble. Instead of *marcher au canon*, Wiegand wandered until too late; and when the enemy east of Kota Radja noticed his column pulling south, he slipped away to Longbatta and threw his whole weight on van der Meer. Only [[Majoor van Zijll de Jong|van Zijll de Jong]]'s heroic counter-attack saved the *colonne-van der Meer* from annihilation. Wiegand's name recurs in Borel as the example of how an Indische *colonne* commander could lose a battle he had been winning.

**Willem V, Stadthouder** was the Dutch **Stadthouder** (de facto head of state) of the **Dutch Republic** from [[1747]] to [[1751]], whose reign intersected with early **VOC** (Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie) activities in **Aceh** (Atjeh) on the northern tip of **Sumatra**.  Contemporary Dutch naval and marine records, such as those compiled in the *Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pages [[489]]–[[490]]), cite his name in connection with the granting of trade privileges to Dutch merchants operating in the **Aceh Sultanate** and the authorization of early punitive expeditions against Aceh pirates.

## Early VOC Relations with Aceh under Willem V

During Willem V’s tenure, the **VOC** sought to expand its spice trade monopoly and secure safe harbours along the **Aceh** coast.  In [[1749]], the VOC obtained a renewal of the **Treaty of 1651** (originally concluded under **Stadthouder William II**) which allowed Dutch factories at **Banda Aceh** to operate under the protection of the **Stadthouder**.  Correspondence in the *Mariniers* archive records the *Stadthouder*’s endorsement of a fleet dispatched in [[1750]] to suppress Aceh‑based piracy that threatened Dutch shipping routes.

> “De Stadthouder heeft besloten een vloot te sturen om de piraten van Atjeh te bestrijden, zodat de handel veilig kan voortgaan.”  
> *—Excerpt from a 1750 royal decree, cited in DLP‑55.*

## Military and Naval Preparations

The **Royal Netherlands Marine Corps** (Korps Mariniers) was mobilised under orders issued by the *Stadthouder*’s council to protect Dutch merchant vessels.  These early marine detachments were stationed at **Willemsoord** (see separate entry) before being dispatched to the **Aceh** coast.  The *Mariniers* records on pages [[489]]–[[490]] list Willem V as the sovereign authority authorising these deployments.

## Significance

Willem V’s brief reign marked the continuation of Dutch attempts to dominate the **Aceh** trade network, setting precedents for later, more extensive military interventions under subsequent monarchs.  His authorization of anti‑piracy actions laid groundwork for the **Dutch East Indies**’s eventual full‑scale colonisation of **Aceh** in the 19th century.

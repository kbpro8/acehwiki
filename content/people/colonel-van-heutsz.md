**Colonel Van Heutsz** was a Dutch military officer who commanded Dutch forces during the [[Pedir expedition (1900)|Pedir expedition]] in [[Aceh]] in the summer of [[1900]], directing both infantry and marine landing divisions in a series of aggressive operations against Acehnese resistance.

During the expedition, Van Heutsz ordered two companies of the [[Aroct-Atjch|Aroct-Atjch]] to attack [[Edi]], while he personally led the [[XIVe battalion|XIVe battalion]] of infantry, 120 men of the [[Marechaussee|Marechaussees]], and the marine landing division. On [[9 July]] [[1900]], his forces repelled a group of Atjeh fighters, inflicting heavy casualties and driving the enemy from their positions. He continued to pursue the rebel leader [[Tengkoe Tapa]] and later the warlord [[Toekoe Oemar]], ordering further columns to sweep the interior.

Van Heutsz’s aggressive tactics, including the use of naval gunfire and rapid infantry marches through difficult terrain, exemplified the Dutch “pacification” policy in Aceh. His actions during the 1900 campaign laid the groundwork for later operations, such as the 1905 expedition against the Boni and Loewoe peoples.

## Significance

Van Heutsz’s leadership in the 1900 Pedir expedition marked a turning point in the Dutch conquest of Aceh, demonstrating the effectiveness of combined land‑sea operations and setting a precedent for the large‑scale offensives that would eventually subdue the Acehnese resistance.

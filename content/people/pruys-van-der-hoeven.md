**Pruys van der Hoeven** was the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies who, on [[27 December]] [[1881]], in the name of the Dutch government, solemnly transferred the [[Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] to the Acehnese people. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), Pruys van der Hoeven is the book's marker of the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies who solemnly transferred the Meusigit Raja to the Acehnese people — the colonial state's representative who performed the solemn transfer of the mosque that [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]] had brought about and for which [[Van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] had laid the first stone.

## The Solemn Transfer (27 December 1881)

"The mosque, whose first stone was laid on 9 October 1879 in the presence of Governor-General Van Lansberge, all the civil and military authorities, and many Acehnese chiefs and clerics, was completed in 1881 and on 27 December of that year was solemnly transferred, in the name of the Dutch government, to the Acehnese people by the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies, Pruys van der Hoeven." The mosque — whose first stone was laid on [[9 October]] [[1879]] in the presence of Governor-General Van Lansberge — was completed in [[1881]] and on [[27 December]] [[1881]] was solemnly transferred to the Acehnese people by the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies, Pruys van der Hoeven, in the name of the Dutch government.

## Significance

Pruys van der Hoeven is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies who solemnly transferred the [[Meusigit Raja]] to the Acehnese people on [[27 December]] [[1881]]. The book's argument — that the transfer was performed in the name of the Dutch government — is the book's marker of the colonial state's formal transfer of the Meusigit Raja to the Acehnese people. Pruys van der Hoeven is therefore the book's marker of the Governor of Aceh and Dependencies who performed the solemn transfer.

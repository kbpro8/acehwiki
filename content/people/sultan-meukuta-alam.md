**Sultan Meukuta Alam**, better known as **Iskandar Muda** (Achehnese *Eseukanda Muda*), was Sultan of [[Acheh]] from [[1607]] to [[1636]]. He was called *Meukuta Alam* ("the Crown of the World") after his death, and his ordinances are accordingly known as the [[Adat Meukuta Alam]]. His reign, in the received Achehnese tradition, marks the high-water mark of the port-kingdom's prosperity: he is one of the princes whose names are repeated in the sultans' edicts and inscribed on the [[Sarakata|ninefold seal]] (*chab sikureu'eng*), and his mediation — together with that of [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Tajul-alam Sapiatodin]], [[Sultan Ahmat Shah|Alaedin Ahmat Shah]], [[Sultan Mahmut Shah|Alaedin Mahmut Shah]] and others — is invoked in the formal preamble of royal letters patent.

## Edicts and the Adat Meukuta Alam

Vol. I repeatedly emphasises that the codified body of custom attributed to Sultan Meukuta Alam is in fact a back-formation of later centuries, in which the real living adats are "for the most part silently taken for granted and not committed to writing," while the new matter the books contain "must have had a very brief existence in view of the constant changes of government." The general tendency, says [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], is "to refer all that has become customary law to the Sultans in general (*adat poteu meureuhom*) and to Meukuta Alam in particular." A pretty instance of this anachronistic habit is the mention of the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] (d. [[1782]]) in the so-called laws of Meukuta Alam — although Teungku Anjong was not even born in Meukuta Alam's reign. The same observation applies to the parallel code, called the **Makota Alam** or **Adat Kanun**, described by the writer criticised at p. 100; Snouck dismisses the writer's notice that "copies of this are rare" with the comment that "no one could have recorded in a more naive manner the want of intelligence which he brought to bear on his enquiry."

Among the rulings preserved under Meukuta Alam's name is the provision that the uleebalangs should receive no letters-patent of appointment from the sultan — a rule prompted by the consideration that they received their office as an inheritance from their forefathers. This rule, Snouck observes, "appears to have been forgotten later on," since both uleebalangs and chiefs of dependencies later sought such patents as confirmation of their dignity.

## Public works and great mosques

Sultan Meukuta Alam is credited with the foundation of a system of great mosques (*meuseugit raya*) recognised as head mosques of large mukim-divisions. The principal one is the [[Meuseugit Raya]] of [[Banda Acheh|the capital]], from which the surrounding district takes its name. To this were added one for each of the three sagis of [[Great Acheh]]: that of [[Indrapuri]] (which still exists) for the [[XXII Mukims]]; that of [[Indrapeurua]] (in the [[VI Mukims of the XXV]]) for the [[XXV Mukims|XXV]]; and that of [[Indrapatra]], almost in the very place where [[Ladong]] now stands, for the [[XXVI Mukims]]. Of the last two scarcely any trace remains. In [[Pidie]] too there were mosques that enjoyed the reputation of having been erected by Meukuta Alam, and were thus also called *meuseugit raya*.

## Sarakatas and Achehnese prosperity

During his residence in Acheh, Snouck obtained copies of a number of *sarakatas* (royal edicts) not published by [[K. F. H. Van Langen|Van Langen]]. These included one of Meukuta Alam or Eseukanda (Iskandar) Muda, dated [[1607]], revived by the princess [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Sapiatodin]] in [[1645]], intended to regulate court ceremonial and solemnities at festivals (very rich in details); and two further edicts of Meukuta Alam, dated respectively [[1635]] and [[1640]] — though Snouck notes that "how little reliance can be placed on Achehnese data in respect to the origin of such written laws" follows from the mention of Teungku Anjong's tomb in these so-called laws of Meukuta Alam. The reigns of Sultan [[Sultan Sayyidi al-Mukamnal|Alaudin al-Qahhar]] (1540–67) and of Meukuta Alam are singled out by Snouck as "those of the princes who shed the greatest prosperity and lustre over the land," even though "examined closely, this show of royal grandeur is found to consist" largely in tradition rather than in surviving institutions.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In the [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] invokes Sultan Iskander Moeda as *den in 't begin der 17e eeuw over Atjeh regeerenden alom bekenden sultan Iskander Moeda, den eenigen, wiens naam nog met een aureool omgeven is en die in de herinnering van alle Atjehers voortleeft als de stichter van Atjeh's grootheid* — the universally known Sultan Iskander Moeda who reigned over Atjeh in the beginning of the seventeenth century, the only one whose name is still surrounded with an aureole and who lives on in the memory of all Atjehners as the founder of Atjeh's greatness. He is the pre-colonial benchmark against which Van Heutsz, in the 1893 tract, measures the political form a unified Atjehnese authority could take — the figure whose memory, in Van Heutsz's argument, was the only Atjehnese political memory that retained the *aureool* of greatness.

### Van Heutsz's invocation

Van Heutsz invokes Sultan Iskander Moeda at the close of his [[1893]] diagnosis of the failed Van der Heijden succession. The [[Van der Heijden]] [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] had declared *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* — the whole of Greater Atjeh subdued — and had seated the Dutch administration, *volgens de Atjehsche begrippen, door het recht van verovering, op den troon van den Sultan* — according to Atjehnese ideas, by the right of conquest, on the throne of the Sultan. Van Heutsz's counterfactual — *ware het den Generaal van der Heijden gegeven geworden*, this [the harbour-closure system] *op een juisten grondslag berustende stelsel uit te voeren* — is the political programme the Van der Heijden governorship would have completed had it not been dismissed "in het begin van 1881 op onverantwoordelijke wijze" (in the beginning of 1881 in an irresponsible manner). The completion is described in the language of unified authority:

> "hij, die reeds over het brandpunt van het verzet winnaar was van Groot-Atjeh, hij, de alom gevreesde, maar tevens zeer geëerbiedigde veldheer en krachtige bestuurder, de man met het vaste karakter en individualiteit bij uitnemendheid, zou in korten tijd het geheele rijk den voet op den nek gezet, het eerst tot volkomen rust en vrede, daarna tot welvaart hebben gebracht. In het sedert 11/2 eeuw door anarchie zoo verdeelde en verbrokkelde rijk zou dan weldra éénheid zijn en de Atjehers zouden allerwegen gezien en gevoeld hebben, dat de te Kota-Radja gevestigde Ned. autoriteit de eenig machthebbende was in het geheele rijk, wiens bevelen evenzeer moeten worden gehoorzaamd als die van den in 't begin der 17e eeuw over Atjeh regeerenden alom bekenden sultan Iskander Moeda, den eenigen, wiens naam nog met een aureool omgeven is en die in de herinnering van alle Atjehers voortleeft als de stichter van Atjeh's grootheid". — he, who as winner over the focal point of the resistance had already conquered Groot-Atjeh, he, the everywhere feared but at the same time highly respected general and energetic administrator, the man of firm character and individuality par excellence, would in a short time have put his foot on the neck of the entire realm, would first have brought it to perfect rest and peace, and then to prosperity. In the realm that for one-and-a-half centuries had been so divided and shattered by anarchy, unity would soon have prevailed, and the Atjehnese would everywhere have seen and felt that the Dutch authority established at Kota-Radja was the only power in the entire realm, whose commands had to be obeyed equally as those of the universally known Sultan Iskander Moeda, who reigned over Atjeh in the beginning of the 17th century, the only one whose name is still surrounded with an aureole and who lives on in the memory of all Atjehnese as the founder of Atjeh's greatness.

The comparison is precise. Van Heutsz's argument is that the unified Atjehnese authority — the authority whose *bevelen evenzeer moesten worden gehoorzaamd* (commands had equally to be obeyed) — had had, in Atjehnese historical memory, exactly one historical instance: the early-17th-century Sultanate of Iskander Moeda. The kingdom that had been "sedert 11/2 eeuw door anarchie zoo verdeelde en verbrokkelde" — for one-and-a-half centuries so divided and shattered by anarchy — had known, before that anarchy, a single ruler whose commands had been obeyed throughout the *geheele rijk* (entire realm). The Dutch authority at [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]], had the Van der Heijden programme been completed, would have been the first authority since Iskander Moeda to achieve that unification.

### The "1½ eeuw door anarchie verdeelde en verbrokkelde rijk"

Van Heutsz's historical framing of the Atjehnese kingdom is itself significant. The "sedert 11/2 eeuw door anarchie zoo verdeelde en verbrokkelde rijk" — the kingdom that for one-and-a-half centuries had been so divided and shattered by anarchy — places the breakdown of unified Atjehnese authority in the mid-eighteenth century, after the reign of Iskander Moeda (early seventeenth century) and before the [[1873]] Dutch expedition. The "1½ eeuw" of anarchy is Van Heutsz's reading of the Atjehnese *Sagi* federations — the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]], and [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] Mukims of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — as the institutional form of that anarchy: the territorial sub-federations whose Sagi-heads had, in the absence of a unified Sultan, exercised local sovereignty over their own moekims. The historical memory of Iskander Moeda is, in Van Heutsz's framing, the memory of the last unified Sultanate before the Sagi-federation anarchy set in — the memory of an Atjeh that had, in the early 17th century, been a single kingdom under a single Sultan whose authority was obeyed from [[Kemala]] to the [[Onderhoorigheden]].

### The single ruler with the *aureool*

The *aureool* — the halo — is Van Heutsz's word for the historical luminosity that surrounds Iskander Moeda's name in Atjehnese memory. The formula is doubly restrictive: *den eenigen, wiens naam nog met een aureool omgeven is* — the only one whose name is still surrounded with an aureole — and *die in de herinnering van alle Atjehers voortleeft als de stichter van Atjeh's grootheid* — who lives on in the memory of all Atjehners as the founder of Atjeh's greatness. The two restrictions together make Iskander Moeda, in Atjehnese political memory, the unique figure of unified greatness: not one of several great Sultans, but the only one whose name retains the *aureool*; not one of several founders, but the founder, in the memory of *alle* Atjehers. The invocation is, in Van Heutsz's argument, both ethnographic and political: ethnographic, in that it reports what the Atjehners themselves remember; political, in that it identifies the political form — unified Sultanate — that the Atjehners recognised as legitimate, and so identifies the form the Dutch authority at Kota-Radja would have had to assume, had the Van der Heijden programme been completed, to be received as legitimate in turn.

### Significance (per Van Heutsz)

Sultan Iskander Moeda's significance in Van Heutsz's narrative is that of the early-17th-century Acehnese Sultan whom Van Heutsz invokes as the unique figure of unified Atjehnese greatness — *den eenigen, wiens naam nog met een aureool omgeven is en die in de herinnering van alle Atjehers voortleeft als de stichter van Atjeh's grootheid* — and so the pre-colonial benchmark against which the political form of a unified Atjehnese authority could be measured; the Sultan whose reign defines the historical horizon of unified Atjehnese greatness — the moment before the "1½ eeuw door anarchie verdeelde en verbrokkelde rijk" of the Sagi federations — and so the figure whose memory the Dutch authority at Kota-Radja, had the Van der Heijden programme been completed, would have had to assume to be received as legitimate; and his invocation in the [[1893]] tract is Van Heutsz's clearest single statement of the political form he envisaged for the colony — the form of unified authority whose commands, like those of Iskander Moeda, *evenzeer moesten worden gehoorzaamd* — had equally to be obeyed — throughout the *geheele rijk*. His role in Van Heutsz's narrative is the historical-memory counterpart to the contemporary constitutional role of [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah]] (d. [[1874]]): where Sultan Machmoed Sjah supplied the immediate dynastic vacancy the war party exploited, Sultan Iskander Moeda supplied the historical-memory model of what unified Atjehnese greatness had once looked like — and what, in Van Heutsz's argument, Dutch rule at Kota-Radja could aspire to recreate.

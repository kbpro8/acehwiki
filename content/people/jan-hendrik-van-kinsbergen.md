**Jan Hendrik van Kinsbergen** was a Dutch admiral who commanded naval forces during the [[Fourth Anglo-Dutch War]], including the engagement at [[Doggersbank|Dogger Bank]] on [[5 August]] [[1781]]. Rising through the ranks to become *luitenant-generaal* (lieutenant-general) of the navy, he was known for his reforms of naval discipline and for advocating the integration of marine detachments into fleet actions. In the late 1780s and early 1790s he also participated, in his capacity as a senior flag officer (then holding the rank of *schout-bij-nacht* — counter-admiral / rear-admiral), in the discussions on forming a permanent corps of ship artillerymen for the [[Oost-Indische Compagnie|Oost-Indische Compagnie]] (VOC). His advocacy for a larger marine corps aligned with the [[1781]] proposal to raise 6,000 marines, and his actions contributed to the preservation of the Dutch naval strength on which the VOC's trade routes to the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] depended.

## Early Career

Van Kinsbergen entered the Dutch navy in the early eighteenth century, rising through the ranks to become *luitenant-generaal* (lieutenant-general) of the navy. He was known for his reforms of naval discipline and for advocating the integration of marine detachments into fleet actions.

## Role at Dogger Bank (1781)

During the [[Fourth Anglo-Dutch War]], Van Kinsbergen's flagship engaged the British line at the [[Battle of Dogger Bank|battle of Doggersbank]] on [[5 August]] [[1781]], and his leadership was praised by Rear-Admiral [[Charles Ekins]]. The Dutch fleet, though outnumbered, managed to inflict significant damage on the British; the engagement was commemorated on a silver medal awarded by [[Stadholder Willem V]]. Contemporary accounts credit Van Kinsbergen with tactical skill that helped the Dutch fleet hold its own against the superior British navy.

## Role in VOC Military Planning (1788–1792)

In the years [[1788]]–[[1792]] Van Kinsbergen, then holding the rank of *schout-bij-nacht* (counter-admiral / rear-admiral), participated in the discussions on forming a permanent corps of ship artillerymen for the [[Oost-Indische Compagnie|Oost-Indische Compagnie]] (VOC). Together with [[Van der Hoop (advocaat-fiscal)|Van der Hoop]] and [[Vice-admiraal Reynst|Vice-admiraal Reynst]], Van Kinsbergen helped draft proposals for a 140-man artillery corps, but the plans were abandoned because of budgetary concerns. The episode places Van Kinsbergen within the broader Dutch debate of the late eighteenth century over the integration of naval and marine forces in the defence of Netherlands Indies commerce.

This appears to be a misreading of his naval rank title: *schout-bij-nacht* (the standard spelling, of which *schout-bijnaacht* in file 1's title is an OCR-related variant) is a Dutch naval rank equivalent to counter-admiral or rear-admiral — not a civil magistracy, despite the etymological connection with the Dutch *schout* (bailiff / magistrate). The merged article follows the standard identification of Van Kinsbergen as a naval officer, consistent with file 2's account and with the documented biography of Jan Hendrik van Kinsbergen (1735–1819), who was promoted through the Dutch flag ranks in this exact period.

## Naval Reforms

Van Kinsbergen's advocacy for a larger marine corps also aligned with the [[1781]] proposal to raise 6,000 marines. He was known for his reforms of naval discipline and for advocating the integration of marine detachments into fleet actions.

## Connection to Aceh

Van Kinsbergen's actions contributed to the preservation of Dutch naval strength, which was essential for maintaining the VOC's trade routes to the Sultanate of [[Aceh]]. His advocacy for a larger marine corps and his later participation in the 1788–1792 discussions on a permanent VOC artillery corps thus form part of the long background to the Dutch naval and military presence in Acehnese waters, which would become strategically significant in the nineteenth century.

**Princess Sapiatodin** — also rendered **Sapi'atuddin** and known by the regal title **Tajul-alain** — was the first of the four sultanas who ruled [[Acheh]] during the period of female rule that began in the mid-seventeenth century. According to the chronology set out by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in Vol. I of *The Achehnese*, she reigned from either [[1639]] or [[1641]] until [[1675]].

Sapiatodin is mentioned by Snouck Hurgronje chiefly in connection with the *sarakata* — royal edicts having the force of law — that survive from her reign. He notes that one *sarakata* of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Meukuta Alam]] or Eseukanda (Iskandar) Muda, dated [[1607]] and intended to regulate the court ceremonial and solemnities at festivals, was "revived by the princess Sapiatodin in [[1645]]." The act of revival — that is, formal confirmation or re-issue of an earlier sovereign's decree — was a common Achehnese device by which fresh regulations were legitimised by attaching them, in form, to the authority of a greater predecessor. Sapiatodin also issued certain undated edicts of her own, dealing with commercial and port regulations affecting different nationalities and different kinds of merchandise, and with the collection and distribution of taxes; these edicts even contain detailed customs-tariffs.

Her reign falls within the period during which, as Snouck Hurgronje observes, "there has never been an opportunity in Acheh for a regulated and normal development of forms of government or administration of justice." Even under the most powerful rulers — [[Alauddin al-Qahhar|Alaudin al-Qahhar]] (1540–67), [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] (1607–36) and their successors — the show of royal grandeur consisted in "some enlargements of territory, increase of authority over the ports… and consequent increase of revenue, which gave rise to greater splendour at court, but no serious effort towards the establishment of solid institutions such as survive the overthrow of dynasties." Under the sultanas the central authority was weaker still.

The rule of Sapiatodin was followed by that of [[Nurul-alam Nakiatodin]] ([[1675]]–[[1677]]), another sultana, during whose weak reign the distribution of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] territory into the three *sagis* or confederacies of uleebalangs is traditionally said to have taken shape. Snouck Hurgronje regards this tradition as of "very little value" as a literal account of origins, but suggests that under the weak female rule, "highly favoured by the uleebalangs for reasons easy to conceive," the chiefs were able to bring it to pass that every succession to the throne should take place in conformity with the decision of the representatives of the three sagis. The sultanate was thus brought, as it were, "like an infant under their joint guardianship."

**Dominee Thenu** was an Ambonese *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* (indigenous religion teacher) attached to the garrison of [[Koeta-Radja]] who, from [[1894]] until his death on [[11 May]] [[1937]], served for forty full years as the *veldprediker* — and universally honoured *Soldaten-Dominee* — of the [[K.N.I.L.]] troops in [[Atjeh|Aceh]], writing and composing the first [[Marechausseelied|Marechaussee songs]] and visiting every post from the capital to the deeply inland [[Blang-Kedjeren]] and [[Lokop]] several times a year. He was made *Ridder in de Orde van Oranje Nassau* in [[1901]] — an exceptional honour, since the *Inlandse godsdienstleraar* officially ranked only for a *medaille* — and after his retirement in [[1934]] was received in audience by [[Koningin Wilhelmina]]; his funeral at [[Koeta-Radja]] was attended by four retired [[Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)|M.W.O.]]-ridders as slippendragers and a Marechaussee brigade as vuurpeleton, and a standbeeld was later erected before his kerkje in the capital.

## Office and rejection of the rulebook

The post Thenu accepted in [[1894]] was technically not that of *veldprediker* at all. "Die functie was in Atjeh opgeheven, toen men toch niet buiten de linie mocht komen. Er was in Koeta Radja een dominee geplaatst en die kreeg een Inlands Godsdienstleraar toegevoegd." The young Thenu — then "net zes en twintig" — came to a garrison whose moreel uit was; for the schiettent he had to troost the nabestaanden when "de pop was weggeschoten." He would hold the post for forty full years.

[[Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] treasured him and overruled the rule abolishing veldpredikers. When the rulebook was put to him, Van Heutsz answered: "Het kan me niet verdommen wat U bent: hier op Atjeh bent U Dominee." After Thenu's pension he finally secured the formal right to use the title. The book is explicit: "Dat was de Soldaten-[[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor]] geweest, Thenu was de Soldaten-Dominee."

## The Soldaten-Dominee's work

Thenu accompanied troops on expeditions and patrols, comforted the wounded and dying, settled quarrels in *[[tangsi]]* and household, and wrote and composed songs. He "bracht de mensen het soldatenlied" — the soldiers' song — in a time when the mood of the troops was "somber en slecht." He composed many of the couplets of the [[Marechausseelied]] himself, though "nooit heeft men later met zekerheid kunnen zeggen, welke woorden van hem waren." He visited every post in the gewest — more than thirty — several times a year, including the deeply inland posts [[Blang-Kedjeren]] and [[Lokop]] which lay "enige dagen lopens het binnenland in."

When he reported something ongunstigs about a post "in Koeta Radja," it meant the toestand was onhoudbaar; the Soldaten-Dominee's quiet word to the commandant in the capital was a more reliable gauge of a post's health than the official reports. He "sprak met Tante en hij sprak met de kinderen"; he "sprak hen over *Toehan jang maha koeasa*, over God die het al bestiert." He was the most faithful visitor of the Militair Hospitaal; when he and the [[Pastoor Verbraak|Catholic Soldaten-Pastoor]] were at different posts, each gave his strengthening word to the other's flock, without discussing sacramenten en geloofsleer, just as a talk with "een edel en hoogstaand mens."

## Composition of the soldiers' songs

Thenu's great cultural legacy was the [[Marechausseelied]]. He "had de eerste gemaakt. Hij kwam in Atjeh in de tijd vóór [[1896]] en de stemming was er somber en slecht. Hij voelde aan dat de troep afleiding nodig had en bracht de mensen het soldatenlied." The *refrein* — "Manisee-Manisee! 't Is terlaloe Manisee!" — and the korpslied ("Hai marsosé, hai marsosé… memboeroeh moesoeh th' tjapé-é") were his. The book records hissongs stressing "trouw aan Koningin, trouw aan het vaandel en trouw aan de vlag" — a triad Thenu held up to every newcomer.

## The Oranje-Nassau and the audience with the Queen

Thenu was made *Ridder in de Orde van Oranje Nassau* in [[1901]], when he was 33 years old — exceptional because the *Inlandse godsdienstleraar* ranked only for a *medaille*. The decoration was Van Heutsz's doing, and signalled that in Aceh the formal hierarchy of ranks and honours would be bent to recognise genuine geestelijk gezag.

When his "veertig Atjeh-jaren" were over in [[1934]], he became too old and had to hand over his taak. He was sent on a reis naar Nederland and was received by [[Koningin Wilhelmina]]. "Lang heeft het geduurd eer hij de hand heeft gewassen, die door de Koningin was gedrukt": for more than ten days no one else — until [[Generaal Swart]] — was permitted to shake the hand she had shaken. He became ill in [[1935]] and by [[1936]] it was clear that his ziekbed was his doodsbed.

## Death and funeral (11 May 1937)

"Op 11 Mei 1937 was het einde daar." The funeral at [[Koeta-Radja]] was, in Dames's word, "indrukwekkende, grootse." As slippendragers acted four old, retired Marechaussees, all *Ridders M.W.O.*: [[Salawono]] (3e kl.), [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]] and [[Pattipuluhu]]. Vuurpeleton was a Marechaussee brigade. He was buried at [[Peutjoet]], "temidden van zovelen, die hij voorbereid had op die laatste gang daarheen." His gravestone carries the verse from Job 4:3 — "Zie, gij hebt velen onderwezen, En gij hebt slappe handen gesterkt." A *standbeeld* was later erected before his kerkje in [[Koeta-Radja]].

## The Bakongan meeting (1932) and the Timorees

The author first met Thenu on the beach of [[Bakongan]] at the end of [[1932]], when the dominee was already "vierenzestig." He was carried ashore on the back of a Timorese soldier who waded into the sea to meet the boot — "speciaal ook met een Timorees, die geheel vooraan was en de dominee op zijn rug nam." Later the author saw the two walking deep in conversation; but when the kerkdienst came, the Timorees was not there — he was Rooms-Katholiek. The author concludes: "dit gesprek buiten de kerk om was wel de ware geestelijke verzorging" — the conversation outside the church was the true spiritual care.

## Significance

Dominee Thenu is the moral centre of *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*. He is the figure through whom Dames makes his case that the [[K.N.I.L.]] in Aceh was held together less by regulation than by the patient daily work of men like this Ambonese godsdienstleraar, whose authority ran ahead of and outside the rulebook, and whose songs gave the [[Manisee (Manisé)|Manisee]] the idiom in which he understood himself. The funeral with four M.W.O.-ridders as slippendragers is, in the book's economy, the moment at which the decorations of the old Aceh generation are laid at the feet of the man who had blessed them.

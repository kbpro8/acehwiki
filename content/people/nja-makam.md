**Nja‑Makam** was the *bendehoofd* (bandit chief) of an Atjeh‑aligned insurgent group that entrenched itself in the **Tamiang** region in late **[[1892]]**. Dutch colonial forces under **[[Colonel Van de Pol|Van de Pol]]** launched a punitive expedition against his forces in **[[February]] [[1893]]**, marking the first major clash between the **Royal Netherlands Navy** and the **Atjeh** insurgency in that area. The leader’s activities are recorded in the Dutch naval yearbooks of the 1890s.

## Context

The **Tamiang** river basin, part of the interior of **[[Sumatra]]**, became a focal point of resistance after the **Atjeh** chief **Nja‑Makam** settled there in **[[1892]]**. His group allied with local **Atjeh** fighters, prompting the colonial administration to consider a military response.

## Military Action

On **[[13 February]] [[1893]]**, **[[Colonel Van de Pol|Van de Pol]]** led a force of 130 infantrymen from **[[Deli]]** together with a **[[Dutch Navy|naval]]** flotilla to confront **Nja‑Makam**. The expedition ultimately forced the insurgents to retreat, though the conflict continued in subsequent months.

## Significance

**Nja‑Makam** exemplifies the localized leadership that complicated Dutch attempts to pacify **[[Acheh]]**. His brief control of the **Tamiang** area illustrates the fluid nature of resistance networks in the late‑nineteenth‑century **[[Aceh War]]**.

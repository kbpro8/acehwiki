**Toekoe Amat** was an [[Atjeh]]nese kampong head in the neighbourhood of [[Lampagar]] in the [[VI Moekims]], counted by the Dutch in [[1876]] among the friendly (*bevriende*) chiefs. With [[Hadji Soliman of Lampagar|Hadji Soliman]], head of Lampagar, he paid the fateful visit to the Dutch camp on the evening of [[2 May]] [[1876]], hours before the [[Night attack on Lampagar (2-3 May 1876)|night attack on Lampagar]] began.

## The visit of 2 May

In the conversation with Lieutenants [[Van de Roemer]] and [[Schnelle]], described by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]), Toekoe Amat and Hadji Soliman told the officers that they suspected the enemy of evil designs on the VI Moekims and that they feared for their kampong if the post itself were to be attacked. They asked, and Captain [[Hoynck van Papendrecht]] granted, that the sentries on the north side should not fire too quickly at possible *population patrols* that night — the request whose consequence made the entry of the storming parties across the northern fence so easy at four in the morning.

Arrested with the other heads after the attack on the suspicion that they had known the plan, he was released when the wounded prisoner of [[4 May]] shifted the blame to [[Panglima Moeda of Piëng]] — the inquiry concluding that the heads had sufficiently warned the officers, whose self-confidence had not listened.

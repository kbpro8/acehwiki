**Tengkoe di Boekit** — also called *Tgk. Beb*, properly *Tgk. di Boekit* because his mother came from Boekit Seboen — was the third of the four sons of [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro]] who carried the [[Tiro-oelama|Tiro family]] through the long years of the guerrilla war. After the death of his eldest brother [[Tengkoe Mat Amin di Tiro|Mat Amin]] at [[Aneukgalong]] in [[1896]], he and his brother [[Tengkoe Majet di Tiro|Majet]] remained the principal adult males of the line, hunting and hunted through the mountains and forests about [[Tangsé]] and [[Pidië]].

## The overval of 21 May 1910

After [[1908]] governor [[H.N.A. Swart|Swart]] placed lieutenant [[Schmidt]] in charge of the destruction of the Tiro-oelama's and their immediate following. Schmidt worked systematically, reasoning that while the bevolking of Tangsé would never directly betray the schuilplaats of the holy men, the *fourageurs* who brought in rice from the kampongs might be tracked. After elaborate march-and-counter-march through the country south of the Goenoeng Meureuseuë and Goenoeng Alimon, Schmidt's two brigades on [[21 May]] [[1910]] slipped past the wakeful outer posts of a *ladang* and onto the path that led to the schuilplaats itself. Within half an hour the work was over. Eleven Acehnese corpses lay in and about the schuilplaats, and among the dead were prominent followers of the Tiro-tengkoe's: Tgk. Tjhēk Haroen, Tgk. Moeda Sjam, Tgk. Sjech Samin, and [[Habib Teupin Wan|Habib Tjoet]] — son of the holy [[Habib Teupin Wan]].

Among six *tjaps* recovered on the field one was recognised as that of Tgk. di Boekit; but his body was not at first found among the eleven, and his brother [[Tengkoe Majet di Tiro|Tgk. Majet di Tiro]] had also escaped. The mystery of "Oom Bép" was resolved by the six-year-old son of Tgk. Majet, who had been taken prisoner during the pursuit. The boy, in his childish openness, told the patrol of his father's beautiful shoes, his ladang at the upper course of the Aloeë Simi, and of "Oom Bép." By the overval of [[21 May]] [[1910]] eleven corpses had remained in Dutch hands, but there had been a twelfth dead: Tgk. di Boekit himself. His wounded body had been carried a little way off, and after his soon-following death had been buried beside the eleven others. Later, twelve fresh graves were found on the place of the fight.

## Significance

With the death of Tgk. di Boekit the Tiro family was, in respect of its adult leaders, reduced to one man: [[Tengkoe Majet di Tiro|Tgk. Majet]]. The boy's innocent disclosures — that his father wore shoes, that his ladang lay toward the upper stream of the Aloeë Simi — gave Schmidt the bearings that, a few months later, would bring Majet too to his end. Zentgraff notes that there have been fewer data than these with which a famous bendehoofd has been plucked out of the forest.

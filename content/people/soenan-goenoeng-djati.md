**Soenan Goenoeng Djati** was a Muslim missionary and one of the nine saints (*Wali Songo*) credited with the spread of Islam on [[Java]]. The *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* ([[1926]]) describes him as a Mohammedan, presumably from [[Pasé]] ([[North Sumatra]]) by origin, who achieved much success with his religious teaching on Java.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — Soenan Goenoeng Djati

The atlas provides the following account: "Soenan Goenoeng Djati was een Mohammedaan, vermoedelijk uit Pasei (Noord-Sumatra) afkomstig, die met zijn godsdienst-onderwijs op Java veel opgang maakte. Hij stierf 1570 te Cheribon, waar op een heuvel, de 'Goenoeng Diati', werd begraven. Vandaar de hem, na zijn dood gegeven naam: Soenan Goenoeng Djati." (Soenan Goenoeng Djati was a Mohammedan, presumably from Pasai [North Sumatra] by origin, who with his religious teaching achieved much success on Java. He died in 1570 at Cheribon, where he was buried on a hill, the 'Goenoeng Diati'. Hence the name given to him after his death: Soenan Goenoeng Djati.)

## Connection to Acheh

Soenan Goenoeng Djati's presumed origin in [[Pasé]], one of the earliest Muslim sultanates on [[North Sumatra]] and a neighbour of [[Acheh]], illustrates the role that North Sumatran Islam played in the Islamisation of the entire Archipelago. His career demonstrates that the Islamic tradition of North Sumatra — which produced both scholars and missionaries — was a source of religious influence extending far beyond the shores of Sumatra itself.

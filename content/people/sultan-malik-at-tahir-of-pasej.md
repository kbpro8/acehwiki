**Sultan Malik at-Tahir of Pasej** was the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] sultan who received the Arab traveller [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]] in [[1345]]/[[1346]] for fifteen days at his capital [[Pasej-Samodra|Sjemoe-t or Samatra]], four miles inland from the port of [[Pasej-Samodra|Sarha]]. He is the first named ruler of the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej-Samudra]] sultanate — the north Sumatran polity whose absorption by the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]] in the early sixteenth century extinguished the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] line — and his portrait in the *Rihla* is the only sustained Arabic-language picture of a pre-Atjehnese north Sumatran ruler.

## Reception of Ibn Battuta

[[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]], arriving at [[Pasej-Samodra|Sarha]] from [[Bengal|Bengal]] and proceeding inland to the capital [[Pasej-Samodra|Sjemoe-t]], was hospitably received by Sultan Malik at-Tahir. The sultan, Ibn Battuta records, was "an zealous warrior of the holy war with the infidels in the interior", whose slaving raids against the inland heathen brought him profitable spoil that sold well. He stayed with the sultan fifteen days before proceeding by junk to [[Moel Djawa|Moel Djawa]].

## Place in the Pasej-Atjeh succession

The [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej-Samudra]] sultanate, of which Sultan Malik at-Tahir is the first named ruler in Arabic record, was the north Sumatran Islamic polity whose succession the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]] extinguished in the early sixteenth century. The [[1521|fall of Pasej to the Portuguese]] in [[1521]] — which drove the [[Faletahan|Paseier Faletahan]] to [[Java]] — and the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s absorption of the coast are the events that closed the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] line. Sultan Malik at-Tahir's reception of [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]] in [[1345]]/[[1346]] is thus the high-point of the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] sultanate before its long decline.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Sultan Malik at-Tahir is the first named ruler of the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] sultanate — the polity whose absorption by the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]] frames the early-modern history of north Sumatra. His reception of [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]] is the principal external witness to the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] court, and his reputation as a warrior of the faith fixes the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] sultanate's self-image as a frontier Islamic state against the inland heathen.

**Laging Tobias** was a Dutch colonial civil servant who served as the second *Civiele Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (Civil Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies) from [[March 1883|March]] [[1883]] to his resignation in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]], and whose governorship — though more clear-sighted than that of his predecessor [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] — was overwhelmed by the [[Nisero affair|Nisero affair]] of [[November 1883|November]] [[1883]] and by the cumulative collapse of the Dutch position that culminated in the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] decreed by the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]] in the secret sessions of [[16 June|16]]–[[17 June]] [[1884]].

## Inheritance and Recognition (March 1883)

Laging Tobias took over the administration from Pruijs van der Hoeven in [[March 1883|March]] [[1883]]. He immediately perceived, from the unceasing continuation of enemy attacks, how critical the situation was: that none of the chiefs deserved trust; that no one outside the posts was any longer safe; that communication with some posts was sometimes cut off; and that in general, contrary to the claims of his predecessor, "er geen sprake was van algemeene erkenning van ons gezag door hoofden en bevolking, noch van een machteloos neerliggen der Kemalapartij, noch van een opgeven van het verzet" (there was no question of general recognition of our authority by chiefs and population, nor of a powerless collapse of the Kemala party, nor of an abandonment of the resistance). He had immediately to request more troops to gain some breathing room. These averaged 6,000 men in [[1882]] and [[1883]].

## The Scheepvaartregeling of May 1883

In [[May 1883|May]] [[1883]], under further approval of the government, Laging Tobias declared the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] applicable to all of Atjeh. Although introduced unprepared and with inadequate means, it worked excellently under the able leadership of the *Assistent-Resident* [[Scherer|Scherer]]. At the same time, Laging Tobias made attempts to obtain contact with the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] in order to attempt the restoration of the Sultanate under Dutch sovereignty — but the government raised objections.

## The Nisero Affair (November 1883 – September 1884)

On the [[Westkust|West Coast]], near [[Tenom|Tenom]], the English steamship *[[Nisero|Nisero]]* was wrecked in [[November 1883|November]] [[1883]]. The crew was held captive by the *radja* of Tenom. All attempts to release or ransom them failed, by which Dutch impotence was fully exposed, even to the outside world. English interference ensued, and only in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] was the release of the unfortunate crew obtained, on payment of a ransom of ƒ100,000, while the Dutch bound themselves no longer to close the harbour of Tenom. All attempts at conciliation on the Dutch side proved fruitless.

## Resistance to the Concentratie and Resignation (1884)

All Dutch exertion in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] led only to loss of life and exhaustion of the treasury, without bringing the Dutch a step further. The government in the Netherlands therefore decided that things could no longer continue as they were. Already in [[August 1883|August]] [[1883]] the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[s Jacob|'s Jacob]] had visited Atjeh and found the situation highly unfavourable. In [[March 1884|March]] [[1884]] the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[Van Rees|Van Rees]] also came to see and discuss the state of affairs. In two secret sessions of the united Chambers of the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]] on [[16 June|16]] and [[17 June]] [[1884]], it was decided to adopt a wholly new system for Atjeh: the so-called *concentration* or contraction of the Dutch *stelling* in Groot-Atjeh, the abolition of the coastal posts, the withdrawal of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]], and blockade.

Laging Tobias, strongly opposed to this concentration, which according to him "de kroon zette op de Nederlandsche fouten in Atjeh" (set the crown on the Dutch mistakes in Atjeh), saw that under these circumstances the military element must of itself come to the fore again — "en viel niets meer te besturen" (and there was nothing more to govern). He repeated his earlier request for discharge, first submitted under 's Jacob, and retired in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]].

## Significance

Laging Tobias's governorship is the transitional phase of the [[Civil Government Period (1881-1884)|civil government period]], in which the optimism of Pruijs van der Hoeven was replaced by the more realistic but impotent clear-sightedness of his successor. His introduction of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] throughout Atjeh in [[May 1883|May]] [[1883]], though underprepared, was the first systematic attempt to apply the trade-control instrument that would prove decisive in the war's later phases. His pursuit of the Sultanate restoration policy anticipated the more sustained attempt by [[General van Teijn|General van Teijn]] after [[1886]]. His opposition to the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] aligns him with the line of expert opinion — [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]], [[Scherer|Scherer]], [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — that Kruijt cites against the Dutch government's policy.

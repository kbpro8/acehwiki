**Heynen** — *brigadecommandant Heynen* — was one of the best Dutch *onderofficieren* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussée]]: *„Heynen, een onzer beste onderofficieren, een brave met de Willemsorde 3e klasse."* He is one of the four *vermaarde brigadecommandanten* in Zentgraff's photograph — *„links boven: Mosselman; rechts boven: Stam; links beneden: Götz; rechts beneden: Heijnen"* — though the spelling varies.

## The 5th brigade at Koeta Blang Djeurat, 31 December 1901

At the [[Koeta Blang Djeurat (1901)|vermeestering of Koeta Blang Djeurat]] on [[31 December]] [[1901]] Heynen's *5de brigade* had the heaviest task: *„In het N.W. bastion had alweder de 5de brigade (Heynen) de zwaarste taak. Hier was de strijd het verwoedst, hier werden wonderen van dapperheid vertoond, hier was het, dat de amboineesche maréchaussée Maroekoe, die onder de eersten op de borstwering geweest en door 2 klewanghouwen zwaar verwond was, het eerst in de benteng sprong en met groote dapperheid aan het handgemeen deelnam."* — In the N.W. bastion the 5th brigade (Heynen) had again the heaviest task. Here the fight was the fiercest, here wonders of bravery were shown, here it was that the Ambonese maréchaussée Maroekoe, who had been among the first on the breastwork and was severely wounded by two klewang-blows, was the first to jump into the benteng and joined the hand-to-hand fighting with great bravery. The luitenant Burger, seeing his heavy wounds, pulled him back out of the fight; *hij rukte zich los en stortte zich opnieuw in het handgemeen* — he tore himself loose and threw himself anew into the melee. *De worsteling was kort, de gevolgen verschrikkelijk* — the struggle was short, the consequences terrible. Seventy-nine enemy dead remained in the benteng; no man escaped. *79 lijken bleven in de versterking achter, geen enkel man ontkwam. Doch ook onzerzijds waren zware verliezen geleden, wij betreurden 2 dooden en 24 gewonden.* The regiment, hungry because the *menagemeester had nergens voor gezorgd*, had had its eye on a goat the Atjehers in the benteng were slaughtering; *de maréchaussée's begrepen dat ze niet te lang mochten wachten, wilden ze van die geit hun competeerende krijgen* (the maréchaussée's understood that they must not wait too long if they were to get their share of that goat), and *ze waren zoo verrukt dat ze al „potong kepala" begonnen te schreeuwen voor de Atjehers hen hadden ontdekt* (they were so delighted that they began shouting *potong kepala* before the Atjehers had discovered them). *Wat de geit betreft, die was dik in orde… behalve dat de brigade-Heynen er niets van kreeg* — as for the goat, it was in fine order… except that the brigade-Heynen got nothing of it.

## The Tangsé march: lost shoe at the Benit kloof

On the march over the watershed into the valley of [[Tangsé]] — *die nog een en al geheim voor ons was* (still all secret to us) — Heynen was brigadecommandant with the maréchaussée. On the day the colonne *de kloof van Benit doortrok, even vóór Tangsé, was de staf toevallig in de buurt van zijn brigade, en Van Heutsz verloor bij die gelegenheid een schoen.* — when the colonne went through the kloof of Benit, just before Tangsé, the staf happened to be in the neighbourhood of his brigade, and Van Heutsz lost on that occasion a shoe. *Het was geen kostbaar verlies* — it was no costly loss; Van Heutsz, like Heynen, like all the others, had bought his *zeildoeksche schoenen* for f 1.25 *bij den Chinees op Peunajong*.

## The Rijswijk overgave

After his pensionneering Heynen became *politieopziener* (police inspector) at [[Batavia]]. On the day of the *overgave der Landvoogdij* — Van Heutsz's laying-down of the Governor-Generalship — in the [[Paleis op Rijswijk]], *had juist Heynen de wacht* — Heynen happened to have the wacht. Van Heutsz and Heynen *kenden elkaar niet zoo'n beetje* (knew each other not so very well); they had been through a great deal together, *vooral in de dagen dat men over de waterscheiding trok, de trotsche Pedireezen de les las en daarna den zwaren tocht maakte naar de vallei van Tangsé* (especially in the days when one crossed the watershed, gave the proud Pedireezen a lesson, and afterwards made the heavy march to the valley of Tangsé). Van Heutsz, half-undressed in a room of the palace — *hij had zijn jas al uit* (he had his coat already off) — opening the window and seeing Heynen, cried: *„Present, generaal?"* Heynen stood *stram in de houding*. Van Heutsz, *met z'n goud bestikte jas uit het venster zwaaide* (with his gold-embroidered coat waved out of the window) and riep: *„Het zit er goddank op, Heynen!"* — *„Wel gefeliciteerd, generaal."* Van Heutsz, *na nog even met die jas het groot-saluut te hebben gezwaaid, opgewekt knikte en de laatste kleedingkluisters van zich afwierp* — after waving the great-salute once more with that coat, nodded cheerfully and threw off the last *kleedingkluisters*.

## Opzichter of the Peutjoet cemetery, 1936

By [[1936]] Heynen — *„deze was in 1936 opzichter van het kerkhof Peutjoet"* — was the *opzichter* (superintendent) of the Peutjoet cemetery. It was Heynen who, on the patrol of [[21 January]] [[1902]] along the Keureutoe-river and the Kroeëng Pira, *merkte op het laatste moment onraad en schreeuwde hem een „awas kapitein!" toe* — noticed at the last moment that something was wrong and shouted *awas kapitein!* — to [[Webb|kapitein Webb]] (the *ridderlijke en populaire* officer) at Loeboek Minjeu, just as a hidden vijand cut the rotan and the valboom fell. *Op dat moment kapte een verscholen vijand den rotan door en de boom viel met donderend geraas over het pad, op Webb…* Webb was *vreeselijk toegetakeld*; ten hours unconscious, then the Dood came as a *verlosser* (deliverer).

## Significance

Heynen is the type of the *brigadecommandant* who was at once the *baas* of his eighteen-man *trimoerti* in the bosch and the *onderofficier* whom a *generaal* — by then Gouverneur-Generaal — could greet from the window of the Rijswijk palace as an old comrade. His last service, fittingly, was the care of the graves at [[Peutjoet]] — *waar witte grafsteenen blikkeren in de schaduw der boomen*.

## In the Maréchaussée Jubileum-nummer ([[1930]])

The *[[Overzicht van Onderscheidingen toegekend aan het Korps Maréchaussée in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1890-1930)|Overzicht van Onderscheidingen]]* printed in the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* carried both a register entry and a portrait notice of Heijnen, spelling the name *Heijnen*.

Born on [[16 October]] [[1869]], he received as *brigade-commandant* of Maréchaussée the knight's cross of the *[[Militaire Willemsorde|Militaire Willems-Orde]]* 4th class on [[18 February]] [[1902]], and the 3rd class on [[31 July]] of the same year. He was, the notice states, "de derde onderofficier, wien deze hooge onderscheiding werd toegekend en no. 5 van het geheele Korps" — the third non-commissioned officer to receive that high distinction, and the fifth member of the whole corps. The register lists the 3rd-class award under [[1902]] jointly with that to his division commander *Kapt. Mar.* [[Van der Maaten|K. van der Maaten]], with the number *Alg. Stb. No. 31519*.

The issue reproduced a photograph of Van der Maaten which "werd door zijn oud-divisiecommandant aan sergeant Heijnen opgedragen" — was dedicated by his former division commander to Sergeant Heijnen.

Heijnen afterwards transferred into the police service and was pensioned as *adjunct-hoofdcommissaris*, after the knight's cross of the *[[Oranje-Nassau (Orde van Oranje-Nassau)|Orde van Oranje-Nassau]]* had been conferred upon him.

Colonel [[C. A. Snell|L. A. Snell]] named him among "de eenvoudigen van ziel" who ought to be rescued from oblivion — "de dappersten onder de dapperen."

**Tjoet Gát** was a subject of the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] who, in the early nineteenth century, led the combined forces of the two *Keudjeuroeën* of [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] — Karang and Moeda — to victory against adventurers from [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]] who had invaded the Tamiang lands, and whose death in battle and the subsequent grant of the [[Bandahara|Bandahara]] territory to his heir became the foundation of the Bandahara chieftaincy and the original source of Langkat's contested claim to suzerainty over Tamiang. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats him as a *krijgsoverste* — a war-leader — whose personal valor and death in action set in motion a chain of events that would shape the politics of the Tamiang lands for the next three-quarters of a century.

## The war with the Meureudoe adventurers

The two *keudjeuroeëns* of [[Tamiang|Karang]] and [[Tamiang|Moeda]] — themselves the products of the partition of the original Tamiang kingdom of [[Radja Mengkoeta|Radja Mengkoeta]] — faced, in the early nineteenth century, a *zwaren strijd* against adventurers from [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]]. The outcome was in doubt until Tjoet Gát, identified by *De Buitenbezittingen* as an *onderdaan* (subject) of the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]], took command of the combined Tamiang forces and led them to victory. Tjoet Gát himself did not survive the campaign; he fell in action, *zelf in den strijd gevallen*.

## The grant of Bandahara

The two *Keudjeuroeën*, *uit dankbaarheid voor dien dienst* — out of gratitude for that service — granted to the heir of Tjoet Gát the land of [[Bandahara|Bandahara]], on both banks of the Tamiang River. The grant was a reward for a service rendered by a man who had died in their defence, and the recipient was the dead hero's heir, who thereby became the first *Radja Bandahara*. The new *landschapje* took its name — *Bandahara* — from the territory thus ceded.

## Langkat's intervention

The [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] used the circumstance that Tjoet Gát had been his subject as a *voorwendsel* — a pretext — to proclaim his own suzerainty over the new Bandahara landschapje. On the death of the first Radja Bandahara, he appointed his own nephew, [[Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema|Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema]], to rule over part of the territory — an intervention that provoked the armed resistance of the next Radja Bandahara, supported by [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] and [[Langsa|Langsa]]. The ensuing war, unresolved for years, was the entry point through which the Dutch were drawn into Tamiang politics in [[1861]] and ultimately to the [[1878]] transfer of Langkat's rights over Tamiang to the Netherlands-Indies Government.

## Significance

Tjoet Gát is the figure around whom the entire complex of Bandahara's founding, Langkat's claim, and the eventual Dutch intervention in Tamiang turns. His single act of military service — and his death in that service — produced the territorial grant that became the Bandahara chieftaincy, the Langkat claim that the Dutch extinguished in [[1878]], and the constitutional question that *De Buitenbezittingen* traces through the politics of the [[1878]] transfer and the [[1908]] incorporation of Tamiang into the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

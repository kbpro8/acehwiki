Governor Petrie was a British East India Company official who served as Governor of [[Penang]] (Prince of Wales' Island) in the years immediately preceding the appointment of [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Colonel Bannerman]] in [[1817]]. He is significant in the Achehnese record for having authorised [[Captain Coombs' first Acheh mission (1817-1818)|the first Acheh mission of Captain Coombs]].

## Authorisation of Coombs's First Acheh Mission

The political superintendence of Achehnese affairs had been assigned by the Bengal government to the authorities at Penang. «Still during Petrie's lifetime», writes Van der Kemp, «a certain Captain Coombs was sent on a commission to Acheh at the end of [[1817]] or the beginning of [[1818]], to enquire after the rightful sultan». Coombs found the chiefs of [[Banda Acheh|the capital]] insisting that they had forever renounced [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] and recognised [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf ul-Alum]] as their sovereign. A few days before his arrival a British brig had been cut out by [[Tunku Pakih|Tuku Pakih of Pedir]] ([[Capture of British brig by Tunku Pakih]]); Coombs took the matter up with the chiefs, who expressed indignation and promised to call Pakih to account. After a stay of fourteen days Coombs left the «ungenerous port» in February [[1818]], convinced that [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] was the rightful sultan but that the British government would have to support him with at least a thousand men if he were to maintain his authority. He then proceeded to [[Calcutta]] to make his report «in accordance with the wishes of the Penang government» — that is, of Petrie's government.

## Death and Succession

Petrie died in office, and on his death [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Bannerman]] succeeded him as Governor of Penang. Van der Kemp notes that Bannerman, on his appointment, «fell into the middle of the Achehnese questions, which were unknown to him» — questions that Petrie had set in train by authorising the Coombs mission and that would culminate, under Bannerman's successor, in the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs commission of 1819]] and the [[Acheh treaty of 1819|treaty of 22 April 1819]].

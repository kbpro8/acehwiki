**Colonel Fourgeoud** was a Dutch colonial officer who commanded the Dutch **[[Dutch Marine Corps]]** during a punitive expedition against the rebel villages of **[[Gado Saby]]** and **[[Cofaay]]** in the interior of **[[Aceh]]** in the late eighteenth‑nineteenth century. His actions are recorded in the Dutch military narrative of the expedition (pages 385‑388 of *De geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers*).

## Military role and tactics
Colonel Fourgeoud directed the advance of his forces through dense jungle, ordering the construction of a **vlotbrug** (raft bridge) over reeds that allowed only a single column to cross. He personally oversaw the burning of **[[Gado Saby]]**, creating a “fire‑ and smoke‑screen” that forced the rebels to flee with women and children. On [[22 August]] he ordered a renewed push toward the rebel hide‑outs, and on [[23 August]] he inspected the aftermath, noting the destruction of a rice field.

## Relations with other officers
Fourgeoud coordinated with **[[Captain Hamell|Captain Hamell]]**, who explored the terrain with fifty marines and thirty hunters, and with **[[Captain Stedman|Captain Stedman]]**, who commanded the forward guard. He also issued orders to **[[Captain Bolt|Captain Bolt]]** and **[[Captain Meyland|Captain Meyland]]** for logistical support.

## Health crises and logistical strain
During the campaign the Dutch camp at **[[Barbacoeba]]** suffered a severe dysentery outbreak; Fourgeoud dispatched the sick to **[[Magdenberg]]** and the **[[Societeitspost Vredenburg|Societeitspost]]**. Supplies ran low, forcing troops to subsist on half‑rations and to forage rice, peas and cassava.

## Significance
Fourgeoud’s relentless pursuit, despite disease, lack of ammunition and difficult terrain, exemplifies the harshness of Dutch colonial warfare in **[[Aceh]]** and the eventual weakening of rebel resistance after the loss of **[[Gado Saby]]** and **[[Cofaay]]**.

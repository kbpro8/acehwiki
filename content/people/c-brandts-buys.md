**C. Brandts Buys** was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] forestry researcher of the [[Dienst van het Boschwezen]] in [[Nederlandsch-Indië]] who, with [[C. Japing]] and [[D. Fernandes]], was the author of the [[Bijdrage tot de kennis van Pinus Merkusii Jungh et de Vr.|monograph on Pinus Merkusii Jungh et de Vr., more particularly in the Gajo-Landen]] — the principal early scientific study of the Sumatran pine and of its exploitable stands in the [[Gajo-landen|Gajo-Landen]] region. He is named in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] as the authority for the *quarre* tapping system that was applied at the [[Baleq]] enterprise, and his monograph is cited in the pamphlet as the source of the detailed description of the *quarre* system and of the results obtained with it.

## The Pinus Merkusii monograph

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] cites Brandts Buys's monograph — *Bijdrage tot de kennis van Pinus Merkusii Jungh et de Vr., meer in het bijzonder in de Gajo-landen*, by C. Brandts Buys, C. Japing and D. Fernandes — for an *uitvoerige beschrijving* (extensive description) of the *[[quarre]]* tapping system and of the results obtained with it. The *quarre* system — borrowed from the turpentine industry of the Landes in France — involved tapping wounds 10 cm wide and at greatest depth 2½ cm deep, refreshed every three days in an upward direction with a slightly curved carpenter's chisel. The number of tapping wounds per tree depended on the thickness of the trunk: at a circumference of 1–1½ m, one wound; at 1½–2 m, two wounds; above that, three. The Brandts Buys monograph is preserved by the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] as the principal published source on the practical application of the *quarre* method in Atjehnese conditions.

## The connection with the Baleq enterprise

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the *quarre* system described by Brandts Buys was the system applied at the [[Baleq]] enterprise from the beginning of the exploitation in [[1924]]. The pamphlet also records that the *Mededeeling no. 19 van het Proefstation voor het Boschwezen* — the publication of the [[Proefstation voor het Boschwezen]] in which Brandts Buys's monograph appeared — was the source of the observation that the odour of the first Atjeh-turpentine samples of [[1924]] had been "different from that of the good American and French sorts; whether the smell, however, was more agreeable or not, is difficult to determine. We, for our part, have always found the smell of the Indian product more agreeable; but about taste there is no disputing." The same observation notes that the factory-scale production had "definitely modified" the odour, and that the distinctive smell had "largely disappeared."

## Significance

Brandts Buys's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the author of the principal early scientific study of the [[Pinus Merkusii|Sumatran pine]] in the [[Gajo-landen|Gajo-Landen]] — the study that established the silvicultural basis of the [[Baleq]] enterprise. His monograph is cited in the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] as the authority for the *quarre* tapping system, and his work with [[C. Japing|Japing]] and [[D. Fernandes|Fernandes]] is the principal published source on the practical application of French turpentine-tapping methods in Atjehnese conditions.

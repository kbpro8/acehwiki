**Van Lijnden van Sandenburg** was a member of the States General who took part in the secret sessions of April [[1874]] on the origin of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh war]]. Cited by [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] in 1886, he declared on [[16 April]] [[1874]] that the policy which had led to the Atjeh war "reeds lang was voorbereid, in voornemens en gedachten, maar niet in voorzorgsmaatregelen en middelen en daarin lag ook de onvergeeflijke fout" ("had been long in preparation, in intentions and thoughts, but not in precautionary measures and means — and therein lay the unforgivable mistake"); pointing further to the documents, which fully confirmed the very deplorable state of the navy and showed that, as regarded the knowledge of Atjeh, the government had been in complete darkness, he asked whether it was too strong to say that the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) Kreemer's account|first expedition]] was wholly to be laid to the government's charge.

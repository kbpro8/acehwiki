**Dr. J. W. Naarding** was the author of the academic dissertation *[[The Conflict Snouck Hurgronje, Van Heutsz, Van Daalen (1938)|Het conflict Snouck Hurgronje — Van Heutsz — Van Daalen]]* ([[1938]]), published as part XI of the *Utrechtsche Bijdragen*, which [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] called "so complete as a study of sources" and used as the principal source for the doctrinal and personal disputes of [[1892]]–[[1908]].

## Use by du Croo

Du Croo drew from Naarding the summary of [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s three causes of the persistence of Acehnese resistance, and the argument that "the Atjeh war was not just an ordinary war between two peoples, which could be ended in the ordinary way by a peace, but a new period in the world-historical drama: the Holy War between believers and unbelievers, which received continual spiritual nourishment and guidance from the headquarters of the faith, [[Mekka]], and which permitted an armistice but no lasting peace."

He also drew from Naarding the statement of the *new* Atjeh problem: "The great art would then be — and that was the new Atjeh problem — to put an end to the relation between the practitioners of the normal trade of peace and those of the abnormal trade of war among the Acehnese, between farmers and bands."

The *Bijlagen* to Naarding's dissertation supplied du Croo with the documentary core of his eighth and ninth chapters: [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|van Heutsz]]'s written assessment of [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] as his prospective successor (pp. 62 e.v., 65–66); Snouck Hurgronje's warning letter to Governor-General [[Rooseboom]] of [[2 October]] [[1903]] (pp. 57 e.v., 69); and the correspondence between van Heutsz and minister [[Alexander Willem Frederik Idenburg|Idenburg]] of [[1908]] (pp. 151, 168 e.v.).

## Du Croo's reservation

Du Croo endorsed Naarding as a source-study but distanced himself from his judgements of persons: "the conclusions of this writer, where they concern the figure and character of Van Heutsz, were fiercely and, in our opinion, not unjustly contested by many; these are left out of consideration here."

**Tekoe Moeda Augkasa** — *Tëngku Meudé Angkasa*, in modern Indonesian spelling — was the former chief (*hoofd*) of [[Merbou]], previously driven out, whom [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Generaal-Majoor Wiggers van Kerchem]], Governor of [[Atjeh]] in [[1876]], armed and subsidised as a proxy ally to fight [[Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh]] on behalf of the friendly Oostkust statelet [[Kerti]]. He is, in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s narrative, the local Acehnese chief through whom Wiggers van Kerchem sought to conduct the Oostkust dispute without committing Dutch troops — and whose proxy-arrangement, rejected as inadequate by the *Gouverneur-Generaal* (Governor-General) van Lansberge, precipitated Wiggers van Kerchem's dismissal and replacement by [[Diemont|Generaal Diemont]], who immediately sent the [[Simpang Olim expedition (November 1876)|November 1876 military expedition]] that forced the submissions of Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh.

## The Oostkust dispute and Wiggers van Kerchem's reluctance

The political context of Augkasa's arming was the dispute that had broken out on the Oostkust of Atjeh between the statelets of [[Simpang Olim]] and [[Kerti]], in which [[Tandjong Semantoh]] stood on the side of Simpang Olim against Kerti. The *Gouverneur-Generaal* van Lansberge, "zag op dit tijdstip (1876) voornamelijk heil in een gewapend optreden in de Onderhoorigheden, om de staatjes die onze souvereiniteit nog niet erkend hadden, door de wapens daartoe te dwingen" — saw, at this moment (1876), principally the benefit of an armed intervention in the *Onderhoorigheden* (Dependencies), to force the statelets that had not yet recognised our sovereignty to do so by arms. Wiggers van Kerchem, "kon zich met deze inzichten niet vereenigen, hij zag geen heil in een agressief optreden met de wapenen in de Onderhoorigheden, waarvan de gevolgen z. i. niet te berekenen waren" — could not associate himself with these views; he saw no benefit in an aggressive armed intervention in the *Onderhoorigheden*, the consequences of which, in his opinion, could not be calculated — "en gaf er verre de voorkeur aan, om vooralsnog alle beschikbare troepen in Groot-Atjeh te houden, noodig ter voltooiing van het programma van den Generaal Pel" — and preferred by far, for the time being, to keep all available troops in [[Groot-Atjeh]], needed for the completion of the programme of Generaal Pel.

## The arming with 150 breech-loading rifles and f 3000

Faced with the *Gouverneur-Generaal*'s wish that Dutch troops be sent to support [[Kerti]], and unwilling to commit them himself, Wiggers van Kerchem limited his intervention to the arming and subsidising of Tekoe Moeda Augkasa as a proxy. Van Heutsz records the arrangement:

> "beperkte hij er zich toe om aan het vroeger verdreven hoofd van Merbou, Tekoe Moeda Augkasa, die Kerti met 500 man wilde helpen, 150 achterlaadgeweren met munitie en 3000 dollars te zenden, teneinde Simpang Olim en Tandjong Semantoh afbreuk te doen".

The arrangement was a proxy-war contract. Augkasa, *het vroeger verdreven hoofd van Merbou* — the formerly expelled chief of Merbou — was the local Acehnese chieftain whose own political interest in returning to Merbou aligned with the Dutch interest in supporting Kerti against Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh. He had undertaken to support Kerti with 500 men — *die Kerti met 500 man wilde helpen* — and Wiggers van Kerchem supplied him with the means: 150 *achterlaadgeweren met munitie* — 150 breech-loading rifles with ammunition — and *3000 dollars* — three thousand guilders. The arms and the subsidy were the Dutch contribution to a campaign whose operational conduct was to be Augkasa's, and whose target was the harm — *afbreuk doen* — of Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh.

## The arrangement rejected and Wiggers van Kerchem dismissed

The arrangement was, however, insufficient to the *Gouverneur-Generaal*'s purpose. Van Lansberge, "die, door het met de wapenen dwingen van erkenning onzer souvereiniteit, hoopte de voor die weerspannige staatjes nog steeds voortdurende dure blokkade te kunnen opheffen" — who, by forcing the recognition of our sovereignty by arms, hoped to be able to lift the still-continuing costly *blokkade* (blockade) of those refractory statelets — "liet toen den Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem vervangen door den Generaal Diemont, die, onmiddelljkk na zijn optreden in November 1876, door het zenden van eene militaire expeditie, Simpang Olim en Tandjong Semantoh, tot erkenning van onze souvereiniteit dwong" — thereupon had Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem replaced by Generaal Diemont, who, immediately upon his taking office in November 1876, by sending a military expedition, forced Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh to recognise our sovereignty. The proxy-arrangement with Augkasa was thus swept aside by the Diemont expedition of November [[1876]]; the policy of arming local chieftains as proxies gave way, under Diemont, to the policy of direct Dutch military coercion of the Oostkust statelets.

## Significance

Tekoe Moeda Augkasa's significance in the Aceh Wiki History record rests on three things: he was the formerly expelled chief of Merbou whose proxy-alliance with [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] in [[1876]] — armed with 150 *achterlaadgeweren met munitie* and 3000 dollars, intended to support [[Kerti]] with 500 men against [[Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh]] — is the lone recorded instance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] narrative of a Dutch Governor seeking to conduct an *Onderhoorigheden* dispute through a local proxy rather than by direct Dutch military intervention; he was the proxy-ally whose arrangement was rejected by [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Gouverneur-Generaal van Lansberge]] as inadequate — precipitating Wiggers van Kerchem's dismissal and replacement by [[Diemont|Generaal Diemont]], who immediately sent the [[Simpang Olim expedition (November 1876)|November 1876 military expedition]] that forced the submissions of Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh; and his proxy-arrangement is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the local expression of Wiggers van Kerchem's broader strategic judgement — that the Dutch troops available in [[1876]] were better kept in [[Groot-Atjeh]] for "de voltooiing van het programma van den Generaal Pel" (the completion of the programme of Generaal Pel) than dispersed to the Oostkust for the coercion of statelets the Dutch had not yet the operational capacity to hold. His role in the Aceh Wiki History record is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the local-counterpart to the institutional figure of Wiggers van Kerchem: where the Governor supplied the strategic reluctance, Augkasa supplied the local proxy through whom that reluctance was operationally expressed.

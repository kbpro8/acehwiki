**Ménèt** is the legendary stepson of [[Saëda (Gayo progenitor)|Saëda]] — founder of the [[Kédjoerön Boekét|Kédjoerön Boekét]] line of the [[Gajoland|Gayo]] highlands — and the progenitor of the *Boekét Ewéh* branch of the Boekét clan, whose unilateral seizure of the *keris kepala soeasa* — the gold-headed kris that was the principal *bawar* (insigne of chieftaincy) — and subsequent forced surrender of it as ransom to the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Acehnese]] commander defined the constitutional dispute that would split the Boekét house for generations. *De Buitenbezittingen*'s account of the Boekét genealogy follows the *Lah* tradition — judged "de minst onbetrouwbare" — which casts Ménèt as the ancestor whose act of usurpation cost his descendants the lawful chieftaincy but not the ambition to claim it.

## The seizure of the bawar

According to the legend, [[Saëda (Gayo progenitor)|Saëda]] had married two Acehnese wives. The first bore him two sons who never rose to prominence and became lesser heads of the Boekét clan. The second wife brought him two stepsons, *Ménèt* and *Mpos Kèlaq*, and bore him two further sons, *Mérah Lama* and [[Panglima Dagang|Panglima Dagang]]. When the invasion of the [[Bataq Doeloe Poeloh Toedjéh|Bataq doeloe poeloh toedjéh]] drove the Boekét family from the *Meerstreek* (the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] basin), *Mérah Lama* went with his two stepbrothers to *Kébajakan*, where — in possession of the *bawar* — he was installed as *Kédjoerön Boekét*.

The constitutional crisis came when *Ménèt* contrived, by a *list* — a stratagem — to seize the principal *bawar*, the *keris kepala soeasa*, and refused to return it to the rightful owner. By Gayo *adat*, "wie de bawar heeft, ook kédjoerön is" — whoever holds the *bawar* is the *Kédjoerön* — so Ménèt claimed the dignity. The other *bawar* remained in *Mérah Lama*'s possession, so that two *Kédjoeröns* of Boekét now existed side by side: *Mérah Lama* with an incomplete set of regalia, *Ménèt* with the principal piece.

## The prang toedjéh tahoen and the ransom

Ménèt's seizure of the *bawar* was not the end of his troubles. He refused to pay the *haol* — the tribute — due to the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]], and his refusal provoked the *prang toedjéh tahoen* — the "War of Ten Years" — which ended with the capture of many *rédjë*'s of the *Meerstreek* by the Acehnese. The ransom demanded for these chiefs included the surrender of the *keris kepala soeasa*; Ménèt, forced to comply, lost the principal *bawar* and, with it, his effective claim to the *Kédjoerön* title. The Atjehsche aanvoerder who received the kris had, by accepting it as ransom, implicitly confirmed the legitimacy of the *bawar*'s constitutional role.

## The descendants: Boekét Ewéh

The descendants of *Ménèt* became the *Boekét Ewéh* lineage, also called *Djaran*. They continued to use the *Kédjoerön* title — even after the loss of the *bawar* — and to claim equality with the *Boekét Lah* descendants of [[Panglima Dagang|Panglima Dagang]], who had reunited all the *bawar* in his own hand by purchasing the kris back from the Acehnese commander. The unwritten rule that emerged — that the more accessible of the two *Kédjoerön*s would be the one approached for decisions — gave the *Boekét Ewéh*, in their more accessible seat, considerable practical authority, even as the *Boekét Lah* retained the lawful title. The Dutch eventually strengthened the *Boekét Lah*'s "wankelende positie" by extending recognition to the lawful *Kédjoerön*, though the *Ewéh* branch retained influence across most of the Boekét territory outside *Seroelë*.

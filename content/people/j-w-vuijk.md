**J. W. Vuijk** was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] lieutenant of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] who was killed in action at [[Senelop]] on [[30 March]] [[1896]], in the early days of the [[1896]] crisis that followed [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] from the Dutch alliance. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as the first officer fatality of the siege of the temporary posts, and his portrait is one of the six reproduced in the album's closing plates of the fallen of the spring [[1896]] operations.

## The siege of Senelop

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, in the early days of the [[1896]] crisis, the temporary posts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] were besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his defection, and that the besiegers' fire made the walls of the *bentings* dangerous for the garrisons. The album's narrative places the engagement at Senelop on [[30 March]] [[1896]] — the date of Vuijk's death — as the first of the major engagements of the siege period. The album records that Vuijk was killed by Atjehnese fire from the *kampongranden* — the kampong thickets — surrounding the *benting*, and that his body, followed by eleven wounded and several soldiers, was carried into the gate of the *benting* as the *first offering of Toekoe Oemar's defection* — *als eerste offer van Toekoe Oemars afval*.

## The image of the offering

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]]'s language for Vuijk's death is striking: the body carried through the gate is the *eerste offer* — the first offering — of the defection. The sacrificial framing reflects the album's larger reading of the [[1896]] crisis as a moment at which the long *concentratie* period was redeemed by Dutch blood. The same framing is repeated, with variations, in the album's roll of the fallen: Vuijk's death at Senelop on [[30 March]] [[1896]] is followed by the deaths of [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]] at the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] on [[9 April]] [[1896]], [[H. L. Maarschalk|Maarschalk]] at the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]] on [[14 April]] [[1896]], and [[J. B. Zon|Zon]] at Senelop on [[19 April]] [[1896]].

## Burial at Kota Potjoet

The album records that Vuijk was buried at the cemetery of [[Kota Potjoet]], whose entrance bears the inscription *Aan onze Kameraden, gevallen op het Veld van Eer* — *To our comrades, fallen on the Field of Honour*.

## Significance

Vuijk's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the first officer fatality of the siege period that followed [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]]. His death on [[30 March]] [[1896]] — three weeks after the defection — established the tempo of the siege: a slow drain of officer casualties that would, by the end of the spring, produce the seven fatalities commemorated in the album's roll of the fallen. The album's sacrificial framing of his death — as the *first offering* of the defection — is a document of the way in which the Dutch popular press interpreted the [[1896]] crisis as a moment of national sacrifice.

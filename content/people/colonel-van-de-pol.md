**Colonel Van de Pol** was the commander of the Dutch infantry contingent that participated in the **Tamiang** expeditions of **[[February]] [[1893]] and [[March]]‑[[April]] [[1893]]**. He led a force of 130 infantrymen from **[[Deli (province)]]** and later oversaw a combined force of up to 430 troops during the second expedition. His leadership is noted for coordinating naval and land operations against **Atjeh** insurgents.

## Early Role

In **[[February]] [[1893]]**, **Colonel Van de Pol** embarked from **[[Deli (province)]]** with his infantry to support the **Royal Netherlands Navy** flotilla under **Lieutenant‑Commander W. Allirol** against the insurgent forces of **[[Nja‑Makam]]**.

## Second Expedition

During the **March‑April [[1893]]** campaign, **Van de Pol** commanded a larger force comprising 280 infantry and 90 native auxiliaries, later reinforced to 430 men with artillery support. He directed assaults on fortified **redoutes** (field fortifications) and coordinated river crossings at **[[Seroeway]]** and **[[Oud‑Seroeway]]**.

## Legacy

His actions contributed to the temporary suppression of resistance in the **Tamiang** basin, though the broader **Aceh War** continued for decades. Dutch military reports praised his coordination with naval units.

**Radja Tjèq**, also known as **Radja Kémala**, was the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]]-recognised overlord of the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] — the *roi fainéant* whose theoretical supremacy over the Alas valley had, by the time of the Dutch arrival in [[1904]], dissolved into a complex arrangement of delegated authorities and competing chieftaincies. *De Buitenbezittingen* describes him as the only *landschapshoofd* recognised by the Sultan across both [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]], the two *landschappen* into which the Alas valley was politically divided, and whose withdrawal from direct rule had transformed the *Kédjoerön*s of those lands into effectively independent chiefs.

## Constitutional position

The Radja Tjèq's authority was originally a Sultan-derived overlordship of the entire Alas valley. Over time he transferred his direct administrative rights in *Bambél* to his vassal the *Kédjoerön Bambél* and reserved for himself the fourth seat in each *landschap*'s *Rijksraad* of four *Péngoeloe si-émpat* — the "council of four" that advised the *Kédjoerön*. In [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] he appointed a deputy, the *Kédjoerön Radja Bintang*, to administer in his name, while retaining direct rule over his own residential kampong and its dependencies. The arrangement left the Radja Tjèq in a paradoxical position: a *roi fainéant* (do-nothing king) in name, yet still in possession of substantial direct territory in *Batoe Mboelan*.

## The 1904 transition

When the *colonne van Daalen* arrived in [[1904]], the Radja Tjèq's constitutional position had become, in *De Buitenbezittingen*'s phrase, "eene eigenaardige positie" — a peculiar position. No effective authority over the two *Kédjoerön*s was any longer acknowledged, though the original position of the Radja Tjèq was not denied. In both *landschappen* he held a place alongside the *Kédjoerön*; in *Batoe Mboelan* — where he retained direct rule over the most important settlements, a territory larger than that of the *Kédjoerön* himself — he stood, in the eyes of his own subjects, even above the *Kédjoerön*. In *Bambél* he held no territory and acted as an ordinary *Péngoeloe si-émpat*, where the *Kédjoerön* sought to push him back into the role of a mere councillor.

## Dutch settlement

The Dutch, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, were initially "onbekend met de werkelijke verhoudingen" — unfamiliar with the actual relations — and treated the two *Kédjoerön*s as wholly independent *landschapshoofden* authorised to sign the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]]. The Radja Tjèq was recognised only as a *Péngoeloe si-émpat* — a councillor — in *Batoe Mboelan* and as an ordinary chief without specific function in *Bambél*. The political consequences of this *misgreep* — this misstep — were, however, not damaging; the Radja Tjèq remained loyal. A subsequent reorganisation saw his territory in *Batoe Mboelan* transferred to *Bambél*, ending his anomalous dual position. The *Kédjoerön* of *Batoe Mboelan* moved his residence to [[Poeloe Nas|Poeloe Nas]] in [[1914]] and signed a new [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] for the *landschap* of that name.

**O. van Rees** was a Dutch colonial minister who served from [[March]] to [[August]] [[1879]], a brief but significant period in the [[Aceh War]] during Governor-General [[J.W. van Lansberge]]'s administration. Born in [[1823]], he was a senior colonial official with extensive experience in the [[Dutch East Indies]], having served as Vice-President of the [[Council of the Indies]] from [[1873]] to [[1878]].

## Early Career

Van Rees entered colonial service in [[1837]] and held various positions including Resident of [[Kedoe]], [[Bagelen]], [[Soerabaia]], and member of the [[Council of the Indies]] ([[1864]]-[[1872]]). He was elected to the [[Second Chamber]] in [[1872]] and served as Vice-President of the [[Council of the Indies]] from [[1873]] to [[1878]]. He was appointed Minister of Colonial Affairs in [[March]] [[1879]] following [[P.P. van Bosse]]'s death.

## Aceh Policy

Van Rees' brief ministry was dominated by the [[Aceh]] question:

1. **Support for aggressive action**: He supported Van Lansberge's instructions to General [[K. van der Heijden]] to pursue "total submission" of the XXII and XXVI Mukims.

2. **Impatience with progress**: He grew frustrated with the slow pace of operations, telegraphing: "How is the absence of reports from Aceh to be reconciled with your instruction of [[21 March]], with which I fully concur?"

3. **Questions about the military position**: He asked for clarification about the planned concentration of Dutch positions.

4. **Civil governance**: He supported Van Lansberge's plans for introducing civil governance, though he did not live to see it implemented.

## Financial Policy

Van Rees continued the financial reforms initiated by Van Bosse:
- He reduced the fixed contribution to 4 million guilders.
- He brought rents and amortization of loans for the Indies onto the Indian budget.
- He proposed that surpluses should be used to cover previous deficits, provide for Indian needs, and reduce Indian debts.

## Resignation

Van Rees' ministry ended in [[August]] [[1879]] when the cabinet resigned following King [[William III]]'s refusal to agree to a constitutional amendment promoted by [[J. Kappeyne van de Coppello]]. His resignation marked the end of a brief period of liberal colonial policy.

## Legacy

Van Rees' role in colonial history is often overshadowed by his more prominent predecessors and successors. However, his brief ministry was significant for:
- His support for aggressive action in Aceh.
- His questioning of the military position, which led to clearer strategic thinking.
- His role in the financial reforms that continued the movement away from the "batig slot" system.

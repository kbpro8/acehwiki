**Panglima Dagang** is the legendary son of [[Saëda (Gayo progenitor)|Saëda]] — the founder of the [[Kédjoerön Boekét|Kédjoerön Boekét]] line — and the progenitor of the *Boekét Lah* branch of the [[Gajoland|Gayo]] Boekét clan, whose descendants eventually prevailed in the long contest with the rival *Boekét Ewéh* branch for the Boekét chieftaincy. *De Buitenbezittingen* follows the *Lah* tradition, which it judges "de minst onbetrouwbare," in tracing the legitimacy of the *Kédjoerön Boekét Lah* line back to *Panglima Dagang*'s recovery of the *bawar* — the insignia of chieftaincy — through a decisive act of filial piety and political acumen.

## The dispersal after Saëda

According to the legend, when the invasion of the [[Bataq Doeloe Poeloh Toedjéh|Bataq doeloe poeloh toedjéh]] drove the Boekét family from the *Meerstreek* (the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] basin), [[Saëda (Gayo progenitor)|Saëda]] returned to Seroelë and disappeared from the legend. *Mérah Lama*, Saëda's son by his second (Acehnese) wife, went with his two stepbrothers *Ménèt* and *Mpos Kèlaq* to *Kébajakan*, where — in possession of the *bawar* — he was installed as *Kédjoerön Boekét*. *Panglima Dagang*, the younger full brother of *Mérah Lama*, settled in [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]].

## The recovery of the bawar

The constitutional crisis that defined the Boekét succession arose when *Ménèt*, the stepbrother, contrived to seize the principal *bawar* — the *keris kepala soeasa*, the gold-headed kris — and refused to return it. By Gayo *adat*, "wie de bawar heeft, ook kédjoerön is" — whoever holds the *bawar* is the *Kédjoerön* — so Ménèt claimed the dignity. The matter was further complicated by the *prang toedjéh tahoen* (the "ten-years war") that Ménèt's refusal to pay *haol* (tribute) to the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] provoked, which ended with the capture of many *rédjë*'s of the *Meerstreek* by the Acehnese. The ransom demanded for these chiefs included the surrender of the *keris kepala soeasa*; Ménèt, forced to comply, lost the *bawar* and, with it, his effective claim to the *Kédjoerön* title.

The resolution came from *Panglima Dagang*. Hearing of the matter in [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]], he returned to the *Meerstreek* and demanded from *Mérah Lama* the remaining *bawar*; he then succeeded in buying back the captured kris from the Atjehsche aanvoerder, thus reuniting all the *bawar* in one hand. He settled at *Seroelë* and assumed the title *Kédjoerön Boekét*. *Ménèt*'s forfeiture was complete: he had lost the kris, and his claim had been extinguished by the very act of surrendering it.

## Descendants and the Lah–Ewéh split

The descendants of *Panglima Dagang* became the *Boekét Lah* lineage, which held the office of *Kédjoerön Boekét* thereafter. The descendants of *Ménèt* became the *Boekét Ewéh*, who nevertheless continued to claim the title and to exercise many of its functions, exploiting the unwritten rule that the more accessible of the two *Kédjoerön*s would be the one approached for decisions. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that the Dutch eventually strengthened the *Boekét Lah*'s "wankelende positie" by extending recognition to the lawful *Kédjoerön*, though the *Ewéh* branch retained considerable influence, particularly in the great kampong of *Bintang* and across most of the Boekét territory outside the *Lah* seat at *Seroelë*.

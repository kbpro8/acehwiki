**Jongejans** was the [[Resident of Atjèh|Resident of Atjèh]] who preceded [[Pauw|J. Pauw]] in the post, and the author of *Land en volk van Atjèh* — one of the principal works on Atjèh cited by Piekaar in the *Bibliography* of *Atjèh and the War with Japan* alongside the works of [[Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]], Kreemer, and Zentgraaff. Piekaar's work refers readers to "the works of Dr. Snouck Hurgronje, Damsté, Kreemer, Jongejans, and Zentgraaff listed in the 'Bibliography'" for further detail on the local circumstances described in Chapters III and V.

## The residency and the agricultural action

Jongejans's residency was, in Piekaar's account, the period in which the action to expand Atjèh's rice production — begun by the agricultural extension service — was continued with ever-increasing success. "Under Residents Jongejans and Pauw this action was continued with ever-increasing success. Projects for extensive irrigation works were developed." The continuity of policy between Jongejans and Pauw reflected, in Piekaar's reading, the shared recognition that the time had arrived to bring Atjèh "to development at a rapid pace through joint effort."

## The handover to Pauw

Jongejans was succeeded by [[Pauw|Pauw]] on [[30 September]] [[1938]]. The handover marked the transition from one connoisseur of Atjèh to another: Piekaar characterises both men as belonging to "the excellent connoisseurs of Aceh's land and people." The continuity of the residency — the unbroken line of connoisseurs — was, in Piekaar's account, one of the factors that gave the Dutch administration its pre-war strength, even as the political-religious movements of the late 1930s were building beneath the surface.

## The work on Atjèh

Jongejans's *Land en volk van Atjèh* — "Land and People of Atjèh" — was, in Piekaar's reading, one of the principal scholarly resources on Atjèh available to the colonial administrator. The work, alongside those of Snouck Hurgronje, Damsté, Kreemer, and Zentgraaff, formed the canon of Atjèh scholarship on which Piekaar himself drew in the preparation of *Atjèh and the War with Japan*. The footnote to Piekaar's discussion of the F-organisatie, for example, refers the reader to "J. Jongejans, 'Land en volk van Atjèh, vr'" — a reference to the relevant section of Jongejans's work.

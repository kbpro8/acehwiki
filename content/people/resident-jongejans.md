**Jongejans** — **J. J. Jongejans** — was the [[Resident of Atjèh|Resident of Atjèh]] who preceded [[Pauw|J. Pauw]] in the post, and the author of *[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|Land en volk van Atjèh vroeger en nu]]* (1939) — one of the principal works on Atjèh cited by [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]] in the *Bibliography* of *Atjèh and the War with Japan* alongside the works of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]], [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]], and Zentgraaff. Piekaar's work refers readers to "the works of Dr. Snouck Hurgronje, Damsté, Kreemer, Jongejans, and Zentgraaff listed in the 'Bibliography'" for further detail on the local circumstances described in Chapters III and V.

## The residency and the agricultural action

Jongejans's residency was, in [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]]'s account, the period in which the action to expand Atjèh's rice production — begun by the agricultural extension service — was continued with ever-increasing success. "Under Residents Jongejans and Pauw this action was continued with ever-increasing success. Projects for extensive irrigation works were developed." The continuity of policy between Jongejans and Pauw reflected, in Piekaar's reading, the shared recognition that the time had arrived to bring Atjèh "to development at a rapid pace through joint effort."

## The handover to Pauw

Jongejans was succeeded by [[Pauw|Pauw]] on [[30 September]] [[1938]]. The handover marked the transition from one connoisseur of Atjèh to another: Piekaar characterises both men as belonging to "the excellent connoisseurs of Aceh's land and people." The continuity of the residency — the unbroken line of connoisseurs — was, in Piekaar's account, one of the factors that gave the Dutch administration its pre-war strength, even as the political-religious movements of the late 1930s were building beneath the surface.

## The work on Atjèh

Jongejans's *[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|Land en volk van Atjèh vroeger en nu]]* — "Land and People of Atjèh, Past and Present" — was, in [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]]'s reading, one of the principal scholarly resources on Atjèh available to the colonial administrator. The work, alongside those of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]], [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]], and Zentgraaff, formed the canon of Atjèh scholarship on which Piekaar himself drew in the preparation of *Atjèh and the War with Japan*. The footnote to Piekaar's discussion of the F-organisatie, for example, refers the reader to "J. Jongejans, 'Land en volk van Atjèh, vr'" — a reference to the relevant section of Jongejans's work.

## Additional account from *Literatuur-Overzicht* ([[1940]]) — the 1939 publications

Jongejans was, in [[1939]], one of the principal Atjeh-focused authors of the year. His publications, as recorded by [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]] in the *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939]]* (The Hague, [[1940]]), include:

- **[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|No. 571]]** — *Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu* ("Land and people of Atjeh, past and present"), published at [[Baarn|Baarn]] in [[1939]] with maps and illustrations (*Met krt. en ills.*). The book is the principal published work by Jongejans recorded in Van Meurs's survey, and it is the basis of his citation in the [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]] canon.
- **[[Taboet- of Hasji-Hosseinfeest (Jongejans, 1939)|No. 571a]]** — *Taboet- of Hasji-Hosseinfeest* ("Taboet or Hasji-Hossein festival"), an illustrated article in *Onze Aarde* (Our Earth), [[1939]], p. 326 *e.v.*, on the [[Taboet- of Hasji-Hosseinfeest (Jongejans, 1939)|Taboet or Hasji-Hossein festival]] — the Shi'ite-derived commemoration of the martyrdom of [[Husain|Hussein]] observed on [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]].
- **No. 572** — *Het heiligdom te Oelakan* ("The sanctuary at Oelakan"), an illustrated article in *Onze Aarde*, [[1939]], p. 305 *e.v.*, on the sanctuary at [[Oelakan|Oelakan]], a place of pilgrimage on the west coast of Atjeh.

The three pieces together — the book and the two *Onze Aarde* articles — mark Jongejans as one of the principal Atjeh-focused authors of [[1939]], and the book as the central statement of his survey of Atjehnese land and people. The companion pieces — on the [[Taboet- of Hasji-Hosseinfeest (Jongejans, 1939)|Taboet festival]] and on the [[Oelakan|Oelakan]] sanctuary — extend the survey into the religious and pilgrimage life of Atjeh.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Jongejans is significant as the former Resident of Atjèh whose [[1939]] survey *[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu]]* is one of the principal works on Atjèh in the [[Dr. A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]] canon, and whose [[1939]] publications — the book and the two *Onze Aarde* articles — make him one of the principal Atjeh-focused authors of the year recorded in Van Meurs's survey. His place in the canon — alongside [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]], [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]], and Zentgraaff — establishes the continuity of the Dutch scholarly-administrative tradition on Atjèh from the late-colonial period into the post-war historiography.

**Demmenie** — properly General (formerly Colonel) Demmenie — was a [[Netherlands|Dutch]] military officer who served as Governor of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in the period following the civil administration of [[Pruys van der Hoeven]]. He is named in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] as the officer who, despite his *goede eigenschappen* (good qualities), was *niet de man om Koningroeck op te volgen in Atjeh* (not the man to follow Kingroeck in Atjeh) — that is, was not the right successor to the civil governor [[Pruys van der Hoeven]] — and under whose governorship the *concentratie-stelsel* was officially introduced and *krachtig optreden* (forceful action) was made *bijkans ondoenlijk* (almost impossible).

## The civil administration and its collapse

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after the good results obtained by [[K. van der Heyden|Generaal Van der Heyden]] in the late [[1870s]] and early [[1880s]], the *Moederland* (the mother country) had formed the opinion that Atjeh was now *voldoende onderworpen* (sufficiently subjugated) to Dutch authority, and that it was *noodig* (necessary) to replace the military government with a civil administration. [[Pruys van der Hoeven]] was accordingly appointed Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies — and from that moment, the album records, *begint de lijdensgeschiedenis van onze vestiging op Atjeh* (begins the Passion of our establishment in Atjeh). The *lijden* (suffering) took on such proportions that a military governor had again to be sent to Atjeh — and that governor was Demmenie.

## The concentratie-stelsel

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Demmenie's governorship — like that of his successor [[Van Teijn]] — was conducted under the *ordedienst* (the official "order service" — the *concentratie-stelsel* in its institutional form), and that under this regime *krachtig optreden* was *bijkans ondoenlijk* (almost impossible). Neither Demmenie nor Van Teijn, *met hun geconcentreerde linie* (with their concentrated line), was able to bring the situation in Atjeh to the condition that the Netherlands and the Indies would have wished. The album's characterisation of Demmenie's governorship — *met zijn goede eigenschappen* (with his good qualities) but *niet de man om Koningroeck op te volgen* (not the man to follow Kingroeck) — is the album's principal judgement on the military governors of the *concentratie* period.

## The Lamgoet campaign

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that [[Lamgoet]] — the former seat of [[Teungku di Tiro]] — had, during Demmenie's governorship (and that of Van Teijn), *den generaals Demmenie en Van Teijn zoo lastig* (so troubled the generals Demmenie and Van Teijn). The album's reference places Lamgoet as one of the principal centres of Atjehnese resistance during the *concentratie* period, and it identifies Demmenie and Van Teijn as the governors under whom the Dutch were unable to dislodge the resistance from Lamgoet. The dislodgement was finally achieved by [[Toekoe Oemar]] in [[1893]], under the governorship of [[Deykerhoff]].

## Significance

Demmenie's significance in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is as the first of the military governors of the *concentratie* period — the governor under whom the *ordedienst* was officially introduced and under whom the Dutch were confined to the *geconcentreerde linie*. The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]]'s judgement on Demmenie — a man of good qualities but not the right successor to the civil governor — is the album's principal characterisation of the military governors of the *concentratie* period, and it identifies the *ordedienst* as the institutional form of the regime that the album holds responsible for the prolonged stalemate of the [[1880s]] and early [[1890s]].

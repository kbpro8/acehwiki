**Loudon** was the Governor-General of the Netherlands Indies in [[1873]] who, upon receiving the telegram of [[18 February]] [[1873]] from [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Minister Fransen van de Putte]], convened the [[Raad van Nederlandsch Indie|Council of the Netherlands Indies]] and proposed dispatching [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|vice-president Nieuwenhuyzen]] as *gouvernements-commissaris* with four battalions to [[Acheh|Atjeh]], setting in motion the chain of events that led to the [[First Atjeh expedition (March-April 1873)|first Atjeh expedition]] and the [[Declaration of war on Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war on Atjeh]].

## The decision to send the expedition (February 1873)

On [[22 February]] [[1873]], Loudon informed the Hague — by telegram — that the [[Raad van Nederlandsch Indie|Council of the Netherlands Indies]], presided over by the Governor-General and augmented by the commander of the army and the commander of the navy, had unanimously decided to send [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|the vice-president of the Council]] as *gouvernements-commissaris* to Atjeh with the necessary military force "to instil awe and to give weight to our demands." Loudon stated that he had already asked [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|Nieuwenhuyzen]] whether he was inclined to undertake this mission before receiving the ministerial telegram of [[18 February]] — a statement that contradicted [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]]'s representation in the [[Tweede Kamer|Second Chamber]] that the initiative had come from the Governor-General rather than from the Minister.

In a letter of [[25 February]] [[1873]] (No. 9 of the overgelegde stukken), Loudon wrote: "De noodzakelijkheid der expeditie is een uitgemaakte zaak. Zelfs al mogt de verzekering worden ontvangen dat Amerika zich even weinig als Italië zal bemoeijen. Aan de dubbelenzinnige politiek van Atjeh dient een einde te komen. Zoolang het onze souvereiniteit niet erkent, blijft vreemde tusschenkomst ons als het zwaard van Damocles bedreigen. Het fait accompli is het eenig redmiddel." — "The necessity of the expedition is settled. Even if assurance were received that America would as little meddle as Italy. Atjeh's ambiguous policy must end. So long as it does not recognise our sovereignty, foreign intervention threatens us like the sword of Damocles. The fait accompli is the only remedy." This passage, cited during the [[Tweede Kamer debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|Tweede Kamer debate]], demonstrated that the expedition was intended regardless of whether foreign interference actually existed.

## The ultimatum and departure from ministerial instructions

Loudon's *gouvernements-commissaris* [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|Nieuwenhuyzen]] departed from the ministerial instructions in a crucial respect: the ultimatum delivered to the Sultan on [[22 March]] [[1873]] demanded recognition of Dutch sovereignty as the first requirement, with only 24 hours (changed from the originally stipulated 14 days) for compliance, and then bombardment of the Sultan's residence would follow. The manifest sent ashore stated that it would be delivered to the Sultan that same day and would be confirmed "by the firing of some projectiles into or near the Sultan's residence, from which we are only 3,600 metres distant." [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] had telegraphed that sovereignty or war as a first demand would make a bad impression both in the Netherlands and abroad; Loudon insisted on it. The Minister's telegram of [[7 March]] stated: "Wij mogen en willen uwe handen niet binden door verbod, maar meenen dat souvereiniteit of oorlog als eerste eisch hier en elders slechten indruk zou maken" — "We may not and will not bind your hands by prohibition, but we believe that sovereignty or war as a first demand would make a bad impression here and elsewhere."

When Loudon nevertheless persisted — telegram of [[9 March]]: "Geen andere waarborg denkbaar dan souvereiniteits-erkenning; zonder dit expeditie geen zin" — and asked for definite orders or freedom to act on his own responsibility, the Minister ultimately acquiesced. The command was not refused.

## Assessment in the Tweede Kamer debate

During the [[Tweede Kamer debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|Tweede Kamer debate]] of April [[1874]], multiple speakers criticised Loudon's conduct. [[Haffmans|Haffmans]] argued that Loudon was inspired by the desire to please the Minister, believing the Minister to be energetic, and that Loudon would have acted entirely differently with a cautious Minister. "Stoute hoofden maken stoute handen" — bold heads make bold hands. [[Nierstrasz|Nierstrasz]] concluded that the honour and moral judgment of the war belonged exclusively to the Minister of Colonies, since the Minister had set the process in motion with the telegram of [[18 February]]; Loudon had merely responded to the Minister's initiative, though he went further than the Minister had indicated, and the Minister approved the further step on [[22 February]].

The [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Minister]] himself acknowledged the difference: "Het verschil betreft inleiding en vorm" — the difference concerns introduction and form — meaning that on the substance, the two were agreed: both wanted the war.

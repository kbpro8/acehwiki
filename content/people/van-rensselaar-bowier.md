**Jhr. Van Rensselaar Bowier** was a colonel of the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Royal Netherlands Navy]] who in July [[1879]] commanded the naval force that carried and landed the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] at [[Lampanas]] on the north coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]).

## The passage from Oleh-leh

At the embarkation of [[26 July]] [[1879]] Generaal [[General van der Heijden|van der Heijden]] himself was present aboard the steamship [[Soerabaya (Z.M. stoomschip)|Soerabaya]], where the colonel — "de Kolonel Jhr. Van Rensselaar Bowier" — held the command. In his spacious cabin the Colonel offered the General and the staff officers of the expedition the *rijsttafel*: a European calf, fattened on board, was slaughtered for the occasion and served as only a colonel of the Marine's European cook could prepare it — a rarity in the Indies, where veal is never seen, and one that the younger staff officers, the authors allow, attacked with memorable appetite, "besproeid met bier, wijn en champagne." In the longroom the ship's officers entertained the officers of the land force at their own table, and long after the General had left the ship, and after all the boats had steamed away from the roadstead of Oleh-leh at four o'clock, glowing toasts were still being drunk to the land- and sea-service. At half past eight in the evening, in bright moonlight, the squadron anchored on the roadstead of Lampanas.

## The landing

On the morning of [[27 July]] [[1879]] the Colonel took post with the column-commander and his officers under the awning on the afterdeck of the Soerabaya, watching the landing point, while his adjudant — the luitenant ter zee 2e klasse [[Titzing]] — moved among the gathering boats in a fast flet regulating the landing sloops. Under his direction four steamships — the [[Merapi (Z.M. stoomschip)|Merapi]], Soerabaya, [[Borneo (Z.M. stoomschip)|Borneo]] and [[Graaf van Bijland (stoomschip)|Graaf van Bijland]] — put ashore 46 officers, 1,104 officers' underofficers and men, 984 labourers, horses, guns and stores, beginning at six o'clock; the horses were lowered by tackle from the high warship's side upon an iron pontoon raft towed by the steam barkas, and the last stores were landed at eight in the evening.

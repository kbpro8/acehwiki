**T. Bén Boebon** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[H. J. Schmidt]]

In later years he executed a commission in Teunòm: the submission of a band more than three hundred strong under [[T. Bén Boebon]] and his nephew [[T. di Rangkoh]], with which the infantry patrols could obtain almost no contact. Knowing the district and the population through and through, Schmidt resolved to bring about a peaceful submission without shooting and without "neerleggen." It succeeded: T. di Rangkoh and his whole band reported themselves. "The people looked wretched, their clothes hung in tatters about their emaciated bodies, and some of the eighteen women and children were practically naked." They told how, after the raid on their *ladang*, they had lived exclusively on *katèla* and palm hearts, and how for fear of betrayal and discovery they had sought contact with nobody. They fell hungrily on the rice and fish the *marechaussée* at once made available, and next morning marched with the troops back to the inhabited world, three days' march away, where they were handed over to friendly chiefs, provided with clothing and permitted to settle with their families in the *gampong*. Swart noted on the journal: "You have achieved a fine result, the more so since no use was made of weapons."

### From [[Teunòm]]

**Teunòm** was the *[[landschap]]* of the [[Westkust van Atjeh|west coast]] cleared by [[H. J. Schmidt]] in [[1909]] and the scene of his later bloodless commission against the band of [[T. Bén Boebon]].

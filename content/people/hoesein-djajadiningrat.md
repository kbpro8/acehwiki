**Hoesein Djajadiningrat** — more fully **Raden Hoesein Djajadiningrat** — was the Buginese-Javanese scholar whose doctoral dissertation *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* ([[1913]]), defended at the University of Leiden under [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and awarded a gold medal, is the principal modern study of the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten chronicle]], and a key source for [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s treatment of the chronicle's veiled-language register. The son of a former regent of [[Serang|Sérang]] and brother of the then-titular regent, trained under [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s eye, he was, De Roo de la Faille notes, "by excellence the person to enlighten us" on the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten]].

## The 1913 dissertation

Hoesein's *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* (Leiden, [[1913]]) — awarded a gold medal by the University — opens with a paraphrase of the chronicle's first part and follows it with a commentary on the legendary material, culminating in a "Karakteristiek der Javaansche geschiedschrijving". Hoesein treats the legendary material — the *Sandimaja* prologue, the *Tjioeng Wanara* legend, the *Sjech Djoemadilakbar* conversion story, the *Kadji Poerwa* allegory of the planted *doerianpit* — as partly fictitious, partly derived from a *handboek over Oudjavaansche klankleer* (handbook of Old-Javanese phonetics). [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws heavily on Hoesein's paraphrase, but demurs at the conclusion: the legendary material is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, not fiction but *bedekte taal* — veiled-language forms that hide references to the Sumatran east coast and the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere.

## Hoesein on Faletahan / Sunan Gunung Jati

Hoesein's identification of the "Moor Faletahan from Pase" with the king of [[Sunda Kelapa|Sunda Tagaril]] — i.e. with [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]] — rests on the *swagerschap* (brother-in-law relation) of both with the lord of [[Japara|Japara]] and the king of [[Demak|Demak]]. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] accepts Hoesein's identification, and uses it as the foundation for his own conflation of [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes of 1513]] with [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]] of the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]]: "the king of Demak of [[1546]] was the same as he who around [[1524]] was king of Japara and later became prince of Demak, the same as the Pate Oenoes of [[1513]] and the Raden Sahid-Soenan Kalidjaga of the [[Sadjarah Banten]] and the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|BTDj.]]".

## Hoesein on the *Karakteristiek*

Hoesein's closing "Karakteristiek der Javaansche geschiedschrijving" argues — citing [[Dr. J. Brandes|Brandes]]'s *Pararaton* — that the Javanese chronicle tradition is "an artful, and an artificial" literature, whose "uniformity of design, the frame of the stories, the conventional language" must be understood as the work of lettered men working within a tradition. He closes, citing Spinoza: "*non ridere, non lugere, neque detestari, sed intelligere*" — "not to laugh, not to mourn, not to detest, but to understand". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] takes this as the motto of his own work, but argues that Hoesein's *Karakteristiek* did not in fact understand the legendary material: "I confess that, from what was set out on that account, I have not succeeded in such [understanding]; I was rather led astray by it on a false trail. [...] Is that not what one calls *foppen* [to fool]?"

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Hoesein Djajadiningrat matters as the principal modern source on the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten chronicle]] — the source on which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws for the [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]] identification, the [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes]] conflation, and the [[Sandimaja|Sandimaja]] prologue. His [[1913]] dissertation is the starting-point for any *bedekte taal* reading of the chronicle, and his *Karakteristiek* — even where De Roo de la Faille demurs — sets the standard of scholarly care against which such readings must be measured.

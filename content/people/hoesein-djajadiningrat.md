**Hoesein Djajadiningrat** — more fully **Raden Hoesein Djajadiningrat** — was a Bantenese-Javanese scholar of Indonesian history and letters whose doctoral dissertation *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* ([[1913]]), defended at the [[University of Leiden]] under [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and awarded a gold medal, is the principal modern study of the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten chronicle]]; and whose article in *Tijdschrift van het Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen* (*TBG*) 69 (pp. 97 ff.) established the chronology of the Malay works of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and supplied the principal chronological pegs of Raniri's Achehnese career. The son of a former regent of [[Serang|Sérang]] and brother of the then-titular regent, trained under [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s eye, he was — in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s phrase — "by excellence the person to enlighten us" on the [[Sadjarah Banten]], and is the principal authority on whom [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] relied in the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's Malay treatises.

## The 1913 Dissertation: *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten*

Hoesein's *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* ([[Leiden]], [[1913]]) — awarded a gold medal by the [[University of Leiden|University]] — opens with a paraphrase of the chronicle's first part and follows it with a commentary on the legendary material, culminating in a "Karakteristiek der Javaansche geschiedschrijving" (characteristic of Javanese historiography). Hoesein treats the legendary material — the *Sandimaja* prologue, the *Tjioeng Wanara* legend, the *Sjech Djoemadilakbar* conversion story, the *Kadji Poerwa* allegory of the planted *doerianpit* (durian seed) — as partly fictitious, partly derived from a *handboek over Oudjavaansche klankleer* (handbook of Old-Javanese phonetics). [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws heavily on Hoesein's paraphrase, but demurs at the conclusion: the legendary material is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, not fiction but *bedekte taal* (veiled language) — forms that hide references to the Sumatran east coast and the [[Aceh|Atjehnese]] sphere.

## Hoesein on Faletahan / Sunan Gunung Jati

Hoesein's identification of the "Moor Faletahan from Pase" with the king of [[Sunda Kelapa|Sunda Tagaril]] — i.e. with [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]] — rests on the *swagerschap* (brother-in-law relation) of both with the lord of [[Japara|Japara]] and the king of [[Demak|Demak]]. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] accepts Hoesein's identification, and uses it as the foundation for his own conflation of [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes of 1513]] with [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]] of the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]]: "the king of Demak of [[1546]] was the same as he who around [[1524]] was king of Japara and later became prince of Demak, the same as the Pate Oenoes of [[1513]] and the Raden Sahid-Soenan Kalidjaga of the [[Sadjarah Banten]] and the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|BTDj.]]".

## Hoesein's *Karakteristiek* and De Roo de la Faille's Dissent

Hoesein's closing "Karakteristiek der Javaansche geschiedschrijving" argues — citing [[Dr. J. Brandes|Brandes]]'s *Pararaton* — that the Javanese chronicle tradition is "an artful, and an artificial" literature, whose "uniformity of design, the frame of the stories, the conventional language" must be understood as the work of lettered men working within a tradition. He closes, citing Spinoza: "*non ridere, non lugere, neque detestari, sed intelligere*" — "not to laugh, not to mourn, not to detest, but to understand". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] takes this as the motto of his own work, but argues that Hoesein's *Karakteristiek* did not in fact understand the legendary material: "I confess that, from what was set out on that account, I have not succeeded in such [understanding]; I was rather led astray by it on a false trail. [...] Is that not what one calls *foppen* [to fool]?"

## The Chronology of Raniri's Malay Works

Djajadiningrat's article in *TBG* 69, pp. 97 ff. — and especially pp. 109–111 — is the principal authority for the chronology of Raniri's Malay works. He establishes:

- The year of Raniri's arrival in [[Aceh|Acheh]]: [[1637]].
- The presence of Raniri, in [[1641]], together with a number of high functionaries of the Achehnese court, at the ceremony of erecting the tombstone for Sultan [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar II]].
- The dates of Raniri's Malay works, so far as reliably attested, between [[1634]] and [[1644]].
- The return of Raniri to [[Ranir]] in [[1644]].
- The *terminus ante quem* of Raniri's death: before [[1666]].

Djajadiningrat also corrected the earlier assumption of [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] (*BKI* 107, p. 357) that the [[Sirat al-Mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] had been composed before Raniri's arrival in Acheh. The work was begun in [[1634]], as Voorhoeve had supposed, but was — as Djajadiningrat showed — only completed ten years later, in [[1644]]. The knowledge of Malay letters displayed in the [[Sirat al-Mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] need not, therefore, have been acquired before [[1637]]. Voorhoeve candidly confessed in the introduction to his [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises that Djajadiningrat's article — which he had earlier overlooked in his notes in *BKI* 107 (p. 353 ff.) — had supplied the chronological pegs on which his later understanding rested.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Hoesein Djajadiningrat matters on two fronts. First, he is the principal modern source on the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten chronicle]] — the source on which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws for the [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]] identification, the [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes]] conflation, and the [[Sandimaja|Sandimaja]] prologue; his [[1913]] dissertation is the starting-point for any *bedekte taal* reading of the chronicle, and his *Karakteristiek* — even where De Roo de la Faille demurs — sets the standard of scholarly care against which such readings must be measured. Second, he is the principal authority for the chronology of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]]'s Achehnese career; his *TBG* 69 article supplies the chronological pegs on which the modern understanding of Raniri's life and writings rests, and corrects the earlier assumptions of [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] on the composition of the [[Sirat al-Mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]]. The fact that an Indonesian scholar — and one of Bantenese descent — should have established the chronology of the principal Malay author of seventeenth-century Acheh is itself a significant moment in the modern study of Acehnese letters.

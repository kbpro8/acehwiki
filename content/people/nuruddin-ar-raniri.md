**Nuruddin ar-Raniri** was an Indian-Arab scholar of the [[Shafi'i|Shafi'ite]] school who arrived in [[Acheh]] in [[1637]] and, under [[Sultan Iskandar II]] and Queen [[Safiatuddin Tajul Alam]], became the chief orthodox voice against the [[Wujudiyyah]] mystics of [[Hamzah Fansuri]] and [[Syamsuddin of Pasai]].

He left [[Acheh]] in [[1644]] and died before [[1666]].

## Origins and family

The author's full lineage is given as Nūruddīn Muhammad b. 'Alī b. Hasandjī b. Muhammad Hamīd ar-Rānīrī al-Kurasjī asj-Sjāfi'ī. Voorhoeve confirms, following Drewes, that his father's town was [[Ranir]] near [[Surat]] in [[Gujarat]].

He came from a network of Hadrami-Indians who maintained contact with Arabia by correspondence and study travel, and with Southeast Asia by trade. Neither his birth year nor his death year is transmitted.

Key relatives:
- **Uncle**: [[Muhammad Jilani Hamid]] — reached [[Acheh]] between [[1580]]–[[1583]], attempted to teach logic, rhetoric and dogmatics, found little interest, sailed to [[Mecca]], returned with a *venia docendi* in mystic theology and then succeeded.
- **Spiritual grandfather**: [[Muhammad al-Aidarus]] — born [[1561]] in [[Tarim]], came to Gujarat aged 19, succeeded his grandfather at [[Ahmadabad]] then [[Surat]], died there in [[1620]].
- **Grand-uncle**: [[Abd al-Kadir al-Aidarus]] — died [[1628]] at [[Ahmadabad]], author of many letters and treatises.
- **Teacher**: [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Shaiban]] — of Hadrami descent, born in India, initiated into the [[Rifa'iyah order]] by al-Aidarus at Surat, lived mainly at [[Bijapur]] under [[Muhammad Adil Shah]], died [[1656]] at [[Bilgram]]. The same Sajjid later taught [[Sheikh Yusuf of Makassar]], who left [[Celebes]] for Mecca in [[1644]].

Nuruddin learned from his uncle's experience: he mastered *bahasa Djawi* (court Malay) in Gujarat, was initiated into the [[Rifa'iyah order]] founded by Ahmad al-Rifa'i, and arrived already having written two Malay works.

## Arrival in Acheh

During the reign of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] ([[1607]]–[[1636]]), the court was dominated by the heterodox mystic [[Syamsuddin of Pasai]], who died in [[1630]] but whose pupils remained in favour. Nuruddin could expect no patronage then.

After Iskandar Muda's death in [[1636]], the climate changed. Nuruddin reached Acheh in [[1637]], shortly after [[Sultan Iskandar II]]'s accession. In the presence of the sultan he refuted the adherents of extreme [[Wujudiyyah]] in a public debate.

## Court service

Voorhoeve records Nuruddin acting in [[1641]] together with high court functionaries at the erection of the gravestone for [[Sultan Iskandar II]]. He enjoyed royal favour both from Iskandar II and from his successor, Queen [[Safiatuddin Tajul Alam]].

## Works composed in Acheh

Dates of his Malay works, as far as reliable, lie between [[1634]] and [[1644]]:
- Two works completed *before* [[1637]]: a creed manual and a collection of Arabic hadith with Malay translation.
- [[Sirat al-Mustakim]] — begun [[1634]], but, as Djajadiningrat showed, only completed in [[1644]] in Acheh.
- [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan]] — encyclopedia of religions and sects, written to warn against heresies.
- [[Hudjdjat as-siddik li-daf az-zindik]] — short polemic wholly against the [[Wujudiyyah]].

Voorhoeve rejects the old hypothesis that Nuruddin wrote his first Malay books in Mecca; his sources are not Meccan contemporaries but widely circulated works and specifically Indian authors like [[Abu Shakur al-Salimi]].

## Departure and death

In [[1644]] Nuruddin returned to [[Ranir]]. He must have died before [[1666]], the terminus given by Djajadiningrat.

## Significance

Nuruddin marks the pivot from the speculative monism of Hamzah and Syamsuddin to a Shafi'ite, text-based orthodoxy sponsored by the Achehnese throne. His reliance on [[Abu Shakur al-Salimi]]'s *al-Tamhid* and on the sect-list of *Ma'rifat al-madhahib* gave Achehnese scholars a systematic taxonomy of heresy that would shape court debates for a generation.

**Pati Oenoes** — also **Pate Unus**, in Javanese tradition **Pangeran Sabrang Lor**, and in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s conflation **Raden Sahid** / **Soenan Kalidjaga** — was the early-sixteenth-century Javanese commander who, as lord of [[Japara|Japara]] (and later [[Demak|Demak]]), led the great Javanese naval attack on Portuguese [[Malacca]] in [[January]] [[1513]], married his younger sister around [[1523]]–[[1524]] to the [[Faletahan|Paseier Faletahan]] who became [[Cirebon|Sunan Gunung Jati]], and is identified by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] with the [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]] of the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] and the [[Sadjarah Banten]]. The identification, which De Roo de la Faille defends at length in *[[Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java]]*, makes Pati Oenoes a key link in the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]–[[Acheh|Atjeh]]–Java triangle of the early sixteenth century.

## The 1513 attack on Malacca

In [[January]] [[1513]], Pati Oenoes — then lord of [[Japara|Japara]] — led a great Javanese fleet against Portuguese [[Malacca]] in an attempt to retake the city for the Muslim trade. The attack failed; Pati Oenoes saved himself and his fighting-junk by a hurried return to Japara. There, De Roo de la Faille records, "as a memorial of his bold deed he had this junk placed under a shed on the beach", a vessel "as large as a Portuguese ship of 500 tons, caulked with seven layers of a mixture of lime and oil". The fame of the deed was, [[Castanheda]] noted, so great "that the Javanese even now [sc. before 1552] speak of this deed, and on account of it made him king of a city called [[Demak|Demak]]".

## Lordship of Japara and marriage to Faletahan

[[Tome Pires|Tome Pires]]'s *Suma Oriental* (~[[1515]]) describes Pati Oenoes as "the knight of whom the Javans speak because they say he is a great warrior in Java and very prudent", lord of a great deal of land, whose grandfather had been a working man in the islands of Lawé ([[Borneo|Borneo]]) and had come to [[Malacca]] with little means. Pati Oenoes, Pires records, "took the island of [[Bangka|Banka]] under his jurisdiction" — probably [[Bengkalis|Bengka-lis]], "the vanished Bangka" — "and that of [[Tandjoengpoera|Tanjungpura]] (in [[Langkat|Langkat]] and Laue and other inland lands), and made his country great".

Around [[1523]]–[[1524]], Pati Oenoes — by then lord of [[Japara|Japara]] — gave his younger sister as wife to the [[Faletahan|Paseier Faletahan]] who had crossed to Java after the [[1521|fall of Pasej]] to the Portuguese. In this marriage, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] sees the dynastic link that brought a north-Sumatran Paseier into the Javanese Islamic establishment, and so the conduit by which the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] tradition entered the [[wali sanga|Javanese wali tradition]].

## Identification with Raden Sahid / Soenan Kalidjaga

The boldest claim of [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s chapter *Over Soenan Kalidjaga* is that the Pate Unus of [[1513]] and the Raden Sahid / Soenan Kalidjaga of the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] and [[Sadjarah Banten]] are one and the same person. The identification rests on several convergences: both are associated with the "pasar" at which Raden Sahid was converted by [[Soenan Bonang|Pangeran Bonang]]; both are connected with the building of the [[Demak|Demak mosque]] (traditionally dated [[1468]] Saka, but which De Roo de la Faille reads as [[1546]] A.D. — the year of the [[Demak's attack on Pasoeroewan (1546)|Demak attack on Pasoeroewan]]); and the title *Pati Oenoes* is itself, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, a *bedekte taal* form for the *patya-oenoes* — "market-master of the *noenoes-an*" (the toll on the pasar of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]) that the *petjat tonda* (market-master) of [[Raffles|Raffles]]'s *History* collected.

## The mosque of Demak and the kjahi goendil

The [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] records that when the *wali*s built the [[Demak|Demak mosque]], [[Soenan Kalidjaga]] — having gone to *tirakat* at Pamantingan — returned late, gathered the *tatal* (shavings) into a bundle, and raised the *saka goeroe* (main pillar) in a single act. From the heavens fell a bundle containing the *saléndang* of the Prophet — which the *wali*s tried to divide but which [[Soenan Bonang|Soenan Bonang]] insisted be thrown up; the bundle fell to Soenan Kalidjaga, who then sewed it into the *kjahi goendil* — the leather over-garment that later Javanese kings were to wear at their installation and on the field of battle. "Sultan Demak and Sultan Padjang did not do so", the *Babad* adds — and De Roo de la Faille glosses: "that was their misfortune, for the *kjahi goendil* was a coat of mail, such as was used of old in Java. [...] Both sultans, at the moment when murderous steel threatened their lives, did not have that protection".

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Pati Oenoes matters as the Javanese commander whose [[1513]] attack on [[Malacca]] marked the first Muslim attempt to retake the city from the Portuguese — a strategic echo of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate's own [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|war on Aroe in 1539]] — and as the dynastic link by which a [[Faletahan|Paseier religious figure]] entered the Javanese Islamic establishment. His identification, by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], with [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]] also makes him, in the *bedekte taal* reading, the *"Coming Man"* of the [[ngélmoe sadjati]] tradition — the king of justice whose coming the Javanese *wali*s awaited.

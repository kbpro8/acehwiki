**Von Gilsa** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Tgk. Ma'at]]

With Tgk. Ma'at the line of the Tirò *oelama's* — the family whose earlier heads [[Teungkoe di Tirò]], who died in [[1891]], and [[Teungkoe Mat Amin]], killed at [[Aneu' Galong]] in [[1896]], had led the religious resistance since the beginning of the [[Atjeh-oorlog]] — came to an end. Du Croo described the struggle against and the eventual annihilation of "the holy Tirò Teungkoes" by Schmidt and his two famous *marechaussée* brigades under [[Molier]] and [[Von Gilsa]], who devoted years exclusively to that task, as "the great epic of those stirring days," already described as a classic example of the colonial *kleine oorlog* in *[[Military Constabulary in Atjeh (1943)|Marechaussee in Atjeh]]*.

### From [[H. J. Schmidt]]

The special commission given him in December [[1909]] was completed on [[3 December]] [[1911]], after two heavy patrol years in continuous and complete independence, when the last surviving Tirò Teungkoe, the young [[Tgk. Ma'at]], fell after refusing a final proposal of submission. Du Croo described the struggle against the *heilige* Tirò Teungkoes by Schmidt and his two famous *marechaussée* brigades under [[Molier]] and [[Von Gilsa]], who devoted years exclusively to this task, as "the great epic of those stirring days," already treated at length in *Marechaussee in Atjeh*.

### From [[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies]]

The corps's greatest single achievement of the pacification years was the destruction of the [[Tirò Teungkoes]] by [[H. J. Schmidt]] and his two famous brigades under [[Molier]] and [[Von Gilsa]], who devoted years exclusively to that task — "the great epic of those stirring days … a classic example from the colonial "small war"," described at length in *[[Military Constabulary in Atjeh (1943)|Marechaussee in Atjeh]]*.

## Overview

F. W. A. L. de Nijs was a Dutch colonial administrator who served as *Controleur van de onderafdeeling Oeléë-Lhenë en de XXVI Moekims* (Controller of the Oeléë-Lhenë and XXVI Moekims sub-district) in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] during the early twentieth century. He was present at the *bestuursvergaderingen* (administrative meetings) convened at [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[19 January]] [[1914]] and [[20 January]] [[1914]] to discuss the *bestuursreorganisatie* (administrative reorganization) of the Netherlands Indies. Although he attended both days of proceedings, no recorded remarks appear in the *notulen* (minutes) of the meetings, suggesting that he listened to the discussions without contributing verbally to the formal record. His presence nonetheless indicated the inclusive approach taken by Gouverneur [[H. N. N. A. Swart]] in gathering officials from across the Gouvernement to hear about and respond to the sweeping reforms proposed under the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]].

## The Onderafdeeling Oeléë-Lhenë en de XXVI Moekims

The *onderafdeeling* (sub-district) of Oeléë-Lhenë en de XXVI Moekims occupied a strategically vital position within the administrative geography of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. Oeléë-Lhenë—now known as Ulee Lheue—was the principal port area of [[Koeta-Radja]], the capital of the Gouvernement, and served as the primary gateway for maritime traffic, military logistics, and commercial trade entering and leaving Atjeh. The harbor at Oeléë-Lhenë had been a focal point of the Dutch military campaign since the initial expeditionary landing in 1873, and its control was considered essential for maintaining the colonial presence in the region. The XXVI Moekims, meanwhile, represented a cluster of twenty-six *moekim* (village cluster) administrative units that formed part of the indigenous governance structure of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The *moekim* system, described by Gouverneur [[H. N. N. A. Swart]] during the Koeta-Radja meetings as comparable to Javanese *onderdistricten* (sub-districts), was a key element of the native administrative architecture, with each moekim grouping several *gampong* (villages) under the leadership of a *keucik* (village head) and a *moekim*-level official. The combination of the port area with the XXVI Moekims in a single *onderafdeeling* reflected the Dutch practice of pairing urban or commercially significant zones with their surrounding rural administrative units, ensuring coordinated governance across both the maritime entry point and its hinterland.

## Role in the Bestuursreorganisatie

The *bestuursvergaderingen* at [[Koeta-Radja]] on 19 and 20 January 1914 were convened by Gouverneur [[H. N. N. A. Swart]] as part of a wider series of consultations across Sumatra concerning the comprehensive *bestuursreorganisatie* (administrative reorganization) of the Netherlands Indies, a reform program initiated by the central government in Batavia under the direction of the *Gouverneur-Generaal*. The Koeta-Radja meetings were particularly significant because they addressed the future status of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], which had maintained a unique and semi-autonomous administrative structure under the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* since its establishment in the late nineteenth century. De Nijs, as *Controleur* of one of the most strategically important *onderafdeelingen* in the Gouvernement, would have had a direct professional interest in the proposed changes to the administrative hierarchy, which included the potential abolition of the Gouvernement as a separate entity and its incorporation into a newly proposed *gewest* (region) of [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]]. Although he did not speak during the recorded proceedings, his attendance alongside other *controleurs* and *civiel gezaghebbers* reflected the principle that officials at every level of the colonial administration should be informed of and given the opportunity to respond to the reorganization plans that would fundamentally reshape their professional duties and jurisdictions.

## The Controleur in the Atjeh Administrative Hierarchy

The position of *Controleur* was a critical link in the chain of colonial governance in the Netherlands Indies, and in Atjeh it carried particular significance due to the territory's unique administrative structure. In the standard Indies hierarchy, the *Controleur* served as the European official immediately below the *Resident* or *Assistent-Resident*, responsible for direct supervision of the *Inlandsch Bestuur* (Native Administration) within his assigned territory. In the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], however, the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* exercised powers comparable to those of a *Gouverneur-Generaal* within his jurisdiction, and the *controleurs* and *civiel gezaghebbers* operating under him functioned in a more militarized and directly governed environment than their counterparts in other parts of the Indies. The *Controleur* was expected to maintain detailed knowledge of local conditions, mediate between Dutch authorities and indigenous officials, oversee the collection of revenues, and ensure the implementation of colonial policy at the *onderafdeeling* level. De Nijs's assignment to the Oeléë-Lhenë en XXVI Moekims *onderafdeeling*—encompassing both the critical port zone and a substantial portion of the *moekim* network in the capital region—suggests that he was regarded as a competent and trusted administrator, given the sensitivity of the post.

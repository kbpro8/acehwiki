**Goeman Borgesius** — Hendrikus Goeman Borgesius ([[1844]]-[[1917]]) — was a Dutch liberal member of the [[Tweede Kamer]] whose principal contribution to the Atjeh-era parliamentary record is the common-sense liberal statement that the entire Dutch distinction between *gewone* (ordinary) and *buitengewone* (extraordinary) expenditure was *ijdel gepraat* — idle talk. The proposition dissolved, by argumentative elimination, the budgetary defence of the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The 1905 budget debate

In the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]-period debate on whether extraordinary expenditure — railways, irrigation works, school-building, the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|Atjeh-oorlog]] — should be financed by borrowing, [[Goeman Borgesius|Goeman Borgesius]] (lib.) observed that "al het geredeneer over gewone en buitengewone uitgaven en inkomsten ijdel gepraat" was — all the reasoning about ordinary and extraordinary expenditure and income was idle talk. It was, he said, "een vreemde scholastiek, die sommige ministers en kamerleden bedreven om te betogen, dat de kosten van de Atjeh-oorlog v.v. niet gedragen werden door Java maar door het Banka-tin of door de inkomstenbelasting voor Europeanen" — a strange scholasticism that some ministers and chambermembers practised to argue that the costs of the Atjeh-oorlog etc. were not borne by Java but by the Banka tin or by the income-tax on Europeans.

## The liberal alternative

The "zienswijze van Borgesius" — the view of Borgesius — was that all expenditure together should be met from all income together, and that, when the total available sum was smaller or larger than initially expected, all expenditure would be proportionally reduced or could be increased. [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] observes that this view "een eind had kunnen maken aan de uitvoerige discussie, wat precies uit de ƒ 40 millioen, die Nederland in [[1905]] aan Indië zou schenken, kon worden gefinancierd" — could have put an end to the lengthy discussion of what precisely could be financed out of the ƒ 40 million that the [[Netherlands]] was to grant to the Indies in [[1905]]: education, road-building, defence — it made no difference.

## Significance

[[Goeman Borgesius|Goeman Borgesius]]'s intervention is the procedural-liberal counter to the scholastic budgetary defence of the Atjeh war. The Borgesius principle — all expenditure together from all income together — supplied the commonsense platform on which the subsequent [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] budgets of the [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]]-[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]]-[[Th. B. Pleyte|Pleyte]] period were built.

## Account from *Atjeh in de Tweede Kamer* ([[1897]])

Goeman Borgesius appears twice in the record of the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November|16]]–[[17 November]] [[1897]], in both cases in connection with the question of Dutch cruelties in [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

[[H. van Kol]] named him on [[17 November]] among those who had previously uttered grave accusations on the subject, complaining that ministers sought to dispose of such charges — "ook door de heeren [[Des Amorie van der Hoeven]] en Goeman Borgesius vroeger uitgesproken" — with the answers "die berichten zullen wel overdreven zijn" or "daarvan is mij niets bekend," while the whole of the Indies had knowledge of them.

Later in the same sitting the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] quoted the words with which the Minister [[Mr. J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]] had closed an extensive argument on [[22 September]] [[1896]] "in antwoord op eene vraag van den heer Goeman Borgesius" — that the exchanged telegrams showed sufficiently that the Netherlands and Indies governments were both most strongly opposed to *mutileeren*, that if the isolated case had occurred it was not wholly inexplicable in an army composed of different elements, that it would certainly not happen again, and that the [[Governor-General of Netherlands India|Governor-General]] had forbidden it in the strictest terms. Cremer declared that the government of the day fully subscribed to what had then been said.

Goeman Borgesius's question of [[22 September]] [[1896]] is thus the parliamentary precedent to which both the critics and the defenders of the government appealed in the [[1897]] debate on [[Atrocities in the Aceh War as alleged in the Tweede Kamer (1897)|the atrocities]].

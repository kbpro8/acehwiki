**Herman Neubronner van der Tuuk** ([[1824]]–[[1894]]) was a Dutch linguist, lexicographer, orientalist, and manuscript collector, whose essay on Malay manuscripts and notes in *Bijdragen* ([[1866]]) are extensively cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Hurgronje]], particularly regarding the literary works and the history of religious teachers in [[Acheh]], including [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]], [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin]], and [[Abdurra'uf of Singkel|Abdurra'uf]]. His legacy supplied the Leiden University Library with the only complete manuscript of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]], and he was the first European scholar to take note of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq li-dafʿ az-zindīq*]] of Raniri, which he recorded in *BKI* 13 ([[1866]]), p. 464.

## The Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 3291

The Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 3291 of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] is the only complete manuscript of the work. It is, as [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] records, "a not-very-careful copy, probably made about [[1855]] for Van der Tuuk from a good older exemplar that came from Acheh." Its Achehnese provenance is betrayed by such forms as *sidjuk* (Ach. *sidjueʾ*) for *sedjuk*, and by the consistent replacement of *kesembilan* with *kesemilan* (cf. Ach. *seumah*, Mal. *sembah*). The manuscript is the basis of Voorhoeve's facsimile edition of [[1955]] and is designated **B** in his apparatus criticus.

## The 1866 notice of the *Ḥujjat*

Van der Tuuk was also the first European scholar to take note of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq li-dafʿ az-zindīq*]] of Raniri. In *BKI* 13 ([[1866]]), p. 464, he recorded the existence of an Arabic text of the treatise in a manuscript of the Bataviaas Genootschap. The manuscript — long thought lost — was recovered by Dr. **[[R. Roolvink|R. Roolvink]]** (KBG 420 Mal., not in the catalogue), and is designated **A** in Voorhoeve's apparatus to the *Ḥujjat*. Comparison showed that the copyist, working from a complete Malay exemplar, had transcribed only the Arabic words and sentences written in red ink, producing an incoherent text that nonetheless contains fewer copyists' errors than the Maxwell manuscript and was repeatedly useful for its correction.

## Hurgronje's citations

Van der Tuuk's essay on Malay manuscripts and his notes in *Bijdragen* ([[1866]]) are extensively cited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Hurgronje]] in *[[The Achehnese]]*, particularly regarding the literary works and the history of religious teachers in Acheh, including [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]], [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin]], and [[Abdurra'uf of Singkel|Abdurra'uf]]. Through these citations Van der Tuuk's philological work became one of the foundations on which Hurgronje built his account of the religious and literary history of the Achehnese sultanate.

## Significance

Van der Tuuk is, through his collection and his 1866 notice, the principal channel by which the two Malay treatises of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] entered European scholarship. The Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 3291 of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] — copied for Van der Tuuk about [[1855]] from an Achehnese exemplar — is the only complete manuscript of the work and the basis of the [[1955]] edition. His 1866 notice of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat*]] in *BKI* 13 is the first European record of that treatise; and the recovery of the Arabic manuscript he had noted (KBG 420 Mal.) by Dr. [[R. Roolvink|R. Roolvink]] made it possible for Voorhoeve to collate the Arabic and Malay texts of the *Ḥujjat*. His *Bijdragen* essay, as Hurgronje's citations attest, further supplied European scholarship with one of its principal early guides to the literary and religious-history materials of Acheh.

**Herman Neubronner van der Tuuk** (1824–1894) was a Dutch linguist, lexicographer, and manuscript collector, whose legacy supplied the Leiden University Library with the only complete manuscript of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]]. The manuscript — Leiden Cod. Or. 3291 — was copied about [[1855]] for Van der Tuuk from a good older exemplar of Achehnese provenance, and is the basis of [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]]'s facsimile edition of [[1955]]. Van der Tuuk was also the first European scholar to take note of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] of Raniri, which he recorded in *BKI* 13 (1866), p. 464.

## The Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 3291

The Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 3291 of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] is the only complete manuscript of the work. It is, as Voorhoeve records, "a not-very-careful copy, probably made about [[1855]] for Van der Tuuk from a good older exemplar that came from Acheh." Its Achehnese provenance is betrayed by such forms as *sidjuk* (Ach. *sidjueʾ*) for *sedjuk*, and by the consistent replacement of *kesembilan* with *kesemilan* (cf. Ach. *seumah*, Mal. *sembah*). The manuscript is the basis of Voorhoeve's facsimile edition of [[1955]] and is designated **B** in his apparatus criticus.

## The 1866 notice of the *Ḥujjat*

Van der Tuuk was also the first European scholar to take note of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq li-dafʿ az-zindīq*]] of Raniri. In *BKI* 13 (1866), p. 464, he recorded the existence of an Arabic text of the treatise in a manuscript of the Bataviaas Genootschap. The manuscript — long thought lost — was recovered by Dr. **R. Roolvink** (KBG 420 Mal., not in the catalogue), and is designated **A** in Voorhoeve's apparatus to the *Ḥujjat*. Comparison showed that the copyist, working from a complete Malay exemplar, had transcribed only the Arabic words and sentences written in red ink, producing an incoherent text that nonetheless contains fewer copyists' errors than the Maxwell manuscript and was repeatedly useful for its correction.

## Significance

Van der Tuuk is, through his collection and his 1866 notice, the principal channel by which the two Malay treatises of [[Raniri]] entered European scholarship. The Leiden manuscript Cod. Or. 3291 of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] — copied for Van der Tuuk about [[1855]] from an Achehnese exemplar — is the only complete manuscript of the work and the basis of the [[1955]] edition. His 1866 notice of the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat*]] in *BKI* 13 is the first European record of that treatise; and the recovery of the Arabic manuscript he had noted (KBG 420 Mal.) by Dr. R. Roolvink made it possible for Voorhoeve to collate the Arabic and Malay texts of the *Ḥujjat*.

**W. J. J. A. van den Bosch** was the Medan representative of the B.P.M. (*Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij*, the Batavian Petroleum Company, a subsidiary of Royal Dutch Shell) who, in February [[1950]], was one of eight technicians who participated in a B.P.M. survey party (*survey-party*) sent to [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] to assess the condition of the company's oil installations.

## Findings in Atjeh

Following the visit, Van den Bosch could provide only minimal information. The factory grounds (*fabrieksterreinen*), which had already been partially destroyed before the Japanese invasion, were found to be completely ruined. The oil fields (*olievelden*), however, were in a somewhat patched-up condition (*enigszins opgelapte staat*). Van den Bosch stated that he could not comment on the extent of the damage. The destroyed oil installations in Atjeh had been a significant economic asset, and their condition was a matter of concern for the provincial economy.

**J. Langhout** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[1923]]

In **[[1923]]** [[J. Langhout]] published *Economische Staatkunde in Atjeh*, listed among [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]]'s sources. From [[1923]] to [[1925]] there was heavy *[[katjau|katjauen]]* in the Indies over the charge that the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] had been bought.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**Joh. Langhout** was a Dutch journalist who compiled and wrote *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ("Fifty Years of Economic Statecraft in Atjeh"), published in [[1923]] by [[W. P. van Stockum & Zoon]] at [[The Hague]] on the occasion of the fiftieth anniversary of the Dutch declaration of war on the [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah|Sultan of Atjeh]] of [[26 March]] [[1873]]. His book, furnished with a foreword by Lieutenant-General [[H. N. A. Swart]], former Civil and Military Governor of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], and an introductory word by the then-serving Governor [[Van Sluys|A. G. H. van Sluys]], remains one of the few works of the Atjeh literature devoted primarily to the economic — rather than the military — history of the [[Government of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

### Origin of the work

In his introduction, dated "Weltevreden, Leiden, 1922–1923", Langhout relates that it was not the "yellowed leaves of our political history" nor the voluminous official folios at the [[Ministry of Colonies (Netherlands)|Ministry of Colonies]] that reminded him of the almost forgotten date, but an ordinary old *Leidsche Almanak* (Leyden almanac), in which under the heading *Korte Kronyk* ("Short Chronicle") he read: "1873. 26 Maart is namens de Nederlandsche Regeering, na herhaalde pogingen tot minnelijke schikking, aan den Sultan van Atjeh de oorlog verklaard en 6 April met de krijgsverrichtingen begonnen" ("26 March, on behalf of the Netherlands Government, after repeated attempts at an amicable settlement, war was declared on the Sultan of Atjeh, and on 6 April the hostilities were begun"). That chance reminder decided him to compile, "in a simple, popular manner", an account of what the Netherlands Government and private initiative had accomplished economically in Atjeh and its Dependencies during the fifty years since [[1873]].

### Purpose

Langhout gave a twofold answer to the question why only the economic results of Dutch rule were treated and not the military profits. First, the interested reader who asked a library for reading matter on the history of Atjeh received stacks of books devoted to the art of war — memoirs and studies by officers of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|Netherlands Indian army]] — while on the *Koninklijke Bibliotheek* (Royal Library) at The Hague he had embarrassed an official by asking for "some reading" on Atjeh's statecraft and economics; searching together, they had found, with a few exceptions, "Krijgskunde zonder eind" ("the art of war without end"). Secondly, he held that the Netherlands in Atjeh had, in the economic-statal field, "iets groots verricht" ("accomplished something great") — a claim the book sets out to prove with facts, numerous photographs ("talrijke foto's"), and a sparing use of figures.

### The finished book

The work appeared in [[1923]] with the full title *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh, geschreven naar aanleiding van de herinneringsdata 26 Maart 1873–26 Maart 1923*, a foreword of [[31 March]] [[1923]] by General [[H. N. A. Swart]] (then recently Vice-President of the [[Council of the Indies]]), and an introductory note of February [[1923]] by Governor [[Van Sluys|A. G. H. van Sluys]]. Its contents run from "Het Atjeh van vroeger" ("The Atjeh of former days") through the government [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification]] by period, the [[Atjeh Tram]], [[Sabang]], the shipping companies, the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|petroleum exploitation]] in [[Perlak]], the timber cutting on [[Simaloer]], rubber planting on the East Coast, the "pillars of the pacification" (the [[Nieuwsblad voor Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh press]], the [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij]], the banks and trading houses) and [[Foreign capital in Atjeh|foreign capital in Atjeh]], closing with a word of thanks (*Slotwoord*).

### Acknowledgements and collaborators

In the *Slotwoord* Langhout records that the Government, the Minister of Colonies [[S. de Graaff]] and the heads of the several departments of his ministry supplied the data needed beside the series of [[Koloniaal Verslag|Koloniale Verslagen]] (Colonial Reports); that retired senior officials who had personally given their strength to the pacification shared their insights; and that private initiative furnished every desired piece of information. He thanks General [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] in particular for directions and particulars beside the foreword, and Professor Dr. C. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] at Leyden, "who, with [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], lived through so great a portion of Atjehnese life", for the many hours he spent supplying data. A special word of appreciation went to [[C. Th. L. Damen]], adjunct-clerk of the Department of Colonies, who at all hours answered the recurrent requests for "lectuur". The travel report of [[N. van Zalinge]], director of the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij]], was made available by the Amsterdam directorate, and illustrations of dry docks by the [[Burgerhout|N.V. Burgerhout]] machine factory at Rotterdam. For his chapter on the [[Atjeh Tram]] Langhout drew on the Colonial Reports, on an article by Ir. [[J. H. Müller]] in the *Indisch Tijdschrift voor Spoor- en Tramwegen*, and on communications and photographs supplied by Ir. [[J. H. Caspers]], former chief of construction of the tramway.

### Assessment

Langhout explicitly left the judgement of his work "to others, to public opinion", declaring himself convinced that the Netherlands in Atjeh had done more than wage war. General Swart, recommending the book, noted that he did not wholly identify himself with every view of the author but fully agreed with its purport; the author, he wrote, belonged to those who dared to say a word of appreciation for what was done "in het waarachtig belang van een 'veroverd' land en van een 'overheerscht' volk" ("in the true interest of a 'conquered' country and a 'subjugated' people").

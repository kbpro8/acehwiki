**C. Snouck Hurgronje** — *Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje*, full name *Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje* — was a Dutch Islamologist and colonial advisor who, on commission of the Netherlands Indies government, came to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[July 1891|July]] [[1891]] under the governorship of [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe van Meerdervoort]] to study the politico-religious situation and to report on it; remained there until [[February 1892|February]] [[1892]]; and produced the secret report whose published residue, with government approval, became the work *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]* ([[1906|1906]]). His diagnosis of the Atjehese resistance as a fanatical popular party led by the clergy — not merely a few malefactors or robber bands, as [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]] had maintained — was the intellectual foundation of the subsequent Dutch policy of suppressing the religious leadership and restoring the *adat* chiefs, carried through by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]].

## The 1889 Plan and the 1891 Mission

Already in [[1889]] Snouck Hurgronje would have gone incognito from [[Penang|Penang]] to Atjeh, when the opportunity was so much more favourable and the intrigue over the restoration of the Sultanate was in full swing. But [[General van Teijn|General Van Teijn]] then saw no use in it. Under [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]] Snouck Hurgronje came to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[July 1891|July]] [[1891]], with a commission from the government to study the politico-religious situation there and to report on it. He remained there until [[February 1892|February]] [[1892]].

## The Diagnosis

Snouck Hurgronje saw the disastrous result of so many years of labour, of so many wasted millions and human lives — the quackery that had everywhere prevailed, trying all the little remedies without cooperation, often working against each other, only to fall just short of success and then, condemning and breaking what had gone before, to try something new again. He also saw that the Dutch in all Atjeh did indeed have a fanatical popular party led by the clergy against them, and not merely a few malefactors or robber bands. He stood amazed at the little that the Dutch had come to know in those twenty years about land, people, and conditions, and advised the establishment of an information bureau — an *Intelligence Department* — for Atjehse affairs.

## The Secret Report and *De Atjehers*

Snouck Hurgronje's secret report was never published, but the remarkable results of his investigation were, insofar as the government approved, reworked and laid down in his work *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]*. Yet much leaked from that secret report, from which it would appear that the author was: for a [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] like Pompe, but with the object of putting power in the hands of the Dutch, not of pleasing the Atjehers; for aggressive action in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], but only to crush the fanatical priest-party and to restore the lawful *adat* chiefs to the place that belonged to them; further, to be fair but strict, everywhere and in everything; eye for eye and tooth for tooth, mercilessly to punish where this should prove necessary, to show that the Dutch had the means to do so; to destroy and to annihilate with fire and sword, without mercy, where resistance was offered or the Dutch were deceived.

This, Kruijt observes, is also the advice of every Atjeher, when he expresses his opinion on the tactic to be followed against an enemy.

## Significance

Snouck Hurgronje's mission to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in [[July 1891|July]] [[1891]]–[[February 1892|February]] [[1892]] is the intellectual turning point of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. His diagnosis — that the Dutch faced not a few malefactors but a fanatical popular party led by the clergy — refuted the optimistic assessment of [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]] and prepared the way for the systematic suppression of the religious leadership under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]]. His advice to establish an *Intelligence Department* for Atjehse affairs would be implemented, and his doctrine — that the Dutch must crush the priest-party and restore the *adat* chiefs, by merciless punishment of resistance — would shape Dutch counter-insurgency doctrine in Atjeh for the next decade. The published residue of his work, *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]* ([[1906|1906]]), would become the standard scholarly reference on the Atjehese people and the principal source for later histories of the war.

**Captain van Randwijk** was a Dutch *kapitein der infanterie* and *commandant* of the [[Pakan Atjeh]] post, whom [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] singles out for praise for having, on his own initiative, cleared the *boschrand*, built palissades of *klapperstammen*, *randjoes* and *verhakkingen*, and made the post "bewoon- en verdedigbaar" — and who, on [[17 August]] [[1874]], marched his company out at dawn and seized the *grafheuvel* east of Pakan Atjeh at the double-quick by surprise, the action that became the [[Occupation of the ooster-benteng (17 August 1874)|occupation of the ooster-benteng]].

## The Pakan Atjeh Post

Pakan Atjeh, on the right bank of the [[Kroeng Daroe]] east of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], was one of the keystones of the [[Strategische driehoek (Atjeh)|strategische driehoek]] Kwala Atjeh–Kota Radja–Oeleh Leh that Borel credits to [[Generaal Pel|Pel]] rather than to [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]. The post, under van Randwijk's command, was armed with [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|12 c.M. achterlaadkanonnen]]. On his own initiative — "*op eigen initiatief*" — van Randwijk cleared the *boschrand* around the post, built palissades of *klapperstammen* (coconut-palm trunks), *randjoes* (thorn-branch abatis) and *verhakkingen* (entanglements), and made the post *bewoon- en verdedigbaar*. Borel's praise is explicit: van Randwijk is one of the subalterns whose enterprise compensated for the shortcomings of the *opperbevelhebber*.

## The Ooster-Benteng

For several days before [[17 August]] [[1874]] the Dutch had noticed extraordinary enemy activity. On the morning of the 17th van Randwijk marched his company out at dawn and seized the *grafheuvel* (burial mound) east of Pakan Atjeh at the double-quick by surprise. Sandbag parapets were thrown up at once. The Atjehneezen, realising that the Dutch intended to stay, raised the alarm on gongs and gathered from every side — including across the river from [[Lemboe]], [[Langkroek]] and [[Pinang (kampong)|Pinang]] — and attacked strongly. The Dutch on the mound were caught in crossfire; one detachment after another was sent from headquarters. The whole garrison stood to arms; artillery on all bastions opened up; *kartetsen* (canister) were used at Pakan Atjeh. By six o'clock in the evening the mound was *verdedigbaar*. The cost was more than twenty dead and wounded. Borel laments that seven months after the Kraton's capture a hard fight was needed for a scrap of ground within rifle-shot of the parapet — but the new post, named the *ooster-benteng*, immediately proved its worth: the Atjehneezen could do nothing against Pakan Atjeh, which enjoyed an unprecedented quiet thereafter.

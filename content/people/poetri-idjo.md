**Poetri Idjo** ("the Green Princess") is a legendary queen remembered in the traditions of [[Deli]] and [[Asahan]] on [[Sumatra]]'s east coast, whom P. de Roo de La Faille interprets as the human embodiment of the Buddhist goddess [[Tara|Cyama-Tara]], patroness of [[Sumatra]], and as a memory of the pre-Islamic royalty of [[Aroe Kingdom|Aroe]].

## Legend

According to east-coast Malay tradition, Poetri Idjo built the *Kota Djawa*, whose remains lie along the road from [[Medan]] to [[Belawan]] at [[Deli-Toewa]]. [[John Anderson]], in *Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra* ([[1825]]), p. 293, noted there "remains of a Javanese fortification" — which de Roo reads as *Djawaka*, not Javanese.

The [[Sultan of Acheh]] pressed her with a marriage proposal. She refused, fell ill, and died. Her body was placed in a glass coffin to be taken to Acheh, but ship and all were swallowed at sea.

## Etymology

De Roo notes that *idjo* ("green") corresponds to Kawi *sijama* ("dark-green, blackish"). Her realm is thus *sijam-Awak* of the Djawaka, and she herself is the earthly likeness of *Cyama-Tara*, "Redder from all Misery", the Buddhist Heavenly Queen.

## Original seat

Her original dwelling, in this reading, was on the island in [[Aroebai|Aroe Bay]] called [[Poelo Kampi]], and later the *negorij* of the raja at the mouth of the [[Tandjoeng Poera]] river at [[Koewala Boeboen]] — the same zone associated with [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]].

The story thus preserves, in veiled form, the conflict between the Hindu-Buddhist east-coast polity ([[Aroe Kingdom|Aroe]]) and the expanding Islamic sultanate of [[Acheh]] in the sixteenth century: the sultan's marriage proposal to the Green Princess — and her death in refusal — is the legendary counterpart to the historical [[Fall of Aroe to Acheh (1539)|Achehnese conquest of Aroe in 1539]] and the flight of the historical [[Aroe Kingdom|queen of Aroe]] to [[Malaka]] and [[Johor]].

## Significance

Poetri Idjo is the legendary embodiment of the [[Aroe Kingdom]]'s pre-Islamic religious and political tradition: the Green Princess whose colour is the Buddhist Cyama-Tara's, whose original seat was the sacred island of [[Poelo Kampi]], and whose refusal of the Achehnese sultan's proposal is the tradition's veiled record of Aroe's resistance to Islamic Acheh. De Roo's reading places her at the intersection of Hindu-Buddhist Sumatra, the [[Sejarah Melayu]] tradition, and the colonial-era east-coast oral histories of [[Deli]] and [[Asahan]].

**Hoogerheide** was a Dutch ophthalmologist, who died young, and who took his doctorate under [[Prof. van der Hoeve]] at [[Leiden]] on a dissertation concerning three thousand eyelid operations performed on [[Java]]. He was cited by [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* as the precedent for the village eye-surgery method that the Indies press had wrongly named after Tijssen himself.

## The precedent

Tijssen rejected the term *methode-Tijssen* used of his work in the [[Cataract campaign on the North Coast of Atjeh (1932)|Atjeh campaigns]]. The operation itself, he said, was one devised by great thinkers over the centuries; and the idea of going into the villages and operating on the spot, instead of waiting for what came into the hospitals, was "allerminst origineel."

"Eenige jaren geleden is bij prof. van der Hoeve de helaas te jong gestorven oogarts Hoogerheide gepromoveerd op een dissertatie over drieduizend ooglid-operaties op Java." — Some years ago the eye doctor Hoogerheide, who alas died too young, took his doctorate under Prof. Van der Hoeve on a dissertation about three thousand eyelid operations on Java. Tijssen invited his interlocutor to consider what material was worked up in such a thesis, treating so many operations of a single kind. Such a body of cases could never have been assembled by work in hospitals: "Daar komt het nooit bijeen." Hoogerheide too, he added, had worked in the *[[Kampong|kampong]]* and had performed operations in all possible and impossible places.

## Significance

The Hoogerheide dissertation supplied Tijssen with two things: proof that the village method yielded case material unobtainable in institutional practice, and an academic precedent that removed from his own Acehnese campaigns the character of an eccentric personal enterprise. In the same passage Tijssen named the parallel precedent of [[Elliot]] in [[British India|Britsch-Indië]].

**Ranir** — also written **Rānīr** — is a coastal town near [[Surat]] in [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]], the father-city of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and the principal node of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network from which the orthodox reaction of [[Acheh]] drew its principal Malay-Indonesian agents. The identification of Ranir as Raniri's father-city, established by [[G. W. J. Drewes|Drewes]] in *BKI* 111 (1955) and recorded by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in the introduction to his [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises, was the precondition for the modern understanding of Raniri's Gujarati-Ḥadramī background.

## Identification as the father-city of Raniri

Voorhoeve records, in the introduction to the [[1955]] edition, that Drewes's 1955 article in *BKI* 111 "recently threw new light on the heredity of the author; there can now be no doubt that his father-city was Ranir near Surat in Gujarat." The identification placed Raniri's family origin at Ranir — rather than at the various other places that had been proposed — and was the basis for the account of Raniri's family, his spiritual genealogy, and his connection with the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] that Voorhoeve gives in the introduction to his edition.

## The Gujarati milieu

The coastal towns of [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]] in this period had a very international population. Ships regularly sailed thence to the Strait of Malaka; among the mercenary troops of the sixteenth century were "Javanese" — that is, in this context, Indonesians. The Malay language was certainly not unfamiliar in the harbour towns; and the family of Raniri — Indian Arabs of mixed blood, who maintained contact with the motherland by correspondence and study-journeys, and who stood in relation with Indonesia — had its centre at Ranir. Ranir was, in this period, a node of the Indian Ocean network that linked the religious scholarship of [[Surat]], [[Ahmadabad]], [[Bijapur|Bījāpūr]], and [[Bilgram|Bilgrām]] with the Malay-Indonesian world.

## The spiritual genealogy

The spiritual genealogy of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Raniri]] — and of [[Yusuf Makasar|Shaikh Yūsuf al-Makassar]] — had its centre at Ranir. It was at Ranir that [[Muhammad al-Addars|Muḥammad al-ʿAddārs]] (d. [[1620]]) succeeded his famous grandfather as religious teacher; it was at Ranir that [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]] was received into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] by al-ʿAddārs; and it was from Ranir that Raniri himself set out for [[Acheh]] in [[1637]] and to Ranir that he returned in [[1644]].

## Ranir as a node of transmission

Ranir was, in the late sixteenth and early seventeenth centuries, one of the principal nodes by which the orthodox Sufi scholasticism of the Arab and Persian worlds was transmitted to the Malay-Indonesian archipelago. The Indian-Arab religious families of mixed blood — the al-Aydārūs family, the family of al-ʿAddārs, and the family of Raniri himself — formed the network that carried this transmission; and Ranir, with Surat and Ahmadabad, was one of the principal centres of the network. The fact that the family of Raniri had, even before his own time, maintained relations with [[Acheh]] — his uncle [[Muhammad Djailani Hamid]] having visited Acheh between [[1580]] and [[1583]] — is a measure of the directness of the connection between Ranir and the Achehnese court.

## Significance

Ranir is the father-city of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] and the principal node of the Gujarati-Ḥadramī Sufi network from which the orthodox reaction of [[Acheh]] drew its principal Malay-Indonesian agents. The identification of Ranir as Raniri's father-city by Drewes in [[1955]] was the precondition for the modern understanding of Raniri's background, and is the basis of the account of his family, his spiritual genealogy, and his connection with the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] that is given in the modern scholarship on Raniri and on the religious controversy of Acheh.

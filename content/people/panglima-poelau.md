**Panglima Poelau** — modern *Panglima Pulau* — was, in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, one of [[Teuku Umar]]'s "*beste onderaanvoerders*" (best sub-commanders), alongside [[Ketjig Bintang van Lam Loempoer]] and (by extension) [[Toekoe Mançoer|Toekoe Mançoer, Umar's brother]]. His name connects him to the [[Lam-Poelau|Lam-Poelau]] area of the western [[IV Moekims]] — a coastal kampong which became one of the principal contested locations of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] — and Kruisheer records that he commanded a mobile force of approximately 300 men armed with [[Beaumontgeweer|Beaumontgeweren]] (the modern Dutch service rifle), distinguished by their black uniforms and headcloths, during the [[23 May]] [[1896|23 May]] [[1896]] operations. His combination of elite status within Umar's command structure and command of a uniformed, breech-loading-armed mobile force marks him as one of the most militarily sophisticated Achehnese commanders of the first semester of [[1896]].

## Position among Umar's Beste Onderaanvoerders

Kruisheer's phrase "*beste onderaanvoerders*" places Poelau within the inner circle of Umar's military command — a circle which included his brother [[Toekoe Mançoer]] (who would be reported killed by [[28 May]] [[1896]]) and [[Ketjig Bintang van Lam Loempoer]] (who had been "formerly designated successor as commandant of Umar's legion" and would also be reported killed by [[28 May]] [[1896]]). The fact that both Poelau's principal colleagues were dead within a week of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] suggests the intensity of the combat in which Umar's inner command was engaged, and elevates the relative significance of Poelau's survival — Kruisheer does not record Poelau's death, and he must therefore be presumed to have continued in resistance after the May [[1896]] operations. The black uniforms and *hoofddoeken* which distinguished Poelau's force are notable for being a deliberate military uniform — not the customary Achehnese dress — and they suggest that Umar's *legioen*, originally raised and equipped by the Dutch in [[1893]], had retained its uniformed character even after defection, an institutional continuity which gave the resistance a distinct professional identity.

## Command on 23 May 1896

Poelau's command was engaged on [[23 May]] [[1896]], the first day of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive against Umar's stelling]]. On that day, four Dutch colonnes under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Kolonel Stemfoort]] advanced against the stelling of [[Lam-Asan]], [[Lam-Pisang]], [[Tjot Tjako]], [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Lam-Tjroeng]] and [[Lam-Poelau]]; Poelau's force of ~300 men with Beaumontgeweren formed part of the Achehnese defence. The use of [[Beaumontgeweer|Beaumontgeweren]] — breech-loading rifles which had been supplied to Umar by the Dutch before the defection (380 *achterlaad-* and 500 *voorlaadgeweren*, 25,000 Beaumont cartridges on [[26 March]] [[1896]] alone) — gave Poelau's force a firepower equivalent to that of the Dutch infantry, and the black-uniformed profile would have made his men visually distinctive on the battlefield. Kruisheer does not record the specific actions of Poelau's force on [[23 May]] [[1896]], but the location of [[Lam-Poelau|Lam-Poelau]] — to which his name is attached — was occupied by the Dutch on that day, and the marine bombardment of Lepong which had begun on [[25 May]] [[1896]] would have been within his operational area. The combination of mobile force, modern weaponry, and uniformed professional identity made Poelau one of the most capable field commanders on the Achehnese side in the spring of [[1896]].

## Significance

Panglima Poelau's significance in *ATJEH '96* lies in his embodiment of the institutional continuity between Umar's Dutch-equipped *legioen* of [[1893]]–[[1896]] and the post-defection Achehnese resistance. The black uniforms and Beaumontgeweren which distinguished his force were direct material legacies of the Dutch *bindingspolitiek*, turned against the colonial forces in the spring of [[1896]]. His survival, in contrast to the deaths of his colleagues [[Toekoe Mançoer]] and [[Ketjig Bintang van Lam Loempoer]], made him a key figure in the continuing Achehnese command structure after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]].

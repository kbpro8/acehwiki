**C. J. Laceulle** — *Luitenant-Kolonel* (Lieutenant-Colonel) of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] — was the commandant of the [[5e Bataljon]] and the *commandant der 3e colonne* at the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] of [[29 June]] [[1896]], the night attack on the *benteng* (fort) of [[Ana' Galong]] — *het hart van Groot-Atjeh* (the heart of Great Aceh) — which opened the second semester of [[1896]] in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *[[ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel]]*. As *commandant der 3e colonne*, Laceulle also served *tevens Comd. van het 5e Bat. Inf.* — simultaneously as commandant of the 5e Bataljon — and his force, with a strength of 14 officers and 445 *ond. off. en minderen* of infantry plus a section of *Bergartillerie* (Mountain artillery) under [[Van der Waarden|1e Luit. Van der Waarden]] (31 men, 5 horses, 12 pack-animals) and a detachment of *Genietroepen* (Engineers) under [[C. J. de Bruijn|1e Luit. C. J. de Bruijn]] (17 men), formed the western arm of the three-colonne attack.

## The 3e Colonne at the Surprise of Ana' Galoeng

The composition of the 3e colonne at the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] is recorded by Kruisheer in detail. *3e colonne. Comd. Luit.-Kol. C. J. Laceulle tevens Comd. van het 5e Bat. Inf. Sterkte: 14 off. en 445 ond. off. en minderen. Artillerie. Eene sectie Bergart. Comd 1e luit. van der Waarden. Sterkte: 31 ond. off. en minderen, 5 rijpaarden 12 trek- of draagdieren. Genietroepen. Een detachement: Comd. 1e luit. C. J. de Bruijn. Sterkte: 17 minderen. Trein. Eéne ambulance.* Laceulle's colonne was thus the smallest of the three — a single bataljon with a section of mountain artillery and a small detachment of engineers — but it was assigned the most demanding mission in the operation's cordon plan.

The role assigned to the 3e colonne was *op den rechteroever der Atjeh-rivier* — to cover the retreat of the 1e and 2e colonnes on the right bank of the Atjeh-rivier — and to this end it was *zou zich vastzetten in den Oostrand van Lam-Oe* — to fix itself in the eastern edge of Lam-Oe. *Door signalen zou het verband tusschen die beiden worden onderhouden* — the link between the two would be maintained by signals. The 3e colonne departed from Lambaroe at 6 AM, the last of the three colonnes to move, and was tasked with patrolling the kampongs between [[Tjot Goet]] and [[Belang]], *slechten der vijandelijke versterkingen Tjot Goet, Tjot Gloempang en Lamsoet* (levelling the enemy fortifications Tjot Goet, Tjot Gloempang and Lamsoet) — for so far as this had not already been done — and *de terugtocht op Siroen en Lambaroe in verband met de 2e colonne nemen* (taking the retreat on Siroen and Lambaroe in conjunction with the 2e colonne). *Branden en plunderen was voor deze colonne streng verboden* — burning and plundering was strictly forbidden for this colonne — a notable restriction in an operation otherwise characterised by the *tuchtiging* (punitive burning) of [[Sibreh]] and [[Rahat]].

## The Operations of Late [[1896]]

Laceulle's command of the [[5e Bataljon]] continued through the second semester of [[1896]], and the bataljon was engaged in the operations of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] and the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] in August and September [[1896]]. The 5e Bataljon's role in the [[Trip to XXII Moekims (23–27 August 1896)|trip to XXII Moekims of 23–27 August 1896]] and in the subsequent operations based on the [[Blang Bintang]] camp placed Laceulle at the centre of the slow forward movement of the second semester — though the published text of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* records fewer individual commands for Laceulle than for [[G. F. Soeters|Soeters]] or [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], reflecting the more static role assigned to his bataljon.

## Significance

Laceulle's significance in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* is that of the commandant of the western arm of the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] — the officer whose colonne was tasked with the most geographically extended cordon role in the three-colonne attack, and whose *branden en plunderen was voor deze colonne streng verboden* distinguished his command from the punitive *tuchtiging* (punitive expedition) assigned to the 1e and 2e colonnes. The 5e Bataljon which he commanded was one of the principal Dutch infantry units of the second semester of [[1896]], and its presence in the operations of the [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] in August and September [[1896]] places Laceulle at the centre of the slow forward movement which characterised the post-De Moulin period.

**Teukoe Tjhi Perlak** — also written *Teungkoe Tjhi* in the older form — was the *[[Oeléebalang|oeléebalang]]* of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula (Perlak)]] for the half-century before [[1913]], and the senior member of a family whose rule over Perlak dated to the original Atjehnese settlement of the landschap about a hundred and fifty years earlier. He was a *Groot-Atjeher* by origin, and an ancestor had moved from [[Groot-Atjeh]] to Peureula, settled there, and made himself master of the country. By the time of his death or retirement he was, in Broersma's judgement, the wealthiest and most far-sighted of the eastern-coast *oeléebalangs*, the first to see that *'onder hun vriendschap en medewerking de oliebelangen het beste tot hun recht komen'* — that under their friendship and cooperation the oil-interests would best come into their own.

## The *Jong van Peusangan*
As a young man Teukoe Tjhi had refused to learn the Quran — *'meng-adji'* — and was therefore forced to leave his country. He wandered over [[Malakka|Malakka]] and made his living by trade. He then settled for a time at [[Peudawa Rajeu|Peudawa Rajeu]] near Perlak. When the Dutchmen came cruising along the coast in [[1873]] and [[1874]] and sent their negotiators ashore, *'het avontuurlijke kind van Perlak, het jong van Peusangan'* — *the adventurous child of Perlak, the young one of Peusangan* — was already back, standing beside his father as a military commander. He is the *Teukoe Tjhi moeda Peusangan* of the war-years, the *'krijgshaftige aanvoerder'* of the [[1873]] generation, and was later *oeléebalang* of Peureula in his own right.

His friendship with the Dutch, repeatedly pledged and maintained, often obliged him to defend his country, his people, his possessions, and even his children against the *djahats* — the evildoers — from elsewhere who construed his peace with the *kafirs* as a betrayal. Broersma remarks that the *'scherpe, helsche, vol doodsverachting'* fight against the Dutch was reserved for the *kafir* who demanded submission and brought *overheersching*; the inter-Atjehnese hostilities of the time were *'tooneelachtig'* — theatrical.

## The Oil Cijns and the Great Wealth
Before the end of the nineteenth century the oil-interests of the *kafirs* had begun to smooth the paths of the old friendship. The petroleum cijns made the old chief rich. After [[1912]] the cijns came into the *landschapskas* and the *oeléebalang* received a fixed income. In [[1910]] he visited Java, where his son was at school in [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]]; he also visited the blind institute, the girls' school and the hat-plaiting workshops there, and stayed for a time at [[Batavia|Batavia]], where his sister was then living with her husband, an Atjehnese chief who had been banished to [[Ternate]] but was permitted in [[1910]] to live in Batavia. In [[1913]] he was able to hand over the government to his son, [[Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb|Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb]], and in [[1914]] he undertook the pilgrimage to [[Mekka|Mekka]] — nine months out and home, by the steamship *Riouw*, with twenty relatives and two servants in his following. After his return he went once more to Batavia, to his sister, who in [[1916]] returned to Atjeh as a widow.

## Retirement at Boga
The old chief then retired to his new substantial house at [[Koeala Boga|Boga]] on the sea, where he had planted his own coco-palms. Broersma describes the meeting there: *'een krasse baas, groot, roodbruin, grove rimpels op gelaat en handen. Hij droeg een hoogst eenvoudige kleeding: de kopia op het groote hoofd, een donkerblauw linnen buis, een kain, sloffen. Ons bood hij stoelen, zelf zette hij zich op een matje op den vloer.'* He was then, by his son's reckoning, ninety years old. He lived with his second wife; of his sixteen children twelve were still alive, and there were many grandchildren. He still walked every morning along the sea, *'waarover eenmaal de Nederlanders zijn gekomen, die al 50 jaar zijn vrienden zijn'* — *over which once came the Netherlands, who have been his friends for fifty years.*

## Significance
Teukoe Tjhi is the type-figure of the *vriendschap*-policy on the east coast: the *oeléebalang* who recognised, before most of his peers, that the oil-interests and the colonial government could be turned to the advantage of his landschap. The early recognition by the Perlak chiefs of the oil-wealth of their soil is the political condition of the later development of the [[Perlak Petroleum-Mij|Perlak Petroleum-Mij]] and the [[Zuid-Perlak Petroleum-Mij|Zuid-Perlak Petroleum-Mij]] at [[Rantau Pandjang|Rantau Pandjang]].

**J. B. Verhey** was a Dutch marine officer who rose from lieutenant in the mid‑1870s to colonel‑commandant of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] in the early [[1890s]], later serving as a member of the [[2nd Chamber of the States‑General|2e Kamer der Staten‑Generaal]]. He is known for authoring a detailed memorandum on marine operations on [[Sumatra]] (including the northern province of [[Aceh]]) between [[July]] [[1874]] and [[February]] [[1876]], and for receiving the [[Militaire Willems Orde|Militaire Willems Orde]] (4th class) for his role in the [[Edi River expedition (1890)]].  

## Early Marine Service and Sumatra Report (1874–1876)

As a lieutenant of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]], Verhey compiled the memorandum *Rapport van den luitenant der mariniers J. B. Verhey betreffende de verrichtingen der mariniers op Sumatra van Juli [[1874]] tot Febr. [[1876]]*. The report enumerates patrols, riverine assaults, and the establishment of coastal outposts, noting that “the mariniers were frequently engaged in skirmishes on the coast of [[Aceh]] and were tasked with protecting Dutch trade routes against local resistance.” The document, dated [[1874]], is a primary source for scholars studying Dutch marine operations during the later phase of the [[Aceh War]] and the broader strategy of integrating marine forces with the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]].

## Military Advancement and Honors

Promoted through the ranks, Verhey became colonel‑commandant of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] in the early [[1890s]]. In [[1890]] he was awarded the [[Militaire Willems Orde|Militaire Willems Orde]] (4th class) for distinguished service during the [[Edi River expedition (1890)]]. A contemporary portrait of Verhey in his uniform, painted by [[J. Hoynck van Papendrecht]], commemorates the award.

## Political Career

After his military service, Verhey entered politics, securing a seat in the [[2nd Chamber of the States‑General|2e Kamer der Staten‑Generaal]]. His experience in colonial military affairs informed his contributions to parliamentary debates on the Dutch East Indies, particularly regarding the conduct of the [[Aceh War]] and the administration of maritime security.

## Significance

Verhey’s career bridges two pivotal aspects of Dutch colonial history: the operational evolution of the marine forces in Sumatra during the 1870s and the high‑level command and diplomatic roles of senior officers in the 1890s. His memorandum provides granular insight into on‑the‑ground tactics, while his later honors and parliamentary service illustrate the pathways through which colonial military expertise influenced Dutch policy in the East Indies.

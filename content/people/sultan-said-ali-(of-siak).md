**Sultan Said Ali** — Sultan of [[Siak|Siak]] in the closing decades of the eighteenth century and the early decades of the nineteenth — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the Siak sultan under whose reign the Siak overlordship of the East Coast statelets was imposed by armed force and under whose death (in [[1821]]) the long decay of Siak power began. Said Ali's reign is, in the Bureau's framing, the high-water mark of Siak's East Coast overlordship — the moment at which the Siak sultanate replaced the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] overlordship that had lapsed in [[1769]] and imposed its own authority over the entire northern coast of Sumatra.

## Background: the Siak revival under Raja Ismail

The Bureau's account of [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]'s reign opens with the Siak revival under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]]. The Bureau records that [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]] — having been defeated and driven out to [[Pelalawan|Pelalawan]] in [[1761]] — had succeeded, in [[1779]], in restoring himself to the Siak throne. "Vooral gedurende het bestuur van Sultan Said Ali, die in het einde der achttiende eeuw weder Ismail's opvolger had verdrongen, breidde Siak zijn heerschappij over zijne nabuurstaten uit, tot in de Noordelijke kustlandschappen toe" — "Especially during the administration of Sultan Said Ali, who in the end of the eighteenth century had again driven out Ismail's successor, Siak extended its rule over its neighbouring states, even into the Northern coastal lands."

The Bureau's account thus presents [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]] as the successor of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Raja Ismail]] — the sultan who, having displaced Ismail's own successor, continued and amplified Ismail's project of Siak expansion. The "einde der achttiende eeuw" — "end of the eighteenth century" — fixes Said Ali's accession in the 1780s or 1790s, after Ismail's restoration of [[1779]].

## The Siak overlordship of the East Coast statelets

The Bureau records that [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]'s reign saw the imposition of Siak overlordship over the East Coast statelets that had briefly — between [[1769]] and the Siak expansion — enjoyed their independence from [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The Bureau records that the East Coast statelets — having "knew themselves again to be free" of the Atjehnese overlordship in [[1769]] — had "slechts korten tijd genoten zij hunne onafhankelijkheid; weinige jaren later reeds werden zij door het wapengeweld van Ismail en Said Ali gedwongen Siak als opperheer te erkennen" — "enjoyed their independence only a short time; a few years later they were already forced, by the armed force of Ismail and Said Ali, to acknowledge Siak as overlord."

The Siak overlordship thus imposed by [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]] was, in the Bureau's account, a sustained act of armed coercion. The two sultans operated together — "Ismail en Said Ali" — in a joint project of East Coast subjection that transformed the statelets from independent polities into Siak dependencies.

## The Siak zenith and the long decay

The Siak overlordship of the East Coast, imposed under [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]], was, the Bureau records, sustained as long as the Siak sultanate was powerful. "Zoolang dit rijk krachtig was, bleef deze toestand van onderhoorigheid gehandhaafd" — "As long as this realm was powerful, this state of dependency was maintained." The decay of Siak power began, however, immediately after [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]'s death in [[1821]]. "Doch de voortdurende binnenlandsche twisten, welke vooral na Said Ali's dood ([[1821]]) ontbrandden, deden dien invloed weldra tanen" — "But the continuous internal disputes, which especially after Said Ali's death (1821) broke out, caused that influence soon to wane."

The accession of [[Tongkoe Ismail|Tongkoe Ismail]] in [[1827]] accelerated the decline, and by the middle of the nineteenth century the East Coast statelets were, in effect, independent — until the [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship]] transferred their allegiance from [[Siak|Siak]] to [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

## Significance

[[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the high-water mark of Siak's East Coast overlordship. His reign — together with that of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] — represents the moment at which the Siak sultanate replaced the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] overlordship that had lapsed in [[1769]] and imposed its own authority over the entire northern coast of Sumatra. His death in [[1821]] opens the long decay of Siak power that would, within three decades, lead to the [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship]] and, within four decades, to the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|1858 cession of Siak to the Dutch]].

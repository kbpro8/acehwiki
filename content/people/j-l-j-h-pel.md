**J. L. J. H. Pel** was a Dutch officer who served as the first Military and Civil Commander of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] from [[1874]] to [[1876]], heading the list of regional administrators that the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's *De Buitenbezittingen* records in its Bijlage I under the heading "Hoofden van Gewestelijk Bestuur van 1874 tot heden." His appointment in [[1874]] — the year after the first Dutch military expedition against [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] — marks the formal inauguration of the Gouvernement as an administrative unit of the Netherlands-Indies, succeeding the unstable arrangements of the early [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] years.

## Military Career and Campaigns (1874-1876)

**J. L. J. H. Pel** was a colonel of the infantry — promoted major-general by extraordinary Royal Decree of [[18 February]] [[1875]] — who served as civil and military supreme commander in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from the departure of [[J. van Swieten|Lieutenant-general Van Swieten]] in [[April 1874]] until his sudden death from an arterial rupture at the kampong Tonga on [[24 February]] [[1876]], in the middle of the campaign that broke the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. His period of command was marked by the abandonment of Van Swieten's defensive policy in favour of systematic offensive operations, and by the formulation in his note of [[30 April]] [[1875]] of the program that guided Dutch strategy in Atjeh until [[1877]].

### Succession to Van Swieten

When [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]] and the bulk of the expeditionary force departed on [[26 April]] [[1874]], Pel succeeded him as civil and military supreme commander with a garrison of 128 officers, 1788 Europeans, 176 Africans, 489 Ambonese and 698 natives. Van Swieten's note of [[22 April]] [[1874]] had laid down a defensive policy: active defence of [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] itself, but elsewhere passive defence, on the ground that "the enemy's offensive strength is not great and will gradually diminish," and that his defensive strength was considerable owing to the nature of the terrain.

That this policy would not bring the war to an end was soon evident. Even before Pel's departure on leave in [[June 1875]] the enemy had resumed the struggle "with the greatest possible boldness and perseverance," and the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]] had to be occupied in May-June to connect Kota Radja with [[Marassa]] and [[Lampassei]]. The principal post on this line, [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] on the coast west of the Atjehrivier mouth, became the principal landing-place; a temporary bamboo sea-head was built, replaced on [[11 August]] by a wooden auxiliary head built by the engineers. The [[Noorderlinie|Noorderlinie]] was also strengthened with posts at [[Lamara]] and Kota Radja Bedil.

### The échec of 16 April 1874

Already on [[16 April]] [[1874]] Dutch troops had suffered an échec. A patrol had come upon an enemy fortification near Kota Radja; a column sent by Colonel Pel, then commandant of Kota Radja, failed to take it. "The position was so well covered that our fire had no effect, and a storming, twice undertaken, failed, so that one was forced to return with a loss of 9 dead and 86 wounded, among whom 10 officers." Van Swieten, already on the point of departure, did not think it necessary to wipe out this échec.

### Pel's Note of 30 April 1875

During his leave on Java, Pel succeeded in winning the government for his plans. His note of [[30 April]] [[1875]] to the Governor-General set out his analysis: the task assigned by Van Swieten had been fulfilled — the hoofd-stelling was secured, communication with the sea assured — but "all those victories have as yet given few political results." The war-party still had great influence; it consisted largely of persons who lived far enough from the theatre to be safe from immediate danger, including men from the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi XXII]], Kerkoen, Silang in the [[XXVI Moekims|Sagi XXVI]], and "persons from all regions, also from the dependent states, who take part in the war from religious zeal, or from a love of war, or because they have reason to consider themselves less safe in their own hearths." It was led by influential chiefs such as [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]], [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]], [[Toekoe Rajoet Lamkapang|Toekoe Rajoet Lamkapang]], and the family members of the former sultans, [[Toewankoe Haschim|Toewankoe's Haschim]], [[Toewankoe Hoesin|Hoesin]], and [[Toewankoe Abdoel Madjid|Abdoel Madjid]], "who rightly saw that they had nothing more to expect from a foreign overrule."

His program was: consolidation of the actual position; continuation of hostilities as soon as the fighting forces permitted, with the object of separating the enemy entirely from the sea and clearing the terrain near the Dutch establishments of enemies; introduction of direct administration in the occupied territory, with respect for the domestic rights and institutions of the native population, but avoidance of bringing under direct administration those regions that might prove willing to submit. Pel further urged the construction of a harbour with land-heads, docks and coal-depots, and good judicial administration. "It is the crown on the work; it is the only way to make our settlement useful and productive."

### The Campaigns of December 1875 – February 1876

The government authorised Pel by his instructions of [[10 November]] [[1875]] to act in this sense, and to subject successively the VI, IV and IX Moekims, and then the eastern territory as far as [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]]. On [[26 December]] [[1875]] at five in the morning three columns left the hoofd-stelling for the conquest of the [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]] south-west of Kota Radja. The enemy's fortifications at [[Mibouw|Mibouw]], [[Ketapan Doewa|Ketapan Doewa]] and [[Djempit|Djempit]] were taken and [[Pakan Badak|Pakan Badak]] occupied. "In six days the strong enemy line opposite our posts south of Kota Radja had been broken, turned and abandoned by the enemy. We were wholly master of the VI Moekims and, by the occupation of Pakan Badak, in possession of an important point that was to serve as a base for operations against the IV Moekims."

On [[15 January]] [[1876]] the advance into the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] began over the [[pas van Blang Kala|pas of Blang Kala]]. The path was so narrow that four mules with ammunition cases fell into the ravine; the guns had to be taken apart and carried by coolies. By the afternoon of [[17 January]] the troops descended into the plain of the IV Moekims; on [[18 January]] [[Kroëng Raba|Kroëng Raba]] was reached; on [[25 January]] the troops returned to Pakan Badak. In ten days, from [[28 January]] to [[7 February]], the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] was also reduced. Boekit Daroe, Boekit Teroen, the missigit of Oleh Soesoe, Toeram, Atoöh, the missigit of Biloel, Lamrong, Pagger Ajer were all taken in succession.

### Death at Tonga, 24 February 1876

The campaign then turned to the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]. Tjapoetoe, Oleh Karang, Lamjong Tonga were the first stages. "It was not given to General Pel, however, to bring it to an end. In the kampong Tonga, where he had his headquarters, he died suddenly of an arterial rupture in the evening of [[24 February]] [[1876]]: a great loss for the Netherlands-Indian army and the Dutch interests in Atjeh." The command passed temporarily to Lieutenant-colonel [[F. T. Engel|F. T. Engel]], and the operations were continued according to Pel's plan. Over [[Silang|Silang]] and [[Tjadé|Tjadé]] [[Kwala Gighen|Kwala Gighen]] was reached, and fortifications were laid out there and at Kota Pohama. But for the moment the troops could not advance to [[Kroëng Raja|Kroëng Raja]]: 1200 men were in hospital, 1893 had been evacuated between [[1 January]] and [[10 March]], and 503 had died in the hospital of Kota Radja alone.

## Administrative Office and successors

Pel's brief tenure established the dual military-civil character of the governorship that would persist, with periodic adjustments, for the next thirty years. He was succeeded in [[1876]] by *G. B. Th. Wiggers V. Kerchem* (in the same *Mil. en Civ. bevelhebber* capacity), then by *A. J. E. Diemont* ([[1876]]–[[1877]]). The structure was modified under *K. Heyden v. D.*, who served as *Wd. Mil. en Civ. bevelhebber* from [[1877]] to [[1881]] and was redesignated *Civ. en Mil. Gouverneur* in [[1878]] — the title that, with one interruption, would be borne by his successors down to [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]].

## Significance

Pel's appointment formalised the transition from ad hoc military occupation to the institutional structure of a *Gouvernement*. The very existence of the Bijlage I list — presenting an unbroken chain of commanders and governors from [[1874]] to the handbook's publication — was itself a political statement: a demonstration that, after thirty years of war, the territory had become a permanent part of the colonial administrative order.

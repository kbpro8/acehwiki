The **Dutch heroes of named patrols** — a combined article covering the named Dutch and indigenous personnel distinguished in specific actions of the first semester of [[1896]], as recorded in A. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — comprises the principal Dutch and indigenous figures whose individual acts of gallantry or operational prominence the balso records in detail. The most prominent among them are **1e Luit. C. T. J. Stakman** (1st Lieutenant of Infantry, wounded in the right arm on [[7 March]] [[1896]]) and **Kapitein H. F. T. van Blokland** (Captain of Infantry; Ana' Galong post commander; patrol commander on [[7 March]] [[1896]]), the principals of the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|famous patrol of 7 March 1896]]. **Wachtmeester Jonker, No. 31845** of the *cavalerie* was reputed — though the text disputes it — to be the first into [[Teuku Umar|Oemar]]'s house at [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]], while **1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh** of the [[5e Bataljon]] was officially the first to reach Oemar's *verblijf* at 10:35 AM on [[24 May]] [[1896]]. **Wongsodikromo, No. 33780** and **Todikromo, No. 28097** were the named *maréchaussée* who, with the Luitenants [[Wagener]] and [[Vis]], reached the [[Tjot Tjako]] summit at 5:43 PM on [[23 May]] [[1896]] during the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. The combined article preserves the individual gallantry which the balso records in exceptional detail and which represents the most personal dimension of the Dutch war effort in the spring of [[1896]].

## Stakman and Van Blokland: The 7 March 1896 Patrol

The [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] was one of the most consequential small engagements of the first semester of [[1896]]. **Kapitein H. F. T. van Blokland** of the [[15e Bataljon]], post commander of [[Ana' Galong]], led the patrol — 1e Luit. **C. T. J. Stakman** (commanding the right section), *Adj. ond. off.* [[Krüger, No. 14477|Krüger]] (commanding the left section), *Off. van gesondh. 2e klasse* [[C. van der Meer|Dr. Van der Meer]], 1 *hoornblazer*, 72 *bajonetten*, 2 *minderen* of the medical service, 9 *dwangarbeiders* with 3 stretchers — along the kampong edges of [[Ana' Batée]], [[Klieng]] and the *sawah* toward [[Sibreh]]. The patrol engaged approximately 250 Achehnese under [[Habib Lhong]], [[T. Mad Amin]] and [[Panglima Hassan]] for five hours. Van Blokland "personally fought with sabre", and "*het gelukte hem persoonlijk met de sabel twee tegenstanders neer te slaan, of which een nagenoeg in tweeën gedeeld werd*" (it was his personal fortune with the sabre to strike down two opponents, one of whom was nearly cut in two). Stakman was "shot through the right arm" on [[7 March]] [[1896]], *Adj. ond. off.* [[Krüger, No. 14477|Krüger]] was wounded (but returned to combat after being bandaged), and *Off. van Gesondheid 2e klasse* [[C. van der Meer|Dr. Van der Meer]] received a "*contourschot aan het hoofd*" (grazing shot to the head). The patrol was eventually relieved by [[Kronouer|Kapitein Kronouer]]'s detachment of 40 *bajonetten* from [[Lamsoet]]/[[Lambarih]] under [[K. H. Westendorp|Lt. Westendorp]] (severely wounded) and [[F. Ketjen|Lt. Ketjen]]. The casualties included the severely wounded fusilier [[Monden, No. 31470|Monden]] (who was "afgemaakt" by the enemy when he could no longer defend himself) and the fusilier [[Laurens, No. 19336|Laurens]] (likewise "finished off"). Stakman and Van Blokland are the principals of the patrol, and Kruisheer's detailed narration of their actions establishes the patrol as one of the most celebrated small-unit engagements of the spring operations.

## Jonker, de Jongh, Wongsodikromo and Todikromo: The May 1896 Operations

The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] against [[Teuku Umar|Umar]]'s stelling at [[Lam-Asan]], [[Lam-Pisang]], [[Tjot Tjako]], [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Lam-Tjroeng]] and [[Lam-Poelau]] produced two of the most celebrated individual acts of the first semester. On [[23 May]] [[1896]], during the storming of [[Tjot Tjako]] by the *bergcolonne* under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] with [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapitein Van Daalen]] as *Chef van den Staf* and [[Jhr. G. J. W. C. H. Graafland|Kapitein Graafland]]'s 10 brigades *maréchaussée* as *voorhoede*, the Luitenants [[Wagener]] and [[Vis]] and the *maréchaussée* **Wongsodikromo, No. 33780** and **Todikromo, No. 28097** were the first to reach the Tjot Tjako summit at 5:43 PM. The naming of the two *maréchaussée* — indigenous personnel, by their service numbers and (typographically Achehnese/Javanese) names — is one of the few cases in *ATJEH '96* where individual indigenous personnel are explicitly named for their gallantry, and their presence at the summit alongside the two European lieutenants represents a moment of integrated heroism which the balso records in unusual detail. On [[24 May]] [[1896]], the capture of [[Teuku Umar|Umar]]'s house at [[Lam-Pisang]] produced a contested claim of priority: **Wachtmeester Jonker, No. 31845** of the *cavalerie* was "reputed" (though "the text disputes it") to be the first into Oemar's house, but the official record — Kruisheer's text — credits **1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh** of the [[5e Bataljon]] as "officially first to reach Oemar's *verblijf* at 10:35 VM on [[24 May]] [[1896|24 May]]". The dispute itself reflects the operational confusion of the moment and the institutional sensitivity of priority claims, and Kruisheer's resolution of the dispute in favour of de Jongh is the authoritative record. [[P. Linck|Kapitein Linck]]'s sectie of the 5e Bataljon entered Lam-Pisang "right after de Jongh", confirming the sequence.

## Significance

The combined article on the Dutch heroes of named patrols preserves the individual gallantry which *ATJEH '96* records in exceptional detail and which represents the most personal dimension of the Dutch war effort in the spring of [[1896]]. The [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] — with Van Blokland's sabre work and Stakman's wounding — was the most celebrated small-unit engagement of the pre-defection period, and the storming of [[Tjot Tjako]] and the capture of Umar's house at [[Lam-Pisang]] — with the priority claims of Wongsodikromo, Todikromo, Jonker and de Jongh — were the most celebrated individual acts of the post-defection counter-offensive. The naming of the two *maréchaussée* Wongsodikromo and Todikromo is particularly significant, because it represents one of the few cases in the balso where individual indigenous personnel are explicitly named for their gallantry, and it confirms the integrated character of the Dutch *maréchaussée* forces.

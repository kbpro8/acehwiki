**Toekoe Mahmoed** was the Acehnese chief of the *[[Kampong|kampong]]* complex surrounding [[Kroeng Raba]] on the west coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], described by [[P. H. R. Beuming]] as well disposed towards the Dutch and as the man whose *vertrouwen inboezemende houding* — confidence-inspiring attitude — made life at the *[[Benting|benting]]* [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] pleasant for soldiers and Acehnese alike at the end of [[1896]].

## The quiet of late [[1896]]

Beuming records that in the closing months of [[1896]] the landscape of Kroeng Raba was unusually quiet, which he ascribed chiefly to the vigorous pursuit of [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] after the [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)|three-day battle in the Beradin pass]]. The population, weary of the adventurer's pressure, returned to the *kampongs* and resumed field labour.

It was through Toekoe Mahmoed's attitude, Beuming writes, that life at Lho-Nga could be called truly pleasant for both parties: *men bewoog zich vrijer onder elkander; geen wanklank werd gehoord* — men moved more freely among one another; no discordant note was heard. It was in consequence of this peaceable state that the garrison commander eventually permitted the soldiers to bathe in the nearby *kali*, the practice that led to the [[The crocodile encounter at Kroeng Raba (1896)|crocodile encounter]].

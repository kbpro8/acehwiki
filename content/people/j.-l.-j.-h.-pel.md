**J. L. J. H. Pel** — *Kolonel Pel*, later *Generaal Pel* — was a Dutch colonel of the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army ([[K.N.I.L.|K.N.I.L.]]) who, on the departure of [[Jan van Swieten|General van Swieten]] for Java on [[26 April]] [[1874]], was left in command of the Dutch forces in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] with 3,000 men and the mission of carrying out Van Swieten's programme of "beveiliging onszer stelling en de gemeenschap naar zee in Groot-Atjeh, gepaard met streven naar onderwerping" (securing the Dutch position and the communication with the sea in Groot-Atjeh, coupled with the pursuit of submission). His tenure, lasting from [[April 1874|April]] [[1874]] to his death in [[March 1876|March]] [[1876]] during active operations, was marked by the elaboration of the so-called *[[Pel's Plan|Pel's Plan]]* — the great expansion of the Dutch *stelling* (defensive position) around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] that would consume Dutch military power for the next decade.

## The Inherited Position (April 1874)

Immediately after Van Swieten's departure, the *stelling* (defensive position) bequeathed by the second expedition proved "geheel onvoldoende" (entirely inadequate) to afford the garrison, under active defence, security and undisturbed communication with the sea. To this was added the obligation to protect an important Atjehese chief, [[T. Nek van Marassa|T. Nek van Marassa]], whose territory lay north of the kraton on the left bank of the [[Atjeh River|Atjeh-rivier]], and who had already submitted to Van Swieten in [[January 1874|January]] [[1874]]. The circumference of [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], the posts along the river, and the territory of T. Nek were attacked from [[27 April]] [[1874]] onwards; the enemy grew ever bolder and displayed a restless activity and watchfulness. The coastal statelets took vigorous part in these attacks.

The [[Atjeh River|Atjeh-rivier]] proved to be an often-unusable line of communication with the sea, and was moreover rendered highly insecure by the enemy's entrenchment on its right bank. A new line of fortifications therefore had to be constructed from [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] outward, by which Dutch friends would be protected and by which better communication with the roadstead would be obtained through [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]]. A head was soon built there, and Oleh-leh became, as it remains, the port of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. In the autumn of [[1876]] Oleh-leh would be connected to [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] by railway. On the right bank of the Atjeh-rivier a series of fortifications likewise had to be formed to secure communication along this route.

## The Expansion of the Stelling (1874–1876)

All this soon demanded an increase of the troops, which by [[December 1874|December]] [[1874]] already amounted to double the 3,000 left by Van Swieten. All around the Dutch position the terrain was, as Kruijt writes, "als bezaaid met versterkingen van den vijand" (as if sown with enemy fortifications). Further extension of the *stelling* was continuously given at great cost, so that by the time Pel went on leave to Java in [[June 1875|June]] [[1875]] to discuss further plans with the government, an unbroken ring of thirty-eight fortifications enclosed [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], enclosing [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] and the [[Atjeh River|Atjeh-rivier]], and demanding a fixed garrison of some 3,000 men. With this, Pel judged that the task assigned to him by Van Swieten in Groot-Atjeh had been fulfilled; but in the judgement of many, Kruijt notes, that task had already been far exceeded, and the expansion had led to a useless dispersal of force.

The enemy continued to attack with a "tact, energie en werkzaamheid zonder voorbeeld in de Indische geschiedenis" (a tactic, energy and activity without precedent in the Indies' history); of any significant submission there was no question. Yet this was not enough for Pel. He wished for further expansion, and to occupy a line from [[Kroeng Raba|Kroeng Raba]] on the [[Westkust|West Coast]] to [[Kroeng Raija|Kroeng Raija]] on the [[Noordkust|North Coast]], in order, as he expressed it, "Groot-Atjeh aldus geheel af te sluiten van de zee en het Atjehsche volk zijne beteekenis naar buiten te doen verliezen" (to cut off Groot-Atjeh entirely from the sea and to make the Atjehese people lose its outward significance). The blockade, he believed, could then be allowed to lapse.

## Pel's Plan and Its Execution (1875–1876)

For this further expansion Pel journeyed to [[Batavia|Batavia]] to press his case. He saw his plan approved by the Netherlands Indies government. His temporary replacement, [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Colonel Wiggers van Kerchem]], had in the interim improved the position, extended it with another post, had damaged the enemy outside it, and repelled his attacks, which grew ever bolder. From [[November 1875|November]] [[1875]] to [[March 1876|March]] [[1876]] the further expansion was carried out under heavy fighting. Pel himself died during the operations, and his plan was only partially executed.

The occupation of the conquered terrain, Kruijt observes, demanded all available strength, and resulted in an even greater dispersal of force without any notable advantage. The enemy stood everywhere as proudly and unflinchingly as before, successfully ambushed Dutch transports, attacked the fortifications, often with consequences tragic for the Dutch — who on occasion had to leave dead, even wounded and weapons in his hands. The Dutch had, moreover, many sick. Further offensive operations were impossible.

## Assessment

Kruijt's judgement of Pel's tenure is severe. The continuous expansion of the *stelling* in Groot-Atjeh, he writes, resulted in "groote uitgaven, machtsverbrokkeling en uitputting" (great expenditure, dispersal of force and exhaustion), coupled with a reckless opening of the coasts that "het doel dezer uitbreiding, den toevoer naar den vijand af te sluiten, reeds daardoor alléén, alle reden van bestaan ontnam" (deprived the purpose of this expansion — to cut off the supply to the enemy — of all reason for existence, by that fact alone). "Als met blindheid geslagen" (as if stricken with blindness), Kruijt writes, the Dutch continued on this path for years.

[[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] would later invert Pel's system: where Pel's *stelling* aimed at defence, Van der Heijden's aimed at *bedwang* (control). But the heritage of Pel's *stelling* — an enormous line of fortifications demanding a fixed garrison far in excess of what could be spared for offensive operations — would burden every subsequent Dutch commander down to the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[1884]].

## Significance

Pel's tenure and his *Plan* mark the institutionalisation of the Dutch military presence in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and the beginning of the *postenlinie* (posts line) strategy that would define the geography of the Dutch position for the next two decades. The port of [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]], established as the seaward terminus of his expanded *stelling*, would remain the principal Dutch naval base in Atjeh throughout the war. The railway connecting Oleh-leh to [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] — a project of his tenure — would become the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]], the principal logistical instrument of the Dutch campaign. And the dispersal of force entailed by the expanded *stelling* would prove, as [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]] argued, the structural defect of the Dutch position until the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration]] of [[1884]] reduced the perimeter.

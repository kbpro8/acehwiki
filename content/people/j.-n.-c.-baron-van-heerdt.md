**J. N. C. Baron van Heerdt** was a Dutch captain of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands Indies Army]] who commanded the patrol of [[25 September]] [[1897]] from the *[[Benting|benting]]* [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] at [[Kroeng Raba]] against the band of [[Toekoe Hassan]] in the [[Goeah Teroes]] cave on the [[Glé Betoes]].

## The patrol of [[25 September]] [[1897]]

Van Heerdt led out a force of three officers, four non-commissioned officers and eighty men, with an Acehnese guide, a hospital soldier with ten *[[Tandoe|tandoes]]* and the necessary forced labourers. When the guide lost the mountain path and the enemy, alerted, opened fire from the rocks, Van Heerdt came forward to the front of the column and ordered 1st Lieutenant [[J. van Greuningen]] to climb to the cave with his section, spread in a wide circle.

After Van Greuningen and three European fusiliers were shot dead at the cave mouth, Van Heerdt took over the command of the advance-guard himself and ordered rapid fire into the cave, driving the Acehnese deeper inside. On penetrating the cave he found it had several exits and no trace of the enemy remained. Seeing the uselessness of further pursuit, he resolved on the retreat, and during the fight had sent off a forced labourer to bring up the *[[Korps Marechaussee|marechaussée]]* brigades from Lho-Nga.

[[P. H. R. Beuming]] records his conduct on the return march: seeing the men dragging themselves along with fatigue when the column had closed up in the open terrain, the commander addressed them with *Komaan, mannen, nog een klein uurtje en dan zijn we thuis* — come on, men, another little hour and then we are home — words which, Beuming notes, did not miss their effect.

**Georg Andreas Ludwig Brockelmann** ([[1869]] – [[1952]]) was a [[German]] [[bibliographer]] whose monumental catalogues of Arabic manuscripts—most famously the *Geschichte der arabischen Litteratur*—are repeatedly cited for listings of *takhmīs* and related works on the [[Burda (poem)]]. He also compiled a comprehensive catalogue of commentaries on the creed poem of [[Siraj al‑Dīn Abu Muhammad ʿAlī ibn Uthmān al‑Ushī]], documenting at least twenty‑nine commentaries in [[Persian]], [[Turkish]], [[Hindustani]] and other languages. Brockelmann’s bibliographic entries facilitated the identification of manuscripts that later entered the Malay scholarly community of the [[Sultanate of Acheh]].

## Bibliographic Contributions

Brockelmann’s most influential work, the multi‑volume *Geschichte der arabischen Litteratur*, systematically enumerated Arabic literary productions, including manuscript traditions of the *Burda* and its associated *takhmīs* (verse‑by‑verse commentaries). Scholars have relied on his entries to trace the transmission of the *Burda* from its Arabic origins to Southeast Asian contexts.

In addition to his Arabic‑literature survey, Brockelmann produced a specialised catalogue of commentaries on the creed poem (*ʿAqīda*) composed by [[Siraj al‑Dīn Abu Muhammad ʿAlī ibn Uthmān al‑Ushī]]. This catalogue records at least twenty‑nine distinct commentaries, revealing a vibrant multilingual scholarly tradition that spanned Persian, Turkish, Hindustani and other linguistic spheres.

## Relevance to Aceh

The Acehnese Malay translation of the *Burda*—documented in early‑modern Malay manuscripts—was identified through Brockelmann’s listings of Arabic sources. His bibliographic framework allowed Acehnese scholars to locate and study the poem’s commentaries, thereby integrating the *Burda* into the literary culture of the [[Sultanate of Acheh]]. Moreover, the breadth of his commentary catalogue underscores the extensive intellectual exchange that made such translations possible.

## Legacy

Brockelmann’s meticulous documentation continues to serve historians of Arabic literature, Southeast Asian manuscript studies, and scholars of Islamic poetry. By bridging Arabic source material with Malay manuscript traditions, his work remains a cornerstone for research on the diffusion of Islamic literary genres across the Indian Ocean world.

**J.B. van Heutsz** was a Dutch colonial officer who later commanded the Dutch forces in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] during the late‑nineteenth‑century [[Aceh War|Aceh–Dutch War]].  The source *De geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* notes that “evenals later voor Van Heutsz … in Atjeh” (see [[403]]), linking him explicitly to later actions in the Aceh region.

## Early Military Career
Van Heutsz entered the Royal Netherlands East Indies Army (KNIL) in the 1860s, serving in various posts throughout the Dutch East Indies.  His early service prepared him for the later, more demanding campaigns in the northern part of Sumatra.

## Campaigns in Atjeh
During the 1870s and 1880s Van Heutsz led a series of punitive expeditions against the independent Acehnese sultanates, culminating in the capture of the Aceh capital [[Kuta Alam]] in [[1903]].  His tactics, including the use of “*oorlogvoering*” (total war) and the establishment of **controleposten* (control posts)*, were decisive in bringing the long‑running resistance to an end.

## Legacy
Van Heutsz is remembered in Dutch colonial historiography as the officer who finally secured Dutch sovereignty over [[Atjeh|Aceh]], though his methods have been subject to extensive criticism for their harshness and the civilian toll they exacted.

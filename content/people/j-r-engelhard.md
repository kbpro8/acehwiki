**J. R. Engelhard** was a Dutch captain of infantry of the [[8e Bataljon|8th Infantry Battalion]] of the Netherlands Indies Army in [[Atjeh]] in [[1876]]. He led the detachment that made the landing and first occupation of [[Occupation of Pidir (31 May - 4 June 1876)|Pidir]] in June [[1876]], and had earlier commanded the three European sections that forced the eastern gate of the enemy fortification at the [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|storming of Lambaroe]].

## Lambaroe, 7 March 1876

At the storming of the enemy work at [[Lambaroe]], Captain Engelhard was called up from the northern face by Major [[General van Teijen|Van Teijn]] to force the work at the east face. Second Lieutenant [[G. M. A. Walpot]] had forced the narrow winding entrance first; Engelhard followed immediately with some European soldiers and was quickly at hand-to-hand fighting with the Atjehers inside, receiving a klewang cut over the hand, while corporal Wager and fusilier Deville sprang forward to his aid and brought down several Atjehers. The work was taken within minutes.

## Pidir, June 1876

On 31 May Engelhard embarked at Olehleh on the steamer *Marion* as commander of the detachment destined for Pidir: 8 officers, 244 non-commissioned officers and men (70 Europeans), with a captain of engineers, 50 native carpenters and labourers, 26 artillerymen with 2 mountain guns, forced-labourers, provisions for two months, 4,000 sandbags, 35,000 randjoes and 20 Frisian chevaux-de-frise. He landed on the roadstead in the evening, reported the next morning to the naval station commander, Captain-Lieutenant [[Meijn]], and on 1 June presented the letter of the civil and military commander to the prince [[Toengkoe Radja Pakik]] at the flagstaff at the beach. When the prince had, after two days' hesitation, removed his objections, the trooops disembarked on [[4 June]] [[1876]], a representative of the prince even welcoming them.

The first days showed how thin the prince's authority over his people was. Hundreds of armed Atjehers pressed around — and inside — the marked emplacement of the fortification, and two European fusiliers were wounded by klewang cuts on [[5 June|5]] and [[6 June]]. Engelhard wrote to Kota Radja that the state of affairs was *"anything but satisfactory"* and asked urgently for reinforcements, especially Europeans, and for palisades or thick planks against the much-feared night surprise. The reinforcements were sent; the fortification was finished at the end of June. His report is quoted by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]), including his judgement: *"Now that we are ashore, we have no reason to complain"* — written on the day of the landing.

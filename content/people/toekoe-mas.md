**Toekoe Mas** was the Achehnese head (*hoofd*) of the coastal kampong of [[Lampanas]] on the north coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]], east of [[Oleh-leh]], at the foot of one of the outrunners of the [[Goudberg]] — the principal local collaborator of the Dutch during the landing and supply of the [[Lamtoba column (1879)|Lamtoba column]] in July–September [[1879]], as recorded in [[W. Cool]] and [[G. B. Hooijer]]'s *[[Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis (Cool & Hooijer, 1889)|Eene schoone bladzijde uit Atjeh's geschiedenis]]* ([[1889]]). He also appears in the book's order of battle as one of the eight guides and interpreters attached to the column.

## The reconnaissance of July 1879

In the last days before the expedition's departure, when Generaal [[General van der Heijden|van der Heijden]]'s well-disposed Achehnese chiefs had been unable to give any significant information about the district destined for the operation, Toekoe Mas came aboard the government steamboat [[Zeemeeuw (gouvernements-stoomboot)|Zeemeeuw]] on the roadstead of Lampanas and conferred with the chief of the military recording brigade — 1e Luitenant [[G. B. Hooijer]] — and with the commander of the naval vessel lying there. Upon his information the landing point was determined, and a figurative sketch of the terrain to be traversed and of the plain of [[Lamtoba]] was composed, which later proved to give a fair image of the reality.

## Headman of the landing place

On the morning of [[27 July]] [[1879]] Toekoe Mas, with the other chiefs of the coastal strip, awaited the column-commander, Luitenant-Kolonel [[Majoor Gerlach|Gerlach]], at the strand as he came ashore at about seven o'clock, the population of the little village remaining for the most part in its houses. When the Dutch met an old woman laden with household goods on her way to hide in the wilderness, it was Toekoe Mas who reassured her, so that she returned to her hovel with a lightened heart. In consultation with him the bivouac site was sought and found, a quarter of an hour from Lampanas, on a hill clothed with short grass, shaded here and there by tall timber and washed on two sides by a rivulet of pure drinking-water.

Throughout the stay of the troops the chief remained the intermediary of the camp: from him the attap- and pinang-trees for the buildings of the coastal camp could be bought, and through his good offices the littoral remained quiet even when, from [[22 August]], a great camp of quarantine with barracks for the whole column rose behind the strand. In the book's distribution of guides and interpreters among the column he is named with [[Toekoe Ali]], [[Toekoe Ladang Baroe]], [[Si Nja Garu]], [[Oetoes Ja]], [[Mas Saäd]], [[Mas Daud]] and [[Nja Assan]] (see [[Guides and interpreters of the Lamtoba column (1879)]]).

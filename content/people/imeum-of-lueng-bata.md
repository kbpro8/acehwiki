A brave commander who fought heroically alongside Teuku Lam Nga in the first and second Dutch attacks of the Aceh War, continuing to fight despite illness. After the conquest of the XXVI Mukims he sheathed his sword and withdrew from public life, resisting later Dutch attempts to bribe him.

## Expanded account from *The Achehnese*, Vol. I — Imeum of Lueng Bata

The **Imeum of Lueng Bata** was the hereditary chief of the *mukim* of [[Lueng Bata]] — one of the three *mukims* (with [[Pagaraye]] and Lam Sayun) which, though properly belonging to the [[XXV Mukims]], had in former times the status of *wakeueh*-districts, free from the control of the *Panglima Sagi*. By the late 19th century the only surviving trace of that status was the independence of the chiefs of these *mukims*. The influential and powerful Imeum of Lueng Bata was, on many occasions, the adviser and ally of the Sultan, but this very relation made it necessary for the latter to hold him in great respect and treat him with marked distinction.

The Imeum was, in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s description, "a rare phenomenon among" the Achehnese chiefs in his combination of a dissolute life with an unyielding hatred of the infidel. Like most of his fellows he sought to be foremost at fights of animals, gambling parties and sanguinary internal forays; but at the same time he possessed those qualities whereby an Achehnese might rise to be an *uleebalang* though not entitled to such a position by birth. Continually surrounded by boys, he complied with their demands for the repair of their kites and toys as generously as with those of his followers when they begged him to put the requisite fine edge or polish on their weapons. In battle and arduous toil he always encouraged the others by his own example, and at the sharing of profit or spoil he forgot no one. Dissolute though his life was when viewed from a religious standpoint, he was unsurpassed in his hatred of the infidel, while not behind the majority of his countrymen in his reverence for saints and *ulamas*.

When the Achehnese war against the Gompeuni (Dutch) began, many sought the coveted death of martyrs to their creed, selling their lives as dearly as they could. Sometimes they fought in separate bands and sometimes they joined the standards of those *adat*-chiefs who took the most zealous part in the defence of the capital — such as the Imeum of Lueng Bata, who was foremost in defence of the capital. Together with [[Teuku Uma]] and the members of his own family, the Imeum was among the energetic adventurers who, in the prevailing confusion, saw a chance of essaying their fortunes afresh.

The Imeum of Lueng Bata died in [[1901]], during the military operations in [[Samalanga]]. His death removed one of the last representatives of the old hereditary military *adat*-chiefs who combined personal valour with political weight. In [[Teungku Tiro (Muhamat Amin)|Teungku Tiro]]'s pamphlet to the Dutch Government he is named among the four — [[Teuku Kali Malikon Ade|Teuku Kali]], [[Teuku Ne' of Meura'sa]], the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] and the Imeum of Lueng Bata — on whose counsel the Sultan was obliged to depend, and whose decision in turn depended, as the *ulama*'s argument continued, on the three *panglimas* of the *sagis* and ultimately on the *ulamas* themselves, "who derive their knowledge from Allah and his Apostle."

The Imeum of Lueng Bata also appears among those *uleebalangs* in the Sultan's territory and the so-called *wakeueh*-districts — including [[Teuku Kali Malikon Ade|Teuku Kali]], the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]], and the [[Imeum of Chadé|Imeums of Chadé]] — who, though not members of the formal board of twelve, had much more weight in the scale than many of its members.

**U. Bernstorff von Sperling** — a count (*Graaf*) and operations manager of the [[Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen|Atjeh state railways]] — was killed on the morning of [[24 February]] [[24 February 1942|24 February 1942]] at a sabotaged bridge approximately 25 km south of [[Kotaradja|Kotaradja]], in the second of the two opening acts of the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] rising. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the murder of Von Sperling — together with the [[murder of Tiggelman at Seulimeum|murder of Tiggelman]] the same night — closes the first action period of the pre-invasion Atjehnese rising and triggers the Dutch operational response that would shape the March campaign.

## The inspection trip of 24 February 1942

On the morning of [[24 February]] [[24 February 1942|24 February 1942]], having learned of the sabotage to the railway track between Kotaradja and Seulimeum, Von Sperling wished personally to inspect the damage done to the track. He was assigned a single brigade of infantry from Kotaradja as escort for the inspection. The column comprised three elements: Von Sperling's car, with his Atjehnese chauffeur, in front; an escort brigade in a bus behind; and the captain company commander on a motorcycle in the rear.

At a bridge over the railway some 25 km south of Kotaradja the column was halted by a barricade — a road-block thrown up across the bridge — defended by a band of about 60 men, armed with edged weapons, an armed Atjehnese band. Most of the band wore for the most part white armbands — bands bearing the letter F, the insignia of the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] that would, on interrogation of a Menadonese soldier some days later, be identified as the sign by which members of the movement were to be marked out for friendly treatment by the Japanese.

## The murder

Against the explicit advice of the military commander, Von Sperling stepped out of his car and proceeded alone in the direction of the bridge. He spoke a few words to the band — apparently mistaking the white-armband men for auxiliary police — and attempted single-handedly to clear the barricade. At this point Mr Von Sperling was suddenly struck by one of the band with a bludgeon to his neck, by which he was fatally wounded. The escort brigade commander responded by shooting the attacker; further soldiers opened fire on the band, which hastily took flight.

The staff history records two details that frame the incident. First, the captain company commander — an officer with little Atjeh experience who had in the meantime also drawn near — ordered the firing stopped, and measures to pursue the band were left undone. Second, the white armbands worn by most of the band bore the letter F — an insignia whose meaning was, at that moment, not understood in Kotaradja, but which would be explained within days by the arrest of a Menadonese soldier who boasted of Japanese sympathies. Under interrogation the soldier confessed that he had been recruited by Atjehnese into a far-reaching so-called F. beweging, whose members were to be considered friends and cooperators by the Japanese and were to wear the white-and-red-F armband on the arrival of the Japanese.

## Significance

Von Sperling's murder is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the moment at which the F. beweging — a Japanese-sponsored underground whose existence the Dutch had not previously suspected — became visible to the Dutch command. The compiler's emphasis on the white armbands, on the Menadonese soldier's confession, and on the Fujiwara identification of the "F." transforms the murder from a single act of sabotage into the *terminus ad quem* of Dutch ignorance of the F-organisation. The murder also closes the first action period of the pre-invasion rising; a period of relative calm follows, broken only by the second sabotage wave of [[7 March|7]]–[[8 March]] [[8 March 1942|8 March 1942]] that runs into the Japanese landings.

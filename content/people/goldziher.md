**Heinrich Goldziher** ([[1842]] – [[1927]]) was a Hungarian‑born Dutch orientalist and pioneering scholar of [[Islamic studies]]. He is best known for his work on Arabic poetry and religious literature, especially his treatise *Abh. Arab. Philologie I* and his studies of the *Burda* poem by [[Al-Busiri]]. Goldziher’s methodological approaches to literary motifs have been widely cited in the interpretation of both Arab and Malay‑language Islamic texts.

## Life and Career

Born in [[Budapest]] (then part of the Austrian Empire) in [[1842]], Goldziher pursued classical studies before turning to Oriental languages. After moving to the [[Netherlands]], he held academic positions at the University of Leiden, where he contributed to the nascent field of Islamic philology. He died in [[Leiden]] in [[1927]].

## Scholarly Contributions

Goldziher’s *Abh. Arab. Philologie I* (published in the late nineteenth century) examined metaphorical language in Arabic poetry. On page 84 of a later source the work is quoted: “*Spot, smaad en schande worden in de Arabische poëzie dikwijls met een halsketen vergeleken…* (zie: Goldziher, *Abh. Arab. Philologie I*, 100).” This passage illustrates his analysis of the motif of a necklace as a symbol of shame and disgrace.

His research on the *Burda* poem is also frequently cited. The source records a French‑language quotation of Goldziher’s commentary: “*les savants les plus distingués…*” highlighting his role in introducing the poem to European academia. Goldziher’s editions and commentaries helped establish the *Burda* as a central text for comparative studies of Islamic devotional literature.

## Influence on Acehnese and Malay Studies

Although Goldziher’s primary focus was the Arab world, his methodological tools have been applied to the study of Malay and Acehnese Islamic literature. Scholars interpreting poetic imagery in the Malay translations of the *Burda*—such as those found in the seventeenth‑century manuscript examined in the source—have drawn on Goldziher’s insights. His work thus indirectly shaped the literary culture of the [[Sultanate of Acheh]] in the [[19th century]], where the *Burda* became part of the religious and poetic repertoire of the Acehnese elite.

## Connection to Aceh

Goldziher’s influence is evident in the way later Acehnese scholars approached the *Burda* and related texts. The methodological framework he provided for analyzing metaphor and symbolism was adopted by researchers studying the Malay‑language versions of the poem, facilitating a deeper understanding of Acehnese devotional practices and literary expression.

**Goldziher** — *Dr. Goldziher* — *Ignác Goldziher* of Budapest — was the Hungarian Islamologist, the principal European Islamicist of the late 19th century alongside [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje], who, on the publication of Snouck Hurgronje's "*Nieuwe bijdragen tot de kennis van den Islâm*" (in *Bijdragen t/d Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van N.-I.*, IV. 6) beginning in [[1882]], baptised Snouck Hurgronje the "*Schöpfer der historischen Betrachtung des Figh*" (creator of the historical consideration of the fiqh) — the scholarly recognition that established Snouck Hurgronje's reputation as the principal Dutch Islamicist of his generation.

## The Recognition

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s in [[1882]] beginning publications — allereerst de "*Nieuwe bijdragen tot de kennis van den Islâm*" (in *Bijdragen t/d T.L. en Vk. v. N.-I.* IV. 6), wherein he, heavily armed with science, came forward and instructed — were the publications that established his reputation as the principal Dutch Islamicist of his generation. Verdiensten wurden erkannt, aber dwalingen aangewezen, open en zoontegenover de dwalers; und die sachen van den Islâm wurden tot zoo lang waren gezien. He onthulde how it stood with it, and how it had so grown, where, whereby, when.

[[L. W. C. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] — the *ambtenaar voor de beoefening van inlandsche talen, tevens adviseur voor oostersche talen en mohammedaansch recht* at [[Batavia|Batavia] — was not pleased. But Dr. Goldziher at Budapest was delighted, and baptised Snouck Hurgronje the "*Schöpfer der historischen Betrachtung des Figh*" — the creator of the historical consideration of the fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence).

## The Significance of the Baptism

The baptism — the "*Schöpfer der historischen Betrachtung des Figh*" — is the principal scholarly recognition of Snouck Hurgronje's early career, and established his reputation as the principal Dutch Islamicist of his generation. The recognition, by the principal European Islamicist of the late 19th century, fixed Snouck Hurgronje's place in the European scholarly community, and was the immediate antecedent of his Mekka journey of [[1884]]–[[1885]] and of all his subsequent Atjèh work.

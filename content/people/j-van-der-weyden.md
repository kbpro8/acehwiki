**J. Van der Weyden** (also rendered Van der Weijden) was a Lieutenant-Colonel of the General Staff of the [[K.N.I.L.|Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] and the son-in-law of [[J. B. van Heutsz]], best known as the author of a controversial defence brochure advocating mandatory military service for the native population of [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]].

## Military Career

Van der Weyden served as a Lieutenant-Colonel on the General Staff of the [[K.N.I.L.|N. I. Leger]] (*Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger*). He was the son-in-law (*schoonzoon*) of [[J. B. van Heutsz]], the former Civil and Military Governor of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and later Governor-General of [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] ([[1904]]–[[1909]]).

## The Defence Brochure

Van der Weyden was the author of [[Van der Weyden's defense brochure|the brochure]] *Weerplicht voor Inlanders en samenstelling van de weermacht in Nederlandsch-Indië*, published by the [[Vereeniging ter Beoefening van de Krijgswetenschap]] at [['s-Gravenhage]]. He first promised this work at the meeting of the Vereeniging on [[27 February]] [[1914]], but the brochure did not appear for more than two years. The brochure proposed the introduction of mandatory military service (*militieplicht*) for the native population of [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] and a reformed army and fleet composition for the defence of the archipelago.

## Collaboration with Van Heutsz

[[Van der Maaten]] revealed in [[Van der Maaten's pamphlet on Van Heutsz and the Defense of Netherlands Indies|his pamphlet of 1917]] that the brochure was actually written in collaboration with [[J. B. van Heutsz]]. Pages 389–395 of the brochure were entirely in Van Heutsz's own hand. Van der Maaten argued the work should properly be called the "brochure Van Heutsz-Van der Weijden."

## Promotion of Van Heutsz

Van der Weyden promoted his father-in-law as "better than anyone, indeed better than the entire [[Staatscommissie voor de Verdediging van Nederlandsch-Indië]] combined, informed about [[India]], its resources, and the Indian defence question." This promotion of Van Heutsz as the supreme authority on colonial defence was central to the broader campaign that led to [[Van Heutsz's Bond van Vrije Liberalen defense speech (13 January 1917)|Van Heutsz's speech at the Bond van Vrije Liberalen]] on [[13 January]] [[1917]].

## Quoting Van Heutsz on the Staatscommissie

At the meeting of the [[Vereeniging ter Beoefening van de Krijgswetenschap]] on [[9 January]] [[1914]], Van der Weyden quoted Van Heutsz's devastating private judgment on the [[Staatscommissie voor de Verdediging van Nederlandsch-Indië]] report, with Van Heutsz's authorisation. At the meeting of [[27 February]] [[1914]], he acknowledged that Van Heutsz's words "Ik ben blijde dat mijn naam niet onder dat Rapport staat" could logically have been omitted, since Van Heutsz would never have signed such a report.

## Defence Proposals and Political Impact

Van der Weyden advocated that [[India]] should bear all defence costs, proposing that India should take out loans "*op eigen naam en voor eigen rekening*" for defence purposes. He claimed there existed a "*middel om de Staatsinkomsten op voor de bevolking billijke wijze aanzienlijk te verhoogen*" (a means to considerably increase state revenues in a manner fair to the population). His defence proposals were generally enthusiastically received and were played against the government in the [[Volksvertegenwoordiging]].

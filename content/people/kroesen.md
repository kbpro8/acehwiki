**Kroesen** was a Dutch colonial civil servant who served in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] during the critical decade of [[1892]]–[[1896]], first as *Assistent-Resident* under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s governorship — where he was the principal Dutch advocate for arming and using [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] as a Dutch auxiliary — and later as *contrôleur* of the *geconcentreerde linie* in [[1896]], when he received [[Toekoe Ketjik Oemar]]'s warning of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|planned defection of 29 March 1896]] and transmitted it in time for the Dutch to take effective counter-measures. He is named in both Kruijt's *De Atjeh-Oorlog* (1896) and the *Atjeh-Album van Eigen Haard* (1896) as a key figure in the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## Arrival as Assistent-Resident (December 1892)

In [[December 1892|December]] [[1892]] the capable *Assistent-Resident* Kroesen came to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s governorship. With his arrival the submission and use of [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] was again raised and carried through. Kruijt notes that many believed this was largely Kroesen's work, because before him Deijkerhoff had always advised a waiting attitude.

## The Submission and Arming of Teuku Umar (1892–1893)

Against the exactions and acts of violence to which the population outside the Dutch line was subjected by the religious party — whose head was [[Tengkoe Mohamed Amin|Tengkoe Mohamed Amin]] — Toekoe Oemar had begun to resist more and more, and when these extended to the [[4 and 6 Moekims|4 and 6 Moekims]], he inflicted some painful losses on the *[[Muslemin|Muslemin]]*. He then asked again to be taken into favour by the administration; and the administration — under Kroesen's influence — put this in prospect, provided he first gave adequate proofs of good disposition by driving out the Muslemin.

Helped by Atjehers from within the line — with people, supplies, rifles, and ammunition — Toekoe Oemar's operations were crowned with success. With the cooperation of Dutch troops, Dutch artillery, and Dutch rijksdaalders, he successively took various points, and on [[10 August]] [[1893]] also the notorious [[Kaloet|Kaloet]], almost without a blow. On the proposal of Deijkerhoff, he was officially pardoned on [[30 September]] [[1893]], under the name of *[[T. Djohan Pahlawan|T. Djohan Pahlawan]]* appointed *[[Panglima Prang Besar|Panglima prang besar]]*, and he and his auxiliaries were granted allowances, food, weapons, and ammunition.

## Departure (October 1895)

Kroesen departed for Europe in [[October 1895|October]] [[1895]] on account of serious indisposition; he had wished to go earlier but had been asked to defer it for another year. His departure removed the principal Dutch advocate of the Toekoe Oemar policy from the scene, four months before the policy collapsed in the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]].

## The Warning of 1896

When, in [[March]] [[1896]], [[Toekoe Oemar|Djohan]] was preparing his defection, [[Toekoe Ketjik Oemar]] — one of Djohan's *volgelingen* (followers) — secretly warned the *contrôleur* Kroesen that Djohan would not march on [[Lamkrak|Lamkrak]] as ordered, but intended to attack the Dutch troops unexpectedly at the most favourable opportunity. The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Kroesen's receipt of the warning *deed den toeleg mislukken* (made the design fail) and allowed the *regeering* to take *onmiddellijk krachtige maatregelen* — the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] and the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]] of [[April]] [[1896]]. The album's tribute: *En aan Toekoe Ketjik Oemar is het te danken, dat de regeering onmiddellijk krachtige maatregelen kon nemen, en dat het verraad van Djohan nog niet ernstiger gevolgen heeft gehad* — identifies the *contrôleur* Kroesen as the institutional channel through which the Atjehese intelligence reached the Dutch military command.

## Significance

Kroesen's career in Groot-Atjeh illustrates the central role of the civil administration in the late phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. As Assistent-Resident he was the principal driver of the [[Pahlawan-klucht|Pahlawan-klucht]] — the policy of arming and paying Toekoe Oemar that Kruijt considered the most dangerous experiment of the entire war. As *contrôleur* in [[1896]] he was the man through whom the warning of the impending defection was transmitted. The irony — that the official most responsible for arming Toekoe Oemar was no longer at his post when the policy collapsed — is one of the incidental observations in Kruijt's indictment of the late period of Deijkerhoff's governorship.

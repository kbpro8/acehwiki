**Al-Ḥallāj** — Ḥusayn b. Manṣūr al-Hallāj (244–309 H / 858–922 C.E.) — was a Persian Sufi mystic, one of the most celebrated and most controversial figures of the early Sufi tradition. He was executed in Baghdad in 309 H / 922 C.E. for his ecstatic utterance *anā al-ḥaqq* ("I am the Truth") — a declaration of the mystic's passing-away (*fanāʾ*) in the divine reality. The doctrine of the [[Shathihiyat|*shathīyāt*]] — the ecstatic utterances of the Sufis — is exemplified above all by al-Ḥallāj, and the scholastic treatment of the *shathīyāt* in the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq li-dafʿ az-zindīq*]] of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] takes his case as its principal example.

## *Anā al-ḥaqq*

The utterance *anā al-ḥaqq* — "I am the Truth" — is the classic example of the *shathīyāt*. Al-Ḥallāj, in a state of mystical intoxication, declared himself to be the Truth — that is, God. The utterance was judged by the jurists of Baghdad to be unbelief, and al-Ḥallāj was executed. The Sufi tradition, however, distinguishes the *shathīyāt* pronounced in a state of *fanāʾ* — which are not judged as unbelief, because the self that pronounces them has passed away in the divine reality — from the same utterances pronounced by one in his senses, which are judged as unbelief and apostasy.

## Raniri's treatment

The scholastic treatment of the *shathīyāt* in the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] of Raniri closes with a notable pronouncement on the *shathīyāt* of al-Ḥallāj. Raniri compares the *shathīyāt* with *latah* — the trance-state familiar in the Malay-Indonesian world, in which the entranced person speaks words that are not his own. Although God will not, on the Day of Judgement, punish the *shathīyāt* — because the words come from Him — yet by the religious law they must be punished, even killed. Whoever, being in his senses, pronounces such *shathīyāt*, must be killed and will burn in hell eternally.

The pronouncement is the scholastic justification of the persecution of the [[Wujudiyyah]] that followed Raniri's [[Raniri's public debate against the Wujudiyyah in Acheh|public disputation]] at the court of [[Iskandar Thani]]. By distinguishing the *shathīyāt* of the entranced mystic — which are to be forgiven — from the same utterances pronounced by one in his senses — which are to be punished with death — Raniri provides the religious authority by which the disciples of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] could be put to death and his books burned.

## Significance

Al-Ḥallāj is the classic example of the *shathīyāt* and the principal authority — by his case — of the scholastic treatment of the ecstatic utterances in the Sufi tradition. His case is the principal example invoked in the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] of Raniri, where it serves as the scholastic justification of the persecution of the [[Wujudiyyah]] of Acheh. The comparison of the *shathīyāt* with *latah* — a Malay-Indonesian trance-state — is a remarkable trace of the localisation of Sufi scholastic discourse in the Malay-Indonesian world.

**Justus Schouten** (also written *Joost Schouten* in the table of contents) was a senior Dutch East India Company official and diplomat whose earlier visit to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — preceding [[Pieter Soury|Pieter Soury]]'s [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|embassy of 1641]] — was a reference point for the Soury mission. Schouten's Atjeh connection is twofold: he had been the VOC official through whom the late Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]] had placed the disputed jewel-order that Soury was sent to deliver, and he was one of the two senior officials who saw the Soury embassy off at [[Batavia]]. Godee Molsbergen refers to "zijn treurig einde" — his sad end — without elaboration in this text.

## Earlier Atjeh visit and the jewel-order of 1641

When Soury's legation reached Atjeh in [[1641]], the consignment of jewels it brought had been ordered the previous year through Schouten, who had been in Atjeh at that time. The chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] personally came to inspect whether the goods ordered "het vorig jaar besteld, toen Schouten in Atjeh was" — the previous year, when Schouten was in Atjeh — had been delivered according to specification. The order was for a gold-enameled *beteldoos* (betel-box) and gold rings, which Soury now delivered to the eunuch's satisfaction, receiving in return a horse, a cow and a buffalo from the Sultana.

## The 1641 jewel sale: ƒ28,000 at 61% profit

Schouten had sold jewels in Atjeh to the value of ƒ28,000 in [[1641]] with 61% profit — and even then had not managed to dispose of the entire parcel, "daar er reeds te veel diamanten in Atjeh waren" — because there were already too many diamonds in Atjeh. This precedent — a successful but partially frustrated sale in a saturated market — was the immediate commercial background against which Soury's mission of [[1641]] had to operate. The unsold remainder from Schouten's parcel presumably added to the problem of placing the new consignment.

## Sending-off of the Soury embassy

In [[1641]], when *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s embassy was sent from Batavia to Atjeh, Schouten — together with the *extra-ordinaris Raad van Indië* [[Joan Maetsuycker|Joan Maetsuycker]] — saw the legation off. Godee Molsbergen pairs the two: "Het gezantschap werd te Batavia uitgeleide gedaan door de extra-ordinaris Raad van Indië Joan Maetsuycker en Justus Schouten, wiens treurig einde we zagen." The reference is to a "sad end" already narrated elsewhere in the book; the Atjeh chapter does not itself describe it.

## Significance

Schouten's significance for [[Acheh|Atjeh]] is primarily as the predecessor of [[Pieter Soury|Soury]] in the Company's Atjeh diplomacy. He is the link between the late Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]]'s order, the sale of [[1641]] — at 61% profit — and the difficult negotiations that Soury would face with the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] over the same consignment. The Schouten–Soury handover also shows how the VOC's Atjeh trade in jewels depended on personal connections between individual merchants and the sultan's court.

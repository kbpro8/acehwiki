Major William Farquhar was a British East India Company officer who served as Resident of [[Malacca]] and, subsequently, as the first Resident of [[Singapore]]. In the Achehnese literature he is chiefly significant for his explorations of [[Borneo|Borneo's]] west coast and the [[Karimon Islands]] in [[1818]] and for the letter of [[19 January 1819]] in which he invited [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] to inspect the latter — an invitation that produced Raffles's [[Raffles' detour to Karimon Islands|sudden detour from the Acheh commission]] and the [[Founding of Singapore|founding of Singapore]].

## Explorations in Borneo and the Rhio–Lingga Archipelago

Farquhar had been dispatched in [[1818]] on an exploratory cruise to the west coast of [[Borneo]] and to [[Lingga]]. At [[Pontianak]] he was refused landing by the Dutch commissioner [[Van Boekholz|Van Boekholz]], who turned him away from the river; at [[Lingga]], on the other hand, he succeeded in concluding a treaty with the Sultan. The news of his rejection at Pontianak and of the subsequent destruction of his Lingga engagement by [[Schout-bij-nacht Wolterbeek|Schout-bij-nacht Wolterbeek]] at [[Riouw]] did much to inflame British opinion against the Dutch and helped to bring about the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Acheh commission of 1819]].

In the course of these voyages Farquhar formed the opinion that the [[Karimon Islands]] would afford an excellent site for a British establishment. The Dutch governor at Malacca, [[Timmerman Thyssen|Timmerman Thyssen]], reported the matter to Wolterbeek in a private letter of [[11 November 1818]], observing that «the Major still hopes to take possession of the Karimon Islands; at Pulo Pinang it seems to be a settled plan to take possession of these and of the kingdom of Acheen on Sumatra».

## The Karimon Letter and the Founding of Singapore

On the afternoon of [[19 January 1819]], while Raffles was making his preparations at [[Penang]] for the [[Acheh]] voyage, Farquhar addressed him a letter expressing his favourable opinion of the [[Karimon Islands]] and inviting him to make a joint inspection. Raffles found the invitation of such importance that he not only postponed the entire Acheh mission, the urgency of which he had hitherto insisted upon, but actually departed without taking leave of either [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Governor Bannerman]] or [[Captain Coombs|Captain Coombs]], taking with him, to Bannerman's indignation, the artillery stores which the Penang government believed had been destined for the Acheh operation. The detour produced, in the space of a few weeks, the [[Founding of Singapore|founding of Singapore]] — «an anti-Batavia», as [[P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp]] puts it.

## Role in the Minto Dispatch

Raffles afterwards sought to justify his decision by Farquhar's concurrence. In his letter to [[O. F. Metcalfe|O. F. Metcalfe]] of [[30 September 1819]], printed as Appendix 5 of Van der Kemp's article, Raffles stated that, having «accidentally» realised that the interests of [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] might suffer during his absence from the Acheh commission, he had consulted Farquhar on the propriety of [[Dispatch of the Minto to Pedir|dispatching the *Minto* to Pedir]], and that «as he fully concurred with me, I detached the *Minto* with the instructions» given to [[Lieutenant Criddle|Captain Criddle]].

## Later Career

Farquhar became the first Resident of Singapore under Raffles, and is generally accounted the co-founder of the settlement. His wider Malaccan career belongs properly to the Singapore-Malacca record, but it is worth noting that his correspondence with Raffles during the Karimon episode was one of the immediate causes of the long quarrel between Raffles and the Penang government, a quarrel whose documentation forms the bulk of the [[Acheh treaty of 1819]] file studied by Van der Kemp.

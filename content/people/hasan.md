**Ḥasan** (also **Hasan ibn Ali**), the elder son of [[Ali]] and Fāṭimah and thus the grandson of [[Prophet Mohammad|the Prophet Mohammad]], is in the religious calendar and folk-lore of [[Acheh]] inseparable from his younger brother [[Husain]]. Together they give their names to the Achehnese month of [[Asan-Usen]] (the first month of the Mohammedan year, *Muharram*) and to the [[Hasan-Husain Festival|Hasan-Husain Festival]] commemorated in the first ten days of that month. Vol. I singles Hasan out for special notice on one curious point: though innocent of all martyrdom, he has been "enrolled in this band of saints" — that is, in the band of Kerbela martyrs — and the Ashura-festival accordingly bears the names of both brothers.

## The enrolment of Hasan among the Kerbela martyrs

The circumstance is introduced by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in the course of his account of the Ashura commemoration. The "absurd tales related of Husain and his companions, the martyrs of [[Kerbela]]," he writes, "are also of the same character as those current in India. There too (and consequently in the Archipelago as well), Hasan, innocent as he was of all martyrdom, has been enrolled in this band of saints, and the Ashura-festival bears the names of both brothers." The pattern is one which Vol. I traces repeatedly: the Deccan form of Mohammedanism — Shafiʿite in ritual, but subjected to strong Shiʿite influences — exercised on the Mohammedanism of the [[East Indian Archipelago|Indonesian Archipelago]] an influence superior in force to that of any other agency, and with that influence came a whole complex of romance and legend, including the Titanic wars and love adventures of [[Amir Hamzah]], the romantic adventures of [[Mohammad ibnu'l Hanafiyyah]], and the exploits of the heroes [[Sam'un]] and [[Raja Badar]].

## Descent and the application of titles

The distinction between the descendants of Hasan and those of Husain is mentioned in Vol. I in connection with the application of the titles *sharif* and *sayyid*. As Snouck explains elsewhere, the application of these titles is subject to local differences, "but as a general rule the descendants of Mohammad's grandson Hasan are called sharifs and those of Husain sayyids. Thus in the Indian Archipelago there are sayyids only, though the name of sharif is here and there applied to the descendants of sayyids born in the country." It is from Husain, therefore, that the Sayyids who play so prominent a part in Achehnese religious life — among them [[Sayyid Abdurrahman Zahir|the Habib]] and [[Teungku Anjong]] — claim descent.

## Survivals in the calendar

The Achehnese name of the first month — *Asan-Usen*, "Hasan-Husain" — and the Achehnese designation of that month as the "fire-month" (*bulěn apuy*) are described by Snouck as further survivals of the old Hasan-Husain commemoration. The first ten days of the month are regarded as unlucky; on them no work of importance is undertaken, no marriage celebrated, no child circumcised, no rice sown or planted out. The "fire-month" name may, Snouck suggests, lurk in the dances of the Hasan-Husain feasters round fires, as practised to this day in [[Trumon]] and in the [[Deccan]]. The cooking of [[Kanji Ashura|kanji Ashura]] on the Ashura day is likewise an Achehnese adjunct of the same complex.

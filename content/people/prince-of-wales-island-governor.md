The **Prince of Wales Island Governor** — that is, the government of [[Penang|Prince of Wales Island]] in its collective capacity, as distinct from any individual governor such as [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Bannerman]], [[W. E. Phillips|Phillips]], [[Governor Petrie|Petrie]] or [[Robert Fullerton|Fullerton]] — is the collective reference used in [[P. H. van der Kemp]] (1900) for the British administration at Penang whenever it acted as a body. The phrase "the Government of Prince of Wales' Island" recurs in the official correspondence reproduced in the appendices, and Van der Kemp himself frequently uses it as shorthand for the settlement's institutional voice.

## The institutional reference

Penang — granted the formal style of "Prince of Wales Island" on its cession by the [[Raja of Quedah|Raja of Quedah]] in [[1786]] — was administered, until 1867, by a Governor-in-Council responsible, through the Governor-General at [[Bengal|Bengal]] (and after 1833 through the Governor-General of India in Council), to the Court of Directors of the East India Company. Decisions taken by the Governor of Penang with the advice of his Council were expressed, in official correspondence, as acts of "the Government of Prince of Wales' Island" or "the Governor in Council of Prince of Wales' Island."

Van der Kemp cites, in Appendix 2, [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]]'s letter to the government secretary at [[Calcutta]] of 31 December [[1819]], which quotes from a despatch of the Governor in Council of Prince of Wales' Island to the Court of Directors of 1 July 1819, in which the Penang government claimed to have had "as full a knowledge of the instructions of the Supreme Government on those matters as Sir Stamford Raffles himself had." Raffles indignantly repudiated the imputation. The phrase is the standard form of reference for the Penang administration in the records.

## Specific acts

Among the specific acts attributed in the article to the "Government of Prince of Wales' Island" are: the proposal, in [[1819]], that a military force consisting of relieved divisions of the 20th regiment native infantry should follow the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs mission]] to Acheh — a proposal that the mission "very properly protested against"; the taking up of ships, at considerable expense, for that purpose; the injunction conveyed to [[Said Akil]] when he proceeded to [[Deli]] that, having gone "contrary to the injunctions of the Government of Prince of Wales' island, he is placed without the protection of the British Government in the event of his infringing the laws or injuring the inhabitants of that country"; and the dispatch of the [[Lucas mission to Telok Semawé (February 1821)|Lucas mission to Telok Semawé]] in February [[1821]], of the [[Bellona mission to Sumatra's East Coast (1827-1828)|Bellona mission]] in 1827–1828, and of [[Fullerton's letter to Sultan of Siak (7 February 1828)|Fullerton's letter to the Sultan of Siak]] and his [[Fullerton's circular to nine Siak chiefs|circular to nine Siak chiefs]] of 1828.

The institutional reference is also used by the Achehnese themselves. The [[Three Sagis]] in their [[Three Sagis' letter to Penang (4 December 1822)|letter to Penang]] of 4 December 1822 address themselves to "our friend the Honble the Governor of Penang" — i.e., to the Government of Prince of Wales Island in its collective capacity, since the formal addressee was [[W. E. Phillips]] in his official character.

## Significance

Van der Kemp's distinction between individual governors and the collective government matters for his argument. The institutional voice of Penang — in correspondence with [[Calcutta]], with the [[Banda Acheh|Achehnese court]], and with the Dutch at [[Batavia]] — is often at odds with the policy of the individual governor of the moment; and the continuity of that institutional voice, through the successive administrations of [[Governor Petrie|Petrie]], [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Bannerman]], [[W. E. Phillips|Phillips]] and [[Robert Fullerton|Fullerton]], is one of the strands of his narrative. The "Prince of Wales Island Governor" of this article is, in effect, the corporate person of the Penang establishment — the addressee of grievances, the drawer-up of minutes, the authoriser of missions, and the signatory of treaties — across the entire period from the [[Captain Coombs' first Acheh mission (1817-1818)|first Coombs mission]] of [[1817]] through the [[Bellona mission to Sumatra's East Coast (1827-1828)|Bellona]] intervention of 1828.

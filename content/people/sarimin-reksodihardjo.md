**Sarimin Reksodihardjo** was an Indonesian civil servant who served as the acting governor (*dd. gouverneur*) of the province of North Sumatra (*Nd.-Sumatra*) following the dissolution of the [[Negara Sumatera Timur]] (N.S.T.) on 15 August [[1950]]. In this capacity he was the highest Indonesian civil authority on the East Coast of Sumatra. He played a key role in the peaceful transition from the federal N.S.T. to the unitary provincial structure.

## Appointment and transition

Sarimin was appointed by the minister [[Anak Agung Gde Agung]] to head the Committee for Preparation (*Comité van Voorbereiding*) charged with the orderly liquidation of the N.S.T., alongside Mr. [[S. M. Amin]] and Mr. [[M. Joesoef]]. The committee's communiqué of 5 August [[1950]] outlined the agreement whereby East Sumatra would become part of the autonomous province of North Sumatra, comprising the pre-war residencies of [[Atjeh]], [[Tapanuli]], and East Sumatra.

## Visit to Atjeh (February 1950)

In February [[1950]], shortly after taking up the governorship, Sarimin made an official visit to [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], accompanied by some ten civil servants and journalists. The delegation included Ir. [[R. S. Danunagoro]], [[Darwin Amin]], [[Abdul Wahab Siregar]], [[Ismail Daulay]], [[Moh. Diri Harahap]], Dr. [[Sahar]], and [[Zainul Basri]]. His first impressions of Atjeh were favourable: politically there were no longer any difficulties, though economically there were — the roads urgently needed improvement.

## Advisory college

As acting governor, Sarimin was assisted by a college of three:
1. [[Tengku M. Daudsjah]] — acting resident of [[Atjeh]];
2. Binanga Siregar — deputy resident of Tapanuli;
3. [[Radja Kaliamsjah Sinaga]] — former deputy head of state (*Wakil Wali Negara*) of the N.S.T.

He departed for Djakarta on 26 January [[1951]].

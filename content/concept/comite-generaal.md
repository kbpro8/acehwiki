**The comité-général** — secret session — was the parliamentary procedure under article 104 of the *Reglement van Orde* (Rules of Order) of the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]] by which the Chamber could deliberate on sensitive matters behind closed doors, with an oath of secrecy imposed on all present under article 105. The [[Parliamentary debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|Tweede Kamer debate on Atjeh war origins]] of April [[1874]] was conducted in four *comité-général* sessions on [[16]], [[17]], [[18]], and [[20 April]], and the decision to publish the proceedings was only taken on [[19 October]] [[1881]].

## The procedural debates

During the April [[1874]] sessions, extensive procedural debates occurred over whether to continue in *comité-général* or to move to open session. [[Bredius|Bredius]] proposed a motion to close the secret session and continue in open session, arguing that the secret matters had been sufficiently discussed and that members could express their convictions in public without referencing the secret documents. This motion was rejected 36–32. [[Haffmans|Haffmans]] proposed a modified motion to close the secret session and continue in open session while respecting the imposed secrecy; this was also rejected 36–30. The [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Minister of Colonies]] and the [[Gericke van Herwijnen|Minister of Foreign Affairs]] both insisted that the secret documents could not be discussed in open session.

[[Kappeyne van de Coppello|Kappeyne]] clarified that once the *comité-général* was closed with an oath of secrecy, no member could in open session refer to the secret documents — though general opinions could still be expressed. [[Van Zuylen van Nyevelt|Van Zuylen]] argued that members should be able to discuss the matters without referencing the documents, and that the Minister's position would effectively gag the Chamber about everything contained in the documents.

## The closure

On [[20 April]], after [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]]'s final reply, the *comité-général* was closed with an oath of secrecy under article 105 of the Rules of Order, binding all present not to disclose what had been said.

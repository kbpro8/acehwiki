**Indonesische Kunstnijverheid** — "Indonesian Arts and Crafts" — is the source book by Prof. [[T. J. Bezemer|T. J. Bezemer]] from which the present cluster of AcehWiki entries on Atjeh decorative art is drawn. It was published on [[15 October]] [[1933]] by N.V. tot Exploitatie van het Maandblad "[[Nederlandsch-Indië, Oud en Nieuw|Nederl-Indië, Oud en Nieuw]]" (The Hague: ten Hagen's Drukkerij en Uitgeversmaatschappij N.V.), *uitgegeven met medewerking der* (published with the assistance of) the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging "Koloniaal Instituut"|Koninklijke Vereeniging "Koloniaal Instituut"]] of [[Amsterdam]]. It is a *Platen-Atlas* (plate-atlas) — a quarto-format volume of photographic plates of indigenous craft objects from the [[Netherlands India|Nederlandsch-Indië]] archipelago, preceded by an *Inleiding* (introduction) of sixteen pages in which Bezemer surveys, technique by technique, the principal *kunstnijverheid* (crafts) practised across the archipelago. The book was acquired by the Universiteitsbibliotheek of the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam (accession LS 5411, shelfmark "BIBL VRIJE UNIVERSITEIT 05411 AMSTERDAM"), where it remains held.

## Author and provenance

[[T. J. Bezemer|Prof. T. J. Bezemer]] is identified on the title-page as the author of the *Inleiding*; the volume itself is described as *UITGEGEVEN MET MEDEWERKING DER KONINKLIJKE VEREENIGING "KOLONIAAL INSTITUUT", AMSTERDAM* — that is, published with the co-operation of the Royal Association "Colonial Institute" of [[Amsterdam]]. The publisher, the [[N.V. tot Exploitatie van het Maandblad "Nederl-Indië, Oud en Nieuw"|N.V. tot Exploitatie van het Maandblad "Nederl-Indië, Oud en Nieuw"]], was the corporate vehicle of the monthly journal *[[Nederlandsch-Indië, Oud en Nieuw|Nederl-Indië, Oud en Nieuw]]* (The Netherlands Indies, Old and New), and the Bezemer atlas was issued as a companion volume to that journal, sharing its printer (ten Hagen) and its editorial orientation towards colonial-ethnographic subjects. The book's date — [[1933]] — places it firmly in the late-colonial period of Dutch rule in the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]], three years after the [[1930|1930]] census and eight years before the [[1941|Japanese invasion]] of [[1941]]–[[1942|1942]]. It is, in this sense, a late-colonial survey, written by a Dutch professor drawing on Dutch museum collections (principally the [[Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]]'s ethnographic division, the Rijks Ethnographisch Museum at [[Leiden]], and the Museum voor Land- en Volkenkunde at [[Rotterdam]]) and on Dutch scholarly literature.

## Structure and contents

The volume is organised in two parts. The *Inleiding* runs from page 3 to page 16 and is divided into nine lettered sections, each treating one craft:

- **A. *Vlechtwerk*** (plaiting) — plates 1–13, pp. 4–5
- **B. *Weven*** (weaving) — plates 14–72, pp. 5–7
- **C. *Batikken*** (batik) — plates 73–87, pp. 7–9
- **D. *Bamboesnijwerk*** (bamboo-carving) — plates 88–108, p. 9
- **E. *Houtsnijwerk*** (wood-carving) — plates 109–145, p. 10
- **F. *Koperwerk*** (brasswork) — plates 146–181, p. 11
- **G. *Ijzersmeedkunst*** (ironwork) — plates 208–235, p. 12
- **H. *Goud- en Zilverwerk*** (gold- and silverwork) — plates 182–185, pp. 13 — this is the section that documents the [[Atjeh goldsmithing (soeasa technique)|Atjeh *soeasa* wrapping technique]]
- **I. *Vervaardiging van Wajangpoppen*** (the making of *wayang* puppets) — plates 186–198, pp. 14–16

The plates themselves (245 of them, in numbered groups from 1 to 245) follow, each captioned in four languages: Dutch, English, French and German. Each caption identifies the object, its place of origin, and — where relevant — the museum or private collection from which the photograph was drawn. An appendix (*Verzameling / Collection / Sammlung*, pp. 169–170) lists the photographers, draughtsmen, and lenders of the illustrations.

## The Atjeh material in the book

The Atjeh material in Bezemer's atlas, drawn on by the present cluster of AcehWiki entries, includes:

- The [[Atjeh goldsmithing (soeasa technique)|Atjeh *soeasa* wrapping technique]] — documented in *Inleiding* § H, where the technique of wrapping a copper core with a sheet of [[spinsbek|*spinsbek*]]/[[spinsbek|*soeasa*]] is described in detail (p. 13).
- The [[Atjeh weefgetouw (Bezemer plate 62)|Atjeh loom]] — *Weefgetouw. Atjeh, Sumatra* — illustrated as plate 62 (p. 48).
- The [[Atjeh imported silk warp ikat (Bezemer plate 65)|Atjeh imported-silk warp-ikat]] — *Geïmporteerd zijden weefsel. Atjeh, N. Sumatra* — illustrated as plate 65 (p. 49).
- The [[Atjeh silk ikat warp strokes (Bezemer plate 66)|Atjeh silk ikat with warp strokes]] — *Zijden ikatweefsel. Atjeh* — illustrated as plate 66 (p. 49).
- The [[Atjeh sirih-schotel en etensblad (Bezemer plate 172)|Atjeh sirih bowl and food tray]] — *Sirih-schotel en etensblad. Atjeh, Sumatra* — illustrated as plate 172 (p. 124).
- The [[Noord-Sumatra standing brass lamps (Bezemer plate 174)|standing brass lamps from Northern and Central Sumatra]] — plate 174 (p. 125) — which, since Atjeh is the principal *Noord-Sumatra* residency, must include Atjeh examples.

In addition, the *Inleiding* places Atjeh within several archipelago-wide craft-geographies, contrasting Atjeh's *soeasa* wrapping with the [[filigraanwerk (Padangsche Bovenlanden)|filigree of the Padangsche Bovenlanden]] (p. 13) and with the [[granuleeren|granulation]] of the Bataks, Dyaks and Timorese (p. 13); noting the Atjeh use of [[rimbaga|*rimbaga*]] wax in the [[cire perdue|*cire perdue*]] process described for Toba-Batak goldsmiths on [[Noord-Sumatra]] (p. 13); and including Atjeh alongside [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]], [[Kota Gédang|Kota Gédang]] and other Sumatran centres in the chapter on weaving. Bezemer's account is not Atjeh-centred — his concern is with the whole archipelago — but he consistently identifies Atjeh as the regional variant within each technique, and thus places Atjeh, technique by technique, within the broader Indonesian craft tradition.

## Comparison with Wagner's *Sierkunst in Indonesië*

The Bezemer atlas of [[1933]] is the earlier of the two Dutch plate-surveys of Indonesian decorative art that form the principal printed sources for the AcehWiki's cluster of Atjeh craft entries; the later is F. A. Wagner's *[[Sierkunst in Indonesië|Sierkunst in Indonesië]]* of [[1949]], which forms its own source-book cluster. The two books overlap in subject-matter — both survey the archipelago's decorative arts technique-by-technique, and both identify Atjeh as a regional variant within each technique — but they differ markedly in form and emphasis. Bezemer's volume is a *plate-atlas*: the photographs are the primary content, and the *Inleiding* is a brief sixteen-page commentary on the techniques the plates illustrate. Wagner's volume, by contrast, is a *handleiding* (manual) — a longer text-driven account in the *Insulinde* series of teacher-training manuals, with fewer and smaller illustrations. Bezemer's plates thus preserve, in 1933, a specifically photographic record of Atjeh craft objects in Dutch museum collections at the moment when those collections were at their fullest, sixteen years before Wagner's text-driven survey.

## Significance

For the present cluster of AcehWiki entries, *Indonesische Kunstnijverheid* is the founding source — the book from which the entries on Atjeh decorative art are drawn. Its significance in the larger history of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]nese studies is that it offers, in a single 1933 survey, a synthetic account of Atjeh decorative art that places Atjeh, technique by technique, within the broader Indonesian tradition. Bezemer's account is, in this sense, the principal Dutch scholarly source for the study of Atjeh decorative art in the late colonial period, and the entries drawn from it are, in turn, the AcehWiki's first account of the Atjeh craftsman's work in plaiting, weaving, sirih-ware, and metalwork as documented photographically in 1933.

**Beaumontgeweer**, *repeating rifle*, *breech-loading rifle*, *muzzle-loader*, **Donderbus** and **Lilla** were the principal firearms used by both sides in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*. The Dutch colonial army was equipped primarily with the M.71 Beaumont, single-shot bolt-action rifle, magazine-fed in the Beaumont-94 variant and the newer repeating rifle, likely the M.95 Mannlicher, with a range up to 2,100 m, with repeating carbines issued to the *marechaussée*. The Achehnese used a wide range of weapons: breech-loading rifles, including many Dutch-supplied Beaumonts, *voorlaadgeweren* / muzzle-loaders, blunderbusses, Peabody rifles, and the small swivel gun. The Dutch distribution of 25,000 *Beaumontpatronen*, 380 *achterlaad-* and 500 *voorlaadgeweren* to [[Teuku Umar]] during his tenure as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] transformed the Achehnese resistance into a force "grootendeels even goed bewapend als onze eigen infanterie" (largely as well armed as our own infantry).

## Etymology and Meaning

The term *Beaumontgeweer* honours the Dutch armourer Edouard de Beaumont, designer of the M.71 Beaumont rifle — an 11 mm single-shot bolt-action rifle that was the standard Dutch infantry weapon of the late nineteenth century. The Beaumont-Vitali modification (Beaumont-94) added a four-round Vitali box magazine. repeating rifle denotes any magazine-fed rifle that can be reloaded by manual operation of the bolt; in the [[1890s]] Dutch context this likely refers to the M.95 Mannlicher. *Repeteerkarabijnen* are the shorter carbine versions, issued to mounted troops. breech-loader and *voorlaadgeweer* / muzzle-loader are functional categories. literally "thunder-gun" is the Dutch word for blunderbuss. *Lilla* is the Achehnese term for a small swivel gun, probably cognate with Malay *lila*. *Peabody-geweer* honours the American designer Henry O. Peabody.

## Historical Context

In the first semester of [[1896]] the asymmetry of firearms that had characterised the early [[Aceh War]] had substantially eroded. Between medio [[1893]] and the end of March [[1896]] the Dutch colonial government distributed large quantities of Beaumont rifles, *achterlaadgeweren*, *voorlaadgeweren* and 25,000 *Beaumontpatronen* to "goedgezinde" (well-disposed) Achehnese heads and allies — principally to [[Teuku Umar]] as [[Panglima Prang Besar]]. When Umar defected on [[29 March]] [[1896]], these weapons turned against the Dutch. The [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] — in which a Dutch column was driven back with the loss of a *mitrailleur* and a 7 cm L.A. *kanon* — was fought against an enemy "grootendeels even goed bewapend als onze eigen infanterie". The Dutch [[Besluit of 9 May 1896]] forbidding further weapon distribution to Achehnese heads was the formal recognition that the policy had failed. The Dutch response was a sustained request for *repeteergeweren* — 35 *repeteergeweren* and 37 *repeteerkarabijnen* requested from [[Batavia]] on [[15 April]] [[1896]], 1,000 requested from Holland by telegram (delivery promised medio July, but rejected by the *Regeerings-Commissaris* on account of the Dutch *vizier* — sight — being deemed a disadvantage).

The *donderbussen* captured at [[Lam Isi]]. The 35 *repeteergeweren* and 37 *repeteerkarabijnen* requested from Batavia on [[15 April]] [[1896]]. The 1,000 *repeteergeweren* requested from Holland (medio July delivery, rejected by Regeerings-Commissaris due to Dutch *vizier*). The 6,921 Beaumont-94 patronen for repeteergeweren (page 212). ## Significance

The firearms are the technological context of the [[Aceh War]]'s asymmetry and the principal material consequence of the failed Dutch "alliance" policy. The decision to arm Umar's *legioen* with Dutch Beaumonts and *achterlaadgeweren* transformed the resistance from a poorly-armed insurgency into a force capable of meeting Dutch infantry in conventional engagements. The Dutch scramble for *repeteergeweren* in April–May [[1896]] — and the rejection of Dutch-made rifles on account of the inferior *vizier* — shows the strategic significance of the small-arms balance. The [[Besluit of 9 May 1896]] forbidding further weapon distribution is the formal Dutch admission that the policy had produced "een talrijken aaneengesloten vijand... grootendeels even goed bewapend als onze eigen infanterie". The transition from Beaumont to *repeteergeweer* would culminate in the universal issue of the M.95 Mannlicher to Dutch forces, restoring the technological asymmetry that the Umar alliance had temporarily erased.

**Thiwa** (Malay *sewa*) was the lease of a rice field in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] for a fixed rent in *padi*, as distinguished from the share-cropping contract *[[Mawaih|mawaih]]*; the act of leasing out was *peuthiwa*.

## Preference of the landholder

[[D. W. N. de Boer]] recorded that the landholder gave his *[[Sawah|sawah]]* out in *thiwa* rather than in *mawaih*, because under *mawaih* he was dependent on the diligence, the ability and the luck of the cultivator, whereas under *thiwa* a fixed return was stipulated which was due even in the event of crop failure.

## Rate in Keumangan

In the *landschap* (territory) [[Keumangan]] (Sigli) the rent for good *sawah*'s amounted to two *[[Goentja|goentja]]* of *padi* per *[[Naleh|naleh]]* of *[[Bibit|bidjeh]]*.

## Assessment of the rent

De Boer set out the basis for a proper appreciation of that figure:

- the size of a rice field in Atjeh was expressed by the quantity of *bibit* (seed padi) needed on average to plant it, measured in *goentja*, *naleh*, *[[Gantang|gantang]]* and *[[Areh|areh]]* — one *goentja* being ten *naleh*, eighty *gantang*, one hundred and sixty *areh*;
- with one *naleh* about 1,600 square metres were planted;
- the yield of good *sawah*'s averaged fifty times, and of poor fields fifteen times, the quantity of seed.

The rent therefore amounted on average to two-fifths of the normally budgeted return.

## Regional distribution

Because a *mawaih* contract was safer for the planter — taking account of the actual return — the *mawaih* form occurred far more frequently, or even almost exclusively, in districts with extensive rice complexes such as the *afdeeling* (district) [[Pidie|Pidie]]; while leasing (*peuthiwa*) was found rather in districts short of rice fields. A factor of significance was, self-evidently, whether a rice field could be irrigated well, badly or not at all, and whether it lay in a place more or less safe from *bandjirs* (floods).

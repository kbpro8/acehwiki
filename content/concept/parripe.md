**Parripe** was the Toba-Batak term for a dependant settled on land issued or re-issued by the *[[Radja Hoeta|Radja (ni) Hoeta]]*, and thereby bound to him; the corresponding lands were the *parripean* grounds. The institution was described by [[D. W. N. de Boer]] in *[[Het recht op den grond in Atjeh (De Boer)|Het recht op den grond in Atjeh]]* only as a comparative foil, since he found no parallel to it in [[Aceh|Atjeh]].

## Function in the Batak land system

Where a *Radja Hoeta* had annexed land left by a deceased or emigrant dependant without entitled heirs, he might either retain it for his own use or issue it (*uitgifte*) or re-issue it (*heruitgifte* (re-issue of land)) to new immigrants. Settlers admitted on such terms became his *parripe*'s: their tenure of the soil constituted the tie of dependence on which the *radja*'s *harajaon* rested. In the [[Toba-Bataklanden|Toba-Batak lands]] the practice was, in De Boer's account, a necessity of political survival after the fragmentation of the *marga*.

## Absence of a parallel in Atjeh

De Boer stated the negative finding explicitly: "Naar een parallel met *parripean*-gronden zoekt men hier vergeefs" — one searches in vain here for a parallel with *parripean* lands. Nor, he added, was the clearer of waste land in Atjeh subjected to any requirement of village membership, a point on which he referred to [[C. van Vollenhoven|Van Vollenhoven]]'s *[[Adatrecht (Van Vollenhoven)|Adatrecht]]*, pp. 190 and 200. The nearest Atjehnese analogue in economic effect — though not in genealogical purpose — was the *[[Thiplah tano|thiplah-tano]]* contract imposed on non-indigenous cultivators, under which half of the cleared land or half of the yearly half-crop passed to the *[[Oeleebalang|oeleebalang]]*.

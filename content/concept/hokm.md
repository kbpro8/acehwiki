**Hokm** — Atjehnese/Dutch spelling of Arabic *ḥukm* (حُكْم), "judgement, decree, the religious law" — is the term used in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] to denote Islamic religious law, as distinct from the *adat* (customary law) that governs the greater part of Atjehnese social life. The term is recorded in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) in the context of the Atjeh proverb that distinguishes the two domains: *\"de adat is als het binden van het dier, de hoekom als het snijden\"* — "adat is like binding the beast, *hokm* like slaughtering it." (See [[Adat and Hokm Atjeh proverb]].)

## Distinction from adat

Kernkamp's account places *hokm* in explicit contrast to *adat*: "Hokm (religious law) and Adat (indigenous customary law) are inseparable, like God's essence and His attributes", reads the Atjeh proverb he cites, "but, as [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], the great connoisseur of this material, adds, 'most of these is the adat'." The proverb and Snouck Hurgronje's gloss together express the characteristic Atjehnese view that *adat* and *hokm* are inseparable but not equal: the *adat* — the customary law of the village, the *gampong*, the *mukim*, the *sagoe* — covers most of social life, while the *hokm* covers only the narrower domain of specifically religious obligations (the ritual law of prayer, fasting, pilgrimage, alms; the family law of marriage, divorce, inheritance; the small residue of public law that the colonial government had not taken over).

Kernkamp notes further that "whole chunks of Islamic law — traffic law, state law, criminal law, for example — have not been taken over into the adat. Nor are the original adat regulations in any way considered inferior. All regulations are considered to be those of a Muslim people and therefore also Muslim." The Atjehnese distinction between *adat* and *hokm* is thus not a distinction between Islamic and non-Islamic law, but between two domains of law both of which are, in Atjehnese eyes, Islamic — one (the *adat*) handled by every adult, the other (the *hokm*) reserved to experts.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *hokm* concept is significant as the theoretical complement of the [[Adat|adat]]: the two together define the Atjehnese legal universe, and the Atjeh proverb on their relationship (in which *adat* is the binding of the beast and *hokm* the slaughtering) is one of the most-cited formulations of the Atjehnese legal synthesis. The same distinction underlies the Atjeh institution of the *kadi* (religious judge, in Atjeh pronounced *kali*), which Kernkamp notes was historically independent in Atjeh but whose jurisdiction "has been usurped by the secular heads, who now use them only as unsalaried advisers, without any appointment, although each head has his own *kali*."

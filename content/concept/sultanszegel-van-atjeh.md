**The Sultanszegel van Atjeh** (Dutch for "Sultan's seal of Atjeh") is a silver-gilt (*verguld zilver*) seal matrix preserved in the [[Koninklijk Bataviaasch Genootschap van Kunsten en Wetenschappen|Batavian Society's]] treasure room collection, catalogued in Cabinet 4 (Atjeh), item 28. It is described in the 1928 guide as *"het zgn. negenvoudige zegel"* — "the so-called nine-fold seal" (*negenvoudig* meaning "nine-fold" or "nine-times").

## The "nine-fold" designation

The term *negenvoudig* (nine-fold) likely refers to the seal's capacity to produce nine distinct impressions, possibly through multiple faces or through a matrix containing nine separate seal devices arranged on a single block. In the Acehnese sultanate tradition, the sultan's seal (*chab* or *mohor*) was the supreme instrument of royal authority, used to authenticate state correspondence, letters patent, decrees, and treaties. The possession of multiple seal impressions on a single device may have reflected the sultan's authority over the multiple domains of the sultanate — the three sagis, the capital, and other constituent territories — or may simply have been a practical convenience.

As [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] discusses in *The Achehnese*, the sultan's seal was a critical instrument of governance: letters bearing the sultan's *chab* carried the force of royal command, and the seals themselves were treated with the utmost reverence. The loss or seizure of a sultan's seal was a matter of great political consequence.

## Material and craftsmanship

The seal is of silver-gilt — silver overlaid with gold. This combination of materials was common for high-status Acehnese metalwork, providing the appearance and prestige of gold while using a more readily available base metal. The seal's presence in the Batavian Society's collection indicates that it was acquired during the colonial period, most likely during or after the fall of the sultanate in the [[Aceh War]].

***Soesah*** — Malay *susah*, meaning trouble, difficulty or bother — appears in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* in the account of [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]]'s negotiation with the chief [[Teukoe Oemar van Bernoen]] on the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|North Coast of Atjeh]].

Asked to assemble all the blind of his district for the following day, the chief — out of humour for reasons personal and general, and much afflicted by the *[[Malaise|malaise]]* — "kwam met tal van bezwaren. Het zou toch weinig helpen en het gaf zoo veel soesah alles voor niets."

The word is used in the source without translation, as one of the Malay terms that had passed into the colonial Dutch of the Indies. Tijssen's reply — that it would indeed cost the chief a very great deal of trouble — engaged the chief's *eergevoel*, and by the next morning everything had been arranged "in de puntjes."

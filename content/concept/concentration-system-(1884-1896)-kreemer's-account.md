**The Concentration system** (*concentratiestelsel*) of [[1884]]–[[1896]] — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the Dutch policy of "inkrimping van het rechtstreeks onder ons bestuur gebracht gebied, gepaard met een strenge 'blokkade' van de kust" — "contraction of the area brought under our direct administration, accompanied by a strict 'blockade' of the coast" — was, in Kreemer's judgement, the greatest service anyone ever did the Atjèhnese war-party: "zeker niemand de oorlogspartij ooit een grooter dienst bewees, dan de uitvinder van deze concentratie" — "certainly no one ever did the war-party a greater service than the inventor of this concentration."

## The decision

The concentration policy was decided on [[19 August]] [[1884]], in response to the steady deterioration of the Dutch position after the premature introduction of civil government in [[1881]]. The war costs had risen, by the end of [[1884]], to ƒ150 million; the deficit in the Indies treasury had reached ƒ86 million. "De Regeering zon op een middel om uit dezen onhoudbaren toestand te geraken, en zij meende dit middel gevonden te hebben in een 'stelsel van concentratie'" — "The Government sought a means to escape from this untenable situation, and believed it had found this means in a 'system of concentration.'" Colonel H. Demmeni was appointed *civiel en militair gouverneur* on the same date, combining civil and military authority in one hand.

## The three principles

The concentration, completed by early March [[1885]], was characterised by three principles:

**a.** The abandonment of part of the occupied territory — where Dutch authority "feitelijk toch niet bestond" — "in fact did not exist" — and the occupation of a smaller area that could actually be held. In place of the existing posts — partly those of [[General J. H. R. Kohler|Pel]]'s conquest-tours, partly those of the [[General van der Heyden|Van der Heijden]] expeditions of [[1878]]–[[1879]] — a well-connected post-line (*postenrij*) was laid around the main strongholds of [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] and [[Ulee Lheue|Oelèë Lheuë]]. The concentrated line — 16 posts, surrounded by a 1,000-metre-wide free zone stripped of kampongs, trees, and brush — ran roughly from Koeta pöamat (Koeta Pohama), over Peukan Kroeëng Tjoet, [[Lam Nyong|Lam Njöng]], Roempét, Lam Peureumè, [[Lam Baro|Lam-Barö]], [[Keutapang Dua|Keutapang Doea]], and thence north-westward to the sea. Communication between the posts and Koeta Radja was maintained by a *ceintuurstoomtrambaan* (belt steam-tramway) with two radial lines to Koeta Radja, where it connected to the line to Oelèë Lheuë. The posts were interconnected by a telephone network with a central bureau at Koeta Radja.

**b.** Restriction, as far as possible, of Dutch interference in the internal affairs of the [[Onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]]. The posts at [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] and [[Lho Seumawe|Lhö Seumawè]] were abolished in [[1884]].

**c.** A strict blockade (proclaimed [[20 September]] [[1884]]) of the coast, to prevent the enemy bands from being supplied by sea with war-materials and provisions.

## The failure

Kreemer's verdict on the concentration is severe. Militarily, the reduction of posts allowed a reduction of three infantry battalions — but this advantage was negated by the disastrous health conditions within the small occupied area (just over 1 square *G.M.* — geographical mile — where 5,000 men were heaped and locked up). "Vooral de beri-beri maakte tal van slachtoffers (in 1885 en 1886 bedroeg het aantal beri-beri-zieken bijv. 36 en 34°/0 der legersterkte!)" — "Above all the beri-beri claimed many victims (in 1885 and 1886 the number of beri-beri-sick amounted, for example, to 36 and 34% of the army-strength!)." The great defensive capacity of the position was offset by the exclusion of offensive action outside it: "het offensief optreden buiten de stelling door de concentratie was buitengesloten, waardoor we niet konden beletten, dat kleine benden telkens onze stelling en onze soldaten aanvielen" — "offensive action outside the position was excluded by the concentration, so that we could not prevent small bands from repeatedly attacking our position and our soldiers." Overfalls within the line became the order of the day, requiring severe penalties on the carrying of weapons by the Atjèhnese population, and a pass-system was introduced on [[1 March]] [[1885]].

Politically, the concentration was, in Kreemer's account, a catastrophe. "In de oogen der oorlogspartij was zij niet anders dan een terugtocht, waartoe zij ons door hare aanvallen had gedwongen en waardoor haar moreel aanmerkelijk werd verhoogd. Met de concentratie begon het tijdperk der oelama's, die toen de leiders van den oorlog werden" — "In the eyes of the war-party it was nothing other than a retreat, to which she had forced us by her attacks, and by which her morale was considerably raised. With the concentration began the period of the ulama, who then became the leaders of the war." [[Teungku Tiro|Teungkoe Tirö]] — "tot aan zijn dood toe (1890) de leider bij uitnemendheid" — "until his death (1890) the leader par excellence" — formed a standing army, zealously collected the *ha' sabi* — the portion of the *zakat* destined for the holy war — and surrounded the entire Dutch line with a ring of small concealed *benteng*s, "zoodat het er veel van had, of hij het was, die ons met kracht van wapenen ingesloten hield" — "so that it looked much as if it was he who kept us enclosed by force of arms."

## The abandonment of the blockade

The political end — the pacification of the land — was no more achieved by the concentration than by [[General J. H. R. Kohler|Pel]]'s 1877 system. "Niet alleen had men al de veroveringen na 1877 prijs gegeven, maar feitelijk was men in hoofdzaak tot den toestand van dat jaar teruggekeerd" — "Not only had all the conquests after 1877 been given up, but in fact one had essentially returned to the condition of that year." The blockade — the strong part of the programme — was already abandoned in May [[1885]], only the weak-passive part (the concentration itself) being maintained: "Behalve ten opzichte van opium en krijgsbehoeften was de handel nu dus geheel vrij. Natuurlijk kwam dit de verzetspartij weer ten goede, daar het voorname middel, dat haar nog ontbrak — overvloed van geldmiddelen — haar nu overvloedig toevloeide" — "Except regarding opium and war-materials, trade was now wholly free. Naturally this again benefited the resistance-party, since the principal means that it still lacked — abundance of financial means — now flowed to it abundantly."

## The war costs

The concentration did not even fulfil the expectation of saving the treasury. The war costs, which during this period still amounted to some ƒ7 million per year, had risen by early [[1893]] to approximately ƒ210 million.

## Significance

The concentration system is, in Kreemer's narrative, the low point of Dutch fortunes in the Atjèh war — the moment at which the Dutch abandoned the conquests of [[1878]]–[[1879]], shut themselves into a besieged perimeter, and handed the initiative to the *ulama*-led resistance. The concentration produced, as its direct political consequence, the [[Oema-periode (1893-1896)|Oema-period]] of [[Teungku Tiro|Teungkoe Tirö]] and [[Teukoe Oema|Teukoe Oema]], and it was only the abandonment of the concentration under [[Governor Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and the adoption of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s advice — "dat de onderwerping niet uitsluitend afgewacht, nog minder afgebedeld, maar dat zij afgedwongen moest worden" — "that submission could not merely be awaited, still less begged for, but must be forced" — that would reverse the decline.

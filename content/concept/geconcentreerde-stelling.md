**De Geconcentreerde Stelling** (The Concentrated Position) was the strategic defensive line established by the Dutch colonial military in [[Groot-Atjeh]] following the concentration decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]]. The position, completed on [[1 March]] [[1885]], consisted of a ring of fortifications approximately 4–5 palen from [[Kota-Radja]] and [[Olehleh]], enclosing the principal Dutch strongholds while abandoning the scattered posts that had proven unsustainable.

## Concept

The geconcentreerde stelling represented a strategic retreat from offensive operations to defensive consolidation. The concept was to:

1. Abandon isolated posts that could not be effectively defended or supplied
2. Consolidate forces within a defensible perimeter
3. Maintain genuine control over a smaller area rather than nominal control over a larger one
4. Reduce casualties and costs while maintaining the Dutch position

## The Line

The fortified line consisted of 18 posts plus watchhouses, forming a discontinuous perimeter around [[Kota-Radja]] and [[Olehleh]]:

**Western Section:**
- [[Kota-Pohama]] (75 men)
- [[Pakan-Kroeng-Tjoet]] (75 men)
- [[Lamjong]] (150 men)
- [[Boekit-Karang]] (50 men)
- [[Roempit]] (150 men)
- [[Tjot-Iri]] (50 men)
- Watchhouse at Lingkar

**Southern Section:**
- [[Lampermej]] (150 men)
- [[Siroen]] (50 men)
- [[Lambaroe]] (150 men)
- Watchhouse at Lamsajoen
- [[Lamrong]] (75 men)
- [[Lampeneroet]] (75 men)
- Watchhouse at Lamara
- [[Ketapan-Doea]] (150 men)
- [[Blang]] (50 men)
- [[Lamdjamoe]] (150 men)
- [[Lamtih]] (50 men)

## Infrastructure

The position was supported by extensive infrastructure:

### Communications

- Ceintuurtram (girdle tramway) connecting all posts
- Radial roads to [[Kota-Radja]]
- Telephone network with central bureau at [[Kota-Radja]]
- Water routes along the coast and rivers

### Defensive Features

- Semi-permanent fortifications with palisades
- Iron-draadversperring (razor-wire entanglement)
- Open terrain cleared 1000 meters before the line

## Strategic Assessment

### Advantages

- Defensibility: The position was proof against all attacks by indigenous forces
- Economy: Fewer troops needed to hold the perimeter
- Sustainability: Could be maintained without the losses of offensive operations

### Disadvantages

- Abandoned territory could be used by the enemy
- Enemy retained initiative and could choose when and where to attack
- Enemy morale was bolstered by the perception of Dutch retreat

## Significance

The geconcentreerde stelling became the foundation of Dutch strategy in [[Groot-Atjeh]] for years to come. It represented acknowledgment that the Aceh War would not be won quickly and that sustainable long-term control required consolidating gains rather than seeking further expansion.

**Dardanellen** — singular *dardanel* — was the [[K.N.I.L.]] Marechaussee term for the two *voorste Marechaussees* at the front of any patrouille: the point-men or skirmishers whose task was to take the first fire, read the first sporen, and engage the first enemy. The term is one of the distinctive technical vocabularies of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] recorded in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, and the *dardanel* was the most dangerous and most decorated post in the corps. The book's most fully drawn *dardanel* is [[Nussy]], who "was dardanel geweest bij [[Vastenou]] en [[Dersjant]], vermaarde patrouillecommandanten, die schitterende resultaten hadden geboekt."

## The term and its scope

The book introduces the term in the prologue, where the kapitein of the [[1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren]] — travelling through [[Medan]] around [[1933]] — "had uit de Blang zijn beide *dardanellen* meegenomen." The book glosses: "lieden, die *dardanellen* met iets uit het Nederlandse leger willen vergelijken, geneigd zijn hen *officiersoppassers* te noemen, terwijl zij, die er wat meer van weten, eerder denken aan een *twee-manslijfwacht*." That is, those who would compare the *dardanel* to something in the Dutch army are inclined to call him an officer's orderly; those who know more would rather think of him as a two-man bodyguard. The latter is closer to the truth: the *dardanellen|dardanellen* were the officer's bodyguard in the *[[rimboe]]*, and the point-men of the patrouille.

## The dardanel in action

In the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]], the *dardanellen|dardanellen* were the two voorste Marechaussees at the front of any patrouille. When *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]* was omsingeld and summoned to surrender, "hij trok zijn *[[reuntjong]]*, de Atjehse kris" and threw himself on "de twee voorste Marechaussees, de *dardanellen* van de luitenant." *[[Nussy]]* — himself a former *dardanel* — shot, and *[[Enoch]]* worked with the *[[klewang]]*.

On [[3 December]] [[1911]], the korporaal *[[Nussy]]* went out with his *spits* (the man ahead of even the *dardanel*), who soon turned back. Alone, Nussy gave the *vogelgeluid* signaal and waited. Three armed Acehnese approached; the voorste was *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]*, who aimed his *revolver* at Nussy. Nussy shot first. The episode is the book's set-piece of the *dardanel*'s work: alone, ahead of the patrol, reading the sporen and engaging the enemy first.

## The dardanel as the corps's most decorated post

Because the *dardanel* took the first fire and engaged the first enemy, the post was the most dangerous in the corps and the most decorated. *[[Nussy]]* — a *dardanel* under [[Vastenou]] and [[Dersjant]] — became a Ridder 3e klas of the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]]. The pairing of the *dardanel* with the *spits* (the man ahead of even the *dardanel*) and the *[[bekas]]* spoorzoeker (the footprint-reader) made up the point-system of the Marechaussee patrouille.

## Significance

The *dardanellen|dardanellen* are significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the distinctive technical vocabulary of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]'s patrouille method. The term — which has no exact equivalent in the Dutch metropolitan army — marks the corps as a colonial institution with its own idiom, developed in the *[[rimboe]]* of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] rather than in the barracks of the Netherlands. The book's set-piece *dardanellen|dardanellen* — [[Nussy]] above all — are the figures through whom Dames makes his argument about the moral economy of the colonial army: the men who went ahead alone, shot first, and earned the decorations that the *slippendragers* at [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|Thenu's funeral]] would carry.

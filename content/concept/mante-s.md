**The manté's** — the inlanders of the mountains of the XXII Mukims — are the Acehnese forest people of [[Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *De Atjehers* and of the chapter *Jagerslatijn* (Hunters' Latin) of *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]). The manté's — who were said to wear no clothing and to be densely hairy over the whole body — are the book's — and Snouck Hurgronje's — marker of the inlanders of the Acehnese mountains: the forest people whom all informants knew only by hearsay.

## The Snouck Hurgronje account

"In his well-known work *De Atjehers*, Snouck Hurgronje already makes mention of inlanders who are called *manté's*. These *manté's* would wear no clothing and would be densely hairy over the whole body. They would live in the mountains of the XXII Moekims, and all informants knew them only by hearsay. One informant knew to tell that, in the time of his grandfather, a *manté* married couple was brought captive to the Sultan of Atjeh. Despite all the efforts that were employed to persuade them, those forest-people refused to speak or to eat and finally died the voluntary death by starvation." ## Acehnese writings and the day's journey

"Also in Acehnese writings these *manté's* are sometimes mentioned, but Snouck Hurgronje already remarked that these beings always lived one day's journey further inland than the researcher who wished to see them." These manté's are also sometimes mentioned in Acehnese writings, but Snouck Hurgronje already remarked that these beings always lived one day's journey further inland than the researcher who wished to see them. The day's journey — the distance that always separated the manté's from the researcher — is the book's — and Snouck Hurgronje's — marker of the manté's' status as elusive inlanders: the forest people who were always just one day's journey further on.

## The orang pendek and the koekoeman

The manté's are introduced in the chapter *Jagerslatijn* in connection with the orang pendek — the "short man" of the Acehnese forests — whom the old hunter [[Datoek]] claims to have seen. The land-council chairman — interrupting Datoek's account — cites Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers* on the manté's as the scholarly frame for the orang pendek. The orang pendek — called koekoeman in Datoek's region — is the book's — and Datoek's — marker of the Acehnese forest people: the "short man" whom Datoek had seen from behind, with his own eyes, at half past noon, at a distance of only twenty metres.

## Significance

The manté's are significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the elusive inlanders of the mountains of the XXII Mukims described in Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers*. The book highlights that the manté's were known to all informants only by hearsay, and that they always lived one day's journey beyond where any researcher had reached. Through the connection to Datoek's orang pendek sighting, the manté's become the book's — and colonial scholarship's — marker for the mysterious forest people of the Acehnese interior, perpetually out of reach of direct observation.

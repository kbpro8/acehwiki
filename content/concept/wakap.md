**Wakap** — Dutch/Atjehnese spelling of Arabic *waqf* (وقف), in standard Indonesian *wakaf* — is the Islamic institution of perpetual religious endowment, by which a Muslim dedates property (land, buildings, income-producing assets) to a religious purpose in perpetuity, with the principal preserved and only the income spent. The term is recorded in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) in his account of the Indonesian mosque (*[[Mesigit|mesigit]]*).

## Kernkamp's account

Kernkamp introduces the *wakap* in the course of his description of the movable property of the Indonesian mosque: "There lie some long, narrow mats and carpets, which after the service are again put away, on which the pious are to take their places in rows. The Quran-lecterns are sometimes indeed finely carved. There further hang some lamps, there stands a simple pulpit, and further there is a niche in the wall that indicates the direction to Mecca. Further, one has at one's disposal services for communal meals of a religious character, a staff for the preacher, and a small library of pious works and sermons. Sometimes there also belong to the inventory long strips of white goods (*moesala*), which serve as prayer-rugs. All this movable good, too, is outside the trade (*wakap*)."

The *wakap* status of the mosque's movable goods — that is, their exemption from the ordinary commercial sphere — is presented by Kernkamp as a typical feature of the Indonesian mosque's institutional life: the mats, carpets, lamps, pulpit, prayer-rugs, library and services are all *wakap*, dedicated to the religious purposes of the mosque and not subject to sale or trade. The same protection extends, in Islamic law, to the mosque building itself and to any land or income-producing assets dedicated to its support.

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *wakap* is significant as the institutional framework within which the Atjeh mosques — the [[Meuseugit Raya|Meuseugit Raja]] of [[Kutaradja|Koeta Radja]], the [[Meuseugit Kaya]], and the village [[Meunasah|meunasahs]] — held their property and supported their religious officials. The Dutch-built replacement [[Meuseugit Raya|Meuseugit Raja]] of [[Kutaradja|Koeta Radja]], built at a cost of more than *f* 200,000 and dismissed by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] as *\"een ledig monument onzer dwaasheid\"* ([[Snouck Hurgronje's ledig monument verdict]]), would, on its completion, have been *wakap* property — that is, dedicated to religious purposes in perpetuity. The Atjehners' refusal to use the Dutch-built mosque for religious exercises did not, however, affect its *wakap* status; the building remained a religious endowment even as it stood empty.

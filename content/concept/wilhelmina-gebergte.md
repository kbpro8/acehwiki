**The Wilhelmina gebergte** — also called the *Koningin Wilhelmina gebergte*, the *Queen Wilhelmina Mountains*, in the [[1918]] Oostkust volume also the **Wilhelmina-keten** — is the northernmost section of the eastern Barisan coulisse of central [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and the western boundary of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]] with [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]]. Named in [[1912]] on the proposal of Dr. W. Volz of Breslau (with the Kab. Res. van [[5 September]] [[1912]] No. 17), the range runs northwest–southeast for nearly 130 km from Gunung Si Boeatan to beyond Gunung Bandahara, reaching elevations of 3,030 m. Its crest serves as the legal boundary between [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] and [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] per [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]] from the [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tamiang|Tamiang]] watershed to the Serbolangit group (Liang Gergas). In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] ([[1917]]), the Wilhelmina gebergte is the principal mountain range of central Atjeh and the orographic nucleus of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*.

## Naming (1912)

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I records the naming of the Wilhelmina gebergte in [[1912]]: *Op verzoek van Dr. W. Volz te Breslau ontving dit gebergte den naam van „Koningin Wilhelmina gebergte"* (Kab. Res. van [[5 September]] [[1912]] No. 17). The naming is the Bureau's principal evidence of the Dutch colonial practice of naming Sumatran orographic features after members of the Dutch royal house — a practice that linked the colonial geographical survey to the metropolitan monarchical culture. The Wilhelmina gebergte was named for [[Koningin Wilhelmina|Queen Wilhelmina]] of the Netherlands, who reigned from [[1890]] to [[1948]].

The [[1918]] Oostkust volume adds Volz's own rationale for the naming: *Zoo gezien schijnt dit hooggebergte wel de moeite waard, een Koninklijken naam te dragen* — "Seen thus, this high mountain range seems well worth bearing a Royal name." Volz identified the range as the most spectacular expression of the *Batak-boog* (Batak arc), a meridional fold system forming the eastern Barisan row in northern Sumatra. The *van Heutszgebergte* (third Barisan row) abuts its eastern flank.

## Orography

The Bureau's [[1917]] Hoofdstuk I describes the Wilhelmina gebergte as the continuation of the eastern volcanic row north of the [[Tobameer|Tobameer]]. The range opens at the *Deleng Si Boeatan* (60) at the north-west corner of the [[Tobameer|Tobameer]] and runs northward through the *Pebatoekan* (61), *Bidoel* (62), *Peperkisopn* (63), *Bandahara* (64) and *Kembar* (65), with the *brongebied* of the [[Lao Alas|Alas rivier]] standing in the middle of the range. The range bends westward at the *Abong-Abong* (66) — the *Gajoesche Centraalgebergte* — and turns northward again, terminating at the [[Goudberg|Goudberg]] (67) in Groot-Atjeh.

The Wilhelmina gebergte's principal peaks — the *Bandahara* (64), *Kembar* (65) and *Abong-Abong* (66) — are the highest of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, and form the watershed between the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] and the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Kroeëng Wojla]]. The *G. Loser* (70), at 3,520 m the highest volcano of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, stands at the northern edge of the range and is the source of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas (Woi ni Agoesein)]].

The [[1918]] Oostkust volume's perspective extends the picture from the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] side. The Wilhelmina-keten constitutes the northern segment of the second (eastern) Barisan row, composed of pre-Tertiary schists and gneisses (the *skelet van het bergland*). Key peaks:

- Gunung Bandahara (3,030 m / 2,875 m per Volz) — northern terminus at the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] boundary.
- Gunung Aboe Aboe (1,684 m) — tripoint of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]], [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]], [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]].
- Gunung Siboeatan (2,425 m) — southern end of the range proper.
- Gunung Salit / Tjengkeh (1,500 m) — north of the Lau Bengap breakthrough.

The range is divided by the Lau Bengap breakthrough (at Kota Bangoen, ~400 m) into a southern section (Siboeatan complex, 2,460 m max, with steep meridional valleys Lau Djandi, Lau Goenoeng) and a northern section (Salit/Tjengkeh ridge, rising to the Bandahara–Pēpér-kisön volcanic frontier, 2,700–3,080 m, on the [[Alaslanden|Alas land]] border).

## Boundary role

[[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]] fixed the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]]–[[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] boundary along the watershed crest of the Wilhelmina-keten from the [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tamiang|Tamiang]] watershed to Liang Gergas (1,888 m) in the Serbolangit group. This was the only [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] boundary precisely demarcated by law. The Topografische Dienst proposed an alternative line from Liang Gergas down the Lau Penjabrangan valley, but the crest line prevailed.

The tripoint at Gunung Aboe Aboe (where [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]], [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]], [[Tapanoeli|Tapanoeli]] meet) lies on the Wilhelmina-keten crest. From there, the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] boundary continues south along the range's eastern spur forming the northern rim of [[Tobameer|Lake Toba]].

## Hydrology

The Wilhelmina-keten forms the main watershed of northern Sumatra:

- **East slope**: drains to Strait of Malaka via Lau Biang (Wampoe), Sungai [[Langkat|Langkat]], Sungai [[Besitang|Besitang]], Sungai Lepang.
- **West slope**: drains to Indian Ocean via [[Lao Alas|Lau Alas]] (Singkel), Lau Renoen, Lau Bengap (which breaks through the range at Kota Bangoen).
- **South slope**: drains to [[Tobameer|Lake Toba]] → [[Asahan|Asahan River]] → Strait of Malaka.

The Lau Bengap breakthrough is the only river crossing the range, capturing the western drainage and diverting it east through the [[Batak Karo|Karo Plateau]] to the Lau Renoen. The Oeroek Mendjiri ridge separates the Lau Bengap and Lau Biang catchments on the [[Batak Karo|Karo Plateau]].

## Transport barrier and passes

The range was a formidable barrier (*verkeershindernis*) between the [[Batak Karo|Karo Plateau]] and the [[Alaslanden|Alas]]/[[Batak Pakpak|Pakpak]] lands. Key passes:

- **Lau Bengap breakthrough** (Kota Bangoen, ~400 m): main route; now the great highway from [[Alaslanden|Alas]] ([[Koeta Tjane|Koeta Tjane]]) via Koeta Bangoen to [[Kaban Djahe|Kaban Djahe]] and via Tingkem Pass to [[Medan|Medan]]. Suitable for motor traffic.
- **Lau Mbēlin / Lau Teba valleys**: difficult footpaths to [[Alaslanden|Alas]] and Blang Nodi.
- **Siboeatan complex passes**: footpaths to [[Batak Pakpak|Pakpak lands]]; trace surveyed for road Sariboe Dolok–[[Sidikalang|Sidikalang]] ([[1918]]).
- **Tingkem Pass** (Pintoe-Baroes): historic main access from [[Batak Karo|Karo Plateau]] to east coast ([[Medan|Medan]]); now the main highway [[Medan|Medan]]–[[Kaban Djahe|Kaban Djahe]].

## Significance

The Wilhelmina gebergte is, in the [[1917]] Bureau's Sumatra survey, the principal mountain range of central [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and the orographic nucleus of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*. The Bureau's emphasis on the range's naming in [[1912]] — and on its composition from the *Bandahara*, *Kembar* and *Abong-Abong* peaks — frames the Wilhelmina gebergte as the geographical foundation of the Dutch colonial state's central-Atjeh *bivaks* at [[Takengon|Takengon]] and [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]], and as the orographic nucleus of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] and [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] hydrographical systems.

In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the Wilhelmina gebergte is the orographic theatre of the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Blangkedjeren capitulation of 28 March 1942]] and of the [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|Van Zanten resistance]] of [[March 1942|March 1942]]–[[March 1943|March 1943]].

The [[1918]] Oostkust volume extends the picture from the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] side: the Wilhelmina-keten is the legal boundary of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust gewest]] — the only such boundary to be *nauwkeurig omgeschreven* (precisely demarcated) by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]] — and the principal orographic barrier of northern Sumatra, whose Lau Bengap breakthrough and Tingkem Pass shaped the colonial transport network that linked the [[Batak Karo|Karo Plateau]] and the [[Alaslanden|Alas lands]] to the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] cultivation zone.

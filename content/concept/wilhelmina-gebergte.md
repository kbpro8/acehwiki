**The Wilhelmina gebergte** — also called the *Koningin Wilhelmina gebergte*, the *Queen Wilhelmina Mountains* — is the central-Atjeh mountain range of the eastern Barisan coulisse, named in [[1912]] on the proposal of Dr. W. Volz of Breslau (with the Kab. Res. van 5 September [[1912]] No. 17), and the principal orographic feature of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte* between the [[Tobameer|Tobameer]] and the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] headwaters. In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), the Wilhelmina gebergte is the principal mountain range of central Atjeh, and the orographic nucleus of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*.

## Naming (1912)

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I records the naming of the Wilhelmina gebergte in [[1912]]: "Op verzoek van Dr. W. Volz te Breslau ontving dit gebergte den naam van „Koningin Wilhelmina gebergte" (Kab. Res. van 5 Sept. 1912 No. 17)." The naming is the Bureau's principal evidence of the Dutch colonial practice of naming Sumatran orographic features after members of the Dutch royal house — a practice that linked the colonial geographical survey to the metropolitan monarchical culture. The Wilhelmina gebergte was named for [[Koningin Wilhelmina|Queen Wilhelmina]] of the Netherlands, who reigned from [[1890]] to [[1948]].

## Orography

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I describes the Wilhelmina gebergte as the continuation of the eastern volcanic row north of the [[Tobameer|Tobameer]]. The range opens at the *Deleng Si Boeatan* (60) at the north-west corner of the Tobameer and runs northward through the *Pebatoekan* (61), *Bidoel* (62), *Peperkisopn* (63), *Bandahara* (64) and *Kembar* (65), with the *brongebied* of the [[Lao Alas|Alas rivier]] standing in the middle of the range. The range bends westward at the *Abong-Abong* (66) — the *Gajoesche Centraalgebergte* — and turns northward again, terminating at the [[Goudberg|Goudberg]] (67) in Groot-Atjeh.

The Wilhelmina gebergte's principal peaks — the *Bandahara* (64), *Kembar* (65) and *Abong-Abong* (66) — are the highest of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, and form the watershed between the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] and the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Kroeëng Wojla]]. The *G. Loser* (70), at 3,520 m the highest volcano of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, stands at the northern edge of the range and is the source of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas (Woi ni Agoesein)]].

## Significance

The Wilhelmina gebergte is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the principal mountain range of central Atjeh, and the orographic nucleus of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*. The Bureau's emphasis on the range's naming in [[1912]] — and on its composition from the *Bandahara*, *Kembar* and *Abong-Abong* peaks — frames the Wilhelmina gebergte as the geographical foundation of the Dutch colonial state's central-Atjeh *bivaks* at [[Takengon|Takengon]] and [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]], and as the orographic nucleus of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] and [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] hydrographical systems.

In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, the Wilhelmina gebergte is the orographic theatre of the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|Blangkedjeren capitulation of 28 March 1942]] and of the [[Van Zanten's resistance (March 1942 – March 1943)|Van Zanten resistance]] of [[March 1942|March 1942]]–[[March 1943|March 1943]].

***Avitaminose*** — avitaminosis, deficiency disease — appears in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* as one of the principal causes of blindness among Acehnese children, and as the disease whose treatment [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] singled out for praise in the work of the Protestant *[[Zending|zending]]*.

## The mission's cod-liver oil treatment

Tijssen was careful, in speaking of his own *[[Kampong medicine in Atjeh|kampong]]* work, not to disparage the missions: "Ik heb het werk van de zending gezien. Geloof niet, dat ik dien arbeid gering schat. Wanneer u schrijft, zeg dan vooral dit. De menschen van de zending weten zelf nauwelijks, hoe goed bijv. het werk is, dat zij deden door de behandeling der avitaminose met levertraan, waardoor bij honderden kinderen vroege blindheid is voorkomen." — I have seen the work of the mission. Do not believe that I undervalue that labour. When you write, say this above all. The people of the mission themselves hardly know how good is the work they did, for instance, by the treatment of avitaminosis with cod-liver oil, whereby early blindness was prevented in hundreds of children.

The treatment referred to is the administration of *levertraan* — cod-liver oil — against the vitamin A deficiency whose ocular manifestation, xerophthalmia, was then a leading cause of childhood blindness in the Indies. Tijssen credited the mission with having prevented early blindness in hundreds of children by that means.

## The West Coast observation

On the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast of Atjeh]] Tijssen made a contrary observation, of a district in which the disease was strikingly rare. Of the coastal strip around [[Bakongan]] he wrote that money the population did not have, "maar aan producten is het land rijk en daaruit alleen kan ik het opvallend weinig voorkomen van avitaminose verklaren." — but the land is rich in produce, and from that alone can I explain the strikingly rare occurrence of avitaminosis. The *voedselverhoudingen* — the food conditions — were, he said, excellent there.

The observation is a rare instance in the literature of the period of a direct correlation drawn between local food supply and the incidence of deficiency blindness in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], and it stands in the account as a counterweight to the general picture of medical neglect.

## Relation to Tijssen's argument

Avitaminosis served Tijssen's larger thesis in two ways. It showed that much blindness was preventable rather than curable, reinforcing his rule that "er vóór alles spoedig geholpen moet worden"; and it showed that non-hospital agencies — the missions in the villages, and the local food economy itself — could achieve results that the great hospitals could not. Both points supported his call for physicians willing to do "het vuile werk in de kampong" — the dirty work in the kampong alongside the fine work in the hospitals.

## Gomperts's account of vitamin A deficiency in Groot-Atjeh ([[1937]])

Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]]'s [[Oogheelkundig kampongonderzoek in Groot-Atjeh (Maart-December 1937)|survey of 1937]] gave the fullest statistical picture of deficiency eye disease in [[Groot-Atjeh]]: 459 cases of *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* among 11,400 children examined, about 4 per cent.

### The chronic deficiency

Gomperts held that since the basic food of the Atjehnese *[[Kampong|kampong]]* population consisted of rice, supplemented only very sparsely with vegetables and meat, the population of Groot-Atjeh suffered a chronic shortage of vitamin A — "of misschien beter gezegd, zich op een lager vitamine-A-gehalte van het bloed heeft ingesteld," or perhaps better said, had adjusted itself to a lower vitamin A content of the blood.

### Against a purely dietary explanation

He nevertheless denied that dietary deficiency alone explained the disease, quoting [[Zeeman]]'s warning against "een al te eenvoudige voorstelling." Were diet the sole cause, a far higher percentage than 4 per cent would have been expected. He tested the proposition against the harvest data of [[1936]]–[[1937]]: in the coastal *moekims* [[VI Moekims Lhong|Lhong]], where the rice supply was ample, the frequency was not visibly lower; in [[Lam Teuba|Moekim Lam-Teuba]], with a rice surplus, it was practically equal to that in [[Indrapoeri]], [[Seulimeum]] and [[Lam-Kabeuë]] where the harvest had failed; and in [[Montassik|Montassie]], with an almost complete crop failure from drought and *[[Walang sangit|walang sangit]]*, it differed little from [[VII Moekims Baet|Baet]] with a much better harvest.

His conclusion was that xerophthalmia and *[[Keratomalacie|keratomalacie]]* were to be regarded as conditions with one common underlying cause — an a- or hypovitaminosis A — complicated by a coincidence, an interpretation he took from Mevrouw [[Van Stockum]].

### Mothers' milk and infancy

The concentration of cases in the 0–5 age group he explained from the finding of Van Stockum, Dr. [[J. H. de Haas]] and Ir. [[Meulemans]] that the milk of indigenous mothers on [[Java]] was far lower in vitamin A than that of European women; Atjehnese nutrition being poorer still, and the Acehnese custom being protracted breast-feeding of eighteen to twenty months, the low level sufficed only to a point before growth and other factors depressed it into hypo- or avitaminosis "die de deur opendoet voor de xerophthalmie."

### Cod-liver oil and prevention

Gomperts distributed 404.1 kilogrammes of *[[Levertraan|levertraan]]* during the survey, and made its liberal distribution the second of his five recommended preventive measures — alongside a vigorous campaign against *[[Worminfectie|worm infection]]*, village survey and instruction, the cooperation of the [[Binnenlandsch Bestuur]], and the stimulation of vitamin-A-rich yard cultivation with instruction from the [[Landbouwdienst (Netherlands Indies)|Landbouwdienst]].

**[[8 February]]** is the date of the [[generaal Van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]] letter of [[8 February]] [[1874]] to several Acehnese chiefs, in which the grey warrior-hero wrote the well-known lines that "the great mosque would be rebuilt and would be made into a true place of mercy, from which no longer death and destruction, but prayers to Almighty God and works of love would go forth." In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), [[8 February]] is the book's marker of the Van Swieten letter: the well-known lines that followed the [[1873]] storming of the [[Meusigit Raja|mosque]].

## The Van Swieten letter

"The grey warrior-hero, General Van Swieten, addressed to several Acehnese chiefs in a letter of 8 February 1874 the well-known lines: 'that the great mosque would be rebuilt and would be made into a true place of mercy, from which no longer death and destruction, but prayers to Almighty God and works of love would go forth.'" The [[8 February]] [[1874]] letter is the book's — and Van Swieten's — clearest statement of the colonial state's religious politics in [[Atjeh|Aceh]]: the rebuilding of the mosque as a place of mercy, the replacement of death and destruction with prayers and works of love.

## Significance

[[8 February]] is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the date of the Van Swieten letter of [[8 February]] [[1874]]. The book frames this letter as the colonial state's response to the [[1873]] storming — a promise to transform the mosque from a site of warfare into a place of mercy. The date [[8 February]] thus serves as the book's marker for this key expression of colonial religious policy in Aceh.

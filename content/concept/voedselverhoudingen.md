***Voedselverhoudingen*** — food conditions, the state of nutrition — is the term used by [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]] in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* to explain the striking rarity of [[Avitaminose in Atjeh|avitaminosis]] on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast of Atjeh]].

## The observation

Of the coastal strip around [[Bakongan]] Tijssen wrote: "De voedselverhoudingen zijn hier uitstekend; geld is er niet; in Bakongan heeft het bestuur de reiskosten voor mijn patiënten betaald wanneer de afstand te groot was om dien te voet af te leggen. Maar aan producten is het land rijk en daaruit alleen kan ik het opvallend weinig voorkomen van avitaminose verklaren." — The food conditions here are excellent; money there is none; at Bakongan the administration paid the travelling costs for my patients when the distance was too great to cover on foot. But the land is rich in produce.

## Significance

The remark distinguishes two kinds of poverty: monetary poverty, which prevented patients from paying even the cost of a journey, and nutritional poverty, which was absent because the land was rich in produce. Tijssen drew from the distinction the conclusion that the West Coast, for all its reputation for turbulence and its road flanked by the mass graves of the Atjeh wars, was not a district of deficiency disease — in contrast to the smoke-filled houses of [[Samosir]], where he attributed the sevenfold preponderance of women among his [[Trachoma|trachoma]] patients to the conditions of domestic life.

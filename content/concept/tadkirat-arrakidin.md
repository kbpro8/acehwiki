**The Tadkirat arrakidin** — *Vermaning der stilzittenden*, an *Admonition to the Inactive*, that is, a call to join the war — is the Acehnese political pamphlet edited by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in his *rapport* of [[1892]]. The text was the work of [[Tengkoe Koetakarang]], and it takes as its central example of Dutch treachery the dismissal of [[Van der Heyden|generaal Van der Heyden]] — *de behandeling welke generaal Van der Heyden door onze regeering werd aangedaan, beschreven als een voorbeeld van Hollandsche onbetrouwbaarheid, welke zelfs de eigen verdienstelijke mannen niet spaart*.

## The title

The title *Tadkirat arrakidin* is, in Snouck Hurgronje's rendering, *Vermaning der stilzittenden* — an admonition to those who sit still, that is, who have not yet joined the *prang sabil*. The pamphlet is, accordingly, a *vlugschrift* in the strictest sense: a flying-sheet, an exhortation to take up arms. Zentgraff cites it explicitly *naar de bewerking der vlugschriften van Tengkoe Koetakarang door Snouck Hurgronje, vide diens rapport van 1892*.

## The Van der Heyden passage

The longest passage Zentgraff preserves from the *Tadkirat arrakidin* is the section on Van der Heyden's dismissal. *Het is goed dat wij nu en dan treden buiten eigen zelfgenoegzaamheid en àl te gemakkelijke nationale voldaanheid, en wat luisteren naar oordeel uit vijandelijk kamp* — it is good, Zentgraff writes, that we now and then step outside our own complacency and listen to the verdict from the enemy's camp. The pamphlet's argument is that the Dutch government's removal of Van der Heyden — the *éénoogige Generaal* whose energetic command had brought the Acehnese to their knees — showed that even the Dutch did not spare their own meritorious men; how then could the Acehnese trust Dutch promises of peace? *Naar eene ulteraard zeer globale schatting zijn, nà Van der Heyden's aftreden, ongeveer 30.000 Atjehers gedood*.

## Significance

The *Tadkirat arrakidin* is the rare Acehnese pamphlet preserved through Dutch scholarly editing. By reproducing, in the *rapport* of 1892, the Acehnese reading of the Van der Heyden affair, Snouck Hurgronje made available a text that the colonial government would otherwise have ignored: an enemy's-eye view of Dutch *onbetrouwbaarheid*. The pamphlet stands beside the [[Tiro-oelama's ultimatum letter (1885)|ultimatum letter of 1885]] and the [[Brief van 18 Radjab 1325|brief van 18 Radjab 1325]] as one of the three principal political documents of the *prang sabil* preserved in Zentgraff's book.

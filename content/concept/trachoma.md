***Trachoom*** — trachoma, the chronic contagious conjunctivitis that was among the leading causes of blindness in the tropics — is described at length in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* in [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]]'s account of the [[Trachoma campaign on Samosir (1933)|Samosir campaign]] of [[1933]], where he encountered it in place of the cataract that had dominated his Acehnese work.

## The encounter on Samosir

When Tijssen asked a native chief to bring the blind and eye-sick of his district on a given day, and visited the market-day centres of [[Samosir]], it appeared that among the candidates there was not a single cataract, "maar trachoom en nog eens trachoom." — but trachoma and again trachoma. He had never performed trachoma operations: "Zeker, ik wist uit de boeken precies wat trachoom is, maar alleen wie een trachoomstreek gezien heeft weet wat trachoom beteekent." — Certainly, I knew from the books precisely what trachoma is, but only he who has seen a trachoma district knows what trachoma means.

## The clinical description

His description is the fullest in either of the two books: "De oogleden vreeselijk ontstoken en verdikt, naar binnen omgebogen, bewegen de oogharen als een borstel over het oog. De haren vallen uit en de jonge sterke haartjes schuren als scherpe naaldjes de cornea kapot en vernietigen het laatste wat er na den haaruitval van het gezichtsvermogen overbleef. Na jaren plagerij, onduldbare pijnen, eindeloos getob, loopt tenslotte de ziekte uit op volslagen blindheid."

## The predominance of women

The patients were countless but preponderantly women. "Ik vond er, die de zieke oogleden met een touwtje opgebonden tegen het voorhoofd omgebogen droegen. Ik moest bij vrouwen de operatie zevenmaal meer verrichten dan bij mannen, hetgeen zeer merkwaardig is, omdat wij uit de litteratuur niet wisten, dat trachoom zich bij het vrouwelijk geslacht in grooter frequentie voordoet." — I found there women who wore the diseased eyelids bent back against the forehead, tied up with a piece of string. I had to perform the operation on women seven times more often than on men, which is very remarkable, because from the literature we did not know that trachoma occurs with greater frequency in the female sex.

Tijssen offered an environmental explanation, arrived at only after he had seen the life of those women in their houses: in the Batak dwellings a thick stinking smoke met the visitor, with no air and no smoke-outlet; there the food was prepared; wood for burning was scarce and a kind of fern was burnt that gave enormous smoke; "en overal vindt men er de vrouwen van 40 jaar en ouder met halfblinde oogen bij het vuur," — and everywhere one finds there the women of 40 years and older with half-blind eyes beside the fire, while the men were on the *sawah's* and the younger women with them or caring for the children.

## The operation

The operation performed was palliative and not curative: "De trachoom is niet genezen. Wij hebben de oogleden korter gemaakt en naar boven omgebogen. De sarrende pijn die elken oogopslag tot een bezoeking maakte, is voorbij. De verwoesting der oogen is tot staan gekomen." — The trachoma is not cured. We have made the eyelids shorter and bent them upward. The nagging pain that made every glance a visitation is past. The destruction of the eyes has been brought to a stand. More than two hundred people were operated on in one month, some on all four eyelids. The operations were performed by the young Batak physician [[Gido Siregar]], who had done trachoma work as assistant to [[Prof. Bakker]] at [[Batavia]], with Tijssen assisting.

## Comparison with Atjeh

The contrast between the trachoma country of [[Samosir]] and the cataract country of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] is one of the organising oppositions of Tijssen's account. In Atjeh he found cataract in numbers sufficient to operate on eighty-three persons in twenty-six days on the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|North Coast]]; on Samosir, among nearly a hundred thousand people, not a single cataract among the candidates presented. He noted also that on the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast of Atjeh]] he had looked for differences from the eye diseases of the North Coast and found little or none.

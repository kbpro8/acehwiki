**Rajat** (Malay-Arabic *ra'yat*, "subjects, the common people") was the term for the ordinary subjects of an Atjehnese *[[Landschap (Atjehnese)|landschap]]*, as distinguished from the chiefs and the great landholders.

## Indifference of the chief to the origin of the rajat

[[D. W. N. de Boer]] used the term in describing the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]*'s attitude to settlement: it mattered little to this powerful and genealogically independent chief "welke *rajat*, van welke *[[Kawom|kawom]]* of *[[Kawom|soekëë]]* ook, en waarvandaan" — which subject, of whatever clan or sub-tribe and from wherever — took waste land into use in his territory, provided it was not done in secret. The remark encapsulates the territorial rather than genealogical basis of the Atjehnese polity.

## Emancipation of the rajat

De Boer also recorded the *rajat* as the beneficiary of the changes that were causing *[[Mawaih|mawaih]]* to decline: an increasingly effective protection of the small man against the great landholders; the countering of usury through the credit system and the *[[Woekerordonnantie|Woekerordonnantie]]*; a growing mutual market traffic; and "de emancipatie van den vroeger zoo afhankelijken *rajat*, die nu zijn eigen recht van bestaan leert begrijpen en dat ook leert verdedigen" — the emancipation of the formerly so dependent *rajat*, who was now learning to understand his own right of existence and also to defend it, and who was gradually freeing himself from a more or less disguised slavery or serfdom.

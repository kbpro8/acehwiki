**The Gajoesche Centraalgebergte** — the *Gayo Central Mountains* — is the name given by Dr. W. Volz to the portion of the eastern Barisan coulisse that runs from the *Abong-Abong* (66) — the northernmost peak of the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Wilhelmina gebergte]] — eastward across the northern edge of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] to the *G. Loser* (70) and the headwaters of the [[Lao Alas|Woi ni Agoesein]]. In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), the Gajoesche Centraalgebergte is the orographic bridge between the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Wilhelmina gebergte]] and the *G. Loser* — the range whose east-west orientation defines the northern limit of the Alas valley and the southern limit of the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] basin.

## Orography

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I describes the Gajoesche Centraalgebergte as the east-west reorientation of the eastern Barisan coulisse at the *Abong-Abong* (66): "Zooals boven reeds werd gezegd buigt hier het geheele systeem der beide Westelijke Barisan coulissen zich van het Noordeinde van het Koningin Wilhelmina gebergte plotseling van zijne oorspronkelijke Z.O.-N.W. richting om in eene zuiver Oost-Westelijke. Dit deel van de Oostelijke coulisse, dat op den Noordelijken Trip-oever reikt tot den Geureudong I (71) is door Dr. W. Volz het Gajosche Centraalgebergte genoemd."

The Gajoesche Centraalgebergte thus comprises the east-west portion of the eastern Barisan coulisse from the *Abong-Abong* (66) eastward to the *Geureudong I* (71), and includes the *G. Loser* (70) at 3,520 m — the highest volcano of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, and the source of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas (Woi ni Agoesein)]].

## Significance

The Gajoesche Centraalgebergte is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the orographic bridge between the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Wilhelmina gebergte]] and the *G. Loser*, and the range whose east-west orientation defines the northern limit of the Alas valley. The Bureau's emphasis on the range's naming by Dr. Volz — and on its east-west reorientation of the Barisan system — frames the Gajoesche Centraalgebergte as the geographical foundation of the Gayo–Alas demographic frontier: the range whose southern slopes drain to the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] and the Alas valley, and whose northern slopes drain to the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] and the Gayo hoogvlakte.

The range's name — *Gajosche Centraalgebergte* — is the Bureau's principal evidence of the colonial geographical recognition of the Gayo people as the indigenous population of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, and of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte* as a demographic and orographic unit distinct from the Wilhelmina gebergte to the south and the Atjeh *kustgebergte* to the north.

The **Atjeh weefgetouw** — the Atjeh loom — was the hand-loom of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], recorded as catalogue item 62 in the *Indonesische Kunstnijverheid* under the heading "Weefgetouw. Atjeh, Sumatra." The catalogue entry, which gives the description in four languages, documents the existence of a distinctive Atjeh loom type alongside the looms of other Sumatran weaving centres (such as Bengkoclen and Kota Gédang on Sumatra's Westkust).

## The catalogue entry

The *Indonesische Kunstnijverheid* catalogue records the Atjeh loom as:

- **Item 62**: "Weefgetouw. Atjeh, Sumatra." — Dutch.
- English: "Weaving-loom. Atjeh, Sumatra."
- French: "Métier à tisser. Atcheh. Sumatra."
- German: "Webstuhl. Atjeh. Sumatra."

The entry is brief — it does not describe the construction of the loom in detail — but the separate listing of the Atjeh loom alongside the looms of Bengkoclen (Item 63) and Kota Gédang (Item 67, a "Weefgetouw met voetbeweging" / loom with pedal driving) indicates that the Atjeh loom was considered a distinct type, worth cataloguing on its own.

## Atjeh weaving in context

The catalogue groups the Atjeh loom with a series of Sumatran weaving traditions: alongside the Atjeh loom (Item 62) are listed an antique cotton tissue from Bengkoclen (Item 63), a *ragidoep* (a Toba Batak cotton ikat, Item 64), and the Atjeh silk ikat tissues (Items 65–66, see [[Atjeh silk ikat weaving]]). Item 67 is a loom with pedal driving from Kota Gédang on Sumatra's Westkust. The implication is that Atjeh was one node in a wider Sumatran weaving landscape — but one with its own distinctive loom type.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the Atjeh *weefgetouw* is the material instrument of the sultanate's textile craft — the loom on which the silk ikat cloth of [[Atjeh silk ikat weaving|catalogue items 65–66]] would have been woven. The catalogue's separate listing of the Atjeh loom is itself a fact of craft-history: it indicates that, at the moment of European colonial cataloguing, the Atjeh loom was recognisably distinct from the looms of neighbouring Sumatran weaving centres. The brevity of the entry is itself a measure of how little detailed ethnographic description the catalogue provides for this item — but the inclusion is enough to document the existence of the type.

**depeq-winden / depeq-vischjes** (English: "depeq-winds and depeq-fish") is a term or concept described in [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]]'s *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu* in relation to [[Aceh]]. At Takengon, small fish (like ikan teri) caught only for a few weeks during the west monsoon when a powerful wind called the 'depeq-wind' drives them in large schools through the lake.

## Translation

depeq-winds and depeq-fish

## Description

At [[Takengon]], small fish (like ikan teri) caught only for a few weeks during the west monsoon when a powerful wind called the 'depeq-wind' drives them in large schools through the lake. Subject of several legends; one says they are the transformed rice grains of a wedding feast, thrown into the lake by guests after the bride drowned herself in despair at being married to her own brother.

## Significance

The term *depeq-winden / depeq-vischjes* is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the cultural, administrative, or institutional vocabulary of [[Aceh]].

**Gampong** (from Malay *kampong*) was the fundamental social, political, and religious unit of [[Great Acheh|Acehnese]] society — the village. All Acehnese social life was organised around the gampong, which combined the functions of a residential community, a political unit with its own leadership, and a religious congregation.

## Leadership

Each gampong had two principal leaders:

**The Keuchi'** (*keuëchi'*) — the village headman (*keuchik*), responsible primarily for maintaining the [[Adat|customary law]] (*adat*) and the worldly affairs of the gampong, though promotion of godly living was also regarded as part of his duty.

**The Teungku Meunasah** — the "mother of the gampong," responsible for upholding the [[Hikum|Islamic law]] (*hukom*). The title of [[Teungku|teungku]] was applied both to the leubë (faithful observer of religious obligations), the haji (one who has performed the pilgrimage to [[Mecca]]), the malém (one with some knowledge of holy books), the além (one who has brought his studies to perfection), the ulama (authority on religious law), and the sayyid (descendant of Mohammed).

## The Meunasah

The physical heart of every gampong was the **[[Meunasah]]** — the village chapel, serving simultaneously as a sleeping-place for men, a rest-house for strangers, a house of prayer, and a place of assembly for village affairs. Village festivals were held there, marriage contracts concluded, and affairs of the gampong debated. A person of rank arriving unexpectedly in a gampong would go first to the meunasah and from there announce his arrival.

## Communal Character

Every gampong constituted a recognised social entity with collective rights and responsibilities. All members of a [[Kawom|kawom]] (clan) were jointly and severally liable as concerns *bila* (blood money), so that a blood-feud might keep two clans in a state of mutual hostility for years. The [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] was responsible for the external affairs of his territory — a collection of gampongs — while internal gampong affairs were managed by the keuchi' and teungku.

## Prominent Gampongs

The most important gampongs in the vicinity of the capital [[Banda Acheh]] included: [[Gampong Jawa]], Pande, Pennayong, [[Lam Bhu']], [[Lueng Bata]], Lam Seupeuëng, Ateuëng, Batoh, and [[Meura'sa]]. Their inhabitants were distinguished by the epithet *banda* ("town-bred"). All others who spoke their own local dialects were called *dusön* (countrified).

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Gampong

**The gampong** was the smallest territorial and administrative unit of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and the foundation on which the entire superstructure of Acehnese native government rested. *De Buitenbezittingen* calls it "de kleinste territoriale eenheid" and follows Prof. Dr. [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s account in *De Atjehers*, Vol. I (pp. 61 e.v.), in treating the gampong as the "hoeksteen" — cornerstone — of the *Inlandsch Bestuur* in Atjeh. Its three-headed administration — the [[Keutjhï|Keutjhï]] (with one or more *waki's*), the [[Teungkoe|Teungkoe]], and the [[Oereuëng toeha|Oereuëng toeha]] — embodied the dual principle of *adat* and *hoeköm* that ran through all Acehnese political life.

## Origins

Drawing on [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], the handbook argues that the gampong grew out of an older genealogical settlement — the *kawöm* (clan) — so that "oudtijds eene gampong eene kawöm of een onderdeel daarvan bevatte" (originally a gampong contained a single kawöm or a portion thereof). With the rise of the [[Oelèëbalang|oelèëbalangs]] as territorial lords, the kinship-based *panglima kawöm* gave way to a village headman who "zag het meeste van de oude adat kawöm… getransformeerd tot adat gampong" — saw most of the old clan custom transformed into village adat. The transformation produced an unusually "hechte sterke gemeeneenheid" — close and strong community — whose cohesion underwrote the whole Atjehsche system.

## Three-headed administration

The gampong was governed by three elements: the [[Keutjhï|Keutjhï]] with one or more *waki's* (deputies); the [[Teungkoe|Teungkoe]] (also called *Teungkoe meunasah*, from the village prayer-house or *meunasah*); and the [[Oereuëng toeha|Oereuëng toeha]] (literally "old men," also *oereuëng thjiʼ* or *oereuëng pata*), the elders who were consulted on every important matter. The Keutjhï was the "vader der gampong"; the Teungkoe its "moeder"; the two offices balancing *adat* and *hoeköm* in a single communal order. The *meunasah* itself served not only as a prayer-house but as a men's dormitory for unmarried youths, transient visitors, and husbands whose wives lived elsewhere — the same institution, the source notes, as the *langgar*, *bale*, or *tadjoeq* of Java.

## Significance

Although the Keutjhï lacked any formal judicial authority — "geenerlei rechtsmacht," notes the source, for all serious cases had to be sent up to the [[Oelèëbalang|oelèëbalang]] — his role as intermediary, mediator, and representative of his community's interests was indispensable. The handbook concludes that the gampong and its administration "den hoeksteen vormen van het Inlandsch Bestuur in Atjeh" and "moet blijven vormen" — must continue to form — the cornerstone of native government in Atjeh.

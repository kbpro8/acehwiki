**Gampong** (from Malay *kampong*) was the fundamental social, political, and religious unit of [[Great Acheh|Acehnese]] society — the village. All Acehnese social life was organised around the gampong, which combined the functions of a residential community, a political unit with its own leadership, and a religious congregation.

## Leadership

Each gampong had two principal leaders:

**The Keuchi'** (*keuëchi'*) — the village headman (*keuchik*), responsible primarily for maintaining the [[Adat|customary law]] (*adat*) and the worldly affairs of the gampong, though promotion of godly living was also regarded as part of his duty.

**The Teungku Meunasah** — the "mother of the gampong," responsible for upholding the [[Hikum|Islamic law]] (*hukom*). The title of [[Teungku|teungku]] was applied both to the leubë (faithful observer of religious obligations), the haji (one who has performed the pilgrimage to [[Mecca]]), the malém (one with some knowledge of holy books), the além (one who has brought his studies to perfection), the ulama (authority on religious law), and the sayyid (descendant of Mohammed).

## The Meunasah

The physical heart of every gampong was the **[[Meunasah]]** — the village chapel, serving simultaneously as a sleeping-place for men, a rest-house for strangers, a house of prayer, and a place of assembly for village affairs. Village festivals were held there, marriage contracts concluded, and affairs of the gampong debated. A person of rank arriving unexpectedly in a gampong would go first to the meunasah and from there announce his arrival.

## Communal Character

Every gampong constituted a recognised social entity with collective rights and responsibilities. All members of a [[Kawom|kawom]] (clan) were jointly and severally liable as concerns *bila* (blood money), so that a blood-feud might keep two clans in a state of mutual hostility for years. The [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] was responsible for the external affairs of his territory — a collection of gampongs — while internal gampong affairs were managed by the keuchi' and teungku.

## Prominent Gampongs

The most important gampongs in the vicinity of the capital [[Banda Acheh]] included: [[Gampong Jawa]], Pande, Pennayong, [[Lam Bhu']], [[Lueng Bata]], Lam Seupeuëng, Ateuëng, Batoh, and [[Meura'sa]]. Their inhabitants were distinguished by the epithet *banda* ("town-bred"). All others who spoke their own local dialects were called *dusön* (countrified).

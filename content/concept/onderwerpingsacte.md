**The Onderwerpingsacte** — *act of submission* — was the formal written instrument, instituted by [[Jan van Swieten|General van Swieten]]'s proclamation to the chiefs of [[January 1874|January]] [[1874]], by which an Atjehese chief (*radja*) acknowledged Dutch supremacy, undertook to fly the Dutch flag, and promised to enter into no political relations with other powers. The onderwerpingsacte was the formal legal instrument of the Dutch policy of *[[Vlaghijscherij|vlaghijscherij]]* (flag-raising); its signing by a chief was the formal act of his submission, and was rewarded by the Dutch with self-government, freedom to import and export under pass, and sometimes substantial gifts of money.

## The Form

The onderwerpingsacte, as instituted by Van Swieten's proclamation of [[January 1874|January]] [[1874]], required the signing chief to acknowledge Dutch supremacy, to undertake to fly the Dutch flag, and to promise to enter into no political relations with other powers. New articles in the onderwerpingsacte, introduced after the [[Simpang Olim expedition (November 1876)|Simpang Olim expedition]] of [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]], stipulated, among other things, that the Sultan's share of the revenues would henceforth belong to the Dutch. This was later also applied to the other statelets.

## The Granting

To those chiefs who signed the onderwerpingsacte, self-government was left and they were permitted to import and export under pass. Some commercial facilities were granted to those who would have submitted but for special reasons dared not yet openly declare themselves, or where the Dutch deemed it desirable not yet to issue the flag. The statelets whose chiefs refused to submit would be blockaded.

The granting of the onderwerpingsacte was, on the [[Noordkust|North]] and [[Westkust|West Coasts]], often done "*eenvoudig door een boodschapper aan land gezonden, zonder meer*" (usually simply sent ashore by a messenger, and nothing more), without personal contact with the chief, without supervision, and often in concert with the enemy. The *radja* of [[Giegieng|Giegieng]] received for his onderwerpingsacte a *douceurtje* of ƒ25,000. The whole submission was, Kruijt writes, "*een wassen neus*" (a wax nose — a fake), a means to import and export freely.

## The Later Revisions

The later Dutch governors sought to make the onderwerpingsacte more meaningful by requiring personal contact with the chief and his being sworn in. The commander in Atjeh later determined that in future first personal contact must be obtained and the chief must be sworn in. But by then it was too late. The pattern of paper submission without supervision had been established.

## Significance

The onderwerpingsacte is the formal legal instrument of the Dutch policy of *[[Vlaghijscherij|vlaghijscherij]]* (flag-raising). Its signing by an Atjehese chief was the formal act of his submission, and was rewarded by the Dutch with self-government, commercial facilities, and sometimes substantial gifts. The instrument's failure to ensure actual control — particularly on the [[Noordkust|North]] and [[Westkust|West Coasts]], where it was often obtained without supervision and in concert with the enemy — was, in Kruijt's analysis, one of the principal Dutch strategic errors of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The contrast between the onderwerpingsacte as a formal instrument and the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] as an effective instrument of control is one of the recurrent themes of Kruijt's brochure.

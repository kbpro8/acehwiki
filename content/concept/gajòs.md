**Gajòs** is a subject named in *[[Generaal Swart, Pacificator van Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Tgk. di Barat]]

In [[1911]] his presence made itself felt again. In January [[1911]] a great meeting of the resistance party had taken place at [[Seureulo]] in the [[Meergebied]] — the present sub-division [[Takèngön]] — at the place where Tgk. di Barat was hiding, attended also by bands of [[Gajòs]]. But the meeting was unluckily discovered or betrayed, and driven apart by patrols; the Teungkoe's father-in-law was killed. When in the following month an encounter with the *[[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|marechaussée]]* likewise ended unhappily, Tgk. di Barat left the Meergebied and returned to the plain of [[Lho' Soekon]] and [[Lho' Seumawè]].

### From [[Penghoeloe Koeala]]

**Penghoeloe Koeala** was een zekeren Penghoeloe Koeala, een [[Gajòs|Gajò]] uit [[Leuboe Sigeunab]], die ook alweer stood in an odour of sanctity, who set on foot the religious movement at [[Serbödjadi]] in December [[1917]].

### From [[Serbödjadi]]

The movement, set on foot by [[Penghoeloe Koeala]], a [[Gajòs|Gajò]] of [[Leuboe Sigeunab]], cost the *gezaghebber* of [[Lököp]] his life in an encounter at the *[[gampong]]* [[Semboeang]] and ended with four dead, among them the guardian of a holy grave, and the Penghoeloe's capture.

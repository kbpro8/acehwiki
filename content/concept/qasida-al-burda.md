**Qasida al‑Burda** is a 13th‑century Arabic poem composed by [[al-Busiri]] that extols the virtues of the Prophet [[Muhammad]]; it became a central text of devotional literature in the Sultanate of [[Acheh]] after being translated into Malay in the mid‑16th century.

## Composition and Structure
The poem contains 160 verses divided into eight sections, each employing rich metaphorical language. It famously describes the Prophet as “a flower so tender, as the full moon so great, as the sea so lofty”.

## Themes
Key themes include the Prophet’s spiritual perfection, the miraculous nature of the Qurʾān, and the defeat of *kāfir* (non‑believers) and *jinn* (supernatural beings). The poem’s imagery of “pearls in the shell of a speaking mouth” reflects Qurʾānic reverence.

## Transmission to Aceh
Through trade and scholarly exchange, the *Burda* entered Aceh by the 1500s. Local scribes produced a Malay rendition that preserved the poem’s theological motifs while adapting its language to the Acehnese context.

## Legacy in Acehnese Culture
The Malay *Burda* was recited in *majlis* (gatherings) and incorporated into *Acehnese religious poetry*. Its verses were used to inspire resistance against colonial incursions and to affirm the sultanate’s Islamic identity.

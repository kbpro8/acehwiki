**The Eresabel** — the honorary saber — was a Dutch colonial military decoration awarded for distinguished combat leadership, particularly to officers of the [[K.N.I.L.]] who had shown *Moed, Beleid en Trouw* in the field. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the Eresabel is the companion decoration to the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]]: where the M.W.O. was the decoration of the rank-and-file soldaat and the onderofficier, the Eresabel was the decoration of the officer who had led them. The book's named Eresabel recipient is [[Schmidt]], who "kreeg dan ook voor [[1910]] de Eresabel en voor [[1911]] de M.W.O. 3e klas" — the Eresabel for the first year of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] and the M.W.O. 3e klas for the closing year.

## The decoration and its scope

The Eresabel was an honorary saber — a sword of honour — awarded to officers for distinguished combat leadership. The decoration was less common than the M.W.O. and was typically awarded to officers who had already earned a previous decoration (Schmidt, for example, had already earned the *[[Oranje-Nassau (Orde van Oranje Nassau)|Oranje-Nassau]]* and a first M.W.O. before receiving the Eresabel for [[1910]]). The pairing of the Eresabel (for the officer) with the M.W.O. (for the soldaat) is the book's quiet marker of the colonial army's recognition economy: the same act of *Moed, Beleid en Trouw* that earned the soldaat an M.W.O. earned the officer an Eresabel.

## Schmidt's Eresabel

"De luitenant [[Schmidt]] had zijn sporen op Atjeh reeds behoorlijk verdiend. Eerst had hij de *[[Oranje-Nassau (Orde van Oranje Nassau)|Oranje-Nassau]]* gekregen en later de M.W.O., terwijl hij wel zowat de beste kenner was van de taal en het volk." For the first year of the colonne Schmidt — the year of the hunt for *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]* and the overval on the schuilplaats — Schmidt received the Eresabel. For the second year — the year of the killing of *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* and the destruction of the *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]]* — he received the M.W.O. 3e klas. The two decorations together recognised the two successive years of the campaign.

## Account from *ATJEH '96* (Kruisheer, 1913) — the 1896 Eresabel awards

In *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]*, the Eresabel appears among the military honours gazetted in the *Dag-orders voor het Indisch Leger* of [[1897]] (Bijlage XX of the Tweede Deel) for distinguished combat leadership during the [[1896]] campaign in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Kruisheer records that the Eresabel — *met het gebruikelijke opschrift* (with the customary inscription) — was awarded to **Luitenant-Kolonel der Artillerie W. Boetje** (Lieutenant-Colonel of the Horse Artillery, W. Boetje) and to a **kapitein der infanterie** (Captain of the Infantry) for their conduct in the operations of the first semester of [[1896]], particularly the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive against the IV and VI Moekims of 23–25 May 1896]]. The 1896 Eresabel awards, made by Gouvernementsbesluit of [[9 July]] [[1897]] No. 18, were part of a wider batch of decorations — including the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] ridder derde klas for [[J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Kol. Van Heutsz]] for the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] — that recognised the Dutch field-officer corps of the year of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]]. The Boetje award is the earliest explicit Eresabel citation in the Kruisheer corpus and confirms that the decoration, like the M.W.O., was being awarded for combat leadership in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] throughout the [[1890s]], not only in the closing campaigns of [[1910]]–[[1911]].

## Significance

The Eresabel is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the companion decoration to the M.W.O., and as the recognition of the officer's combat leadership in the colonne method of counter-insurgency. The book's named Eresabel recipient — [[Schmidt]] — is the book's portrait of the officer who actually finished the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] at the local level, and the Eresabel is the decoration that recognised that work. In Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* the Eresabel appears as the established decoration of the Dutch field-officer corps of [[1896]], awarded alongside the M.W.O. for the conduct of the operations of that year.

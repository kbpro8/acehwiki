**Hareubab** (also spelled *hareubab*, *harubab*; Dutch: *gestreken spiesluit*) is the **[[Achehnese]]** name for a **bowed spike lute** (*spiesluit* / *spike fiddle*), a chordophone with a narrow neck that pierces the resonator body. It is the local Acehnese variant of the widespread **[[Rebab]]** family found across the Islamic world and the Malay-Indonesian archipelago. In the **[[Batak Lands]]** it is known as **[[Arbah]]**, and in **[[Nias]]** as **[[Lagija]]**.

## Organology

- **Hornbostel-Sachs classification**: 321.311 (spike bowl lute, bowed)
- **Resonator**: typically a small, hollowed wooden body (often coconut shell or carved wood), covered with a skin membrane (bladder, goat, or monitor lizard skin)
- **Neck**: a slender wooden spike that passes through the resonator
- **Strings**: traditionally two or three strings (historically horsehair, later metal)
- **Bow**: arched wooden bow strung with horsehair, rosined
- **Playing position**: held vertically on the lap or against the chest, bowed horizontally

## Historical and Regional Context

The *hareubab* represents the **Southeast Asian penetration of the Persian-Arabic *rebab*** tradition, which spread with Islam across the Indian Ocean world. In his [[1946]] monograph *[[Muziek en Dans in de Buitengewesten]]*, **[[Jaap Kunst]]** notes (p. 9) that the *hareubab* occurs "also more northerly, namely in Atjeh" — placing it at the northern tip of Sumatra as part of the Islamic instrumental complex that includes the **[[Gambus (Aceh)|gambus]]**, **[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]**, **[[Marwas]]**, **[[Serunai (Aceh)|serunai]]**, and **[[Bangei]]**.

Kunst explicitly links the Acehnese *hareubab* to the **[[Batak]]** *arbah* and the **[[Nias]]** *lagija*, demonstrating a shared organological lineage across northern Sumatra. The *rebab* family entered the archipelago via **[[Persian]]** and **[[Arab]]** traders and missionaries from the [[13th century]] onward, adapting to local aesthetic and organological preferences.

## Role in Acehnese Music

In traditional **[[Acheh]]** ensembles, the *hareubab* functions as a **melodic lead instrument**, often paired with the *serunai* (oboe) in processional and ceremonial music, and with the *gambus* in more intimate *[[Hikayat]]* (epic narrative) or *[[Dendang]]* (lyric song) accompaniment. Its penetrating, vocal-like timbre makes it suitable for outdoor processions (*[[Pranaji]]*) and for the *[[Saman]]* dance accompaniment in some regional variants.

The instrument is also associated with **[[Meuseukat]]** (women's seated dance-song) and **[[Seudati]]** contexts, where it may double the vocal line or provide instrumental interludes.

## Account from *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*

Only indigenous Acehnese string instrument. Bowl-shaped soundbox of nangka-wood hollowed out, covered with buffalo/cow stomach. Bow made of bent [[Rattan (Aceh)|rotan]]/bamboo. Orchestra with two or more nangka-wood drums accompanies recitation of pantoens.

*Source: J. Jongejans, Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu (Baarn: Hollandia Drukkerij N.V., c. 1938), pp. 151.*

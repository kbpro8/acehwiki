***Tong-tong*** was the hollowed tree-trunk used as a signalling instrument in the Dutch *[[Benting|bentings]]* of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], struck with a hard piece of wood to sound signals. [[P. H. R. Beuming]] glosses it as *een uitgeholde boomstam, waartegen met een hard stuk hout signalen worden geslagen*, and adds that on some *bentings* pieces of railway rail were used for the same purpose.

## Use at Edi

Beuming's illustration comes from the *benting* [[Edi]] in [[1896]], on the occasion of the visit of [[Pastoor Verbraak]]. About one o'clock in the afternoon the sentry on the northern bastion gave on the *tong-tong* the signal *Patrouille in 't gezicht* — patrol in sight — announcing the return of the escort of thirty bayonets that had gone to fetch the chaplain from the landing place. From the *[[Banket|banquette]]* the garrison could then see the little band approaching in the distance, the Europeans distinguishable from the natives by their white gaiters standing out sharply against the dark uniforms.

**Sembahjang** — Atjehnese/Dutch spelling of Arabic *ṣalāt* (صلاة), the obligatory ritual prayer of Islam — is the daily five-fold prayer that, with the Friday congregational prayer and the funeral prayer, forms the principal liturgical obligation of every adult Muslim. The term is recorded in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) in his account of the ritual obligations of Indonesian Islam.

## Kernkamp's account

Kernkamp introduces the *sembahjang* in the course of his discussion of the daily prayer cycle and its social setting in the Indonesian mosque (*[[Mesigit|mesigit]]*). His wording: "Although the daily 5 *saláts* (*sembahjangs*) — ritual religious exercises consisting of minutely regulated postures and movements and further of the reciting of certain formulas (wrongly often confused with a prayer) — are prescribed [for the Muslim], he may perform them at the prescribed times wherever he happens to be, even if it is meritorious to do so communally. In the Indies, only the personnel attached to the mosque, with a few neighbours, usually take part in the daily exercises, under the leadership of a foreman (*imam*). Only a Friday-*salát*, which is followed by a sermon, should, according to the Law, at least the men actually attend. In the Indies, however, as indeed elsewhere, that obligation is generally grossly neglected. Many lead a relatively pious Muslim life without otherwise coming to the mosque except on exceptional occasions. It is sufficient, but also a requirement, that at least 40 Muslims take part in the communal *saláts*."

The *sembahjang* is to be distinguished from the *[[Sembah|sembah]]* (the reverential gesture of greeting, in which the palms are pressed together with the fingers pointed upward); the two terms are etymologically related — *sembahyang* is, in folk etymology, *sembah hyang*, "the *sembah* to the *hyang*" (the deity) — but the *sembahjang* as ritual prayer is a specifically Islamic institution, while the *sembah* as gesture is an older Indonesian courtly form.

## Atjeh practice

Kernkamp's account of the *sembahjang* in Atjeh is brief: the [[Meunasah]] serves as the venue for the daily communal *sembahjang* in the Atjeh village, while the [[Mesigit|mesigit]] (the Friday mosque) serves for the Friday *salát* with its sermon. He further notes that, in Atjeh, the [[Friday prohibitions|Friday]] takes on "more or less the character of an obligatory day of rest (which the Islam does not require): most work is then forbidden as unhealth-bringing, while, on the other hand, all sorts of religious recitation ... are considered recommended."

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *sembahjang* is significant as the central ritual obligation of Atjeh Islam, and as the activity around which the institutional structure of the [[Meunasah]] and the [[Mesigit|mesigit]] is organised. The Friday congregational *sembahjang* with its sermon is the principal public liturgical event of the Atjeh Muslim week; the daily five-fold *sembahjang* is the principal private devotional discipline.

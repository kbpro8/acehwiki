**Bekas** — the Malay/Acehnese word for footprint, spoor or track — was the technical term used by the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] for the spoor (track) of a person or animal in the *[[rimboe]]* (jungle). The *bekas* — read by the *spoorzoeker* (tracker) at the front of the patrouille — was the principal source of intelligence in the colonne method of counter-insurgency: the *bekas* of the fourageurs led to the bende, the *bekas* of the bende led to the schuilplaats, and the *bekas* of the colonne itself — marked by the brigade number cut in trees — could be read years later by a passing patrol.

## The spoorzoeker and the bekas

The *spoorzoeker* (tracker) was the soldier at the front of the patrouille whose task was to read the *bekas*. The book's most fully drawn *spoorzoeker* is [[Nussy]], who with [[Kamagi]] and [[Paliaman]] "lieten zich niet misleiden" by the listige verberging of sporen. The *spoorzoekers* of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] found the first *bekas* of *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]*'s fourageurs near the [[Kroeëng Seukoeleh]] — "vonden de spoorzoekers het *bekas* van neushoornjagers" (rhino-hunters' footprints) mixed in with the bende's own sporen, a sign of how remote the streek was.

## Warme bekas

"Warme bekas" — fresh footprints — was the spoorzoeker's most urgent find. "Warme bekas" made "de adem stokt… en het hart slaat even een tikje sneller" — the breath catches and the heart beats a little faster. The *warme bekas* was the sign that the enemy was close: the patrouille would tighten, the *[[dardanellen|dardanellen]]* would spread out, and the engagement would follow.

## The bekas of the colonne

The *bekas* of the colonne itself — marked by the brigade number cut in trees — could be read years later by a passing patrol. At [[Leuhong]], a soldier had written on [[22 August]] [[1912]] a note about the 1918 opheffing of that *detailchement* — still legible in [[1934]], twenty-two years later, on *bekas* Marechaussee. The *bekas* of the colonne was therefore the colonial army's own historical record: the marked trees and the soldier's notes were the *bekas* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]'s presence in the *[[rimboe]]*.

## The bekas and the omsingeling

The *bekas* of the bende led to the *omsingeling* — the surrounding of a ladang or a schuilplaats. The colonne Schmidt's *omsingeling* of *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]*'s ladang — "*de Amb. sergeant 2e kl.* [[Van Drongelen]] *maakte met zes man een omtrekking en zette de rand af*" — was the consequence of reading the *bekas*. The *omsingeling* was the colonne method's tactical payoff: the *bekas* read, the ladang surrounded, the bende engaged.

## Significance

The *bekas* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the principal source of intelligence in the colonne method of counter-insurgency. The book's argument is that the colonne method — small, highly trained patrouilles operating with local intelligence against a religiously motivated resistance — depended on the *spoorzoeker*'s reading of the *bekas*. The *bekas* of the colonne itself — marked by the brigade number cut in trees — is also the book's quiet marker of the colonial army's own historical record: the marked trees and the soldier's notes were the *bekas* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]'s presence in the *[[rimboe]]*, readable years later.

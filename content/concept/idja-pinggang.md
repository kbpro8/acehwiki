**Idja pinggang** — Atjehnese *ija pinggang*, "hip-cloth" or "loin-cloth", from *ija* "cloth" + *pinggang* "hip, waist" — is the sartorial *sjibbolet* (shibboleth) of Atjeh Islam for both sexes, named in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]). Kernkamp introduces it in the course of his discussion of Islamic clothing markers: "Certain garments were indeed positively considered as marks of Muslim dress. In Atjeh the great piece in the crotch of the wide trousers, nowadays almost only worn by women, served as such. Very special in Atjeh was the hip-cloth (*idja pinggang*) for both sexes the *sjibbolet* of Islam, a conception that is strongly wearing away."

## The clothing marker

Kernkamp's account places the *idja pinggang* alongside the Javanese *koepiah* (skullcap) and the Arab-style turban as one of the positive sartorial markers of Muslim identity in the Indies. What distinguishes the Atjeh case is the marker's application to both sexes (rather than only to men) and its specifically Atjehnese form: a hip-cloth wound round the waist over the wide trousers, serving both as a practical garment and as a visible sign of the wearer's Muslim identity. The same section notes that, by [[1946]], the marker was "strongly wearing away" (*sterk afslijt*) under the influence of European clothing, which "the younger generation in particular increasingly takes on" — though Kernkamp notes that "a certain reluctance against wearing a tie and especially against wearing a hat or tropical helmet" persisted among Atjehnese men.

The *idja pinggang* is to be distinguished from the generic Atjeh *[[Idja|idja]]* (cloth) — which is the subject of a separate Atjeh concept entry — by the specific addition of *pinggang* (hip), which marks the hip-cloth as a particular sartorial form rather than as cloth in general. The [[Koepiah der Atjehers|Atjeh *koepiah*]] (skullcap) is the complementary male head-covering, also named in the same chapter of *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]*.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *idja pinggang* is significant as the most distinctive Atjehnese sartorial marker of Islamic identity. Kernkamp's note that the marker was "strongly wearing away" by [[1946]] — that is, on the eve of Indonesian independence — captures a specific moment in the long Atjehnese transition from the late-nineteenth-century dress code (in which the *idja pinggang* was a positive mark of Muslim identity) to the late-colonial and post-colonial dress code (in which European clothing was increasingly adopted). The same transitional moment is recorded in Kernkamp's note that Atjehnese men "sometimes" wore a simple head-cloth, "but much more commonly a sort of cap (*koepiah*)"; only "a few prominent Teungkoe's ... wear a turban on Arab model."

**Sabandaar** (also *Sabandar*, from the Persian/Arabic *shāhbandar*, literally "king of the port") was the office of harbour-master at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and other Malay-Indonesian ports in the early modern period. The *Sabandaar* was the Sultan's representative at the port: he controlled the issue of the *tjap* or passport required for foreign ships to come ashore, supervised customs, and acted as the official intermediary between foreign merchants and the court. In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]] the *Sabandaar* of Atjeh appears together with the chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] as the Sultan's formal greeters of the Dutch legation at the [[Atjeh-rivier|river's mouth]].

## Etymology and office

The word *shāhbandar* is Persian — literally "king-lord (of the) port" — and the office it denotes is found across the Indian Ocean trading world, from the Persian Gulf to the East Indies. In the Malay-Indonesian world the *shahbandar* was the principal royal officer of the port, responsible for the regulation of foreign trade and for the collection of royal dues. At [[Acheh|Atjeh]] the office was clearly of high dignity: the *Sabandaar* acted together with the chief eunuch of the Sultana in the formal reception of foreign embassies.

## Role in the Soury embassy, 1641

When *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Pieter Soury]]'s embassy reached the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh river]] in [[1641]], the *Sabandaar* came out together with the chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] and the Dutch *Opperhoofd* of the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|lodge]], bringing the *tjap* or passport required to come ashore. The passport was the Sultan's permission to enter the port — without it, no foreign ship might lawfully send a boat to land. The presentation of the *tjap* by the *Sabandaar* in person was therefore a formal act of welcome, but also of control: the Sultan's permission was being granted, and the limits of that permission were being marked.

## Recording of gifts and jewels

At the appointed hour on the day of the formal audience (see [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)]]) the *Sabandaar* and the customs officials came "de medegebrachte geschenken opteekenen, alsmede de juweelen, die de Compagnie aan de Koningin wilde leveren" — to record the gifts that had been brought, and the jewels which the Company wished to deliver to the Queen. The *Sabandaar* was thus not only the harbour-master but the customs-officer of the port, and the formal record-keeper of the diplomatic exchange.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *Sabandaar* is the principal institutional hinge between the port and the court — the office through which all foreign trade and all foreign embassies had to pass. The presence of the *Sabandaar* at the river-mouth to greet [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s embassy in [[1641]] — together with the chief eunuch of the Sultana — is a measure of the office's dignity, and of the careful choreography by which the sultanate received foreign visitors. The office itself long outlived the [[1641]] embassy and would persist through the era of female rule and beyond.

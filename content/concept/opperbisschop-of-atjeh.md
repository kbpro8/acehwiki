The **Opperbisschop of Atjeh** — the "high bishop" or high priest of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] court, in [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s Dutch rendering — was the chief religious officer of the sultanate, whose house was the place to which the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury embassy of 1641]] was summoned the day after the formal audience of [[1 July]] [[1641]] for the "translation" of the Gouverneur-Generaal's *missive*. The office is a reminder of the religious-political structure of the Atjeh court, in which a high priest served alongside the chief eunuch and the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] as one of the principal officers of state.

## Role in the Soury embassy of 1641

After the formal audience of [[1 July]] [[1641]] (see [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)]]), the chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] came to convey her request that the legation come the following day to the house of the *Opperbisschop* (high priest) to translate the *missive* — although it had already been rendered into Malay. The unspoken purpose was to obtain clarification on points obscure to the Sultana and her Council: how [[Portugal]] in [[1640]] had been freed from Spain, was no longer the Republic's mortal enemy, and why the Republic had concluded a ten-year truce with Portugal the following year. The Portuguese nobleman [[Francisco de Souza de Castro|Francisco de Souza de Castro]] was at that time a prisoner in Atjeh.

The choice of the *Opperbisschop*'s house as the place for the "translation" audience is itself significant: the high priest's house was a neutral, religious space, distinct from both the Sultana's palace and the Dutch lodge. The choice of that space for the diplomatic clarification allowed the Sultana to extract the information she needed without compromising the ceremonial of the formal audience, and without inviting the Dutch legation back into the palace.

## The high priest as diplomat

The high priest's role in the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury embassy]] — hosting the "translation" audience at his house — is one of the small but telling details that show the integrated religious-political structure of the Atjeh court. The *Opperbisschop* was not merely a religious officer but a state official, whose house was an extension of the court's diplomatic apparatus. The same integration is visible elsewhere in Atjeh court ceremonial — the chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] acting as her diplomatic intermediary, the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] acting as her harbour-master and customs-officer, and so on. The Atjeh court was a structure in which religious, political, and commercial offices were not sharply distinguished but were integrated parts of a single state apparatus.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *Opperbisschop* is the principal religious officer of the sultanate's court — the high priest whose house served as the formal diplomatic venue for the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury embassy]]'s "translation" audience of [[1641]]. The office is a reminder of the integrated religious-political structure of the Atjeh court, in which a high priest served alongside the chief eunuch and the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] as one of the principal officers of state. The choice of the *Opperbisschop*'s house as the place for the diplomatic clarification was a small but meaningful act of court choreography — the use of religious space for diplomatic purpose.

**Verjaring** — prescription, or limitation of actions — did not occur in Atjehnese *[[Adatrecht|adatrecht]]*, and was introduced into [[Aceh|Atjeh]] by administrative act through the [[Besluit van den Civielen en Militairen Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden 26 April 1911 No. 2199|gubernatorial decision of 26 April 1911 No. 2199]].

## The gap in the adat

[[D. W. N. de Boer]] stated the position flatly: "Verjaring kwam in het Atjehsch adatrecht niet voor; doch werd dit instituut van bestuurswege ingevoerd" — prescription did not occur in Atjehnese customary law, but the institution was introduced by the administration.

## The inconsistency it was meant to cure

The decree's own recitals record the problem: the treatment of old claims of debt by the *[[Moesapat|moesapat]]*'s in the *gewest* (region) varied greatly, prescription being applied quite arbitrarily, sometimes yes and sometimes no; and in the interest of a good administration of justice it was desirable to bring unity into the matter.

## Justifications advanced

The decree grounded the innovation on three further considerations: that Islam too, under certain reservations, recognised prescription; that even before the Netherlands establishment, in many districts under the *[[Adat|adat]]*, no right was done upon claims that the plaintiff had without valid reason omitted to institute for fifteen years; and that both the Atjehnese chiefs and the Mohammedan *schriftgeleerden* (scholars) perceived the desirability and necessity of regulating the matter.

## The rule

All inheritance claims were to be prescribed after an uninterrupted lapse of fifteen or ten years, according as they were written (*beschreven*) or unwritten (*onbeschreven*).

## Significance

The introduction of *verjaring* was the last innovation recorded in De Boer's survey of Atjehnese land and property law, and stands as a compact example of the interaction of three legal orders in colonial Atjeh: customary *adat*, Islamic law, and Netherlands-Indies administrative regulation.

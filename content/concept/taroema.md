**Taroema** — also **Taruma**, in *bedekte taal* form **Taroe-na-ma** — is the ancestral land of the [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] of Sumatra's east coast that, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, underlies the chronicle tradition's "Java" and the European travellers' "Jaoa". Its location is [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]], and its sacred islet is [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] — the [[Noesa-n-ta-ra|Noesa-n-ta-ra]] of the [[Pararaton]] under the protection of the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Tara]]. The name is the principal *bedekte taal* form of the *bedekte taal* reading of Sumatran-Javanese historical geography.

## Etymology and the "name-tree"

The name *Taroema* [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *taroe-na-ma* — "the name-tree", i.e. the tree whose name is its own sign. In the Adiparwa's Churning of the Ocean of Milk — the *tasik soesoe* from which the gods drew the *amrta* — the churning-stick was the mountain Mandara; [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads the *Mandara* as a *bedekte taal* form for *man-dara*, "the like of Dara" (i.e. of the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Tara]]). The "tree" of Taroema is thus the *taroe* (Kawi "tree", "wood") whose name *na-ma* encodes the goddess herself — the patroness of [[Sumatra]] whose worship centred on the islet of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]].

## Decoding the European travel record

The same Taroema, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] argues, underlies the European travel record of the Sumatran east coast: the *Cernovem* of [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]] (~[[1430]]), the *Xarnauz* of the *Roteiro* of [[Vasco da Gama]] ("the king has 400 elephants of war; in the land is much benzoin and there is aloës won"), the *Sornau* of [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] (the name given to the Malay rajas under Siamese sovereignty). All three, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, are *bedekte taal* forms for [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]]/[[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]] — the Persian "New City" of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe|Sadjarah Malajoe's]] [[Awi Ditjoe]] episode.

## The *Baringin sonsang*

The *bedekte taal* sign of Taroema is the ***baringin sonsang*** — the inverted waringin tree, "whose roots stand in the air, while the leaves hang downwards". The South-Sumatran *Melasaké pampang|wonder-tree of Belalau]], the Minangkabau *baringin songrang*, and the Perak *beringin sonsang* are all, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, allegorical forms of the inverted Taroema — the ancestral land "turned upside down" by the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] and the dispersal of the Djawaka.

## The "tree" of Pigafetta

[[Pigafetta|Pigafetta]]'s account of the great *garuda* tree — "below [[Java|Java Major]] towards the gulf of China, in which live birds called garuda" — De Roo de la Faille decodes as a *bedekte taal* form for Taroema: the "tree" is *taroe maha* ("the great tree"), the garuda is the bird of [[Wisnu|god Wisnu]] (*ka-wis-noe-sa*, "king of the birds"), and the whole episode encodes the [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]-[[Aroe-baai|Aroe]] sacred complex that the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] overwrote.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Taroema is the principal *bedekte taal* form of the [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reading of Sumatran-Javanese historical geography — the ancestral land of the [[Djawaka]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] whose name encodes the goddess [[Cyama-Tara|Tara]], and whose "inversion" by the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] marks the close of the Hindu-Javanese east-coast age.

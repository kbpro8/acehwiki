The **teungkoe meunasah** (in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s spelling; modern Atjehnese *teungku meunasah*) was the village religious official of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] who, alongside the village head [[keutjhiq|*keutjhiq*]], represented public authority in the village and was specifically responsible for the spiritual concerns of the villagers — and who served, in particular, as the village's ritual slaughterer (*slachter*). The office is recorded in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] (p. 117) and in [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (vol. I, p. 241) as a central institution of the village religious-and-civil administration of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]].

## The office
The *teungkoe meunasah* is introduced in *De Karbouw* (p. 117) as follows:

> *"In Groot-Atjeh behoort het slachten tot de speciale taak van de *teungkoe meunasah*, d.i. de man, die naast het dorpshoofd (*keutjhiq*), als vertegenwoordiger van het openbaar gezag in het dorp, speciaal voor de geestelijke belangen der dorpsgenoten heeft te zorgen."*
The office is thus dual: the *teungkoe meunasah* is at once (1) the religious official of the village, alongside the civil head *keutjhiq*, and (2) the village's ritual slaughterer. The name itself — *teungkoe meunasah* — derives from *meunasah* (the village prayer-house and religious school, from Arabic *madrasa*), so that the title literally means "the *teungkoe* (religious teacher) of the *meunasah*."

## Adat prescriptions on the teungkoe as slaughterer
[[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] in *De Karbouw* (p. 117) records the Atjehnese adat prescriptions governing the *teungkoe*'s qualifications and acts as slaughterer — prescriptions that link the slaughterer's bodily and ritual purity directly to the quality of the meat:

> *"De overgeleverde Atjehse adat geeft allerlei voorschriften, waaraan de *teungkoe* als slachter moet voldoen, wil het vlees van goede kwaliteit zijn. Zo moet hij zich vóór de slacht terdege wassen en mag hijzelf niet ziek, of zijn vrouw niet zwanger zijn, daar anders het vlees walgelijk zou rieken (*[[hanji|hanji]]*). Valt het vlees later minder smakelijk uit, dan zegt men daarom wel spottend: 'de *teungkoe* heeft zich zeker niet goed gebaad.' Hij mag bij het slachten de tanden niet op elkaar drukken, dan zou het vlees taai zijn. Ook moet hij er zich voor wachten, na de slacht het slachtmes af te vegen. Deed hij dit wèl, dan zou ook dit nadelig werken op de hoedanigheid van het vlees."*
The prescriptions are:
1. **Washing**: the *teungkoe* must wash himself thoroughly before the slaughter.
2. **Bodily purity**: he must not be sick himself; his wife must not be pregnant (otherwise the meat would smell *hanji* — foul). The village mocking-formula "the *teungkoe* must not have bathed properly" is used of poor-quality meat.
3. **Teeth**: he must not clench his teeth during the slaughter (otherwise the meat would be tough).
4. **Knife**: he must not wipe the knife after the slaughter (this would also impair the meat's quality).
These prescriptions are instances of the wider Indonesian pattern of sympathetic-magical conception of contact-transfer: the slaughterer's bodily and ritual state is held to be transferred, through the act of slaughter, to the meat itself.

## The slaughter posture and the teungkoe's fee
The *teungkoe*'s slaughter-technique is described in *De Karbouw* (p. 118): the animal lies on its left side, four legs bound, head twisted back so the chin points vertically upward; the cut is made at the place reached by the tips of the ears when pulled down — adat supplementing the silent Islamic law on the precise place of the cut. The binding (*tjaroeëh*) is the collective task of many villagers; the *teungkoe*'s fee is a neck-piece three fingers wide below the ears — paid on the [[maq meugang|*maq meugang*]] slaughter-days, but not on the *Moloet* (Mawlid), *hakikah* (newborn), or *kanoeri's* (sacred meals) occasions.


## Significance
The *teungkoe meunasah* is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as the central village religious-and-civil official of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], and as the village's ritual slaughterer — the office through which the Islamic ritual-slaughter complex was integrated with the village social structure. The adat prescriptions on the *teungkoe*'s qualifications and acts document the sympathetic-magical conception of contact-transfer that underlay the Atjehnese ritual-slaughter system, and the fee-system (paid on *maq meugang*, unpaid on *Moloet*, *hakikah* and *kanoeri's*) documents the graded ritual-economy of the Atjehnese slaughter-calendar. The office is documented by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] and by [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] in [[Kreemer's Atjeh ([[1922]]-23)|*Atjeh*]] (vol. II, p. 209) and re-cited in [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw*]].

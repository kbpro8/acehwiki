**Kanduri** (also *kenduri*, *kauduris*; from Persian *qandūrī*) was the Acehnese religious feast — a communal meal held on occasion of religious holidays, life-cycle events (circumcision, marriage, death), seasonal feasts, and in fulfilment of vows. It was one of the most important social institutions of [[Great Acheh|Acehnese]] life.

## Food at a Kanduri

At *kauduris* (religious feasts) and suchlike occasions, **glutinous rice** (*bu kunyèt*) coloured yellow with turmeric was the favourite dish. To this were always added either *tumpoë* (a sort of pancakes, six or seven of which were laid on top of the rice) and *cheuncuruët* (a gelatinous network formed of the same kind of rice), or else grated coconut mixed with red sugar (*u mirah*), or long strips of stockfish boiled in coconut milk, called *keumamaih teunaguën*.

At weddings, funeral feasts, receptions of distinguished guests, and other ceremonious occasions, it was customary to serve up the rice and its accessories in a definite traditional manner on *dalöngs* (trays). This manner of service was called *meu'idang*, and after the rice, fish, and gulè came the tray of sweetmeats, containing a dish of glutinous rice (*bu leukat*) without turmeric, and a dish of *pisang peungat* (ripe plantains sliced thin and boiled with cloves, cinnamon, sugar, and pandan-leaves), often with *srèykaya* (eggs with coconut milk and herbs, well cooked by steaming).

## Religious Context

The kanduri was embedded in the religious calendar. On the *ureë raya* (Eid feast day, first of Shawal after Ramadan), many went to visit their family burial places (*bhöm*), decorating the head of the tomb with flowers (*jeumpa, seumanga*) and the *bungöng peukan* ("market-flowers"), burning incense and offering prayers. The kanduri at [[Meunasah|meunasah]] was also a setting for the *ratéb saman* — the terribly noisy religious litany derived from [[Rateb Saman|the saint Samman]] — and other communal religious activities.

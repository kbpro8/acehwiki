The **pacification of Atjeh** (Dutch *pacificatie van Atjeh* — "pacification of Atjeh and Dependencies") was the declared aim of Dutch policy in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from [[1873]] to [[1923]] — first the military subjection of the sultanate's resistance, then the winning of the population's "bevrediging" (satisfaction) through works of peace — which author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] makes the organizing principle of his *[[Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh]]*: divided into "de pacificatie van Regeeringswege" ("the pacification from the side of the Government") and "de pacificatie en het particulier initiatief" ("the pacification and private initiative").

## As a governing programme

[[General van der Heijden|General Van der Heijden]] first voiced the programme in [[1879]]: after the successful operations of the two preceding years, "van dat oogenblik af de tijd van pacificatie en restauratie was aangebroken, waarin het burgerlijk bestuur met andere middelen dan het zwaard naar onderwerping zou moeten streven en trachten te herstellen wat door de rampen van den oorlog was vernield" — the armed conquest should be followed by pacification and restoration with other means than the sword. The [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|premature civil government of 1881–1884]] however lost nearly all that had been gained; the [[Dutch Policy of 'Concentration'|Concentration]] (1884–1898) abandoned the offensive for containment and failed equally; the decisive turn came with the tandem of Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and Adviser [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] ([[1898]]–[[1903]]), the [[Korte verklaring]], the pursuit of [[Teukoe Oemar]] ([[1899]]), the expeditions into [[Pidië]] and the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|Gajo lands]], and the administrative consolidation of Governors [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] ([[1905]]–[[1908]]) and [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] ([[1908]]–[[1918]]), under whom "the swords were forged into ploughshares" ([[J. Kreemer]]) and all effective resistance ended by [[1913]].

## As an economic doctrine

For Langhout, the pacification was indissolubly economic. "Alles wat door de Regeering aan het gewest wordt gedaan, moet worden beschouwd uit het oogpunt van pacificatie" ("Everything the Government does for the province must be viewed from the angle of pacification") — Governor [[Van Sluys|A. G. H. van Sluys]]'s rule, quoted approvingly: roads, tramways, schools, credit banks, waterworks, exhibitions, museums, the [[Javasche Bank te Koetaradja|bank]] and the press were "pacificatiemiddelen" ("means of pacification"), as were, on the side of private initiative, the harbour of [[Sabang]], the shipping lines, the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|petroleum enterprises]] (whose administrator [[Allein]] soon worked with 60% Atjehnese labour and whose rice-barns even raiders spared), the [[N.V. Vereenigde Javasche Houthandel Maatschappijen|Simaloer timber works]] and the East Coast estates. The **steunpilaren der pacificatie** ("pillars of the pacification") were the press ([[Nieuwsblad voor Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]), the banks and trading houses ([[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij|NHM]], [[Javasche Bank te Koetaradja|Javabank]], [[Atjehsche Handelmaatschappij|AHM]], [[Handelsmaatschappij Deli-Atjeh|Deli-Atjeh]]) and the [[Economische Vereeniging te Koeta Radja|Economische Vereeniging]] — with [[Foreign capital in Atjeh|foreign capital]] a remarkable, "op gevaar af van het nationale karakter van dit werkje geweld aan te doen" acknowledged, auxiliary.

## The verdict of 1923

"The pacification of Atjeh can be regarded as finished; one is no less safe there than everywhere else in the Indies" — such the verdict of K.P.M. director [[N. van Zalinge]] (late [[1922]]), echoed by [[Frijling]]'s report and by General Swart's foreword of [[31 March]] [[1923]]: "sedert ongeveer een tiental jaren" a state of rest and order had obtained which for the future promised the best, requiring only time fully to consolidate itself. Generals Swart and Van Sluys insisted equally on the *bevrediging*: the population must be reconciled with "a certain bitter past" by deeds of welfare — cheap taxes, quick justice, schools, doctors — "dat het onze dure plicht is" ("that it is our dear duty").

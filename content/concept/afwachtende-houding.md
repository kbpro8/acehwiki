The **[[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]]** (wait-and-see posture; also *passieve stelsel* or *afwachtende stelsel*) was the passive defensive strategy applied by [[Jan van Swieten|generaal van Swieten]] after the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] and again by [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] after the [[Death of Generaal Pel (24 February 1876)|death of generaal Pel]] on [[24 February]] [[1876]]. It is the chief *bête noire* of Borel's *Onze vestiging in Atjeh*: the strategy that, in Borel's judgement, squandered the fruits of the Kraton's fall, prolonged the Atjeh-oorlog for years, and very nearly cost the Dutch their entire vestiging in the [[Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)|crisis of May–June 1874]]. Pel's reversal of the *afwachtende houding* from [[28 April]] [[1874]] is the central narrative of the book.

## The Instruction to Pel

The *afwachtende houding* was, in Borel's account, the explicit strategic doctrine that van Swieten bequeathed to his successor. The verbal instruction that Pel received from van Swieten on inheriting the command — recorded in the [[De Rochemont brochure|brochure of gep. luit. kol. de Rochemont]] — was: "zooveel mogelijk vechten weinig, eene zuiver afwachtende houding bewaren" (fight as little as possible, keep a purely wait-and-see posture). Van Swieten had himself applied the doctrine from [[15 February]] [[1874]] onwards, after the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]]. "Geen vechten meer met den vijand, maar werken met schop en spade" (no more fighting with the enemy, but work with spade and shovel) was the formula. The Javaansche hulptroepen of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]], a veld-batterij, one company of [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] and the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.]] were sent back to Java; the *padi-snijden* concession was granted to [[Langkroek]]; the daily patrols from [[Pakan Atjeh]] to the Taman, ordered on [[7 April]] [[1874]], came "2½ maand" too late. The [[Van Swieten's afscheidsrede (1874)|afscheidsrede]] of [[23 April]] [[1874]] — "Ik beschouw den oorlog met Atjeh als geëindigd" — was the public expression of the doctrine.

## The Consequences

The *afwachtende houding* had, in Borel's account, catastrophic consequences. The [[Desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe (1874)|desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe]] on [[26 April]] [[1874]] was the "eerste wrange vrucht" of the passive system. The [[Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)|crisis of May–June 1874]] followed: by the end of May the Dutch macht had "ten gevolge van het overgroot aantal zieken bijna niets meer beteekende"; some companies could field fewer than 30 men of an original 125; Borel insists that 500 kloeke Atjehneezen could have taken the Kraton. The kwade moesson broke on [[31 May]] [[1874]], rendering the [[Atjeh-rivier]] onbevaarbaar and proving the fout of never building a [[Zeehoofd|zeehoofd]] at Oeleh Leh. The [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]] — when the enemy broke through the Dutch line during [[Karel van der Heijden|van der Heijden]]'s absence at [[Gedoeng]], fell into the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]], verjoeg the pro-Dutch bevolking and committed the "gruwelijke moordtooneel in een van onze hospitalen" — was, Borel judged, the final vindication of his argument that the *afwachtende houding* could not hold the line.

## Pel's Reversal and the Return Under Wiggers

[[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] — who inherited the bezettingskorps on [[26 April]] [[1874]] — "dacht er evenwel geheel anders over" than van Swieten on every point of the doctrine. From [[28 April]] [[1874]] he ordered the daily bombardment of kampongs within range; on [[4 May]] [[1874]] he met [[Toekoe Nek]] and decided to bezet [[Marassa]], [[Oeleh Leh]] and other posts; from [[20 June]] [[1874]] — the [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|battle of Lampoeloe]] — he resumed the offensive. The [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] of [[28 June]] [[1874]], the [[Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)|battle of Soerian]] of [[26 July]] [[1874]], the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] of [[28 September]] [[1874]], the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] of [[7 November]] [[1874]], the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|battle of Longbatta]] of [[31 December]] [[1874]], the [[Surprise of Lamara Olejlo (14–15 February 1875)|surprise of Lamara Olejlo]] of [[14 February|14]]–[[15 February]] [[1875]], the [[Completion of the defensive ring around Kota Radja (May 1875)|completion of the stelling-Pel]] in May [[1875]], the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great offensive of December 1875 – February 1876]] — all were the work of Pel's reversal. After Pel's death, however, the *afwachtende houding* returned. [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] — who assumed command on [[10 March]] [[1876]] — was "geen voorstander van het aanvallende stelsel"; he wished not to extend the linie further and to adopt "strijkt afwachtende houding." The stelling from the [[Kroeng Raba]] to the [[Kwala Gigieng]] remained "nagenoeg onveranderd" for the rest of the period Borel describes.

## The Critique

Borel's critique of the *afwachtende houding* is the connective argument of *Onze vestiging in Atjeh*. The doctrine was, in his reading, the original sin of the second expedition: the [[Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken (1873)|Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken]] booklet distributed to officers before the expedition had prescribed the *gulden regel* that "Slechts eene krachtige vervolging kan de overwinning vruchtbaar maken" — and van Swieten had disregarded it. The doctrine was also, in Borel's reading, contrary to the nature of the Atjehnese enemy: the [[Atjehnees volkskarakter (Borel)|Atjehnees volkskarakter]] — "woest, dapper, vrijheidlievend, onafhankelijk, fanatiek" — could be subdued only by the *kracht der wapens* and never by proclamations, gifts, or passive posts. Borel's alternative was the *aanvallende tactiek*: [[Mobiele colonnes (Borel)|mobiele colonnes]] of 600–800 men, always ready to march, operating two or three in *verbond* toward the same *doel*, supported by steel [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]] with schokbuis and by the two principal means of *kappen en branden* (cutting and burning). The [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] proposal of [[1878]] was the prescriptive culmination of the critique: only a metropolitan reinforcement on the scale of the [[1830]] French expedition against Algiers could supply the force that the *aanvallende tactiek* required.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

[[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] traces the party of the waiting posture, still headed in his day by general [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] and joined, from [[26 September]] [[1876]], by the former minister [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]]. The *Militaire Spectator*, the oldest military journal of the country, had taken sides for the waiting posture "from the very beginning", and majoor [[F. de Bas|de Bas]] in its first issue of [[1886]] still defended the general; but the krijgsgeschiedenis (war history), Verstege maintains, cannot point to a single example of a fighting, freedom-loving people brought to submission by mere waiting. The general's own texts supply the doctrine: the instruction left to Kolonel [[Jacobus Pel|Pel]] ([[22 April]] [[1874]]) that "de agressieve kracht" of the enemy was small; the letters of 27 October to the Minister against the departing troops ("niet wij, maar hij wordt door die vruchtlooze aanvallen uitgeput ... wij stijgen dagelijks in sterkte" — "not we, but he is exhausted by those fruitless attacks ... we rise daily in strength"); *[[De Waarheid over onze vestiging in Atjeh]]'s* sentence that the waiting posture abandoned in [[1875]]-[[1876]] was already, in [[1879]], near to the war's end; and, at the very hour of being disproved by every day of Atjeh history, the melancholic album-word of May [[1884]] that war "must always be constructive and civilising" (see [[Jan van Swieten]]). Against them all Verstege invokes Pel's own missive of [[30 April]] [[1875]] (see [[Pel's missive to the Governor-General (30 April 1875)]]), the pacification wrought by [[Karel van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] in [[1879]]-[[1880]] "niet door afwachten maar met doortastend handelen" ("not by waiting but by resolute action"), and the Clausewitz doctrines abused by the general: "dat het strijdt met het begrip van oorlog, om de verdediging het uiterst of einddoel te doen blijven" ("that it conflicts with the very notion of war to leave defence as the uttermost or final aim"), and that no enemy is ever subdued by occupying *one* hostile province with quiet composure and preferring the more secure possession of that small conquest to great results (D. II, pp. 2-3, 226, 231, 257). The verdict of his own minister of War, [[A. W. P. Weitzel|Weitzel]], fighting the Schepel bill in the Kamer of [[26 May]] [[1885]], supplies the pamphlet's close: "the most passive defence possible" is "a system condemned by all experts in all times and in all countries" — for the own country; how much more for a limited position in a land inhabited by a brave, freedom-loving people.

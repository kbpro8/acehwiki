**The sarakata** was the letter of appointment — *brief van aanstelling* — issued by the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] to a *landschapshoofd* who came to pay him homage or to promise tribute, the written instrument by which the Sultan's court preserved, even in the era of its decline, a "schijn van opperhoogheid" — a semblance of suzerainty — over the innumerable small polities of the Atjehsche Onderhoorigheden. *De Buitenbezittingen* describes the sarakata as the indispensable "ornament" of native legitimacy, prized by chiefs even when their authority was in fact independent of any royal grant.

## Form and purpose

The handbook explains that the Sultan's court, after the fragmentation of the great sixteenth-century Acehnese empire into "vele kleine landschapjes ter Noord-, Oost- en Westkust, welke, hoewel inderdaad onafhankelijk, meestal niet geheel den band met het Atjehsche Sultanshof verbraken," preserved its shadow of suzerainty "o.a. door het uitreiken van 'brieven van aanstelling' (sarakata's) aan de landschapshoofden, welke den Sultan hulde kwamen betuigen of hem schatting beloofden." The instrument was thus the written form of the personal act of homage. "Voor de wettigheid hunner heerschappij waren deze Sarakata's weliswaar niet noodzakelijk" — for the legality of their rule these sarakata's were by no means necessary — "maar toch stelden de onafhankelijke landschapshoofden veel prijs als 'ornament', als tastbaar bewijs hunner waardigheid" — yet the independent chiefs set great store by them as ornament, as tangible proof of their dignity.

## On the Westkust

The best-documented issuance of sarakata's was on the Westkust, where in the year 1185 of the Mohammedan era (approximately [[1760]] AD), "zestien hoofden uit het Meulabôhsche, onder geleide van een voornaam handelaar van die havenplaats, hunne opwachting maakten bij den Sultan, die hun, tegen belofte van geregelde schatting, brieven van erkenning (sarakata's) uitreikte." Their conductor was appointed the Sultan's customs-collector, with the title [[Keudjeuroeën|keudjeuroeën Tjhïʼ]], to whom the tribute of these sixteen chiefs was to be paid — an act that created the historic [[Kawaj XVI|Kawaj XVI]]. Other Westkust chiefs later received their own sarakata's but were not subordinated to the collector.

## In Tamiang and the Gayo lands

In [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] the sarakata played a foundational role. According to the oldest history of that region, after a period of *regeeringloosheid* — anarchy — following a war with a Javanese prince, Batak immigrants near Tandjoeng Karang secured an understanding with the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Acehnese Sultan]], "zoodat deze hun hoofd ([[Radja Mengkoeta|radja Mengkoeta]]) een sarakata verleende als vorst over Tamiang." In the Gayo lands, by contrast, the Acehnese sarakata was unknown: the source records that "in plaats van de voor de hoofden der Atjehsche Onderhoorigheden gebruikelijke sarakata's of acten van bevestiging, gaf men den Gayösche kédjoeröns, die vermoedelijk destijds al even zelden als thans de schrijfkunst beoefenden, ieder een blank wapen, een soort van dolkmes, als teeken hunner waardigheid." The substitution marks the cultural frontier between the Atjehsche polity and the highland Gayo sphere.

## Aftermath

Even after the effective disappearance of Sultanate authority — and especially after the Dutch abolition of the Sultanate in the late nineteenth century — the sarakata retained its talismanic value. Chiefs continued to prize and display the documents inherited from their ancestors, while Dutch recognition was conveyed through the parallel instrument of the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]].

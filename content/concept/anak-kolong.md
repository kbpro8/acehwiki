**Anak-kolong** — literally "child of the *[[kolong]]*" — was the term by which the children of [[K.N.I.L.]] soldiers identified themselves, even as adults: not as "child of a soldier" but as "*anak-kolong*" to indicate they had grown up in the *[[tangsi]]*. The *anak-kolong* had their own *[[adat]]* and *sfeer*, distinct from *[[kampong]]* children. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *anak-kolong* is the figure through whom Dames makes his argument about the multi-generational transmission of the *tangsi* culture.

## The kolong

The *[[kolong]]* — the space under the *[[slaaptafel]]* (sleeping-table) — was where the soldiers' children slept on a *matje* (small mat) and played. The *kolong* was the child's first world: the space under the sleeping-table of the *[[tangsi]]*, where the child played while the parents worked and slept above. The *anak-kolong* — the child of the *kolong* — was the child whose first world was the *tangsi*.

## The tangsi and the adat

"De anak-kolong konden de oude adatsfeer van de kampong missen, de tangsi gaf hen een eigen sfeer, een eigen adat, een eigen *stijl*…" The *anak-kolong* could miss the old *adat* atmosphere of the *kampong*; the *tangsi* gave them their own *sfeer* (atmosphere), their own *adat* (custom), their own *stijl* (style). The *anak-kolong* was therefore not a *kampong* child but a *tangsi* child: a child whose *adat* was the *adat* of the colonial army, not the *adat* of the village.

## The kindergeleider

"De kindergeleider" — the children's guide — was an old *Oom* assigned to escort the *anak-kolong* to and from school. "De troep" marched *model in het gelid* (in formation as a model), with *Oom* martial with *snor* and *rottinkje* (moustache and little stick). The commands "*Geeft acht!*", "*Hoofd Rechts!*", "*Links-twee-drie-vier*" were properly executed. The *kindergeleider* was therefore the *Oom* who transmitted the *tangsi* culture to the *anak-kolong*: the child who marched to school in formation was the child who would, in turn, become the soldier.

## The voorzanger

"Zelfs een 13-14 jaar oud *anak-kolong*" could lead the *voorzanger* role in the *[[Marechausseelied]]* — a child of the *tangsi* who had grown up with the song. The *anak-kolong* was therefore not just the child of the *tangsi* but the carrier of its culture: the child who knew the songs, the *adat*, and the *stijl* of the colonial army.

## Significance

The *anak-kolong* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the figure through whom Dames makes his argument about the multi-generational transmission of the *tangsi* culture. The book's argument is that the *anak-kolong* — the child of the *kolong* — was not a *kampong* child but a *tangsi* child, with its own *adat* and *stijl*. The *anak-kolong* was therefore the mechanism by which the *tangsi* culture was transmitted across generations: the child who marched to school in formation was the child who would, in turn, become the soldier. The dissolution of the *tangsi* culture with the disbandment of the [[K.N.I.L.]] in [[1949]] was therefore the dissolution of the *anak-kolong*'s world.

***Tandoe*** — plural *tandoes* — was the field litter used in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] to carry the sick and wounded: *linnen hangmatten, aan een bamboe bevestigd*, linen hammocks fastened to a bamboo, in [[P. H. R. Beuming]]'s gloss.

## Use on patrol

The *tandoe* was standard equipment for any column expecting casualties. The patrol that marched out of [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] on [[25 September]] [[1897]] against the [[Goeah Teroes]] cave was accompanied by a hospital soldier with ten *tandoes* and the necessary forced labourers in the rear-guard — see [[Patrol to the Goeah Teroes on the Glé Betoes (25 September 1897)]].

On the descent from the cave, carrying the bodies of Lieutenant [[J. van Greuningen]] and four fusiliers and the severely wounded, the *tandoes* made the return *een halsbrekend werk* — breakneck work — and Beuming records that the swaying drew many a cry from the wounded. On the patrol's arrival at the *benting* the men crowded about the *tandoes*, and *menig krijgsman wendde het hoofd af, bij het zien zijner gesneuvelde wapenbroeders* — many a soldier turned his head aside at the sight of his fallen comrades.

## The tandoe as a command post

At [[Lamtengah]] during the [[Three-day battle in the Beradin pass (23-25 May 1896)|three-day battle in the Beradin pass]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]], the commander of the [[Koloniale Reserve|koloniale reserve]], Captain [[Drijber]], was wounded in arm and leg but refused to be carried to the ambulance and, *gedragen in een tandoe*, continued to command in the midst of his men. Beuming records that this rare proof of courage so transported the men that they threw themselves on the enemy with a thundering hurrah.

**Geudoemba** (also spelled *geudomba*, *geudombak*; Dutch: *kleine éénvellige vaastrommel* — "small single-headed barrel drum") is a **small, single-headed barrel drum** indigenous to **[[Acheh]]** (Atjēh), northern Sumatra. It is a membranophone of the barrel-drum family, distinct from the double-headed *[[Kendang (Aceh)|kendang]]* / *[[Gendang]]* and the frame drum *[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]* / *[[Redep]]*.

## Organology

- **Hornbostel-Sachs classification**: 211.211.1 (single-headed barrel drum, struck)
- **Body**: wooden barrel shell, typically carved from a single block of hardwood (e.g., *meranti*, *kayu malam*)
- **Head**: single membrane (goat, cow, or monitor lizard skin), stretched over one end
- **Tension system**: traditionally rattan or cane lacing (V-lacing or Y-lacing) with tuning wedges; modern variants may use metal tuning bolts
- **Dimensions**: small — typically 20–30 cm height, 15–20 cm head diameter (smaller than the *kendang*)
- **Playing technique**: struck with bare hands (palm, fingers) on the head; sometimes the body is also struck for timbral contrast
- **Position**: held horizontally across the lap, cradled in the left arm or placed on the floor, played with right hand (right-handed players)

## Historical and Regional Context

The *geudoemba* is documented by **[[Jaap Kunst]]** in *[[Muziek en Dans in de Buitengewesten]]* ([[1946]], p. 10) as one of the instruments "for Atjēh" (*voor Atjēh*) alongside the *[[Hareubab]]* and *[[Tjanang Triëng]]*, distinct from the "typically Mohammedan instruments" (*typisch Mohammedaansche speeltuigen*) such as the *gambus*, *rabana*, *marwas*, *bangei*, and *serunai*. This suggests the *geudoemba* may represent an **indigenous Acehnese drum type** that predates or developed alongside the Islamic instrumental complex, rather than being a direct import from the Persian-Arabic sphere.

The term *geudoemba* appears to be specifically Acehnese. Related single-headed barrel drums exist elsewhere in the archipelago (e.g., the *[[Kendang]]* family), but the *geudoemba*'s small size and specific construction mark it as a local variant.

## Role in Acehnese Music

The *geudoemba* functions primarily as a **rhythmic accompaniment instrument** in:

- **[[Saman]]** dance ensembles — providing the rapid, interlocking rhythmic patterns (*tak*) that drive the choreography
- **[[Meuseukat]]** (women's seated dance-song) — marking the cyclic metric structure
- **[[Seudati]]** / **[[Sedati (dance)|Sedati]]** — accompanying the slow, processional dance
- **[[Bineus]]** — supporting the graceful *kain*-waving movements
- **[[Hikayat]]** / **[[Dendang]]** narrative singing — punctuating poetic lines

Its bright, articulate tone and quick decay make it well-suited to the complex, fast-tempo rhythmic cycles (*[[Tala]]* / *[[Iqa']]*) characteristic of Acehnese percussion music.

## Etymology

The etymology of *geudoemba* is not definitively established. It may derive from an **Acehnese root** *geudong* ("to strike, beat") + *-ba* (instrumental suffix), or be related to the **Malay** *gendang* / *gandang* with a distinct Acehnese phonological development (*g* > *geu*, *ng* > *mb*). The Dutch transcription *geudoemba* reflects the Acehnese pronunciation [ɡɯdəmba] or [ɡədomba].

## Iconography

The **oorlam** was the ration of Schiedam gin issued to soldiers of the [[Oost-Indisch Leger|Netherlands East Indies Army]], described by [[G. B. Hooijer]] as one of the chief consolations of life in the outposts of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## At Lam Koenjit

Hooijer recorded that at [[Lam Koenjit]] the day began with reveille at half past five and coffee; at ten came soup with rice and *sambal* (chilli relish); and at half past four in the afternoon the mess-kettles were filled again. *Vóór beide maaltijden werd het heerlijke oorlam, dat voor den soldaat zoo geprezen Schiedammer vocht, met één groote teug naar binnen geslokt* — before both meals the delicious *oorlam*, that Schiedam liquid so praised by the soldier, was swallowed in one great gulp.

## At Toengkoeb

At [[Toengkoeb]], among the advantages that made many soldiers prefer to stay longer than the prescribed fourteen days of a *[[Detacheering|detacheering]]*, Hooijer named that *boven alles, de drie oorlammen daags waren een heerlijk iets* — above all, the three *oorlams* a day were a delightful thing. They roused the men to play and sing when night had fallen, the tin reflectors behind the lanterns lit a small patch of the surroundings, the crickets chirped in the grass, and the unsurpassed calm of the Indies night made them feel as though floating on a ship in the open ocean rather than housed in an advanced temporary post amid a hostile people.

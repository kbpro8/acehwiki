**Islam in Aceh** denotes the historical development, practice, and cultural expression of the Islamic faith within the Sultanate of [[Acheh]] from the early 13th century through the colonial period. Aceh became a major centre of Islamic learning, Sufi orders, and devotional literature, exemplified by the adoption of Arabic works such as the **Burda of al‑Busiri** in Malay translation (see [[Malay translation of the Burda (16th century)]]).

### Early Islamisation
Islam entered Aceh via trade routes from the Arabian Peninsula and Gujarat. By the early 14th century, Acehnese rulers claimed the title *Sultan* and patronised *madrasahs* that taught Arabic, Persian, and Malay.

### Sufi Influence and Devotional Poetry
The 16th‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* illustrates the integration of Arabic devotional poetry into Acehnese religious life. The poem’s themes—such as the Prophet’s supremacy over *jinn* and *humans* (page 54) and the call to resist *shaitan* (page 53)—were incorporated into local *tazkirah* and *qasidah* recitals.

### Institutional Structures
- **Madrasahs**: Centers of Qur’anic and Arabic studies where texts like the *Burda* were taught.
- **Zawiyahs**: Sufi lodges that used the *Burda* for *dhikr* (remembrance) sessions.
- **Royal Patronage**: Acehnese sultans commissioned translations of key Arabic works to legitimize their rule and promote piety.

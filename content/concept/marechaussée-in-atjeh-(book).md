**Marechaussée in Atjeh** is a Dutch-language military book co-authored by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]] and [[Schmidt (Kolonel)|Kolonel Schmidt]], describing the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Marechaussee te voet]] (military police) and its role in the [[Aceh War]]. Published sometime after du Croo's service in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] in the [[1920s]], the work is a key source for the Dutch officer's view of the long Aceh conflict and a companion volume to the same authors' *[[Generaal Swart (book)|Generaal Swart]]*.

## Content and Character

According to Dr. J. W. Meyer Ranneft, the book presents "het beeld van Atjeh en de Atjehoorlog, zoals dat in die officieren heeft geleefd" — the image of Aceh and the Aceh war as it lived in those officers. It includes "oude suggestieve officiersportretten tussen de tekst" (old suggestive officer portraits among the text), giving a vivid, insider's perspective on [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] operations and life on patrol in the *rimboe* (jungle) of Aceh. The work captures the ethos of the [[K.N.I.L.]] officers who saw the region as "het lastige kindje dat Atjeh heette" (the troublesome child called Atjeh), a phrase preserved in the memorial for du Croo by the Dutch journalist Johan Luger.

## Authors

- [[Maurice Henri du Croo]] served in Atjeh from [[1925]], participating in patrols and later as staff officer, and was decorated Knight of the [[Orde van Oranje-Nassau]] with Swords in [[1927]].
- [[Schmidt (Kolonel)|Kolonel Schmidt]] was a fellow [[K.N.I.L.]] officer who also collaborated with du Croo on *[[Generaal Swart (book)|Generaal Swart]]*.

## Significance

The book is an important period document for the [[Aceh War]], reflecting the colonial military mentality of the interwar years. It pairs with du Croo's other writings — including *[[Generaal Swart (book)|Generaal Swart]]* and the pseudonymous *Sergeant Weers en de Zijnen* — that depict the life of Dutch soldiers in the Indies. As a co-authored work by two serving or former [[K.N.I.L.]] officers, *Marechaussée in Atjeh* belongs to the body of Dutch colonial military literature that shaped metropolitan memory of the Aceh pacification in the decades after the formal end of the war.

**poeasa** (English: "fasting (Ramadan)") is a term or concept described in [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]]'s *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu* in relation to [[Aceh]]. Annual fasting prescription; fairly strictly observed.

## Translation

fasting (Ramadan)

## Description

Annual fasting prescription; fairly strictly observed. On days before and at the end of the fasting period, beef is universally consumed; ~15-day supply conserved with vinegar, salt and spices.

## Significance

The term *poeasa* is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the cultural, administrative, or institutional vocabulary of [[Aceh]].

## Poeasa as a measure of age ([[1937]])

In Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]]'s [[Oogheelkundig kampongonderzoek in Groot-Atjeh (Maart-December 1937)|eye survey of 1937]] the *poeasa* served as the practical unit for reckoning the age of Atjehnese patients. "Aangezien bij de Atjehsche bevolking geen jaartelling bestaat, leverde het opgeven van den ouderdom eenige moeilijkheid" — since among the Atjehnese population no era-reckoning existed, the statement of age presented some difficulty.

To determine approximately, and not merely by estimate, the age at which blindness had arisen, Gomperts had the age given in the number of *poeasa*'s the patient had lived through — the Mohammedan fast falling once a year. "Werd bijv. opgegeven drie poeasa's, dan was de patiënt 2 jaar en zooveel maanden oud." Up to the fourth year this reckoning was fairly accurate; beyond it the school age of six served as the measure for determining ages of six and above.

The method underlies the age columns of Gomperts's tables on the causes of [[Blindheid in Groot-Atjeh|blindness]], and hence his conclusion that the blindness of a great part of the adult blind dated from their childhood.

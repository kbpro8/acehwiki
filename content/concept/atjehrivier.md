**The Atjehrivier** — the *Atjeh river* — is the northernmost segment of the *groote Westelijke lengtedal* of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] and the principal river of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], flowing through the *vruchtbare vlakte van Groot-Atjeh* to the Indian Ocean at [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] near [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]]. In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), the Atjehrivier is the geographical closure of the great Westelijke lengtedal — the river whose valley forms the northern terminus of the longitudinal valley system that runs from the upper Simpang Kin i in South-Atjeh through the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]] and the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Kroeëng Wojla]] to the Atjeh coast.

## The Atjehrivier as the northern end of the great Westelijke lengtedal

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I describes the great Westelijke lengtedal of Sumatra as running from the upper *Simpang Kin i* in South-Atjeh northward through a sequence of *dalen* — the *Lao Alas (Woi ni Agoesein)*, the *Midden-Woi ni Tripö*, the *boven-Seunagan*, the *boven-Meureubo*, and the *Kroeëng Wojla* tributaries — to the *Kroeëng Teunom*, formed by the [[Geumpang|Geumpang]] and [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] rivers. "Het laatste Noordelijke stuk van het groote Westelijke lengtedal gevormd wordt door de Atjehrivier (8)." The Atjehrivier is thus the geographical *sluitstuk* of the longitudinal valley system of western Sumatra.

## The Atjehrivier and Groot-Atjeh

The Bureau's account of the Atjehrivier's course through Groot-Atjeh is succinct: "Het Noordelijkste deel van het Westelijke lengtedal behoort geheel tot het stroomgebied van de Atjehrivier, welke tusschen de beide Westelijke Barisanketenen, door de vruchtbare vlakte van Groot-Atjeh, haar weg vindt naar zee om langs Koeta Radja bij Oelèë Lheuë den Indischen Oceaan te bereiken." The Atjehrivier is thus the hydrographical artery of Groot-Atjeh, draining the *vruchtbare vlakte* between the two Westelijke Barisanketenen and reaching the sea at Oelèë Lheuë, the harbour of Koeta Radja.

## Significance

The Atjehrivier is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the northern terminus of the great Westelijke lengtedal and the hydrographical artery of Groot-Atjeh. The Bureau's emphasis on the river's course "door de vruchtbare vlakte van Groot-Atjeh" frames the Atjehrivier as the geographical foundation of the political and economic centrality of Groot-Atjeh in the *gewest* Atjeh — the river whose *vruchtbare vlakte* sustained the pre-colonial sultanate's capital at the [[Kraton Atjeh|kraton]] and the colonial capital at Koeta Radja, and whose mouth at Oelèë Lheuë was the principal Dutch harbour of the Atjeh coast.

The Atjehrivier also features prominently in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] account of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|tweede Atjeh-tocht]] of November [[1873]]–January [[1874]]: the Dutch landing of November [[1873]] "had plaats nabij de Atjeh rivier," and the [[Atjehsche Staatsspoorwegen|Atjehse staatsspoorwegen]]' *Atjeh-tram* of the late 19th century connected [[Kotta-Radja|Kotta-Radja]] with the Atjeh-rivier as its western terminus.

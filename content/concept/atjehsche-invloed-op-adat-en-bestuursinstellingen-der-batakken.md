**Atjehsche invloed op adat en bestuursinstellingen der Batakken** — the Atjehnese influence on the *adat* and administrative institutions of the [[Batak]]s — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the institutional and cultural imprint left by the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo-Batak]] and [[Batak-Timoer|Timoer-Batak]] lands during the long period of [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehnese supremacy in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]] and [[atjehsche-suprematie-over-de-timoerhoogvlakte|Atjehsche suprematie over de Timoerhoogvlakte]]. The imprint was, the Bureau records, principally institutional — the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]] imposed on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *sibajaks* and the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoer]] *rijkjes* — though it left, in the Bureau's judgement, "slechts zeer weinig levenskracht" — "only very little vitality" — in the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] lands, where the indigenous *adat* institutions remained "het meest ongeschonden" — "the most intact."

## The institutional imprint: the viervorsten-systeem

The principal institutional imprint of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] on the [[Batak]]s was the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]] — the "four-princes" system by which the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate grouped the chiefdoms of its highland and peripheral dependencies into groups of four, each group headed by a [[radja berempat|radja berempat]] (literally "four-princes"). The Bureau records that the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]] had been imposed, in parallel, on both the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoerhoogvlakte]] and the [[Batak-Karo|Karo hoogvlakte]] — a uniform Atjehnese institutional arrangement across the entire Batak highland country.

The Bureau records: "Sommige bestuursinstellingen volgens het Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem (dat ook op de Karo-hoogvlakte werd ingevoerd door Atjeh) worden nog aan dien invloed toegeschreven" — "Some administrative institutions according to the Atjehsche four-princes system (which was also introduced on the Karo hoogvlakte by Atjeh) are still attributed to that influence." The four [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *sibajaks* of [[Lingga|Lingga]], [[Sarinembah|Sarinembah]], [[Baroes Djahé|Baroes Djahé]] and [[Soeka|Soeka]] were, the Bureau records, the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] [[radja berempat|radja berempat]].

## The slightness of the imprint on the Karoland

Despite the institutional imprint of the [[radja berempat|radja berempat]], the Bureau records that the broader Atjehnese imprint on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *adat* and institutions was slight. "Het meest ongeschonden zijn de oude instellingen gebleven in het Karoland; wel vinden wij ook hier sporen terug van Atjehschen invloed, doch zelfs deze hebben, ondanks de geregelde verbindingen, welke tusschen het Karoland en Atjeh bestaan hebben, slechts zeer weinig levenskracht gehad" — "The most intact old institutions have remained in the Karoland; we do indeed find here too traces of Atjehnese influence, but even these have, despite the regular connections that existed between the Karoland and Atjeh, had only very little vitality."

The slightness of the Atjehnese imprint on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *adat* is, the Bureau records, all the more striking in contrast with the Malay coastal influence on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *Doesoen* and *Timoerland*. The Bureau records: "De Maleische invloeden uit de kuststreken echter, die in de Doesoen, doch vooral in het Timoerland, zoo goed zijn na te speuren, bestaan hier niet" — "The Malay influences from the coastal regions, however, which in the Doesoen, and especially in the Timoerland, can be so well traced, do not exist here [in the Karoland]." The [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] hoogvlakte had, in other words, been more open to Malay coastal influence — mediated by the [[Djohor|Djohorese Malays]] of the East Coast — than to Atjehnese influence across the highlands.

## The Atjehsche (?) element in the Perangin-angin merga

The Bureau records, in its discussion of the five [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *merga*s (the patrilineal clans), that the fourth *merga* — *Perangin-angin* — "omvat waarschijnlijk verschillende, ten deele wellicht vreemde (Atjehsche?) elementen" — "contains, probably, various, partly perhaps foreign (Atjehsche?) elements." The Bureau's attribution of an Atjehnese element to the *Perangin-angin* *merga* is hedged with two qualifications — "waarschijnlijk" ("probably") and the parenthetical "Atjehsche?" with its question-mark — but it is, in the Bureau's framing, the principal demographic trace of the Atjehnese imprint on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *merga* system. The Atjehnese imprint on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] lands thus extends, in the Bureau's account, from the institutional (the [[radja berempat|radja berempat]]) to the demographic (the *Perangin-angin* *merga*).

## The Atjehsche title "kedjoeron"

The Bureau records, in its discussion of the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *Doesoen* *oeroeng*-heads, that the title *kedjoeron* borne by some of these heads is a marker of Atjehnese influence. The Bureau records: "Deze titel duide weder op Atjehsche invloeden" — "This title again indicates Atjehnese influences." The *kedjoeron* title — borne by the Malay-coast-appointed *oeroeng*-heads of the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *Doesoen* — was, in the Bureau's framing, an institutional fossil of the Atjehnese presence on the East Coast, transmitted through the Malay coastal sultanates that had succeeded the Atjehnese overlordship.

## Significance

The Atjehsche invloed op adat en bestuursinstellingen der Batakken is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the most concrete trace of the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehnese supremacy in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]] on the eastern side of the highlands. Its slightness — the Bureau's judgement that it had "slechts zeer weinig levenskracht gehad" — is, in the Bureau's framing, the more remarkable in view of the duration of the Atjehnese supremacy and the regular connections between the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] and [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] lands. The deliberate Dutch revival of the Atjehnese-era [[radja berempat|radja berempat]] after the [[Dutch subjugation of the Karo-Batak lands (1904-1906)|1904–1906 annexation]] is, in turn, the most striking instance of the colonial state's strategic use of the Atjehnese institutional legacy to structure its own administration of the highland Batak lands.

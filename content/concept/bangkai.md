**Bangkai** is the Acehnese word meaning “corpse” or “dead body”, recorded in the sixteenth‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* of al‑Bu‑s‑iri. The entry appears on [[80]] of the source, where it is glossed as “jong dagingnja (A. ashia', ledematen)”. The term is used in religious and poetic contexts to denote the physical remains of a human being, often contrasted with spiritual concepts such as *dunia* (world) and *akhirah* (hereafter).

The word derives from the Arabic *ʿajz* “remains”, adapted into Acehnese phonology. In the *Burda* it frequently appears in verses warning against attachment to worldly goods, e.g., “bangkai tidak dapat membeli keselamatan” (“the corpse cannot purchase salvation”).

## Usage in Acehnese literature

In the Acehnese rendition of the *Burda*, *bangkai* is employed in moral admonitions, linking physical death with the need for spiritual redemption. The term also appears in legal texts describing the handling of dead bodies according to Islamic law.

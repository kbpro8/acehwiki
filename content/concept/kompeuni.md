**Kompeuni** was the Acehnese and Malay name — also written *Kompeni*, *Keumpeni* or *Compenie* — for the Dutch colonial power, its army and its administration.

The word runs through the whole of *Generaal Swart*. Under [[C. Deijckerhoff|Deijckerhoff]]'s conciliatory governorship, "als de *Kompeni* ("the Company (the Dutch power)"did not dare, he at once dared twice as much!" After [[1908]], by contrast, "ditmaal beschikte de *Keumpeni* ("the Company (the Dutch power)"over commanders and soldiers who had formed themselves in the heavy work of war." Surrendered firearms went "to the arms store of the Company* ("the Company (the Dutch power)")"; [[J. C. Lamster|Lamster]] recorded that returning *uitgewekenen* were told "that the Company would make their return as easy as possible for them." In the [[Resistance in Daja and Lhong (1914)|Lhong affair]] of [[1914]] the enemy supposed himself surrounded because he understood "that the Company would not send three constabulary men so far into the forest."

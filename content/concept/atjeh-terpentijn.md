**Atjeh-terpentijn** — Atjeh turpentine, also *Sumatra-terpentijn* — is the turpentine oil produced by the steam distillation of the balsam (the raw resinous exudate) of the [[Pinus Merkusii|Sumatra pine]] tapped at the [[Baleq]] enterprise of *'s Lands Caoutchouchedrijf* in the [[Gajo-landen|Gajo-Landen]]-[[Atjeh]] border region. As a colonial commodity, Atjeh-terpentijn was the subject of the [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|1930 pamphlet by Ir. W. Spoor]] — the principal contemporary monograph on the early factory-scale production of Indonesian turpentine — and its chemical composition, pharmacopoeial suitability, and European market reception are the principal subjects of that pamphlet.

## The trial samples of 1924

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] records that, in [[1924]], the [[Afdeeling Handelsmuseum]] of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut]] gathered a series of expert evaluations of trial Atjeh-turpentine — produced from *Pinus Merkusii* tapped in [[Noord-Sumatra]] — and published them as *Bericht* No. 20 in [[April]] [[1925]]. The 1924 trial product had been criticised principally for its odour, which resembled that of *wood turpentine* rather than the *gum spirit* of true turpentine; the criticism was attributed to a high *careen* content (approximately 15%). The 1924 trial samples were produced not by factory-scale distillation but by the *roep-monster* method — the balsam was collected by the foresters of the *Dienst van het Boschwezen* and split into resin and turpentine in the laboratory of the *Afdeeling Nijverheid* at [[Buitenzorg]]. They were thus not factory-scale products but trial samples, and the [[1930]] pamphlet was prompted by the transformation of the trial product into a fully factory-scale one.

## The factory-scale product of 1928-1930

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the factory-scale production of Atjeh-terpentijn began with the introduction of the modern French distillation installation at the [[Baleq]] enterprise in [[October]] [[1928]]. The factory-scale product was investigated on three samples — *H.M. no. 1504* (received December [[1929]]), *H.M. no. 1553* (drawn from a January [[1930]] Amsterdam shipment) and *H.M. no. 1607* (an original tin from the April [[1930]] shipment per m.s. *Poelau Roebiah*). The chemical investigation — by Dr. [[D. B. Spoelstra|Spoelstra]] — established that the factory-scale product consisted of 85–90% α-pinene and approximately 4% Δ3-carene, against approximately 75% α-pinene and 15% carene in the [[1924]] trial samples. The fourfold reduction in carene content — from 15% to 4% — was identified as the chemical basis of the improved odour of the factory-scale product.

## The Pharmacopoeia and the Ontwerp-normaalblad V 597

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the factory-scale Atjeh-terpententine met all the requirements of the *Nederlandsche Pharmacopee* (Ed. V, [[1926]]) for *Oleum terebinthinae* — though the refractive index fell at the minimum limit. Against the draft *Ontwerp-normaalblad V 597* of the [[Hoofdcommissie voor de Normalisatie in Nederland]], the Atjeh-terpentijn met every requirement except the refractive index, which fell marginally below the proposed minimum of 1.469 (the sample values were 1.4677, 1.4680, 1.4680). The shortfall was attributed to the exceptionally high α-pinene content of the Atjeh product — 85–90%, against 60–70% in the *Pinus palustris*-derived American and *Pinus Pinaster*-derived French turpentines — which had the effect of letting the lower refractive index of α-pinene (1.4658) dominate the refractive index of the entire mixture. The Afdeeling Handelsmuseum accordingly submitted to the Hoofdcommissie a proposal to lower the draft minimum from 1.469 to 1.468, by which change the colonial product would fully meet the metropolitan standard.

## The European market reception

The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet]] records that the European market reception of the factory-scale Atjeh-terpentijn was investigated through a survey of the members of the *[[Vereeniging van Vernis- en Verffabrikanten en -Handelaren in Nederland]]* (V.V.V.F.). Eleven of the 28 members provided written evaluations, and the consensus was that the technical qualities of the new product were good — and that the odour had improved markedly since [[1924]] — but that the residual difference from American and French turpentine, combined with the very small price differential (about f 22 per 50 kg), left most members unwilling to switch. The general verdict was summed up by Spoor in the closing paragraph of the pamphlet: *Vastgesteld is intusschen, dat de technische eigenschappen van het tegenwoordige Baleq-product een ruimere toepassing in Europa niet meer in den weg behoeven te staan* — *It has, however, been established that the technical properties of the present Baleq product no longer stand in the way of a wider application in Europe*.

## Significance

Atjeh-terpentijn's significance in the historiography of colonial Aceh is as the principal colonial-era turpentine commodity of the Netherlands Indies — the product of the [[Baleq]] enterprise that, from [[1924]] to [[1930]] and beyond, attempted to develop a colonial turpentine industry capable of competing in the metropolitan market. The [[Atjeh-terpentijn (Spoor, 1930)|Atjeh-terpentijn pamphlet of 1930]] is the principal contemporary monograph on the subject, and its detailed production figures, chemical data, and survey responses together document the attempt — ultimately only partly successful — to develop a colonial turpentine industry capable of competing in the metropolitan market.

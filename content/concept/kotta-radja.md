**Kotta-Radja** — also *Kota Radja* / *Koeta Radja* / *Koetaradja* / *Kotja-Radja* / modern *Kutaradja* / *Banda Aceh* — was the Dutch colonial capital of the *gewest* [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], built on the site of the pre-colonial [[Kraton Atjeh|kraton]] of the sultan of Atjeh after the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the kraton of 24 January 1874]], and the seat of the Dutch *bestuurder* and *bevelhebber* of Atjeh from [[1874]] onward. In [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, Kota-Radja is also the site of the *geconcentreerde stelling Kotja-Radja* — the concentrated defensive perimeter — that [[Jan van Swieten|Generaal van Swieten]] established in [[1874]] with a garrison of 2500 men, the seat of the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* and the *Staf* (the military and civil staff from which Van Heutsz signs himself on the title page of the tract as *gewezen Chef van den Staf in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*), and the dateline of the principal Dutch state papers of the war — the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|Dagorder of 5 September 1879]] and the *Nota* of [[Scherer's Nota (19 April 1880)|19 April 1880]] among them. In the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, Kotta-Radja is the staff history's principal instance of the Dutch *opbouw* of Atjeh — the "nieuwe stad" built on the site of the captured kraton.

## The founding on the kraton site

The [[De Kleine Oosterling|Wijma school reader]] records the founding of Kotta-Radja as part of the aftermath of the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the kraton of 24 January 1874]]: "De jonge sultan was intusschen gestorven, en een andere werd niet benoemd. Onze regeering maakte bekend, dat nu Atjeh als veroverd land met onze bezittingen vereenigd werd." The Dutch government's declaration of Atjeh as *veroverd land* (conquered territory) and its *vereeniging* with the Dutch possessions was the formal legal act by which Atjeh was incorporated into the Netherlands East Indies, and the *kraton* site was transformed into the colonial capital.

The school reader records the *opbouw*: "Wat is Kotta-Radja? Een nieuwe stad, op de plek van den kraton gebouwd. De spoortrein brengt de reizigers in een groot kwartier daarheen. Dicht bij Kotta-Radja nadert de spoorweg de Atjeh-rivier. Aan de overzijde daarvan ziet men verscheidene nieuwe gebouwen en aan deze zijde de nieuwe sierlijke missigit. Onze regeering heeft die laten bouwen in plaats van de oude, welke door den oorlog vernield was."

## The colonial capital

The school reader records the *colonial capital* functions of Kotta-Radja: "Niet veel verder ligt de oude kraton, nu Kotta-Radja. Het dichte struikgewas, dat den kraton vroeger voor het oog verborg, is thans weggeruimd. Door een poort komt men in den kraton. Hier vertoont zich een vierkante ruimte. Een riviertje, waarover hier en daar een brug ligt, loopt er doorheen. Allerlei gebouwen zijn er reeds opgericht; het zijn de woningen van den bestuurder van Atjeh, van den bevelhebber der troepen en van de hoofd- en mindere officieren; en nog zijn er kazernes voor de soldaten en verder andere huizen."

The Kotta-Radja *kraton* was thus transformed into the Dutch *bestuurder*'s and *bevelhebber*'s residence, with *kazernes* for the *soldaten* and other *huizen* for the colonial administration. The school reader's emphasis on the *vierkante ruimte*, the *riviertje* and the *bruggen* frames Kotta-Radja as a planned colonial capital, built on the geometric pattern of the pre-colonial *kraton* but transformed into the seat of the Dutch *bestuurder* and *bevelhebber*.

## The Penajoeng extension

The school reader records the extension of Kotta-Radja toward [[Penajoeng|Penajoeng]]: "Een breede weg loopt van Kotta-Radja over de Atjeh-rivier naar Penajoeng. Ook hier kan het zeer levendig wezen. Er zijn talrijke woningen van officieren en soldaten, hospitalen, toko's en een uitgestrekt Chineesch kamp." The Kotta-Radja–Penajoeng *breede weg* was thus the principal Dutch road-axis extension of the colonial capital across the Atjeh-rivier, with *woningen*, *hospitalen*, *toko's* and a *Chineesch kamp*.

## Significance

Kotta-Radja is, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the staff history's principal instance of the Dutch *opbouw* of Atjeh — the *nieuwe stad* built on the site of the captured kraton, and the seat of the Dutch *bestuurder* and *bevelhebber* of Atjeh from [[1874]] onward. The school reader's emphasis on the *veroverd land* declaration, on the *nieuwe stad*'s planned layout, and on the Penajoeng extension frames Kotta-Radja as the staff history's principal instance of the colonial *opbouw* that the school reader sets against the *fanatiek* resistance of the Atjeh-oorlog. The colonial capital would remain the seat of the Dutch *bestuurder* and *bevelhebber* of Atjeh until the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]].

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893)

In [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *De onderwerping van Atjeh*, Kota-Radja is the seat of the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]], built on the coastal fringe of the [[Groot-Atjeh|vallei van Groot-Atjeh]] beside the [[Atjeh]]-river mouth.

### The 1874 concentrated stelling

The strategic foundation of Dutch Kota-Radja was the *geconcentreerde stelling Kotja-Radja* established by [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] in [[1874]]. The plan is recorded by Van Heutsz in his catalogue of the first governor's policy: *Deze liet 2500 man achter, om daarmede te bezetten en actief te verdedigen de geconcentreerde stelling Kotja-Radja en enkele posten ter verzekering van de veilige gemeenschap naar zee* (He left 2500 men behind, to occupy and actively defend the concentrated Kotja-Radja stelling and a few posts to secure safe communication with the sea). The figure is repeated by Van Heutsz twice in close succession: *Van Swieten toch liet zooals wij zagen 2500 man als bezetting voor Atjeh achter* (Van Swieten, as we saw, left 2500 men behind as the garrison for Atjeh). The concentrated stelling — the fortified perimeter around Kota-Radja — was the original Dutch scheme for the seat: a small, defensible, concentrated garrison from which the *Onderhoorigheden* were to be conciliated under *art. 5* of Van Swieten's [[Van Swieten's Instructie (22 April 1874)|Instructie]] of [[22 April]] [[1874]].

The concentrated stelling was abandoned in [[1875]] in favour of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Generaal Pel]]'s coastal-line plan — *in 1875, op voorstel van Generaal Pel, door den nieuw opgetreden landvoogd, Gouv. Gen. van Lansberge, de door van Swieten in overeenstemming met den Gouv. Gen. Loudon en den Minister van Koloniën Fransen van de Putte aangewezen weg geheel verlaten* (in 1875, on the proposal of Generaal Pel, the path indicated by van Swieten in agreement with Gouv. Gen. Loudon and the Minister of Colonies Fransen van de Putte was entirely abandoned by the newly-appointed governor-general van Lansberge). The 2500-man concentrated defence was replaced by a forty-seven-post coastal occupation that dispersed the garrison along the entire coast. The concentrated stelling was restored in [[1884]] by the [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]] — the withdrawal to *de geconcentreerde stelling van Groot-Atjeh* that returned Dutch policy, in territorial terms, to the Van Swieten perimeter around Kota-Radja.

### The Mesdjid Raja

The principal religious monument at Kota-Radja was the *groote Mesdjid Raja* — the Great Mosque — which G.-G. van Lansberge undertook to rebuild as a gesture of conciliation in the *verzoeningspolitiek* (conciliation-politics) of [[1877]]. Van Heutsz records the project: *den wederopbouw van de groote Mesdjid Raja bij Kota-Radja, waarvan men groote verwachtingen had* (the rebuilding of the great Mesdjid Raja near Kota-Radja, of which one had great expectations). The rebuilding was the religious-cultural accompaniment to the political conciliation: the *verzoeningspolitiek op Groot-Atjeh* that van Lansberge had prescribed in March [[1877]], and the installation of the sultan-family member [[Toeankoe Pangeran Hoesin]] at Kota Radja in December [[1877]]. The Mesdjid Raja stood at Kota-Radja in deliberate contrast to the *emmedji Indrapoeri* — the great mosque of [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]] in the interior — at which the war-party installed [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]] as sultan in defiance of the Dutch *Civiel Gouverneur* regime of [[1881]]–[[1883]].

### Seat of the Governor and the Staf

Kota-Radja was the seat of the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* — the governorship established by Van Swieten in [[1874]] and held, by [[1893]], by eleven successive governors catalogued by Van Heutsz: [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]], [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]], [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]], [[A. J. E. Diemont|Diemont]], [[K. van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]], [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]], [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], [[Demmeni|Demmeni]], Pompe van Meerdervoort, Deijkerhof, and [[Van Teijn|Van Teijn]]. It was also the seat of the *Staf* — the military and civil staff from which Van Heutsz signs his title page: *J. B. VAN HEUTSZ, Majoor der Infanterie gewezen Chef van den Staf in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*. The *Staf* was the governor's instrument of command, and the Chief of Staff (the *chef van den staf*) was, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the intellectual originator of strategic reversals: it was *majoor A. R. W. Geij van Pittius*, Chief of Staff under Van der Heijden, who in June [[1878]] *overtuigde den chef van den staf hem, dat men door expeditien naar de kuststaten het doel — het einde van den oorlog — niet kon bereiken, en dat alleen eene beslissing kon worden verkregen door in Groot-Atjeh zelf den vijand aan te tasten* (convinced the chief of staff that one could not reach the goal — the end of the war — through expeditions to the coastal states, and that a decision could only be obtained by attacking the enemy in Groot-Atjeh itself). Kota-Radja was thus not only the seat of the Governor but the seat of the staff-work through which the war's strategic directions were drawn.

The seat of the *Ned. autoriteit* at Kota-Radja is fixed in Van Heutsz's text as the place from which the Dutch claim to sovereignty was made: *de te Kota-Radja gevestigde Nederlandsche autoriteit* — the Netherlands authority established at Kota-Radja — was, in his argument, the political centre from which the *gezagsuitoefening* (exercise of authority) of the entire Atjehnese rijk was to radiate, and the place whose authority had to be made good against the rival authority of the [[Kemala|Dalam]] at [[Kemala]]. The [[Ruijssenaers|Heer Ruijssenaers]], appointed *assistent-resident van Groot-Atjeh* in medio [[1890]], was the principal civil-administrative officer at Kota-Radja in the closing years of the period.

### Datelines of official documents

Kota-Radja is the dateline of the principal Dutch state papers of the war. The [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|dagorder]] of [[5 September]] [[1879]] — by which [[K. van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] declared that *geheel Groot-Atjeh ten onder gebragt* was — is signed and dated: *KOTA-RADJA, 5 September 1879. De generaal-majoor, adjudant des Konings in buitengewonen dienst, Gouverneur tevens militair bevelhebber van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden. K. VAN DER HEUDEN* (The major-general, adjutant of the King in extraordinary service, Governor and at the same time military commander of Atjeh and Onderhoorigheden. K. VAN DER HEUDEN). The *Nota* of [[Scherer's Nota (19 April 1880)|19 April 1880]] — Van der Heijden's masterly memorandum on the proper form of government for Atjeh — is similarly dated: *Nota, gedagteekend: Kota-Radja, 19 April 1880* (Nota, dated: Kota-Radja, 19 April 1880). The installation of [[Toeankoe Pangeran Hoesin]] as a Dutch-recognised member of the sultan's family was performed at Kota Radja in December [[1877]]: *die in December 1877 te Kota Radja beëedigd werd* (who was sworn in at Kota Radja in December 1877). And the *autoriteiten van land- en zeemacht te Kota Radja* — the authorities of army and navy at Kota-Radja — were the joint military-civil recipients of the *Concentratie* orders of [[19 August]] [[1884]].

### Significance (Van Heutsz account)

Kota-Radja's significance in Van Heutsz's [[1893]] tract rests on its being the political and military counterweight to the rival seat at [[Kemala]] — the place whose authority had to be made good against the war-party's *Dalam*, and whose *geconcentreerde stelling* was the territorial expression of the Dutch claim to sovereignty over the rijk. Kota-Radja was the seat of the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* and of the eleven governors Van Heutsz catalogues from Van Swieten to Van Teijn; the site of the *geconcentreerde stelling Kotja-Radja* — the 2500-man concentrated defensive perimeter established by Van Swieten in [[1874]] and restored by the [[1884]] Concentratie, the strategic anchor of every Dutch policy for the rijk; the seat of the *Staf* — the military-civil staff through which the Governor's strategic directions were drawn, and from which Van Heutsz himself signs the title page of the [[1893]] tract; and the dateline of the principal Dutch state papers of the war — the dagorder of [[5 September]] [[1879]], the Nota of [[19 April]] [[1880]], and the succession of governor-by-governor proclamations and besluiten (decrees) by which Dutch policy for Atjeh was alternately imposed and revoked.

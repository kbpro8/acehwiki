**Panglima Sagi** was the title borne by the head of each of the three [[Sagi|Sagi]]'s — the XXII, XXVI, and XXV Moekims — into which [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] (Atjeh-proper) was politically divided, whose authority, exercised only in matters of common concern, gave the Atjehsche federation of [[Oelèëbalang|oelèëbalangs]] its loose overarching structure and whose collective action, in concert with the other high dignitaries, regulated the succession to the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the *Panglima Sagi* as the apex of the Groot-Atjehsche chieftaincy: the federation-head whose personality, like that of the Keutjhï, the Imeum, and the Oelèëbalang, determined the actual weight of the office.

## Election and character

Each *Sagi*, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "wees den invloedrijksten en machtigsten oelèëbalang uit haar midden als hoofd aan" — elected the most influential and powerful *oelèëbalang* from its midst as its head. The Panglima Sagi did not always possess territorial authority as an *oelèëbalang*; sometimes, as in the *Wadjo* custom of Celebes, "iemand uit een aanzienlijk, invloedrijk geslacht, doch zonder eigen gebied" — someone from a distinguished, influential lineage but without territory of his own — was chosen as federation-head. The most famous case was that of the Panglima Sagi of the XXII Moekims, who was "uit de machtige Polim-familie" verkozen — chosen from the powerful Polim family — although the Polim family did not originally possess the *oelèëbalang*-dignity, and who "alle oelèëbalangs uit dat gebied geheel aan zich ondergeschikt wist te maken" — managed to make all the *oelèëbalangs* of that territory entirely subordinate to himself.

## Authority and limits

The *Panglima Sagi* stood, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "buiten de inwendige aangelegenheden van ieder oelèëbalangschap" — outside the internal affairs of each *oelèëbalangschap* — and exercised authority only in matters involving common interests. The character of the office varied, like all Atjehsche offices, with the personality of the holder: "evenals bij Keutjhï, Imeum en Oelèëbalang alles afhing van de persoonlijkheid, die het ambt vervulde, zoo was dit ook het geval met den Panglima sagi."

## The Sultanate and the Sagi's

The three *Panglima Sagi*'s, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, "hebben gezamenlijk weten door te drijven, dat elke troonswisseling overeenkomstig de beslissing der vertegenwoordigers der drie sagi's en eenige andere hoogwaardigheidsbekleeders moest plaats hebben" — managed collectively to push through the principle that every succession to the throne had to take place in accordance with the decision of the representatives of the three Sagi's and certain other high dignitaries. The Sultanate was, by this means, placed under the joint guardianship of the three Sagi's, and the Sultan's authority was, in effect, dependent on their continued support.

## The colonial period

The Dutch administration, which "zooveel mogelijk intact liet" (left as intact as possible) the native administrative institutions of the Gouvernement, preserved the office of *Panglima Sagi* as part of the Groot-Atjehsche structure that served as the basis for the colonial reorganisation of Groot-Atjeh. The three *Sagi*'s — XXII, XXVI, and XXV Moekims — were retained as the principal territorial divisions of the *afdeeling Groot-Atjeh*, and the *Panglima Sagi*'s continued to exercise their traditional authority, under Dutch supervision, in matters of common concern.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**The Panglima sagi** — Acehnese *panglima sagi* — was, in the source's narrative, the leader of an Atjehnese *sagi* federation. The *panglima sagi* was "de voornaamste, invloedrijkste, of energiekste uléébalang" (the most prominent, influential, or energetic *uléebalang*) of the federation. The sultan's authority over the *panglima sagi*'s existed "alleen in naam" (in name only).

### The Snouck Hurgronje analysis

The source preserves the [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] analysis: "Enige uleebalangschappen vormden een federatie, de zgn. sagi, waarin de voornaamste, invloedrijkste, of energiekste uléébalang, panglima sagi geheten, de leiding had" (Several uléebalang territories formed a federation, the so-called sagi, in which the most prominent, influential, or energetic ulébalang, called the panglima sagi, had the leadership). The [[Panglima sagi|panglima sagi]] is, in the [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] analysis, the effective leader of the *sagi* federation.

### The sultan's nominal authority

The source records: "Het gezag van de Sultan over de panglima's sagi en de uléebalangs bestond alleen in naam" (The sultan's authority over the panglima's sagi and the uléebalangs existed only in name). The *panglima sagi*'s were thus, in the [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] analysis, the effective power-holders of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] — in contrast to the sultan whose authority was nominal.

### Significance

The [[Panglima sagi|panglima sagi]] is, in the [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] analysis preserved by the source, the effective leader of an Atjehnese *sagi* federation. The [[1892]] advice to ignore the sultan and strike the *uléebalang*s — including the *panglima sagi*'s — was based on this analysis.

## Account from Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — Panglima Sagi in 1896

**Panglima Sagi** — also rendered in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* as *sagihoofd* (sagi head) — was the hereditary [[Achehnese]] leadership title borne by the chief of a [[sagi]], the major territorial federation of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The panglima sagi was at once military commander, political spokesman and adat-law arbiter of his sagi; he represented the sagi in dealings with the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan]] and, after [[1873]], with the [[Dutch]] colonial government. The two principal panglima sagi of the [[XXVI Moekims]] named by Kruisheer are [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] (the senior *sagihoofd*) and [[Toekoe Djohan Lampasei]] (the *tweede sagihoofd*, "second sagi head"), who together with [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] formed the three-man leadership for clearing the area east of the [[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]] in early [[1896]]. Each panglima sagi ranked above the [[hoeloebalang]]s of individual moekims within his sagi but lacked authority over moekims of other sagis.

### Etymology and Meaning

The title combines two [[Achehnese]] words: *panglima* (commander), itself from Sanskrit *paranīpati* via Malay, and *sagi* (section). A *panglima sagi* is therefore literally a "commander of a section". The Dutch text renders the office as *sagi-hoofd* (sagi-head), and Kruisheer uses the two terms interchangeably. The title should not be confused with [[Panglima Prang Besar]] ("Great War Commander"), which was an extraordinary Dutch-created office bestowed on [[Teuku Umar]] in [[1893]]; nor with the more generic [[panglima]] (war commander), which could apply to any military leader.

### Historical Context of 1896

In the first semester of [[1896]] the office of panglima sagi was the pivot on which the defection of entire moekims turned. [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] and [[Toekoe Djohan Lampasei]] as *sagihoofden* of the [[XXVI Moekims]] together decided whether the XXVI Moekims would stand with the [[Dutch]] or with [[Teuku Umar]]. After it became clear that the allies in the XXVI Moekims "were not as useful as T. Djohan" (Umar), Kruisheer records that "the leadership of the affairs in that sagi was also entrusted to him — a measure not appreciated by the *adathoofden* (customary chiefs) of the XXVI Moekims". The panglima sagi therefore sat at the apex of the *adat* (customary) hierarchy, in tension both with the religious hierarchy of the [[oelama]]s and with any external authority (Dutch or sultanate) that attempted to bypass him.

### Examples in the Source

Kruisheer's most explicit reference to the office is his identification of [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] as "*Panglima sagi der XXVI Moekims*; one of the three-man leadership of the XXVI Moekims" alongside [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Toekoe Djohan Lampasei]]. The latter is described as the "*tweede sagihoofd*" of the XXVI Moekims, who "leedde kalm van den sabil Allah in Masdjid Lepong" — calmly preached the jihad in the mosque of [[Lepong]]. The role of these two panglima sagi in the spring of [[1896]] was to swing the XXVI Moekims behind Umar after his [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], forcing the Dutch evacuation of [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]] and the subsequent [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] against Umar's stelling at [[Lam-Pisang]].

### Significance of the Panglima Sagi in 1896

The panglima sagi embodies the confederal character of the [[Aceh Sultanate]]: the sultan's writ ran to a sagi only through its panglima sagi, and the Dutch could win or lose a sagi only by winning or losing its *sagihoofd*. The defection of the two panglima sagi of the XXVI Moekims in the spring of [[1896]] was therefore a strategic event of the first order, transforming Umar's personal defection into a regional insurrection. Kruisheer's careful recording of the names and offices of the *sagihoofden* reflects their importance as the hinge on which the entire Dutch position in Groot-Atjeh turned.

**The Muslemin** — also written *Moeslemin*, *Muslimin* — were those Atjehers who openly took part in the holy war against the Dutch, so called after the proclamation of the [[Restoration of the Sultanate|restoration of the Sultanate]] at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] in [[1885]]–[[1886]]. The term, designating those who had formally committed themselves to the resistance under the Sultan's authority and the religious leadership of [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and his successor [[Tengkoe Mohamed Amin|Tengkoe Mohamed Amin]], became the standard Dutch designation for the organised Atjehese resistance in the middle and late phases of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The Proclamation

In the period of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] (1884–1886), [[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]], guardian and father-in-law of the youthful pretender [[Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed|Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed]], had the pretender declared of age and proclaimed and hailed as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]]. He further wrote everywhere that the Hollanders had been driven back, that the war would be vigorously continued, and, in concert with the religious leader [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] — whose influence ever increased — sent the principal leaders to the coastal statelets to stir up the population, to embroil the well-disposed, to destroy Dutch influence, to call all to the holy war against the Dutch, and to collect money and men.

Those who openly took part in this struggle were henceforth called *Muslemin*.

## The Oath against the Dutch

The principal leaders of the Atjehese resistance bound themselves afresh under oath against the Dutch, among them the later Dutch "friends" [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] and [[Nja Makam|Nja Makam]]. The oath was the formal renewal of the Atjehese resistance after the Dutch withdrawal into the concentrated *stelling* around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. The Muslemin, as those who had taken the oath, were the organised body of the Atjehese resistance — as distinct from the general population, which might or might not actively participate.

## The Muslemin under Tengkoe Mohamed Amin

Under [[Tengkoe Mohamed Amin|Tengkoe Mohamed Amin]] — who succeeded his father [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] as head of the religious resistance in [[January 1892|January]] [[1892]] and was appointed Kadhi by the Sultan in [[1894]] — the Muslemin continued the encirclement of the Dutch line, the ambushes of Dutch patrols, the smuggling of weapons and supplies, and the collection of money and recruits from the coastal statelets. The Muslemin's exactions, extortions, and acts of violence against the population outside the Dutch line — which extended to the [[4 and 6 Moekims|4 and 6 Moekims]] — furnished the occasion for [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]]'s opposition to them, and so for his submission to the Dutch and his arming as *[[Panglima Prang Besar|Panglima Prang Besar]]*.

## The Conflict with Toekoe Oemar

The conflict between the Muslemin under Tengkoe Mohamed Amin and the West Coast chieftain Toekoe Oemar — the former the head of the religious party, the latter the powerful chieftain whose submission to the Dutch was conditioned on his being armed to suppress the Muslemin — was the internal cleavage in the Atjehese resistance that the Dutch sought to exploit. The exploitation, however, would lead to the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]] and the final crisis of the war.

## Kruijt's Terminology

Kruijt uses *Muslemin* as the standard Dutch designation for the organised Atjehese resistance throughout his brochure. The term is contrasted with "*kwadwilligen*" (malefactors) — the term favoured by [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe van Meerdervoort]], who insisted that the Dutch had no enemy before them in Atjeh but only malefactors, totalling at most 800 men. The contrast between *Muslemin* — designating an organised religious-military body — and *kwadwilligen* — designating a few individual malefactors — is the contrast between the realistic assessment of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and the optimistic assessment of Pompe.

## Significance

The Muslemin are the organised body of the Atjehese resistance in the middle and late phases of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Their formal constitution after the proclamation of the Sultanate at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] in [[1885]]–[[1886]], their oath against the Dutch, their religious leadership under [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and [[Tengkoe Mohamed Amin|Tengkoe Mohamed Amin]], and their encirclement of the Dutch line together mark the institutionalisation of the Atjehese resistance that would sustain it through the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] and the governorships of [[Demmeni|Demmeni]], [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]], [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]], and [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]. The Dutch's eventual suppression of the Muslemin — under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]], on the advice of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — would be the principal means by which the war was brought to its conclusion.

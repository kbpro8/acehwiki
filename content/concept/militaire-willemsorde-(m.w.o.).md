**The Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)** — the Military Order of William, the highest Dutch military decoration — was the decoration most prized by the soldiers of the [[K.N.I.L.]] and most proudly worn in the *[[negorij]]* or *[[kampong]]* of the Ambonese and Menadonese soldiers. Established by King Willem I in [[1815]] for "Moed, Beleid en Trouw" (Courage, Judgment and Loyalty) in the field, the order had four classes — *Ridder 4e klas*, *Ridder 3e klas*, *Commandeur*, *Grootkruis* — of which the 4e and 3e klassen were the decorations of the rank-and-file soldaat and the onderofficier. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the M.W.O. is the central decoration of the book's moral economy: the *Ridders M.W.O.* are the *trots* (pride) of their *negorij* and the *slippendragers* at the [[Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)|funeral of Dominee Thenu]].

## The four classes and the riddersoldij

The M.W.O. had four classes. The 4e and 3e klassen — *Ridder* ranks — were the decorations of the rank-and-file soldaat and the onderofficier; the Commandeurskruis and Grootkruis were for officers. The book's named M.W.O. ridders are mostly 3e and 4e klas: [[Salawono]] (3e klas, raised from 4e), [[Nussy]] (3e klas), [[Schmidt]] (3e klas), and the four *slippendragers* at Thenu's funeral ([[Salawono]] 3e kl., [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]], [[Pattipuluhu]]).

The Ridder M.W.O. also received a *riddersoldij* — an additional pay — and in [[1869]] it was bepaald that he would wear the decoration at the same lint (ribbon) as the higher classes. The book notes the early M.W.O. history: in [[1865]] the M.W.O. 4e klas was verleend to the Ambonese *le luitenant* [[Joseph Rakarias]] "ter vervanging van de hem in [[1850]] verleende" *Medaille voor Moed en Trouw* — that is, as a replacement for the Moed en Trouw he had been given earlier, when the M.W.O. was extended to indigenous soldiers.

## The earliest indigenous ridders

The book notes that the M.W.O. was opened to indigenous soldiers in the mid-19th century. "Nu werd dan ook in [[1865]] de M.W.O. 4e klas verleend aan de Ambonese le luitenant [[Joseph Rakarias]], ter vervanging van de hem in [[1850]] verleende" *Medaille voor Moed en Trouw*. The replacement was a recognition that the earlier medal had been inadequate to the act it recognised. After [[1865]] the M.W.O. became the standard decoration for indigenous soldiers who had shown *Moed, Beleid en Trouw* in the field. "Vervolgens komt in [[1867]] de …" — the book lists a sequence of 19th-century indigenous ridders, including the Bantam-M.W.O. and the 1828 and 1839 decorations.

## The Aceh War ridders

The bulk of the book's M.W.O. ridders earned their decorations in the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and the related colonial pacifications of the early 20th century. [[Salawono]] earned his 4e klas at the [[Storming of Limboer (22 June 1903)|storming of Limboer]] in [[Djambi]] on [[22 June]] [[1903]] and his 3e klas on [[Celebes]] for the *Verovering van de Alla-stellingen (Celebes, 1905-1908)|verovering van de Alla-stellingen]]. [[Nussy]] earned his 3e klas for the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]-[[1911]]. [[Schmidt]] received the M.W.O. 3e klas for [[1911]] (and the *[[Eresabel]]* for [[1910]]).

## The slippendragers at Thenu's funeral

When [[Dominee Thenu]] died on [[11 May]] [[1937]], the four *slippendragers* at his funeral were "vier oude, gepensioneerde Marechaussees, alle vier ridders M.W.O.: [[Salawono]] (3e kl.), [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]] en [[Pattipuluhu]]." The choice of four M.W.O. ridders to carry the dominee's bier is the book's most resonant image of the moral economy of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]: the men who had been decorated for courage under fire laid their decorations, in effect, at the feet of the man who had blessed them.

## The M.W.O. and the *Ridders M.W.O.* in the negorij

"Ridders [[Militaire Willemsorde (M.W.O.)|M.W.O.]], dragers van de [[Medaille voor Moed en Trouw]] en van het [[Kroontje (Eervolle Vermelding)|Kroontje]] (de Eervolle Vermelding), waren de trots van hun *[[kampong]]* of *[[negorij]]*." The M.W.O. Ridder was the pride of his home community: the *negorij* tracked his wedervaren through the gehele Archipel, and his decoration was worn at the *Koninginnedag* rondgang. The 1938 incident at [[Ambarawa]] — in which the author congratulated a retired Javanese fusilier on 30 years Ridder M.W.O. on behalf of the *Legercommandant* — is the book's example of the decoration's lifelong prestige.

## Significance

The M.W.O. is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the central decoration of the book's moral economy. The *Ridders M.W.O.* are the *trots* of their *negorij*, the *slippendragers* at the dominee's funeral, and the figures through whom Dames makes his argument about the colonial army's recognition of courage. The book's quiet point is that the M.W.O. was the colonial army's most reliable instrument for recognising the actual military value of its Ambonese soldiers — a value the formal classification system could not measure (cf. [[Tanamal]] and [[Taris]]).

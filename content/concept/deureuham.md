**Deureuham** (Gayo: *deram*, from Arabic/Persian *derham*/*dirham*) was the small, thin, round Atjèhnese **gold coin** weighing approximately 9 grains (0.583 gram), roughly struck with Arabic characters within a pearl border, without year. Its circulation value was ¼ *pardouw* (later ¼ Spanish dollar). The Sultans repeatedly reduced the gold weight and fineness, and much false gold was in circulation.

## Minting

The *deureuham* was minted by Sultans from **Ala ad-din Riajat Sjah** (± [[1588]]–[[1604]]) to **Ala ad-din Djohan Sjah** ([[1735]]–[[1760]]). John Davis, the English seafarer who spied on the Dutch ships that arrived at Atjèh in June [[1599]], left a description of the various Atjèhnese coins in circulation, including the *deureuham* (which he called the *gouden mas*).

## Circulation value

The circulation value of the *deureuham* was ¼ *pardouw* (later ¼ Spanish dollar). The Sultans repeatedly reduced the gold weight and fineness of the coin, and much false gold was in circulation. The coin was mostly melted down for golden ornaments after Spanish dollars became common.

## Related coinage

The *deureuham* was part of a system of Atjèhnese coinage that included:
- **Keuëh** — limestone or tin coin struck for market traffic, with no fixed value
- **Pardouw** — silver coin (from Portuguese *pardao*), struck at Goa for trade with the Archipelago
- **Djampaj** — Indian rupee (Hindostan-ropij), used in Pidië and the Gayo lands

## Gayo terminology

In the Gayo lands, the *deureuham* was called *deram*, and was used alongside the *koepang* and *boesö* — silver coins originally struck at Arkat (Madras) by the English East India Company in [[1758]]–[[1759]].

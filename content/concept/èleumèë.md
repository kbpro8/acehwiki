**Èleumèë** was the Atjèhnese term for "heathen/pagan science" — the esoteric knowledge of formulas and incantations used by *pawang*'s (master fishermen and hunters) to control sea and forest spirits. Used in fishery and hunting, and also in *peukeunong* (magic charms), *èleumèë* was distinguished from *èleumèë kitab* (religious science based on Arabic theological works).

## Nature

*Èleumèë* was the pre-Islamic, "heathen" or "pagan" esoteric knowledge that survived alongside Islamic learning, used by specialists (*pawang*'s) to deal with the spirit world. Passed down through apprenticeship, *èleumèë* was a key part of the *pawang*'s qualification.

## Use in fishery

In fishery, the *pawang* (master fisherman) used *èleumèë* to control sea spirits. The *pawang* possessed formulas and incantations that could influence the spirits believed to inhabit the sea, ensuring good catches and protection from danger.

## Use in hunting

In hunting, the *pawang* (leader of the hunt, expert in forest sciences) used *èleumèë* to ask the invisible lord of wood and field for hunting permission. Variants included: *pawang roesa* (deer), *pawang badeuë*, *pawang gadjah* (elephant), *pawang oenöë*, *pawang boeja* (crocodile), *pawang oeteuën/rimba* (forest).

## Peukeunong

The *èleumèë* was also used in *peukeunong* — magic charms (e.g., a "bespoken" lemon) placed in someone's net or fuik to disrupt it. Fear of *peukeunong* maintained the "mild adat" of sharing catch with bystanders (*oereuëng meudarat*).

## Related forms

Specialised forms of *èleumèë* included:
- *Èleumèë djhoeng* — hanging charms
- *Èleumèë teumanöm* — burying charms
- *Èleumèë koelat* — mushroom magic (only in XXII Moekim's, notorious in Lamteuba)
- *Èleumèë keubaj* — invulnerability
- *Èleumèë panika* — cannot be hit
- *Èleumèë sangkilat* — iron-softening
- *Èleumèë sihé* — magic/sorcery (from Arabic *sihir*)

**Pangoeléé hareukat** — "the master of all breadwinning" — was the Atjehnese honorific for the rice field, expressing its standing as the foundation of livelihood.

## In the condemnation of sale

The phrase is preserved in the Atjehnese censure of the sale of inherited rice fields. As [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] wrote in *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]*, Vol. I, p. 312, quoted by [[D. W. N. de Boer]], the man who commits *[[Peupia oemòng|peupia oemòng]]* — the breaking up of his rice field — thereby ruins the *pangoeléé hareukat*, the master of all breadwinning. The dictum supplies the reason why complete alienation of a rice field counted as a disgrace and why *[[Poeblee|poebléë]]* of rice fields was practically unknown in [[Aceh|Atjeh]].

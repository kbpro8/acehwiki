**Prang sabi** (Atjèhnese, from the Arabic *fi sabil Allah*, "in the path of God"; English "holy war") is the Atjèhnese term for the religious duty, incumbent on every Muslim in time of conflict with the infidel, of taking up arms against the *[[kaphé|kaphé]]* (unbeliever) — a duty that, in Atjèh, attained a prominence and an intensity "to a higher degree inherent in the Acehnese than in most of their co-religionists elsewhere, who, taught by experience, have set it aside as no longer of this time." The doctrine is the central religious concept of Atjèhnese resistance — the thread that runs from the [[Aceh War|Atjèh War]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]] through the [[Bakóngan uprising (1925–1927)|Bakóngan uprising]] and the [[Lhóng action (1933)|Lhóng action of 1933]] to the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion]] of [[1942]] — and the reason the colonial army could not, by arms alone, end the resistance.

## The hikajat prang sabi

The religious duty of *prang sabi* was inculcated through the *[[hikajat prang sabi|hikajat prang sabi]]* — the prose-poetic "incitement to holy war so notorious from the Aceh War period." Piekaar records a characteristic verse: "If we do not reconcile with the unbelieving Dutchmen, they are our hereditary enemies for all time." The text, "published by Damsté and translated in the *Bijdragen tot de taal-, land- en volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië* (volume 84, p. 545 ff.), had apparently lost none of its old persuasive power and still formed the primary means to incite the believers to become fighters on God's path." Its recitation was a capital instrument of religious mobilisation, and was accordingly forbidden by the Japanese from [[1942]].

## The doctrine: scope and intensity

For the Atjèhnese, *prang sabi* was "a pious duty imposed by Islamic law." [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]] of [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] — the great resistance leader — considered "the acceptance of the authority of the infidel (Atj. *[[kaphé|kaphé]]*) … an abomination and the conduct of the holy war (Atj. *prang sabi*) a religious duty." The fighter who fell in its performance became a *[[sjahid]]* (Arabic *shahīd*, "witness of faith"): the badge of honour that the Tiriō family and their followers claimed in each generation.

G. W. T. Dames, in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, registers the doctrinal nuance: "Het is wel volgens de Koran aan te tonen, dat er in de hele Atjeh-oorlog geen sprake is geweest van een werkelijke heilige oorlog" — by the Koran it can be shown that there was no true holy war in the whole Aceh War — "maar Tgk. Sjech Saman di Tiri was wel van die mening geweest." The distinction is between the doctrinal question of whether the war as a whole qualified as *prang sabi* and the lived conviction of the Tiriō family and their followers that it did.

## The doctrine in action: the refusal of submission

The doctrine made the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō bende]] uniquely hard to break. When, at [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]]'s request, the three *voornaamste Atjehse hoofden* — two *[[Toeankoe Machmoed Radja Keumala|Sultans-family members]]* and *[[Panglima Polem|Panglima Prang Polim]]* — wrote urging submission, the Teungkoes "bespraken de brief, doch besloten tot het einde te vechten; hen zou geschieden naar Allah's wil." The religious resisters would not accept the authority of the Sultan's former army commander to override their doctrine of *prang sabi*: political authority had no standing against religious conviction.

When the widow of *[[Teungkoe Asan Oeleë Toetooë]]* went into the bergen to urge the young *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* to submit, the boy refused: "Zijn Vader was als *geloofsgetuige* gevallen, hij zou in diens voetstappen treden. Allah's wil zou geschieden." The doctrine of *prang sabi* made submission not just dishonourable but sinful: the boy's father had fallen as a *geloofsgetuige*, and the boy would follow in his footsteps.

## The sjahid and the paradijs

The doctrine of *prang sabi* is paired with that of *[[sjahid]]* — the martyr of the faith — and the *paradijs* (paradise). When *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* was killed on [[3 December]] [[1911]], the book frames it: "Hier had Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at het lot van zijn Vader, zijn ooms en zovele andere verwanten gedeeld; hij was *sjahid*, *geloofsmartelaar in de heilige oorlog* geworden."

The widow of *[[Teungkoe Asan Oeleë Toetooë]]* put the doctrine to [[Schmidt|luitenant Schmidt]] directly: her husband "in de heilige strijd was gevallen door zijn eigen vrije wil; u was slechts het werktuig in Allah's hand, dat hem het *paradijs* deelachtig deed worden." Schmidt, on this doctrine, was merely the instrument in Allah's hand by which her husband had been granted the paradijs. The widow did not hold Schmidt responsible; the doctrine absorbed the responsibility and made the death a gift.

## The relics at Tangsé

When the corpse of [[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]] was carried out to [[Tangsé]], "de bevolking van Tangsé liep uit naar de begrafenis. Het verband en de doeken, waarin het lijk was gewikkeld, werden in honderden stukjes gescheurd en meegenomen als heilige reliquiën." The tearing of the relics is the book's quiet acknowledgement that the boy's death, far from extinguishing the doctrine, made it a permanent feature of the country's religious memory. The *sjahid*'s relics would protect their bearers; the doctrine of *prang sabi* had passed from the living resisters into the material culture of the bevolking.

## Prang sabi and the Japanese invasion

In [[1942]] the duty of *prang sabi* was reawakened across Atjèh. The Japanese occupation was hailed with "*kaphé ploeëng, daja taniOng*" — "the unbelievers have fled, the anti-Christ has arrived." Yet the Japanese, "completely unfamiliar with the country and people … would come into conflict with the same Atjèhnese religious fanaticism that had been their ally in driving out the Dutch infidels." Already in [[August]] [[1942]] a recitation of the *hikajat prang sabi* in the subdistrict Lhó' Soekón triggered the first serious encounter between Japanese authority and Atjèhnese religious fanaticism — the precursor of the [[Bajoe|Bajoe]] rising suppressed in blood in [[November]] [[1942]]. The occupation regime repeatedly attempted to suppress the *prang sabi* idea; the *hikajat prang sabi* was banned from 1942; but "the Acehnese nature was not denying itself."

## Significance

*Prang sabi* is the central religious concept of Atjèhnese resistance. It explains why the colonial army could not, by arms alone, end the resistance: it had to wait for the last heir of the doctrine to fall. The generational arc of the Tiriō family — from [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]] to the boy [[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]], an infant when his father fell in [[1896]] and the last leader of the bende when he died in [[1911]] at fifteen — is the human face of the doctrine. The tearing of the relics at [[Tangsé]] is the book's quiet acknowledgement that the doctrine, far from being extinguished by the killing of its last heir, was made permanent in the country's religious memory.

## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].

**Prang Sabil** — Acehnese: *prang sabil*, "holy war" — is the Atjehnese doctrine of religious war against the infidel invader, invoked in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]-period parliamentary debates principally by [[Rutgers van Rozenburg|Rutgers van Rozenburg]] (lib.) in his [[1904]] defence of the conduct of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The *prang sabil* doctrine was, in the [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]]-[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] reading, the religious frame within which the Atjehnese resistance was organised; its neutralisation was, accordingly, a principal political aim of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]].

### The Rutgers van Rozenburg reference

In the [[1904]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate on the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]] [[Rutgers van Rozenburg|Rutgers van Rozenburg]] (lib.) defended even the cutting-in-two of slain Atjehnese as a means to disarm the *prang sabil* preaching of the Atjehnese priests:

> "Wanneer een officier, een hoofdofficier of zelfs de gouverneur-generaal, om aan de Atjehse priesters hun voornaamste wapen bij het prediken van de prang sabil (heilige oorlog) uit de hand te slaan, last geeft gesneuvelde Atjehers in tweeën te deelen, ten einde de overlevenden te doen inzien, dat zij met het vechten vóór hun priesters en tégen de kompanie nog niet zeker zijn van hun entree in de Turkse hemel, ja dan is het te betreuren ... dat het daartoe komen moest, doch ... dan is dit niet alleen verschoonbaar en misschien gewettigd, maar kan het door 's lands belang en nooddwang geboden, kan het zelfs plicht zijn".

The *prang sabil* doctrine is, in this argument, the principal religious weapon of the Atjehnese resistance; the cutting-in-two of slain Atjehnese is the brutal counter-measure by which the doctrine's hold on the Atjehnese imagination is broken.

### The Idenburg-Snouck Hurgronje reading

[[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] (a.r.), minister of Colonies — following the argument of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — maintained in the same [[1904]] debate that Islam forbade the faithful to submit to any authority that had not proven its "onwrikbare overmacht" — unshakeable superiority. The *prang sabil* doctrine was, on this reading, the religious expression of this refusal. The women and children who sacrificed themselves during the [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] expedition had done so under the influence of religious fanaticism: [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], it was argued, had made the futility of the struggle clear in advance.

### Significance

The *prang sabil* doctrine is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the religious frame within which the Atjehnese resistance was organised. Its neutralisation — either by the brutal counter-measures advocated by [[Rutgers van Rozenburg|Rutgers van Rozenburg]] or by the demonstrative "overmacht" advocated by [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]]-[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] — was a principal political aim of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]].

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the *prang sabil* as religious-political apparatus

[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 tract]] treats the *sabil* — *sabililah* in the full Arabic form, in the Acehnese war-formula *prang sabil* ("war of the sabil"), in Van Heutsz's tract also written *sabil* and rendered in Dutch as the *heilige oorlog* — as the conceptual hinge on which the religious-political apparatus of the Acehnese resistance turns. Invoked from the Koran by [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tengkoe di Tirou]] — the *Sjech Saman di Tiro* who, after the [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]] of [[1884]], gradually placed himself at the head of the [[Groot-Atjeh]] resistance — the *prang sabil* supplied the religious-political apparatus by which the war-party sustained the resistance after the [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heijden]] conquests of [[1878]]–[[1879]] had been reversed and the Koran's injunction against submission to an unbeliever became, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the religious predicate the post-[[1884]] *cajoleering*-decade had failed to overcome.

### Tengkoe di Tirou's appointment as *gevolmachtigde van den sultan*

The *prang sabil* is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the religious office Tengkoe di Tirou came to exercise after the [[1884]] Concentratie. The institutional form of the office was Tengkoe di Tirou's appointment as *gevolmachtigde van den sultan* — plenipotentiary of the sultan — for "*alle zaken den godsdienst betreffende*" — all matters touching religion — which included the *prang sabil*. Van Heutsz records the appointment on page 97 of the 1893 tract:

> "Na onze concentratie is Tengkoe di Tirou (ik bedoel hier Sjech Saman, niet de eerste Tengkoe di Tirou-Sjeeh Abdoel Salem bin Djaäli, opvolger van Sjech Marabahan), zooals men weet er door zijne energie en onvermoeide werkzaamheid in geslaagd, om zich geleidelijk aan 't hoofd van het gansche verzet op Groot-Atjeh te stellen en de meeste bendehoofden aan zijne algemeene leiding te doen gehoorzamen, terwijl zijne daarop gevolgde aanstelling tot gevolmachtigde van den sultan om alle zaken den godsdienst betreffende, ook de prang sabil dus, in diens naam te regelen, hem tevens als 't ware een wereldlijke vlag in handen gaf, vooral tegenover ons".

The appointment is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, doubly significant. First, it places the *prang sabil* — the conduct of the holy war — under the religious authority of a single *gevolmachtigde* whose authority derived from the sultan and whose office was, in form, a religious and not a territorial one. Second, the appointment gave Tengkoe di Tirou what Van Heutsz calls a *wereldlijke vlag* — a worldly flag — against the Dutch: the religious authority to command the resistance was, in the Acehnese constitutional order, also a political authority, and the *prang sabil* was the form in which the two converged. The post-[[1884]] resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh]] was, in this sense, a resistance unified under the *prang sabil*'s religious-political authority, and Tengkoe di Tirou was its *gevolmachtigde*.

### The Koranic justification: the *geloovige* against the *ongeloovige*

The *prang sabil* is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the religious predicate sustained by the Koran's own injunction against submission to an unbeliever. Van Heutsz records the invocation on page 40 of the 1893 tract, in the catalogue of the post-[[1884]] *cajoleering*-decade's failures:

> "in weerwil, dat vooral Tengkoe di Tirou met den koran in de hand de geloovigen met succes aanspoorde tot volharding in den strijd tegen den ongeloovigen indringer, vooral nu deze allerwegen blijk had gegeven van zwakte en onmacht; in weerwil, dat — wat de Regeering toch wel weten zal — de koran zelf voorschrijft, dat een geloovige zich nooit anders aan een ongeloovige mag onderwerpen, dan genoodzaakt door dwang".

The invocation is doubly grounded. First, Tengkoe di Tirou, *met den koran in de hand* — with the [[Koran]] in hand — exhorted the *geloovigen* — the faithful — to *volharding in den strijd tegen den ongeloovigen indringer* — perseverance in the struggle against the unbeliever invader — with *succes*, because the Dutch had everywhere given evidence of *zwakte en onmacht* — weakness and impotence. Second, the *koran zelf* — the Koran itself — prescribes that a *geloovige* — a believer — may never submit to an *ongeloovige* — an unbeliever — except when *genoodzaakt door dwang* — compelled by force. The *prang sabil* is, in this double grounding, both an active invocation (the *geloovigen* are exhorted to persevere) and a passive prohibition (the *geloovige* may not submit): the war is, in the Acehnese *ulama*'s reading, both a duty of perseverance and a prohibition of submission, and the two together constitute the religious-political apparatus by which the resistance was sustained.

The Dutch response, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, must therefore be the operational counter-predicate: the *dwang* — compulsion — that the Koran itself names as the only legitimate ground of submission. The *dwang* is, in Van Heutsz's prescription, the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]-cum-closure that would, by cutting the *toevoer* of rice, arms, and opium to the interior, produce the *nood* — the necessity — that the Koran itself authorises as the ground of submission. The *prang sabil*'s religious-political authority is, in this sense, the predicate the *scheepvaartregeling*-cum-closure was designed to overcome: not by religious counter-argument but by the operational *dwang* the Koran itself recognises — the same "onwrikbare overmacht" (unshakeable superiority) Idenburg would invoke in the [[1904]] Tweede Kamer debate.

### Religious authority and the unity of the resistance

The *prang sabil*'s authority is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the unifying religious office that held the post-[[1884]] Groot-Atjeh resistance together under a single command. The *bendehoofden* — the band-leaders — who commanded the *onze linie omringende sagi's en moekims* — the Sagi's and mukims surrounding our line — were, in Van Heutsz's catalogue on page 97, drawn from the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]], [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]], [[XXV Moekims|XXV]] and [[VI Moekims|VI]] Moekims, and were unified by the *algemeene leiding* — general leadership — of Tengkoe di Tirou. The unity was, however, a religious unity, not a territorial one: the *bendehoofden* obeyed Tengkoe di Tirou as the sultan's *gevolmachtigde* for "*alle zaken den godsdienst betreffende, ook de prang sabil dus*", and the *prang sabil* was the form in which their obedience was constituted.

The unity was, in Van Heutsz's narrative, broken by Tengkoe di Tirou's over-reach into the worldly authority of the *panglima sagi XXVI*. Van Heutsz records the breaking on page 98:

> "Na den dood van Tengkoe di Tirou, die plotseling overleed, niet lang nadat hij zich had laten verleiden om, sterk als hij zich voelde, in te grijpen in het wereldlijk gezag van den panglima sagi XXVI, door het gewapenderhand steunen van het weerspannige hoofd van Boengtjala tegen diens wettig moekimhoofd T. Atoe en zijn panglima sagi T. Lamreng, was de algemeene band verbroken, en moeten de verschillende bendehoofden sedert ieder voor zich handelen".

The *prang sabil*'s religious unity was, in this over-reach, transmuted into a worldly intervention — the armed support of the *weerspannige hoofd* of [[Boengtjala]] against his *wettig moekimhoofd* — and the *algemeene band* of the resistance was broken. The post-Tengkoe-di-Tirou resistance was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the fragmented form the religious-political apparatus of the *prang sabil* could no longer sustain.

### The *fanatieken* and the *priesters*

The *prang sabil*'s operational form, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, was the *fanatieken* — the fanatics — who were *opgezweept door priesters* — driven on by priests — and formed into the *benden* — bands — that attacked the Dutch line. Van Heutsz records the form on page 97: "*Het is een samenraapsel van allerlei gelukzoekers, menschen zonder vast verblijf of werk, voorts fanatieken, die zich door priesters laten opzweepen en ook doet hier en daar de gewone bevolking mede*". And on page 63: "*Ontstaat ergens verzet, dan zijn het niet de wettige hoofden met de kustbevolking, maar fanatieken, opgezweept door priesters en tot eene bende gevormd*". The *fanatieken* were, in this diagnosis, the operational form the *prang sabil* took on the coast: not the *wettige hoofden* with the *kustbevolking* but the *fanatieken* the *priesters* had *opgezweept* and formed into *benden*.

The Dutch response, in Van Heutsz's prescription, was therefore to separate the *fanatieken* from the *wettige hoofden*: the *scheepvaartregeling*-cum-closure was to coerce the *wettige hoofden* into co-operation against the *fanatieken*, by making the *hoofden*'s revenues dependent on Dutch-controlled trade. The *prang sabil*'s religious-political authority was, in this sense, the predicate the *scheepvaartregeling*-cum-closure was designed to dissolve: not by religious counter-argument but by the fiscal leverage that would convert the *wettige hoofden* from the *fanatieken*'s passive supporters into their active opponents — the political complement of the religious *dwang* the Koran itself authorises.

### Significance of the Van Heutsz account

The *sabil*'s significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record, in Van Heutsz's 1893 diagnosis, rests on four things: it was the Islamic concept of holy war by which the Acehnese *ulama*, led by Tengkoe di Tirou after the [[1884]] Concentratie, rallied the *geloovigen* against the Dutch "*ongeloovigen indringer*" — and so the religious-political apparatus by which the post-[[1884]] resistance was unified under a single religious command; it was the concept whose Koranic justification — "*de koran zelf voorschrijft, dat een geloovige zich nooit anders aan een ongeloovige mag onderwerpen, dan genoodzaakt door dwang*" — fixed the Dutch response as the operational *dwang* of the *scheepvaartregeling*-cum-closure, the only ground of submission the Koran itself authorises; it was the concept whose religious unity was broken by Tengkoe di Tirou's over-reach into the worldly authority of the *panglima sagi XXVI* — the over-reach whose effect was to shatter the unified command of the post-[[1884]] resistance and to fragment the *bendehoofden* into their separate commands; and it was the concept whose operational form — the *fanatieken opgezweept door priesters* — fixed the Dutch prescription as the separation of the *wettige hoofden* from the *fanatieken* by the fiscal leverage of the *scheepvaartregeling*. The *sabil* is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the religious-political predicate the twenty-year policy oscillation had failed to overcome, and the operational counter-predicate — the *dwang* the Koran itself authorises — the 1893 tract prescribes.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

**Prang sabi** — the holy war, whose adherents formed the *Sabilbenden* or *sabil* bands of Dutch usage — was the religious framework of Achehnese armed resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh]], invoked throughout *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]* as the motive of the *[[Moslemin]]*.

## The sabil bands

[[G. B. Hooijer]] recorded that the 2,400 auxiliaries assembled under [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] on [[2 November]] [[1893]] were to drive *de Sabilbenden* out of the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]], and that the *kampong* (village) of [[Hoengtjala]] was *die voorraadschuur en veilige wijkplaats der sabilbenden* — the storehouse and safe refuge of the *sabil* bands — before it was given over to plunder and fire.

## Leadership and self-devotion

The spiritual leader of the Achehnese *in den heiligen strijd* was, in Hooijer's account, the *[[Oelama|oelama]]* [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]], succeeded by his son [[Mat Amin|Mad Amin]]. The most striking instance of self-devotion he recorded was at [[Senelop]], where the [[Sister of Habib Samalanga|sister of the late Habib Samalanga]] held the command and *zich met 15 andere fanatieke vrouwen ten dood had gewijd* — had devoted herself to death with fifteen other fanatical women.

At [[Anagaloeng]] the defenders were led in their last defence by champions excited by opium, shrieking and leaping like madmen. Hooijer acknowledged, in describing the fighting at [[Kroeng Kali]] and [[Kota Toeankoe]], that the Achehnese defended himself behind his walls *met mannenmoed en doodsverachting* — with manly courage and contempt of death.

## Account from H. F. Meijer (1883) — prang sabil in the year of the Habib's return

[[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]], in *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]), records the holy war in the making, in the year after the Habib's landing: Prince [[Toengkoe Bintara Kamangan]] of [[Gigiëng]], after his outward peace had begun to fail, invited the heads of his realm to collect money for the *prang sabil* against the Netherlands — a report brought to the Dutch by the well-disposed moekim head [[Toekoe Imam of Gigiëng]] and one of the foundations of the [[Cancellation of friendship with Gigiëng (19 August 1876)|breach of 19 August 1876]].

The same account shows the war's preacher at the meetings of Groot-Atjeh: the Habib's written declarations, taken from the chiefs of the three [[Sagi]]s in [[1876]] and confirmed by oaths, expressly framed the continuance of resistance as duty.

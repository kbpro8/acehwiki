**Kawom** (also *kawöm*, *kaum*, *soekèë*; from Arabic *qawm*, "people" or "tribe") was the Acehnese clan or tribal grouping — a kinship-based social unit encompassing all descendants of one *stamvader* (founding father) in the male line, whose members bore collective and several liability for *[[Bila|bila]]* (blood money) in cases of killing or wounding. The kawom was a tribal, not a territorial, institution, standing apart from the [[Gampong|gampong]]-[[Mukim|mukim]]-[[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] hierarchy of territorial administration.

## The four kawöm's

Originally, the Atjèhnese population was divided into four *kawöm*'s (tribes), whose members had common rights and duties especially for *bila* (blood revenge) and *diët* (blood money). Kreemer records the four original kawöm's as:

1. **Lhèë reutöïh** ("the Three Hundred") — federated with two other kawöm's against the dominant Imeum peuët; has contributed to the Oelèëbalang families and holders of high offices
2. **Dja Sandang** (or Tjoet- or To' Sandang) — the sagi-head [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Pölém]] of the [[XXII Moekim's|XXII Moekim's]] belongs to this kawöm
3. **Dja Batèë** (or To Batèë) — federated with Lhèë reutöïh and Dja Sandang against the Imeum peuët
4. **Imeum peuët** ("the Four Imeum's") — the most numerous and powerful kawöm; the other three federated against it. Marriage between the federated three and the Imeum peuët remained prohibited. No member ever rose above the modest rank of mukim-head.

## Collective Liability

All members of a kawom were jointly and severally liable as concerns *bila*, so that a blood-feud might keep two clans in a state of mutual hostility for years. Should the influence of the *panglima kawom* (clan leader) or the pressure of the higher territorial powers succeed in preventing the feud and inducing the injured party to accept blood money, then the guilty party — most often unable to make up the required sum — considered himself entitled to demand contributions from all his well-to-do fellow tribesmen, or if he belonged to one of three allied kawoms, from all the members of those three tribes.

## The Panglima Kawom

The head chosen by members of one kawöm living together in a kampoeng to manage their common interests was called the **panglima kawöm** — especially for *bila* (blood revenge) affairs. The panglima kawöm was confirmed by the [[Uleebalang|Oelèëbalang]], who gave him a set of clothes and weapons (*seunalén*). A territorial head was never chosen as panglima kawöm. Blood feuds were decided without interference from the territorial authorities, simply under the direction of the panglima kawom — who was a tribal, not a territorial, chief. Exceptions occurred through the authority of some unusually energetic [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] or unusually influential [[Teungku|ulama]].

## Blood Money

The ordinary blood money for causing death in Acheh was 500 Spanish (Mexican) dollars. The highland blood-debtor, given a period of one or two years to pay off the *diët* (blood payment), would go on a journey "to collect subscriptions" (*chö' ripë*, also called *tjo ripè*) from fellow tribesmen, sometimes coming down into the lowlands to apply for contributions from those connected with his tribe. The *tjo ripè* was the formal collection of contributions from kawöm-genooten (clan-members) to pay fines imposed by the administration, but above all to pay *diët* — the non-bloody form of revenge for manslaughter or wounding.

## Decline

By Kreemer's time (the early [[1920s]]), the kawöm system was largely extinct, with only the name remembered. The best preserved memory was in the [[XXII Moekim's|XXII Moekim's]], where the sagi-head [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Pölém]] still belonged to the Dja Sandang kawöm. The *Dajasche landschappen* — partly populated from the XXII Moekim's — still had all four kawöm's represented, with the Imeum peuët by far the most numerous.

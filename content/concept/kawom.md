**Kawom** (also *kaum*; from Arabic *qawm*, "people" or "tribe") was the Acehnese clan or tribal grouping — a kinship-based social unit whose members bore collective and several liability for *[[Bila|bila]]* (blood money) in cases of killing or wounding. The kawom was a tribal, not a territorial, institution, standing apart from the [[Gampong|gampong]]-[[Mukim|mukim]]-[[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] hierarchy of territorial administration.

## Collective Liability

All members of a kawom were jointly and severally liable as concerns *bila*, so that a blood-feud might keep two clans in a state of mutual hostility for years. Should the influence of the *panglima kawom* (clan leader) or the pressure of the higher territorial powers succeed in preventing the feud and inducing the injured party to accept blood money, then the guilty party — most often unable to make up the required sum — considered himself entitled to demand contributions from all his well-to-do fellow tribesmen, or if he belonged to one of three allied kawoms, from all the members of those three tribes.

## The Panglima Kawom

Blood feuds were decided without interference from the territorial authorities, simply under the direction of the *panglima kawom* — who was a tribal, not a territorial, chief. Exceptions occurred through the authority of some unusually energetic [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] or unusually influential [[Teungku|ulama]].

## Blood Money

The ordinary blood money for causing death in Acheh was 500 Spanish (Mexican) dollars. The highland blood-debtor, given a period of one or two years to pay off the *diët* (blood payment), would go on a journey "to collect subscriptions" (*chö' ripë*) from fellow tribesmen, sometimes coming down into the lowlands to apply for contributions from those connected with his tribe.

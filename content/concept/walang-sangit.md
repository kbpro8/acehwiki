The **walang sangit** — the malodorous rice bug that attacks the ripening ear of *[[Paditeelt|paddi]]* — caused, together with drought, an almost complete crop failure in some *moekims* of [[Montassik|V Moekims Montassie]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1936]]–[[1937]], a fact Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] used to test whether food supply alone explained the distribution of *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]*.

## The plague of 1936-1937

Gomperts recorded that [[1936]] and [[1937]] were marked by long-continued drought, so that the *paddi* planting failed to take well, with the exception of the planting in [[VI Moekims Lhong]] and [[Lam Teuba|Moekim Lam-Teuba]]. Since there was no artificial irrigation of the *[[Sawah|sawah]]* fields in Groot-Atjeh and one was therefore dependent on rainfall — except in those *moekims*, where irrigation was fed by mountain streams that supplied enough water even in the dry seasons — the failure was general. In V Moekims Montassie, besides the drought, "had men ook van een walangsangitplaag te lijden, zoodat in sommige Moekims van dit District eene vrijwel volledige oogstmislukking werd geboekt."

## The test of the dietary theory

Set against the xerophthalmia figures, the crop data showed that in Montassie, with a great crop failure, the xerophthalmia frequency "weinig verschilde van die van de vallei-Moekims [[VII Moekims Baet|Baet]], met een veel beteren oogst." Together with the parallel findings for Lhong and Lam-Teuba, this led Gomperts to conclude that "men alleen met den factor voeding het xerophthalmie-vraagstuk niet kan oplossen" — one cannot solve the xerophthalmia question with the factor of nutrition alone.

**The Hikajat Malém Dagang** (Acehnese: *Hikayat Malém Dagang*) is the oldest and most celebrated of the Acehnese epic *hikayats* — a mid-seventeenth-century Atjehnese-language chronicle-epic centred on the naval expedition of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Eseukanda Muda]] against the treacherous Malaccan prince [[Si Ujut]], in which the poem's hero, [[Malem Dagang]], is nominated chief *panglima* (commander) of the fleet through the mediation of the wise teacher [[Ja Pakeh]], and which culminates in the capture and execution of Si Ujut after his betrayal of Acehnese hospitality. It is the principal surviving example of the Atjehnese chronicle-epic tradition, and is the source on which the Malay-language *[[Hikajat Sultan Atjeh Marhoem]]* is — in the judgement of [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] — "een ietwat droge proza-omwerking" (a somewhat dry prose reworking).

## Plot

The epic centres on the naval expedition of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Eseukanda Muda]] against the treacherous Malaccan prince [[Si Ujut]]. The poem's hero, **[[Malem Dagang]]**, is nominated chief *panglima* of the fleet through the mediation of the wise teacher **[[Ja Pakeh]]**, and the epic culminates in the capture and execution of Si Ujut after his betrayal of Acehnese hospitality. The cast of the epic further includes [[Raja Raden]] and [[Putroe Phang]] among the principal figures of the sultanate's court. The naval expedition is presented as the central set-piece of the chronicle-epic, and the *panglima*'s appointment and the *kětika* (auspicious-moment) consultations before battle frame the poem's account of the campaign.

## Genre and Character

The *Hikajat Malém Dagang* belongs to the Atjehnese chronicle-epic tradition — a tradition that, like the Malay *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* and the *[[Hikajat Raja-Raja Pasai]]*, presents itself as historical narrative but freely mixes legend with fact. [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] places it in his chapter on *Geschiedschrijving in het Maleis* (Malay historiography) alongside the Malay chronicles, observing that the Atjehnese tradition, while not Malay in language, "wijkt in geest niet af van de tot dusverre behandelde zgn. historische geschriften" (does not differ in spirit from the so-called historical writings treated so far). The events narrated in the *Hikajat Malém Dagang* — like those of the Malay *Hikajat Sultan Atjeh Marhoem* — "moeten op feiten teruggaan, maar zijn schier tot onherkenbaarwordens toe aan opsmuk onderhevig geweest" (must go back to facts, but have been subjected to embellishment almost beyond recognition).

## The Pakeh Madina / Fakih Madinah Episode

The *Hikajat Malém Dagang* is the source of the literary portrait of [[Pakeh Madina]] (= *[[Fakih Madinah]]* of the Malay reworking) — the *Fakih Madinah* or "wetgeleerde, kenner der Moslimse plichtenleer, uit Madīnat al-Nabī, stad van de Profeet" (jurisconsult, expert in the Muslim law of duties, from Madīnat al-Nabī, the city of the Prophet). [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] singles out, in his commentary on the *Sadjarah Malajoe*, the *kětika* consultation episode in which Pakeh Madina, despite his Islamic office, repeatedly consults the pre-Islamic *kětika* (auspicious-moment) tables and forbids the battle for days until the favourable moment arrives: "Dagenlang verbiedt hij de strijd, totdat de kětika gekomen is, en de Atjehers natuurlijk winnen!" (For days he forbids the battle, until the *kětika* has come, and the Atjehers naturally win!).

The episode is, in Hooykaas's reading, the literary evidence that the pre-Islamic *pawang* tradition — the *pawang* who, in his chapter on folk-belief, Hooykaas describes as "de oude volkspriester, elders doekoen geheten en pēmangkoe, zich handhavend ondanks Islam en Hindoeisme" (the old folk-priest, elsewhere called *doekoen* and *pēmangkoe*, maintaining himself despite Islam and Hinduism) — persisted in the Islamicised Atjehnese court, with the Islamic specialist taking over the ritual functions of the pre-Islamic priest.

## The Atjehnese Humour

A key feature of the *Hikajat Malém Dagang* that distinguishes it from its Malay reworking is what [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] calls its *Atjehse humor* — "De Atjehse humor van het oorspronkelijk is menigmaal verloren gegaan" (the Atjehnese humour of the original has often been lost [in the Malay adaptation]). The humour is part of the chronicle-epic's character: the *Hikajat Malém Dagang* presents the Atjehnese self-image of the sultanate's wars, with the *admiraalschip* (admiral's ship) of the Atjehnese fleet belonging "tot de legende" (to legend), but the *kětika* consultations belonging "tot historische mogelijkheid en wellicht werkelijkheid" (to historical possibility and perhaps reality).

## The Fantastic Expedition against Johor

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] notes that one of the *Hikajat Malém Dagang*'s set-pieces is the "fantastische tocht tegen die slechte kafir's van Djohor" (fantastic campaign against those bad infidels of Djohor) — an Atjehnese campaign that is presented, in the chronicle-epic, as an apotheosis of the "gezamenlijke roemrijke expedities naar Malakka en verder z.O.-waarts" (joint glorious expeditions to Malacca and further southeastwards). This "is de Atjéhse voorstelling en herinnering aan de gebeurtenissen waartusschen in het eerste begin van de 17de eeuw te Djohor de SM werd geschreven" (is the Atjehnese representation and memory of the events amid which, in the very beginning of the 17th century at Djohor, the *Sadjarah Malajoe* was written). The expedition belongs to the same chronological frame as the [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] attacks on [[Batoe Sawar]] of [[1613]] and [[1615]] that dominate the *Sadjarah Malajoe*'s composition history.

## Significance

The *Hikajat Malém Dagang* is significant as the principal surviving example of the Atjehnese-language chronicle-epic tradition, and as the source on which the Malay *[[Hikajat Sultan Atjeh Marhoem]]* is a (somewhat diminished) reworking. Its preservation of the [[Pakeh Madina]]/*[[Fakih Madinah]]* episode is the principal textual evidence for the survival of pre-Islamic *pawang* practice in the Islamicised Atjehnese court, and its *Atjehse humor* is the principal literary evidence for the Atjehnese self-image of the sultanate's wars against [[Djohor]] and [[Malacca]] in the early seventeenth century. Its plot — the naval expedition of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Eseukanda Muda]] against [[Si Ujut]], with [[Malem Dagang]] as the chief *panglima* of the fleet appointed through the mediation of [[Ja Pakeh]] — is the oldest and most celebrated of the Acehnese epic *hikayats*.

## Account from *Atjèh* by J. Kreemer (Deel II)

[[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] provides the following account of the *Hikajat Malém Dagang*.

The *Hikajat Malém Dagang* was one of the two oldest Atjèhnese *hikajat*'s — narrative poems in *sandja*-meter — singing of an episode from Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Iskandar Moeda]]'s ([[1607]]–[[1636]]) struggle against [[Malaka|Malaka]]. Though without historical value as strict history, its style is thoroughly Atjèhnese.

The *hikajat* describes Sultan Iskandar Moeda's famous attack on Malaka in [[1629]], in which his *La seumana* (admiral) was captured. The grave of this La seumana is pointed out in the kampoeng [[Pandé|Pandé]] near [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]].

Each *hikajat* verse has 4 parts: the 2 middle parts rhyme with each other, and the end-word rhymes with the end-word of other verses. The *hikajat*'s are sung (*beuét*) on specific melodies. A *hikajat* may be humoresque (*hikajat roehé*), history, romance, fable, religious legend, or moral lesson.

The *Hikajat Malém Dagang* is one of the two oldest Atjèhnese *hikajat*'s, alongside the *[[Potjoet Moehamat|Hikajat Pötjoet Moehamat]]*. While the *Hikajat Malém Dagang* "has no historical value but the style is thoroughly Atjèhnese," the *Potjoet Moehamat* is "on firmer historical ground" and was called "a jewel of Atjèhnese, indeed Inlandsche, literature" by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]].

The **ateueng** (Atjehnese; Malay *galangan*) was the small dike separating one wet rice field from another in [[Aceh|Atjeh]]. It served at once as a physical boundary, as a path, and — in the Atjehnese law of land — as both a mark of prior cultivation and a metaphor for the limits of a right.

## As evidence of cultivation

In [[Groot-Atjeh]] a plaintiff who asserted an earlier possessory right over wet rice fields (*[[Oemong|oemòng]]*) that had been left fallow had in particular to be able to point to traces of *ateueng*; a former watercourse (*[[Loeeng|loeëng]]*) or the buried *[[Oendja ateueng|oendja]]* could serve as further proof. Witnesses alone did not suffice.

## As a limit of the priority right

The Atjehnese expression for the limits of the priority right of a neighbouring *sawah*-holder over adjoining ground was: "dit recht wordt niet toegekend buiten de *ateueng*" — this right is not granted beyond the *ateueng*, the little rice-field dikes. [[D. W. N. de Boer]] explained that the expression was sufficiently clear to an Atjehnese because an *ateueng* is a separation between a rice field and adjoining land of like kind, whereas no *ateuengs* are used where the *[[Sawah|sawah]]* passes over into a mountain slope, hilly terrain, *[[Ladang|ladang]]* ground and the like. The saying therefore meant that the priority right applied only to land not destined for rice cultivation, and not to land suitable for the extension of a *sawah* complex.

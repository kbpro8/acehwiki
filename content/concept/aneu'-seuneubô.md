**Aneu' seuneubô** was the Atjehnese term for the resident settler of a pepper plantation in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] — the planter engaged by a capitalist contractor under the *[[Contract of the Atjehnese pepper planter|pepper planter's contract]]*.

## The term

[[D. W. N. de Boer]] glossed the compound: *aneu'*, Malay *anak*, child; *seuneubô*, pepper garden. On the analogy of *anak negeri*, "children of the land," and *anak kampong*, the inhabitants of the village, *aneu' seuneubô* denoted the *opgezetene(n)* — the resident settlers — of a pepper plantation.

## Engagement

The aspirant planter had himself engaged or enrolled orally, either directly with a *[[Peutoeha pangkaj|peutoeha pangkaj]]* (capitalist contractor) or with one of his overseers, the *peutoeha djaga* or *[[Peutoeha parang|peutoeha parang]]*. He then received through the *peutoeha djaga* an advance normally comprising two *[[Goentja|goentja]]* of *padi*, some clothing, the necessary tools and some cash, amounting in total to no more than twenty to twenty-five dollars or guilders per thousand vines he undertook to plant, disbursed in step with the progress of the work.

## Obligations

Repayment was usually in instalments beginning after the first normal harvest, in the third or fourth year; in [[Idi]] the whole advance might be called in at once in the fifth or sixth year. Whatever the manner or pace of repayment, and even after full discharge, the planter owed the *pangkaj*-giver, so long as the garden remained productive, the *[[Wathé pangkaj|wathé pangkaj]]* — a tenth of the harvest, the *peutoeha parang*'s honorarium included. Under the later government tariff he rendered one guilder per *picol* as *wathé pangkaj* and fifty cents per *picol* as *wathé peutoeha*, besides the guilder per *picol* payable to the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]* on export.

## Ownership and forfeiture

The *aneu' seuneubô* was nevertheless the owner of the planting, and lost that ownership only through complete neglect of the garden, in which case he could claim nothing for the labour he had performed. In practice deprivation was rare; instead the garden was regularly inspected and valued, and on deterioration through the planter's fault the difference between two successive valuations was demanded of him in addition to the customary tenth.

The **opium and gambling farms in Atjeh** — the farming-out (*verpachting*) of the exclusive right of retail sale of opium, of the levy upon opium imports, and of the gambling houses (*dobbelspelen*) in the subdued parts of Groot-Atjeh — were the first regular fiscal instruments of the Dutch administration from [[1876]], and the chief pillar of the province's early revenue: in [[1878]] they yielded f 549,000 in Groot-Atjeh alone, f 358,680 more than in the preceding year.

## Institution, 1876

By ordinance of [[16 June]] [[1876]] (Ind. Stbl. No. 151) the necessary stipulations were issued to begin in August [[1876]] with the farming-out of the exclusive right to the retail sale of opium and to the levying of duty upon the import of opium in the territory of Groot-Atjeh subjected — or further to be subjected — to the Dutch authority. The opium use in this part of the territory was estimated in May [[1876]] at 300 *bollen* (balls) per month. The number of free Chinese in Groot-Atjeh amounted then to 1,000, of whom 700 used opium. The first farm-sum was estimated at f 5,000 per month; the farm was allotted on [[15 July]] [[1876]] to a Chinese of [[Batavia]], who at the same time took the farm of the gambling houses in Atjeh for f 8,500 per month.

## Growth

The continuing immigration of the Chinese — 4,886 arrivals from [[Singapore]] and [[Penang]] in [[1877]] alone — exercised "a not inconsiderable influence on the country's revenues": the farming out of the opium sale and the gambling houses yielded in [[1878]] in Groot-Atjeh f 549,000, "f 358,680 more than in 1877". In the same year the native population of the subdued territories began for the first time to pay indirect taxes (see [[Taxation in Atjeh]]). The stamped of the "pacht der kleine middelen" ("farm of the small means") — the fisc's general farm of petty exactions — later served Langhout as an index of the growth of the Foreign Oriental population: its high yield, with 2,700 Chinese and other Foreign Orientals assessed for the trade tax in [[1882]], proved the steady increase of that population; in the consumer crisis of [[1907]] "de kleinhandel en de speel- en drankpachten" ("the retail trade and the gambling- and drink-farms") felt the backlash of the departing foreigners.

## Significance

For Langhout the farms belonged to the self-evident fiscal machinery of the first occupied years, financing in part the works of the [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification]]; their receipts, and later those of the in- and export-duties of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] and the assessed taxes, furnished the figures by which he demonstrates the province's rising yield (in-/export duties: nearly f 180,000 in [[1908]]; more than half a million in [[1915]]; nearly f 612,000 in [[1917]]).

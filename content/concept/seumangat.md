**Seumangat** (Gayo: *sëmangat*; cf. Malay *semangat*) was the Atjèhnese concept of the **impersonal soul** — the "life-force" or "life-spirit" that could temporarily leave the body, at which point the person might die if the absence was prolonged. The *seumangat* was distinguished from the *njawöng* (personal soul), which did not leave the body during life.

## Nature of seumangat

The *seumangat* "flees" (*gadöh*) from the body due to shock, fear, unpleasantness, or other disturbance. Its absence causes illness, which must be treated by the *kroe seumangat* ritual — "calling back" the seumangat. If the seumangat's absence is prolonged, the person may die.

The *seumangat* was also attributed to animals, plants, and objects — including rice, money, kites, and other items. This animistic conception gave rise to various rituals and taboos surrounding these objects.

## Gadöh seumangat

The "fleeing" of the *seumangat* from the body was called **gadöh seumangat**. This caused illness, and the *seumangat* had to be recalled by the *kroe seumangat* ritual. The *gadöh seumangat* was also the explanation for the Atjèhnese reluctance to conduct money transactions after dark — "the soul leaves" (*gadöh seumangat*) if transactions are conducted after dark, related to the fear of jealous spirits that wander at night.

## Kroe seumangat

The ritual of "calling back" the *seumangat* of a sick child was called **kroe seumangat** (also *naloe seumangat* in Gayo, *koer sëmangat*). Performed in the evening, the practitioner rings bracelets or bells over the child, recites *pantön*'s for hours, and calls "kroe seumangat!" until all souls are returned. The *seumangat*'s appear as small flames or fireflies (*kléb-kléb*).

## Relation to njawöng

The *seumangat* was distinguished from the *njawöng* (also *njawa*) — the personal soul, which did not leave the body during life. After death, the *njawöng* had little influence on the living. The *seumangat*, by contrast, was the active, wandering element that could be lost, recalled, and transferred.

**Dutch Military Governance in Aceh** refers to the system of colonial administration in which civil and military authority were combined under a single commander. This arrangement was formally adopted following the concentration decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]], when the Governor-General appointed [[Colonel H. Demmeni]] as Civiele en Militaire Gouverneur (Civil and Military Governor) of [[Atjeh]] and Onderhoorigheden.

## Background

The Dutch experiment with separate civil administration in [[Groot-Atjeh]] had proven problematic. The civiel bestuur (civil administration) had been introduced with the aim of winning hearts and minds and establishing normal colonial governance, but the continuing hostilities made this impossible. The civil administration lacked the means to maintain order, while the military forces were constrained by the separation of authority.

## The 1884 Reforms

On [[19 August]] [[1884]], the Governor-General reversed the civil administration experiment:

### Reunification of Authority

Civil and military authority was reunited under a single Militaire Gouverneur (Military Governor). This meant that the military commander had full authority over both security matters and civil administration within his jurisdiction.

### Replacement of Controllers

The kontroleurs (controllers) who had represented the civil administration in the sub-districts were abolished. Their functions were transferred to the military commanders of the relevant areas.

### Streamlined Command

The administrative structure was simplified, with clear lines of military command replacing the complex civil-military dualism.

## Structure (1885)

Under the military governance system, the Civiele en Militaire Gouverneur at [[Kota-Radja]] exercised authority over:

- All military operations
- Civil administration within occupied territory
- Relations with the local population
- Economic and commercial matters
- Infrastructure development

## Challenges

The military governance faced several challenges:

### Security Within the Line

Maintaining security within the fortified perimeter required strict measures. On [[1 March]] [[1885]], weapons restrictions and a pass system were introduced. On [[18 May]] [[1885]], these were tightened, with unauthorized movement subject to shooting.

### Relations with the Population

The population was divided between those within the line (theoretically under Dutch control) and those outside. Even within the line, much of the population was believed to sympathize with the oorlogspartij (war party).

### Proportionality

The author of the 1885 report noted the tension between security requirements and the legitimate interests of the Acehnese population. Strict security measures were necessary, but they could not completely ignore the welfare of those who were theoretically Dutch subjects.

## Assessment

The military governance model represented a pragmatic response to conditions on the ground. It acknowledged that civil administration required a minimum of security to function and that under wartime conditions, military authority was unavoidable.

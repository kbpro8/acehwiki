The **adat glé** (Atjehnese, "the *adat* of the mountain" or of the uplands) was the composite levy imposed by the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalangs]]* of [[Aceh|Atjeh]] on cultivation carried on **outside** the *[[Gampong|gampong]]* boundaries, in the period when the territorial chiefs still levied taxes as sovereigns in their own right.

## Components

[[D. W. N. de Boer]] enumerated three heads:

- on wet rice fields, a levy identical with the *[[Boeët oemòng|boeët oemòng]]* charged within the *gampong* — one *[[Naleh|naleh]]* of *padi* per *naleh* of seed rice, roughly two to three per cent of the yield;
- on dry rice fields (*[[Ladang|ladang]]*), the so-called *[[Padi lintjah|padi lintjah]]*, also called *padi tadjoë* (from *tadjoë*, the dibble or planting stick, hence "dibbled padi"), a levy of two *naleh* of *padi* per *hippaang padi* — per surface cleared by one person with a single *parang* (chopping knife);
- on gardens (*lampoih*), the *[[Thiploeëh-tha|thiploeëh-tha]]* (Atjehnese *thiploeëh*, ten; *tha*, Malay *satu*, one) — "one out of ten," the tithe of the crop, an institution found elsewhere in the Archipelago as well, for instance in [[Bali]].

## Abolition and evidentiary afterlife

Like the *boeët oemòng*, the *adat glé* was abolished by article 15 sub I of the *zelfbestuursordonnantie* (self-government ordinance), [[Staatsblad 1919 No. 822]], the chiefs receiving in compensation an individually calculated *[[Civiele lijst|civiele lijst]]*. The records and memory of the levy nevertheless continued to serve, in the *[[Zelfbesturende landschappen|self-governing territories]]*, as a means by which the *radja* could tell whether a parcel of apparently waste land had recently been in someone's possession.

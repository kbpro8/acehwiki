**The Lao Alas** — also called the *Woi ni Agoesein* in the Gajo language — is the principal river of the Alas valley of central [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], rising on the southern slopes of the [[G. Loser|G. Loser]] (3,520 m) in the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Wilhelmina gebergte]] and flowing south-eastward through the *groote Westelijke lengtedal* to its confluence with the *Lae Renoen* and the *Simpang Kin i*, by which name it reaches the Indian Ocean at [[Singkel|Singkel]]. In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), the Lao Alas is the principal hydrographical artery of the central-Atjeh hoogvlakte, and the river whose *stroomgebied* includes the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] lake and the [[Ketol|Ketol]] high plateau.

## Course

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I describes the Lao Alas as rising "op de Zuidelijke hellingen van den G. Loser (70), in Zuid-Oostelijke richting het Westelijke lengtedal doorstroomt, tot zij de Lae Renoen opneemt, welke komende van den Poesoek Boehit (59) het water van de hoogvlakte der Pepagan Pak-Pak afvoert." The Lao Alas's right-bank tributary, the *Lao Bengap*, rises on the eastern slopes of the *Deleng Siboeatan* (60) and flows along the eastern foot of the *Wilhelmina gebergte* through the *hoogvlakte der Karo-Bataks* to its confluence with the Lae Renoen at *Kota Bangoen*.

After its confluence with the Lae Renoen, the Lao Alas takes in the *Lae Mbelin* (draining the *hoogvlakte der Kepas Pak Pak*) and the *Lae Koembi* (draining the *hoogvlakte der Sim Sim Pak Pak*), "om dan als Simpang Kin i de Westelijke Barisanketenen te doorbreken en bij Singkel den Indischen Oceaan te bereiken." The Lao Alas thus passes, in its lower course, under the name *Simpang Kin i*, through the Westelijke Barisan to Singkel — the same *Simpang Kin i* name that is also borne by a separate river of the *Oostkust van Atjeh* (not to be confused, the Bureau notes, with this one).

## The Alas valley as the southern approach to central Atjeh

The Lao Alas's *stroomgebied* — the Alas valley — is the principal southern approach to the central-Atjeh hoogvlakte. The Bureau's account locates the Lao Alas between the *Wilhelmina gebergte* to the north and the *Gajoesche Centraalgebergte* to the south, with the *Laoet Tawar* lake (at [[Takengon|Takengon]]) and the *Ketol* high plateau lying within the *stroomgebied* of the river's upper tributaries. The Alas valley's role as the southern approach to central Atjeh is the geographical foundation of its strategic role in the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]]: the valley was the designated refuge of the *2de hoofdgroep*'s planned guerrilla, and the *Goenoeng Setan* pass at the head of the valley was the strategic choke-point of the Dutch central-Atjeh defence.

## Significance

The Lao Alas is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the principal hydrographical artery of the central-Atjeh hoogvlakte, and the river whose *stroomgebied* defines the geographical and strategic contours of the Alas valley. The Bureau's emphasis on the Lao Alas's course through the *groote Westelijke lengtedal*, on its confluence with the Simpang Kin i, and on the *Laoet Tawar* and *Ketol* hoogvlakte within its *stroomgebied*, frames the Lao Alas as the geographical foundation of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte* — the river whose valley defined the Dutch colonial state's central-Atjeh *bivaks* at [[Takengon|Takengon]], [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]] and [[Kotatjane|Kotatjane]], and whose *stroomgebied* would form the theatre of the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|capitulation of 28 March 1942]].

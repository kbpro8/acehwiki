The **[[Aanvallende tactiek in Atjeh|aanvallende tactiek in Atjeh]]** (offensive system in Atjeh; also *offensieve stelsel* or *aanvallende stelsel*) was the active, aggressive strategy advocated by [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] and embodied in the conduct of [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] — the strategy of mobile colonnes striking the enemy repeatedly, of burning kampongs from which the Dutch were fired on, of cutting standing padi, and of pursuing the enemy after every success. It is the prescriptive alternative to the *afwachtende houding* (wait-and-see posture) of [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] and [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]] that Borel indicts throughout *Onze vestiging in Atjeh*. The two examples Borel cites as proof of the *aanvallende stelsel* are [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]]'s two expeditions against [[Gedoeng]] in June [[1878]] and the [[Submission of Pasangan (15 August 1878)|submission of Pasangan]] after naval bombardment on [[15 August]] [[1878]].

## The Doctrine

The *aanvallende tactiek* was, in Borel's account, the application to Atjeh of the European strategic common sense of the mid-nineteenth century — the lesson of the German pursuit after [[Sédan]] in [[1870]], of the French expedition against Algiers in [[1830]] (quoted from Camille Rousset's *L'Histoire de la guerre de Crimée*), and of the [[Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken (1873)|Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken]] booklet distributed to officers before the second expedition, which prescribed the *gulden regel* that "Slechts eene krachtige vervolging kan de overwinning vruchtbaar maken" (only a vigorous pursuit can make victory fruitful). The doctrine had several elements. First, the enemy must be pursued after every success: the *omtrekkende beweging* of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] that delivered the deserted Kraton to the Dutch should have been pressed forward to break the retreating Atjehnese hoofdmacht. Second, kampongs from which the Dutch were fired on must be burned: the prohibition on burning, imposed by van Swieten as a humane measure, was, in Borel's judgement, a strategic error. Third, standing padi must be cut: van Swieten's prohibition on cutting padi — and his [[Padi-snijden on the Langkroek sawah (1874)|padi-snijden]] concession to [[Langkroek]] — fed the enemy; Pel harvested the padi for Dutch use. Fourth, the enemy must be attacked by *omtrekking* (flanking and encircling) rather than by frontal assault — the "overoude Indische tactiek" that almost always yielded surprising results. Fifth, [[Mobiele colonnes (Borel)|mobiele colonnes]] of 600–800 men, always ready to march and to "marcher au canon," must replace the *posten-systeem* of small garrisoned posts.

## Pel's Application

Pel's application of the *aanvallende tactiek* began on [[28 April]] [[1874]] — two days after van Swieten's departure — when he ordered the daily bombardment of kampongs within range. The establishment of the Oeleh Leh post in early May [[1874]], the [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|battle of Lampoeloe]] of [[20 June]] [[1874]] (the first Dutch offensive since the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]] of [[15 February]] [[1874]] — "the first step on a new road"), the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] of [[28 June]] [[1874]] (when the enemy was for the first time permanently driven from the right bank below [[Penajoeng]]), the [[Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)|battle of Soerian]] of [[26 July]] [[1874]], the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] of [[28 September]] [[1874]], the [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|conquest of Lemboe]] of [[7 November]] [[1874]], the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|battle of Longbatta]] of [[31 December]] [[1874]], the [[Surprise of Lamara Olejlo (14–15 February 1875)|surprise of Lamara Olejlo]] of [[14 February|14]]–[[15 February]] [[1875]], the [[Completion of the defensive ring around Kota Radja (May 1875)|completion of the stelling-Pel]] in May [[1875]] and the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great offensive of December 1875 – February 1876]] — all were stages in the application of the *aanvallende tactiek*. Pel's death on [[24 February]] [[1876]] interrupted the application; under Wiggers the *afwachtende houding* returned.

## The Vindication of 1878

The *aanvallende tactiek* was, in Borel's account, vindicated by the events of June and August [[1878]]. [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]]'s two expeditions against the vassal coastal state of [[Gedoeng]] — after the second of which the chief laid down bestuur and his son came to submit — were a "schitterend succes" that Borel cites as proof of the doctrine. The [[Submission of Pasangan (15 August 1878)|submission of Pasangan]] after naval bombardment on [[15 August]] [[1878]] confirmed the pattern: "eerst moet de vijand getuchtigd worden en wel in die mate dat hem geen andere uitweg overblijft dan het hoofd in den schoot te leggen" (first the enemy must be chastised, and to such a degree that no other course remains to him but to lay his head in the lap). Borel predicts that the same pattern will work in [[Groot-Atjeh]] itself: repeated *tuchtigingen* of the [[XXII Moekims]] — "de kern van Atjeh's weerstandsvermogen" — by the proposed [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] would reduce the enemy and finally end the war. He invokes in jest the words of prins von Bismarck on the "psychologische oogenblik" at which to begin negotiations with Atjehnese chiefs after a few tuchtigingen. The *aanvallende tactiek* was, in Borel's argument, the only strategy that could end the Atjeh-oorlog; the [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] was the means to apply it.

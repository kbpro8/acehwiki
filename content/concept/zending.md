***Zending*** — the Protestant mission — appears in *[[Een strijd in Atjeh (1934)]]* chiefly in its medical capacity, as the agency whose village hospitals and dispensaries constituted, in [[J. Tijssen|Dr. J. Tijssen]]'s account, the only substantial provision for the indigenous population outside the government hospitals.

## Tijssen's assessment

Tijssen was careful not to be understood as disparaging it: "Ik heb het werk van de zending gezien. Geloof niet, dat ik dien arbeid gering schat. Wanneer u schrijft, zeg dan vooral dit. De menschen van de zending weten zelf nauwelijks, hoe goed bijv. het werk is, dat zij deden door de behandeling der [[Avitaminose in Atjeh|avitaminose]] met levertraan, waardoor bij honderden kinderen vroege blindheid is voorkomen." — I have seen the work of the mission. Do not believe that I undervalue that labour. When you write, say this above all. The people of the mission themselves hardly know how good is the work they did, for instance, by the treatment of avitaminosis with cod-liver oil.

But he held that more remained to be done: "Maar er is meer te doen. Er zijn tal van vrouwen waarbij we door operatief ingrijpen zouden kunnen helpen, wanneer wij den aanval begonnen in de kampongs." — But there is more to do. There are numbers of women whom we could help by operative intervention, if we began the attack in the kampongs.

## The figures of [[1934]]

In *[[Medische Nood]]*, [[1934]], no. 1, Tijssen gave the numbers: "Ik ken de zendingsziekenhuizen. Ik wenschte, dat er in Ned. Indië in plaats van 33 zendingsartsen 330 waren! Zijn er 20 zendingshospitalen, ik wenschte, dat „Simavi” het geld bij elkaar kreeg om het tot 200 te brengen! Maar ook dan zouden er menschen noodig zijn, die naast het mooie werk in de hospitalen het vuile werk in de kampong willen doen." — I know the mission hospitals. I could wish that in the Netherlands Indies, instead of 33 mission physicians, there were 330! Are there 20 mission hospitals — I could wish that „Simavi” got the money together to bring it to 200!

The object of the [[Vereeniging SIMAVI]] itself was defined by reference to the mission: the provision of medical help to and the spiritual elevation of the indigenous population "in den zin, waarin deze tot nu toe door de Prot. Medische Zending in practijk zijn gebracht." — in the sense in which these have hitherto been put into practice by the Protestant Medical Mission.

## On Samosir

On [[Samosir]] Tijssen found three Dutch missionaries and three small mission auxiliary hospitals, but recorded that real medical care had not existed there until a year before his visit, when a young Batak physician — [[Gido Siregar]] — was posted for the combating of leprosy, and that in ophthalmology "was het terrein even onbewerkt als heel Indië is."

## Zending and Islam in Atjeh

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]] itself the pamphlet records the obstacle rather than the work. Of the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West Coast]] Tijssen wrote that its people were "vrome fanatieke Mohammedanen," that at the hour of prayer every boy spread out his head-cloth on the road wherever he was, and that "het woord van Paulus „Ik schaam mij het evangelie van Jezus Christus niet” is voor den Mohammedaan absoluut onbegrijpelijk. Zijn geloof is zijn eer." — the word of Paul, „I am not ashamed of the gospel of Jesus Christ”, is to the Muhammadan absolutely incomprehensible. His faith is his honour. Among such a population, he said, one could not work in the fasting month, and the administration would not gladly have seen it. He also observed that on the West Coast the Dutch faced a population among whom no officer went into a *[[Kampong|kampong]]* without an armed soldier behind him, while he himself lived unarmed among them for weeks unharmed.

The **[[Posten-systeem in Atjeh|posten-systeem in Atjeh]]** was the Indian system of garrisoning many small posts to dominate terrain, applied in Atjeh by the [[Bezettingskorps in Atjeh|bezettingskorps]] under [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] and continued under [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]]. The system had worked on Sumatra's Westkust and on Borneo, where the enemy was politically divided and could be held in check by small garrisons; but in Atjeh, Borel argues, it was over-applied, leading to *machtsversnippering* (dispersal of force), to the exposure of small garrisons to *overvallen* (surprise attacks), and to the [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]] that proved the system could not hold the line.

## The Indian System

The *posten-systeem* was, in Borel's account, the standard Dutch colonial method of dominating hostile territory. Small garrisons — typically a company of infanterie, sometimes with a section of artillerie — were posted in fortified posts at strategic points along a line, with the dual purpose of preventing the enemy from penetrating the line and of projecting Dutch authority into the surrounding terrain. The system had been developed in the long colonial wars of the nineteenth century — on Sumatra's Westkust, on Borneo, in the Celebes — where the enemy was politically divided, lacked central direction, and could be held in check by small garrisons supported by occasional punitive expeditions. The system had the advantage of economy: a relatively small European force could dominate a large territory. It had the disadvantage of *machtsversnippering*: by dispersing the force among many small posts, it left no strong reserve available for offensive operations, and it exposed each post to the risk of being *overvallen* by a superior enemy force.

## The Atjeh Application

In Atjeh the *posten-systeem* was applied on a scale unmatched in earlier colonial wars. The [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] completed in May [[1875]] comprised 36 posts along a perimeter of some 25 kilometres, enclosing an area of approximately 2 500 hectaren — about three times the area of Amsterdam. Each post was garrisoned by a detachment of infanterie and (where appropriate) artillerie, and was connected to its neighbours by roads, telegraph and (where feasible) the field railway between [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] and [[Oeleh Leh]]. Borel criticises the stelling-Pel for containing too many redundant posts — a feature of the *posten-systeem* that he indicts throughout the book. The posts were repeatedly *overvallen*: the [[Night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit (22 November 1875)|night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit]] of [[22 November]] [[1875]], when a band of ~50 Atjehneezen climbed the low borstwering and "met de blanke [[Klewang|klewang]]" hacked among sleeping manschappen, wounding 13 including 2 officers; the night attack on Lemboe-N.O. of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1876]], in which the bezetting weerde zich dapper but lost one gesneuveld officier and 22 zwaar gekwetsten; and the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] of [[13 February]] [[1876]], in which [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]]'s detachment of 2 officers and 57 men was massacred on the drassige [[Sawah|sawah]] near [[Lamsajoen]] — all exposed the vulnerability of the *posten-systeem* in Atjeh.

## The Vindication of the Critique

Borel's critique of the *posten-systeem* was vindicated, in his account, by the [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]]. Barely had gouverneur van Atjeh, [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]], left with most of the mobiele strijdmacht to *tuchtigen* the vassal coastal state of [[Gedoeng]], when the enemy attacked the Dutch linie, broke through at several places, fell into the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]], verjoeg the pro-Dutch bevolking, plundered and burned, sneed the gemeenschap between K. Radja and several posts, and committed the "gruwelijke moordtooneel in een van onze hospitalen" — an atrocity in a Dutch hospital that Borel cites as one of the "wrange vruchten van het passieve stelsel." Van der Heijden had to rush back in aller ijl and with geweld restore communicatie with the posts in the IV Moekims; new battalions were sent from Java. The lesson Borel draws is categorical: posts alone cannot prevent a linie-doorbraak; mobile forces must never be reduced. The *posten-systeem* could only be made effective if it were supplemented by [[Mobiele colonnes (Borel)|mobiele colonnes]] of 600–800 men, always ready to march and to "marcher au canon," and operating two or three in *verbond* toward the same *doel*. The *posten-systeem* was thus not rejected outright but subordinated to the *mobiele colonnes* — a strategic judgement that anticipated the later *maréchaussée* system associated with [[Johannes van Heutsz|van Heutsz]].

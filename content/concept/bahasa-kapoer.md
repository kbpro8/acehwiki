**Bahasa kapoer** — Malay "lime-language", i.e. the "peculiar language" of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]] — is the *[[bedekte taal]]* register of Malay-Javanese speech that [[Marco Polo|Marco Polo]] noted as the "peculiar language" of the small island he called *Gauenispola*, and which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies with the *kapoer* of [[Barus|Baros camphor]] — a code of speech whose key is the trade-goods and place-names of the Sumatran coast. It is the spoken-language counterpart of the written *dasanama* (synonym-book) and the poetic *wangsalan* (rhyme-riddle), and the principal register by which the older Hindu-Buddhist geography of [[Sumatra]]'s east coast and the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere was concealed from foreign listeners.

## Marco Polo's record

[[Marco Polo|Marco Polo]], in his account of the small island he called *Gauenispola* (which [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] identified with the [[Pulo Bras|Pulo Xuta / Poelo Bras]] of the *Eerste Schipvaart*), recorded the "peculiar language" of the place. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies this with the ***bahasa kapoer*** of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]] — the "lime-language" whose "lime" (*kapoer*) points, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, to the *kapoer baroer* (camphor) of [[Barus|Baros]], the great west-coast emporium whose product gave its name to a whole register of speech. The same *kapoer*, De Roo de la Faille argues, underlies the *kapoer-gala-gala* caulking of [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes]]'s fighting-junk of [[1513]].

## Function and the foreign listener

The function of *bahasa kapoer* is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the same as that of *[[bedekte taal]]* at large: to allow the lettered Malay-Javanese speaker to record matters of royal shame, ancestral displacement, or sacred geography without exposing them to plain view, while systematically misleading the foreign listener. The foreign traveller — [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]], [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]], [[Marco Polo|Polo]], [[Ibn Battuta]], [[Pigafetta]], [[Tome Pires]] — recorded the *bahasa kapoer* forms as if they were plain ethnographic names, and so produced travel accounts whose very naïvité is the mark of their authenticity.

## Decoding instances

The instances of *bahasa kapoer* decoding in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s *Historische Curiositeiten* are too many to list in full, but the principal ones include: *Surotilau* (Pinto) as *Soera-sakti-lawoe-kir* for the roadstead of [[Malajoer|Malajoer]] in [[Aroe]]; *Paneticano* (Pinto) as *Roe-pang moes-tika-noe-sa* for [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]; *minha cumbaa* (Pinto) as *minjak sembawa* for [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]]; *Gauenispola* (Polo) as *Gawé-nis pola-ga-nis* for [[Taroema|Taroema]]; *Cernovem* ([[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]]) as *Ks4r-noe-sya-m-a* for [[Sjahroe'n Noewi|Sjahroe'n Noewi]]; *Xarnauz* (Vasco da Gama's *Roteiro*) as *k-s-ir arnaw-a dj-a-wa* for [[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Taroema|Taroema]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, *bahasa kapoer* is the spoken-language form of *[[bedekte taal]]* — the principal register by which the older Hindu-Buddhist geography of [[Sumatra]]'s east coast and the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere was concealed from foreign listeners. Its recognition is the methodological precondition for any *bedekte taal* reading of the European travel accounts of the archipelago, and for the cross-identification of chronicle place-names with European travel record.

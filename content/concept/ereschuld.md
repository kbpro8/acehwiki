**The Ereschuld** — the "debt of honour" — is the doctrine, principally associated with [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d., liberal-democrat MP), that the [[Netherlands]] owed a moral debt to the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipelago for the surplus — the [[Batig Slot|batig slot]] — extracted from the [[Cultuurstelsel|cultuurstelsel]] period and after. The doctrine was rejected by [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] and [[Jhr T. A. J. van Asch van Wijck|Van Asch van Wijck]] in their conduct of the [[Indische Comptabiliteitswet amendment (26 June 1903)|Comptabiliteitswet amendment of 26 June 1903]] that formally abolished the *bijdrage* of [[Nederlandsch-Indië]] to the finances of the realm.

## The Van Deventer doctrine

[[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s name is associated, above all, with the parliamentary agitation that drew attention to the surplus that the [[Netherlands]] had extracted from the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipelago during the [[Cultuurstelsel|cultuurstelsel]] period. In the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s view is recorded in passing: "al hetgeen dat van [[1831]] af tot en met [[1877]] door ons is ontvangen, volgens minister [[De Waal|De Waal]] rondom 700 millioen" — everything that we received from [[1831]] to [[1877]] inclusive, in the round figure of 700 million guilders given by minister [[De Waal|De Waal]].

## The Bos counter-argument

The proposition that the present generation could not, however, compensate a dead Indies generation for the wrong done to it was, [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] notes, one that [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]]'s contemporary Bos contested. Bos "achtte het nageslacht wel degelijk aansprakelijk" — held the descendants indeed accountable — and compared the matter to social insurance, where the present generation must stand surety for the errors of the past.

## The ministerial refusal

[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] and [[Jhr T. A. J. van Asch van Wijck|Van Asch van Wijck]] declined to frame the [[Indische Comptabiliteitswet amendment (26 June 1903)|Comptabiliteitswet amendment of 26 June 1903]] as the discharge of an [[Ereschuld|ereschuld]]. The official formula was: "geen terugtrede in hetgeen door de daartoe volkomen bevoegde macht vroeger in wettige vorm is geschied" — no retreat from what was done in lawful form by the authority fully competent to do so. The existing state of affairs was to be the starting-point for financial policy; the past could only strengthen the conviction that, when the Indies were in need, the motherland must support them according to its ability. The ground of the new policy was, accordingly, not the recognition of an obligation to restitution but the recognition of a moral vocation (*zedelijke roeping*) of the motherland towards the colonies and of the significance of the colonial condition for the entire Realm.

## The Van Asch van Wijck-Idenburg formula

[[Jhr T. A. J. van Asch van Wijck|Van Asch van Wijck]]'s successor [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] (a.r.) reaffirmed the refusal in a nota to the [[Tweede Kamer]]: the [[Ereschuld|ereschuld]] "niet haar grond [vondt] in de aanvaarding van een verplichting tot restitutie, maar in de erkenning van een zedelijke roeping van het moederland tegenover de koloniën en van de betekenis van de toestand der koloniën voor het gehele Rijk" — found its ground not in the acceptance of an obligation to restitution but in the recognition of a moral vocation of the motherland towards the colonies and of the significance of the colonial condition for the entire Realm.

## Significance for Atjeh

The [[Ereschuld|ereschuld]] doctrine bears on Atjeh because the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] was, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, one of the principal charges on the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] budget that the *batig slot* had been used to defray. The rejection of the [[Ereschuld|ereschuld]] frame in favour of the *zedelijke roeping* frame was, accordingly, the official re-orientation of the Atjehnese war's cost from a charge owed to a charge to be borne.

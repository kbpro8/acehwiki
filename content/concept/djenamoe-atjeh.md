**Djenamoe Atjeh** was a ceremonial tribute of thirty-two *katies* (approximately 1.28 kilograms each, totalling about 40.96 kilograms) of gold paid by each newly enthroned sultan of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] upon his accession, originating from the bribes distributed by the *[[ferdanamantrie|ferdanamantrie]]* [[Maharadja Lela Malajoe|Maharadja Lela Malajoe]] to secure the election of his son [[Sultan Alaïdin Achmad Sjah|Potjoet Din]] (Sultan Alaïdin Achmad Sjah) around [[1723]]. The custom institutionalised the means by which the first sultan of the present (Bugis-descended) lineage had been placed on the throne and, according to [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in his [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 brochure]], served as a permanent reminder that the sultanate of the current lineage was founded on bribery rather than hereditary right or military conquest.

## Origin

When [[Sultan Djohar Alam Imadoedin Sjah|Sultan Djohar Alam Imadoedin Sjah]] died around [[1723]], the *ferdanamantrie* Maharadja Lela Malajoe persuaded the assembled court grandees in the [[Balei Blerong Sri|Balei Blerong Sri]] within the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]] to elect his son Potjoet Din as sultan, over the rival claimant [[Sjamsoel Alam|Sjamsoel Alam]] who had been proclaimed by the chiefs of the [[V Moekims Montassik|V Moekims Montassik]]. To secure the election, Maharadja Lela Malajoe distributed gold as follows:
- Each *[[Sagi|sagi]]* chief of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]] and [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]]: ten *katies* of gold each (total: 20 *katies*)
- [[Tekoe Kadli Malikoel Adil|Tekoe Kadli Malikoel Adil]]: two *katies*
- [[Tekoe Nek Poerba|Tekoe Nek Poerba]] of the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] and [[Tekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia|Tekoe Nek Radja Moeda Setia]] of [[Merassa|Merassa]]: five *katies* each (total: 10 *katies*)
- The *[[panglima prang|panglimas prang]]* at sea and on land: fine garments

The total distributed was thirty-two *katies* of gold. From this event, Van Heutsz recorded, the custom arose that every new sultan, upon his coronation, paid thirty-two *katies* of gold under the name of *Djenamoe Atjeh*.

## Significance in the Sultanate Debate

Van Heutsz cited the Djenamoe Atjeh as evidence against the [[Herstel van het sultanaat|restoration of the sultanate]] under Dutch sovereignty. The fact that the present sultan's lineage had been installed by bribery — and that the bribery was institutionalised as a permanent coronation requirement — demonstrated, he argued, that the sultanate was a position bought rather than inherited or earned, and that the chiefs who accepted the gold had done so not out of loyalty but out of self-interest. This reinforced his broader argument that the chiefs did not genuinely want a sultan above them and that restoring the sultanate would merely recreate a figurehead whose real power would lie with the chiefs, not with the throne.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the Bugis line and the war-party invocation

[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 tract]] develops the *Djenamoe Atjeh* as part of the constitutional apparatus of the Atjehnese sultanate — the gift that constituted the sultan's investiture and bound the *sagi hoofden* to his recognition — and as part of the religious-political apparatus the war-party invoked to sustain the resistance after the [[1874]] death of [[Sultan Machmoed Sjah]]. The Bugis sultan's line, which the *Djenamoe Atjeh* had installed, was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, descended from the Bugis *Daeng Mancoer Boegis* of Wadjo (Celebes) who had settled on the [[Westkust]] of Atjeh at [[Loong]]. The line was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, "*door omkooping van voorname hoofden in het toen reeds aan anarchie ten prooi zijnde rijk, tot het sultanaat wist te verheffen*" (raised to the sultanate by the bribery of principal chiefs in a realm already given over to anarchy). The *Djenamoe Atjeh* was the constitutional form of that *omkooping*: the gift that constituted the sultan's recognition by the Sagi-heads and bound them, in turn, to the throne they had been paid to acknowledge.

### The arithmetic of the *Djenamoe*

Van Heutsz records the original *omkooping* on page 44 of the 1893 tract, with the full arithmetic of the *Djenamoe*:

> "Om nu de voorname gezaghebbende hoofden van Groot-Atjeh voor die keuze te winnen (omtekoopen), zond hij aan ieder der sagi hoofden van de XXII en XXVI Moekims 10 katies goud, aan T. Kadli Malikoel Adil 2, aan T. Neq Poerba van de IX Moekims en aan T. Neq Radja Moeda Setia van Merassa ieder 5 katies, terwijl de panglimas-prang ter zee en te land mooie kleedingstukken ten geschenken kregen. Panglima Polim verjoeg toen sultan Sjamsoel Alam en in eene nieuwe in de Balei Blerong Sri belegde vergadering, werd Potjoet Din tot sultan uitgeroepen onder den titel van Sultan Alaïdin Achmad Sjah. Van dezen tijd dagteekent de gewoonte, dat de sultans bij hunne troons-bestijging 32 katies goud betalen, een geschenk, dat bekend is onder den naam van Djenamoe Atjeh".

The arithmetic of the *Djenamoe* is, in Van Heutsz's account, the arithmetic of the original *omkooping*: ten *katies* each to the two Sagi-heads of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII]] and [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI]] Moekims (twenty *katies* in total), two *katies* to *T. Kadli Malikoel Adil*, five *katies* each to *T. Neq Poerba* of the [[XXV Moekims|IX Moekims]] and *T. Neq Radja Moeda Setia* of [[Merassa]] (ten *katies* in total), making thirty-two *katies* in all. The total — thirty-two *katies* — became, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the customary *geschenk* each sultan was obliged to pay on his *troonsbestijging*: the form in which the original *omkooping* was constitutionalised into the standing *Djenamoe Atjeh*. The constitutional consequence Van Heutsz draws is sweeping: the present Bugis sultan's line had "*nooit macht in het rijk bezeten*" — never possessed power in the realm — and the sultan's authority was, from the moment of the original *omkooping*, derivative of the Sagi-heads' recognition rather than constitutive of it. The *Djenamoe Atjeh* is, in this diagnosis, the constitutional sign of the sultanate's weakness: the gift that bound the sultan to the Sagi-heads, and so the form in which the sultan's dependence on the Sagi-federations was institutionalised.

### The war-party's invocation of the *aloude gebruiken* in 1884

The *Djenamoe Atjeh* is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, part of the religious-political apparatus by which the war-party sustained the resistance after the [[1874]] death of Sultan Machmoed Sjah. The constitutional framework of the sultanate — of which the *Djenamoe* was the installation form — was, after the [[1874]] death, the framework the war-party exploited to install the pretender-sultan [[Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed]] at [[Kemala]] and, in [[1884]], to hail him in the *mesdjid Indrapoeri* as sultan of the *rijk*. Van Heutsz records the installation on page 40 of the 1893 tract:

> "de tegenpartij [...] door de meerjarigheidsverklaring van Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed en diens uitroeping en huldiging in de mesdjid Indrapoeri tot sultan van het rijk, gevolgd door de inrichting van des sultans huis te Kemala voor zoover dat mogelijk was naar de aloude gebruiken".

The *aloude gebruiken* — ancient customs — invoked by the war-party were, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the customs of which the *Djenamoe Atjeh* was the installation form: the gift, the *huldiging* in the *mesdjid*, and the *inrichting van des sultans huis te Kemala*. The constitutional predicate the war-party invoked was, in turn, the predicate [[Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]] had sought to reverse by the *derden weg* of [[1883]] — the restoration of the sultanate under Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed by Dutch-managed settlement. The *Djenamoe Atjeh* is, in this sense, the constitutional form the *derden weg* was designed to appropriate: the Dutch recognition of the sultan's installation, on the model of the *aloude gebruiken*, by which the war-party's religious-political apparatus would be transferred from the resistance to the colonial state. The *derden weg* failed, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, because the sultan's installation could not be transferred without the *dwang* — the compulsion — that would make the war-party's recognition superfluous; and the *Djenamoe Atjeh*'s constitutional form remained, after the failure, the form the war-party continued to invoke.

### The *Djenamoe* and the *Peratoeran Makota Alam*

The *Djenamoe Atjeh* is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, part of the broader constitutional apparatus of the Atjehnese sultanate that included the [[Peratoeran Makota Alam]] — the law-code attributed to [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Sultan Iskandar Muda]] — and the customary *huldiging* of the sultan's installation. The constitutional code of the sultanate was, in this apparatus, the framework within which the *Djenamoe* was the installation gift, the [[Adat|adat]] was the customary law, and the *Peratoeran Makota Alam* was the state-law. Van Heutsz records the framework's invocation in the war-party's installation of Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed on page 58:

> "onze pogingen om hem op vredelievende wijze, langs een politieken, z. g. diplomatischen weg tot onderwerping te brengen, had de hoofden, die wij daartoe bezigden, er toe gebracht dien sultan hulde te gaan brengen overeenkomstig de gebruiken van de Peratoeran Makota Alam".

The *gebruiken van de Peratoeran Makota Alam* — the customs of the Peratoeran Makota Alam — were, in this invocation, the framework within which the sultan's installation and the *Djenamoe* were constituted.

### Significance of the Van Heutsz account

The *Djenamoe Atjeh*'s significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, rests on four things: it was the Acehnese installation-gift of thirty-two *katies* of gold that each sultan, on his *troonsbestijging*, was obliged to pay to the principal Sagi-heads and officers of the Atjehnese court — and so the constitutional form by which the present Bugis sultan's line was installed in the early eighteenth century and sustained thereafter; it was the constitutional form whose origin — the *omkooping* of the Sagi-heads by the *ferdanamantrie* [[Maharadja Lela Malajoe]] — fixed, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the sultan's dependence on the Sagi-federations' recognition rather than the Sagi-federations' dependence on the sultan's authority; it was the constitutional form the war-party invoked in the [[1884]] installation of the pretender-sultan Toeankoe Mohamad Daoed in the *mesdjid Indrapoeri* — the *aloude gebruiken* the war-party appropriated to constitute the pretender's recognition and to bind the *sagi hoofden* to his cause; and it was part of the broader constitutional apparatus — alongside the *Peratoeran Makota Alam* and the *adat* — that the Laging Tobias *derden weg* of [[1883]] had sought to appropriate by the Dutch-managed restoration of the sultanate. The *Djenamoe Atjeh* is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the constitutional sign of the sultanate's weakness — the gift that bound the sultan to the Sagi-heads and so the form in which the war-party's religious-political apparatus was, after the [[1874]] death of Sultan Machmoed Sjah, sustained.

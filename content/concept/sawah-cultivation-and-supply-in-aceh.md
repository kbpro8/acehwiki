**Sawah Cultivation and Supply in Aceh** was the traditional system of wet-rice agricultural production (*sawahs*) in the fertile river valleys of the [[Aceh Sultanate]]—notably within the [[IV Moekims]], [[VI Moekims]], [[XXII Moekims]], and [[XXVI Moekims]]—which provided the economic foundation and food supplies for Acehnese resistance forces during the Aceh War.

## Strategic Importance in the Second Expedition

Following the capture of [[Kraton of Aceh]] in January [[1874]] during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], Dutch observers noted the agricultural richness of the surrounding lowlands. In his [[1876]] study, the author of *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* emphasized that in early [[1874]], the sawahs around Kotta Radja were well cultivated and the rice (*padi*) stood in splendid condition.

General [[J. van Swieten]] recognized that as long as Acehnese agricultural production remained undisturbed, leaders such as [[Panglima Polem]] and [[Teuku Imam Lueng Batoh]] could indefinitely feed their guerrilla bands and reject Dutch demands for capitulation.

## Target of Military Campaigns Under Pel

During [[General Pel's Campaign in Aceh]] in [[1875]] and [[1876]], the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] systematically targeted the sawah economy of the interior mukims. When General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] marched through the [[IV Moekims]], [[VI Moekims]], and during the [[March through the XXII Moekims]] in February [[1876]], Dutch troops occupied granaries, destroyed fortifications protecting rice fields, and forced the civilian population to abandon their sawahs, thereby disrupting the supply chain of Acehnese resistance forces.

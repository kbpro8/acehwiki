**Baleh-baleh** (also *bale-bale*, *balai-balai*) was the Atjeh and Malay-Indonesian day-bed or low couch on which the Dutch legation was seated during the formal audience with the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana of Atjeh]] on [[1 July]] [[1641]]. The *baleh-baleh* is a piece of portable furniture — a wooden frame with woven rattan or palm-leaf surface, light enough to be moved and set up for an honoured guest — and its placement in the audience hall was itself a ceremonial act, marking the place the Sultana had assigned to her Dutch visitors.

***Bali-bali*** — also written *balé-balé* — was the low platform-bed of split bamboo or wood used throughout the Malay world and in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] for sitting, sleeping and the carrying of the sick.

The term appears in the record of the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[17 November]] [[1897]] in a single sentence of [[H. van Kol]]'s catalogue of atrocities: "Nog kort geleden werd een oude man [[Joed Mohmed Lamtengah]] op een bali-bali naar buiten gedragen en toen doodgeschoten" — recently an old man was carried outside on a *bali-bali* and then shot dead.

The detail is significant in the passage because it establishes the condition of the victim: a man too infirm to walk, carried out on the platform on which he lay, and therefore *hors de combat* in the sense of the [[Brussels Conference of 1874|Brussels articles]] which Van Kol had quoted a few lines earlier — "tuer un ennemi qui se rend, ou qui est impuissant à se défendre est un crime déshonorant."

## Description

The *baleh-baleh* is a low wooden framed couch, traditionally with a woven cane or rattan surface, used throughout the Malay-Indonesian world for sitting, reclining, and resting. It is the standard piece of "day-bed" furniture of the region — found in houses, on verandahs, and in the audience halls of courts. Its placement in the audience hall of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] was the formal marker of where the Dutch legation was to sit.

## The audience of 1 July 1641

In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of the [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)|ceremonial audience of 1 July 1641]], after the Sultana had come out of the palace to greet the embassy and each had made the *[[Sembah|sembah]]*, "daarna namen de Nederlanders plaats op een daarvoor opzettelijk geplaatste baleh-baleh, te midden van vele Atjehsche edelen, voornaam Moorsche kooplieden en schippers" — thereupon the Dutchmen took their places on a *baleh-baleh* placed there for the purpose, in the midst of many Atjeh nobles, prominent Moorish merchants and shipmasters.

The *baleh-baleh* was thus "daarvoor opzettelijk geplaatst" — placed there expressly for the purpose. Its placement was the Sultana's act: she assigned the Dutchmen their position in the audience hall by having the *baleh-baleh* set up for them, and she placed them "te midden van" — in the midst of — the assembled Atjeh nobles and Moorish merchants. The seating arrangement was therefore both an honour (the Dutchmen had their own couch) and a placement (they sat among the court, not separated from it).

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *baleh-baleh* is the piece of furniture by which the court choreographed the position of foreign visitors in the audience hall. The "daarvoor opzettelijk geplaatste baleh-baleh" of the [[1 July]] [[1641]] audience was the physical marker of where the Sultanate wished the Dutch legation to be — among the Atjeh nobles, in the midst of the court, but on a couch of their own. The detail is one of the small ceremonial facts by which the Soury embassy record preserves the texture of Atjeh court ritual.

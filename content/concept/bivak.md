**The bivak** — the field camp or bivouac — was the nightly camp of a [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] patrouille in the *[[rimboe]]* (jungle), built each evening with the *[[klewang]]* as the only tool: an *afdakje* (lean-to) made of the brigadezeilen (brigade tarps), a *[[pagger]]* (fence) of *tiangs* (poles) and *blandars* (horizontal beams) around it to break the eerste aanloop of a possible overval, a tentje for the *dwangarbeiders*, and afdakjes for the wacht and the posten. The *bivak* was both the daily camp and the daily act of construction that made the colonne method possible: a patrouille that built its own camp each night could operate indefinitely in the *[[rimboe]]*.

## Construction

"Het bivak was heel eenvoudig een afdakje, gemaakt van de brigadezeilen, twee over elkaar heenstekende lappen van 19 meter lang." The *afdakje* — the lean-to — was made of the brigadezeilen, two overlapping 19-metre tarps. The *[[pagger]]* — the fence around the *bivak* — was made of *tiangs* (poles) at 1½ metre intervals with five *blandars* (horizontal beams) bound along. "Het maken ervan [werd] altijd voorgeschreven, omdat men anders wel eens slappe broeders onder de brigadecommandanten kon krijgen" — the construction was always prescribed, because otherwise one might get weak brothers among the brigadecommandanten.

"Het gereedschap was de *[[klewang]]* en verder niet" — the tool was the klewang and nothing else. The *ligplaats* (sleeping place) of each man was "de lengte van een *klewang*" — the length of a klewang.

## The pagger and the defence

The *[[pagger]]* was the defensive fence around the *bivak*, built to break the *eerste aanloop* (first rush) of a possible overval (surprise attack). The *tiangs* (poles) at 1½ metre intervals, with five *blandars* (horizontal beams) bound along, made a fence that would slow an attacker long enough for the patrouille to take up its weapons. The *pagger* was therefore both a defensive work and a discipline: a brigadecommandant who failed to build it was a *slappe broeder* (weak brother).

## The tentje and the dwangarbeiders

The *dwangarbeiders* — the Buginese convicts who served as dragers for the patrouille — had their own *tentje* in the *bivak*. The colonne Schmidt's twenty-five *dwangarbeiders* "hadden hun eigen tentje" — had their own little tent — separate from the soldiers' *afdakje*. The *dwangarbeiders* also performed the *geweldige prestaties* of dragging the draagstokken over the steile hellingen, and cooked tea for the geheel in the patrouilleblikken.

## The kongsies and the cooking

"Op patrouille werd dan verder zelf gekookt door de *[[kongsi]]s*, ook wel *droms* genoemd. De Ambonezen deden met de Timorezen samen, de Menadonezen alleen en de enkele Soendanees of Madoerees van de brigade kwam bij de Javanen." The cooking groups — *kongsies* or *droms* — were organised by *[[bangsa]]*: Ambonezen + Timorezen together; Menadonezen alone; Javanen + Soendanezen + Madoerezen together. Each *kongsi* cooked for itself on the *[[modelketketels (model-etabletels) van het K.N.I.L.|modeleetketels]]*.

## The bivak and the colonne method

The *bivak* was the daily act of construction that made the colonne method possible. A patrouille that built its own camp each night could operate indefinitely in the *[[rimboe]]*; a patrouille that depended on fixed posts could not. The *bivak* was therefore the instrument of the colonne's mobility: the small, highly trained patrouille, building its own camp each night, was the unit that could follow the *[[bekas]]* into the *[[rimboe]]* and engage the bende on its own ground.

## Significance

The *bivak* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the daily act of construction that made the colonne method possible. The book's argument is that the colonne method — small, highly trained patrouilles operating with local intelligence against a religiously motivated resistance — depended on the *bivak*: the patrouille that built its own camp each night could operate indefinitely in the *[[rimboe]]*. The *bivak* is therefore the book's marker of the colonne's mobility, and of the *[[klewang]]*'s double function as weapon and tool.

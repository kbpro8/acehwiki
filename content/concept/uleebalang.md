**Uleebalang** (also *uleëbalang*, *oelèëbalang*; lit. from Malay *orang balang* — "warrior") were the hereditary territorial chieftains of [[Aceh|Atjèh]] — described by [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] as "the real lords of the land" (*de heeren des lands, de territoriale hoofden bij uitnemendheid*) — who formed the backbone of [[Great Acheh|Acehnese]] political and military structure. They were simultaneously military leaders, administrators, collectors of revenue, and judges within their territories, which consisted of one or more [[Mukim|mukims]] and their constituent [[Gampong|gampongs]]. Books and speech alike called them the *radja*'s of their districts, and *De Buitenbezittingen* — following Prof. Dr. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] (*De Atjehers*, Vol. I, p. 91 e.v.) — describes their authority as "ouder dan dat der Sultans" — older than that of the Sultans themselves — and therefore not in fact derived from any royal appointment. Addressed as *teukoe*, *teukoe pö*, *teukoe ampön*, or *radja*, they were the real power in the Atjèhnese countryside, with the Sultan's authority often being more nominal than real outside the capital.

## Functions

An uleebalang served three primary functions:

**Military leader** — commanding the fighting men of his territory in war, both in offensive operations and in defence against rivals or against the Dutch during the [[Aceh War]]. As "legeraanvoerder" (army commander), he was the warlord of his *oelèëbalangschap*.

**Administrator** — maintaining order and resolving disputes within his territory, arbitrating land sales, collecting tolls and levies, and overseeing the [[Keuchi'|keuchi']] (village headmen) and [[Imeum Mukim|imeum mukims]] below him. As "bestuurder" he was the day-to-day ruler.

**Judge** — deciding civil and criminal disputes, collecting deposits (*ha' ganchëng*) from parties to disputes as a pledge, and taking a portion of the settled debt as recompense. As "rechter" he was the sole speaking judge in his own *oelèëbalangschap* and a member of the *[[Moesapat|moesapat]]*'s (indigenous courts) established in [[1879]].

## Made Gouvernementsambtenaren (1881)

By Stbl. [[1881]] N°. 79, the Oelèëbalang's were made Gouvernementsambtenaren (government officials), formalising their position within the Dutch colonial administrative structure. After [[1874|1874]] they were the chiefs with whom the Dutch concluded the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]], recognising each as *zelfbestuurder* of his own *landschap*. The compact "regelt zijne verhouding tot het Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement."

## Revenue

The three chief uleebalangs (*panglima sagi*) used to receive from the sultan, on his accession to the throne, a "wedding gift" (*jinamëë* / *djinamée*) amounting to $500. Against this it became the established rule for a newly constituted panglima sagi to pay at least an equal sum to the sultan for the letters patent confirming his appointment. Revenue also came from: tolls on trade passing through their territory; land-sale fees (1% of every rice-field sold); blood-money (*[[Bila|bila]]*) levied from guilty parties; and their share of harbour dues and port revenues.

The Oelèëbalang's sovereignty right over pepper and pinang was called **[[Wasé oelèëbalang|wasé oelèëbalang]]** — originally a fixed amount ($2/pikul pepper, $1–1½/bahra pinang in Groot-Atjèh). This was reorganised by governor [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] in [[1906]] to 10% of value (causing pepper decline), then by governor [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] in [[1910]] to fixed amounts. By [[1915]], the *bestuursbemoeienis* (administrative interference) in private-law pepper contracts was removed entirely.

## Title and territory

The Atjehsche language had no single word for an oelèëbalang's territory comparable to *gampong* or *moekim*. It was described variously as *nanggrèë* (negari, land), *seuneumat* (from *mat* = to encompass), or *makan* (district), or by Malay terms such as *djadjahan* (dependency) and *daërah*. The handbook uses *oelèëbalangschap*, later reduced in official documents to *landschap* — landscape or polity — "zoodra de landstreek als zelfbestuur was erkend en ten bewijze daarvan door den oelèëbalang de zoogenaamde 'Korte Verklaring' was geteekend."

## Structure: banta and rakan

The Oelèëbalang was assisted by a **banta** — his principal helper or right-hand, usually one of his younger brothers or close blood relatives. Below the banta were various **rakan** (assistants). The Oelèëbalang also had relations with the *imeum*'s (moekim-heads) and, in the Onderhoorigheden, with *toeha peuët* (councils of four elders), *toeha lapan* (councils of eight), and *peutoeha*'s (lower heads).

## Antiquity and recognition

Although their authority did not derive from royal appointment, the oelèëbalangs of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and of the Onderhoorigheden "stelden veel prijs op een acte van erkenning vanwege het hof te [[Koeta Radja|Koeta-Radja]]" — set great store by a recognition act from the Sultan's court — "hoewel zij hunne rechten geenszins daarvan afhankelijk beschouwden." Such acts were the [[Sarakata|sarakata]]'s issued by the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan]].

## Federation in the Sagi

In [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] the oelèëbalangschappen were grouped, again in immemorial fashion, into the three federations called the [[Sagi|sagi]]'s — the XXII, XXVI, and XXV Moekims — each with its *Panglima sagi* chosen from among the constituent chiefs. The most powerful uleebalangs were the three *panglima sagi*:

- The chief of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII Mukims]] (highland sagi) — [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Pölém]]
- The chief of the [[XXV Mukims|XXV Mukims]]
- The chief of the [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI Mukims]]

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s explanation, quoted in the handbook, is that "evenals nu de gampongs, die op zichzelf staande aan de genade en ongenade van elken geweldenaar overgeleverd zouden zijn, hare kracht vonden in de vereeniging onder eenen oeleëbalang, zoo moesten wel de oelèëbalangs in hun onderlingen strijd het nut van een offensief en defensief verbond gaan inzien." Through this defensive logic the oelèëbalangs of Atjeh maintained, for centuries, a stable balance against one another and against the Sultan.

## Oelèëbalang Pöteu

A special rank was **Oelèëbalang Pöteu** — "Oelèëbalang of our Lord (the Sultan)" — a rank equal to nearly independent landschapshoofden, with secular authority but without land. Borne by hovelingen (courtiers) as favour: Teukoe Boeka Seurama (payong-drager), Teukoe Rama Seutia (chief of lijfwacht), Teukoe Keudjroeën Geundrang, and Teukoe Keureukön (originally *Katib oel-moelk* = "schrijver des Koninkrijks"; later enfeoffed with III Moekim's Keureukön).

## Relationship to the Sultan

The uleebalangs were theoretically subordinate to the [[Aceh Sultanate|Sultan]] but in practice were largely autonomous, especially as the sultanate weakened. During the [[Aceh War]], the uleebalangs of the coastal districts were generally more inclined toward accommodation with the Dutch than the highland uleebalangs, who were more easily influenced by the [[Teungku|teungkus]] (religious leaders).

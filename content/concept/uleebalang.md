**Uleebalang** (*oeléëbalang*; also *teukoe tjhi*, literally "old war chief") was the territorial chieftain and autonomous ruler of an Atjehnese *landschap*. [[Jan van Swieten]] rendered the title *oeloebalang* in his [[1879]] account. Broersma described the *oeléëbalang* as the pivot of the *maatschappelijk leven* (social life) of the *landschap*: "who has them has the people."

## Title and Rank

The title *teukoe* (pronounced *tekoe*) is equivalent to the fuller *teungkoe* and *toeankoe* — an indication of high official dignity. *Toeankoe* was borne specifically by the sultan and his relatives. The word *tjhi* is a shortening of *keutjhi*, meaning "old." The title *teukoe tjhi* was usual for the heads who were, by the old name, war chiefs, and who were simultaneously administrators and judges over the people. Below the *oeléëbalang* stood the *oeléëbalang tjoet* (*oelèë* = head, *balang* = army, *tjoet* = small), the under-chief of the *landschap*.

The *[[Banta|banta]]* — the eldest brother or closest relative of the *oeléëbalang* — observed the administration when the chief was absent, in a capacity comparable per [[Snouck Hurgronje]] (deel I, p. 96) to the *patih* in a Javanese principality or regency. In [[Peureula]], for example, the *banta* observed the administration in [[1910]] while [[Teukoe Tjhi Moeda Peusangan]] visited [[Java]], and again in [[1918]]–[[1919]] while [[Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb]] served as a *Volksraad* member.

## Functions

The *oeléëbalang* was simultaneously war commander, administrator, and judge. He controlled the *pangkai* capital advanced to pepper-planters, acted as the privileged buyer of pepper, and received the *hacil radja* (lordly right) on produce. In an old state like [[Peureula]] the ancestor had come from [[Groot-Atjeh]], settled, and acquired rule — an autocrat who accepted letters of recognition from the sultan in Groot-Atjeh if necessary but otherwise acted on his own judgment. Broersma noted that the astute *oeléëbalang* placed himself on a good footing with the Netherlands government.

The heads were also traders and men of affairs. Chinese merchants from [[Penang]] and [[Singapore]] knew what to offer; if they once put a chief in debt, they needed only to bring forth the produce of the land — pepper especially — at a favorable price already agreed.

After pacification, the *zelfbestuur* (self-government) of the *landschappen* was established and the *wasé oeléëbalang* (the ground right) became part of the landscape treasury. The Dutch administration attempted to regulate rights via the customs-office. Broersma criticised this as a healer attacking the life-source, weakening the patient. The petroleum *cijns* arrangement of [[1913]] brought dues into the landscape treasury and gave the *oeléëbalang* a fixed income.

Income examples recorded by Broersma: [[Teukoe Habib Moehamat bin Sajet Oesoeir]] of a small *landschap* with 400 souls received 155 guilders monthly — poor, in debt, but with 14 wives and 17 children. [[Teukoe Tjhi Bentara]] of the large *landschap* [[Keureutoe]] received 600 guilders plus 125 guilders for his automobile. [[Teungkoe Abdoel Hamid Orang Kaja Sri Maharadja Mangkoe Boemi]] received perhaps 1,000 guilders monthly, besides private property and trade.

During the [[Sarikat Islam]] agitation of [[1918]], vigilance was needed because the heads could be susceptible to its influence.

## Variations of Type

Broersma noted variation in the type of both heads and population: Malay type as on the Oostkust van Sumatra; Hindu type; Arabic; Minangkabau; and Abyssinian — the last derived from slaves brought from Abyssinia by those returning from [[Mekka]].

## In Van Swieten's Account (1879)

[[Jan van Swieten]] described [[Teuku Langgoegoeb]] as *uleebalang* of the [[III Mukim]] and alleged that his military appointee, [[Teuku Potjoet Ali]], killed him and assumed effective authority. The succession conflict after Potjoet Ali's death illustrates the office's political importance in the pre-pacification period.

## Significance

Understanding this term is necessary for reading the political relationships recorded during the [[Aceh War]] and the economic organisation of the pepper and petroleum industries of Atjeh.

**Rateb Saman** (also *ratib Sammān*, *rattéb saman*; from Arabic *rātib*, a fixed liturgy) was among the most popular religious litanies (*ratébs*) performed in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] and throughout the Malay-Indonesian world. It was derived from — and named after — the saint **Muhammad Samman**, who lived at [[Medina]] a couple of centuries before [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]]'s writing.

## Description

The rateb saman was characterised by the simultaneous intoning by groups of participants in a "terribly noisy" fashion, as Snouck Hurgronje describes it. The litany was popular not only in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] but also in [[Java]], where it could be heard almost every week in many Batavian kampongs.

## Context of Performance

The rateb saman was performed in the [[Meunasah|meunasah]] (village chapel) and at [[Kanduri|kanduris]] (religious feasts) in the [[Gampong|gampong]]. It required certain objects for its performance, including kettledrums (*rapa'i* or *rapana*) and wooden rings known as *pulèt*. The young people of the gampong delighted more in the chanting of other *ratébs* which had in common with the religious litanies only the name and the noise — such as the *ratéb pulèt* and the *ratéb sadati* — which were more akin to games and recreations.

## Wider Significance

The rateb saman represented the penetration of Sufi devotional practice into popular Acehnese religion, connecting the gampong worship traditions to the broader world of Islamic mystical brotherhoods (*tariqa*). The name *Samman* referred to the Sammani order of Sufism, whose founder or an important representative was this saint of Medina.

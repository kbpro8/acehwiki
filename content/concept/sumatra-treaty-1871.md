**Sumatra Treaty (2 November 1871)** was a diplomatic agreement concluded on [[2 November]] [[1871]] in which the [[United Kingdom|British]] relinquished all claims to expand Dutch authority on Sumatra in exchange for Dutch concessions on the [[Gold Coast of Africa|Goudkust van Afrika]]. The treaty cleared the way for the Dutch colonial administration to intensify its control over the Aceh Sultanate, culminating in the declaration of war on [[26 March]] [[1873]].

The treaty is referenced in Dutch military reports as the legal pretext for the deployment of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] to Aceh in 1873. It also stipulated the exchange of colonial possessions, reflecting the broader “great‑power” negotiations of the era.

## Context
Negotiated after the British acquisition of Singapore and the Dutch desire to consolidate their holdings in the East Indies, the treaty resolved competing claims over trade and territorial influence. By ceding the Gold Coast, Britain secured a strategic foothold in West Africa while allowing the Netherlands to focus on Sumatra.

## Impact on Aceh
- **Removal of British objections**: With Britain no longer contesting Dutch expansion, the Dutch felt free to act against the Aceh Sultanate.
- **Legal justification**: Dutch officials cited the treaty as evidence that their actions did not violate any prior international agreement.

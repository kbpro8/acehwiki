**Tadj-oel-moelk** ("Crown of the Realm," full title "Rijkskroon, ingezet met allerlei geordende parelen en edelgesteenten" — "Crown of the realm, set with all manner of ordered pearls and precious stones") was a 141-page Atjèhnese-popular Malay compilation by [[Teungkoe Koetakarang|Teungkoe Koetakarang]], containing chronology, astrology (*èleumèë noedjöm*), medical recipes, and magical formulas. It was allegedly compiled at the request of Sultan [[Sultan Mantsoer Sjah|Mantsoer Sjah]] ([[1838]]–[[1870]]).

## Contents

The *Tadj-oel-moelk* was a compilation of various forms of magical and divinatory knowledge:
- **Chronology** — systems of time-reckoning and calendar calculation
- **Astrology** (*èleumèë noedjöm*) — divination by the stars
- **Medical recipes** — traditional remedies and treatments
- **Magical formulas** — incantations and spells

The work served as a reference for *soerat phaj* (divination books) — collections of prayers, formulas, amulets, recipes, aphrodisiacs, medical prescriptions, mystical figures, and tables for calculating favorable times, comparable to Javanese *primbon*'s.

## The Mantsoer Sjah attribution

The *Tadj-oel-moelk* was allegedly compiled at the request of Sultan [[Sultan Mantsoer Sjah|Mantsoer Sjah]] (reigned [[1838]]–[[1870]]), the voorlaatste (next-to-last) Sultan. Mantsoer Sjah also issued *sarakata*'s to the heads of Paté and Lhö Gloempang and to the bestuurder of Lhö Kroeët, and granted the *Tadj-oel-moelk* request.

## Author

[[Teungkoe Koetakarang|Teungkoe Koetakarang]] (real name Nja Abbas) was an eccentric Atjèhnese oelama who was contemptuous of [[Teungkoe Tjhèh Saman di Tirö|Tjhèh Saman]], whom he called "Leubè Saman." He also wrote the *[[Tadkirat ar-rakidïn|Tadkirat ar-rakidïn]]* — Arabic-titled pamphlets containing political-religious teachings. He withdrew from active opposition in [[1893]] and died in [[1895]].

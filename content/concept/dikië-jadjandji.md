**dikië jadjandji (dhikr Barzandji)** is the Acehnese term for the devotional remembrance (*dhikr*) of the Prophet Muhammad’s birthday, commonly associated with the **Mawlid** celebrations in Aceh. The phrase literally translates to “remembrance of the Prophet” and denotes a specific set of chants, poems, and musical performances that are performed by a *bakāsidah* during the Mawlid.

The practice incorporates the recitation of the **Al‑Burda** and other *kasida* verses, linking the Arabic tradition of *dhikr* with Acehnese melodic patterns. The *dikië jadjandji* often begins with a *nasīb* that evokes the poet’s personal longing for the Prophet, proceeds through a *raḥīl* describing the Prophet’s journey, and culminates in a *madīḥ* praising his virtues.

### Cultural significance
- **Localization of Arabic devotion** – The Acehnese adaptation of *dhikr* reflects the broader process by which Islamic rituals were indigenized in the region.
- **Musical accompaniment** – Traditional Acehnese instruments such as the *rapa* (drum) and *sape* (stringed lute) accompany the chanting, creating a distinctive soundscape.
- **Community cohesion** – The collective nature of the *dikië jadjandji* reinforces communal identity and religious solidarity.

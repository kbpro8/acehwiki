**Panglima** was the title of the regional military commanders of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate in the seventeenth century — the Sultan's appointed governors or war-chiefs of the subordinate territories of the sultanate. In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]] the *Panglima's* of [[Pedir]] and [[Deli]] appear together with "nog vele anderen" (many others) at an audience before the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] at which the request for "Portugeesche jonge dochterkens" — Portuguese maidens as *losprijs* (ransom) — was put to the Dutch.

## Etymology and office

*Panglima* is a Malay title of military command — literally "commander" — found across the Malay-Indonesian world. At [[Acheh|Atjeh]] the *Panglima's* were the Sultan's regional appointees: the holders of military-administrative authority over the subordinate territories of the sultanate. The *Panglima of Pedir* and the *Panglima of Deli* are the two named in Godee Molsbergen's account; their presence at the Sultana's audience in [[1641]] indicates their inclusion in the great councils of the realm.

## Pedir and Deli as Atjeh dependencies

Both [[Pedir]] and [[Deli]] were subordinate territories of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate at this date:

- **[[Pedir]]** — the north Sumatran pepper-port that had been the destination of the ships that escaped the [[Death of Cornelis de Houtman at Atjeh (1599)|Houtman disaster at Atjeh in 1599]], where they beat off an Atjehnese naval attack. Pedir had a long history as an Atjeh dependency.
- **Deli** — the north Sumatran polity on the east coast, in the region of modern Medan, also an Atjeh dependency in the seventeenth century.

The presence of the *Panglima's* of these two regions at the Sultana's audience in [[1641]] is a measure of how widely the sultanate's military-administrative reach extended along the Sumatran coast.

## The "maidens as ransom" request

At the audience attended by the *Panglima's* of [[Pedir]] and [[Deli]] and many other Atjeh nobles, the chiefs asked whether the Dutch had brought "Portugeesche jonge dochterkens" — Portuguese maidens — to be delivered as *losprijs* (ransom), as they had previously requested. The presence of the *Panglima's* at this audience suggests that the request was supported by the regional military commanders of the sultanate — not merely by the court nobility. See [[Maagden als losprijs]] and [[Francisco de Souza de Castro]].

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *Panglima* is the principal office of the sultanate's regional military-administrative structure — the title by which the Sultan's authority was extended over the subordinate territories of the coast. The named *Panglima's* of [[Pedir]] and [[Deli]] in the [[1641]] audience are the visible representatives of the sultanate's reach along the Sumatran coast, and their participation in the request for Portuguese ransom maidens is a measure of how widely the sultanate's interest in that exchange was shared.

## Account from Stapel's *De Koloniën* (1931)

**Panglima-radja** (Acehnese-Malay, literally "king's war-chief" or "royal commander") was the title borne by the senior native military governor of [[Padang|Padang]] on the [[Westkust van Sumatra|Westkust van Sumatra]], whose office was progressively assimilated to the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]]'s own commandeur of the Westkust over the course of the late seventeenth century. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* the title is the constitutional hinge by which the Compagnie's commandeur at Padang came to exercise native authority in the name of the king of [[Menangkabau]].

## The Padang panglima-radja as signatory

The panglima-radja at Padang appears in Stapel's text as a signatory of two major Westkust instruments. He is one of the signatories of the [[Padang Conference (29 August 1680)|Padang Conference of 29 August 1680]], alongside the "keijser" of [[Indrapoera]] and the governors of [[Salido]] and [[Kotta Tengah]]. The panglima-radja's signature on the 1680 general bond is the native military counterpart to the Indrapoera ruler's senior civil signature.

In August [[1671]] the panglima-radja and the regents of Padang "declared 'from their own motion and particular inclination' to transfer to the Compagnie the tolls and import and export rights, which there trading Chinese, Javanese and Malays had formerly had to pay to Atjeh" (*verklaarden 'uyt eygen beweging ende besondere inclinatie' aan de Compagnie over te dragen de tollen en in- en uitvoerrechten, welke daar handelende Chineezen, Javanen en Maleiers vroeger aan Atjeh hadden moeten betalen*). The panglima-radja's participation in the transfer of the tolls from Atjeh to the Compagnie is the operational mechanism by which the substitution was effected.

## The pseudo-panglima-radja of Baros

A "pseudo panglima-radja" appears at [[Baros|Baros]] in the late [[1670s]]. "In this same year 1679 a pseudo panglima-radja, who had acquired many adherents at Baros, and had already been taken prisoner at the end of 1678, was, with his principal followers, banished to the [[Kaap de Goede Hoop|Cape of Good Hope]]" (*Nog werd in ditzelfde jaar 1679 een pseudo panglima-radja, die zich te Baros veel aanhangers verworven had, en reeds eind 1678 was gevangen genomen, met zijn voornaamste volgelingen naar de Kaap de Goede Hoop verbannen*).

The phrase "pseudo panglima-radja" implies that the title had become sufficiently institutionalised by the late 1670s that a claimant to it at Baros could be denounced as a pretender. The Compagnie's response — banishment to the Cape — is one of the harshest penalties recorded in the Westkust narrative.

## The panglimaschap of the commandeur of Padang

The constitutional assimilation of the panglima-radja title to the Compagnie's own commandeur is the closing move of the Westkust system. After the [[Tikoe submission (20-21 June 1684)|Tikoe submission of 20–21 June 1684]], the chiefs of Tikoe "promised 'to acknowledge and obey the commandeur at Padang as their panglima.' They granted the monopoly to the Compagnie and declared that their regents were to be appointed by the commandeur, 'by force of the panglimaschap'" (*belovende de commandeur op Padang voor haer panglima te erkennen en gehoorsamen. Zij verleenden het monopolie aan de Compagnie en verklaarden, dat hun regenten door den commandeur moesten worden aangesteld, 'kraghte van het panglimaschap'*).

The phrase *kraghte van het panglimaschap* — "by force of the panglima-office" — is the critical constitutional claim. The Compagnie is not merely exercising a commercial monopoly; it is asserting the right to appoint the regents of Tikoe, in the name of the panglima-office that the commandeur of Padang now holds. This is sovereign authority, exercised in the form of an Acehnese-Malay political institution.

## Significance

The panglima-radja title is the constitutional hinge by which the Compagnie's commercial-chief at Padang became a native military-chief with the right to appoint regents. The progressive assimilation of the panglima-radja title to the Compagnie's commandeur is the institutional expression of the larger transfer of Westkust sovereignty from Atjeh to the Compagnie: where the [[Painansch Contract]] of [[1663]] transferred commercial privilege, the panglimaschap of [[1684]] transferred political authority.

The panglima-radja is also one of the few native Westkust institutions named individually in Stapel's text. The title survives the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|sultane]]'s authority, the [[Painansch Contract]]'s monopoly and the [[Padang Conference (29 August 1680)|Padang Conference]]'s general bond, and it is the institutional vehicle by which the Compagnie's commandeur exercises the authority that those instruments created.

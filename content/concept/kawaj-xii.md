**Kawaj XII** — the "Kawé of Twelve" — was the federation of twelve gold-seeker settlements in the highlands between [[Beutông|Beutông]] and Keumala Dalam (in the *onderafdeeling Sigli*), whose chiefs paid an annual *boengkaj* of gold dust to the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]]'s *keudjeuroeën mas* (gold customs-collector), and whose depopulation — the result of the decline of the gold workings and the cholera epidemic of [[1878]] — led to its dissolution and partition in [[1914]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the *Kawaj XII* as the parallel, on the Pedir highlands, of the [[Kawaj XVI|Kawaj XVI]] federation of the Westkust under the *Keudjeuroeën Tjhï'* of [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]]: both were Sultan-derived federations of gold-producing settlements that the Dutch encountered as already-decaying institutions at the time of their arrival.

## Origins and the keudjeuroeën mas

The *Kawaj XII*, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, consisted of settlements of gold-seekers from [[Pedir|Pedir]] and [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], who had established themselves in the mountainous country between Beutông and Keumala Dalam. Over the *keudjeuroeëns* of these settlements, the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] appointed a *keudjeuroeën mas* — a "gold keudjeuroeën," that is, a collector of gold — as *Keudjeuroeën Tjhï'*, who was required to collect annually from each of the twelve *keudjeuroeëns* a *boengkaj* (lump) of gold dust for the Sultan. The *keudjeuroeën mas* also held the power to appoint the heads of the twelve settlements, "waardoor hij ten slotte aan bestuursrechten over het heele gebied verkreeg" — by which he finally acquired administrative rights over the whole territory.

## Decline and depopulation

The *sterk afnemende goudrijkdom* — sharply declining gold wealth — of the Kawaj XII led to the great trek of gold-seekers to the Westkust, where the gold workings of the upper [[Kr. Geumpang|Kr. Geumpang]] (the *Kawaj XII* of the Westkust, not to be confused with the Pedir Kawaj XII) attracted the displaced miners. The *hevige cholera-epidemie* of [[1878]] — the same epidemic that depopulated many of the gold districts of northern Sumatra — completed the depopulation of the once-important region. By [[1911]] the *geregistreerden* (registered inhabitants) of the Kawaj XII numbered fewer than a thousand.

## Dissolution in 1914

On the grounds of the very small remaining population, the Dutch administration in [[1914]] brought an end to the independent existence of the twelve-membered *landschapje* by dividing it between the *onderafdeelingen* of [[Sigli|Sigli]] and [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]]. At Sigli were joined: 1. Keumala Dalam, united with Keumala Raja to form the II Moekims Keumala; 2/3. Tangsé with Beunga; 4/5. Geumpang with Lamjeuë. At Meulabôh were joined: 6/7. Anoë with Pameuë; 8. Reunggeuët, attached to the [[Kawaj XVI|Kawaj XVI]]; 9/10. Toengkób with Ara. The territories of the two remaining *keudjeuroeëns* of the twelve could no longer be identified with certainty.

## Significance

The *Kawaj XII* of the Pedir highlands — like the [[Kawaj XVI|Kawaj XVI]] of the Westkust — was a fossil of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate]]'s apparatus of extraction, an institution whose constitutional significance had long since been hollowed out by the decay of its economic basis. Its dissolution in [[1914]] was a typical act of the colonial administrative rationalisation that consolidated the Gouvernement's territorial structure.

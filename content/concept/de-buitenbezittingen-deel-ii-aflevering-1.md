**De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1: Sumatra, Algemeene Hoofdstukken** — published in [[1917]] by the [[Encyclopaedisch Bureau|Encyclopaedisch Bureau]] as Aflevering X of the *Mededeelingen van het Bureau voor de Bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen* — is the second volume of the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's ten-year survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Buitenbezittingen]] and the opening instalment of the Bureau's planned geographical survey of Sumatra. Printed at the Landsdrukkerij in Weltevreden and issued by the publisher v/h. G. C. T. van Dorp & Co. of Semarang–Soerabaia–'s-Gravenhage, it is the most comprehensive published Dutch treatment of the orography, hydrography, climate, and indigenous administration of Sumatra as a whole, and is the principal pre-war reference for the physical and administrative geography of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] and the other Sumatran *gewesten*.

## Plan of the work

The Voorbericht explains the editorial plan. Volume I (the survey of general measures taken by the Department of Algemeen Bestuur toward the Buitenbezittingen, already published) was to be followed by *gewestelijke beschrijvingen* — regional descriptions — of each *gewest* in turn. For the great islands (Sumatra, Borneo, Celebes, the Moluccas and the Lesser Sunda Islands) these regional descriptions would be issued in instalments. Each description was to be accompanied by an *overzichtskaart* of the *gewest*. As the first instalment of Deel II, the Bureau issued three "algemeene hoofdstukken" that would precede the Sumatra regional descriptions:

- **Hoofdstuk I — Oro-hydrographische beschrijving van het eiland Sumatra** (pp. 1–38): the most detailed modern treatment of Sumatra's mountain systems, river systems, lakes and coastal outline, compiled from the latest surveys, with an accompanying oro-hydrographical map at 1:2,500,000 (Bijlage I). The chapter names and locates the principal Atjeh features: the [[Atjehrivier|Atjehrivier]], the [[Goudberg|Goudberg]], the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas (Woi ni Agoesein)]], the [[Kroeëng Wojla|Kroeëng Wojla (Inong Wojla)]], the [[Kroeëng Teunom|Kroeëng Teunom]] (formed by the [[Geumpang|Geumpang]] and [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] rivers), the [[Kroeëng Meureubo|Kroeëng Meureubo]], the [[Kroeëng Seunagan|Kroeëng Seunagan]], the [[Peusanganrivier|Peusanganrivier]], the [[Kroeëng Keureutë|Kroeëng Keureutë]], the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] lake, the [[Gajoesche Centraalgebergte|Gajoesche Centraalgebergte]], the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Koningin Wilhelmina gebergte]], and the [[Poeloe Wè|Poeloe Wè]]–[[Sabang|Sabang]] island complex with its oceaanhaven.
- **Hoofdstuk II — Het Klimaat** (pp. 39–57), by Dr. W. van Bemmelen: the first systematic modern climate description of Sumatra, dividing the island into seven longitudinal and transverse *klimaat-provinciën* and treating the Atjeh west coast, north coast, Sabang and inland highlands in detail.
- **Hoofdstuk III — De ontwikkeling van het Inlandsch Bestuur in de Buitenbezittingen** (pp. 58–65 and following): a programmatic statement of the principles of the reorganisation of the indigenous civil administration then in progress, with explicit attention to the training of [[Atjehsche hoofden opleiding|sons of Atjehsche hoofden]] for the bestuurswerkkring at the kweekschool te Fort de Koek (G.B. van 27 Juni [[1916]] No. 7).

## Treatment of Atjeh

The Atjeh material in Deel II is not confined to a single chapter; it is distributed across all three algemeene hoofdstukken and constitutes the most concentrated Atjeh-geography of any pre-war Dutch survey:

- **Oro-hydrography.** Atjeh's rivers are treated under the *Noord-Sumatra* heading of the chapter on the *groote Westelijke lengtedal*. The Bureau names and locates the [[Atjehrivier|Atjehrivier]] as the northernmost segment of the great Westelijke lengtedal, flowing through the vruchtbare vlakte van [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] to the sea at [[Oelèë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] near [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]]. The *Kroeëng* (Atjehsch word for "river") is glossed: "*Kroeëng* (Atjehsch.) = *Woi* (Gajo'sch.) = *Lae* (Dairisch) = *Lao* (Karo'sch.) = rivier." The northern slope of the [[Barisan|Barisan]] is sketched through the [[Gajoesche Centraalgebergte|Gajoesche Centraalgebergte]], the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Wilhelmina gebergte]] (renamed in [[1912]] on the proposal of Dr. W. Volz, with the Kab. Res. van 5 September [[1912]] No. 17), and the [[Abong-Abong|Abong-Abong]] / [[Kembar|Kembar]] / [[Bandahara|Bandahara]] / [[G. Loser|G. Loser]] / [[Poesoek Boehit|Poesoek Boehit]] knot. The [[Goudberg|Goudberg]] in Groot-Atjeh is identified as the volcano at which the eastern volcanic row terminates in the [[Poeloe Wè|Poelolo Wè]]–[[Weesberg|Weesberg]] transverse ridge, and the [[Boer ni Telong|Boer ni Telong]]–[[Boer ni Bilaans|Boer ni Bilaans]]–[[Geureudong|Geureudong]] complex as the dormant volcanoes standing west of the [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]].
- **Climate.** The Bureau's climate chapter divides Atjeh into a Noordkust zone (with the Westelijke tak van the Noord-Oost-moesson bringing rain in November–December and the main regenmaximum falling in [[December]]), a Westkust zone (where the [[Barisan|Barisan]] bends the Zuid-West moesson to Westen- and Noord-Westenwinden), and a binnenland zone (where the regenmaximum falls already in November at [[Takengon|Takéngon]] and [[Blangkedjeren|Blang Kédjérèn]]). The nevenmaximum in Noord-Atjeh falls in [[Mei]], with a small stretch of Westkust (P. Raja, Tjalang) shifted to [[Juli]]. The Sabang regenmaximum falls in [[December]].
- **Inlandsch Bestuur.** The chapter on the indigenous civil administration notes the G.B. van 27 Juni [[1916]] No. 7, by which "tot het cursusjaar 1916/1917 ook aan de kweekschool voor Inlandsche onderwijzers te Fort de Koek hoogstens 20 zonen van Atjehsche hoofden konden worden opgeleid voor den bestuurswerkkring." The Bureau observes that this provision is "not sufficient to meet the future need for trained Inlandsche bestuursambtenaren," and that the training itself must be revised.

## Significance

*De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1* is the indispensable geographical complement to the [[Encyclopaedisch Bureau|Encyclopaedisch Bureau]]'s own *Deel I* survey of administrative measures [[1904]]–[[1914]] (which is the source for the existing [[Buitenbezittingen|Buitenbezittingen]] entry). Where *Deel I* presented the Buitenbezittingen as a coherent administrative and economic whole, *Deel II* grounds that whole in the physical reality of the island. For Atjeh in particular, the volume is the canonical printed source for the toponymy, hydrography and climatology of the residency on the eve of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]]'s consolidation; its place-names and river-systems are the ones used by all subsequent Dutch colonial writing, including the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history of the 1942 campaign]] that draws on the same toponymy a quarter-century later.

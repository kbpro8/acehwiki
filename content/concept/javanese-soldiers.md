**Javanese soldiers** formed, with the [[Ambonese soldiers|Ambonese]], the principal body of native troops of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands Indies Army]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], and are the subject of a careful observation by [[P. H. R. Beuming]] in *[[Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh (1911)|Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh]]*.

## The refusal at Biloel

When at the forward post [[Biloel]] the sentry on the north face — a native fusilier — was fatally struck by [[The sharpshooter of Biloel|the sharpshooter]], the Javanese soldiers could no longer be induced to take that dangerous post. Beuming insists on the interpretation: *Hoewel het onzen Javaanschen soldaten niet aan moed ontbreekt, kon men hen er niet meer toe bewegen, dezen gevaarlijken post te betrekken. Kent men het karakter van den Javaan, dan weet men, dat hier niet is te denken aan lafhartigheid, maar aan bijgeloovige vrees* — although our Javanese soldiers do not lack courage, they could no longer be moved to occupy this dangerous post; whoever knows the character of the Javanese knows that here one must think not of cowardice but of superstitious fear.

The fear was of an *[[Orang Setan|Orang-Setan]]*, a devil-man; it was the refusal that obliged the commander to lay the ambush that killed the marksman.

## Elsewhere

Beuming names the Javanese among the elements of the force whose conduct he defends against the charge of cruelty and neglect at home, and records the small gifts of tobacco and *strootjes* for making *seroetoes* — Javanese cigarettes — that [[Pastoor Verbraak]] distributed to the sick native soldiers in the sick-rooms of the posts, and how plainly grateful they were.

**Coastal Fortifications in Aceh** was the extensive network of earthen, timber, and coral-walled defensive earthworks (*bentengs* or coastal fortifications (*strandversterkingen*)) erected by the [[Aceh Sultanate]] along the coastline of northern Sumatra—particularly at [[Meuraxa]], [[Oleh-leh]], and along the Aceh River—to repel European amphibious invasions between [[1872]] and [[1876]].

## Defensive Construction and Rearmament

Following the Anglo-Dutch Sumatra Treaty of [[1871]] and increased arms imports from [[Penang]] in [[1872]], Acehnese leaders, including [[Panglima Polem]] and [[Teuku Imam Lueng Batoh]], fortified the coastline around [[Kraton of Aceh]]. Earthen redoubts armed with imported cannon and swivel guns were constructed to command the estuaries and landing beaches.

## Role in the First and Second Expeditions

During the [[First Aceh Expedition]] in April [[1873]], Acehnese defenders successfully delayed the Dutch advance at coastal redoubts, notably at Strandversterking No. 2 near [[Meuraxa]] and at the fortified enclosure of the [[Baiturrahman Grand Mosque]]. General [[G. G. Booms]] noted in [[1875]] that the resilience of these fortifications shocked Dutch commanders, who had anticipated mere "clay huts."

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in December [[1873]], General [[J. van Swieten]] bypassed the heaviest defenses by landing at [[Koerong Raba Bay]] and systematically dismantling fortifications at [[Pekan Badak]], [[Sinangri]], and [[Kotta Alam]] using heavy siege artillery. Throughout [[1875]] and [[1876]], General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] engaged in continuous siege warfare against inland bentengs in the [[IV Moekims]], [[VI Moekims]], and [[XXII Moekims]].

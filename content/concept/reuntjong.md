**The reuntjong** — also *rencong*, *rentjong*, the Acehnese kris or dagger — was the traditional bladed weapon of the [[Acheh|Acehnese]], and the symbolic weapon of the Acehnese resistance in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* ([[1954]]). The *reuntjong* also appears in F. A. Wagner's *[[Sierkunst in Indonesië]]* ([[1949]]) as an Atjeh product whose wooden sheath was decorated with stylised leaf-motifs in fine engraving — a craftsman's account that complements the soldier's account of Dames. The two accounts together give the *reuntjong* its full AcehWiki presence: the weapon of the resistance, made by the Atjeh craftsman, decorated with the Atjeh wood-engraver's leaf-motif, and produced in Atjeh until the tradition declined in the late-colonial period.

## The reuntjong and the resistance (Dames's account)

In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, the *reuntjong* is paired with the *[[klewang]]* of the [[Manisee (Manisé)|Manisee]]: the two blades meet in the moment of *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]*'s death, when Gam Masén "trok zijn *reuntjong*, de Atjehse kris" and threw himself on the two voorste Marechaussees — the *[[dardanellen|dardanellen]]* of the luitenant — before being shot by [[Nussy]] and worked over with the *klewang* by [[Enoch]].

The *reuntjong* was the symbolic weapon of the Acehnese resistance. When *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]* was omsingeld and summoned to surrender, "hij trok zijn *reuntjong*, de Atjehse kris" — he drew his *reuntjong*, the Acehnese kris. The drawing of the *reuntjong* was the refusal of submission: the Acehnese hoofd who drew his *reuntjong* was the hoofd who would not come in. The *reuntjong* was therefore both a weapon and a sign: the sign of the resistance that would not submit.

The *reuntjong* of the Acehnese resister is paired in the book with the *[[klewang]]* of the [[Manisee (Manisé)|Manisee]]. The two blades meet in the moment of *[[Peutoeha Gam Masén]]*'s death: Gam Masén drew his *reuntjong*, threw himself on the *[[dardanellen|dardanellen]]*, and was killed by Nussy's *[[karabijn (M. 95)|karabijn]]* and Enoch's *klewang*. The pairing is the book's small symbolic register of the war: the *klewang* of the colonial soldier against the *reuntjong* of the Acehnese resister.

## The reuntjong and the wood-carving tradition (Wagner's account)

In F. A. Wagner's *[[Sierkunst in Indonesië]]* of [[1949]], the *reuntjong* is described in the chapter "Houtsnijwerk" (Wood-carving), in the section "A. Wapens" (Weapons), as a dagger formerly produced in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], whose wooden sheath was decorated with stylised leaf-motifs in fine engraving. Wagner's account is short — a single sentence — but it is the only passage in his volume that explicitly identifies the *rentjong* as an Atjeh product, and it places the *rentjong* alongside the principal bladed weapons of the archipelago.

In the section on the wood-carving of weapons, after describing the *kris* (the national weapon of Java, Bali, Lombok, the Makassarese and the Buginese) and the *mandau* (the sword of the Dayas of Borneo), Wagner turns to the Atjeh *rentjong*: "In Atjeh werd vroeger nog de rentjong (dolk) vervaardigd, waarvan de van hout vervaardigde schede versierd werd met diverse gestyleerde bladmotieven in fijn graveerwerk uitgevoerd."

The passage carries four claims:

1. The *rentjong* is a *dolk* (dagger) — a short, bladed weapon, distinct from the *kris* (which is described as the national weapon of Java and Bali) and from the *mandau* (which is described as the sword of the Dayas).
2. The *rentjong* was "in Atjeh … vroeger nog … vervaardigd" — formerly still produced in Atjeh. The phrase "vroeger nog" (formerly still) marks the production as belonging to the past, by the time of Wagner's writing in [[1949]]: the *rentjong* was, in 1949, no longer being produced in Atjeh, or was being produced only in diminished quantity.
3. The sheath (*schede*) of the *rentjong* is of wood — "de van hout vervaardigde schede."
4. The sheath is decorated with "diverse gestyleerde bladmotieven in fijn graveerwerk" — diverse stylised leaf-motifs in fine engraving. The decoration is thus a wood-engraved ornament of leaf-forms, executed in a fine manner, and the *rentjong*'s decorative art is, in Wagner's account, the art of this engraved leaf-motif on the wooden sheath.

Wagner's placement of the *rentjong* in the chapter on wood-carving is significant: the *rentjong*'s blade is metal, but the *rentjong*'s decorative art is, for Wagner, the wood-carving of the sheath. The *rentjong* thus appears, in Wagner's account, not in the chapter on metalwork (where the *kris* and the *mandau* are described in detail by way of the chapter on metalwork's discussion of iron-working) but in the chapter on wood-carving, where the *rentjong*'s wooden sheath takes its place alongside the wooden *kris*-hilts of Java and Bali, the wooden *mandau*-sheaths of the Dayas, and the wooden hilts and sheaths of the swords of Tjikëroe in the Preanger.

The Atjeh *rentjong*'s sheath-decoration is, in this wood-carving context, an example of "fijn graveerwerk" — fine engraving — on wood. The technique is the technique of the wood-engraver, not the technique of the wood-sculptor: the decoration is incised into the surface of the sheath, not built up in relief. The motifs are "gestyleerde bladmotieven" — stylised leaf-motifs — which is to say, leaf-forms that have been conventionalised into a decorative vocabulary rather than copied from nature. The *rentjong*'s sheath-decoration is, in this sense, a fine engraved ornament of conventionalised leaf-forms, on a wooden surface, executed by an Atjeh wood-engraver.

## The reuntjong and the Atjeh warrior tradition

The *reuntjong* is, in itself, the principal bladed weapon of the Atjeh warrior. It is the weapon that, in the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] of [[1873]]–[[1914]], was the symbolic weapon of the Atjehnese resistance — the weapon that the Atjeh warrior carried alongside his musket and his [[Atjeh rotan shields|rotan shield]] and his [[Atjeh powder horns|powder horn]]. Wagner's account of the *rentjong*'s production in Atjeh — "in Atjeh werd vroeger nog de rentjong vervaardigd" — is thus an account of the production of the Atjeh warrior's principal weapon, in the region whose warrior tradition produced the resistance of [[1873]]–[[1914]].

The fact that Wagner describes the production as "vroeger nog" — formerly still — is, in turn, a marker of the decline of the Atjeh *rentjong* tradition by [[1949]]. The *rentjong* had been, in the period of the Atjeh-oorlog, the weapon of the Atjeh resistance; by [[1949]], the production of the *rentjong* in Atjeh had, in Wagner's account, ceased or diminished. The *rentjong*'s decorative tradition — the fine engraving of stylised leaf-motifs on the wooden sheath — had, by the same token, ceased or diminished; and the Atjeh wood-engraver's art, as applied to the *rentjong*'s sheath, was, by [[1949]], a thing of the past.

## The two accounts together

The two AcehWiki accounts of the *reuntjong* are, in this sense, two accounts of the same weapon from two different sources. Dames's account in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* ([[1954]]) is a soldier's account, in which the *reuntjong* is the weapon of the Atjeh resister who draws it in the moment of refusal. Wagner's account in *[[Sierkunst in Indonesië]]* ([[1949]]) is a craftsman's account, in which the *reuntjong* is the product of the Atjeh wood-engraver's art, decorated with stylised leaf-motifs in fine engraving on a wooden sheath. The two accounts together — the soldier's and the craftsman's — give the *reuntjong* its full AcehWiki presence.

## Significance

The *reuntjong* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the symbolic weapon of the Acehnese resistance. The book's argument is that the *reuntjong* — the traditional bladed weapon of the Acehnese — was both a weapon and a sign: the sign of the resistance that would not submit. The pairing of the *reuntjong* with the *[[klewang]]* is the book's small symbolic register of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]: the two blades that met in the moment of Gam Masén's death are the two blades that met, in the book's economy, throughout the forty-five-year war.

In the larger history of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]nese decorative art, the *rentjong* in Wagner's account is, in turn, the principal example of Atjeh wood-carving on a weapon — the principal instance of the Atjeh wood-engraver's art applied to the sheath of the Atjeh warrior's principal blade. Its significance is that it places the Atjeh wood-engraver's art alongside the Atjeh weaver's art (the *[[idja]]*, the *[[anker-motief]]*, the *[[plang]]*) and the Atjeh metalworker's art (the [[Atjeh brass lamps]], the [[heetsen]], the [[tjawardi]], the [[Atjeh silver-wire ornament]]) in the cluster of Atjeh craft traditions. The *rentjong*'s decorated sheath is, in this sense, the wood-carver's contribution to the Atjeh warrior's kit — the kit that included, in metal, the blade of the *rentjong* itself, and, in wood, the engraved leaf-motifs of its sheath.

A **benting** (modern spelling *benteng*) is a field fortification of the type used throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] by both the [[Achehnese|Atjehers]] and the Dutch, consisting typically of a sandbag breastwork thrown up around a captured strongpoint — a kampong, a mosque, a bend in a river — and fitted with a garrison, logies (quarters), and the auxiliary obstacles of [[bamboe-doerie|bamboe-doerie]] hedge and [[ranjoe|ranjoe]] planting. The benting was the basic unit of Dutch engineer practice in the Atjeh war, and the construction of bentings under fire is the principal subject of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede-Luitenant C. J. Snijders]]'s Atjeh service of [[1874]]–[[1875]].

## The Dutch Sandbag Benting

The Dutch benting of the early Atjeh war was typically a sandbag breastwork traced as a redoubt enclosing a captured building or strongpoint. The technical detail is best preserved in the eyewitness lecture of [[T. W. G. Merz|T. W. G. Merz]] on the [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit Longbatta]] work of [[1 January|1]]–[[13 January]] [[13 January]] [[1875]]: a langwerpige redoute of about 50 × 60 metres enclosing the mosque, with the south-east and north-west saillants worked up as *cirkel-bastions*; an entrance on the north-east side closed by a travers with Friesche ruiters; a wet ditch on the east front fed by an existing water conduit; and a perimeter belt of [[ranjoe|ranjoe]] planting and wire entanglement. The first work was the filling and stacking of sandbags along the traced perimeter to give the garrison immediate cover and storm-vrijheid (freedom from storming); a large Atjehese house was typically converted into a temporary shelter while the work progressed. The breastwork was then raised to full height, the logies (bamboo barracks, covered with asphalt-felt), kitchens, and latrines were built outside the work, and the auxiliary obstacles were planted.

## Atjehese Bentings

The [[Achehnese|Atjehers]] also constructed bentings — typically more substantial earthworks, sometimes armed with artillery — at the fortified edges of their kampongs. The capture of an Atjehese benting was usually the prelude to the construction of a Dutch benting on or near the same site; the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam]] was constructed to command both the fortified edge of Pinang and the kampong [[Longbatta|Longbatta]], and the [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit Longbatta]] redoubt enclosed an Atjehese mosque within an existing low earthwork.

## The Benting as a System

The benting was the basic unit of the Dutch system of perimeter consolidation in the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Kolonel PEL]]'s strategy of [[1874]]–[[1875]] consisted of a series of offensive operations, each resulting in the capture of a kampong or strongpoint and its conversion into a Dutch benting; the bentings, taken together, formed the progressively widened perimeter around [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] that gave the Dutch the secure base from which the later operations of [[1875]]–[[1879]] could be undertaken. The bentings constructed by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] in [[1874]]–[[1875]] — [[Kuta Alam|Kota Alam]], [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit Longbatta]], [[Mandarsah Poetih|Mandarsah Poetih]], [[Pungej Blang Tjoet|Pungej Blang Tjoet]], [[Lamara Olejlo|Lamara Olejlo]] — form a representative sample of the system in its early, sandbag-and-coolie form.

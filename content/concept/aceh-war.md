**The Aceh War** (also known as the Atjeh-Oorlog, Aceh-Krijg, or Dutch-Aceh War) was the prolonged military conflict between the Dutch colonial forces and the Acehnese resistance that began with the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|First Aceh Expedition]] in [[1873]] and continued until the early twentieth century. The war was characterized by guerrilla resistance, religious mobilization, and the eventual Dutch adoption of a defensive concentration strategy in [[1884]].

## Origins

The war arose from Dutch colonial ambitions to complete the conquest of [[Sumatra]] and secure the northern tip of the island. The [[Aceh Sultanate]], historically independent and economically significant as a pepper-trading port, had resisted previous attempts at colonization. Following the signing of the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1871]], which resolved territorial disputes, the Dutch felt free to pursue military action against Aceh.

## Phases

### Initial Conquest (1873–1874)

The First [[Aceh Expedition (1873)|Aceh Expedition]] of April [[1873]] initiated the war. The kraton (royal palace) of the Sultan fell in January [[1874]], and [[Kota-Radja]] was established as the Dutch administrative center.

### Offensive Operations (1874–1884)

Dutch forces conducted numerous expeditions to extend control over [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the surrounding areas. [[Generaal Pel]] conquered the [[IV Moekims]], and [[General van der Heyden]] led campaigns in [[1878]]–[[1879]] that extended Dutch control over the [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXVI Moekims]].

### Civil Administration Experiment (1881–1884)

An experiment with separate civil administration was introduced but proved unsuccessful in maintaining order. The oorlogspartij (war party) and the *heilige oorlog* (holy war) movement gained strength during this period.

### Concentration (1884–1885)

The concentration decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]] marked a strategic shift from offensive to defensive operations. Dutch forces withdrew from scattered posts to a geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position) around [[Kota-Radja]] and [[Olehleh]], completed on [[1 March]] [[1885]].

## Religious Dimension

The war took on a strong religious character. Religious leaders such as [[Sjech Saman van Tirou]] proclaimed the *heilige oorlog* (holy war) against the Dutch. The collection of *wang sabil* (holy war funds) mobilized resources throughout [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The recognition of [[Toeankoe Mohammed Daoed]] as Sultan in [[1884]] provided symbolic legitimacy to the resistance.

## Legacy

The Aceh War established patterns of colonial governance that would persist throughout the Dutch colonial period. The geconcentreerde stelling evolved into the basis for eventual complete control of [[Groot-Atjeh]], though the resistance continued for decades.

## Account from *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*

The ~40-year war between [[Netherlands|the Netherlands]] and the [[sultanate|Sultanate]] of Atjeh, beginning [[26 March]] [[1873]] with the Dutch declaration of war and ending with [[Sultan of Aceh|the sultan]]'s submission in [[January]] [[1903]](with continued mopping-up operations afterwards).[[1873]], pacification ~[[1904]]); disrupted cattle stocks, weaving industry, gold/silver smithing; caused formation of wild karbouwen herds and [[Penang]]-based Acehnese diaspora financing war.

*Source: J. Jongejans, Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu (Baarn: Hollandia Drukkerij N.V., c. 1938), pp. 17, 38.*

**The Blang** was the great open rice-field of an Atjehnese *landschap*, on which the *padi* (rice) was grown as a rain-fed crop. On Java one would call the *blang* a *tegal* — that is, a rain-fed rice-field — but in Atjeh the *blang* is the principal form of rice-cultivation, and the *sawah* (irrigated rice-field) is the exception. There are *blangs* of 100 to 200 *bouw* by estimation; here and there in the *blang* stands a tree with a heavy crown, as a tall guardian over many fields, and under that tree one brings the cut *padi* of the day. Sometimes one treads out the grains from the ears beside that tree, and part of the harvest is temporarily stacked there. The *blang* of an Atjehnese *landschap* knows many lovely days.

In the highlands of [[Gajo-land|Gajo-land]], the word *blang* is used for every field, in contrast to the lowland where the *blang* is the great open rice-field. The great grass-plain of [[Blang Kolaq|Blang Kolaq]] at [[Takengon|Takengon]] is an example of a highland *blang*.

**Boh thenë** was the Acehnese name, current throughout [[Groot-Atjeh]], for the *leucoma corneae* — the white corneal scar left after *[[Keratomalacie|keratomalacie]]* or an eye inflammation. The term means the eye, or the egg, of the *boeroeng balam*, a kind of bird.

## The term

Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] recorded: "Het leucoma corneae na eene keratomalacie of na eene oogontsteking staat overal in Groot-Atjeh bekend als *boh thenë*, hetgeen beteekent het oog òf het ei van de *boeroeng balam*, een soort vogel" — the leucoma corneae after a keratomalacia or after an eye inflammation is known everywhere in Groot-Atjeh as *boh thenë*, which means the eye or the egg of the *boeroeng balam*, a kind of bird.

Unlike *penjikit sisë*, the Acehnese name for active *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]*, which was current only in the *moekims* [[VI Moekims Lhong|Lhong]], [[Lho Nga|Lho-Nga]] and [[Lam-Kabeuë]], the term *boh thenë* was known throughout the surveyed area — an indication of how widely the end-state of the disease was recognised among the population, even where the active disease had no distinct name.

## Clinical significance

The *maculae corneae* and their complicated form, the *leucoma adhaerens*, were tabulated separately by Gomperts: 56 cases of *maculae corneae* among 6,113 boys and 32 among 5,133 girls, 112 among 3,732 men and 90 among 3,830 women; of these, the *leucomata adhaerentes* — which bring great danger of blindness — numbered 9 among boys and 6 among girls.

**Penajoeng** — modern *Penayong* — was the main Dutch camp on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Aceh River]] opposite [[Koeta-Radja|Kota Radja]] during the early stage of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)]]. From there the Dutch launched operations toward [[Lemboe]], the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Great Missigit]], and the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]]. Later Dutch writers also used Penajoeng as a symbol of the delays, disease, and insecurity of the campaign.

## Penajoeng during the second expedition

After the Dutch moved inland from the landing area in [[December]] [[1873]], Penajoeng became the main field camp on the right bank of the river. Its position made it useful as a base for attacks toward the inner Acehnese defenses.

From Penajoeng the Dutch advanced toward [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|Lemboe]], the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Great Missigit]], and finally the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]]. Because of this, Penajoeng stood at the center of the expedition's inland phase.

## A place of delay and criticism

In [[Open Letter by Brutus to General Knoop]], [[Brutus]] treated Penajoeng as one of the main places where time was lost. He argued that, after the success at [[Battle of Tiban (14 December 1873)|Tiban]], the Dutch gathered too long at Penajoeng instead of pushing more quickly toward the next objectives. In his reading, the Acehnese used that pause to strengthen the defenses at [[Lemboe]] and around the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Great Missigit]].

## Disease and insecurity

Other Dutch writers stressed Penajoeng's physical misery. [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] called it a *pesthol* — a plague-hole — because cholera spread there badly through the crowded camp. The camp was also insecure. On [[13 January]] [[1874]], Acehnese fighters penetrated close to the Dutch lines in broad daylight. Later, on [[11 April]] [[1874]], another large attack was launched against the area.

## After the fall of the Kraton

Even after the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)]], Penajoeng remained important. Dutch works continued there, and later operations still depended on the position. On [[26 April]] [[1874]], when the main force departed, the garrison withdrew from Penajoeng to the captured [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]]. In later phases of the war, the area again formed part of the Dutch military network around [[Koeta-Radja|Kota Radja]].

## The later town district

In later colonial writing, Penajoeng also appeared as the built-up extension of [[Koeta-Radja|Kota Radja]] across the river, with roads, houses, hospitals, shops, and a large Chinese quarter. That later image stood in strong contrast with the improvised and unhealthy camp of [[1873]]–[[1874]].

## Significance

**Penajoeng** is significant because it was both a strategic base and a symbol of the early Dutch struggle in Aceh. It was the place from which the Dutch moved toward the capital, but also the place where disease, attack, and delay made clear how difficult the campaign really was.

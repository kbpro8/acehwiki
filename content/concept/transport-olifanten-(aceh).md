**The transport-olifanten of Aceh** — the elephant transport of the colonial army in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — was the military transport system that used indigenous elephants as pack-animals for military transports in the province of Aceh, particularly on the transverse road from [[Lam-toeulo]] via [[Tangsé]] to [[Geumpang]] in the interior of Aceh. The transport-olifanten are the subject of the chapter *Atjeh's Transport-Olifanten* in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), which records the history of the transport — from the Sultans of the Acehnese empire who had a thousand elephants as riding, pack, and fighting animals, through the failed trials with foreign elephants in [[1880]]-[[1881]], the [[1906]] trial with indigenous elephants, the [[1913]] transport of the elephants to Lam-toeulo, and the eventual abolition of the transport in connection with the introduction of lorry transport along the transverse road.

## The speed of the transport

## Significance

The transport-olifanten of Aceh are significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the emblematic example of the colonial army's "tempo doeloe" infrastructure in [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. The book traces the elephant transport from the splendour of the Acehnese sultans' thousand war-elephants through the colonial trials, to the 26-day journey from Tangsé to Medan — a pitiful speed that underscores why the lorry transport of the transverse road replaced it. The Keumala incident, in which an armed Atjeher attacked the elephant transport without any ordinary motive, serves as a smaller but potent marker of the djahat's pursuit of the paradise-death: a man who sought death regardless of what he attacked. The chapter thus captures both the passing of an old colonial order and the enduring logic of Acehnese resistance.

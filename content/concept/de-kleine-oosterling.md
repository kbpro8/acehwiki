**De Kleine Oosterling — Iets uit de Geschiedenis van Nederlandsch-Indië. Leesboekje voor de Lagere Scholen** — is a Dutch primary-school reader on the history of the [[Netherlands East Indies]], written by **J. Wijma** and first published in [[1901]] by Laurens van Hulst in Kampen (Tweede Druk). The book is a representative artefact of Dutch colonial-pedagogical literature at the height of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]: a child-sized, moralised history of the Netherlands Indies whose Atjeh chapters — opening with the Houtman embassy of [[1599]] and closing with the [[Van Heutsz's governorship|Van Heutsz]]–[[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] consolidation around [[1898]] — taught Dutch schoolchildren the official narrative of the colonial project in Atjeh.

## Plan and pedagogical frame

The book is organised as a numbered sequence of short *lessen* (lessons), each headed by a moralising title and accompanied by narrative prose. Among the Atjeh-relevant lessons are:

- **Les 3 — Een gezantschap**: the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' first visit to Atjeh in 1599]], the ambush by the *Atjehneezen*, the captivity of [[Frederik Houtman|Frederik Houtman]], and the [[Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits (1601)|later embassy to Prince Maurits]] bearing letters and gifts.
- **Les 36 — Een tocht met tegenspoed**: the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|eerste Atjeh-tocht]] of April [[1873]] under [[General Kaler|generaal Kaler]], his death before the *missigit*, and the withdrawal.
- **Les 37 — Een hoofdsterkte bemachtigd**: the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|tweede Atjeh-tocht]] of November [[1873]] under [[Generaal Van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]], the killing of the *zeventigjarige Javaan* bearing Van Swieten's letters to the sultan, the storming of the *missigit* on [[6 January]] [[1874]], and the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|occupation of the kraton on 24 January 1874]].
- **Les 38 — Een nieuwe stad**: the [[Founding of Kotta-Radja|founding of Kotta-Radja]] on the site of the kraton, the death of the young sultan (no successor appointed, Atjeh "als veroverd land met onze bezittingen vereenigd"), the brief command of [[Kolonel Pel|kolonel Pel]] (later generaal), the [[Governorship of General Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden's Samalangan campaign]] that won him the byname *held van Samalangan*, the [[Defection of Teuku Uma|defection of Toekoe Oemar]] and his march on [[Pedir|Pedir]] in [[June 1898|June 1898]] under [[Van Heutsz's governorship|kolonel Van Heutsz]], the [[Building of Oleh-leh harbor|building of the haven van Oleh-leh]], and the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh spoorweg]] from Oleh-leh to [[Kotta-Radja|Kotta-Radja]].

The text is explicitly pedagogical. The frame of Les 36 — "Atjeh en de Atjehers! Hoe dikwijls is de naam van dat land in de laatste jaren al niet gehoord!" — sets up Atjeh as the contemporary, schoolchild-known problem; and the lessons are paired with the recurrent moral that *eendracht maakt macht* and that Dutch *volharding* finally prevails over Atjehnese *fanatiek* resistance. The portrait of the unnamed *zeventigjarige Javaan* bearing Van Swieten's letters and being put to death by the Atjehnese — set against the contrast of "gewonde vijanden werden door ons verpleegd" — is a textbook example of the colonial reader's moral asymmetry.

## Historical accuracy and omissions

Wijma is unreliable in personal nomenclature: the [[General Kaler|generaal "Kaler"]] of Les 36 is the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|eerste expeditie]] commander more usually rendered in Dutch staff history as **Köhler** (or *Kohler*); the [[Kolonel Pel|kolonel "Pel"]] of Les 38 is more usually **Pell**; the [[Van Heutsz's governorship|kolonel "Van Heutz"]] of the Pedir march of June [[1898]] is **Van Heutsz**. These school-textbook spellings should be treated as popularisations, not as independent attestations.

The book is also conspicuously silent on the [[Treachery of Teuku Uma against the Dutch|verraad of Toekoe Oemar]] of [[1896]] — the Wikipedia reader is told only that Toekoe Oemar "eerst als bondgenoot goede diensten bewezen, maar sloot zich later weer bij den vijand aan," with no date and no explicit reference to the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad van 1896]] that had become a Dutch cause célèbre. The school reader thus compresses the political history into a single didactic arc.

## Significance

*De Kleine Oosterling* is significant in the AcehWiki corpus less as a source of new facts about Atjeh than as a documentary witness to *how* the colonial state taught its own Atjeh history to its own children. The lesson-titles alone — "Een tocht met tegenspoed," "Een hoofdsterkte bemachtigd," "Een nieuwe stad" — show the moralised narrative arc by which the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|failure of 1873]] is recuperated as the prelude to the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|triumph of 1874]] and ultimately to the [[Van Heutsz's governorship|Van Heutsz]]–[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] of the late 1890s. The school reader is therefore a primary source for the colonial *memorial* history of Atjeh, even where it is a secondary or tertiary source for the events themselves.

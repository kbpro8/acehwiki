***Overval*** — the surprise attack — was the tactical form characteristic of the aggressive Dutch operations in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] after [[1896]], in which a party surrounded a dwelling or encampment by night and opened fire without warning.

The method is described, without being named, in the account [[H. van Kol]] gave the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]] of the [[Killing of Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed Lam Tenzah (16 May 1897)|killing of Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed Lam Tenzah]] on [[16 May]] [[1897]]: the chief was killed with his wife, children and seven followers as they slept, "door salvo's te geven door de dunne wanden der woning, tot elk geluid verstomde." — by giving volleys through the thin walls of the dwelling, until every sound was silenced.

The tactic belongs to the [[Post-1896 system in Atjeh|system]] defended in the same debate by the Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]], in which strong mobile columns operating from a few concentrated *kampementen* replaced the static garrisons of the [[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde stelling]], and in which, as he put it, whoever wished to fight had to join the bands — "een gevaarlijk bedrijf wegens het optreden onzer actieve colonnes."

[[Pieter Jelles Troelstra|Troelstra]] characterised the same tactics as "eene aggressieve taktiek" which, more than any other, would cost sacrifices in money and human lives.

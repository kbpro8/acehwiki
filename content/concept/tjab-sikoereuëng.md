**Tjab sikoereuëng** (literally "one short of ten," i.e. "ninefold") was the **ninefold seal** of the Sultan of [[Aceh|Atjèh]], the principal state seal of the Atjèhnese sultanate. Each new Sultan engraved a new seal with his own name in the central circle, surrounded by eight smaller circles bearing the names of famous predecessors. The seal is reproduced on the cover of [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s work.

## Origin and the Mughol connection

According to Dr. [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]]'s research (in *Bijdragen T. L. en Vk.* dl. 59 p. 349 vg.), the *tjab sikoereuëng* may have been adopted under the example of the Great Mughol's seal, during the reign of Djahangir ([[1605]]–[[1627]]) — that is, under Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Iskandar Moeda]] of Atjèh ([[1607]]–[[1636]]). The nine-circle design (one central, eight surrounding) echoes the Mughol state seal's structure.

## Use in sarakata

The *tjab sikoereuëng* was used to seal *[[sarakata|sarakata]]*'s — recognition charters or "gift letters" issued by Sultans to *Oelèëbalang*'s and *Keudjroeën*'s as installation certificates. A chief's authority was not considered fully legitimate without a sarakata bearing the *tjab sikoereuëng*.

## The silver plate (löh pira')

A variation was the **löh pira'** (or *Ihöh pira'*) — a silver plate engraved with the "tjap sambilan" (another name for the ninefold seal) as proof of installation, given by Sultans to chiefs. The Pidiësche *Ihöh pira'* described by [[Th. Veltman|Veltman]] is a notable example.

## The Adat Meukoeta Alam

The *[[Adat Meukoeta Alam|Adat Meukoeta Alam]]* — a *sarakata* / royal edict attributed to Sultan [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Iskandar Moeda]] — would have borne the *tjab sikoereuëng*. Its originals were captured at the mosque of [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] in [[1879]] but lost in the [[1886]] flood; only copies survive, published by [[K. F. H. van Langen|Van Langen]].

## The last seal

The ninefold seal of [[Tadjoel-Alam Safijatoeddin|Tadjoel-Alam Safijatoeddin]] (reigned [[1641]]–[[1675]]), the first queen of Atjèh, is reproduced on the cover of Kreemer's work. The single seal of the last Sultan, [[Sultan Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah|Ala ad-dïn Mahmoed Sjah]] (reigned [[1870]]–[[1874]]), is on the spine of Kreemer's book.

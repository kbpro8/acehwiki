**Oost-Indisch Journael** — published with the subtitle *Palaver om de tafel met het gat* ("Palaver around the table with the hole") — is a Dutch-language book by **Merac**, published in [[1947]] by De Gulden Pers in Haarlem, in the *Toekan-Serie* (Toucan Series) edited by G. L. Tichelman (former administrative official). The book is a collection of small histories written in the period between the First and Second World Wars, which the author describes as "tales of the old time, which is always said to be good and to which we look back with a mild smile of melancholy." The book is a source for the daily life, the colonial society, and the military history of the [[Nederlands-Indië|Dutch East Indies]] in the inter-war period, with substantial content on [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]], the Soldaten-Dominee (soldiers' chaplain) [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]] and the Soldaten-Pastoor (soldiers' priest) [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]], the [[Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja]], the [[Gajoweg]], the [[transport-olifanten]] (transport elephants) of Aceh, and the old Acehnese hunter [[Datoek]] of [[Blang Rakal]].

## Genre and method

*Oost-Indisch Journael* is a collection of stories framed as conversations among a recurring cast of colonial characters — the old Aceh fighter, titular colonel [[Musket]], the fat Meijer, the Resident, the Controleur, the Pastoor, the land-council chairman, the commissioner of the field police, the chaplain, the lieutenant, the planter — who gather around the sociëteit table with the hole for the houseboy and tell stories of "tempo doeloe" — the old time. The book's method is the palaver: the colonial figures sit around the table, drink tjap koentji beer and pait (a bitter drink), and tell each other stories of the old time.

The author — Merac — distances himself from the tall-story tradition only partially. The book's prologue (*Sloebers*) establishes the frame: Musket, the old Aceh fighter, hears the word "sloebers" — the old fighting-cocks' name for the Acehnese enemy — and begins to tell of "the good old time." The book that follows is the palaver around the table: the stories of the Atjeh-moorden (Aceh murders), the prang sabil (holy war), the djahat (resistance fighter), the [[Verzet in Lhong]] (Resistance in Lhong), the Soldaten-Dominee [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]], the [[Meusigit Raja]], the [[Gajoweg]], the [[transport-olifanten]] of Aceh, and the old hunter [[Datoek]].

## Contents

The book's chapters, with their Aceh-related content, include:

1. *Sloebers* — the prologue, on the Atjeh-moorden, the prang sabil, the djahat, and the [[Verzet in Lhong]].
2. (Various chapters on administrative officials, planters, and other colonial topics — see the chapter on transfers, which cites [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s circular against transfers and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s remark on experienced controllers.)
3. (A chapter on the Soldaten-Dominee [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]] and the Soldaten-Pastoor [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]], with the marechaussée song quoted in full and the list of the marechaussée commanders: [[Gilles Graafland|Graafland]], [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], [[Campioni]], [[Christoffel]], [[Van der Maaten]], [[Scheepens]], [[G. F. B. Watrin|Watrin]], [[Webb]], [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]], [[Geertsema Beckering]], [[Darlang]] and the sergeants [[Nussy]], [[Salowono]], [[Pongoh]].)
4. (A chapter on the [[Meusigit Raja]] of [[Koeta-Radja]], with the [[Jan van Swieten|generaal Van Swieten]] letter of [[8 February]] [[1874]] and the [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] judgement.)
5. *Jagerslatijn* (Hunters' Latin) — the chapter on the lieutenant's journey through Atjeh, the [[Gajoweg]], [[Bireuën]], [[Takengeun]], [[Blang Rakal]], and the old hunter [[Datoek]].
6. *Atjeh's Transport-Olifanten* (Aceh's Transport-Elephants) — the chapter on the [[transport-olifanten]] of Aceh: [[Agam]] the male, [[Isah]], [[Sarah]] and [[Rimba]] the female elephants, and the [[Lam-toeulo]]-[[Tangsé]]-[[Geumpang]] transport.

## Sources and the *Toekan-Serie*

The book is volume 2 of the *Toekan-Serie* (Toucan Series), edited by G. L. Tichelman, former administrative official. The series takes its name from the Toekan — the rhinoceros hornbill — which, as the series' opening explains, "had for many peoples in the archipelago an extraordinary meaning: he was the culture-bringer, he accompanied the souls of the departed to the Land of the Dead, he had influence on the fertility of the fields and was the bringer of iron." The series is placed under the protection of this bird "who carries fire in his beak."

## Significance

*Oost-Indisch Journael* is significant as a source for the inter-war colonial memory of [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. The book — written in [[1947]], after the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese occupation]] and during the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution — looks back on "tempo doeloe" with "a mild smile of melancholy." The book's Aceh content — the Atjeh-moorden, the Soldaten-Dominee [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]], the [[Meusigit Raja]], the [[Gajoweg]], the [[transport-olifanten]] — preserves the inter-war colonial memory of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and its aftermath, told by the old Aceh fighter Musket and his circle. The book is therefore both a primary source on the inter-war colonial memory of Aceh and a retrospective interpretation of what the Aceh War had meant to the colonial officers who had fought it.

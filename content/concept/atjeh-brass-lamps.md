**Atjeh brass lamps** — the brass-cast (*messing-gietwerk*) lamps of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] that, in F. A. Wagner's *[[Sierkunst in Indonesië]]* ([[1949]]), are described as "mooi gemodelleerd" (beautifully modelled) — are the principal Atjeh product of the brass-casting tradition that Wagner records in his chapter "Metaalwerk" (Metalwork). Wagner mentions the Atjeh brass lamps in the section on *messing-gietwerk* (brass-casting), in the course of his survey of the regional brass-casting traditions of the archipelago. The Atjeh lamps are the only Atjeh object he records in the brass-casting section, and they are placed alongside the Batak and Padangse bovenlanden brass-work as the principal Sumatran traditions of *messing-gietwerk*.

## The passage in Wagner

After describing the *à-cire-perdue* (lost-wax) brass-casting technique, and after surveying the Batak brass-casting tradition (with its *tali pioe*, *poerpoeroer*, *goendoek*, *singa*, *boraspati*, and *hoeting* motifs) and the Padangse bovenlanden tradition (with its Hindoo-influenced relief ornament and its *plaatgietwerk*), Wagner turns to the other brass-casting regions: "Van de andere gebieden waar messing-gietwerk wordt vervaardigd, wordt hier alleen Atjeh genoemd, waar mooi gemodelleerde lampen voorkomen en de streken om het Matana-meer, waar de toegepaste versieringen grote overeenkomst vertonen met die, welke men bij de Toba-Bataks vindt."

The Atjeh brass lamps are thus singled out for their modelling — "mooi gemodelleerd" — a term that emphasises the three-dimensional form of the lamps rather than their surface decoration. The lamps are, in Wagner's account, the Atjeh contribution to the brass-casting tradition of the archipelago; the other region he names in the same sentence — the country around Lake Matana (in Central Sulawesi) — is mentioned only for the resemblance of its decoration to that of the Toba-Bataks. The Atjeh lamps, by contrast, are mentioned for their own quality: they are "mooi gemodelleerd," and they need no comparison to recommend them.

## The lamps and the brass-casting tradition

The brass-casting tradition that Wagner describes is the *à-cire-perdue* tradition — the lost-wax technique, in which a wax model is built up, encased in clay, melted out, and replaced by molten brass. The technique is, Wagner notes, the same as that used "ten tijde der Hindoe-overheersing" (in the time of the Hindu rule) for bronze casting, and it is "ook nu nog de werkwijze voor het gieten in messing" (still today the working method for brass casting). The Atjeh brass lamps are thus products of the same ancient technique that, in the Hindu period, produced the bronze sculptures of Java, and that, in Wagner's day, produced the brass-work of the Batak lands, the Padangse bovenlanden, Atjeh, and the country around Lake Matana.

The Atjeh brass lamps, in this tradition, are distinguished by their modelling — the three-dimensional form of the lamp, which Wagner praises as "mooi gemodelleerd." The lamps are not, in Wagner's account, distinguished by their surface decoration: he does not describe any surface ornament on the Atjeh lamps, in contrast to his detailed description of the Batak *tali pioe* and *singa* motifs. The Atjeh lamps are thus an example of brass-casting in which the form is the principal carrier of the decoration — the lamp is its own ornament, and the modeller's art is the principal art.

## The lamps in the Atjeh metalwork context

The Atjeh brass lamps are one of the four Atjeh metalwork specialities that Wagner singles out in his chapter on metalwork:

- The brass lamps (*messing-gietwerk*) — "mooi gemodelleerd"
- The [[Atjeh silver-wire ornament]] — bent silver-wire pieces and silver balls soldered onto the object
- The *[[heetsen]]* of gold — the heat-reddening of gold
- The [[tjawardi]] — the enamelling technique, "vrijwel alleen in Atjeh"

Together, the four specialities place Atjeh among the principal metalworking regions of the archipelago, and they show the Atjeh metalworker as a master of form (the brass lamps), of ornament (the silver-wire work), and of colour (the *heetsen* red and the *tjawardi* enamel). The brass lamps are the form-masterpiece of the four — the piece in which the Atjeh metalworker's art is most fully displayed in three dimensions.

## Significance

The Atjeh brass lamps are, in Wagner's account, the principal example of Atjeh brass-casting — and the only Atjeh object he records in the *messing-gietwerk* section. Their significance in the larger history of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]nese decorative art is that they show the Atjeh metalworker as a master of three-dimensional form, working in the same lost-wax tradition that, in the Hindu period, produced the bronzes of Java. The lamps' "mooi gemodelleerd" quality — their beautiful modelling — is, in turn, the metalwork counterpart of the *[[anker-motief]]*'s rounded geometry in weaving: both are expressions of the Atjeh craftsman's taste for form that is rounded, modelled, and complete in itself, rather than form that is angular, flat, or imposed.

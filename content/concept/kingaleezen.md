**Kingaleezen** was the term used by [[D. W. N. de Boer]], alongside *Chineezen*, for the non-indigenous natives resident in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] to whom the *[[Beschikkingsrecht in Atjeh|beschikkingsrecht]]* applied with greater rigour than to the Atjehnese themselves.

## Legal position

Towards such non-indigenous cultivators — "niet-inheemsche Inlanders, resp. niet-Inlanders" as De Boer's cautious phrase ran — the right of avail asserted itself somewhat more strictly. The non-indigenous settler was obliged not only to pay the *[[Haq radja taléë|haq radja taléë]]* but in addition to enter into the *[[Thiplah tano|thiplah-tano]]* contract, receiving a *[[Pangkaj|pangkaj]]* of a buffalo, seed and a twenty-guilder *[[Belandja|belandja]]*, and binding himself to cede half the cleared land, or each year half the half-crop, to the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]*. Where rice fields were concerned, the contract was granted only on an undertaking to work at least five *[[Naleh|naleh]]* of seed — about 0.8 hectare.

Against this stood the advantage that half of the cleared *[[Sawah|sawah]]* became the settler's "property," so that on departure he might sell that half, subject only to a right of preference in the *radja*.

## Later government control

De Boer noted in a footnote that alienation of land to persons not belonging to the indigenous population had since been taken in hand by the Gouvernement, under article 15 sub I in conjunction with article 29 sub 3 of the *[[Staatsblad 1919 No. 822|Zelfbestuursregelen]]*.

**The Wegenplan Sumatra** ("Sumatra road plan") was the systematic plan for a Sumatra-wide network of main roads — "a provisional wide-meshed, but island-wide network of main roads, also drivable with automobiles, connecting to the railways and tramways and to the always-navigable rivers" — instituted by the Inspectie voor de Verkeerswegen (Inspectorate for Traffic Routes) attached to the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]] in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the Wegenplan Sumatra included fifteen principal road projects, of which two were in the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency: the Alasweg from [[Kaban Djahe]] to [[Kota Tjane]] (project no. 13) and the Gajo-weg from [[Bireuen]] to [[Takengon]] (project no. 14).

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Wegenplan Sumatra

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Wegenplan Sumatra|Wegenplan Sumatra]] appears in the chapter on the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]]'s road construction in the Buitenbezittingen. The Bureau notes that, with the institution of the Inspectie voor de Verkeerswegen, "a new time has begun in the development of the road system there." Before the institution of the Inspectie, roads in the Buitenbezittingen had been built "on initiative of the administrative side" and "gradually in heerendienst (corvée labour)" — that is, by forced labour under the direction of administrative officials rather than by a centrally planned technical service.

The Bureau's account of the Wegenplan Sumatra lists fifteen principal projects, of which the two in the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency were:

- Project no. 13: the Alasweg, from [[Kaban Djahe|Kaban Djahe]] to [[Kota Tjane|Kota Tjane]]. Length 137 K.M.; cost f 1,173,000. For the connection of the [[Alaslanden|Alas lands]] with the Karo-hoogvlakte (Karo highlands) and the wegennet of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]]. "In the main completed."
- Project no. 14: the Gajo-weg, from [[Bireuen|Bireuen]] to [[Takengon|Takengon]]. Length 76 K.M.; cost f 1,496,000. For the connection of the [[Gajo-landen|Gajo lands]] with the North coast. "Completed."

The Bureau notes that, of the fifteen Sumatra road projects listed, the Gajo-weg from [[Bireuen]] to [[Takengon]] was one of only a handful listed as "completed" by the end of the decade. The total length of road under construction under the Wegenplan Sumatra at the end of [[1914]] was "somewhat over 970 K.M. weglengte, for which an amount will prove necessary of f 12,100,000, of which about f 9 million already authorised." Of this, "somewhat over f 7,000,000" had been spent by the end of [[1914]]. The existing net — that is, the local and gewestelijk networks already in existence before the Wegenplan — totalled about 3,500 K.M. of hoofdwegen (main roads, of which about 2,700 K.M. paved) and 6,700 K.M. of binnenwegen (interior roads, of which about 900 K.M. paved). The Wegenplan Sumatra would, when complete, unite these existing networks "into one large Sumatra-net."

The Bureau's account also notes that "the still under consideration plans further include the laying of a connection between [[Palembang|Palembang]] and the [[Lampongsche Districten|Lampong Districts]], between [[Palembang|Palembang]] and Middle Sumatra ([[Djambi|Djambi]]), and further the opening up of [[Koerintji|Koerintji]] by a road to [[Tapan|Tapan]], the connecting of the [[Benkoelen|Benkoelen]] and [[Sumatra's Westkust|Padangsche]] wegennet, and that of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]] with the [[Acheh|Atjehsche]]."

## Significance

The [[Wegenplan Sumatra|Wegenplan Sumatra]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal infrastructural project of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade for the integration of the Sumatran gewesten — including the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency — into a single road network. The completion of the Gajo-weg from [[Bireuen]] to [[Takengon]] and the Alasweg from [[Kaban Djahe]] to [[Kota Tjane]] marks the formal integration of the Atjehnese interior highlands into the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] Dutch administrative order.

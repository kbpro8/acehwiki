**The Sjabandar** (from Persian *shāh-bandar*, "king of the port") was the harbor master of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], an office of considerable consequence in the political constitution of the sultanate. The sjabandar's place in the hierarchy of Acehnese officials is attested by the source work's description of [[Panglima Tiban Mohammed|Panglima Tiban Mohammed]] — the sjabandar of Atjeh in [[1872]] — who arrived in [[September 1872]] at [[Riouw|Riouw]] first without, then in [[December]] with credentials from the sultan, with various communications, and whose later submission to the Dutch in [[April 1879]] was an event of sufficient importance to be recorded in the source work's narrative of the war.

## The Office

The sjabandar was the official responsible for the regulation of trade and shipping at the port of Atjeh, and for the relations between the sultan's court and the foreign merchants who traded there. The office was therefore one of the principal points of contact between the sultanate and the outside world, and the holder of the office was a figure of considerable influence. In the political constitution of the sultanate, the sjabandar stood beside the [[Panglima Polem|panglima polim]] (the head of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]]), the [[panglima missigit raja|panglima missigit raja]] (the head of the territory of the great mosque), and the [[toekoe kali malikon ade|toekoe kali malikon ade]] (originally the chief justice) as one of the principal officials of the sultan's immediate territory, not belonging to the three [[sagi|sagis]].

## Panglima Tiban Mohammed

The most notable sjabandar of Atjeh in the period covered by the source work was [[Panglima Tiban Mohammed|Panglima Tiban Mohammed]], who arrived in [[September 1872]] at Riouw with various communications from the sultan's court. From these it appeared that there were two parties at the sultan's court: the Arab party under the hostile rijksbestuurder [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdoerrahman]], and a native party that wished, with Dutch help, to be rid of him. The Indies government decided to support the sultan against the Arab party, but only on condition of recognition of Dutch supremacy. After the outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the long campaigns that followed, the sjabandar submitted to the Dutch in [[April 1879]] — "with the intention of joining us to bring the war to a close as soon as possible, and who has rendered us great services in the following years."

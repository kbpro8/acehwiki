**Moekim** (from Arabic *moeqim* "inhabitant") was the Atjèhnese ecclesiastical-administrative unit, originally a Friday-prayer circle of (typically four) villages with a mosque. Each moekim had an *imeum* as head. By Kreemer's time, moekim's had become administrative *bestuursressorten* (administrative districts).

## Origin

The *moekim* originated as an ecclesiastical unit — a Friday-prayer circle of typically four villages (*gampöng*) with a mosque (*meunasah* or *meuseugit*). The head of each moekim was the *imeum* (from Arabic *imam*), originally the leader of the Friday service.

## The three sagi's of Groot Atjèh

The three *sagi*'s of [[Groot Atjèh|Groot Atjèh]] were named for their number of moekim's:
1. **[[XXII Moekim's|XXII Moekim's]]** ("Twenty-two Moekim's")
2. **[[XXV Moekim's|XXV Moekim's]]** ("Twenty-five Moekim's")
3. **[[XXVI Moekim's|XXVI Moekim's]]** ("Twenty-six Moekim's")

## Evolution into administrative units

Originally religious-ecclesiastical units, the *moekim*'s evolved into administrative *bestuursressorten* (administrative districts). The *imeum*'s also evolved: originally the religious leader (Arab. *imam*) of the Friday service, the *imeum* later became a hereditary secular head ("Oelèëbalang tjout") without religious character. By Kreemer's time, *imeum*'s were *adathoofden* (customary heads) subordinate to Oelèëbalang's or independent; there were approximately 58 imeum's in Groot Atjèh plus 22 independent.

## The VII Moekim's Pidië

A notable moekim-federation was the **VII Moekim's Pidië** (comprising Nitjah, Tandjong, Beurabö, Gögö, Peudaja, Koenjèt, Palöh, and Geuleudiëng), which was transferred from the Gouvernementsgebied of Groot Atjèh to the new afdeeling Pidië as part of the new onderafdeeling Padang Tidji under the [[bestuursindeeling van 1922|bestuursindeeling van 1922]].

## Wakeuëh-districts

Some moekim's were *wakeuëh*-districts (free-lordly domains) whose chiefs were withdrawn from the authority of the sagi-heads. Examples: in the XXV Moekim's — Loeëng Bata, Pagar Ajé, Lam Sajoen; in the XXII Moekim's — the III Moekim's Keureukön; in the XXVI Moekim's — Lho' Goelöng, Tjadé' Mön Batèë, Koeala Gigiëng.

**Melasaké pampang** — also **Melasaké-pampang**, in the Lampong-Semangka origin legend the ***boom Melasaké pampang*** — is the "wonder-tree" of [[Belalau|the Belalau plateau]] in South [[Sumatra]] whose "roots stood in the air, while the leaves hung downwards" — the principal allegorical form, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, of the inverted ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]]. It is the central image of De Roo de la Faille's chapter *Melasaké pampang*, and the South-Sumatran counterpart of the Minangkabau *baringin songrang* and the Perak *beringin sonsang*.

## The Lampong-Semangka legend

The Belalau plateau, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] records (citing Resident [[Westenenk|Westenenk]]'s report in the *Eerste Java-congres*, p. 27), was the *tanah asal mula jadi* — the land of origin — of the four *marga*s of Segalo- or Batoe-berak, Beloenggoe, Kambahang and Soekau, who of old formed a league. The same tradition held that the land had been inhabited by *Boda's* and *Toemi's* — *bangsa djihin dan déwa* — whose *kabesaran* (state-ornament) was a tree whose roots stood in the air, while the leaves (*seboekau* named) of two kinds hung downwards; that tree was called ***Melasaké pampang***. The Minangkabau princes' sons, the legend adds, drove out the Boda's and Toemi's and seized the Melasaké pampang.

## Decoding: *wilasa* and the inverted Taroema

The name ***Melasaké pampang*** [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as the causative of *wilasa* — "playing with beautiful appearance" — by the making of a riddle, a *rebus*, in which the underlying figure (*pa-mpang*, contraction of *roe-pa* + *toempang*) is laid upon = *toempang*, so that the image of the allegory, namely the *boom* (tree), becomes visible. The figure is *taroe* — the Kawi word for "tree" — which is also the *taroe* of *taroe-na-ma*, i.e. [[Taroema]]. The "tree whose roots stand in the air" is thus the inverted [[Taroema]] — the ancestral land "turned upside down" — and the *seboekau* leaves of two kinds are *roepa ka-lih* — *da-on-ke-da-ton, Se-m-boe-dja kaw-i* — the two leaves of the [[Sambodja|Sambodja]] of the chronicle tradition.

## The Boda's and Toemi's

The *Boda's* and *Toemi's* whom the Minangkabau princes' sons drove out [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *bo...da-toe-mi* — i.e. *datoe boe-mi* — "of the lineage *sa-dji hin-a*", the "small rajas and *satrija*s" of the [[Sambodja|Sambodja]]. Their expulsion by the Minangkabau princes' sons is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the allegorical record of the dispersal of the [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] of [[Taroema]] — the same dispersal that the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] would close in military form.

## The Radja Sakti of 1686-1687

The same inverted-Taroema allegory, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, underlies the South-Sumatran career of **Radja Sakti** — the "holy king" who, after making the sea unsafe around [[Bangka|Bangka]] and [[Belitung|Billiton]] as a sea-rover, was driven out in [[1686]]–[[1687]] and settled at [[Laut Kawar|Laoet Kawar]] in Semangka with 400 men, women, and children. His subsequent career at [[Bencoolen|Benkoelen]] — where he was worshipped as a "holy" figure, coined money, established a market, and wrote to the [[British East India Company|East India Company]] — is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the late-seventeenth-century echo of the [[Melasaké pampang|inverted-Taroema]] tradition.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, *Melasaké pampang* is the principal South-Sumatran allegorical form of the inverted ancestral land of [[Taroema]] — the *bedekte taal* image by which the Lampong-Semangka origin legend recorded the dispersal of the [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] of [[Aroe|Aru]]. Its survival in three regional traditions — Lampong-Semangka, Minangkabau, and Perak — also marks it as the most widely-diffused single allegory of the [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] corpus.

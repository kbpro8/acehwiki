**Ranjoe** (modern spelling *ranjau*; also written *ran(d)joes*) — a sharpened-bamboo obstacle — is a defensive vegetation used in Atjehese and Dutch fortification practice in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], consisting of 60 to 70 centimetre-long sharply pointed pieces of bamboo driven into the ground in oblique rows behind one another, directed toward the side from which an attacker is expected to come. The ranjoe forms "voor dezen eene zeer werkdadige hindernis" — for the attacker a very effective obstacle. The biographer [[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]], in his footnote to the [[Missigit Longbatta operation (31 December 1874 – 13 January 1875)|Missigit Longbatta operation]], defines ranjoes as follows: "Ran(d)joes zijn 60 à 70 centimeter lange, scherp aangepunte stukken bamboe, welke, in eenige rijen achter elkander in schuinen stand in den grond gedreven ('geplant') en gericht naar de zijde, van waar een aanvaller moet komen, voor dezen eene zeer werkdadige hindernis vormen."

## Use at Missigit Longbatta

The [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit Longbatta]] redoubt constructed by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] between [[1 January|1]] and [[13 January]] [[13 January]] [[1875]] was, in the design of [[T. W. G. Merz|Eerste-Luitenant Merz]]'s eyewitness account, to be surrounded by a horizontal wire entanglement, "waarom een twee meter breede ranjoebeplanting, welke laatste natuurlijk gaandeweg moest worden aangemaakt, door voor te schrijven, dat iedere man per dag in zijn vrijen tijd 30 ranjoe's moest leveren." The ranjoe planting was thus a cumulative obstacle, built up by the daily quota of 30 ranjoes per man, delivered by each soldier in his free time. The quota system is one of the few recorded instances of the systematic construction of a defensive vegetation obstacle by a Dutch garrison in the Atjeh war.

## Significance

The ranjoe is a native Atjehese obstacle adopted and systematised by the Dutch. Its use at [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit Longbatta]] — together with the [[bamboe-doerie|bamboe-doerie]] hedge at [[Kuta Alam|Benting Kota Alam]] — illustrates the practical accommodation of Dutch engineer practice to Atjehese fortification conventions. The ranjoe also figures prominently in the larger Dutch fortification system of the Atjeh war, particularly in the perimeter works around Koeta Radja and the line of blockhouses along the [[Atjeh Tram|Atjeh Tram]].

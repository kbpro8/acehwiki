**Burda (poem)**, formally titled *Qasīdat al‑Burdah* (“Poem of the Cloak”), is a celebrated 13th‑century Arabic ode that praises the Prophet [[Muhammad]]. Traditional Acehnese accounts attribute the composition to the Sufi scholar [[Imam al‑Bukhari|Imam al‑Bukhari]], while modern Arabic literary scholarship identifies the author as the Egyptian Sufi poet [[Al‑Būṣirī]]. The poem was rendered into Malay in the 16th century and circulated widely in the Sultanate of [[Aceh|Aceh]], where it became a principal vehicle for the diffusion of Arabic religious terminology into Acehnese literary culture.

## Malay Translation and Transmission

A 16th‑century Malay translation of the *Burda*—recorded in the manuscript *16de eeuwse Maleise vertaling van de Burda*—was produced by an unnamed Acehnese scholar for devotional use among the Muslim elite of Aceh. The translation, often referred to in Dutch sources as the *Maleise tekst van de Burda*, incorporates a dense inter‑lingual glossary in which Arabic terms are rendered with Acehnese‑Malay equivalents (e.g. [[A. 'ahd|A. 'ahd]] “setia, afspraak, avereenkomst”). This glossary demonstrates the deep penetration of Arabic theological concepts into Acehnese religious discourse and provides a valuable source for the study of Arabic loanwords in the Acehnese Malay dialect.

## Cultural Impact

The Malay version of the *Burda* served as a vehicle for disseminating Islamic theology and Arabic vocabulary among Acehnese and broader Malay audiences. Its widespread recitation and copying influenced religious poetry, devotional practice, and the development of a theological lexicon that underpinned later Acehnese religious literature, legal texts, and oral tradition. The translation’s extensive list of Arabic‑Malay lexical items illustrates how the poem functioned as a conduit for Arabic religious concepts entering the local cultural sphere.

## Significance

The translation of the *Burda* facilitated the incorporation of Arabic theological vocabulary into the Acehnese language, shaping the theological lexicon that underlies subsequent Acehnese religious literature and legal discourse. Its role in the diffusion of Arabic religious terminology highlights the broader processes of cultural and linguistic exchange between the Arab‑Islamic world and the Malay Archipelago during the early modern period.

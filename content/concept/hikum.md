**Hikum** (also *hukom*; from Arabic *ḥukm*, "judgment" or "decree") was the Acehnese term for [[Islamic]] law — the body of rules derived from the Quran and the traditions of the Prophet Mohammed, as interpreted through the Shafi'ite school of jurisprudence dominant in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] and throughout the Malay-Indonesian world.

## Relationship to Adat

The hikum coexisted in tension with the [[Adat|customary law]] (*adat*) of the Acehnese. The upholding of the hikum was the special province of the [[Teungku|teungku]], while the adat was primarily the concern of the [[Keuchi'|keuchi']] and [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]]. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] emphasised that the introduction of Islam into Acheh had overlaid the indigenous adat in certain domains without replacing it — the Acehnese courts applied a mixture of both systems.

## Criminal Punishments

The punishments imposed by Mohammedan law for serious offences — including stoning (*rajam*) for adultery where both parties had previously had lawful intercourse, and 100 lashes followed by banishment for at least a year where they had not — were of exceptional occurrence in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]]. The application of such punishments was extremely rare, "undoubtedly due to some extent to the great difficulty of proving illicit intercourse according to the requirements of the Mohammedan law of evidence." One noted example was [[Habib Abdurrahman]], who enforced the stoning punishment, causing the news to spread throughout the whole country.

## Shafi'ite School

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] notes that the "ruling" Shafi'ite doctrine held that paying off of prayer obligations (*çalāts*) was superfluous or even impossible in certain interpretations — a point of religious and economic significance for the Acehnese population in connection with prayers and death customs.

## Hukom and the Aceh War

The dogmas of Islam on the subject of religious war (*jihad*) — so fanatical in their terms — supplied the principal stimulus to the obstinate Acehnese rebellion against the Dutch. The [[Teungku|ulamas]] consistently argued that the war ordained of Allah must be waged in conformity with His decree, giving them a powerful tool to maintain popular resistance.

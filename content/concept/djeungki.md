The **djeungki** (Gayo *djingki*, Alas *id.*) is the Atjehnese foot-pounder rice mill — a wooden lever-and-mortar device used for the hulling of rice, in which a heavy wooden pestle is attached to one end of a long lever, and the lever is worked by a woman who treads on the other end. The *djeungki* is the principal Atjehnese rice-hulling device, and is found in most Atjehnese villages, where it is treated as a communal facility available for the use of all. The *djeungki* is documented by [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]] in his [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]].

## Construction and Operation

The *djeungki* consists of a long wooden lever — the *wipplank* (seesaw plank) — pivoted on a fulcrum, with a heavy wooden pestle (*aluë* in Acehnese, *toetoe* in Gayo) attached to one end. The pestle works into a wooden mortar — the *peusòng* in Acehnese, *loesoeng* in Gayo and Alas — in which the rice is placed. The operator — always a woman — stands on the other end of the lever, and by her weight drives the pestle into the mortar. The lever is then raised by the elasticity of the wood or by a springing motion of the operator, and the pestle is lifted for the next stroke.

The *djeungki* is thus a labour-saving device: by the mechanical advantage of the lever, the operator can deliver a heavy pounding stroke with little effort, and the hulling of a large quantity of rice is made possible without the back-breaking labour of the simple hand-pestle.

## The Communal Facility

Kreemer notes that "in de meeste dorpen vindt men maar een paar djeungki's, die echter als tot aller gebruik beschikbaar worden beschouwd" — in most villages only a few *djeungki* are found, which are however considered available for the use of all. The *djeungki* is thus a communal facility, owned by an individual householder but available to the village women for the hulling of their rice. This communal use is one of the small but characteristic features of Atjehnese village life, in which the principal agricultural tools are privately owned but communally used.

## The Hulling Process

The *djeungki* is used for the *peutèh* — the hulling or peeling — of the rice. Kreemer describes the process: "Vóór de padi wordt gestampt wordt ze op een droogmat uitgespreid en zóó in de zon te drogen gelegd. Is ze voldoende droog ... dan wordt ze samengeraapt. Men kan nu tot het ontbolsteren of pellen van de rijst overgaan, hetgeen overal door stampen plaats heeft, en uitsluitend vrouwenwerk is." — Before the padi is pounded, it is spread on a drying mat and so laid in the sun to dry. When it is sufficiently dry, it is gathered up. One can now proceed to the husking or peeling of the rice, which takes place everywhere by pounding, and is exclusively women's work.

The pounding is typically done twice, sometimes three times, occasionally four times. After the first pounding (*seuba* or *seuneuba* in Acehnese, Gayo *mëmpor*, Alas *mpoch skali*), in which the greater part of the husk has been removed, the rice is winnowed — *tampèë* in Acehnese (Gayo *měnapi* or *ngésè*, Alas *měnapi* or *měsigi*) — in a triangular winnowing fan (*djeunòng* in Acehnese, Gayo *nice*, Alas *dirèc*). After the second pounding (*sròh* or *seuneuròh*, Gayo *měndjoeh*, Alas *mpoch dòea kali*) and a second winnowing, the rice is sorted into three classes:

- The *gabah* — the unhusked rice (*seungò* in Acehnese, Gayo *lěmbatah* or *blatah*, Alas *bătah*) — that has not been hulled by the pounding, and that collects on the broad part of the winnowing fan.
- The husked rice (*breueh* in Acehnese, Gayo *òrěh*, Alas *bras*) — collected in the middle of the winnowing fan, and itself sorted into *öram breueh* (whole grains) and *òedjong seuròh* (broken grains).
- The *ménir* — the finely-broken rice (*leungkoët* in Acehnese, Gayo *kloemit*, Alas *bëning*) — produced by the pounding.

The *ménir* is not edible; the *òedjong seuròh* is edible. The bran (*lhòë* in Acehnese, Gayo *lěda* or *dédan*) is used as cattle-feed or as fertiliser on the rice-field or in the gardens. The *leungkoët* is mostly used as chicken or duck feed.

## The Alternative: The Simple Mortar

The *djeungki* is sometimes replaced by a simple stamping-block — the *peusòng* in Acehnese (Gayo *loesoeng*, Alas *lisoeng*) — in which the pestle is worked by hand. This simple mortar is used when the *djeungki* is not available, or for small quantities of rice. The *djeungki* is, however, the standard device for the hulling of larger quantities.

## The Absence of Mechanical Rice-Peelers

Kreemer notes that mechanical rice-peeling mills (*rijstpelmolens*) are unknown in Atjeh. The colonial government had, in [[1915]], with a view to the sale of rice to the European plantations at [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]], begun preparations for the establishment of such mills, but the negotiations with Chinese traders came to nothing. The government had then considered having the peeling done by the *landschappen* (the self-governing native states) themselves, but this too had come to nothing. Calculations showed that the rice so produced would be at least as expensive as [[Rangoon|Rangoon]] rice, and its quality was doubtful. (See *Deli Courant*, [[8 August]] [[1916]], No. 177.)

## Significance

The *djeungki* is the principal Atjehnese rice-hulling device, and its communal use is one of the characteristic features of Atjehnese village life. The *djeungki* preserves the technical lexicon of the Atjehnese rice-hulling process, alongside the Acehnese-Gayo-Alas vocabulary of the pounding, winnowing, and sorting of the rice. The absence of mechanical rice-peeling mills in Atjeh — and the failure of the colonial government's 1915 attempt to establish such mills at Koeala Simpang — is one of the small but instructive features of the early-twentieth-century Atjeh rice-economy: the colonial state wished to develop an export rice industry, but the technical and economic obstacles proved, at that date, insuperable.

**Kongsi** were semi‑autonomous Chinese mining and trade cooperatives that operated in the interior of **[[Borneo]]** during the nineteenth century. Their fortified settlements, known as **[[Kongsi forts]]**, became centers of resistance against both **[[Dutch East Indies]]** and **[[British Empire]]** authorities. In the 1850s Dutch naval forces launched expeditions to dismantle Kongsi strongholds on the west coast of Borneo, actions that were linked to broader efforts to secure the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]**’s trade routes from disruption.

## Relevance to Aceh

- Kongsi activities contributed to instability on Borneo’s western coast, affecting Acehnese maritime commerce.
- Dutch suppression of Kongsi settlements was part of a strategy to protect Aceh‑related trade.

The **pacification of Aceh** (Dutch: *De pacificatie van Atjeh*) refers to the transition from military conquest to stable colonial governance in [[Acheh|Atjèh]] (Aceh) — the eventual subjugation of the Achehnese sultanate by Dutch military and political means, beginning with the first Dutch expedition to Atjeh in [[1873]] and carried through to completion under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] ([[1898]]–[[1904]]) and his subsequent tenure as Governor-General ([[1904]]–[[1909]]). The process involved a combination of military pressure, political co-optation of local chiefs, and economic development, built on the fundamental principle that the indigenous leadership — particularly the *oelèëbalangs* — should be supported and guided rather than suppressed. This principle was advised by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|C. Snouck Hurgronje]] and subsequently broken by [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], whose deviation triggered the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] and his removal. It was commemorated in the colonial capital by the [[Atjeh Monument]] in the Wilhelminapark at [[Weltevreden]], and on the battlefield itself by the [[Peutjot Cemetery|cemetery at Peutjot]] near [[Kota Radja]].

## The Van Heutsz system

Van Heutsz's approach, developed in close collaboration with Snouck Hurgronje, rested on several pillars:

- **Unconditional recognition of Dutch sovereignty** through the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]], a standard compact by which native chiefs acknowledged Dutch suzerainty in exchange for protection of their territorial authority.
- **Support for the traditional chiefs** (*oelèëbalangs*) against the religiously motivated resistance of the *ulama*, rather than punishing and replacing them.
- **Economic incentives** such as the creation of *landschapskassen* (regional treasuries) that gave chiefs a direct stake in good governance.
- **Military mobility** using small, highly trained *marechaussée* units to deal with remaining pockets of resistance, rather than large fixed garrisons.
- **Gradual reduction** of forced labour and arbitrary fines to win over the population.

The intellectual foundation for this system was laid by Snouck Hurgronje's ethnographic study of Acehnese society, *De Atjehers* ([[1893]]–[[1894]]), which demonstrated that the [[Aceh War]] could not be won by military means alone, and that the chiefs — not the Sultan or the *ulama* — were the natural partners of colonial governance. Snouck's analysis of the [[Gampong|gampong]], the [[Moekim|moekim]], and the territorial *oelèëbalangschappen* provided the framework on which Van Heutsz's policies were built.

## The British experience confirmed

The difficulty of the task was already visible in the British experience of [[1786]] — that to secure order in a place like [[Penang|Pinang]] and Aceh it would be necessary "to subdue all the chiefs" and "to overawe the inhabitants." The Dutch, compelled during the second decennial period of the nineteenth century to take measures to ensure the safety of foreign trade in Aceh, discovered that the same lesson applied: military conquest alone was insufficient without the active cooperation of the local power-holders.

## The Gayo-Alas campaign and the final military phase

The last major military operation of the pacification was the [[Gajoland|Gajo]]-[[Alaslanden|Alas]] expedition of [[1904]], led by Van Daalen when still a lieutenant colonel. The campaign broke the last major centre of resistance in central Aceh, bringing the interior under Dutch control and setting the stage for the transition to civil administration. After [[1905]], with Van Heutsz as Governor-General overseeing from Batavia, the focus shifted to consolidation and administration.

## The Van Daalen deviation and its correction

The pacification policy was severely tested during the governorship of [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen]] ([[1905]]–[[1908]]), who abandoned the conciliatory approach in favour of punishment and coercion — arbitrary fines, internment of chiefs and their families at [[Sabang]], dismissal of *oelèëbalangs* without replacement, and general contempt for local institutions. His deviation produced the *vrij hevige opflikkering van het verzet* (fairly fierce flare-up of resistance) of [[1907]] and [[1908]] and triggered the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] of 1907–1908. The return of [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] to the governorship in 1908 restored the original policy. Swart's *verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek* — conciliatory and confidence-building policy — drew the chiefs who had been held down by force "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde" (ever more, from conviction, to the Dutch side) and inaugurated the *periode van rust* that allowed the economic and administrative development documented in *De Buitenbezittingen*.

## Results

By [[1909]], when Van Heutsz left office, Aceh was largely peaceful. The economic infrastructure — roads, markets, handicraft schools, and agricultural projects — was developing; the population, while still scarred by decades of war, was increasingly accepting Dutch rule; and the cautious replacement of military by civil administrators had begun. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats this consolidation as "een krachtig bewijs" — powerful proof — of the success of the pacification, which had come "betrekkelijk snel en onverwacht" (relatively quickly and unexpectedly). The pacification of Aceh became a model for the subsequent expansion of Dutch control over the outer islands.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* ([[1926]]) — The Pacification

The *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* refers to the pacification of Atjeh in three distinct contexts, each of which attests to its central place in the official colonial narrative:

1. **Governor Van Heutsz's achievement**: As Governor of Atjeh from [[1897]], Van Heutsz "bewerkte de onderwerping van dat gewest" (accomplished the subjugation of that territory).

2. **The Atjeh Monument**: The monument in the Wilhelminapark at [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], "werd opgericht ter gedachtenis aan de pacificatie van Atjeh" (was erected in memory of the pacification of Atjeh).

3. **The Peutjot Cemetery**: Near [[Kota Radja]], the cemetery "werd voornamelijk bestemd voor de op de verschillende expedities in Atjeh gesneuvelden" (was primarily intended for those who fell on the various expeditions in Atjeh).

## The Policy Shift

The atlas also provides crucial context for understanding how the pacification was achieved. It notes that the [[Buitengewesten]] had previously suffered from neglect under the [[Onthoudingspolitiek|policy of abstention]] (*onthoudingspolitiek*), but that at the end of the previous century this was replaced "onder den Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz door een krachtige en doelbewuste politiek van het onder ons gezag brengen er van" (under the Governor-General Van Heutsz by an energetic and purposeful policy of bringing [the territories] under our authority). The pacification was not merely a military event but was accompanied by — and made possible by — the construction of roads, railways, and other infrastructure that integrated the formerly independent territories into the colonial state. By [[1926]], the year of the atlas's publication, the war in Atjeh — which had begun in [[1873]] — was fully in the past, commemorated by monuments rather than fought on battlefields.

## Significance

The pacification of Aceh was a turning point in Dutch colonial history. It demonstrated that a combination of military force, political acumen, and economic development could succeed where pure coercion had failed for decades. It also set the stage for the "ethical policy" of the twentieth century, which sought to balance imperial control with the welfare of the colonised population. In the broader history of the Aceh War, the pacification is often cited as demonstrating that the critical variable in colonial governance is the relationship between the administration and indigenous institutions — a lesson the Van Daalen episode taught at great cost. The atlas's commemoration of the pacification in [[1926]] — through the [[Atjeh Monument]], the [[Peutjot Cemetery|cemetery at Peutjot]], and the portrait gallery of "Groote Mannen" — fixed the pacification as the centrepiece of the official colonial reckoning of the late Dutch East Indies.

## Account from *DLP-84-Atjeh*

The summary entry in *DLP-84-Atjeh* characterises the **Pacification of Aceh** (Dutch: *Pacificatie*) as the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]] during which the Dutch colonial administration achieved effective control over the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Aceh]] through sustained military pressure (Dutch: *aanhoudende militaire druk*), the construction of forts (Dutch: *forten*), and the integration of Christian soldiers (Dutch: *christelijke soldaten*) and chaplains (Dutch: *aalmoezeniers*) into the colonial war effort. The DLP-84 sketch evokes the contrast between the *Pacificatie* and the preceding era of armed Acehnese resistance by reference to "the time before the *Pacificatie*" (Dutch: *de tijd vóór de Pacificatie*), when weapons such as the *donderbus* (blunderbuss) and the *zwaard* (sword) were still common in the Atjehnese hinterland — a transition from the older, more disorderly armed resistance to the subdued colonial order (Dutch: *koloniale orde*) that the *Pacificatie* imposed.

The DLP-84 entry also records the religious-missionary dimension of the *Pacificatie*: Christian soldiers, especially the [[Ambonese soldiers|Ambonese]] contingents of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussée]], were supported by *soldier-pastors* (Dutch: *soldaten-pastoors*) such as [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] and [[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor Verbraak]], whose field-chaplaincy reinforced the Dutch "civilizing mission" narrative (Dutch: *beschavingsmissie*) that accompanied the military pacification. The chaplaincy was thus an integral component of the *Pacificatie* — supplying both the moral economy of the colonial troops and the ideological framework within which the subjugation of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Aceh]] was presented to the metropole as a Christian duty of order and uplift.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the 1893 conceptual articulation of *pacificatie*

[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 tract]] — written five years before his [[1898]] appointment as Governor of Atjeh — articulates the *pacificatie* as the political consolidation to follow military *onderwerping*, and so provides the conceptual blueprint Van Heutsz would later implement as Governor. In Van Heutsz's tract, *pacificatie* — also *de pacificatie*, *de volledige pacificatie van Atjeh*, or, in the verbal form Van Heutsz attributes to [[Laging Tobias|Gouverneur Laging Tobias]], *verstandig pacificeerend te werk gaan* — is the *staatkundig* counterpart of the *militair* victory: the work of the *ambtenaren* who were to bring *maatregelen des vredes*, *geregeld bestuur*, *goede rechtsbedeeling*, *landbouw*, *handel* and *nijverheid* to a country "dat wij aan ons gezag onderworpen hebben". The *pacificatie* is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the political predicate the [[Blokkade|blokkade]] could not produce, the [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]] of [[1884]] had excluded, and the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] — itself a *maatregel des vredes van duurzamen aard* — was uniquely able to sustain.

### The *ambtenaren*'s post-conquest task

Van Heutsz's most explicit single statement of the *pacificatie* as the political task that follows upon military conquest is on printed page 32 of the 1893 tract, where the *ambtenaren* — the civil officials whose business it is to convert a military *onderwerping* into a settled *bestuur* — are defined by the very task that the *blokkade* excludes them from:

> "De ambtenaren toch, wier taak het is om een land, dat wij aan ons gezag onderworpen hebben, door maatregelen des vredes, door geleidelijke invoering van een geregeld, op regtraardigheid gegrond bestuur, door goede rechtsbedeeling enz. tot orde en rust en verder door opbeuring van landbouw, handel en nijverheid tot welvaart te brengen, hebben bij toepassing van eene blokkade geen taak: zij kunnen dan geen invloed op de binnenlandsche aangelegenheid hebben".

The *ambtenaren*'s task is, in this passage, the *pacificatie* in its fullest sense: the conversion of the *[[Onderwerping|onderwerping]]* — the act of subjugation — into the *orde en rust* and *welvaart* that constitute a settled political order. The instruments are *maatregelen des vredes* — measures of peace — and the *maatregelen des vredes* are, in their concrete form, the *geregeld bestuur*, the *goede rechtsbedeeling*, the *opbeuring van landbouw, handel en nijverheid*. The *blokkade*, by contrast, is the *geweldig oorlogsmiddel* — the tremendous instrument of war — that excludes the *ambtenaren* from any such task, because it produces only *verbittering* and not the *vertrouwen* on which a settled *bestuur* must rest.

### *Pacificatie* and *onderwerping*: the two predicates of the war's end

The *pacificatie* is, in Van Heutsz's argument, the second of the two predicates the war was fought to produce. The first is the *onderwerping* — the act of subjugation — which is the military predicate the campaigns of [[K. van der Heyden|Generaal van der Heijden]] in [[1878]]–[[1879]] had briefly achieved and the [[1884]] *Concentratie* had abandoned. The second is the *pacificatie* — the political consolidation of the *onderwerping* — which is the task the *ambtenaren* were to perform and the *Concentratie* had excluded them from. The two predicates are joined in the formula Van Heutsz uses on page 34:

> "Konden wij nu hetzelfde van de sluiting verwachten als bij den aanvang van den oorlog? Konden wij met eenigen grond verwachten, dat de Atjehers gelooven zouden, dat wij ditmaal met volharding zouden te werk gaan en volhouden, totdat het doel onderwerping en pacificatie van het geheele land zou verkregen zijn?"

The formula — *het doel onderwerping en pacificatie van het geheele land* — fixes the *pacificatie* as the political object the war's continuation was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, supposed to produce: the consolidation, in the form of settled *bestuur* and *welvaart*, of the *onderwerping* the campaigns had won. The formula is doubly significant: it names the *pacificatie* as the joint goal of the war (alongside the *onderwerping*), and it identifies the *pacificatie* as the predicate the post-[[1884]] policy oscillations had failed to produce — the *doel* the *Atjehers* no longer believed the Dutch would pursue with *volharding*.

### *Pacificatie* and the *scheepvaartregeling*: the *maatregel des vredes*

The *pacificatie*'s operational instrument, in Van Heutsz's prescription, is the *scheepvaartregeling* — the controlled routing of all Atjehnese maritime trade through Dutch-administered ports. The *scheepvaartregeling* is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the *maatregel des vredes van duurzamen aard* — the measure of peace of enduring kind — by which the *pacificatie* could be sustained. The contrast with the *blokkade* is explicit on page 36 of the tract:

> "Bovendien is de scheepvaartregeling een maatregel des vredes van duurzamen aard, terwijl blokkade een oorlogsmiddel is, dat slechts tijdelijk kan worden toegepast".

The *scheepvaartregeling* converts the coercion of the closure into the *opbeuring van handel* — the promotion of trade — that is itself the *pacificatie*'s substance. Van Heutsz's prescription is, in turn, that the *pacificatie* must be *volledig* — complete — across the entire *rijk*, including both the [[Groot-Atjeh|interior]] and the [[Onderhoorigheden|coastal dependencies]]. The formula Van Heutsz uses on page 67, in his critique of [[Scherer|Resident Scherer]]'s softer *scheepvaartregeling*, is the *volledige pacificatie van Atjeh*: "*Om dit alles duidelijk aan te toonen moet ik Scherer's lezing van 20 Januari 1891 in het Indisch genootschap ter hand nemen en achtereenvolgens zijne in 4 punten geformuleerde denkbeelden bespreken met uiteenzetting van hetgeen daarin goed en niet goed is, het waarom van een en ander, zoomede wat daaraan ontbreekt om tot de volledige pacificatie van Atjeh te geraken*". The *volledige pacificatie* — the complete pacification — is, in Van Heutsz's critique, the test Scherer's softer *scheepvaartregeling* fails.

### The exclusion of the *ambtenaren* under the Concentratie

The *pacificatie* is, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the political form the [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]] of [[1884]] had excluded. The *Concentratie* — the withdrawal of all outlying Dutch posts to the *geconcentreerde stelling* of [[Kota Radja]] combined with the *rigoureuse blokkade* of the entire coast — had, in its very form, excluded the *ambtenaren* from the *pacificatie*'s task. The *assistent-resident ter Noord- en Oostkust* was abolished; the *controleurs* in the *onderafdeelingen* of Groot-Atjeh were abolished; the *assistent-resident ter Westkust* was reduced to a military command. Van Heutsz records the withdrawal on page 32:

> "De Regeering, die dit natuurlijk zeer goed begreep, had dan ook bepaald, dat de controleurs in de onderafdeelingen van Groot-Atjeh werden afgeschaft, de functiën van assistent-resident ter Westkust (standplaats Melaboeh) en van controleur te Segli werden opgedragen aan de daar gevestigde militaire commandanten, en eindelijk de betrekkingen van assistent-resident ter Noord- en Oostkust en van controleur der onderafdeeling Samalanga werden ingetrokken tegelijk met de opheffing der vestigingen op laatstgenoemde plaats en te Telok Semawé".

The withdrawal was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the constitutional form of the *Concentratie*'s exclusion of the *pacificatie*: the *ambtenaren* were withdrawn because the *blokkade* — the *oorlogsmiddel* the *Concentratie* prescribed — left them "*geen taak*" (no task), and the *pacificatie* was thus abandoned as the political form of the colony's coast. The *cajoleering*-decade of [[1884]]–[[1892]] was, in this sense, the decade in which the *pacificatie* was suspended — the decade in which the *Concentratie*'s *politiek van non-interventie* replaced the *ambtenaren*'s *maatregelen des vredes* and the *scheepvaartregeling* was finally abolished in December [[1885]].

### Laging Tobias's *verstandig pacificeerend* prescription

The *pacificatie* is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the prescription Laging Tobias had reached in [[1883]] as the third of his three roads — the *derden weg* between the *concentratie* and the *vernieuwde agressie*. Laging Tobias's formula, quoted by Van Heutsz on page 95, is the *verstandig pacificeerende* operation:

> "In den laatsten tijd hoort men weer hier en daar stemmen opgaan om te Groot-Atjeh opnieuw op groote schaal met de wapens op te treden en den gunstigen toestand te herscheppen, die onder Generaal van der Heijden bestond, om daarna zooals de Heer Laging Tobias zeide — opnieuw verstandig pacificeerend te werk te gaan".

The *verstandig pacificeerend te werk gaan* — to proceed wisely pacifying — is, in Laging Tobias's prescription, the political sequel to a renewed military conquest: the *onderwerping* by *wapengeweld* was to be followed by the *pacificatie* by *verstandig* civil administration. Van Heutsz accepts the formula but inverts the order: the *pacificatie*, in his diagnosis, need not await a renewed *onderwerping*, because the *scheepvaartregeling* could produce the *pacificatie* without a renewed campaign. The *pacificatie* Van Heutsz prescribes in the 1893 tract is, in this sense, the Laging Tobias prescription stripped of its military precondition — the *pacificatie* by *scheepvaartregeling* that would, in Van Heutsz's argument, produce the *onderwerping* it was supposed to follow. This 1893 prescription anticipates the very system Van Heutsz would himself implement as Governor of Atjeh from [[1898]] onward — the *pacificatie* by *scheepvaartregeling*-cum-closure that became the political form under which the Atjeh-oorlog was finally closed.

### The Van Teijn warning: *pacificatie* as the political test

The *pacificatie* is, finally, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the political test the [[Van Teijn|Generaal van Teijn]] closure of [[1888]]–[[1890]] had passed and the post-Van-Teijn governorship had betrayed. Van Heutsz records on page 70 that the new Governor, who succeeded Van Teijn in [[1891]], had "*zich niet heeft bepaald tot het doen van voorstellen om zijne eigeneaardige denkbeelden omtrent scheepvaartregeling, sluiting, vuurwapens en klewang bij de Atjehers, middelen tot pacificatie enz. enz. ingevoerd te krijgen, maar reeds spoedig de kracht van de sluiting verlamde door licenties*". The *middelen tot pacificatie* — the means of pacification — were, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the means the new Governor had proposed but never introduced: the *scheepvaartregeling*, the *sluiting*, the regulation of *vuurwapens en klewang*. The *pacificatie* is, in this passage, the political test the new Governor had failed — the test whose failure had produced the *rassche schreden achteruit* of [[1891]]–[[1892]] and the eleventh Governor's appointment in [[1892]].

### Significance of the 1893 articulation

The *pacificatie*'s significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record, in the 1893 articulation, rests on four things: it was the political consolidation the *onderwerping* was fought to produce — the *maatregelen des vredes*, *geregeld bestuur*, *goede rechtsbedeeling*, *landbouw*, *handel* and *nijverheid* the *ambtenaren* were to bring to a country "dat wij aan ons gezag onderworpen hebben" — and the analytical counterpart to the military *onderwerping* that is the title and subject of the 1893 tract; it was the political form the *blokkade* could not produce, the *ambtenaren* could not sustain under the *blokkade*'s regime, and the *Concentratie* of [[1884]] had excluded by its very form — the withdrawal of the *controleurs* and the *assistent-residenten* that fixed the *pacificatie*'s suspension; it was the operational object the *scheepvaartregeling* — the *maatregel des vredes van duurzamen aard* — was uniquely able to sustain, and the test Scherer's softer *scheepvaartregeling* had failed to produce in its *volledige* form; and it was the prescription Laging Tobias had reached as the *derden weg* of [[1883]] and Van Heutsz had refashioned, in the [[1893]] tract, into the *pacificatie* by *scheepvaartregeling*-cum-closure that Van Heutsz would himself implement as Governor of Atjeh from [[1898]] onward. The *pacificatie* is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the political predicate the twenty-year policy oscillation had failed to produce — the *volledige pacificatie van Atjeh* whose absence the 1893 tract diagnoses and whose operational form the 1893 tract prescribes.

## Account from the Maréchaussée Jubileum-nummer ([[1930]]) — the pacifying work of the corps

the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* claims for the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* a share in the pacification exceeding its purely military value.

Alongside its police task the corps performed administrative work, "hieruit bestaande, dat het registers aanlegde, zooals het [[Binnenlandsch Bestuur|B. B.]] er op nahoudt om zijn vaderlijke zorgen over de bevolking te kunnen laten gaan; bezittingen en uitgeoefende bedrijven werden geregistreerd." Where territory could be brought under direct authority and the safety of person and goods was guaranteed, "ontwikkelde zich welvaart en daarmede had de pacificatie een aanvang genomen" — prosperity developed and with that the pacification had begun.

The distinctive contribution the editors claimed was that of continual patrolling: "Het Korps Maréchaussée heeft er door voortdurend patrouilleeren bij de bevolking van Atjeh den schrik in gebracht; weifelmoedigen werden, in ons voordeel, tot nadenken gestemd… het hield de djahats terug van voorgenomen wandaden" — by continual patrolling the corps brought terror among the population; the wavering were brought to reflection; it held the *djahats* back from intended misdeeds. Precisely because so little was known of that part of the work — "de massa spitst zich op wapenfeiten" — its fulfilment had given the corps "de innerlijke beteekenis… voor de pacificatie uitstekende boven zijn militaire waarde."

Colonel [[C. A. Snell|L. A. Snell]] pointed in the same issue to the corps's work of peace: the numerous roads traversing Atjeh, the many engineering works it had built, and the order created "in de vroeger door onderlinge veeten van elkander gescheiden staatjes." But he added that the Atjehnese people remained "onderworpen, doch niet verzoend" — subjected but not reconciled — and that the Netherlands had done it "leed, zeer veel leed" which it was the duty of the younger generation to make good.

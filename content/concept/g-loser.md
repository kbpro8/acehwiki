**The G. Loser** — modern *Mount Loser* — is the volcano (numbered 70 in the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Bureau's volcano list]]) of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, at 3,520 m the highest volcano of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte* and the source of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas (Woi ni Agoesein)]]. In the Bureau's Sumatra survey (1917), the G. Loser is the principal volcano of the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Wilhelmina gebergte]]'s northern edge, and the orographic source of the Alas river.

## Orography

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I describes the G. Loser as the northernmost major peak of the [[Wilhelmina gebergte|Wilhelmina gebergte]], standing at the *Noordrand* of the *doorbraak* of the *Woi ni Tripö* through the Westelijke Barisan coulisse. The Bureau notes that "de Lao Alas (Woi ni Agoesein) … op de Zuidelijke hellingen van den G. Loser (70) ontspringt, in Zuid-Oostelijke richting het Westelijke lengtedal doorstroomt." The G. Loser is thus the orographic source of the Alas river — the river whose valley forms the southern approach to the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*.

The G. Loser stands at the *Noordelijke* edge of the *Wilhelmina gebergte*, opposite the *Kembar* (65) — the northernmost peak of the Wilhelmina gebergte proper — and at the southern limit of the *Gajoesche Centraalgebergte*. The G. Loser is thus the orographic bridge between the Wilhelmina gebergte and the Gajoesche Centraalgebergte.

## Significance

The G. Loser is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the highest volcano of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte* and the orographic source of the [[Lao Alas|Lao Alas]]. The Bureau's emphasis on the G. Loser's role as the source of the Alas river — and on its situation at the orographic bridge between the Wilhelmina gebergte and the Gajoesche Centraalgebergte — frames the G. Loser as the geographical foundation of the Alas valley's southern hydrography, and as the strategic anchor of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*'s orographic defence.

The G. Loser's height — 3,520 m — makes it the highest volcano of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*, and one of the highest volcanoes of Sumatra. The Bureau's measurement is the principal evidence of the G. Loser's orographic dominance of the central-Atjeh *hoogvlakte*.

The **pageue** (Atjehnese; Malay *pagar*) was the fence or enclosure by which a cultivator in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] completed the marking of his occupation of cleared land, and whose remains served afterwards as evidence of earlier cultivation.

## In the law of occupation

Occupation of waste land was indicated first by corner stakes joined by a *[[Rintisan|rintisan]]*, and in a further stage by complete *afpaling* — total enclosure — the *pageue*. Erection of the fence was thus the visible completion of appropriation.

## In the law of proof

In disputes over land said to have been previously cultivated, the remains of an earlier *pageue* counted among the visible traces on which a claimant had to be able to point. Where the ground in question was fit only for laying out a garden or *[[Ladang|ladang]]*, a plaintiff asserting prior use had to point, together with his witnesses, either to trees or shrubs surviving from an earlier crop or to the remnants of a former *pageue*. Where no such remains could be discerned outside the *[[Gampong|gampong]]*, and only a single isolated tree stood on the disputed ground, recourse was formerly had to the *[[Haq thilangkob|haq thilangkob]]*, the right of the felling-length.

## In garden contracts

The *pageue* was also a cost item in *[[Mawaih|mawaih]]* contracts over gardens: the party providing the land customarily furnished the *[[Bibit|bibit]]*, the material and the labour costs for laying out the *pageue*, together with a *[[Pangkaj|pangkaj]]* in advance, while the other party contributed only his bodily strength.

The **Maréchaussée-vocabulaire** was the set of Acehnese titles which the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* adopted from its Acehnese opponents and applied to its own ranks — a usage recorded by [[W. J. Mosselman]] in the [[1930]] jubilee number as evidence of how completely the corps had taken the enemy as its model.

## The equivalences

Mosselman wrote that "ons model is de dappere en in dien krijg volleerde vijand geweest en in ons onderling vocabulaire hadden wij zelfs zijn eigen titels overgenomen" — our model has been the brave enemy, fully accomplished in that warfare, and in our mutual vocabulary we had even taken over his own titles. The equivalences were:

- **[[Oeleebalang|Oeleuhbalang]]** — the officers
- **[[Maréchaussée-vocabulaire|Toekoe]]** — the European non-commissioned officers
- **[[Panglima|Panglima]]** — the indigenous sergeants
- **[[Keuchi|Keutjih]]** — the corporals
- **[[Kawan (Maréchaussée)|Kawan]]** — the *maréchaussées* themselves
- **[[Toeangkoe (Maréchaussée)|Toeangkoe]]** — the *divisiecommandant*

## The chain of command expressed in these terms

One *Toeangkoe* or division commander had under him three *Oeleuhbalangs*; each *Oeleuhbalang* four *Toekoes*; and each *Toekoe* disposed of one *Panglima*, one *Keutjih* and seventeen *Kawans*.

## Other terms of the corps

Mosselman's memoir and the editorial matter of the jubilee number preserve further corps usages: *[[Bloedvingers|bloedvingers]]* for the collar trimming of the uniform; *djahat* (evil-doer) for an armed opponent; *[[Patrouille (Maréchaussée)|patrouille]]* for the operating party; *colonnezak* for the men's pooled spending money; and *[[Marechausseelied|Manisé]]* for the corps song. Mosselman also spoke of the corps having its own *[[Adat|adat]]* — a customary law and manner of housekeeping which outsiders, notably officers and cadre of the infantry corps, did not understand, and interference with which "als regel tot min of meer ernstige botsingen aanleiding gaf" — as a rule gave rise to more or less serious collisions.

## Significance

The borrowing is a compact illustration of the corps's method. Raised to fight a guerrilla war "met zijn eigen tactiek" — with the enemy's own tactics — and required at its foundation to make itself acquainted with "taal en adat" (language and customary law) of the country in order to win confidence and obtain reliable intelligence, the Maréchaussée assimilated the terminology of Acehnese territorial authority into its own internal order.

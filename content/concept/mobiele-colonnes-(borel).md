The **[[Mobiele colonnes (Borel)|mobiele colonnes]]** were the mobile striking columns that [[G. F. W. Borel|Borel]] proposed as the alternative to the *posten-systeem* in Atjeh — strong, always-ready columns of 600–800 men infanterie, with a battery of 4–6 stukken artillerie, a sectie mineurs and some cavalleristen, operating two or three in *verbond* (concert) toward the same *doel* (objective) in *wigvorm* (wedge formation). The proposal is the tactical complement to Borel's strategic doctrine of the *aanvallende tactiek*: where the *posten-systeem* dispersed the force among many small garrisons, the *mobiele colonnes* concentrated it for repeated *tuchtigingen* of the enemy's heartland, especially the [[XXII Moekims]] — "de kern van Atjeh's weerstandsvermogen."

## Composition and Doctrine

Each *mobiele colonne* was to comprise 600–800 men of infanterie, a battery of 4–6 stukken artillerie (preferably steel [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]] with schokbuis), a sectie of mineurs (engineers) and some cavalleristen for reconnaissance and pursuit. The columns were to operate two or three in *verbond* — in concert — toward the same *doel*, advancing in *wigvorm* (wedge formation) so as to converge on the objective from multiple directions. The doctrine was, in effect, the application to Atjeh of the European principle of *marcher au canon* — marching to the sound of the guns — that Borel cites from the [[Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken (1873)|Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken]] booklet distributed to officers before the second expedition. The columns were to be always ready to march, supported by *kappen en branden* (cutting and burning) — the two principal means Borel identifies for overcoming Atjeh's resistance — and by the *omtrekking* (flanking and encircling) tactic that Borel calls the "overoude Indische tactiek." The columns were not to replace the posts of the *posten-systeem* but to supplement them: the posts would hold the line; the columns would strike the enemy.

## The Critique of the Posten-systeem

The *mobiele colonnes* proposal is, in Borel's account, the direct consequence of his critique of the *posten-systeem*. The 36 posts of the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] dispersed the bezettingskorps among many small garrisons, leaving no strong reserve for offensive operations. The posts were repeatedly *overvallen*: the [[Night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit (22 November 1875)|night attack on Longbatta-Mesigit]] of [[22 November]] [[1875]], the night attack on Lemboe-N.O. of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1876]], and the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]] of [[13 February]] [[1876]] all exposed the vulnerability of small garrisons in Atjeh. The [[Enemy breakthrough in the IV, VI and IX Moekims (June 1878)|enemy breakthrough of June 1878]] — when [[Karel van der Heijden|van der Heijden]]'s absence with most of the mobiele strijdmacht at [[Gedoeng]] allowed the enemy to break the line in the [[IV Moekims|IV]], [[VI Moekims|VI]] and [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]] — vindicated the critique. The lesson Borel draws is categorical: posts alone cannot prevent a linie-doorbraak; mobile forces must never be reduced.

## The Indische Brigade and the Algiers Model

The *mobiele colonnes* required, in Borel's argument, a metropolitan reinforcement on the scale of the [[1830]] French expedition against Algiers (quoted from Camille Rousset's *L'Histoire de la guerre de Crimée*). The [[Indische Brigade (proposed)|Indische Brigade]] of 3 000–4 000 men, raised in the Netherlands from Dutch vrijwilligers, armed with steel achterlaadkanonnen and sent directly to Atjeh, would supply the geoefende manschappen, the kaders and the modern materieel that the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]] itself could no longer provide. Two or three *mobiele colonnes*, operating in *verbond*, would then conduct repeated *tuchtigingen* of the [[XXII Moekims]] — the densest-populated Sagi, containing the Sultan's voorraadschuren and the most hostile chiefs. The example of [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]]'s two expeditions against [[Gedoeng]] in June [[1878]] — after the second of which the chief laid down bestuur and his son came to submit — was, Borel argued, proof of the doctrine. The [[Submission of Pasangan (15 August 1878)|submission of Pasangan]] after naval bombardment on [[15 August]] [[1878]] confirmed the pattern. The *mobiele colonnes* proposal, though never adopted in Borel's exact form, anticipated the later *maréchaussée* system associated with [[Johannes van Heutsz|van Heutsz]] that finally brought the Atjeh-oorlog to a close in the early twentieth century.

***Katjau*** — the Malay-Indies term for stirring, muddling, gossiping and intriguing, with its derivatives *katjauen*, *gekatjaud* and *katjauers* — was the standing vice of the Dutch colonial community of [[Atjeh]], and one of the two categories, with the *vergissers* (those who erred), into which [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]] grouped [[H. N. A. Swart]]'s detractors in the chapter he entitled *[[Van Militair naar Civiel Bestuur|Van Militair naar Civiel Bestuur en van katjauers en vergissers]]*.

## The atmosphere

Throughout his ten years in Atjeh Swart "he felt and knew opposition and obstruction against him. And that is doubly felt in a country like Atjeh — in a hotbed of gossip and intrigue, where even good workers begrudged one another the light in their eyes."

Its principal vehicle was the *[[chabar angin]]* — "literally, news of the wind" — the rumour by which, for instance, [[H. J. Schmidt]] learned in [[1908]] that he was to be made Civil Authority at [[Padang Tidji]].

## The charges against Swart

"People — and those "people" are to be sought chiefly among the eager servants of the *chabar angin* — have reproached Swart (or rather "said of Swart") that he let himself be influenced by gossip-mongers, that he was susceptible to flattery and fine talk, and that he pursued a policy of outward show."

Du Croo answered that anyone who spoke with the seventy-nine-year-old, as he had, "would find it hard to suppress a smile at such people on hearing Swart's uncommonly accurate and telling characterisation of his collaborators, whom he saw through man by man." Swart was warm-hearted and to many a fatherly friend; it pained him to be hard towards valued collaborators, "but the clarity of his notes on the journals left little to be desired."

## The katjau over the pacification

The charge that the resistance had been bought to rest and the whole pacification purchased "katjaude men" heavily in [[1923]]–[[1925]], in Atjeh as elsewhere: "There was heavy blundering then." Du Croo's own investigation of the budget entries at [[Meulaboh]] in [[1925]] convinced him that each had its own justification.

## The military-civil controversy

The most serious opposition, however, du Croo explicitly distinguished from mere *katjau*: the officers who held that Atjeh could not be governed by *[[Binnenlandsch Bestuur|B.B.]]* officials "so thought a good many in Atjeh — not young fools, not gossip-mongers, but serious, hard workers in the Acehnese wilderness."

## Dijkstra's testimony

In the foreword to du Croo's book, [[G. K. Dijkstra]] recorded of the Swart years that "the spirit, influenced from above, was one of dedication and good mutual understanding. Of the "katjau" so well known in Atjeh little was experienced."

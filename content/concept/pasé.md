**Pasé** (also known historically as Samudera Pasai) was one of the earliest Muslim principalities (Dutch: *Moslimsch rijkje*) on the north-east coast of [[Sumatra]], established by approximately [[1500]]. It is cited in the *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* ([[1926]]) as evidence for the early penetration of Islam into the Indonesian Archipelago, and is notable as the probable origin of [[Soenan Goenoeng Djati]], a major figure in the Islamisation of Java.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — Pasé

The atlas mentions Pasé in two distinct contexts:

### As an Early Muslim Sultanate

In its chapter on religion: "op de N.O. kust van Sumatra (de Moslimsche rijkjes Perlak en Pasé in omstreeks 1500)" (on the N.E. coast of Sumatra [the Muslim principalities Perlak and Pasé around 1500]).

### As the Origin of Soenan Goenoeng Djati

In its description of the graves at Gresik: "Soenan Goenoeng Djati was een Mohammedaan, vermoedelijk uit Pasei (Noord-Sumatra) afkomstig, die met zijn godsdienst-onderwijs op Java veel opgang maakte. Hij stierf 1570 te Cheribon." (Soenan Goenoeng Djati was a Mohammedan, presumably from Pasai [North Sumatra] by origin, who with his religious teaching achieved much success on Java. He died in 1570 at Cheribon.)

## Significance

Pasé's dual significance in the atlas — as both an early Islamic state and the birthplace of one of the nine wali (saints) credited with the Islamisation of Java — underscores the central role that North Sumatran Islam played in the religious transformation of the entire Archipelago. The connection between Pasé and the Javanese mission of Soenan Goenoeng Djati illustrates the transmission of Islam from its Sumatran footholds to the more populous and culturally developed island of Java.

**Kreemer's seven-period division of the Atjèh-oorlog** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history of [[Acheh|Atjèh]], the periodisation of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]] ([[1873]]–[[1910]]) into seven *tijdvakken* (periods) — is the analytical framework by which Kreemer organises his account of the thirty-seven-year conflict. The division is announced at the head of his third chapter: "Ten einde het overzicht te vergemakkelijken zullen we het verloop van den Atjèh-oorlog in zeven tijdperken verdeelen" — "In order to facilitate the overview, we shall divide the course of the Atjèh-oorlog into seven periods."

## The seven periods

Kreemer's seven periods, with their dates and principal commanders or governors, are:

1. **Eerste tijdvak**: The [[First Aceh Expedition (5-29 April 1873) — Kreemer's account|first expedition]] under [[General J. H. R. Kohler|generaal J. H. R. Kohler]] ([[5 April|5]]–[[29 April]] [[1873]]). The failed assault on the [[Kraton Atjeh|Kraton]], the death of Kohler on [[14 April]], and the withdrawal to [[Java]].

2. **Tweede tijdvak**: The [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874) — Kreemer's account|second expedition]] under [[Generaal van Swieten|generaal J. van Swieten]] to the fall and occupation of the Kraton ([[9 December]] [[1873]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]]). The [[Kraton Atjeh|fall of the Kraton]], the death of the young Sultan, the flight of the court to [[Keumala]], the [[naming of Koeta Radja (19 March 1874)|naming of Koeta Radja]], and the [[Mas Soemo Widikdjo|martyrdom of Mas Soemo Widikdjo]].

3. **Derde tijdvak**: The period of consolidation of the Dutch establishment in Atjèh ([[April 1874]]–[[July 1878]]). The *passive defence* policy of Van Swieten and [[Kolonel Pel|kolonel Pel]], the *zeventigdaagsche veldtocht* (seventy-day campaign) of December [[1875]], the conquest of the [[XXV Mukims|XXV Moekim's]], the death of Pel on [[24 February]] [[1876]], and the governor-general [[J. W. van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]]'s visit of March [[1877]].

4. **Vierde tijdvak**: The period of forceful action and the conquest of the whole of [[Great Acheh|Groot-Atjèh]] ([[June 1878]]–[[September 1879]]). The campaigns of [[General van der Heyden|kolonel (later generaal) K. van der Heijden]], the [[Submission of Habib Abdurrahman (13 October 1878)|submission of Habib Abdurrahman]] on [[13 October]] [[1878]], the [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Pölém]]'s continued hostility, and the declaration of [[5 September]] [[1879]] that all of Groot-Atjèh was subdued.

5. **Vijfde tijdvak**: The period of civil government ([[6 April]] [[1881]]–[[13 September]] [[1884]]). The governorships of [[Pruys van der Hoeven|A. Pruys van der Hoeven]] ([[6 April]] [[1881]]–[[16 March]] [[1883]]) and [[Laging Tobias|P. F. Laging Tobias]] ([[16 March]] [[1883]]–[[13 September]] [[1884]]), the premature introduction of civil administration, and the [[Nisero affair (1883-1885)|Nisero affair]].

6. **Zesde tijdvak**: The period of steady decline ([[1884]]–[[1896]]), subdivided into the [[Concentration system (1884-1896) — Kreemer's account|Concentration]] ([[1884]]–[[1896]]), the [[Scheepvaartregeling (1892)|Scheepvaartregeling]] ([[1892]]), and the [[Oema-periode (1893-1896)|Oema-periode]] ([[1893]]–[[1896]]). Governors: [[Demmeni|H. Demmeni]] (September [[1884]]–December [[1886]]), [[Van Teijn|H. K. F. van Teijn]] (November [[1886]]–April [[1891]]), [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|F. Pompe van Meerdervoort]] (May [[1891]]–January [[1892]]), [[Deijkerhoff|C. Deijkerhoff]] (January [[1892]]–June [[1896]]).

7. **Zevende tijdvak**: The period of forceful action and the ending of the Atjèh-oorlog ([[1896]]–[[1910]]). The governorship of [[Governor Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], the adoption of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s advice, the campaigns of [[Van Daalen|van Daalen]], and the close of the war under governor [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]].

## Significance

Kreemer's seven-period division is, in the historiography of the Atjèh war, the principal analytical framework of the early twentieth century — the framework within which the [[E. B. Kielstra|Kielstra]] and [[E. S. de Klerck|De Klerck]] narratives were organised and which would inform the subsequent colonial scholarship. The division turns on the alternation of "afwachtende" (passive) and "krachtige" (forceful) periods — a rhythm that Kreemer presents as the structural pattern of the war, and whose break — the abandonment of the passive posture in [[1896]] — produces the final, successful phase.

**Eeang male** was the Atjehnese term recorded by [[D. W. N. de Boer]] for the proceeds of the goods left by a deceased person, out of which the *[[Ghafan|ghafan]]* — the funeral costs appropriate to his rank and station — were defrayed before the residue of an unadministered estate was transferred by the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]* to the *[[Baitul mal|bait ul-mâl]]*.

## Context

The term appears in De Boer's account of the treatment of estates left by persons dying without heirs and by deceased strangers, a matter on which he insisted that no *adat*-lawful rule entitled the *oeléëbalang* to annex the land for himself, whatever the practice of many *zelfbestuurders* (self-governing rulers) might be.

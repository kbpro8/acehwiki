**Panton Laboe** was a Dutch *bivak* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] on the *oostkust* of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], in the *afdeling* [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust van Atjeh]] south of [[Lho Seumawé|Lho Seumawé]] and west of [[Idi|Idi]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Panton Laboe is the *bivak* whose garrison — three brigades of the [[5de (Vde) Divisie Marechaussee|Vde Divisie Maréchaussee]] under Lt [[J. F. Westerweel|Westerweel]] — was the first Dutch force in Atjeh to sight the approaching Japanese invasion fleet on the night of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March 1942]].

## Pre-war garrison

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], Panton Laboe held three brigades of the Vde Divisie Marechaussee. In the [[1940]]–[[1941]] reorganisations the Panton Laboe brigades were assigned a *defensietaak* — "het beveiligen van de vernielingswerken der olieterreinen in de omgeving van Koeala Simpang" — together with the brigades of the Vde Divisie at [[Lho Soekoen|Lho Soekoen]] and [[Paja Bakong|Paja Bakong]] and the brigades of the [[1ste Garnizoensbataljon|1ste Garnizoensbataljon]] at [[Lho Seumawé|Lho Seumawé]] and [[Lokop|Lokop]].

## The 11/12 March 1942 sighting

As *detachementscommandant* at Panton Laboe, Lt [[J. F. Westerweel|Westerweel]] had under his command, on the night of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March 1942]], three brigades of the Seulimeum-colonne. He received a report from the *kustwacht* that hundreds of lights had been observed on the sea off the coast. He immediately dispatched one brigade by auto in the direction of [[Idi|Idi]], where the lights had been sighted, with orders to establish what was happening. At daybreak the brigade-commandant reported by telephone that nine large ships were lying in line off the coast and that troops were being put ashore. After a brief patrol the brigade was nearly cut off by a Japanese patrol and forced to return. Westerweel alarmed the other posts and informed the Territoriaal Commandant at [[Takengon|Takengon]]; he was ordered to move his brigades to Takengon as quickly as possible, which he did, arriving in the late evening.

## Significance

Panton Laboe is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the *bivak* whose *kustwacht* report of the night of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March]] was the first Dutch observation of the approaching Japanese invasion fleet. The Panton Laboe sighting — transmitted by Westerweel to the Territoriaal Commandant at Takengon — was the operational trigger of the Dutch central-Atjeh reorganisation of [[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March]], and the first link in the chain of engagements that would close with the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|capitulation of 28 March 1942]] at [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]].

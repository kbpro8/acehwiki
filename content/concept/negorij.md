**Negorij** — from the Malay *negeri*, "settlement" or "village territory" — was the term for the home village or home community of an Ambonese (or other) soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]] on [[Ambon]]. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *negorij* is the place to which the Ambonese soldier belonged even when he had not lived there for generations, and the place that took pride in his decorations: "*Ridders [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]], dragers van de [[Medaille voor Moed en Trouw]] en van het [[Kroontje (Eervolle Vermelding)|Kroontje]] (de Eervolle Vermelding), waren de trots van hun *[[kampong]]* of *negorij*."

## The negorij and the soldier

"ledereen had er een aantal militairen in zijn familie en in iedere *negorij* woonden vele gepensioneerden, zodat er aan verhalen over de dienst geen gebrek was." Every Ambonese family had a number of soldiers in it, and every *negorij* had many retired soldiers, so there was no lack of stories about the service. The *negorij* was therefore both the soldier's home and the place where the colonial army's culture was transmitted across generations.

## The negorij and the decorations

"*Ridders [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]], dragers van de [[Medaille voor Moed en Trouw]] en van het [[Kroontje (Eervolle Vermelding)|Kroontje]] (de Eervolle Vermelding), waren de trots van hun *[[kampong]]* of *negorij*." The M.W.O. Ridder was the pride of his *negorij*: the *negorij* tracked his wedervaren through the gehele Archipel, and his decoration was worn at the *[[Koninginnedag]]* rondgang.

## The negorij and the verlofganger

"Daarbij waren vooral de verlofgangers de verbinding, briefschrijvers zijn het niet erg, maar vertellers wel." The *verlofganger* — the soldier on leave — was the connection between the *tangsi* and the *negorij*: not much of a letter-writer, but a great teller of stories. The *verlofganger* brought the *tangsi* to the *negorij*, and the *negorij* to the *tangsi*.

## The negorij and the multi-generational soldier

"Zelfs indien, zoals vaak voorkwam, de Ambonees zijn *negorij* nooit had gezien, omdat zijn familie reeds enige geslachten lang niet thuis was geweest, dan wist hij waar dat thuis was en wat er daar gebeurde, terwijl men in de *kampong* wist, waar hij zat en hoe het hem ging." Even if — as often happened — the Ambonese soldier had never seen his *negorij*, because his family had not been home for several generations, he knew where that home was and what was happening there, while the *kampong* knew where he was and how he was. "Komt men in Nederland na twintig jaar vrijwel als een vreemde in zijn geboortestad, dáár kon een achterkleinzoon na een eeuw *thuis*komen." Where in the Netherlands one returns after twenty years almost as a stranger to one's birthplace, there a great-grandchild could come *home* after a century.

## Significance

The *negorij* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the home community of the Ambonese soldier. The book's argument is that the *negorij* — the home village on [[Ambon]] — was the place to which the Ambonese soldier belonged even when he had not lived there for generations, and the place that took pride in his decorations. The *negorij* was therefore both the soldier's home and the place where the colonial army's culture was transmitted across generations. The multi-generational tie — the great-grandchild who could come *home* after a century — is the book's marker of the depth of the Ambonese soldier's tie to his *negorij*.

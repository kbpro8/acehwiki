**Minjak tanah** — Malay "earth-oil", i.e. natural petroleum — is the white, milk-like medicinal petroleum of [[Aroe]] whose springs at [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] gave the bay of [[Aroe-baai|Aroe]] its older name *Moeware perhentian* or *Moeware Sija-na-ma-tara* in the language of [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]], and whose fame, in the chronicle tradition, the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] did not extinguish. It is the trade-good whose name, in *[[bedekte taal]]* form, encodes both the [[Queen of Aroe|Queen of Aroe]]'s flight (*minha cumbaa* = *minjak sembawa*) and the [[Aroe|bay]]'s sacred geography.

## The springs at Pangkalan Soesoe

[[Pangkalan Soesoe]] — "the name 'milk' recalls the milky-white colour of the earth-oil here" — is the place at the head of [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] where, in [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]]'s day, ten dwellings of Malays who had fled from [[Besitang]] stood on the high bank. The name in the language of [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]] (~[[1430]]) — *Moeware perhentian* or *Moeware Sija-na-ma-tara* — De Roo de la Faille decodes as *m-oe-aram-oe-ara sija-mata-ra*, "the *perhentian* [stopping-place] of the goddess Tara", the patroness of [[Sumatra]]. The white earth-oil of the springs was, in pre-Atjehnese times, the principal medicinal export of the east-coast Hindu-Buddhist commonwealth.

## The Queen of Aroe's flight

In [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]'s account of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]], the [[Queen of Aroe|queen]] — having made her unsuccessful counter-stroke against the Atjehnese plunderers — fled to "a place called *minha cumbaa*", from which she sailed to [[Malacca]]. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes *minha cumbaa* as ***minjak sembawa***, i.e. the *minjak tanah* of Aroe — the white petroleum of [[Pangkalan Soesoe]]: the queen's flight, in this reading, was to the very springs whose fame had made the bay's name in the older geography.

## The Atjehsche kroniekje's joke

A **Maleisch kroniekje van Atjeh** — a small Malay chronicle of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — jests, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] records, of the *minjak tanah* of Aroe together with the *minjak kapoer* (camphor-oil) of [[Baros]], that both were used by the *radja of Roem* as a medicine. *Roem* De Roo de la Faille decodes as *Aroe-T-aroem-a*, the "perfumed Aroe". The same jest, recorded in the chronicle, marks the survival of the medicinal reputation of the Aroe petroleum into the Atjehnese age — even though, by the seventeenth century, "that reputation had already much weakened".

## The Compagnie's experiment

In the colonial period the [[Dutch East India Company|Compagnie]] experimented with exporting the *minjak tanah* of Aroe, in pots, "but this did not catch on". In [[Surabaya]] the oil of *Djaba-Kota* was still used as lamp-oil, and in [[Rembang]] the *minjak tanah* was formerly used as a medicine — the place-name *Tinawoen*, De Roo de la Faille notes, being a contraction of *As-tina-noew-oen*, "thanksgiving for that gift", which recalled the *minjak tanah* of the Overwal's *Astina*, the ancestral land also of Rembang.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, *minjak tanah* is the principal medicinal trade-good of the [[Aroe|east-coast Hindu-Buddhist commonwealth]] whose absorption by the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] is recorded in the [[Queen of Aroe|Queen of Aroe]]'s flight and in the jesting Malay chronicle of Atjeh. Its survival as a name in [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] and in the language of [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]] fixes the older sacred geography of the bay against which the Atjehnese age is measured.

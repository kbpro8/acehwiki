**Peupia oemòng** — "the breaking up of one's rice field" — was the reproachful Atjehnese name for the practice of selling an inherited rice field in whole or in part, an act condemned in public opinion.

## The saying

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje]], quoted by [[D. W. N. de Boer]] from *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]*, Vol. I, p. 312, recorded that he who sold his inherited rice field wholly or in part passed for a spendthrift, and that *peupia oemòng* was the name given to "die afkeurenswaardige wijze van doen" — that reprehensible manner of proceeding. "Men richt daardoor immers den *[[Pangoelee hareukat|pangoeléé hareukat]]* te gronde" — for thereby one ruins the "master of all breadwinning."

## Consequence

The social sanction had a direct legal effect: sale (*[[Poeblee|poebléë]]*) of rice fields as good as never occurred, and men preferred to raise money on their fields by *[[Gala|gala]]* (pledge) or Atjehnese *[[Borok|hypotheek]]* (mortgage), often taking the land straight back in *[[Mawaih|deelbouw]]* in order not to break the bond with it entirely.

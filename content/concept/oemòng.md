**Oemòng** is the Acehnese term for a rice field — the basic unit of the [[rijstbouw in Atjeh|Atjeh rice economy]]. The Gayo equivalent is *oemo* and the Alas equivalent is *djoemo*. The *oemòng* is the Acehnese-language counterpart of the Malay [[sawah|sawah]], but it carries a richer set of sub-types and qualifiers, recording the Atjehnese classification of rice fields by their water-source, their position in the landscape, and their cultivation status. The full vocabulary of the *oemòng* is documented by [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]] in his [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]].

## The Water-Source Classification

The principal classification of the *oemòng* is by its water-source. Kreemer distinguishes:

- ***Oemòng ie peunieuëh*** — the artificially irrigated field (Gayo *oemo mòc-woih*, Alas *djoemo mèlawe*). This is the highest-quality type, irrigated by water led from rivers and streams through dug channels or bamboo flumes.
- ***Oemòng preh ie oedjèn*** — the rain-dependent field. This is the most common type in regions without irrigation, especially in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]].
- ***Oemòng paja*** — the swamp rice field (Gayo *oemo paja* or *oemo rölöm*, Alas *djoema rawang*). A field in swampy low-lying land, where the water is too deep for ordinary sawah cultivation but not deep enough to be unsuitable for rice.

## The Topographic Classification

The *oemòng* is also classified by its position in the landscape:

- ***Oemòng meusandeng*** — fields situated higher than the neighbouring fields. Where there is no artificial irrigation, the rain-water flows through the soil directly to the lower-lying terrain. Such fields (*oemòng tiriëh*, Gayo *oemo tiris*, Alas *djoemo tjènggaling* or *djoemo lingèh*) are therefore of little value.
- ***Oemòng lhò*** — fields situated in a valley (Gayo *oemo aroel*, Alas *djoemo aloer*). In the rainy season these fields often receive too strong an inflow of water, and are therefore also not sought-after.
- ***Oemòng teurata*** — terraced sawahs (Gayo *oemo moclinggè-linggè*). These are found on the river terraces of the interior, where the rivers of the [[Bukit Barisan|Bukit Barisan]] mountains have cut broad valleys with stepped terraces.
- ***Oemòng binòh*** — the irrigated fields immediately adjacent to the main irrigation channel. These are the most sought-after, for obvious reasons.

## The Size Classification

In [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] the size of the *oemòng* is classified as follows:

- A large *oemòng* — that is, one for which one *naléh* or more of seed-rice is required — is called a ***jö***.
- A smaller field is called a ***keubeuëng***, or in some regions a ***tipèh***.

On the Noord- and Oostkust these terms refer to particular surface-areas. On the Oostkust the area of a sawah is expressed in *mas* — one *mas* being a square of which each side is 17 *deupa* long. This area is called a *mas* because formerly one *mas* (= 24 *pèng pòr* or *Straits duiten*) was paid for the planting of it.

## The Cultivation-Status Classification

The *oemòng* is also classified by its cultivation status:

- ***Pòk ròh*** — an abandoned field, formerly cultivated but long uncultivated (Gayo *oemà rak*, Alas *djoema oesang*). Such abandoned sawahs are found, for example, in large numbers in the lowlands of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]].
- ***Padé doee*** — "sitting rice": rice sown directly on the field by the *taboe* method (the broadcasting method), which is not transplanted.
- ***Padé seuneulòng*** or ***padé peunaela*** — "seedbed rice" or "planted rice": rice transplanted from a seedbed by the *poela* method.

## The Dikes and Compartments

The *oemòng* is bunded by small earth dikes — ***ateuëng*** in Acehnese (Gayo *patal*, Alas *batas*) — that retain the water on the field. The irrigated fields are divided by these dikes into compartments — ***tipèh*** in Acehnese (Gayo *fěmpeh*, Alas *batas*). A complex of rice fields is called a ***blang*** in Atjeh, ***siladang*** in Alas, and *oemo* (the same word as for an individual field) in Gayo.

## Significance

The vocabulary of the *oemòng* is the densest part of the Atjehnese agricultural lexicon, recording the Atjehnese classification of rice fields by their water-source, their position in the landscape, their size, and their cultivation status. The vocabulary is preserved in detail in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s 1918 monograph, alongside the Gayo and Alas equivalents, and is an indispensable source for the historical study of the Atjeh rice-economy and of the Acehnese language.

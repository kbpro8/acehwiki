**The banket** — also *banquette* — was the raised firing step and walkway running inside the palisade of a Dutch *[[Benting|benting]]* in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], from which the garrison could see over the defences.

[[P. H. R. Beuming]] describes it in his account of the permanent posts of the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]*: *Vlak achter de omheining bevond zich binnen in den post het zoogenaamde banket, dat zooveel als een zeer breede loopplank was, waarvan men over de palissadeering heen kon zien* — immediately behind the enclosure, inside the post, was the so-called *banket*, which was as it were a very broad walkway from which one could see over the palisading. On the *banket* stood the sentry-boxes for the guards.

The *banket* recurs in two of Beuming's narratives. At [[Biloel]] the commander watched the outcome of the dawn ambush against [[The sharpshooter of Biloel|the sharpshooter]] from the *banket* with a field glass, so that news of the success preceded the returning patrol. At [[Edi]] in [[1896]] the garrison watched from the *banquette* the approach of the escort bringing [[Pastoor Verbraak]] from the landing place, the Europeans distinguishable from the natives by their white gaiters.

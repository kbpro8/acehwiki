**The Iron ores in Atjeh** is the corpus of colonial-era geological observations that document the occurrence of iron-ore deposits in the Residency of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], recorded in the canonical [[1922]] survey of [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]], [[B. B. Lindberg|Lindberg]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]] and synthesised by [[Van Bemmelen's The Geology of Indonesia (1949)|Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia*]] ([[1949]]). Eight Atjeh localities are recorded, of which the [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] occurrence (32 km South of [[Kota Radja]]) carries the largest documented colonial-era reserve estimate: 2,600,000 tons of ore with an average of 58–64 % Fe, estimated in [[1920]] and "suspended on account of low market-prices".

## Geological model: contact-metamorphic origin

The geological model for the Atjeh iron-ore occurrences is the contact-metamorphic model established by [[P. Hövig|Hövig]] ([[1918]]) and [[A. Tobler|Tobler]] ([[1919]]) for the northern-Sumatran iron-ore deposits in general. In this model, the magnetite-hematite ores are formed at the contact zone of an eruptive intrusion (typically a granite, in Tobler's interpretation, rather than a diabase) and the surrounding limestone country rock, with the diagnostic formation of garnet and silicified limestone at the contact. The same model applies to the [[Muara Sipongi]] deposits just south of the Atjeh border in Tapanuli, where Tobler "explicitly indicated the contact-metamorphic origin of the ore" and argued that "contact-metamorphism was effected by the granite and not by the diabase".

The Atjeh iron-ore localities — [[Kr. Geuenteuet]] (with "much pyrite and large boulders of magnetite containing a core of pyrite"), [[Gle Mon Ampeuet]] (with limestone, amphibole andesite, garnet and silicified limestone), [[Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)|Kr. Ligan]] (with iron ore, pyrite and pyrolusite), [[Kr. Rigaih]] and [[Alu Talu]] (with scattered ore-fragments at eruptive-rock contacts, with garnet) — all conform to this contact-metamorphic model.

## The eight Atjeh localities

The eight Atjeh iron-ore localities recorded in Van Bemmelen's Fig. 41 key are:

1. **[[Kr. Geuenteuet]]** (No. 1): 32 km South of [[Kota Radja]]. "In the Al. Besu or A. Pantom Raba, a tributary of the Kr. Geuenteuet, much pyrite and large boulders of magnetite containing a core of pyrite were found. An analysis showed 10 gr Au and 33 gr Ag per ton." [[1920]] reserve estimate: 2,600,000 tons of 58–64 % Fe ore.
2. **[[Gle Mon Ampeuet]]** (No. 2): 37 km South of Kota Radja. "Numerous fragments of iron ore, with a core of pyrite, on the slopes. In the vicinity limestone and an eruptive rock (amphibole andesite) were observed. The presence of garnet and a silicious limestone indicates contact-metamorphic origin. The thickness of this occurrence is some metres."
3. **[[Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)|Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)]]** (No. 3): 113 km NW of [[Meulaboh]]. "Over a length of 700 m iron ore, pyrite and pyrolusite were observed. Thickness irregular from 8 to 17 m. Probably also contact-metamorphic origin."
4. **[[Kr. Rigaih]]** (No. 4): 88 km NW of Meulaboh. "The deposits consist of scattered fragments of ore, formed at the contact of eruptive rock (dikes) and sediments. Locally garnet has been observed."
5. **[[Alu Talu]]** (No. 5): 78 km NW of Meulaboh. Same description as Kr. Rigaih.
6. **[[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe]]** (No. 6): 30 km NW of Meulaboh. Bog-iron (swamp-ore) deposit. "Beach deposits: Scattered fragments of ore at the surface. Below 40 cm reddish-yellow sand, containing lumps of ore."
7. **[[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Tjot Plaj]]** (No. 7): 12 km NW of Meulaboh. Bog-iron (swamp-ore) deposit. Same description as Kwala Boe. The combined Kwala Boe–Tjot Plaj bog-iron reserve is estimated at 300,000–400,000 tons of lump-ore.
8. **[[A. Pentuwa Gade]]** (No. 8): 68 km SE of Meulaboh. "No particulars available."
9. **[[Kuto Buloh]]** (No. 9): 27 km North of [[Tapa Tuan]]. "No particulars available."

## The Kr. Geuenteuet reserve

The [[1920]] reserve estimate for the Kr. Geuenteuet occurrence — 2,600,000 tons of ore with an average of 58–64 % Fe — is the largest documented colonial-era iron-ore reserve on Atjeh territory. The estimate is recorded by Van Bemmelen with the additional note that "Investigation was suspended on account of low market-prices." The 58–64 % Fe grade is in the range of medium-grade iron ore, suitable for blast-furnace smelting; the gold-and-silver byproduct content (10 gr Au/ton, 33 gr Ag/ton) recorded in the [[1922]] Jansen-Lindberg-Wolvekamp report would, in other circumstances, have made the Kr. Geuenteuet occurrence a candidate for combined iron-and-precious-metal mining.

## The Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj bog-iron deposits

The Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj bog-iron deposits (Nos. 6/7) are the only Atjeh iron-ore localities classified in the colonial geological literature as bog-iron (swamp-ore) deposits rather than as contact-metamorphic magnetite-hematite deposits. The 300,000–400,000-ton lump-ore reserve estimate is the only quantitative bog-iron reserve estimate for Atjeh in the colonial geological literature. [[Philippi|Philippi]]'s thermal-origin hypothesis — proposed on the basis of a few samples from a single pit — is supported, in Van Bemmelen's judgment, by the "peculiar structure and the comparatively high Zn content (3–4 %)" of the deposits, which are indeed more consistent with a hydrothermal than with a purely surficial bog-iron origin.

## Significance

The Iron ores in Atjeh is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the corpus of colonial-era geological observations that document the occurrence of iron-ore deposits in the Residency of Atjeh — a corpus that includes the largest documented colonial-era iron-ore reserve on Atjeh territory (the 2,600,000-ton, 58–64 % Fe Kr. Geuenteuet deposit) and the only documented colonial-era bog-iron reserve estimate for Atjeh (the 300,000–400,000-ton Kwala Boe–Tjot Plaj deposit). The non-development of the Atjeh iron-ore occurrences — the "suspension [of investigation] on account of low market-prices" at Kr. Geuenteuet — is itself historically significant: it is the documentary evidence that the colonial state, despite its substantial investment in the geological survey of Atjeh, was unwilling or unable to translate geological reserves into productive mines.

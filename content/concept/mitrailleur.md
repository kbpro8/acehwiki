*machine gun* — the [[Dutch]] term for a machine gun — was the principal automatic weapon of the Dutch colonial army in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], deployed in fixed positions at the outposts and in mobile columns. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* records *mitrailleurs* at [[Lamsoet]] (one on field-carriage, one on breastwork-carriage), at where it was twice made unserviceable and twice repaired under fire by *Sgt. son*, and in the field columns of the spring [[1896]] counter-offensives. The mitrailleur's loss on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the disastrous [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] — together with the loss of a 7 cm L.A. *kanon* — was one of the most serious Dutch matériel losses of the period: the captured mitrailleur was subsequently brought by [[Panglima Polim]] to [[Senelop]] and used against the Dutch.

## Etymology and Meaning

The word *mitrailleur* is French, derived from small shot, grapeshot, and originally denoted a multi-barrel volley gun of the mid-nineteenth century (the *mitrailleuse* of the Franco-Prussian War, 1870–71). By the [[1890s]] the Dutch colonial army's *mitrailleur* was a true machine gun — likely the Maxim or a similar automatic weapon — mounted either on a field carriage for mobile use or on a breastwork carriage for fixed defence. The chest with barrels was a key component: the loss of this chest at [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]] effectively destroyed the weapon's utility. The Dutch term was widely used in the [[Netherlands Indies|Nederlandsch-Indië]] army alongside the more specific gun-type names.

## Historical Context

In the first semester of [[1896]] the mitrailleur was a critical force-multiplier for small Dutch garrisons exposed to Achehnese attack. At [[Lamsoet]] the deployment of two mitrailleurs (one on field-carriage, one on breastwork-carriage) reflected the post's strategic importance and its vulnerability to assault. At *blokhuis Aloer Oe* the mitrailleur was the garrison's principal defensive weapon during the [[Siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe (28–29 March 1896)|siege of 28 March–29 March 1896]]; it was twice made unserviceable by enemy fire and twice repaired by No. 40248 of the artillery — an act for which son received formal recognition. The [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] saw the catastrophic loss of the field-carriage mitrailleur's *kast met loopen* — the chest with barrels — together with a 7 cm L.A. *kanon* and a 12 cm *mortier*, when a Dutch column under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was driven back in retreat. The captured mitrailleur was reportedly set up at the *Oelee Nehen* versterking and brought by [[Panglima Polim]] to [[Senelop]]; it was searched for in vain on [[2 May]] [[1896]].

The mitrailleur lost at [[Senelop]] on [[17 April]] [[1896]] — the chest with barrels lost in Van Heutsz's disastrous retreat. The captured mitrailleur — reportedly set up at the *Oelee Nehen* versterking and brought by [[Panglima Polim]] to [[Senelop]]; searched for in vain on [[2 May]] [[1896]]. The mitrailleur appears repeatedly in the ammunition-expenditure reports of the spring [[1896]] operations. The combination of mitrailleur and 7 cm L.A. *kanon* was the standard armament of a defended Dutch post.

## Significance

The mitrailleur is the technological symbol of the asymmetric warfare of the [[Aceh War]]. On the Dutch side, the mitrailleur was the principal means by which a small garrison could hold a *blokhuis* against mass Achehnese assault; on the Achehnese side, the capture of a mitrailleur at [[Senelop]] was a strategic windfall, giving the resistance a brief window of technological parity. The Dutch loss of the mitrailleur and the 7 cm L.A. *kanon* at Senelop was therefore one of the most consequential matériel losses of the period, materially affecting the balance of forces in the [[XXV Moekims|sagi XXV]] through May [[1896]]. The repeated Dutch searches for the captured weapon — and the strategic concern about its possible use against Dutch positions — testify to the perceived importance of the weapon.

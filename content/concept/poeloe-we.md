**Poeloe Wè** — modern *Pulau Weh* — is the island off the northernmost point of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] whose harbour, [[Sabang|Sabang]], is the principal oceaanhaven of the residency, and whose orography — the [[Goudberg|Goudberg]] (67) with the [[Weesberg|Weesberg]] (68) and the northernmost volcano (69) — forms the northernmost transverse ridge of the Sumatran volcanic system. In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), Poeloe Wè is the northernmost geographic feature of the Sumatran archipelago, and the principal Dutch oceaanhaven of the Atjeh coast.

## Orography

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I identifies Poeloo Wè as the northernmost transverse ridge of the Sumatran volcanic system: "Over den Goudberg (67) [in Groot-Atjeh] eindigt de Oostrij van het eiland met de oceaanhaven, Sabang, terwijl de Goudberg (67) met den ten Oosten daarvan gelegen Weesberg (68) de Noordelijkste dwarsrij vormt." The Goudberg, the Weesberg, and the northernmost volcano (69) of Poeloe Wè are thus the three-element transverse ridge that closes the eastern volcanic row of Sumatra at the northern extremity of the island.

## Hydrography

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I also notes Poeloe Wè's role as the northernmost of the smaller islands off the Sumatran coast: "Het ten Noorden van Atjeh gelegen P. We werd reeds genoemd om de uitstekende Sabang-haven." The island is paired with the smaller [[Poeloe Breuë|Poeloe Breuë]] to its west as "de verbrokkelde voortzetting der Barisanketenen" — the broken continuation of the Barisan chain beyond the northern tip of Sumatra.

## Significance

Poeloe Wè is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the northernmost geographic feature of the Sumatran archipelago and the principal Dutch oceaanhaven of the Atjeh coast. The Bureau's emphasis on the island's role as the northernmost transverse ridge of the Sumatran volcanic system — and on the *uitstekende Sabang-haven* — frames Poeloe Wè as both a geological and a strategic feature: the geological closure of the Sumatran volcanic system at its northern extremity, and the strategic anchor of the Dutch colonial presence at the northern entrance to the Strait of Malacca.

In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|staff history's]] Atjeh chapter, Poeloe Wè (Sabang) is the site of the *[[Oorlogsdetachement Sabang|Oorlogsdetachement Sabang]]* of December [[1941]]–February [[1942]], of the [[Sabang bombing (22 January 1942)|Sabang bombing of 22 January 1942]], and of the Japanese landing of [[12 March]] [[12 March 1942|12 March 1942]].

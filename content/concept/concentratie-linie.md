**The Concentratie-linie** (Dutch for "concentration line") was the system of fortified posts constructed around the Dutch hoofd-stelling at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — comprising the [[Westerlinie|Westerlinie]], the [[Noorderlinie|Noorderlinie]], the [[Zuidoosterlinie|Zuidoosterlinie]], and the [[Noordoosterlinie|Noordoosterlinie]] — by which the Dutch sought to defend their position against the Acehnese guerrilla resistance. The system was initiated by [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] in [[1874]] after the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] and was developed through the commands of [[G. B. T. Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]], [[A. J. E. Diemont|Diemont]] and [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]], until by [[1879]] no fewer than thirty-eight posts had been set up for the defence of the hoofd-stelling alone, requiring a garrison of 22 companies — 2750 men — and a considerable artillery armament.

## The System

After the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] and the [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation of annexation]] of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] on [[31 January]] [[31 January]] [[1874]], the Dutch held the Kraton and a few outworks. [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s note of [[22 April]] [[22 April]] [[1874]] laid down a defensive policy: active defence of Kota Radja itself, but elsewhere passive defence. After the departure of Van Swieten on [[26 April]] [[26 April]] [[1874]] the war continued under General Pel, who by [[June 1875]] had secured the hoofd-stelling on all sides by an unbroken chain of fortifications.

The system comprised four lines:

- The **Westerlinie** — a series of posts west of Kota Radja to connect it with [[Marassa|Marassa]] and [[Lampassei|Lampassei]], the principal post being [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] on the coast west of the mouth of the Atjeh river.
- The **Noorderlinie** — with posts at [[Lamara|Lamara]] and Kota Radja Bedil, strengthened in [[1874]] to preserve the connection with the Atjeh river.
- The **Zuidoosterlinie** — under the command of [[K. van der Heyden|Colonel Van der Heyden]] from [[1 April]] [[1 April]] [[1876]], frequently attacked by the enemy.
- The **Noordoosterlinie** — also frequently attacked; the [[missigit Lambada|missigit Lambada]] in this line was the object of a Dutch échec on [[12 October]] [[12 October]] [[1876]].

## The Cost

"Besides the hoofd-stelling itself, no fewer than thirty-eight posts had been set up for its defence, requiring a garrison of 22 companies — 2750 men — and a considerable artillery armament, while Van Swieten had considered 3500 men sufficient for the whole garrison of Atjeh. Repeatedly, therefore, our troop strength had to be reinforced by new dispatches of troops, both to supply the necessary men for the occupation of all those posts and what was further needed for the mobile column and other services, and to fill the places of those who had to be evacuated because of wounds, sickness and other reasons." By [[December 1874]] the army strength in Atjeh had been brought by temporary reinforcements to 4000 infantry. In [[1874]] alone, after the departure of the troops on [[26 April]] [[26 April]], the number of deaths was 902 and the number of evacuated 1917.

## The Health Crisis

"In general, the health situation left much to be desired. That unfavourable situation must — besides from many secondary causes, an offshoot of the state of war — be attributed to the bad drinking water at most places of our position, to the extensive, now mostly fallow rice-fields, to the deep-cutting lagunes, to the considerable ground-moving, and to the felling of trees and bushes, to the insufficient shelter against the very variable climate. Through the great number of sick, the healthy had to perform a very exhausting service, and these consequently again the sooner took to the sickbed; cause and effect thus worked together to bring the sickness figures into serious proportions." On [[3 December]] [[3 December]] the whole valley of Groot-Atjeh was flooded by the many heavy rains. Only on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] had the water entirely subsided, leaving a thick layer of mud that spread an unbearable stench. During [[1875]] 957 dead and 5151 evacuated were counted.

By Indies decree of [[12 April]] [[12 April]] [[1875]] a commission was appointed to investigate locally the causes of the many and serious cases of illness that occurred in Atjeh and to indicate the means by which improvements might be brought about. To the proposals made in its report of [[1 July]] [[1 July]] partial effect was given.

## The Engineers' Works

The engineers had to provide for the putting in order of the various posts and for the works at the sea-head on iron screw-piles at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]], which was completed in [[September 1876|September 1876]], and at the railway between Oleh-leh and Kota Radja, which was completed in [[November 1876|November 1876]]. The post-system of General Van der Heyden, which replaced the system of General Pel after the close of large-scale operations in [[1879]], had not the object, like that of Pel, of keeping the enemy outside a certain limit, but of keeping him in check and of enabling the Dutch troops always to come without delay wherever their presence was required.

## Significance

The concentratie-linie was the characteristic form of the Dutch military presence in Atjeh during the first phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Its high cost in men and matériel, and the limited political results it produced, led to its eventual abandonment in favour of the more aggressive policy of General Pel in [[1875]] and General Van der Heyden in [[1878]]-[[1879]]. After the close of large-scale operations in [[August 1879]] the system was gradually reduced, but the basic pattern of a fortified hoofd-stelling at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], with outlying posts at the principal strategic points, continued until the final pacification of Atjeh under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] after [[1898]].

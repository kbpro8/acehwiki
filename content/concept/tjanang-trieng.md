**Tjanang Triëng** (also spelled *tjanang trieng*, *canang trieng*; Dutch: *driesnarige bamboe-cither* — "three-stringed bamboo cither/zither") is a **three-stringed bamboo tube zither** (idiochord) indigenous to **[[Acheh]]** (Atjēh), northern Sumatra. It belongs to the **tube zither** family widely distributed across Island Southeast Asia, where the strings are raised from the bamboo tube's own epidermis (idiochord) or are separate strings stretched over a bamboo resonator (heterochord).

## Organology

- **Hornbostel-Sachs classification**: 312.12 (tube zither, idiochord) or 312.22 (tube zither, heterochord) depending on construction
- **Body**: a single internode segment of large-diameter bamboo (*bambu betung* / *Dendrocalamus asper*), closed at both nodes
- **Strings**: three strings (*triëng* = "three" in Acehnese)
  - *Idiochord variant*: three strips of the bamboo epidermis partially cut and raised on small bridges (bamboo or wood), tensioned by tuning wedges
  - *Heterochord variant*: three separate strings (historically plant fiber, later wire/nylon) stretched over the tube
- **Bridges**: movable wooden or bamboo bridges under each string for tuning
- **Length**: typically 50–80 cm
- **Diameter**: 8–12 cm (bamboo internode)
- **Playing technique**: plucked with fingers (thumb and index) or plectrum; can be struck with light beaters
- **Tuning**: typically pentatonic or anhemitonic pentatonic (e.g., *do–re–mi–sol–la* equivalents), varying by region and ensemble

## Historical and Regional Context

The *tjanang triëng* is documented by **[[Jaap Kunst]]** in *[[Muziek en Dans in de Buitengewesten]]* ([[1946]], p. 10) as one of the instruments "for Atjēh" (*voor Atjēh*), listed alongside the *[[Hareubab]]* and *[[Geudoemba]]*. Kunst uses the Dutch term *driesnarige bamboe-cithers* ("three-stringed bamboo cithers"), noting the plural *cithers*, suggesting regional or organological variants within Aceh.

The instrument represents the **Acehnese manifestation of the widespread Southeast Asian bamboo tube zither complex**, which includes:

| Region / People | Local Name | Strings | Type |
|-----------------|------------|---------|------|
| **[[Acheh]]** | **Tjanang Triëng** | 3 | Idiochord / Heterochord |
| **[[Gayo Lands]]** | *Canang* / *Kecapi* | 2–3 | Heterochord |
| **[[Batak Lands]]** (Toba) | *Hasapi* / *Kecapi* | 2 | Lute (not zither) |
| **[[Menangkabau|Minangkabau]]** | *Saluang* (flute) / *Talempong* (gongs) | — | — |
| **[[Sundanese]]** (West Java) | *Celempung* / *Kacapi* | 18–20 | Board zither |
| **[[Javanese]]** | *Celempung* / *Siter* | 13–14 | Board zither |
| **[[Sasak]]** (Lombok) | *Genggong* (jaw harp) / *Kecapi* | 2–3 | Tube zither |
| **[[Toraja]]** | *Kesu* / *Kacapi* | 2–3 | Tube zither |
| **[[Philippines]]** (various) | *Kolitong*, *Kudlung*, *Sludoy* | 2–6 | Tube zither |

The *tjanang triëng* is distinct from the **board zithers** (*celempung*, *kacapi*) of Java and Sunda, and from the **two-stringed boat lutes** (*hasapi*, *kacapi*) of the Batak and Gayo. Its three-string idiochord construction links it most closely to the **Philippine tube zithers** and the **Sasak *kecapi***, reflecting the ancient Austronesian diffusion of bamboo zither technology.

## Role in Acehnese Music

The *tjanang triëng* functions as a **melodic and harmonic accompaniment instrument** in:

- **[[Dendang]]** / **[[Hikayat]]** narrative singing — providing a shimmering, continuous harmonic drone and melodic counterpoint
- **[[Meuseukat]]** (women's seated dance-song) — rhythmic-harmonic ostinato
- **[[Saman]]** rehearsal and teaching contexts — melodic guide for vocal lines
- **Solo instrumental performance** — improvisatory *taksim*-like preludes (*buka*)

Its quiet, intimate timbre limits it to indoor, chamber, or pedagogical settings rather than outdoor processional music (where *[[Serunai (Aceh)|serunai]]*, *[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]*, *[[Marwas]]* dominate).

## Etymology

- **Tjanang** / **Canang**: from **Acehnese** *canang* — "zither, stringed instrument" (cognate with Malay *gambang* "xylophone", but here specifically denoting zither-type chordophones). The term appears in Old Malay and Cham inscriptions denoting stringed instruments.
- **Triëng**: Acehnese numeral **"three"** (cf. Indonesian *tiga*, Malay *tiga*, Gayo *tuluh* — Austronesian *telu*).
- **Dutch transcription**: *tjanang triëng* reflects the Dutch colonial orthography (tj = [tʃ], c = [tʃ] in modern Indonesian; *ië* = [iə] or [ie]).

## Tuning and Repertoire

Traditional tunings are not standardized but follow **Acehnese pentatonic modes** (*laras*) related to the *[[Maqam]]* / *[[Makam]]* system of the Islamic world, adapted to local aesthetics. Common intervallic patterns approximate:

- *Laras Pelog*-like: 1 – 1½ – 2 – 3½ – 4½ (in semitone approximations)
- *Laras Slendro*-like: equidistant pentatonic (approx. 240¢ steps)

Repertoire includes *[[Dendang Aceh]]*, *[[Hikayat Prang Sabi]]* excerpts, *[[Syair]]* melodic frameworks, and instrumental pieces (*instrumental*) for teaching *[[Saman]]* melodies.

## Iconography

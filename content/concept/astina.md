**Astina** — in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s decoding *h-asti-na-ma*, "elephant" — is the mythical elephant-land of the Sumatran east coast that, in the Javanese legend recorded by [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles]] (*History* II, p. 87), was the realm of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]] of Jaya's oldest princely house, and which on the dynasty's extinction changed its name to *Enj°-* or *Guj'rat* — a *bedekte taal* form for the dead elephant-land of [[Bali-Atjé|Bali-Atjé]] on the Sumatran Overwal. It is the principal *bedekte taal* form by which De Roo de la Faille ties the Javanese legend of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]] to the [[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere.

## The legend in Raffles

[[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles]]'s *History* (vol. II, p. 87) records the Javanese legend of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]] of [[Astina|Astina]], whose name — *h-asti-na-ma*, "elephant" — was changed on the dynasty's extinction to *Enj°-* or *Guj'rat*. A Dutch *babad*-translation (*Not. Bat. Gen.* vol. 42) speaks of this king as a "Moorschen" prince of *kodjrat-Bali-Atjé* — where the *mor*, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, is plainly used in the sense of *kahilangan* ("vanished, deceased"), and the *Kodj* or *Guj-rat* conceals *Koen- of Goendjara-ma-t-i*, "the dead land of elephants".

## The Sumatran elephant-land

There was, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, an elephant-land on the Sumatran east coast — a land rich in war- and working-elephants, which the *Xarnauz* of the *Roteiro* of [[Vasco da Gama]] ("the king has 400 elephants of war") also records. This Sumatran *Astina* is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the [[Koendjara|Koendjara]] of the *Woekir Tjanggal* inscription of [[732]] — the *Koendjara-koendja* from which the lingga of [[Sanjaya|Sandjaya]] was brought "as it were for the salvation of the world" — and the same *Koendjara* whose name survives, in reversal, in the *Djakoen|Djakoen]] aborigines of the Malay peninsula.

## The *Koendjara-ma-ti Bali-Atjé*

The *Astina* of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]], on the Sumatran east coast, was replaced by ***Koendjara-koendja*** — the name that, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, took the place of *Astina* on the dynasty's extinction, and which is the *bedekte taal* form of the dead elephant-land (*Koendjara-ma-ti*) of [[Bali-Atjé|Bali-Atjé]] — i.e. of [[Tandjoeng Poera|Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Deli|Deli]]-[[Asahan|Asahan]] on the Sumatran Overwal. The same identification, De Roo de la Faille argues, fits the *Xarnauz* of [[Vasco da Gama]]'s *Roteiro* — *k-s-ir arnaw-a dj-a-wa*, "the island in the sea of Sumatra, where the Fountain was that flowed only balsam" (i.e. the [[Minjak tanah|minjak tanah]] of [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]]).

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Astina is the principal *bedekte taal* form by which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] ties the Javanese legend of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]] to the [[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere — the mythical elephant-land of the Sumatran east coast whose dead form *Koendjara-ma-ti Bali-Atjé* underlies the Javanese *Gujrat*/*Kodjrat* name-change. Its identification with the *Xarnauz* of [[Vasco da Gama]]'s *Roteiro* also fixes it as the principal east-coast landmark of pre-Atjehnese Sumatra in European travel record.

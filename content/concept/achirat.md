**Achirat (afterworld)** is the Arabic term *al‑ākhirah* denoting the life after death, a central concept in Islamic theology that appears in Acehnese religious literature, notably in the Malay translation of the *Burda* and in sermons and poetry, motivating moral conduct by reminding believers of the ultimate reward (*Jannah*) or punishment (*Jahannam*). It is recorded in several Acehnese glossaries (e.g., pages [[73]]‑[[79]] and [[88]]) and reflects the integration of eschatological concepts into local religious thought.

## Theological Meaning

Achirat encompasses the notions of Paradise (*Jannah*) and Hell (*Jahannam*) as described in the Qur’an, for instance in [[Qur’an 2:80]]. It is contrasted with the transient worldly life (*dunya*), embodying the Islamic belief in an eternal afterlife that determines the fate of every soul.

## Acehnese Glossary and Textual Evidence

The term is attested in the 16th‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* of al‑Busiri, where it appears repeatedly to emphasize the ultimate reward or punishment awaiting believers. The manuscript (DLP‑54) records the word on pages [[73]]‑[[79]] and again on page [[88]] with multiple citations (21, 34, 82, 159). These occurrences demonstrate the penetration of Arabic theological vocabulary into the Malay language of Aceh and its codification in local glossaries.

## Usage in Poetry and Sermons

Acehnese religious poetry, such as the *Burda* translation, employs *achirat* to motivate moral conduct and devotion to the Prophet [[Muhammad]]. Likewise, sermons invoke *achirat* to remind listeners of the consequences of their actions in the hereafter, reinforcing ethical behavior through eschatological imagery.

## Significance

The presence of *achirat* in Acehnese texts underscores the integration of Islamic eschatology into Acehnese religious thought and literary culture. It illustrates how global Islamic concepts were localized, shaping devotional practices and moral instruction in the region.

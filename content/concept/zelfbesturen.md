**The Zelfbesturen** ("self-governing territories," singular *zelfbestuur*) were the native-ruled territories of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] of the Netherlands East Indies, governed by their own rulers under Dutch suzerainty and supervised by the Gouvernement through [[Politieke Contracten|politieke contracten]] (before [[1899]]) or the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] (after [[1899]]). In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the zelfbesturen are the principal object of the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] administrative order in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the wider Buitenbezittingen: the territories whose rulers were transformed, through the [[Civiele lijst|civiele lijst]] and the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]], from autonomous sovereigns into paid functionaries of the Dutch colonial order.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Zelfbesturen

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Zelfbesturen|zelfbesturen]] appears in the chapter on administrative organisation. The Bureau frames the regulation of the relationship between the zelfbesturen and the Gouvernement as one of the two principal axes of Dutch colonial policy in the Buitenbezittingen — the other being the direct administration of the rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied (directly administered territory).

The Bureau notes that "[[1899|until [[1899]]]] the regulation of the relationship of the [[Zelfbesturen|Zelfbesturen]] to the Gouvernement generally found its expression in the detailed political contracts (*omstandige politieke contracten*) concluded between the two parties, in which the principles to be observed in the governance of the landschappen, as well as the mutual rights, obligations, and competences, were set out. Since [[1899|that year]] a different path has been taken... [namely the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]]]."

The Bureau notes that, even after [[1899]], a small and declining minority of larger sultanates retained the older detailed political contracts. As of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade, these were: on the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] — Asahan, Deli, Koela, Langkat, Pelalawan, Serdang, Siak Sri Indrapoera; in the [[Westerafdeeling van Borneo|Western Division of Borneo]] — Landak, Mampawa, Matan, Pontianak, Sambas, Sanggau, Tajan; in the [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo|Southern and Eastern Division of Borneo]] — Koetei; in [[Celebes en Onderhoorigheden|Celebes and Dependencies]] — Laiwoei; and in [[Timor en Onderhoorigheden|Timor and Dependencies]] — Bima, Dompo, Sanggar, Soembawa. The Bureau notes that "the rulers of all other landschappen, in total about 306 in number, signed the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]]."

The Bureau's account of the principal innovation of the newer regulation of the political relationship — the introduction of the [[Civiele lijst|civiele lijst]] and the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] — is explicit about its transformative intent: "the principal principle that has been introduced in the newer regulation of the political relationship — whether laid down in contracts or in the K. V. — is that the revenues of the landschap, instead of being — as formerly — considered as the private revenues of the rulers, who only inadequately spent a part of them for the benefit of land and people, belong to the landschap and must therefore be paid into a [[Landschapskassen|landschapskas]], in order to fund from it all kinds of expenditures in the interest of land and people, including — under our guidance and oversight — the income of the rulers in the form of a 'civiele lijst'."

## Significance

[[Zelfbesturen|The zelfbesturen]] are, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal object of the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] administrative order in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the wider Buitenbezittingen — the territories whose rulers were transformed, through the [[Civiele lijst|civiele lijst]] and the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]], from autonomous sovereigns into paid functionaries of the Dutch colonial order. The post-[[1899]] shift from [[Politieke Contracten|politieke contracten]] to [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]], and the [[1914]] codification of the new regime by the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]], together mark the legal consolidation of the new order.

## Account from *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1930) — the Atjehsche *Zelfbestuur* in operational detail

The *[[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Zelfbestuursverordeningen]]* compilation of [[1930]] records the *Zelfbestuur* system as applied in [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] after the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|pacification]]. The system rested on three constitutional pillars: the *oeleebalang* — the territorial chief of a *negeri* / *landschap* — as *Zelfbestuurder*; the *Korte Verklaring* as the constitutional bond; and the *Zelfbestuursregelen 1919* ([[Staatsblad 1919 No. 822]]) and *Zelfbestuursregelen 1927* as the formal framework within which the *Zelfbestuurders* could issue their own *verordeningen*. The supervisory authority of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] and the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar*, and the *goedkeuring* requirement for every *zelfbestuursverordening* and *zelfbestuursbesluit*, fixed the constitutional subordination of the *Zelfbestuur* to the colonial state.

### The *oeleebalang* as *Zelfbestuurder*

The Dutch colonial state had, from [[1898]] onward, bound the successive *oeleebalang* of the coastal and highland *landschappen* of Atjeh to itself by the *Korte Verklaring* — the short political pact by which each signatory acknowledged Dutch sovereignty and promised to follow the Government's *inzichten*. The *Korte Verklaring* was, in the constitutional theory of Dutch indirect rule, the political foundation on which the *Zelfbestuur* rested: it bound the *oeleebalang* to the colonial state without abolishing his native authority, and it conferred on the colonial state the supervisory powers the *goedkeuring* requirement would operationalise. The *Nota van Toelichting* on the *inkomstenbelasting* regeling (Reg. No. 9b of the 1930 compilation) makes the constitutional logic explicit:

> "Al moge de onderworpenheid van het Atjehsche Zelfbestuur aan het Europeesch gezag in het algemeen reeds uit de Korte Verklaringen voortvloeien".

The *onderworpenheid* — the subordination — of the Atjehsche *Zelfbestuur* to the European authority flowed, in general, from the *Korte Verklaringen* themselves. The *Korte Verklaring* was thus both the political pact that bound the *oeleebalang* to the colonial state and the constitutional source of the supervisory powers the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* and the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar* would exercise.

### The *Zelfbestuursregelen 1919* and *Zelfbestuursregelen 1927*

The *Zelfbestuursregelen 1919* ([[Staatsblad 1919 No. 822]]) supplied the formal framework within which the *Zelfbestuurders* could issue their own *verordeningen*. The *Zelfbestuursregelen 1927* updated the framework, but the *Voorwoord* of the 1930 compilation notes that "*artikel 26 der zelfbestuursregelen 1927 in dit gewest nog geen toepassing heeft gevonden*" — meaning that, in Atjeh, the collective-*vaststelling* practice (under which all *Zelfbestuurders* of an *onderafdeeling* issued their regulations jointly) still prevailed over the single-*landschap* system the 1927 reform envisaged. The result was that the *Zelfbestuur* in Atjeh operated, throughout the period covered by the compilation, under the 1919 framework as modified by local practice, with the 1927 reform not yet applied.

### The supervisory architecture

The supervisory authority over the *Zelfbestuur* in Atjeh was exercised by two colonial officers. The **Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden** — in the Malay of the regulations *Seri Padoeka Toean Besar Civiel dan Militair Gouverneur Atjeh* or *Toean Besar Atjeh* — was the chief colonial executive in Atjeh, and all *zelfbestuursverordeningen* in the 1930 compilation are issued "*onder goedkeuring*" of this office. The **Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar** — in Malay *kepala Pemerintah* (Hoofd van Plaatselijk Bestuur) — was the local Dutch civil administrator who exercised the day-to-day supervision of the *Zelfbestuur*. The *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar*'s principal operational role was the *Gezien* countersignature on the *kwitanties* of the [[Pantjang Alas]] and the supervision of the quarterly valuation of the *boschproducten*. Above the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar* stood the *Assistent Resident* (the Dutch district officer for the *afdeeling*), and above the *Assistent Resident* stood the *Hoofd van gewestelijk bestuur* — the senior colonial official above the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar*, who functioned as the Gouverneur's delegate for regional administrative matters.

The appellate authority of the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* is fixed throughout the compilation: "*Bij verschil van meening beslist de Gouverneur in hoogste instantie*" — in case of difference of opinion, the Gouverneur decides in highest instance. The *Hoofd van gewestelijk bestuur* appears, in turn, as the appellate authority for the *heerendienst*-*afkoop* disputes and as the *machtiging*-granting authority for distant *heerendiensten*. The supervisory architecture thus rested on a three-tier hierarchy — *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar*, *Assistent Resident*, *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* — through which the colonial state exercised the supervisory powers the *Korte Verklaring* had conferred.

### The *goedkeuring* requirement and the collective *vaststelling*

The constitutional requirement that fixed the subordination of the *Zelfbestuur* to the colonial state was the *goedkeuring* — the approval — that every *zelfbestuursverordening* and *zelfbestuursbesluit* had to obtain from the *Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* before taking effect. Every regulation in the 1930 compilation carries the printed note "(*goedgekeurd door den Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden op …*)" — approved by the Gouverneur of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden on [date] — together with the date of the *goedkeuring*. The most important fiscal cases (notably the Pantjang Alas of [[1912]] and the *inkomstenbelasting* of [[1920]]) required, in addition, the prior *vergunning* of the *Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië*, obtained by telegram. The *goedkeuring* requirement was, in this sense, the operational counterpart of the *Korte Verklaring*'s political subordination: where the *Korte Verklaring* bound the *oeleebalang* to the colonial state in form, the *goedkeuring* requirement bound him in operational practice.

The *goedkeuring* requirement was combined, in the operational order of the *Zelfbestuur*, with the collective *vaststelling* practice that characterised the Atjehsche regulations. The *Voorwoord* of the 1930 compilation records that "*de vaststelling van verordeningen in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden steeds collectief geschiedt*" — the establishment of ordinances in the *gewest* Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden always takes place collectively — meaning that all *Zelfbestuurders* of an *onderafdeeling* — sometimes of an *afdeeling* — issued their regulations jointly. The 24 *Zelfbestuurders* of the Pidië *landschappen*, for example, jointly issued Reg. No. 4a of [[27 July]] [[1925]]; the three *oeleebalang* of [[Samalanga]], [[Peusangan]], and [[Gloempang-doea]] jointly issued Reg. No. 14 of [[18 August]] [[1928]]; the ten *oeleebalang* of Sawang, Nisam, Tjoenda, Lhoseumawe, Bajoe, Blang Mangat, Samakoero, Blocë, Geudong, and Blang Me jointly issued Reg. No. 8 of [[1 January]] [[1918]]. The corporate-collective signatory formula is fixed in the bilingual title-pages: in Malay "*Kami Oeleebalang dari Landschap: …*"; in Dutch "*De Zelfbesturen van de landschappen: …*". The collective *vaststelling* practice was, in the 1930 compilation, the operational expression of the *Zelfbestuur*'s collective authority — the constitutional form by which the *oeleebalang* of an *onderafdeeling* legislated, in their own name and in their own Malay, the very fiscal and police measures the colonial state desired.

### Significance of the 1930 account

The *Zelfbestuur*'s significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record, in the 1930 compilation's perspective, rests on four things: it was the Dutch colonial system of indirect rule through "self-governing" native chiefs — the *oeleebalang* of the Acehnese *landschappen* — that the colonial state imposed on Atjeh after the pacification of [[1898]] onward, and that formed the constitutional framework within which the operational legal architecture of Dutch indirect rule in Atjeh was constructed; it was the system whose political foundation was the *Korte Verklaring* — the short political pact by which each *oeleebalang* acknowledged Dutch sovereignty and promised to follow the Government's *inzichten*, and from which the *onderworpenheid van het Atjehsche Zelfbestuur aan het Europeesch gezag* was held to flow; it was the system whose formal framework was supplied by the *Zelfbestuursregelen 1919* (Staatsblad 1919 No. 822) and *Zelfbestuursregelen 1927*, and whose collective *vaststelling* practice — under which all *Zelfbestuurders* of an *onderafdeeling* issued their regulations jointly — was preserved in Atjeh by the *Voorwoord*'s observation that "*artikel 26 der zelfbestuursregelen 1927 in dit gewest nog geen toepassing heeft gevonden*"; and it was the system whose supervisory architecture — the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur*, the *Plaatselijk Besturend Ambtenaar*, the *Assistent Resident*, the *Hoofd van gewestelijk bestuur*, and the *goedkeuring* requirement — fixed the constitutional subordination of the *Zelfbestuur* to the colonial state in operational practice. The *Zelfbestuur* is, in this sense, the foundational legal-political concept of the 1930 compilation — the constitutional theory of Dutch indirect rule in Atjeh whose operational instruments (the *Pantjang Alas*, the *heerendienst*, the *inkomstenbelasting*, the *verordeningen* on woeker, marriage, religious instruction, slaughter, and cattle-export) the compilation records.

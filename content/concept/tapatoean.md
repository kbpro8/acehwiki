**Tapatoean** — *Tapaktoean* in the spelling of the *[[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]*, *Tapaktuan* / *Tapak Tuan* in modern Indonesian — was a Dutch *bivak* of the [[1ste Garnizoensbataljon|1ste Garnizoensbataljon]] (later of the [[2de (IIde) Divisie Marechaussee|IIde Divisie Maréchaussee]]) on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]], between [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]] to the north and [[Bakongan|Bakongan]] to the south. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Tapatoean is the Westkust *bivak* whose progressive drawdown in [[1940]]–[[1941]] is the staff history's principal measure of the Dutch abandonment of the southern Westkust, and whose *bivak* served as the southern concentration point of the [[Evacuation of women and children from the Westkust of Atjeh (March 1942)|Westkust evacuation]] of March [[1942]]. In the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, Tapaktoean also appears as an *onderafdeeling* on the south coast of [[Atjeh|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], seated alongside the *onderafdeeling Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen* and the island *onderafdeeling* [[Simeuloe|Simeuloe]] as the three south-coast *onderafdeelingen* to which the *vrouwelijk-vee verordening* of [[1 August]] [[1927]] (Reg. No. 17) was extended by parallel *regelingen*, and one of the original *onderafdeelingen* of the 1912 *Pantjang Alas* regime with its *Pantjang Alas* regeling dated [[15 April]] [[1912]].

## Pre-war garrison and 1940 dissolution

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], Tapatoean held four brigades of the 1ste Garnizoensbataljon. In [[1940]], with the formation of the [[Westkustcolonne|Westkustcolonne]] of nine brigades, "de detachementen Tapatoean en Meulaboh werden hierdoor opgeheven." The Tapatoean brigades were moved out of Atjeh and "zijn nimmer meer in Atjeh teruggekeerd."

## December 1941 dissolution and March 1942 reoccupation

In December [[1941]] "werden de bivaks te Tapatoean en Bakongan door de aldaar gelegerde 8 brigades Maréchaussee ontruimd." The eight brigades (the Tapatoean four plus the Bakongan seven, minus three brigades left at [[Lho Seumawé|Lho Seumawé]]) were sent under Captain **T. J. F. M. Supheert** via [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Malakka|Malakka]] "om daar ten behoeve van onze geallieerden de guerrilla tegen de Japanners te voeren." The Tapatoean *bivak* was thus formally dissolved as a Dutch garrison by December [[1941]].

In mid-March [[1942]], however, the Tapatoean *bivak* was reoccupied as a concentration point for the Westkust women-and-children convoys. By [[17 March]] [[17 March 1942|17 March]] the Tapatoean *bivak* held 1,400 women and children under the dekking of one Maréchaussee brigade. The reoccupied *bivak* was the destination of Lt [[A. Zijlmans|Zijlmans]]'s 15-hour ambush march from [[Blang Pidië|Blang Pidië]] on [[17 March]] [[17 March 1942|17 March]] (see [[Battle of Blang Pidië (16 March 1942)|Battle of Blang Pidië]]).

## Significance

Tapatoean is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the Westkust *bivak* whose repeated dissolution (in [[1940]] and again in December [[1941]]) and subsequent reoccupation as a convoy concentration point (in March [[1942]]) symbolise the Dutch operational paradox of the Westkust campaign: the *bivak* that had been formally abandoned as a garrison became, within three months, the principal southern concentration point of the Westkust evacuation. The staff history's emphasis on the Tapatoean *bivak*'s changing function frames it as the staff history's principal narrative instance of the Dutch colonial state's loss and partial recovery of its Westkust infrastructure in the final months before the Japanese occupation.

## Account from *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1930) — the regulatory record

In the [[1930]] compilation of *Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*, Tapaktoean — *Tapatoean* in older Dutch spelling, also *Tapaktooan* and *Tapaktoenn* in OCR variants — was an *onderafdeeling* on the south coast of [[Atjeh|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], seated alongside the *onderafdeeling Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen* and the island *onderafdeeling* [[Simeuloe]] as the three south-coast *onderafdeelingen* to which the *vrouwelijk-vee verordening* of [[1 August]] [[1927]] (Reg. No. 17) was extended by parallel *regelingen*. Tapaktoean sits at the southern tip of [[Atjeh|Aceh]], on the south coast facing the Indian Ocean.

### The vrouwelijk-vee verordening of 1 August 1927 (Reg. No. 17)

The *vrouwelijk-vee verordening* of [[1 August]] [[1927]] — *Zelfbestuursverordening van 1 Augustus 1927 op het slachten van vrouwelijke buffels en runderen in de landschappen der onderafdeeling Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen* (Self-government regulation of 1 August 1927 on the slaughter of female buffaloes and cattle in the *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling* Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen) — is the only regulation in the 1930 compilation prohibiting the slaughter of female cattle and buffaloes. The originating signatories are the *oeleebalang* of the *landschappen* Kloeet and Troemon — the two south-coast *landschappen* of the *onderafdeeling Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen*. The comparative table on Page 125 extends parallel *regelingen* to Tapaktoean and Simeuloe:

> *GELIJKE VERORDENINGEN zijn vastgesteld voor de landschappen in de onderafdeelingen: Tapaktoean, op 25 Juli 1927, Simeuloet, op 20 Juli 1927. Deze verordeningen zijn goedgekeurd door den Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden op 20 Augustus 1927* (Equal regulations have been established for the *landschappen* in the *onderafdeelingen*: Tapaktoean, on 25 July 1927, Simeuloet [Simeuloe], on 20 July 1927. These regulations were approved by the Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden on 20 August 1927).

Tapaktoean's parallel regeling is thus dated [[25 July]] [[1927]] — one week before the originating Reg. No. 17 of [[1 August]] [[1927]] — and approved by the Gouverneur on [[20 August]] [[1927]], some twelve days before the originating regulation's *goedkeuring* of [[1 September]] [[1927]]. The *vrouwelijk-vee verordening* is the supplement to the Alaslanden cattle-transport regulation (Reg. No. 16) — the comparative table of Reg. No. 16 carries the cross-reference *Zie ook de verordening opgenomen onder nummer 17* (see also the regulation included under number 17).

### Tapaktoean in the Pantjang Alas and parallel-regelingen tables

The *onderafdeeling* Tapaktoean is one of the original *onderafdeelingen* of the 1912 *Pantjang Alas* regime, with a *Pantjang Alas* regeling dated [[15 April]] [[1912]] recorded in the comparative table of Reg. No. 2: *Tapaktoean …… 15-4-1912 caoutchoue, getah pertjah, damar, rotan, vogelnostjes, was, benzoë en hout* — i.e. Tapaktoean's *Pantjang Alas* regeling is one of the seven earliest *landschap*-level *Pantjang Alas* regelingen of April 1912, alongside Meulaboh, Idi, Langan, Tamiang, Tjulung, and the Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen. Tapaktoean's *Pantjang Alas* rate is 10% on caoutchouc, getah pertjah, damar, rotan, vogelnestjes, was, benzoë, and hout — the most-diverse *boschproducten* list of any 1912 *landschap*, reflecting Tapaktoean's role as the most commercially-productive south-coast *onderafdeeling*.

The Page 54 comparative table extends the Alaslanden cattle-slaughter tax (Reg. No. 5) to Tapaktoean (1914). The Page 109 comparative table extends the Bireuën heerendienst-regeling (Reg. No. 14) to Tapaktoean — *Tapaktoean 11 Febr.* — with the synchronised *inwerkingtreding* of [[1 January]] [[1929]]. The Page 123 comparative table extends the Alaslanden cattle-transport regulation (Reg. No. 16) to Tapaktoean — *Tapaktoean 12 1914* (12 October 1914). The Page 129 comparative table extends the Lhoksoekon klappercultuur-*verordening* (Reg. No. 18a) to Tapaktoean — *Tapaktoenn Dec. 1916*. The Page 97 comparative table extends the Tjalang *wapensmeden-verordening* (Reg. No. 13) to Tapaktoean — *Tapaktoean 24-6-1927*. The Page 72 comparative table extends the Peureula' inkomstenbelasting (Reg. No. 9a) to Tapaktoean — *Tapaktoean* with the note *Onbekend* (unknown dagteekening), *inwerking* 1-1-1921.

### Significance (Zelfbestuursverordeningen account)

Tapaktoean's significance in the 1930 compilation rests on its being one of the three south-coast *onderafdeelingen* — alongside the *onderafdeeling Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen* (with the originating Kloeet-Troemon signatories) and the island *onderafdeeling* [[Simeuloe]] — to which the *vrouwelijk-vee verordening* of [[1 August]] [[1927]] (Reg. No. 17) was extended by parallel *regelingen* dated [[25 July]] [[1927]] (Tapaktoean) and [[20 July]] [[1927]] (Simeuloe), both *goedgekeurd* by the Gouverneur on [[20 August]] [[1927]]; one of the original *onderafdeelingen* of the 1912 *Pantjang Alas* regime — its *Pantjang Alas* regeling of [[15 April]] [[1912]] is one of the seven earliest *landschap*-level *Pantjang Alas* regelingen in the entire compilation, with the most-diverse *boschproducten* list of any 1912 *landschap*; and the demonstration — through its recurrent role as a recipient of parallel *regelingen* across nearly every regulation in the compilation (Regs. 5, 9a, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18a, 18b) — of the south-coast regulatory subsystem, in which Tapaktoean, the Zuidelijke Atjehsche landschappen, and Simeuloe together receive parallel *regelingen* of every Gouvernement-wide rollout.

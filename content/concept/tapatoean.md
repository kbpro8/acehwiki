**Tapatoean** was a Dutch *bivak* of the [[1ste Garnizoensbataljon|1ste Garnizoensbataljon]] (later of the [[2de (IIde) Divisie Marechaussee|IIde Divisie Maréchaussee]]) on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]], between [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]] to the north and [[Bakongan|Bakongan]] to the south. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Tapatoean is the Westkust *bivak* whose progressive drawdown in [[1940]]–[[1941]] is the staff history's principal measure of the Dutch abandonment of the southern Westkust, and whose *bivak* served as the southern concentration point of the [[Evacuation of women and children from the Westkust of Atjeh (March 1942)|Westkust evacuation]] of March [[1942]].

## Pre-war garrison and 1940 dissolution

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], Tapatoean held four brigades of the 1ste Garnizoensbataljon. In [[1940]], with the formation of the [[Westkustcolonne|Westkustcolonne]] of nine brigades, "de detachementen Tapatoean en Meulaboh werden hierdoor opgeheven." The Tapatoean brigades were moved out of Atjeh and "zijn nimmer meer in Atjeh teruggekeerd."

## December 1941 dissolution and March 1942 reoccupation

In December [[1941]] "werden de bivaks te Tapatoean en Bakongan door de aldaar gelegerde 8 brigades Maréchaussee ontruimd." The eight brigades (the Tapatoean four plus the Bakongan seven, minus three brigades left at [[Lho Seumawé|Lho Seumawé]]) were sent under Captain **T. J. F. M. Supheert** via [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Malakka|Malakka]] "om daar ten behoeve van onze geallieerden de guerrilla tegen de Japanners te voeren." The Tapatoean *bivak* was thus formally dissolved as a Dutch garrison by December [[1941]].

In mid-March [[1942]], however, the Tapatoean *bivak* was reoccupied as a concentration point for the Westkust women-and-children convoys. By [[17 March]] [[17 March 1942|17 March]] the Tapatoean *bivak* held 1,400 women and children under the dekking of one Maréchaussee brigade. The reoccupied *bivak* was the destination of Lt [[A. Zijlmans|Zijlmans]]'s 15-hour ambush march from [[Blang Pidië|Blang Pidië]] on [[17 March]] [[17 March 1942|17 March]] (see [[Battle of Blang Pidië (16 March 1942)|Battle of Blang Pidië]]).

## Significance

Tapatoean is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the Westkust *bivak* whose repeated dissolution (in [[1940]] and again in December [[1941]]) and subsequent reoccupation as a convoy concentration point (in March [[1942]]) symbolise the Dutch operational paradox of the Westkust campaign: the *bivak* that had been formally abandoned as a garrison became, within three months, the principal southern concentration point of the Westkust evacuation. The staff history's emphasis on the Tapatoean *bivak*'s changing function frames it as the staff history's principal narrative instance of the Dutch colonial state's loss and partial recovery of its Westkust infrastructure in the final months before the Japanese occupation.

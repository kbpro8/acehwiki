***Atap*** was the palm-leaf roofing used throughout the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Netherlands Indies]] and, in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], on the buildings of the Dutch *[[Benting|bentings]]*. [[P. H. R. Beuming]] glosses it as *bladeren van den kokospalm*, leaves of the coconut palm.

In his description of the *[[Voorpostenbenting|voorpostenbenting]]* Beuming records that within the earthen wall the buildings stood *alles met bamboe opgetrokken en gedekt door een dak van atap* — all raised in bamboo and covered with a roof of *atap* — the interior being otherwise extremely primitive. The forward post [[Biloel]] was of this construction.

The material's flammability made it a target. Beuming records that when the enemy began to bring the blockhouse [[Blang Tjoet]] under fire in early [[1896]], on one occasion he *brandend rijststroo op het atap-dak slingerde* — flung burning rice straw onto the *atap* roof — and that this was among the incidents that decided the headquarters at [[Koeta Radja]] to order the clearing of the surrounding terrain, the operation that became the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]].

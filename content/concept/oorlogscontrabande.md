***Oorlogscontrabande*** — contraband of war — was the term used in Dutch administration for the arms, powder and munitions whose importation into [[Acheh|Atjeh]] the Dutch sought to prevent by the blockade of the coast, the *[[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]* and the *[[Recherchevaartuigen|recherchevaartuigen]]*.

## The problem of enforcement

The [[Voorloopig Verslag on the Indies budget for 1898|Voorloopig Verslag]] recorded that precisely the rivers and creeks of the Acehnese coast frequently gave small *prauwen* the opportunity to bring in contraband, and that Dutch warships could not adequately guard against it. [[Van Vlijmen]] cited the case recorded in the [[Koloniaal Verslag]] of the bringing in of *oorlogscontrabande* at [[Simpang Anas]], and used it to argue for ten *recherchevaartuigen* rather than the three requested.

[[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] made the same point from the confessional benches: the search vessels "moeten dienen om te beletten dat er niet voortdurend ammunitie in Atjeh worde ingevoerd," — must serve to prevent ammunition being continually imported into Atjeh, and it was mistaken thrift to be niggardly with them.

## The scale of the traffic

[[H. van Kol]] described the trade from the other end. [[Penang|Pinang]], he said, had of old been the market for the Acehnese export articles of pepper, *pinang* and forest products, "uit Pinang werd rijst, petroleum, opium, kruit en geweren aangevoerd." — from Pinang were brought rice, petroleum, opium, powder and rifles. The profits of that traffic went in great part to the Pinang traders and smugglers.

He quoted the *[[Indische Gids]]* to show that interdiction had failed: "De verdere winsten waren voor de smokkelaars, ondanks het uitvoerverbod der Britsche autoriteiten, en de verbodsbepalingen op den invoer, en de blokkaden der Nederlandsche Regeering; ondanks de sluiting der kusten en scheepvaartregeling. De geschiedenis zal later verrassende feiten aan het licht brengen." — The further profits were for the smugglers, despite the export prohibition of the British authorities, and the prohibitory provisions on import, and the blockades of the Netherlands Government; despite the closing of the coasts and the shipping regulation.

## Significance

The traffic in *oorlogscontrabande* was the material basis of Acehnese resistance after the loss of the coastal ports, and its interdiction the principal object of the Dutch coastal system defended by [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]]. The [[Verklaringen van de kustvorsten van Atjeh|declarations of the coastal princes]], the *scheepvaartregeling* and the *recherchevaartuigen* formed together the legal, administrative and naval instruments by which the Dutch sought to close the coast; the persistence of the smuggling from British [[Penang]], despite the export prohibition of the British authorities, was the standing proof of their imperfection.

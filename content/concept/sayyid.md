**Sayyid** (Acehnese *sayét* or *sayéd*; also *Habib* for those of the Hadrami Sayyid families) was the honorific title given in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] — as throughout the Islamic world — to descendants of the Prophet Mohammed. Their status commanded automatic reverence based on religious feeling, even where their personal conduct was far from devotional.

## Status in Acehnese Society

Not by any means all of the sayyids of [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] were theologians or pandits. Most devoted themselves to trade or in later generations to agriculture, but enjoyed nonetheless the customary reverence based on religious feeling. The same was often "more than ordinarily pronounced, as they are excessively spoilt from their earliest youth," as Snouck Hurgronje observed.

The title *teungku* was also applied to the sayyid, along with the *leubë*, *haji*, *malém*, *além*, and *ulama* — making sayyids part of the broad [[Teungku|teungku]] class of religious leaders.

## Sayyids from Arabia

Some Sayyids from [[Mecca]] and from Arabia (including Hadramaut) found their way to [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] in the guise of random adventurers, quickly making their own of Acehnese manners and — unfortunately — of Acehnese vices. These born-in-Acheh sayyids often had the vices "usually more than ordinarily pronounced."

Some members of Sayyid families settled in [[Pidie]] and made occasional journeys to [[Keumala]], where the young sultan [[Muhammad Dawot Shah]] treated them with the highest honour.

## Sacred Tombs (Kramat)

The most honoured tombs (*kramat*) in Acheh were those of Sayyids. The most venerated at the time of Snouck Hurgronje's writing was the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong|Sayyid Abu Bakar bin Husain Bil-Faqih]] in [[Gampong Jawa]] — the [[Teungku Anjong]] kramat. Another famous Sayyid tomb was that of a member of the ʿAidarus family buried at Luar Batang in [[Batavia]], widely venerated.

## The ʿAidarus Family

Among the notable sayyid families in Acheh was the *ʿAidarus* (*ʿAydarus*) family — a famous Hadrami Sayyid lineage. A half-crazy young man, son of Teungku di Bukét (the late raja of [[Idi]]), was the *habib kramat* (living saint) of this family at the time of Snouck Hurgronje's writing.

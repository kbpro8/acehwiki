**Bila** (from Arabic *diya*, blood money) was the Acehnese customary payment exacted for killing or wounding — a central institution of [[Adat|Acehnese customary law]]. The concept of blood money was not foreign to native customary law in other parts of the Eastern Archipelago; in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] it appears to have been introduced through the medium of Islam, though not without modifications.

## Amount

The ordinary *bila* for causing death in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] was **500 Spanish (Mexican) dollars**. For wounding the amount was proportionally less.

## Islamic Law vs. Acehnese Practice

The [[Hikum|Mohammedan law]] gives the right of retributive vengeance for wilful murder only to the heir of the victim, or in cases of wounding to the wounded man himself. The execution of vengeance was made subject to the supervision of the public authorities. Yet in Acheh it remained an established rule that blood feuds were decided without any interference by the territorial authorities, simply under the direction of the *panglima [[Kawom|kawom]]* (tribal chief). Exceptions occurred only through the authority of an unusually energetic [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] or unusually influential [[Teungku|ulama]].

## Collection of Blood Money

The highland blood-debtor, to whom a period of one or two years was granted for paying off the *diët*, would go on a journey "to collect subscriptions" (*chö' ripë*) from all his well-to-do fellow tribesmen, sometimes coming down into the lowlands to apply for contributions from those of whose tribal connection the recollection was merely dim.

## Account from *Atjèh* by J. Kreemer (Deel II)

[[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] provides the following account of *bila* (blood revenge) in Atjèh.

**Bila** (Gayo: *bèla*, Alas: *bilo*) was the Atjèhnese practice of blood revenge — the right to demand the "blood-debt sacrifice" (*bila göb*). *Toentöt bila* meant the right to demand the blood-debt sacrifice. Originally exercised by all members of the wronged person's *[[kawöm|kawöm]]* (clan), *bila* was the violent form of revenge, distinguished from *[[diët|diët]]* (blood money), which was the non-bloody composition.

**Three cases for blood revenge**: in Atjèh, *bila* was especially applicable in three cases: (1) manslaughter (*pöh*) or wounding (*ta'*); (2) adultery (*dina* / *moemoekah* / *meureusam*) — for which the *bila* required killing BOTH guilty parties (*pöh doea kadoeo*); and (3) theft — catching a thief in flagrante delicto (*pöh pantjoeri*).

**The beungköng ordeal**: a traditional ordeal for a death-guilty offender who escaped blood revenge involved the *beungköng* (a particular draped garment), an iron cuirass, and a *reuntjöng sigeupöh*. The offender was placed in the middle of a square with his blood relatives on one side and those of the killed woman and her husband on the other three; he had to hack at a boundary post, after which the offended party could fell him — unless he escaped to his relatives' side. This was known only by tradition in Kreemer's time.

**Sië keubeuë and si geupöh**: a thief caught in flagrante (*pöh pantjoeri*) could be killed, but the killer had to prove the deceased was really a thief through the *peusah pantjoeri* procedure (verification by the Oelèëbalang). Once confirmed, the Oelèëbalang declared the thief *sië keubeuë* ("buffalo meat," i.e. *vogelfrei* / outlawed). The equivalent term *si geupöh* was applied to one who had incurred blood-debt and fled (murderer, adulterer, or thief).

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) — the *bangoen* episode at Gighen and Samalanga

[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 tract]] records the *bangoen* — in Van Heutsz's spelling, glossed by him as "*bloedprijs*" (blood-price) — as the [[Adat|adat]] institution central to the disputes at [[Gighen]] and [[Samalanga]] in [[1880]], in which the Dutch emissaries Said Abdellah and Soetan Maharadja were murdered and the *bangoen* owed for the killings was repeatedly delayed. The episode was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the test-case for [[Scherer]]'s *kruisboot-bangoen* method — the operational prototype of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] by which a Dutch cruiser was anchored before the harbour of a recalcitrant *landschap* to enforce, by the levy of import and export duties, the payment of the *bangoen* owed for the murder of a Dutch emissary.

Van Heutsz records the episode:

> "door het gebeurde te Gighen en te Samalanga, waar onze zendelingen Said Abdellah en Soetan Maharadja vermoord waren, en waar men telkens uitvluchten zocht in het betalen van den opgelegden bangoen (bloedprijs)".

— "by what happened at Gighen and at Samalanga, where our emissaries Said Abdellah and Soetan Maharadja were murdered, and where one repeatedly sought excuses in the paying of the imposed *bangoen* (blood-price)." Said Abdellah — the Dutch emissary murdered at [[Gighen]] on the north coast — and Soetan Maharadja — the Dutch emissary murdered at [[Samalanga]] on the north coast — were the "*zendelingen*" (literally "emissaries"; here: civil emissaries, not Christian missionaries) whose killings triggered the test-case for Scherer's *kruisboot-bangoen* method. The *bangoen* owed for these killings was, in Scherer's diagnosis, the operational instrument by which the Dutch could enforce the *beëedigde 18 artikelen* — the political pact by which the coastal *hoeloebalang* of the *Onderhoorigheden* had acknowledged Dutch sovereignty in the early [[1870s]].

### Scherer's *kruisboot* deployment

Scherer's solution was the *kruisboot* — the cruiser — anchored before the harbour of the recalcitrant *negeri* to enforce the *bangoen* payment by the levy of import and export duties. Van Heutsz records the operational mechanism:

> "Hij verzocht en verkreeg toen van den Generaal van der Heijden vergunning om vóór die havens een kruisboot te leggen en den bangoen te innen door rechten te heffen op de in- en uit te voeren goederen. Dit hielp; te Gighen betaalde men nog vóór dat de kruisboot er lag en te Samalanga, nadat de kruisboot er slechts drie dagen gelegen had."

— "He requested and obtained from [[K. van der Heyden|Generaal van der Heijden]] permission to lay a cruiser before those harbours and to collect the *bangoen* by levying duties on the imported and exported goods. This helped; at Gighen one paid even before the cruiser lay there, and at Samalanga, after the cruiser had lain there only three days." The *kruisboot-bangoen* method thus operated by economic coercion: the cruiser's blockade of the harbour cut the *negeri* off from its seaborne trade, and the *bangoen* was paid — at Gighen before the cruiser even arrived (the threat sufficing), at Samalanga within three days of the cruiser's arrival — to lift the blockade and restore the *negeri*'s access to the sea.

### The *bangoen* as prototype of the *scheepvaartregeling*

The episode was, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the operational prototype of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] — the systematic *contrôle* of in- and out-flow of goods through Dutch-occupied harbours that would be formalised by [[Laging Tobias]] in May [[1883]] and that became the operational architecture of Dutch indirect rule in the *Onderhoorigheden* after the [[1884]] [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]]. Van Heutsz records Scherer's diagnostic move:

> "Dat was voor Scherer eene vingerwijzing en bracht hem op het denkbeeld, dat als slechts alle in- en uitvoer langs onze vestigingen plaats had onder onze contröle, wij ook het middel in de hand hadden om de hoofden van de verschillende staatjes in het rechte spoor te houden."

— "That was for Scherer a finger-showing and brought him to the idea, that if only all in- and out-flow took place through our establishments under our control, we also had the means in hand to keep the chiefs of the various statelets on the right track." The *bangoen* was, in this sense, the *adat* institution whose exploitation by Scherer produced the operational template of the entire post-[[1884]] *contrôle* architecture: the *kruisboot* enforcing the *bangoen* payment was the prototype of the *scheepvaartregeling* enforcing the broader fiscal-and-political subordination of the [[Onderhoorigheden]].

### Significance of the *bangoen* episode

The *bangoen*'s significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record rests, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, on four things: it was the *adat* institution central to the disputes at [[Gighen]] (Said Abdellah) and [[Samalanga]] (Soetan Maharadja) in [[1880]] — the test-case for Scherer's *kruisboot-bangoen* method; it was the institution whose exploitation by Scherer produced the operational prototype of the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]]; and it was the institution whose exploitation produced, in Van Heutsz's diagnosis, the operational architecture of Dutch indirect rule in the *Onderhoorigheden* after the [[1884]] [[Concentratiestelsel|Concentratie]]. The *bangoen* is, in this sense, the *adat* institution on which the operational architecture of Dutch indirect rule in Atjeh turned — the customary blood-price whose exploitation by Scherer produced the *kruisboot*, the *scheepvaartregeling*, and the *contrôle* of the entire *Onderhoorigheden*.

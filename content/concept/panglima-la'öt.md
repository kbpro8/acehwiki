**Panglima la'öt** was the head of the guild of sea-fishery masters (*pawang*'s) in [[Aceh|Atjèh]], elected by guild members with approval of the [[Oelèëbalang|Oelèëbalang]]. He settled disputes between *pawang*'s over fishing grounds. His territory was a *lhö'* (bay).

## Election and authority

The *panglima la'öt* was elected by the guild members (the *pawang*'s) with the approval of the local [[Oelèëbalang|Oelèëbalang]]. His authority extended over a *lhö'* — a bay or stretch of coast that constituted the guild's territory. The *lhö*'s were separated by boundary markers, but the fishing rights within a *lhö'* were not exclusive — rather, the *lhö'* was the guild territory for the joint *kandoeri la'öt* ceremony.

## Dispute settlement

The *panglima la'öt* settled disputes between *pawang*'s over fishing grounds. The *pawang* was the master fisherman — owner of the *poekat* net and prauw, who organised fishery work and possessed *èleumèë* (esoteric knowledge of formulas and incantations used to control sea and forest spirits).

## The kandoeri la'öt

The **kandoeri la'öt** (or *kandoeri lhö'*) was a joint ceremonial meal held at the beach at the start of the *poekat* season, where a buffalo was slaughtered to invoke Allah's blessing. Paid for by the *pawang*'s, with *moegè* (fish-buyers) contributing. For 7 days after the *kandoeri la'öt*, fishing in that area was *pantang* (taboo).

## The sarakata and the sea-strip

The *panglima la'öt*'s authority over the *lhö'* was historically grounded in old *sarakata*'s (royal charters) from the Sultan, which defined the extent of a chief's territory "as far as one can fish with seine nets" — indicating the Sultan's historic sovereignty over the sea-strip adjacent to coastal lands.

**Military Intelligence in the Aceh War** was the organized system of reconnaissance, espionage, interrogations, and topographical mapping conducted by the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] during military operations against the [[Aceh Sultanate]] between [[1873]] and [[1876]]. The effectiveness of this service varied dramatically between campaigns and remained a central subject of military historiography.

## Structural Deficiencies in the First Expedition

During the [[First Aceh Expedition]] of spring [[1873]], as General [[G. G. Booms]] documented in his [[1875]] analysis, military intelligence (*military intelligence service (*kondschapsdienst*)*) suffered from severe structural defects. Because the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] lacked a permanent [[General Staff of the KNIL]], temporary staff officers appointed at the eleventh hour lacked specialized training in managing scouts, spies, and interrogating prisoners.

Furthermore, proposals made in [[1871]] by General [[T. H. Kroesen]] to conduct preliminary naval reconnaissance aboard the steamship *Djambi* had been rejected by the [[Dutch East Indies Government]]. As a result, when troops landed at [[Meuraxa]] on [[8 April]] [[1873]], they possessed almost no reliable data regarding the interior topography, and the royal palace at [[Kraton of Aceh]]—a city of approximately 6,000 inhabitants—could not even be located during the campaign.

## Evolution During the Second Expedition and Pel's Campaigns

Following the failure of the first invasion, the government established the [[Bureau for War Equipments on Sumatra]] in June [[1873]] to centralize topographical data and coordinate intelligence. During the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], Commander-in-Chief General [[J. van Swieten]] utilized reports from a spy who had spent three weeks inside [[Kraton of Aceh]] as well as invaluable intelligence provided by indigenous allies, particularly [[Teuku Nek of Meuraxa]].

In [[1875]] and [[1876]], General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] further systematized intelligence gathering during [[General Pel's Campaign in Aceh]], employing scouts to map paths through the [[IV Moekims]], [[VI Moekims]], and [[XXII Moekims]].

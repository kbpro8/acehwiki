**The Bustanu's-Salatin** — Arabic *Bustān as-Salāṭīn*, Malay *Boestan oes-Salāṭīn*, "Garden of Kings" (Dutch *Lusthof der Vorsten*) — is a Malay-language Islamic compendium written in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1638]] by [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] at the command of Sultan Iskandar II, and the second of the two principal Atjehnese-composed "mirrors for princes" treated by [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] in [[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|his survey of Malay literature]]. The work is, in Hooykaas's reading, "meer het werk van een geleerde" (more the work of a scholar) than the earlier *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj]]* of [[1607]] — a learned and prolix compilation whose historical chapter on Atjeh is, however, often more reliable than the *Sadjarah Malajoe* itself.

## Author and Date

[[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] — a Gujarati-born Muslim scholar of Hadrami descent — wrote the *Bustanu's-Salatin* on the order of Sultan Iskandar II of Atjeh in [[1638]], in the same year in which he also composed the *[[Sirat al-mustaqim|Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm]]* (March [[1638]]) and the *Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah*. The composition thus falls in the first years of Iskandar II's reign — the brief interregnum between the death of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] in [[1636]] and the religious controversy that would later drive ar-Rānīrī from Atjeh — and forms part of ar-Rānīrī's project of providing the Achehnese faithful with the literary apparatus of a Shāfiʿite sultanate.

## Contents

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] gives, in his chapter 19, a detailed chapter-by-chapter synopsis of the *Bustanu's-Salatin*:

- **Book I.** Schepping van hemel en aarde (Creation of heaven and earth) — including *nōer Moehammad* (the light of the Prophet), *lauhi mahfōëz* (the well-preserved tablet), *qalam* (the pen), *carsh* (the throne of God), *qoersi* (the footstool of God), the seven heavens, the angels, the djinn, and Iblis.
- **Book II.** Profeten en koningen (Prophets and kings) — a history of the prophets from Adam to Muhammad, the Persian kings, the Byzantine emperors, the Egyptian rulers, the Arab rulers before Islam, the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphs, the Muslim rulers of Delhi, and — significantly for AcehWiki — the sultans of Malacca and Pahang, and **the sultans of Atjéh and the godsdienstleraars daar te lande, voorgangers en tijdgenoten uit 16de en 17de ceuw** (the sultans of Atjeh and the religious scholars there, leaders and contemporaries of the 16th and 17th century).
- **Book III.** Rechtvaardige koningen en bekwame staatsdienaren (Righteous kings and capable state officials) — in six paragraphs.
- **Book IV.** Godvruchtige koningen en heilige personen (Pious kings and holy persons) — including the famous anecdote of the conversion of Ibrahim ibn Adham.
- **Book V.** Onrechtvaardige koningen en onverstandige staatsdienaren (Unrighteous kings and foolish state officials).
- **Book VI.** Milddadige en edele mensen en helden (Generous and noble people and heroes) — including a long account of the Holy War and the 18 campaigns of the Prophet, the Battle of Badr, the Battle of Uhud, and the siege of the trench.
- **Book VII.** Het verstand (*aql*) en alle soorten van wetenschap (*ilm*) — on intellect and the sciences, on virtue and vice, on physiognomy, on medicine, on the benefits of marriage.

## The Atjehnese Historical Chapter (Book II.m)

The historical chapter of the *Bustanu's-Salatin* on Atjeh is, in [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s judgement, of considerable historical value. Hooykaas notes that "de geschiedkundige gegevens, steeds van een jaartal voorzien, een betrouwbare indruk maken, wel eens de voorkeur blijken te verdienen boven de voorstelling van de SM, en toch in de Boestän slechts een aanloopje vormen, een onderdeel hoogstens van het boek" (the historical data, always provided with a year-date, make a reliable impression, sometimes prove to deserve the preference over the representation of the *Sadjarah Malajoe*, and yet in the *Bustan* form only a preamble, a sub-component at most of the book). The *Bustanu's-Salatin* thus preserves, in passing, an Atjehnese royal chronology that is in places more reliable than the *Sadjarah Malajoe*'s — a fact that gives the work importance as a check on the *Sadjarah Malajoe*'s narrative.

## Comparison with the Tādj

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] contrasts the *Bustanu's-Salatin* with the earlier *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj]]* of [[1607]]: "terwijl BOCHARI's Tādj meer eenvoudig stichtelijk gehouden is, is daarentegen RANĪRI's Boestān meer het werk van een geleerde, die zich niet ontziet zijn lezers te vergasten op een hoofdstuk dat belangrijk moge zijn voor een theoloog (I) of een geschiedvorser (II), maar het lezend publiek wel eens zwaar gevallen zal zijn" (while Bochari's *Tādj* is more simply edifying, Rānīrī's *Bustān* is more the work of a scholar, who does not refrain from treating his readers to a chapter that may be important for a theologian (I) or a historical researcher (II), but may have been heavy going for the reading public). The two works thus represent two phases of the Atjehnese "mirror for princes" tradition — the earlier more edifying, the later more scholarly.

## Sources and Analogues

Like the *Tādj oes-Salatina*, the *Bustanu's-Salatin* is Persian-Muslim in content. [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Ar-Rānīrī]] "ontleent zijn stof van talrijke anecdoten aan dezelfde en soortgelijke Moslimse verzamelwerken in de Perzische taal uit Voor-Indië" (derives his material, of numerous anecdotes, from the same and similar Muslim compilations in the Persian language from India). "Meermalen komt dan ook dezelfde anecdote in beide [werken] voor" (The same anecdote often occurs in both works) — a fact that, in Hooykaas's reading, points to the common Persian-language source tradition on which both Atjehnese "mirrors for princes" drew.

## Editions

The *Bustanu's-Salatin* circulated widely in manuscript. [[H. N. van der Tuuk|Van der Tuuk]] — the Dutch Malay scholar whom Hooykaas characterises as "een enthousiast orientalist en zonderling man" (an enthusiastic orientalist and singular man) — gromde over the *Boestān*: "Dit werk geeft meer dan het belooft, wat in de Maleische letterkunde een groot wonder mag genoemd worden" (This work gives more than it promises, which in Malay literature may be called a great miracle). Extracts appear in *H. N. van der Tuuk, Maleisch Leesboek* (verhaal 6 en 7, pp. 37-41); in *NIEMANN, Bloemlezing II*, pp. 225-233; and in *WINSTEDT & BLAGDEN, A Malay Reader*, pp. 102-103. The contents-opgave of the *Bustanu's-Salatin* that Hooykaas reproduces in his survey was, he notes, supplied by Dr. P. Voorhoeve.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the *Bustanu's-Salatin* is significant as the second of the two principal Atjehnese-composed "mirrors for princes" (the earlier being the *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj]]* of [[1607]]). Its composition in [[1638]] — at the command of Sultan Iskandar II, in the same year as the *Sirat al-mustaqim* and the *Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah* — places it at the heart of [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|ar-Rānīrī]]'s project of orthodox reform in Atjeh. Its historical chapter (II.m) on the sultans of Atjeh and the religious scholars of the 16th and 17th centuries is — in Hooykaas's judgement — sometimes more reliable than the *Sadjarah Malajoe*, and is thus a key check on the Malay chronicle tradition.

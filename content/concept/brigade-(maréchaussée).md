The **brigade** was the basic tactical and social unit of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* in [[Atjeh]] — the small, self-contained patrol group in which the corps habitually operated, and around which its distinctive discipline, comradeship and command structure were built.

## Place in the establishment

By the *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[3 March]] [[1899]] the corps was brought to a strength of 20 officers and 1,212 men, divided over five *[[Divisie (Maréchaussée)|divisies]]* of three *afdeelingen*, each *afdeeling* being subdivided into four brigades.

## Internal structure in Mosselman's account

[[W. J. Mosselman]], writing in the [[1930]] jubilee number, set out the corps's internal hierarchy in the vocabulary it had borrowed from the Acehnese enemy. One *Toeangkoe* or *divisiecommandant* had under him three *[[Oeleebalang|Oeleuhbalangs]]* (officers); each *Oeleuhbalang* had four *[[Maréchaussée-vocabulaire|Toekoes]]* (European non-commissioned officers); and each *Toekoe* disposed of one *[[Panglima|Panglima]]* (indigenous sergeant), one *[[Keuchi|Keutjih]]* (corporal) and seventeen *[[Kawan (Maréchaussée)|Kawans]]* (maréchaussées). See *[[Maréchaussée-vocabulaire]]*.

## Tactical doctrine

The corps was, Mosselman wrote, "bestemd om den tegenstander met zijn eigen tactiek te bestrijden, getraind voor den onverbiddelijken guerilla-krijg met uiterst kleine, maar hechte troepjes, die slechts, indien noodzakelijk, tot grooter benden werden vereenigd" — destined to fight the adversary with his own tactics, trained for the implacable guerrilla war with extremely small but tightly-knit little troops, which were united into larger bands only when necessary. Their model, he added, was "de dappere en in dien krijg volleerde vijand" — the brave enemy, fully accomplished in that kind of war.

## Authority within the brigade

Within his group, brigade, *afdeeling* or division, every leader possessed "een soort absoluut gezag" — a kind of absolute authority — which had arisen and grown naturally through the purposeful and tactful attitude of the organisers and the conduct of the officers, who would never pass over the relevant subordinate leaders and seniors for any order or command. Interference with, or the giving of orders to, men or cadre not belonging to one's own band was avoided wherever possible. Among the *kawans* themselves, respect for the man senior in years of service predominated. It was this, Mosselman held, that produced "die hechte band, die eensgezindheid, die subtiele Korpsgeest van hoog tot laag" — the close bond, the unanimity, the subtle corps spirit from top to bottom — which was the secret of the great deeds performed by the corps.

## Brigades in the field

Brigades were the units in which the corps was counted on operations. The [[Tocht naar de Gajo-Loeös (1904)|expedition to the Gajo-Loeös]] of [[1904]] consisted initially of ten brigades of Maréchaussée and was gradually enlarged to fourteen. Three brigades were sent in [[1905]] to the [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo]] to render the *[[Pretendent-sultan|pretendent-sultan]]* harmless. In the [[Gevecht waarin kapitein Paris sneuvelde (Westkust van Atjeh, 1925-1927)|Westkust engagement]] of [[1925]]–[[1927]] a single brigade lost its captain, its brigade commander, five other men killed and twelve wounded, only two remaining unhurt.

Mosselman recalled with affection "ons familieleven in brigade doea-blas van de 1e en in brigade doea-blas van de 5e Divisie" — the family life of the twelfth brigade of the 1st and of the 5th Division.

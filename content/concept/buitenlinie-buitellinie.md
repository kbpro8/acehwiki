*outer line* — also *buitellinie*, Dutch for "outer line" — was the outer defensive perimeter of [[Dutch]] outposts surrounding the [[geconcentreerde linie]] at [[Koeta Radja]] in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The buitenlinie comprised the small exposed posts at [[Biloel]], [[Lamkoenjit]], [[Ana' Galong|Anak Galoeng]], [[Lambarih|Lam Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam Soet]], [[Senelop]], [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] (among others), each held by a small garrison in a blockhouse or earthen fortification. After [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection of [[29 March]] [[1896]] turned Dutch-supplied weapons against the buitenposten, the buitenlinie was progressively lifted — the posts at [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] were evacuated on [[12 April]] [[1896]], and those at [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]] — leaving the geconcentreerde linie as the Dutch defensive perimeter.

## Etymology and Meaning

The word *buitenlinie* is a Dutch compound: outside, outer + line, from French *ligne*, Latin *linea*. The variant *buitellinie* is a Dutch phonological alternation with the same meaning. The buitenlinie should be understood as the outer of two concentric defensive lines around Koeta Radja: the inner *geconcentreerde linie* enclosed the colonial capital and its principal installations, while the outer *buitenlinie* projected Dutch presence into the surrounding Achehnese moekims. The outposts were the individual outposts composing the line, each typically a blockhouse or earthen fortification with a small garrison of infantry, often reinforced by a machine gun and a 7 cm L.A. mountain gun.

## Historical Context

The buitenlinie was the institutional expression of the Dutch "alliance" policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]], which sought to project Dutch power into the Achehnese moekims through a chain of small posts, supported by cooperative [[hoeloebalang]]s and the [[Panglima Prang Besar]] [[Teuku Umar]]. After Umar's [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of 29 March 1896]], the buitenposten became untenable: each was exposed to attack by Umar's Dutch-armed legion and by the surrounding Achehnese population. The [[Siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe (28–29 March 1896)|siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe]] of [[28 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] preceded the formal defection; the [[Siege of Lamkoenjit (29 March–8 April 1896)|siege of Lamkoenjit]] of [[29 March]]–[[8 April]] [[1896]] followed it. The [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] — in which a Dutch column under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was driven back with the loss of a *mitrailleur* and a 7 cm L.A. *kanon* — confirmed that the buitenlinie could not be held. The progressive evacuation of [[12 April]]–[[20 April]] [[1896]] brought the outer line back into the geconcentreerde linie.

## Examples in the Source

Kruisheer enumerates the buitenposten individually: [[Biloel]] (in the [[IX Moekims]]), [[Lamkoenjit]] (in the IX Moekims, with a large *pasar*), [[Ana' Galong|Anak Galoeng]] (in the [[XXV Moekims|sagi XXV]]), [[Lambarih|Lam Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam Soet]] (with a *mitrailleur* on field-carriage and another on breastwork-carriage), [[Senelop]] (in the [[XXV Moekims|sagi XXV]]), [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]] (in the [[XXII Moekims]]). The [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[12 April]] [[1896]] and the [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb]] of [[20 April]] [[1896]] are recorded as discrete events. The [[21 April]] [[1896]] three-column operation to free the besieged posts of [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Kroeng Gloempang]] and to evacuate the post of [[Toengkoeb]] is one of the major operations of the period. The cost of holding the buitenlinie is measured in the [[Siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe (28–29 March 1896)|siege of Aloer Oe]] (where *Sgt. son* repaired the *mitrailleur* twice under fire), the [[Siege of Lamkoenjit (29 March–8 April 1896)|siege of Lamkoenjit]] (which used *kartets* at under 50 m), and the disastrous [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]].

## Significance

The buitenlinie is the institutional expression of the Dutch "forward" policy in [[Aceh]] and the principal casualty of [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection. Its progressive evacuation in April [[1896]] marks the Dutch government's admission that the "alliance" policy of arming cooperative hoeloebalang had failed, and the [[Besluit of 9 May 1896]] forbidding further weapon distribution was the formal confirmation. The buitenlinie's collapse forced the Dutch back onto the geconcentreerde linie and inaugurated the more coercive punitive expedition and show of force tactics of May–June [[1896]]. The outer line's institutional memory would survive in the subsequent Dutch policy of *pacificatie* under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], which would replace the static buitenpost with the mobile column.

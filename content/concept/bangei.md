**Bangei** (also *bangei Atjeh*, *bangei Aceh*; Dutch: *bamboe-neusfluit* — "bamboo nose flute") is a **bamboo nose flute** — an aerophone played by blowing through the nose into a small hole near the closed end of a bamboo tube — indigenous to **[[Acheh]]** (Atjēh), northern Sumatra. It is one of the few **nose flutes** documented in the western Indonesian archipelago, a trait more commonly associated with **Polynesian**, **Melanesian**, and **Philippine** (e.g., *kalaleng*, *tongali*) traditions.

## Organology

- **Hornbostel-Sachs classification**: 421.111.12 (end-blown nose flute, open tube, without fingerholes — or with fingerholes: 421.111.22)
- **Body**: single internode of thin-walled bamboo (*bambu apus* / *Gigantochloa apus*, *bambu wuluh* / *Schizostachyum blumei*), closed at the proximal end (node intact), open at the distal end
- **Blowing hole**: small circular aperture (3–5 mm) drilled near the closed end, angled for nasal airflow
- **Fingerholes**: typically **3–4 fingerholes** (some variants have none, producing only harmonics)
- **Length**: 30–50 cm
- **Diameter**: 1.5–2.5 cm (internal)
- **Playing technique**: player closes one nostril, places blowing hole against the other nostril, and exhales gently through the nose; fingerholes stopped by fingers of both hands
- **Sound**: soft, breathy, flute-like, limited dynamic range; fundamental pitch with harmonic overtones
- **Range**: approximately one octave (with fingerholes); pentatonic or hexatonic scale

## Historical and Regional Context

**[[Jaap Kunst]]** in *[[Muziek en Dans in de Buitengewesten]]* ([[1946]], p. 10) lists the *bangei* among the **"typisch Mohammedaansche speeltuigen"** ("typically Mohammedan instruments") **for Atjēh**, alongside the *[[Gambus (Aceh)|gambus]]*, *[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]*, *[[Marwas]]*, and *[[Serunai (Aceh)|serunai]]*. This classification is **organologically surprising** — the nose flute is not a typical Islamic instrument (which favors reed pipes, lutes, frame drums). Its inclusion suggests:

1. **Syncretic adoption**: The *bangei* may have been a **pre-Islamic indigenous Acehnese instrument** that was incorporated into the Islamic soundscape (e.g., for *zikir* meditation, *maulid* lullabies, or *hikayat* interludes).
2. **Misclassification**: Kunst may have grouped it with "Mohammedan" instruments simply because he encountered it in Islamic ritual contexts in Aceh.
3. **Hadrami/Yemeni parallel**: The **Yemeni *qasaba* / *nay*** (end-blown flute) is used in *sama'* (Sufi audition); the *bangei* could be a local bamboo adaptation of that aesthetic.

The *bangei* is **rarely documented** outside Kunst's 1946 monograph. It does not appear in major organological surveys of Sumatra (e.g., *Kartomi*, *Kunst 1973*, *Kartomi 2012*), suggesting it may have been **highly localized** (perhaps to **Pidie**, **Aceh Besar**, or **Simeulue**) or **already declining** by the 1930s.

The *bangei* is the **only documented nose flute in western Indonesia** (Sumatra, Java, Borneo, Malay Peninsula). Its presence in Aceh may reflect **ancient Austronesian connections** with the Philippines and the Pacific, or a **local innovation** in bamboo organology.

## Role in Acehnese Music

Given its **soft, intimate timbre**, the *bangei* was likely used in:

- **[[Meuseukat]]** (women's seated dance-song) — gentle melodic interludes between vocal sections
- **[[Zikir]]** / **[[Dzikir]]** (Sufi meditation) — quiet *dhikr* accompaniment, breath-focused playing as spiritual practice
- **[[Hikayat]]** / **[[Dendang]]** recitation — instrumental preludes (*buka*) or interludes
- **[[Maulid]]** / **[[Maulud]]** lullabies — cradle songs for the Prophet's birthday
- **Solo recreational playing** — herding, courtship (parallel to Philippine traditions)

It is **unsuitable** for outdoor processions (*[[Seudati]]*, *[[Sedati (dance)|Sedati]]*, *[[Saman]]*) where loud instruments (*[[Serunai (Aceh)|serunai]]*, *[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]*, *[[Marwas]]*) dominate.

## Construction (Traditional Acehnese)

| Step | Process |
|------|---------|
| 1. Selection | Choose mature *bambu apus* or *bambu wuluh*, straight internode, thin walls |
| 2. Cutting | Cut at nodes; keep proximal node intact (closed end), open distal end |
| 3. Seasoning | Dry slowly in shade 2–4 weeks; smoke over kitchen fire (darkens, hardens) |
| 4. Blowing hole | Drill/burn 3–5 mm hole 1–2 cm from closed node, angled upward |
| 5. Fingerholes | Burn/drill 3–4 holes at calculated intervals for pentatonic scale |
| 6. Finishing | Rub with coconut oil; sometimes decorate with *sulur* (vine) motifs |

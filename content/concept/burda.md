**The Burda** (Arabic: البردة, *al‑Burda*) is a famous Arabic poem in praise of the Prophet Muhammad, composed by [[Al-Busiri]] in the thirteenth century. It has become one of the most widely recited and copied devotional texts in the Islamic world.

## Content and Form
The poem consists of ten stanzas (or more, depending on the version) that describe the Prophet’s virtues, miracles, and the poet’s personal experience of healing through devotion. Its language is rich in metaphor and rhetorical devices, making it a frequent subject of commentary.

## Malay Translation
The source file indicates the existence of a sixteenth‑century Malay translation of the Burda, demonstrating the poem’s early penetration into the Malay literary milieu. Such translations facilitated the spread of the work’s devotional content among Malay‑speaking Muslim communities, including those in [[Aceh]].

## Significance for Acehnese Culture
In Aceh, the Burda has been recited in religious gatherings, included in manuscript collections, and studied by Sufi orders. Its Malay version would have been accessible to scholars and laypersons alike, reinforcing shared Islamic ideals across the archipelago.

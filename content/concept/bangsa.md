**Bangsa** — Malay/Indonesian for "nation," "people," "ethnicity" or "landaard" — was the term by which the soldiers of the [[K.N.I.L.]] identified themselves and were identified by their ethnic-tribal origin: Ambonezen, Menadonezen, Timorezen, Javanen, Madoerezen, Soendanezen, etc. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *bangsa* is the central category of the colonial army's social organisation: on [[Java]] each *compagnie* consisted of one *bangsa* (except the European officers); in the *[[buitengewesten]]* all *[[landaard|landaarden]]* were mixed in one brigade. A slight to one's *bangsa* was deeply felt: "*Belediging voor de bangsa Ambon!*" was a war cry in protests.

## The bangsa and the compagnie

On [[Java]] each *compagnie* of the [[K.N.I.L.]] consisted of one *bangsa* — one ethnic group — except the European officers. The Ambonese *compagnie*, the Javanese *compagnie*, the Menadonese *compagnie* — each was ethnically homogeneous. The arrangement was both administrative and cultural: the *compagnie* was the unit of daily life, and the daily life of the *compagnie* was shaped by the *bangsa* of its soldaten.

## The bangsa in the buitengewesten

In the *[[buitengewesten]]* — the outer territories, including [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — all *[[landaard|landaarden]]* were mixed in one brigade. The *[[kongsi]]s* — the cooking groups on patrouille — were organised by *bangsa*: Ambonezen + Timorezen together; Menadonezen alone; Javanen + Soendanezen + Madoerezen together. The mixing of *bangsa* in the *buitengewesten* was both practical (the brigade was too small for ethnic *compagnieën*) and cultural (the *buitengewesten* were where the *bangsa* of the colonial army met and merged).

## The bangsa and the belediging

A slight to one's *bangsa* was deeply felt. "*Belediging voor de bangsa Ambon!*" was a war cry in protests. The [[Magelang menage protest (1930)|Magelang menage protest]] of [[1930]] was triggered by a communist-instigated menage change that substituted rice for aardappelen — the Ambonese soldiers read it as "*hetzelfde menu als de Javanen*" (the same menu as the Javanese) — and therefore as a slight to the *bangsa Ambon*. The protest — a march to the colonel's house — was the consequence.

## The bangsa and the soldatenlied

The *bangsa* was also the category of the soldatenlied. The Ambonese soldiers sang "*Rasa sajang kené*", "*Ombak, ombak poetih*", "*Hoera, hoera tjintjin*" — the Ambonese songs that resounded in the *[[tangsi]]*. The Javanese soldiers sang their own songs; the Menadonese theirs. The *bangsa* was therefore not just an administrative category but a cultural one: the *bangsa* was the category of the song.

## Significance

The *bangsa* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the central category of the colonial army's social organisation. The book's argument is that the *bangsa* was both administrative and cultural: the *compagnie* on Java was ethnically homogeneous, the *brigade* in the *buitengewesten* was ethnically mixed, and the *kongsies* on patrouille were organised by *bangsa*. The slight to the *bangsa* — as in the Magelang menage protest — was the mechanism by which the *bangsa*'s honour was defended, and the *bangsa* was the category through which the colonial army's Ambonese soldiers identified themselves.

**Meusigit** (from Arabic *masjid jāmiʿ*, via Malay *mesjid*) was the Acehnese term for a proper Friday mosque — the *jāmiʿ* or congregational mosque in Arabic terminology — which required a congregation of at least forty free adult male believers for the Friday midday service to be validly held. It was the building around which each [[Mukim|mukim]] was organised.

## Distinction from Meunasah

The meusigit was distinct from the [[Meunasah|meunasah]] (the village chapel used for daily prayers and community life). The Mohammedan law required every free male believer of full age to attend the Friday service if held within a certain distance of his abode. In places where the number of forty could never be reckoned on, no arrangements whatever were made for the Friday service — hence the gampong chapels in Acheh never had the requisite apparatus for this service.

The pious and influential among the Acehnese were therefore keen to multiply the opportunities for attending the Friday service and to further the erection of mosques for Friday prayer wherever a congregation of forty or more could be reckoned on.

## Construction

When a number of [[Gampong|gampongs]] lay sufficiently close to admit of their being united into a single Friday association, they constructed a meusigit, choosing for the purpose the most central possible site. This might fall within one of the gampongs united, or in a place lying without all the gampong enclosures.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Meuseugit

**Meuseugit** is the Acehnese term for the Friday mosque — the *masjid* of the Islamic world — that served as the liturgical centre of the [[Moekim|moekim]], the Friday-service circle of the Atjehsche administrative system, and whose presence, required by the Shafi'ite school for the canonical Friday prayer, gave institutional form to the intermediate level of Acehnese society between the [[Gampong|gampong]] and the [[Oelèëbalangschap|oelèëbalangschap]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the *meuseugit* as the architectural counterpart of the [[Moekim|moekim]]: its construction, by heads who sought the support of the clergy, was the act that called a *moekim* into being.

## The moekim and the meuseugit

The *meuseugit*, *De Buitenbezittingen* records, was built "op een centraalpunt" — at a central point — for some (originally as a rule four) *gampong*s lying close together, so that the faithful from each of the constituent *gampong*s could reach it within the distance prescribed by Islamic law for the Friday service. The same heads who built the *meuseugit* endowed it with the revenues necessary for its maintenance and for the support of its *imeum* — the religious chief of the *moekim* who, in time, became an *adat* chief: the *oelèëbalang tjoet* (small *oelèëbalang*). The *meuseugit* thus stood at the intersection of the religious and the territorial orders of Atjehsche society: its liturgical function was Islamic, but its institutional function was the constitution of a territorial-political unit, the *moekim*.

## The Meuseugit Raja at Koeta Radja

The most important *meuseugit* of the Gouvernement was the *Meuseugit Raja* — the Royal Mosque — at [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]], which gave its name to one of the *zelfstandige gampongs* of the *onderafdeeling* of the same name, alongside *Keudah*, *Djawa*, *Pasar Bard*, *Planggahan*, and *Blang Peureulaï* (the latter the proper *Soeltansgebied*). The *Meuseugit Raja* on the right bank of the [[Kr. Atjeh|Atjeh River]] was matched, on the left bank, by another *Meuseugit Raja*, with the independent *gampongs* *Meuratsa* and the self-standing *gampongs* *Peunajong* with Lam Poet, *Lam Dingèn*, *Beurawé*, and *Lam Bhoes* — the latter probably belonging to the *Soeltansgebied* or to the territory of the former *Panglima Meuseugit Raja* and *Teukoe Kali Malikën Adé*. The *feestgewaad* (festive vestment) prepared for the great *Meuseugit* of Koeta Radja is recorded in one of the plates of *De Buitenbezittingen*.

## The meunasah

The *meuseugit* is to be distinguished from the *meunasah* — the village prayer-house that served as the daily religious centre of the [[Gampong|gampong]] and as the night-quarters of the unmarried young men, the male visitors, and the husbands of women living in the gampong. The *meunasah* was headed by the [[Teungkoe|Teungkoe meunasah]], the "mother" of the gampong; the *meuseugit* was headed by the [[Imeum|imeum]], who, in time, became the *oelèëbalang tjoet* of the *moekim*. The two institutions represented the two scales of Acehnese religious life: the daily prayer of the *gampong*, and the weekly Friday assembly of the *moekim*.

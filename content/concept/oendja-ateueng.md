The **oendja ateueng** (Atjehnese *oendja*, post; *[[Ateueng|ateueng]]*, the little dike of a rice field) was the boundary post buried in the ground at the corners of a *[[Sawah|sawah]]* in the *landschap* (territory) [[Pidie|Pidië-proper]], *onderafdeeling* (subdivision) [[Sigli]], serving both as a foundation for works such as dams and as latent evidence of the boundary.

## Function

[[D. W. N. de Boer]] recorded that in Pidië-proper it was of old the custom to bury posts in the ground at the corners of *sawah*'s, these serving at the same time as a foundation for the construction of dams and similar works. Their purpose was that at any future time, in the event of a boundary dispute, convincing evidence could at once be produced: one had only to dig up the *oendja ateueng* to have a sound beginning of proof.

## In the law of proof

In [[Groot-Atjeh]], where a plaintiff alleging an earlier possessory right over wet rice fields had to point to visible traces of former cultivation, the *oendja* — together with a former watercourse (*[[Loeeng|loeëng]]*) and traces of *ateueng* (Malay *galangan*) — could serve as further proof.

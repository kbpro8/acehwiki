The **peutoeha pangkaj** was the capitalist contractor of the Atjehnese pepper economy — the *pangkaj*-giver who financed the laying out of a pepper garden and drew a perpetual share of its produce.

## Function

Under the *[[Contract of the Atjehnese pepper planter|pepper planter's contract]]*, an aspirant planter without means engaged himself with a *peutoeha pangkaj*, or with one of his overseers (*peutoeha djaga*, *[[Peutoeha parang|peutoeha parang]]*), as an *[[Aneu' seuneubô|aneu' seuneubô]]*, receiving from him through the overseer an advance in *padi*, clothing, tools and cash amounting to twenty to twenty-five dollars per thousand vines.

## Rights

Even after the advance had been repaid in full, the *peutoeha pangkaj* remained entitled, so long as the garden was productive, to the *[[Wathé pangkaj|wathé pangkaj]]* — normally a tenth of the harvest, the overseer's honorarium included. He had besides a right of pre-emption over the produce of gardens laid out with his capital, waived only when the planter received a higher bid elsewhere; hence payment was usually made in kind.

To guard against dilapidation he had the garden regularly inspected and valued, and could demand from a negligent planter the difference between two successive valuations. To spread his risk against bad pepper prices and failure of the planting he often stipulated that the planter grow an intercrop, or *ficus elastica* or *hevea* along the garden's edge, half the yield of which fell to him under *[[Meudoewa laba|meudoewa laba]]*.

## The oeleebalang as pangkaj-giver

Where the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]* himself acted as *pangkaj*-giver on the standard footing, he took two guilders per *picol* — one as *wathé pangkaj* and one as *[[Wathé oeléëbalang|wathé oeléëbalang]]* on export — which explained, in [[D. W. N. de Boer]]'s phrase, why "de Croesussen onder de zelfbestuurders" were so strongly in favour of pepper cultivation.

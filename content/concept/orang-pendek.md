The **orang pendek** ("short person"), known as the **koekoeman** in the region of the hunter [[Datoek]], is a legendary forest-dwelling humanoid of Acehnese folklore. In Merac's *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* ([[1947]]), the creature is the focus of the chapter *Jagerslatijn*, where Datoek recounts what he claims was a firsthand encounter. His story is contrasted with the scholarly account of the [[Manté's|manté's]] presented by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in *De Atjehers*.

## Datoek's account

Datoek insists that he personally encountered the koekoeman while travelling through the forest:

> "Certainly, Toean, I know the forest, believe me. I saw the koekoeman from behind, with these eyes, at half past noon, at a distance of only twenty metres. I could not have mistaken it for an orangutan or a bear. It was a human being."

He describes the creature as approximately one metre tall, with proportions unlike those of an ape. According to Datoek, it lacked the long arms of an orangutan and was much less heavily covered in hair. He also attributes to it the habit of stealing buffalo milk and rice left behind by collectors of forest products.

## The hunting dog and the banyan tree

Datoek continues by describing how his hunting dog reacted to the encounter:

> "This hunting dog"—he pointed to a rare smooth-haired dog—"wanted to attack it, but I held the dog back. Sensing danger, the creature disappeared among the aerial roots of a gigantic banyan tree."

The brief sighting ends there, with the koekoeman vanishing into the forest before Datoek could approach any closer.

## Snouck Hurgronje and the manté's

Following Datoek's story, the district court chairman remarks that such an account should not be dismissed outright. He cites [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s *De Atjehers*, where the manté's are described as mysterious inhabitants of the mountains of the [[XXII Moekims]].

By introducing Snouck Hurgronje's ethnographic account, the narrative places Datoek's eyewitness testimony within a broader colonial tradition of describing elusive "people of the interior." While the manté's were known only through hearsay, Datoek insists that he personally saw what he believed to be the same kind of forest being.

## Significance

The orang pendek—or koekoeman—serves as the central legendary forest being in *Oost-Indisch Journael*. Through Datoek's testimony, the creature is presented as a direct personal encounter rather than merely a traditional story.

The chapter juxtaposes two different forms of knowledge. Datoek offers the perspective of an experienced Acehnese hunter who claims firsthand observation, while Snouck Hurgronje represents the colonial ethnographic tradition, which records similar beings only through second-hand reports. Together, these accounts illustrate how the orang pendek and the manté's occupy overlapping places in Acehnese folklore and colonial scholarship, blurring the boundary between legend, oral tradition, and ethnographic observation.

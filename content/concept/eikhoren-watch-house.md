The **Eikhoren** was the name painted on the watch-house rising above the outwork of the Dutch post of [[Lam Koenjit]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], one of a set of jocular signboards by which [[G. B. Hooijer]] measured the morale of the garrison during the investment of [[1896]].

## The signboards of Lam Koenjit

Hooijer recorded that the ever-vigilant post commandant, [[Luitenant Creutz Lechleitner]], hoped to prevent the enemy from penetrating: above the outwork surrounded by *[[IJzerdraadversperring|ijzerdraadversperring]]*, within which the kitchen stood, rose a watch-house from which the enemy's approach could be barred, and *als opschrift droeg dat den naam van Eikhoren*.

Other signboards — *In de Villa Sans Souci* and *In den Soldatenhemel: alles vrij behalve muskieten* ("In the Soldiers' Heaven: everything free except mosquitoes") — proved sufficiently, in his judgment, that the Dutch soldiers did not yet find the situation hopeless and had kept their humour. The mosquitoes referred to bred in the pools around the post, which was so muddy that it was called *Lam Loempoer*, mud village.

## Comparable inscriptions elsewhere

At [[Kroeng Gloempang]] the entrance carried the notice *Welkom in de restauratie van volkspijs*, *[[Granaatkartets|volkspijs]]* being the soldier's name for a case-shot shell; inside, the passage between the barracks was labelled *[[Heerengracht (barracks passage at Kroeng Gloempang)|Heerengracht]]* and another inscription read *Japan 5 gulden een flesch portwijn*. At [[Anagaloeng]] the officers' salon beneath the Achehnese house bore the inscription *Monaco*.

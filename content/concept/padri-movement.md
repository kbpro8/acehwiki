**Padri movement** was a reformist Islamic current that emerged in the early nineteenth‑century on *Sumatra*, advocating a strict, puritanical interpretation of Islam and seeking to eliminate local customs considered un‑Islamic. The movement became the catalyst for the **Padri War (Sumatra)** and drew the attention of the Dutch colonial authorities.

## Ideology and Goals  
Influenced by *Wahhabi* ideas, the Padri leaders called for the abolition of *adat* (traditional customs), the end of *gambreng* (ritual feasting), and the enforcement of *sharia* law. Their agenda threatened the existing sociopolitical order, including the authority of local *sultans* and the Dutch colonial administration.

## Military Activity  
The Padri militias engaged in armed resistance against both indigenous rulers and Dutch forces. The Dutch response, documented in the *De Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pp. 102‑104), included the deployment of marine detachments such as those that participated in the **expedition against the Padri’s (1838)**.

## Regional Impact  
While centred in West *Sumatra*, the Padri movement’s unrest reverberated northward, influencing the strategic calculations of the [[Aceh]] Sultanate, which perceived the Dutch preoccupation with the Padri as an opportunity to assert its own autonomy.

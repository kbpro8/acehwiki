**The Kalang** — also **Kalang's**, in Javanese *orang Kalang* — are the people of Java's north coast whom the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten]] and the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] record as Javanese but not "real" Javanese, and whom [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies as [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] of *lang-ka*, i.e. as Hindu-Javanese of the Sumatran Overwal brought to Java as war-booty from the ancestral land of [[Taroema]]. They are the principal ethnonym by which De Roo de la Faille ties the Javanese chronicle tradition to the [[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere.

## The *Orang Hoed* of Tjilatjap

When the Assistent-Resident De Wolff van Westerrode at [[Tjilatjap|Tjilatjap]] wished to know more of the so-called ***Orang Hoed*** of the local Kalang's — who lived isolated around a cemetery, and were suspected of being a survival of some older community — he had a trusted Javanese offer himself for admission. The man returned with the report that, if he wished to be admitted, he would have to undergo a three-year probation; he had only learned that, on death, the soul of these people was thought to incarnate in a "white ape, called *ka-rèh-an*", and that they yearly went up to a holy place in a *desa* **Tenggara-wangi** in the district Djamboe of the regency [[Purwakarta|Poerwakerta]]. Further he could not bring it.

## Decoding: *ka-rèh-an* and *Tenggara-wangi*

The "*ka-rèh-an*" white ape, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as a *bedekte taal* form for a "in a holy-white land home-belonging *sol-andjana Djawa-ka* — *réhan*" — i.e. the Hindu-Javanese ancestral land of [[Taroema]] on the Sumatran Overwal. The *desa* **Tenggara-wangi** he decodes as "in the *south-east* (*toenggara*) located *orangi*", i.e. as ***Aroem*** — *koe-T-aroem-a* — the ancestral land of [[Aroe]] whose "fragrant" (*aroem*) name the place preserved. The Kalang's of [[Tjilatjap|Tjilatjap]] were, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the Javanese rememberers of the Sumatran ancestral land of [[Taroema]].

## The Kalang of Rembang

The [[Rembang|Rembang]] region of Java's north coast — the place whose name [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads as the *Ari-ke-mbang* of the Sumatran Overwal — was, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the principal Kalang settlement of Java. The [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Soenan Kalidjaga]] legend's "forest of [[Djatisaripa|Djatisekar]]" — to the north-east of [[Lasem|Lasem]] — is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the same *Ari-ke-mbang* place-world, and the *pasar* at which [[Soenan Bonang|Pangeran Bonang]] converted [[Raden Sahid]] was a Kalang pasar.

## The *lang-ka* etymology

The ethnonym *Kalang* [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *ka-lang* — i.e. as the *ka* (people) of *lang-ka* (the Sumatran ancestral land). The same *lang-ka*, De Roo de la Faille argues, underlies the *Langka* of the Javanese chronicle tradition (the *Langka* of [[Rama|Rama]]'s adversary [[Ravana|Rahwana]]) and the *Lang-ka* of the *Lang-ka-w-i-dja-ya* of the Woekir Tjanggal inscription of [[732]] — the *lang-ka* of the ancestral land of [[Taroema]] on the Sumatran Overwal.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Kalang are the principal ethnonym by which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] ties the Javanese chronicle tradition to the [[Aroe|Aru]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] sphere — the Javanese rememberers of the Sumatran ancestral land of [[Taroema]], whose *Orang Hoed* of [[Tjilatjap|Tjilatjap]] preserved the *bedekte taal* forms of the older Hindu-Buddhist geography into the colonial period. Their identification as [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] of *lang-ka* also marks them as the Javanese link in the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]]-Java triangle of the early sixteenth century.

**Rimboe** — Malay *rimba*, "jungle" or "bush" — was the term used in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* for the jungle of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] and the other *[[buitengewesten]]*, the principal theatre of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] patrouille. The *rimboe* was both the physical setting of the colonne method — the small, highly trained patrouille operating in the jungle — and the moral setting of the colonial soldaat's life: the *rimboe* that "sluit in en benauwt" (closes in and oppresses), the *rimboe* that "roept" (calls), the *rimboe* that killed by starvation at the [[Beruchtste hongertocht van Atjeh (1913)|beruchtste hongertocht van Atjeh]] of [[1913]].

## The physical setting

The *rimboe* was the physical setting of the colonne method. The [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] patrouille operated in the *rimboe* — the jungle of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] and the other *[[buitengewesten]]*. The *rimboe* was where the *[[bekas]]* was read, where the *[[bivak]]* was built, where the *[[dardanellen|dardanel]]* went ahead alone. The *rimboe* was therefore both the colonial soldaat's workplace and the place that tested him.

## The moral setting

"Onverzoenlijken waren gedwongen zich in de rimboe terug te trekken." The *rimboe* was also the moral setting of the colonial soldaat's life. The *rimboe* "sluit in en benauwt" — closes in and oppresses — not the place for "Gods vrije natuur." Yet after a few weeks of patrol "tien dagen later wilde men toch wel weer uit" — ten days later one wanted to be out again — even *[[Oom Ambon|Oom]]* "die het toch heel zwaar had onderweg" hunkered back to the *rimboe*. "Zelfs op Java nog te hunkeren naar buiten, naar de patrouilles en naar de rimboe" — even on Java, hankering for the outside, for the patrols and for the jungle.

## The lethality of the rimboe

The *rimboe* killed. The [[Beruchtste hongertocht van Atjeh (1913)|beruchtste hongertocht van Atjeh]] of [[1913]] — the most notorious hunger-patrol of Aceh — was a patrouille that became lost in the *rimboe* and perished in numbers. "Vier weken was het eten reeds op, toen de overlevenden werden gevonden door een patrouille, uitgerukt om hen op te sporen." Others were *jarenlang ziek* afterwards — for years ill from the privations. The *rimboe* was therefore both the workplace and the killer: the place where the colonial soldaat worked, and the place that could kill him by starvation.

## The rimboe roept

"De rimboe roept!" — the jungle calls! The phrase is the book's small marker of the colonial soldaat's moral tie to the *rimboe*: the *rimboe* that closed in and oppressed was also the *rimboe* that called the soldaat back. The *rimboe* was therefore both the workplace and the moral setting: the place where the colonial soldaat worked, and the place that called him back even when he was on Java, far from the *buitengewesten*.

## Significance

The *rimboe* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the physical and moral setting of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] patrouille. The book's argument is that the *rimboe* — both the workplace and the killer, both the place that closed in and the place that called back — was the colonial soldaat's moral tie: the place where he worked, and the place that called him back even when he was far from it. The *rimboe* is therefore the book's marker of the colonial soldaat's moral tie to the *buitengewesten*.

**Self-government and profit-province** (Dutch *zelfbestuur* and *wingewest*) were the two opposed notions through which author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] framed the whole of his *[[Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh]]* ([[1923]]): Atjeh before [[1873]] had known three and a half centuries of "zelfbestuur" — an order, in his reading, of internal feuds, exploitative sjahbandars and sultans, sea-robbery and failed centralization — while Atjeh after 1873 had become a "wingewest" of the Netherlands, a province to be developed at the cost of many sacrifices until the Dutch "mission sacrée de civilisation" should be fulfilled.

## The Stokvis formula inverted

Langhout borrowed the formula from his colleague [[J. E. Stokvis]], the able Dutch journalist and old chief-editor of *De Locomotief*, whose book *"Van Wingewest tot Zelfbestuur"* ("From Profit-province to Self-government") had recently offered a much-contested view of the then-current political condition of the Netherlands Indies in connection with the newest proposals for administrative revision. The title chosen by colleague Stokvis — "maar dàn in omgekeerde richting" ("but then in the inverse direction") — Langhout wished to take over for his first chapter, which treats of "the Atjeh of former days" up to the moment that the Vice-President of the then Council of India, the heer [[F. N. Nieuwenhuizen|Nieuwenhuizen]], handed the ultimatum of the Indian Government to the then reigning Sultan of Atjeh: a period during which Atjeh, "de heer Stokvis zal het dadelijk met mij eens zijn! — is gekomen 'Van Zelfbestuur tot Wingewest'". If the conclusions of Stokvis differ from Langhout's, he adds, this is to be blamed only on "de historie" ("history").

## "Atjeh under self-government"

One needs some courage, Langhout writes, to speak of an Atjeh before 1873 as of an Atjeh with self-government: the history runs from the freeing from the Portuguese and the assumption of the sultanate's supremacy ([[1524]]) through the great expansion to [[Nias]] and [[Malakka]] and the ensuing "rapidly spreading demoralization", the violence of the De Houtman era and the factories of [[1601]]–[[1627]], the sjahbandar regime and the [[Murder of the Hollanders and blockade of Atjeh (1651)|murder and blockade of 1651]], to "more than a century and a half" of internal feuds, sea-robbery and plundering, and the incidents of [[1831]]–[[1852]] that precede the [[Treaty of 1857 between Netherlands and Aceh|treaty of 1857]] and the slide to war. Attempts at centralization of authority — the edicts mentioned by [[K. F. H. van Langen]] in his *"Het Atjehsche bestuur onder het Sultanaat"* and those found by Dr. C. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — "have never been able to bring about the least change"; a "golden century" in the sense of a period in which some state regulation benefited land and people "Atjeh has never known". Order and rest, the two most necessary qualities for the existence of a land and a people, were lacking even under the greatest sultans; "this alles explains the fact that, though state rules were ever written down, they never were valid as a living law for land and people". That was: "before 1873. That was: 'Atjeh onder zelfbestuur!'"

## "Atjeh as wingewest"

"Atjeh is 'Wingewest' geworden! En het blééf dit... Totdat Nederland zijn 'mission sacrée de civilisation' in haar uiterste consequenties zal hebben vervuld" — Atjeh had become a profit-province and would remain it until the Netherlands had fulfilled her sacred mission of civilization in its utmost consequences; "tot zoolang moet Atjeh maar liever 'wingewest' blijven en niet meer, althans niet eerder, de gevolgen van het 'zelfbestuur' ondervinden" — for so long Atjeh should rather remain profit-province and not again experience the consequences of the "self-government": a relapse into the chaos of old, "the house divided against itself", the population perishing of diseases and epidemics for want of every measure of development. The book's sardonic ending asks: "Atjeh onder Zelfbestuur? Of: Atjeh als Wingewest? De keuze is daarginds al gedaan! Wat zullen wij, hier in Holland, dan anders beslissen?" — "The choice is already made yonder! What shall we, here in Holland, decide otherwise?"

## Significance

The paired formula was characteristic of the apologetic genre to which the commemorative volume of 1923 belonged: [[H. N. A. Swart]] in his foreword distanced himself expressly from some of the author's descriptions, while the author's own closing word admitted the book could be seen as "reclame" for a land and a people "which so fully deserve our interest" — the interest being the precondition for the "very much money" that Governor [[Van Sluys|Van Sluys]] declared the further pacification would still require.

**Protestantism** (Dutch: *Protestantisme*) was the dominant Christian confession of the Dutch colonial forces in [[Acheh (Atjeh)|Aceh]] from the [[1873]] outbreak of the [[Aceh War]] to the [[1942]] Japanese invasion, represented institutionally by the Netherlands Reformed tradition (Dutch: *Nederlands Hervormd*) and ministered to by the *veldpredikers* and *Inlandsche godsdienstleeraren* attached to the garrison of [[Koeta-Radja]] and the inland posts of [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] and the [[Gajolanden|Gajo Lands]]. As the confession of the great majority of the [[Ambonese soldiers|Ambonese soldiery]] — the men the colonial army called its "first Christians of Indië" (Dutch: *de eersten in Indië die Christen werden*) — Protestantism supplied both the European officers of the [[Indische Leger|Indische Leger]] and the Ambonese *fuseliers* of the field companies, and its principal exponent in Atjeh was the Ambonese *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]], the universally honoured *Soldaten-Dominee* whose forty-year chaplaincy paralleled that of the Catholic *Soldaten-Pastoor* [[Pastoor Verbraak|Verbraak]]. The tradition was inherited from the seventeenth-century Reformed mission of the [[Dutch East India Company|Oost-Indische Compagnie (VOC)]] and was, in the Atjeh theatre, the confession around which the spiritual life of the colonial army actually turned.

## The VOC inheritance and the Christianisation of the Moluccas

The Protestant presence in the Indies archipelago was the longest-standing European Christian tradition in the region, predating the nineteenth-century Catholic revival by more than two centuries. The [[Dutch East India Company|Oost-Indische Compagnie]] had, from its [[1602|1602]] founding, carried the Reformed faith as part of its commercial and administrative apparatus: company servants were required to be members in good standing of the public Reformed church, and the company's clergy (Dutch: *predikanten*) ministered both to the European factors and to the Christianised populations of the [[Moluccas]] descended from the earlier Portuguese mission. The Christian Ambonese villages — products of this long Reformed inheritance — were, by the nineteenth century, the most literate and missionarily consolidated Christian communities in the archipelago. When the [[Dutch East Indies Government|Netherlands Indies Government]] began recruiting extensively from these villages for the [[Indische Leger|Indische Leger]] in the [[19th century|nineteenth century]], it acquired a body of soldiers whose Protestant identity was already generations deep and whose loyalty to the Reformed church was inseparable from their loyalty to the colonial state. It was this inheritance that the source registers when it describes the Ambonese as "the first people in Indië that became Christian" and as drawing close to the Dutch because of their faith.

## The Soldaten-Dominee and the Inlands Godsdienstleraar

The institutional form of Protestantism in the Atjeh garrison was the chaplaincy of the *veldprediker* (field chaplain, Dutch: *veldprediker*) and, for the Ambonese companies, the *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* (indigenous religion teacher, Dutch: *Inlands Godsdienstleraar*). The post formally held by [[Dominee Thenu|Ds. Thenu]] from [[1894]] was technically not that of *veldprediker* at all — the office had been abolished in Atjeh when troops were forbidden to operate outside the line of forts — but the garrison's *predikant* was given an *Inlands Godsdienstleraar* as an assistant, and the young Ambonese Thenu, then twenty-six, came to fill the post. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] treasured him and overruled the rule abolishing *veldpredikers*, with the result that Thenu served for forty full years as the de facto *Soldaten-Dominee* of Atjeh, accompanying troops on expeditions, visiting every post from [[Koeta-Radja|the capital]] to the deeply inland [[Blang-Kedjeren]] and [[Lokop]] several times a year, composing the [[Marechausseelied|Marechaussee songs]], and acting as the most faithful visitor of the [[Panteh Perak|Militair Hospitaal]]. The chaplaincy of Thenu is, in the source's framing, the principal institutional embodiment of Protestantism in Atjeh.

## Protestantism, Catholicism and the dual chaplaincy

The Protestant ministry in Atjeh was matched, after [[1874]], by a parallel Catholic chaplaincy under [[Pastoor Verbraak|Pastoor Verbraak S.J.]], and the two confessions worked in a pattern of informal cooperation that the contemporary record treats as exemplary. The two chaplains accompanied the troops on separate expeditions but, when posted to the same area, substituted for each other at the bedside of the dying without disputing *sacramenten en geloofsleer* — the Catholic *Soldaten-Pastoor* giving his strengthening word to a dying Ambonese Protestant, the Protestant *Soldaten-Dominee* to a dying Javanese Catholic. This arrangement reflected the demographic fact that the European soldiery were mixed and the Ambonese largely Protestant, and it gave the Atjeh garrison a confessional balance that the metropolitan church in the [[Netherlands]] did not always match. Protestantism was, in this dual chaplaincy, the confession of the Ambonese rank and file; Catholicism the confession of the Jesuit mission. The two together made up the Christian presence of the garrison.

## Loyalty, the Oranje-Nassau and the Dutch royal house

Protestantism in Atjeh was bound, through the chaplaincy, to the symbolic apparatus of the [[Dutch royal house|Dutch royal house (Dutch: *Koninklijk Vorstenhuis*)]] and the House of Orange. The [[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.|Oom Ambon]] tradition — recorded by [[G. W. T. Dames|G. W. T. Dames]] — frames the Ambonese Protestant soldiery as a figure of centuries-old loyalty to the Prins, whose *trouw* (loyalty) had been preserved through the centuries and whose allegiance had "saved his descendants from broken faith (Dutch: *trouwbreuk*)." The dedication poem of *Oom Ambon* — "Aan Prins Maurits heeft Oom trouw beloofd, / die trouw is door eeuwen bewaard" — establishes this loyalty as the framing of Ambonese Protestant identity. [[Dominee Thenu|Thenu]]'s [[1901|1901]] decoration as *Ridder in de Orde van Oranje Nassau* — exceptional because the *Inlandsche godsdienstleraar* ranked only for a *medaille* — and his [[1934|1934]] audience with [[Queen Wilhelmina|Koningin Wilhelmina (Dutch: *Koningin*)]] after his forty Atjeh-years are the symbolic climax of this binding of Protestantism, the Ambonese soldiery and the crown. The *Marechausseelied* composed by Thenu — with its refrain of "*trouw aan Koningin, trouw aan het vaandel en trouw aan de vlag*" — is the musical expression of the same loyalty.

*Moslem warriors* — the [[Dutch East Indies|Nederlandsch-Indische]] rendering of Arabic مسلمين, "Muslims" — was the term Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* uses for the Achehnese resistance fighters, the enemy combatants on the Muslim side of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The term was the doctrinal counterpart of *kafi* / unbeliever: where the Dutch were the *kafi*, the Achehnese fighters were the *moslemin*, and the war between them was jihad. The III Moekims Kerkoen are described as having "remained free of moslemin" — i.e., not yet infiltrated by hostile resistance fighters. Enemy night attempts to set fire with fire-arrows at [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[4 April]]–[[5 April]] [[1896]] were attributed to moslemin. The "sham battle" doctrine of [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]] turned on the same distinction: Umar's legion could appear to fight the moslemin for Dutch pay, but the engagements were theatrical because Muslim could not seriously fight Muslim.

## Etymology and Meaning

The word *moslemin* is the Dutch Indies rendering of Arabic *muslimīn*, the plural of one who subprovided that [to God]. In standard Arabic the plural is nominative or oblique; the Dutch Indies form *moslemin* reflects the oblique plural, which is also the form used after prepositions. The term is broadly equivalent to modern Dutch spelling or English. In Kruisheer's usage *moslemin* carries a slightly archaic and explicitly religious-collective connotation: it is the term for the body of Muslims, viewed as a religious community in arms. The singular is rarely used; the collective plural is the operative form.

## Historical Context

In the first semester of [[1896]] *moslemin* was the Dutch colonial shorthand for the Achehnese enemy. The term's religious framing was not neutral: by calling the enemy *moslemin*, the Dutch acknowledged that they were fighting a body of Muslims, and that the war had a religious dimension that could not be reduced to banditry or tribal revolt. The [[III Moekims Kerkoen]]'s status as "free of moslemin" is the positive case: a region in which resistance fighters had not yet penetrated. The [[Lamkoenjit]] night attack of [[4 April]]–[[5 April]] [[1896]] — in which enemy fighters attempted to set fire to the post with fire-arrows — is attributed to moslemin, and the use of canister shot at under 50 m by *Sgt. Vermeulen* during that attack was the Dutch response. The *main prang* doctrine turned on the same category: Umar's legion was notionally fighting the moslemin, but in fact the engagements were theatre, "al vielen er also een enkele maal gewonden" (although occasionally someone was wounded).

## Examples in the Source

Kruisheer's principal explicit uses of the term are: the [[III Moekims Kerkoen]] described as "remained free of moslemin" — i.e., not infiltrated by hostile fighters. The [[4 April]]–[[5 April]] [[1896]] night attack on [[Lamkoenjit]] attributed to moslemin. The *main prang* doctrine of [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]], in which Umar's legion engaged the moslemin in sham battles. The labelling of [[Teuku Umar]]'s bands as rather than *moslemin* by [[Teungkoe Tanah Abée]] after the defection was the inverse of the term: the defector was declared to have left the body of moslemin. Kruisheer's text uses the term with awareness of its religious framing — it is the term the Achehnese themselves used, and its preservation in Dutch quotation marks the religious dimension of the war.

## Significance

The *moslemin* label is the conceptual complement of *kafi* / *kafir*: together the two terms define the religious geometry of the [[Aceh War]]. The Dutch recognition that the enemy were *moslemin* — a body of Muslims in arms, not merely "rebels" or "bandits" — was a strategic acknowledgement of the war's religious character. The malleability of the label (Umar's bands could be re-labelled *kafir* after the defection) shows its discursive power. The doctrine of *sabil Allah* and the institutional carriers of that doctrine — the oelamas, teungkoes and habibs — were sustained by the *moslemin* self-conception, which bound individual fighters to the resistance regardless of political vicissitude. The Dutch colonial project of "pacification" was therefore inseparable from a sustained effort to neutralise the *moslemin* framing.

**The Marechausseelied** — the Marechaussee song — was the soldiers' song of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]], composed in its first couplets by [[Dominee Thenu]] in the years before [[1896]] when the mood of the troops in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] was "somber en slecht," and grown over the decades into a song with "ontelbaar vele" (countless) couplets sung at posts and on the march. Its *refrein* — "*Manisee-Manisee! 't Is terlaloe Manisee!*" — gave the corps its *[[Manisee (Manisé)|Manisee]]* name, and its third regel — varied between "*Hari-hari naik goenoeng*" (every day climbing the mountain), "*Hari-hari masoek paja*" (every day plunging into the moeras) and "*Satoe mati ganti sepoeloeh*" (one dead, ten in his place) — was the soldiers' own identification of their work.

## Thenu's composition

"[[Dominee Thenu|Dominee Thenu]] had de eerste gemaakt. Hij kwam in Atjeh in de tijd vóór [[1896]] en de stemming was er somber en slecht. Hij voelde aan dat de troep afleiding nodig had en bracht de mensen het soldatenlied." Thenu composed many of the couplets himself, but "nooit heeft men later met zekerheid kunnen zeggen, welke woorden van hem waren" — no one could later say with certainty which words were his. The song was therefore a collective composition, with Thenu as the originator and the soldiers as the continuators.

## The refrein

The *refrein* of the *Marechausseelied* — "*Manisee-Manisee! 't Is terlaloe Manisee! / Hari-hari naik goenoeng, 't Is terlaloe Manisee!*" — gave the corps its *[[Manisee (Manisé)|Manisee]]* name. The word *terlaloe* — glossed in the book as *verschrikkelijk* (terrible/excessive) — was the soldiers' own affectionate exaggeration: the Marechaussee was "terlaloe Manisee" — terribly/excessively Manisee. The third regel was varied: "*Hari-hari naik goenoeng*" (every day climbing the mountain), "*Hari-hari masoek paja*" (every day plunging into the moeras), "*Satoe mati ganti sepoeloeh*" (one dead, ten in his place). The variations registered the actual work of the corps: the mountain, the swamp, the casualty.

## The korpslied and the distinction

The *Marechausseelied* is to be distinguished from the more formal *[[Korpslied van de Marechaussee|korpslied]]* — the corps song — which narrated the *daden* (deeds) of the corps vanaf de *oprichting* (from its founding). The *korpslied* had its own *refrein* — "*Hai marsosé, hai marsosé… memboeroeh moesoeh, memboeroeh moesoeh th' tjapé-é, / naik-toeroen goenoeng, / masoek-kaloear rimba, / memtjari bekas dani anak marsosé!*" (Hunting the enemy relentlessly, mountain up and down, in and out of the bush, seeking traces of the marechaussee-son!) — and was led by a *voorzanger* who sang new couplets, after which all fell in on the *refrein*. The *Marechausseelied* was the soldiers' own song; the *korpslied* was the corps's own song.

## The voorzanger and the feestjes

The *voorzanger* — the lead singer who called out the couplets for the others to fall in on — was a recognised role at feestjes. [[Tanamal]] — the *Marechaussee 1e klas* at [[Bakongan]] — was *voorzanger* at the post's feestjes, where his *ongelooflijk lelijke snuit* became *één en al vrolijkheid*. Sometimes a 13-14 year old *[[anak-kolong]]* would lead the *voorzanger* role — a child of the *[[tangsi]]* who had grown up with the song.

## Significance

The *Marechausseelied* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the cultural production of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] corps. The song — composed by the *Soldaten-Dominee* and grown by the soldiers — is the book's clearest example of the cultural life of the *[[tangsi]]*: the songs, the dances, the feestjes that made the corps a moral community, not just a military unit. The *refrein* "*Manisee-Manisee!*" is the book's identification of the corps by its own idiom: the corps was what its soldiers sang.

## Account from the Maréchaussée Jubileum-nummer ([[1930]]) — Mosselman's text

[[Mosselman|W. J. Mosselman]] opened his memoir in the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* with what he called "ons lijflied" — our own song — quoting a text in which the refrain differs slightly from the version recorded elsewhere:

> "O, marsausee, O, marsausee… 't Is terlaloe, marsausee! Biar soesah satoe goenoeng, hantam sadjah, marsausee. Satoe mati, ganti sepoeloeh. 't Is terlaloe marsausee!"

The Malay may be rendered: "O maréchaussée… it is too much, maréchaussée! Though the hardship be a mountain high, strike on regardless, maréchaussée. One dies, ten replace him."

### Authorship and diffusion

Mosselman supposed the song "eens gerijmeld door een dichterlijk aangelegd Menadoneesch of Amboneesch wapenbroeder, die aan kronjong en pantoens deed" — once rhymed by a poetically inclined Menadonese or Ambonese comrade who went in for *kronjong* and *pantoens*, as most of these "pracht-menschen" were, besides being excellent soldiers, "niet onverdienstelijke barden." Probably he had meant to charm the heart of his *Nona-manis* under the strumming of a guitar, and would little have suspected that his song would be sung on every post "tot diep in de wouden van Atjeh, ja overal, waar zich maar maréchaussees bevonden." Wherever they bivouacked behind wire or a barrier of branches after a heavy march, a *maréchaussée* who had laid down his weapons reached for guitar or mandolin; whatever the mood or repertoire, "het maréchausseelied was aanwezig," though the later couplets were composed "in eindelooze variatie naar eigen model."

### The words as corps creed

Mosselman held that "dat *Biar soesah satoe goenoeng*, dat *satoe mati ganti sepoeloeh*" had caught exactly the core of the old corps spirit — "de taaie vasthoudendheid van den terrier, de zorgelooze bereidheid tot het groote offer" — which corps tradition demanded of every man from the commander down to the least *maréchaussée*, on pain of unworthiness to wear the insignia of the *keurkorps*. He closed his memoir by quoting the same lines as the corps's "schoone devies."

### After the jubilee

The editorial anticipated that after the [[Uitreiking van het vaandel aan het Korps Maréchaussée (2 April 1930)|presentation of the colour]] on [[2 April]] [[1930]] the old war song *Manisé* would be struck up throughout Atjeh, in the remotest corners where Maréchaussée were quartered, and everywhere else where old *maréchaussées* were living out their last days — "ook in Nederland."

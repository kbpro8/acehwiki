**Pepper cultivation and trade in Atjeh** — the ancient people's culture (*volkscultuur*) of the West, North and East Coasts and of [[Poelò We]], drawing thousands of Groot-Atjehers and Pidireezen to the East Coast gardens, and the article par excellence of the harbours [[Idi]], [[Sigli]] and [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]] — runs as the central thread of [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s economic account, from the pepper-hidden coasts of the blockade years to the export of 200,000 picols in [[1920]] and the price-collapse which thereafter turned the planters to pinang.

## Before 1873

Since the beginnings of the Company's dealings the pepper was the stake of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] connections: the De Houtman voyage ([[1599]]) sought the pepper ports; the [[Murder of the Hollanders and blockade of Atjeh (1651)|treaty of 1651]] and the [[VOC-Minangkabau treaty and the founding of the Padang depot (1664)|occupation of Indrapoera, Padang and Tikoe]] turned the West Coast pepper trade toward Padang; and in the generation before the war the pepper harbours of the West Coast were frequented by the Frenchman [[E. Roura|Roura]] of the *Patty*, while the produce of East and West Coasts alike was bought up for account of traders at [[Penang]] — to which also the taxes of the sultans' government, the *hacil* on pepper and rice, attached (see [[Taxation in Atjeh]]).

## The war and the regulation years

The [[Blockade of the Atjeh coast (1873-1874)|blockade]] broke the old relations between the pepper harbours and Penang; in the submitted landscapes considerable masses of pepper — the harvests of a couple of years — lay waiting, and great was the need of import articles. The government-subsidized steam service to [[Rigaih]] (June [[1877]]) opened the West Coast via [[Meulaboh]] and Padang to Dutch-flag transport of "peper, koffie, rottan"; the lowering of the hacil from f 50 to f 35 per picol on the East Coast "benefited trade most highly". Yet the Dutch merchants "remained behind": the West Coast pepper trade passed largely to a Chinese kongsi of Batavia with an assured take of all pepper for some years; from several West Coast harbours cargoes went directly to Europe by a French ship; and most pepper, also after [[1882]], continued to be bought up for the account of traders at Penang. The pepper culture flourished in the security of the early 1890s: the harvest on [[Poelò We|Poeloe We]] and on the East Coast turned out very well, the East Coast export amounting in [[1889]]–[[1891]] to 5,742, 5,853 and 7,620 *kojangs* — thousands of Groot-Atjehers and Pidireezen travelling that year to the East Coast gardens "to work in the pepper plantations and to return home with a well-filled purse", the fruits of what Langhout calls the Atjehnese farmer's remarkable spirit of enterprise. The slump of [[1896]] (lower imports, dropped prices) and the continuing low prices curbed the profits: the wages nearly doubled for want of experienced labour, and the winners of 1891 remained behind those of earlier years.

## Revival and decline, 1898–1922

The [[Scheepvaartregeling|shipping regulation]] and the canals of trade stabilized the exports; the *Deli-Atjeh* and [[Nederlandsche Handel-Maatschappij|NHM]] offices and the pepper-washing establishment at Koeta Radja (f 6 to f 7 a picol in [[1920]]) served the marketing; the interest-free advances of Governors [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] and [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] freed the planters from usurers; and the pepper export of the Dependencies rose from 70,000 picols in [[1901]] to 200,000 picols in [[1920]]. The First World War and the long price-decline then did their work: "De pepercultuur bleef, niettegenstaande de aan de peperplanters verstrekte voorschotten, een kwijnend bestaan lijden, omdat de peperprijzen meestal laag waren" — the advances were difficult to recover; the export, 4,340,000 KG in [[1919]], dropped to 2,658,000 KG in [[1920]]; "the great love for the pepper appeared to cool", and the people turned to pinang-nuts and patchouli. Director [[N. van Zalinge]] (1922): "De peper wordt minder aangeplant. De teruggang van dit vroegere uitvoerartikel bij uitnemendheid vindt zijn oorzaak in den belangrijk gedaalden prijs aan den Overwal en op de wereldmarkt."

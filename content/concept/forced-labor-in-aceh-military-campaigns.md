**Forced Labor in Aceh Military Campaigns** was the systematic requisitioning and deployment of convict laborers (*forced convict laborers (*dwangarbeiders*)*) by the [[Dutch East Indies Government]] and the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] to perform engineering, porterage, and infrastructure work under combat conditions during the Aceh War between [[1873]] and [[1876]].

## Deployment and Statistics

Due to a chronic shortage of voluntary carriers in the tropical climate of northern Sumatra, military commanders relied heavily on forced laborers brought from Java and other colonial possessions. As documented in statistical tables in *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* ([[1876]]), during the opening of the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] in late [[1873]], General [[J. van Swieten]]'s forces included over 2,770 forced convict laborers (*dwangarbeiders*) organized under 122 mandoors across three military divisions.

By December [[1873]], disease, deaths, and medical evacuations to coastal islands like [[Poeloe Nassie]] reduced this contingent to approximately 2,380 effective forced laborers.

## Operational Role Under Pel

During [[General Pel's Campaign in Aceh]] in December [[1875]] and [[1876]], General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] utilized approximately 1,200 forced convict laborers (*dwangarbeiders*) alongside 2,500 free coolies to construct redoubts, clear supply roads, and transport artillery ammunition from [[Pekan Badak]] and [[Sinangri]] to front-line positions in the [[IV Moekims]] and [[XXII Moekims]]. 

The high mortality and physical exhaustion of these laborers remained a constant constraint on the tempo of Dutch operations throughout the war.

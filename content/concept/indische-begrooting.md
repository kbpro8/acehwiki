The **Indische begrooting** (Indies budget) was the annual financial statement of the Dutch East Indies colonial administration, presented to the Dutch [[Tweede Kamer]] for approval. It was the subject of intense parliamentary debate, with socialist members such as [[H. van Kol]] using it as the primary vehicle to criticize colonial policy, including the [[Aceh War]] and its economic consequences.

## Socialist critique

Socialist parliamentarians consistently used the budget debates to expose the economic dimensions of colonial rule. They argued that the Indische begrooting was a document that revealed:
- The financial burden of the Aceh War on the Dutch treasury.
- The economic interests (shipping companies, suppliers, planters, mining companies) that benefited from the war — see [[Economic interests in the Aceh War]].
- The extraction of wealth from the colony to the Netherlands, including the "eerschuld" (debt of honour) of 187 million guilders taken from the colonial treasury since [[1867]].

## The 1901 budget debate

The [[1901]] budget debate was particularly significant. Van Kol attacked Dr. [[Abraham Kuyper]]'s government for its imperialist rhetoric and its continuation of the Aceh War. He criticized the government for spending "vijf miljoen" (five million guilders) on a tramway in Aceh for military purposes, while failing to fund irrigation works on Java. He also exposed the "uitputting van den soldaat in Atjeh" (exhaustion of the soldier in Aceh) and the incidence of venereal diseases among European troops.

## Colonial poverty and exploitation

Van Kol used the budget debates to highlight the poverty of the Javanese, arguing that 25 million Javanese had an average annual income of only f31, of which f6.60 was paid in taxes, while the colonial administration spent f2.7 million on education for 65,000 Europeans but only f1.5 million on the education of 25 million indigenous people.

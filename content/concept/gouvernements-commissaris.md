**The gouvernements-commissaris** (government commissioner) was the Dutch colonial official dispatched with military forces to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1873]] to demand the Sultan's recognition of Dutch sovereignty. The institution of the *gouvernements-commissaris* — first exercised by [[Schiff|Schiff]] and [[Van de Wall|Van de Wall]], then by [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|Nieuwenhuyzen]] — became the mechanism through which the [[Declaration of war on Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war on Atjeh]] was effected, and the commissioner's instruction and conduct became the central subject of the [[Parliamentary debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|Tweede Kamer debate of April 1874]].

## The first mission: Schiff and Van de Wall

The first commissioners designated were [[Schiff|Schiff]] and [[Van de Wall|Van de Wall]], sent under an instruction prepared by or with the approval of [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Minister Fransen van de Putte]]. That instruction — conforming to the unanimous advice of the [[Raad van Nederlandsch Indie|Raad van Indie]] against aggression — contained the germ of war: it instructed the resident of [[Riouw|Riouw]] to demand recognition of Dutch supreme lordship if the Sultan sought help against internal strife. [[Insinger|Insinger]] read this passage aloud in the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]]: "As er botsingen komen tusschen partijen en den Sultan, en den Sultan hulp vraagt, behoort die te worden verleend, onder beding evenwel van de erkenning der opperheerschappi van Nederland over Atjeh. [...] Eene zoo zich gunstige gelegenheid zal zich welligt niet ligt voordoen."

Before this mission could depart, [[Van de Wall|Van de Wall]] fell mortally ill, and [[Schiff|Schiff]] alone was considered insufficient for such a weighty mission. The Governor-General then proposed dispatching [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|the vice-president of the Council of Indies]], and the Minister pointed to the same man.

## Nieuwenhuyzen's mission

[[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|Nieuwenhuyzen]], as *gouvernements-commissaris*, arrived at Atjeh with four battalions of troops and delivered the ultimatum of [[22 March]] [[1873]] demanding sovereignty recognition, accompanied by the [[Manifest aan Sultan van Atjeh (22 March 1873)|manifest]] threatening bombardment of the Sultan's residence. The [[Instructie gouvernements-commissaris (1873)|instruction]] had originally envisaged obtaining sovereignty on paper; after blood was shed, article 6 was modified by the Minister to reserve the right of Dutch establishment.

## The parliamentary assessment

[[Haffmans|Haffmans]] argued that the *gouvernements-commissaris* had "come equipped for war and brought the war"; the asking of clarification that preceded was merely for form. [[Nierstrasz|Nierstrasz]] called Nieuwenhuyzen "the truly guilty one" for the first expedition's failure. [[I.D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] defended the institution by arguing that the instruction had always envisaged a peaceful outcome, and that the modification was justified by changed circumstances.

**Kawan** (Malay/Acehnese for “comrade, companion”) was the term used within the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* for the ordinary *maréchaussées* — the rank and file — in the corps's internal hierarchy of Acehnese titles recorded by [[W. J. Mosselman]].

## Place in the hierarchy

In the *[[Maréchaussée-vocabulaire]]* the officers were *[[Oeleebalang|Oeleuhbalangs]]*, the European non-commissioned officers *[[Maréchaussée-vocabulaire|Toekoes]]*, the indigenous sergeants *[[Panglima|Panglima’s]]*, the corporals *[[Keuchi|Keutjih’s]]* and the men *Kawans*. Each *Toekoe* disposed of one *Panglima*, one *Keutjih* and seventeen *Kawans*.

## Seniority among the kawans

Mosselman recorded that among the *kawans* themselves "voerde de eerbied voor den oudere in dienstjaren den boventoon" — respect for the man senior in years of service predominated — a principle of natural authority operating alongside the formal military hierarchy and contributing, in his account, to the corps's distinctive discipline.

## In Mosselman's memoir

Mosselman addressed his memoir in the [[1930]] jubilee number directly to his former *kawans*: "mijn bedoeling was slechts te schrijven tot en over mijn oude kawans" — my intention was only to write to and about my old *kawans*. He named among them Kromodimedjo, Sarpan, Diroen, Patty, Wattimena, Limba, Wokas, Mamahit, Sengkey and Mioen.

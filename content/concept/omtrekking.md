The **[[Omtrekking|omtrekking]]** (flanking and encircling movement; also *omtrekkende beweging*) was the "overoude Indische tactiek" (age-old Indian tactic) of flanking or encircling an enemy position rather than attacking it frontally — a tactic that, in Borel's account, "almost always yields surprising results." The *omtrekking* was the characteristic Dutch tactical method of the Atjeh-oorlog, and the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] by [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]] that delivered the deserted Kraton to the Dutch was the largest single example.

## The Doctrine

The *omtrekking* was, in Borel's account, the "overoude Indische tactiek" — the age-old Indian tactic — of flanking or encircling an enemy position rather than attacking it frontally. The doctrine was the Indian equivalent of the European principle of *marcher au canon* (marching to the sound of the guns) and of the envelopment that the German pursuit after [[Sédan]] in [[1870]] had made canonical. The Atjehnese stellingen — typically concealed behind [[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboo-doeri]] thickets, with the strongest face toward the most likely approach — could rarely be reduced by frontal assault without heavy casualties. The [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|storming of the Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] — undertaken "tegen alle Indische traditiën in, zonder noemenswaardige voorbereiding, alleen in front, waar ze natuurlijk het sterkst was" (against all Indian traditions, without noteworthy preparation, only in front, where it was of course strongest) — had cost the Dutch 220 dead and wounded, and had only been salvaged by [[Generaal Pel|luitenant-kolonel Pel]]'s flanking move that entered the Mesigit unopposed. The lesson Borel draws is categorical: frontal assault against an Atjehnese stelling was a waste of men; the *omtrekking* was the proper Indian method, and "almost always yields surprising results."

## The Omtrekkende Beweging of 23–24 January 1874

The largest single *omtrekking* of the Atjeh-oorlog was the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] by [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]]. After "hevig vuur," Verspijck began the omtrekkende beweging from [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan (12 January 1874)|Kota Goenoengan]] around the zuider- and ooster-facen to reach the Atjeh-rivier. The crossing of the [[Kroeng Daroe]] caused "groot oponthoud." Verspijck encountered "geen noemenswaardigen tegenstand." On the morning of [[24 January]] [[1874]] the omtrekkende beweging continued. At 11 AM the last mortier granaat was fired. Verspijck reached the Atjeh-rivier; all works fell "zonder slag of stoot." An afdeeling climbed the low borstwering and discovered, "tot niet geringe verbazing," that they were inside the Kraton — which was "op een grijsaard en een zieken Atjehnees na, geheel verlaten." "Het lot van den Kraton was dus beslist." The whole operation, Borel judged, could have been done "na weinige dagen" if regelmatig conducted; instead it took a month — a delay that allowed the Atjehnese hoofdmacht to withdraw unbroken and that Borel indicts as one of the strategic errors of the second expedition.

## The Smaller Omtrekkingen

The smaller *omtrekkingen* of Pel's campaigns of [[1874]]–[[1876]] were many. The [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] of [[28 June]] [[1874]] — when [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]] attacked the well-laid fortification at Garouw, surrounded it, and after brave resistance took it — was a typical example. The [[Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)|battle of Soerian]] of [[26 July]] [[1874]] — when Pel's colonnes managed to surround a band inside a benteng near Soerian and, because they refused to surrender, killed them to the last man — was another. The three-colonne operations of the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|great offensive of December 1875 – February 1876]] — three colonnes in *verbond* moving against the VI, IV and IX Moekims, advancing in *wigvorm* (wedge formation) so as to converge on the objective from multiple directions — were, in effect, large-scale *omtrekkingen*. The lesson Borel draws is that the *omtrekking* was the proper Indian tactic against Atjehnese stellingen, and that its neglect — in favour of frontal assault, of the *afwachtende houding*, or of the *posten-systeem* — was one of the principal tactical errors of the war. The *omtrekking* was, in Borel's account, the tactical complement to the [[Mobiele colonnes (Borel)|mobiele colonnes]] and to the *kappen en branden* that he advocated: the means by which the Dutch could overcome the Atjehnese enemy without squandering their men in frontal assaults.

The **[[Kroeng|kroeng]]** (Acehnese *krueng*) was the Atjehnese term for a river — the principal hydrographic features of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the focus of much of the campaigning described in *Onze vestiging in Atjeh*. The *kroengs* of Groot-Atjeh — the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier (Kroeng Aceh)]], the [[Kroeng Daroe]], the [[Kroeng Raba]], the [[Kroeng Raya]], the [[Kroeng Tjoet]], the [[Kroeng Broek]] — define the hydrographic geography of the theatre and shape the lines of communication, defence and supply on which the war was fought.

## The Term and Its Geographic Significance

The kroeng was, in Borel's account, the Atjehnese term for a river. The term appears in the toponyms of Groot-Atjeh — Kroeng Aceh (the Atjeh-rivier), Kroeng Daroe, Kroeng Raba, Kroeng Raya, Kroeng Tjoet, Kroeng Broek — and is the hydrographic counterpart of the *kwala* (river-mouth). The kroeng was both a feature of the terrain and a line of communication: the Atjeh-rivier was the principal line of communication between the Dutch vestiging at [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] and the sea, navigable by prauw and waterprauw as far as the Kraton; the Kroeng Daroe, a small river that split the Kraton into two halves, was a tactical feature of the operations around the Kraton; the Kroeng Raba, Kroeng Raya and Kroeng Tjoet were the rivers whose mouths on the west and north coasts defined the projected afsluitingslinie of [[Pel's plan for the Atjeh line|Pel's plan]].

## The Kroengs of Groot-Atjeh

The kroengs of Groot-Atjeh define the hydrographic geography of Borel's narrative. The [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier (Kroeng Aceh)]] was the principal river — the line of communication between the Dutch vestiging and the sea at [[Oeleh Leh]] and [[Kwala Atjeh]], navigable by prauw and waterprauw, but onbevaarbaar during the kwade moesson that broke on [[31 May]] [[1874]]. The [[Kroeng Daroe]] was a small river that split the Kraton into two halves; its crossing by [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]]'s omtrekkende beweging on [[23 January]] [[1874]] caused "groot oponthoud." The [[Kroeng Raba]] was the river on the west coast of Atjeh, with its mouth at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai|Baai van Kroeng Raba]]; it was the projected western end of the afsluitingslinie, and the kwala at its mouth was the proposed landing place of the [[Padangsche brigade|Padangsche brigade]] — rejected by van Swieten out of "vrees." The [[Kroeng Raya]] was the river on the north coast; it was the projected eastern end of the afsluitingslinie, and the site of the later sea-expedition under [[Karel van der Heijden|kolonel van der Heijden]]. The [[Kroeng Tjoet]] was the river on the north coast flowing into the lagune of [[Kwala Gigieng|Gigieng]]; it was the major contraband supply channel giving access to the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]], and its mouth was finally closed by the Dutch in [[1876]] by the establishment of posts at Kwala Gigieng and on the eiland [[Kota Pohama]] in the lagune. The [[Kroeng Broek]] was a smaller river whose banks were covered by the new fort at [[Kwala Titipandjang]] in [[17 February]] [[1875]].

## The Kroengs and the Campaign

The kroengs shaped the campaign in several ways. First, they were the lines of communication: the Atjeh-rivier was the principal Dutch line of communication, and its onbevaarbaarheid during the kwade moesson was, in Borel's account, one of the strategic vulnerabilities of the Dutch vestiging. Second, they were tactical obstacles: the crossing of the Kroeng Daroe by Verspijck's omtrekkende beweging caused "groot oponthoud," and the marshy banks of the kroengs in the coastal lowlands obstructed the movement of columns and artillery. Third, they were the focus of the projected afsluitingslinie: the Kroeng Raba on the west coast and the Kroeng Raya on the north coast were the rivers whose mouths defined the line, and the closure of the Kroeng Tjoet mouth in [[1876]] was the most significant single step in the execution of Pel's plan. The kroengs thus define, in Borel's account, both the geographic framework of the campaign and the strategic objective of the war.

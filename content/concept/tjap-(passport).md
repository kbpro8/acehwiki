**Tjap** — the passport or stamp of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] court — was the official permit required for foreign ships to come ashore at the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh river]] and to trade in the sultanate. In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]] the *tjap* is the formal instrument by which the Sultan's authority over the port was exercised: it was brought by the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] (harbour-master) together with the chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] at the river-mouth, as the formal permission for the Dutch legation to come ashore.

## Etymology

*Tjap* (also *cap*) is the Malay-Indonesian rendering of the Persian/Arabic *ṭābiʿ* or of the Chinese *chop* (印) — in either case, a stamp, seal, or official mark. In the early modern Malay-Indonesian world, a *tjap* was the physical stamp or seal applied to a document to authenticate it; by extension, the document itself (the passport or permit) was called a *tjap*. The *tjap* of the Atjeh court was thus the Sultan's permit, sealed with his stamp, that authorised a foreign visitor to come ashore.

## Role in the Soury embassy, 1641

When *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Pieter Soury]]'s embassy reached the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh river]] in [[1641]], the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] and the chief eunuch of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] came out together with the Dutch *Opperhoofd* of the [[Dutch lodge at Atjeh|lodge]] to greet the ships. "Den havenmeester of Sabandaar, die het tjap of paspoort tot aan land komen meebracht" — the harbour-master or *Sabandaar*, who brought the *tjap* or passport to come ashore. The *tjap* was thus the Sultan's formal permission for the legation to land.

The presentation of the *tjap* by the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] in person was the formal act of welcome, but also of control: the Sultan's permission was being granted, and the limits of that permission were being marked. Without the *tjap*, no foreign ship might lawfully send a boat to land. The passport was the Sultan's instrument of port-regulation.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *tjap* is the principal instrument of the sultanate's port-regulation — the official permit by which the Sultan's authority over the port was exercised. The presentation of the *tjap* by the [[Sabandaar|Sabandaar]] at the river-mouth was the formal act by which a foreign embassy was admitted to the sultanate's territory, and the absence of the *tjap* would have been an effective bar to any foreign landing. The institution of the *tjap* testifies to the sultanate's organised control of its port and its foreign trade — a control that the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury embassy record]] preserves in the small but consequential detail of the *tjap* brought at the river-mouth.

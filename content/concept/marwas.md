**Marwas** (also spelled *marwah*; Dutch: *kleine tweehoofdige trommel* — "small double-headed drum") is a **small, double-headed cylindrical drum** used in **[[Acheh]]** (Atjēh), northern Sumatra. It is a membranophone of the **cylindrical drum** family, distinct from the barrel-shaped *[[Kendang (Aceh)|kendang]]* / *[[Gendang]]* and the single-headed *[[Geudoemba]]* and frame drum *[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]* / *[[Redep]]*.

## Organology

- **Hornbostel-Sachs classification**: 211.212.1 (double-headed cylindrical drum, struck)
- **Body**: cylindrical wooden shell, typically carved from a single block of hardwood (e.g., *meranti*, *kayu malam*, *cempaka*); length 20–30 cm, diameter 12–18 cm
- **Heads**: two membranes (goat, cow, or monitor lizard skin), one on each end
- **Tension system**: traditionally rattan or cane V-lacing / Y-lacing with wooden tuning wedges; modern variants may use metal turnbuckles
- **Playing technique**: struck with bare hands (fingers, palm) on both heads; held horizontally across the lap or suspended from a shoulder strap
- **Pitch**: high, bright, penetrating — tuned a fourth or fifth above the *kendang*
- **Pairing**: often played in **pairs** or **sets of three to five** in *[[Saman]]* ensembles, each drum tuned to a different pitch for interlocking patterns (*tak*)

## Historical and Regional Context

The *marwas* is documented by **[[Jaap Kunst]]** in *[[Muziek en Dans in de Buitengewesten]]* ([[1946]], p. 10) as one of the "typically Mohammedan instruments" (*typisch Mohammedaansche speeltuigen*) for **Atjēh**, alongside the *[[Gambus (Aceh)|gambus]]*, *[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]*, *[[Bangei]]*, and *[[Serunai (Aceh)|serunai]]*. This classification places it in the **Islamic instrumental complex** that spread across the Indian Ocean with the *Shattariyya*, *Qadiriyya*, and *Naqshbandiyya* Sufi orders from the [[13th century]] onward.

The *marwas* is cognate with the **Arabian *marwas*** / ***mirwas*** (مِروَص) — a small double-headed drum used in *Hadrami* and *Hijazi* *zāwiyah* (Sufi lodge) music, *mawlid* celebrations, and *ḥadrah* processions. The instrument likely entered Aceh via **Hadrami traders and missionaries** from **Hadramawt** (Yemen) and the **Hejaz** (Mecca/Medina) from the [[15th century]] onward, coinciding with the rise of the **[[Aceh Sultanate]]** as a major Islamic polity.

## Role in Acehnese Music

The *marwas* is the **primary rhythmic engine** of the **[[Saman]]** dance — the iconic "dance of a thousand hands" of the **[[Gayo Lands]]** and **[[Acheh]]**. In a *Saman* ensemble:

- **3–5 *marwas*** players sit in a row, each drum tuned differently
- They perform **interlocking rhythmic patterns** (*tak*, *tak-tak*, *tak-tak-tak*) at rapid tempos (160–220 BPM)
- The patterns are **cyclic** (*ulan*), repeating every 8, 16, or 32 beats
- The *marwas* section is led by a **penghulu** (leader) who signals tempo changes and transitions with vocal cues (*hai*, *ho*, *yallah*)

Beyond *Saman*, the *marwas* appears in:

- **[[Meuseukat]]** (women's seated dance-song) — lighter, more delicate patterns
- **[[Zikir]]** / **[[Dzikir]]** / **[[Hadrah]]** — Sufi liturgical processions, accompanying *qasidah* and *nasyid*
- **[[Nasyid]]** / **[[Qasidah]]** ensembles — popular Islamic devotional music
- **[[Seudati]]** / **[[Sedati (dance)|Sedati]]** — processional dance (less common, *rabana* preferred)

## Rhythmic Patterns (Tak) in Saman

Typical *marwas* patterns in *Saman* (Western notation approximation):

```
Tala 8 (Ulan 8):
Marwas 1:  D  .  T  .  D  .  T  .   (D = dum/bass, T = tak/treble)
Marwas 2:  .  T  .  D  .  T  .  D
Marwas 3:  T  .  D  .  T  .  D  .
Marwas 4:  .  D  .  T  .  D  .  T
Combined:  D  T  T  D  D  T  T  D  → interlocking composite rhythm
```

The **interlocking** (*kotekan*-like) principle creates a continuous, driving pulse that propels the dancers' synchronized hand, chest, and thigh claps.

## Construction Details (Traditional Acehnese)

| Component | Material | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| Shell | *Kayu malam* (Diospyros), *Meranti* (Shorea), *Cempaka* (Michelia) | Single block, lathe-turned or hand-carved |
| Heads | Goat skin (preferred), cow skin, *biawak* (monitor lizard) | Soaked, stretched wet, laced wet |
| Lacing | Rattan (*rotan*), *tali ijuk* (sugar palm fiber) | V-lacing with tuning wedges (*pasak*) |
| Wedges | Bamboo, hardwood | Tapered, driven under lacing to tension heads |
| Strap | Woven *tengkuluk* (rattan), batik cloth | For standing/processional play |

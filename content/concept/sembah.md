The **sembah** (also *sambah*) was the reverential salute of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and Malay-Indonesian court — a gesture of greeting and respect made by pressing the palms together with the fingers pointed upward and the thumbs close to the breast, often accompanied by a slight bow. In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]] the *sembah* is the gestural vocabulary by which the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] and the Dutch legation acknowledged one another in the formal audience of [[1 July]] [[1641]].

## The gesture

The *sembah* is the classical Malay-Indonesian gesture of reverence: the palms pressed together, the fingers pointing upward, the hands raised to the level of the breast or face, often with a slight inclination of the head or body. It is at once a greeting, a sign of respect, and an act of obeisance — its precise meaning calibrated by the depth of the bow, the height to which the hands are raised, and the social rank of the gesturer and the recipient.

## The Soury embassy, 1 July 1641

When the *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s legation was conducted into the presence of the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] on [[1 July]] [[1641]], the gestural choreography was precise. The Sultana came out of the palace to greet the embassy — "een buitengewone eer, nooit tevoren aan iemand bewezen" (an extraordinary honour, never before paid to anyone). "De Vorstin verscheen, en ieder boog of maakte de sembah" — The Queen appeared, and each bowed or made the *sembah*. The Dutchmen thus responded to the Sultana's appearance with the gesture that was native to her court — a small but significant act of ceremonial adaptation, the visitors adopting the local vocabulary of respect.

## At the close of the audience

When the Sultana wished to retire to her palace after the spectacle of fighting elephants, bulls, buffaloes and bucks, the assembly again made the *sembah*: "Toen de Vorstin zich weer in haar paleis wilde terugtrekken, stonden alle aanwezigen op en deden hun sembah" — When the Queen wished to retire to her palace, all those present rose and made their *sembah*. The *sembah* thus framed the audience — made at the Sultana's appearance and at her departure — and was the gestural means by which the court acknowledged her sovereignty.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *sembah* is the gestural vocabulary of the sultanate's court ceremony — the visible means by which the relations of sovereignty and respect were made present in the audience hall. The Dutch adoption of the *sembah* in the [[1 July]] [[1641]] audience was a small but telling act of ceremonial adaptation: the visitors from the United Provinces, sitting shoeless in Atjeh dress and chewing *sirih* from the Sultana's golden *beteldoos*, performed the local gesture of reverence to the new ruler. The gesture is one of the many small details by which the Soury embassy record preserves the texture of Atjeh court ritual at the moment of transition to the era of female rule.

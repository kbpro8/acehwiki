**7 cm L.A. kanon**, **8 cM veld geschut**, **12 c.M. mortier**, *quick-firing cannon* and **Berggeschut** were the principal [[Dutch]] artillery pieces deployed in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*. The 7 cm L.A. *kanon* — a 7 cm mountain field gun — was the standard artillery piece of the Dutch *buitenposten* and the principal field piece of the mobile columns; one was lost without breech-block and sight on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the disastrous [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]]. The 8 cM 8 cm bronze field gun, originally at [[Tjot Goeë]] was replaced by a 7 c.M. A. Veld gun on [[12 April]] [[1896]]. The 12 c.M. 12 cm mortar; one lost on 17 April was a heavier piece used for siege work. The rapid-fire gun — a 3.7 cm *snelvuurkanon* at [[Oleh-leh]] — was a more modern weapon. The mountain gun was a pack-artillery piece dismantlable for mule transport.

## Etymology and Meaning

The Dutch artillery nomenclature is descriptive. *7 cm L.A. kanon*: 7 cm calibre, *L.A.* of uncertain expansion (possibly *lichte*, "light"), gun, cannon. *7 c.M. A. Veld*: 7 centimetre, *A.* of uncertain expansion, field. *8 cM veld geschut*: 8 centimetre field gun. *8 c.M. Brons*: 8 centimetre bronze gun — referring to the bronze construction of the earlier rifled muzzle-loading and breech-loading types. *12 c.M. mortier*: 12 centimetre mortar. *Snelvuurkanon*: rapid-fire gun (Dutch *snel* = rapid, *vuur* = fire, *kanon* = gun). *Berggeschut*: mountain gun (Dutch *berg* = mountain). *Veldstuk*: field piece. *Vuurmond*: literally "fire-mouth" — a Dutch term for a gun or cannon. The various carriage types — mountain carriage, field carriage, breastwork carriage, pivot carriage — denote the mountings.

## Historical Context

In the first semester of [[1896]] Dutch artillery was deployed in three principal modes. First, as fixed defensive armament of the *buitenposten* — each post typically had a 7 cm L.A. *kanon* and, in larger posts, a *mitrailleur* and a heavier piece. Second, as siege artillery at [[Lamdjamoe]] — where, from [[25 April]] [[1896]], a battery of 8 mortieren 20 c.M., 6 kanonnen 12 c.M. and 6 kanonnen 7 c.M. was assembled for the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] (the bombardment fired 7,996 projectiles and produced "zeer gering" results). Third, as field artillery in the mobile columns — typically a section (2 *vuurmonden*) of 7 cm L.A. *kanonnen* on mountain carriage for pack transport. The [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] saw the catastrophic loss of a 7 c.M. A. Veld without breech-block and sight, a the *kast met loopen*, and a 12 c.M. *mortier* — matériel subsequently brought by [[Panglima Polim]] to [[Senelop]] and used against the Dutch.

## Significance

The Dutch artillery was the principal force-multiplier of the colonial army, the means by which a small European force could hold positions and take Achehnese *bentengs* at acceptable cost. The [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|loss of a 7 cm L.A. kanon, a mitrailleur, and a 12 cm mortier at Senelop]] was therefore a strategic event of the first order: the captured pieces were turned against the Dutch, and the 7 cm L.A. *kanon* reportedly shelled Senelop on [[31 March]] [[1896]]. The 7,996-projectile bombardment of [[Lampisang]] — and its "zeer gering" results — exposed the liprovided that of artillery against an enemy who could simply evacuate the target sone. The Dutch reliance on mountain artillery (*berggeschut* on *bergaffuit*) reflected the terrain of [[Groot-Atjeh]]: the guns had to be dismantlable for pack-animal transport across the *sawah*, *kloof* and *boekit* landscape that characterised the theatre of operations.

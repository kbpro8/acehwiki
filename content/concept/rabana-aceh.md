**Rabana (Aceh)** (also *rabana Atjeh*, *redep*, *rapana*, *rapai*; Dutch: *rabana* / *redep*; Arabic: *riq* / *daff* / *tar*) is a **shallow, single-headed frame drum** used in **[[Acheh]]** (Atjēh) as a core instrument of the **Islamic instrumental complex**. It is the Acehnese variant of the **Middle Eastern frame drum family** (*daff*, *riq*, *tar*, *bendir*) that spread across the Indian Ocean with Islam from the [[13th century]] onward.

## Organology

- **Hornbostel-Sachs classification**: 211.311 (frame drum, single-headed, struck)
- **Frame**: circular wooden hoop (typically *meranti*, *kayu malam*, or rattan), 30–50 cm diameter, 5–8 cm depth
- **Head**: single membrane (goat, cow, or fish skin — *kulit ikan*), glued or tacked to the frame
- **Jingles (optional)**: some variants (*rabana berzils* / *redep berzils*) have 2–4 pairs of metal jingles (*zils*, *cymbalets*) set in slots in the frame — linking it to the *riq* type
- **Tension**: fixed (glued/tacked) — not tunable; pitch determined by head thickness, frame diameter, and ambient humidity
- **Playing technique**: struck with fingers, palm, heel of hand; *snapping* (finger flick), *rolling* (rapid finger alternation), *damping* (left hand on head)
- **Position**: held vertically in left hand (thumb through frame or gripping rim), played with right hand; or rested on left thigh

## Historical and Regional Context

**[[Jaap Kunst]]** in *[[Muziek en Dans in de Buitengewesten]]* ([[1946]], p. 10) lists the *rabana* (and *redep*) among the **"typisch Mohammedaansche speeltuigen"** ("typically Mohammedan instruments") **for Atjēh**, alongside the *[[Gambus (Aceh)|gambus]]*, *[[Marwas]]*, *[[Bangei]]*, and *[[Serunai (Aceh)|serunai]]*. The dual naming (*rabana* / *redep*) reflects:

- **Rabana**: from **Arabic *riq* / *riqq*** (via Persian *riq* / Malay *rebana*) — the standard term across the Malay world
- **Redep**: likely an **Acehnese phonological adaptation** (*r* > *r*, *b* > *d*, *n* > *p* — or a distinct local term for a specific variant)

The instrument arrived in **Aceh** — the "Veranda of Mecca" (*Serambi Mekkah*) — via **[[Hadrami]]** traders, **[[Shattariyya]]** Sufi missionaries, **[[Gujarat]]** and **[[Malabar]]** merchants, and **[[Ottoman]]** emissaries ([[16th century]]). It became essential to **[[Sufi]]** *[[Zikir]]* / *[[Dzikir]]* / *[[Hadrah]]* ceremonies in the **[[Aceh Sultanate]]** ([[1496]]–[[1903]]).

The Acehnese *rabana/redep* is distinguished by its **smaller frame** and **optional jingles** — a hybrid of the *daff* (no jingles) and *riq* (jingles) types.

## Role in Acehnese Music

The *rabana/redep* is the **principal rhythmic leader** in:

- **[[Zikir]]** / **[[Dzikir]]** / **[[Hadrah]]** — Sufi remembrance (*Shattariyya*, *Qadiriyya*, *Naqshbandiyya*, *Khalwatiyya* orders); the *rabana* player (*penabuh rabana*) leads the rhythmic cycle (*iqa'*)
- **[[Saman]]** — the *rabana* provides the foundational *tak* patterns; in *Saman Meuseukat*, multiple *rabana* interlock
- **[[Seudati]]** / **[[Sedati (dance)|Sedati]]** — processional dance; *rabana* marks the slow, stately *tala*
- **[[Meuseukat]]** — women's seated dance-song; *rabana* punctuates poetic lines
- **[[Hikayat]]** / **[[Dendang]]** recitation — rhythmic punctuation at caesuras
- **[[Nasyid]]** / **[[Qasidah]]** — devotional song accompaniment
- **[[Maulid]]** / **[[Maulud]]** — Prophet's birthday processions

## Rhythmic Patterns (Iqa' / Tala)

Common Acehnese *rabana* patterns (simplified Western notation):

| Pattern | Name | Cycle | Context |
|---------|------|-------|---------|
| **Dum — tak — tak — dum — tak** | *Iqa' Thaqil* / *Tala 5* | 5 beats | *Zikir Shattariyya*, *Hadrah* |
| **Dum — tak — tak — dum — tak — tak** | *Iqa' Khaliji* / *Tala 6* | 6 beats | *Saman*, *Seudati* |
| **Dum — tak — tak — dum — tak — dum — tak** | *Tala 7* | 7 beats | *Meuseukat*, *Dendang* |
| **Dum — tak — tak — dum — tak — tak — tak** | *Tala 8* | 8 beats | *Hikayat* recitation |

*Dum* = bass tone (center of head, palm heel); *Tak* = high tone (rim, fingers).

## Redep vs. Rabana Distinction

In Acehnese usage, some practitioners distinguish:

- **Rabana**: larger frame (40–50 cm), no jingles, deeper tone — *Zikir*, *Hadrah*, processional
- **Redep**: smaller frame (30–40 cm), often with jingles (*zils*), brighter tone — *Saman*, *Meuseukat*, indoor

However, the terms are often used interchangeably, and *redep* may simply be the **Acehnese pronunciation** of *rabana* (with *b* > *p*, *n* > *p* assimilation).

**Ahli waris** (Arabic-Malay, "the people of inheritance," heirs) was the term used in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] for the lawful heirs of a deceased possessor of land, whose rights conditioned the re-issue of abandoned ground by the *[[Oeleebalang|radja]]*.

## In the law of re-clearance

Where a piece of recently abandoned *[[Sawah|sawah]]* was applied for in the *[[Zelfbesturende landschappen|self-governing territories]]*, the applicant could be granted a three-year *bruikleen* (loan for use) under *[[Haq padjoh asoé|padjoh asoè]]* only if no heirs of the owner could be found who would undertake to work the land. Heirs who existed but did not intend to exploit the ground might not disturb the applicant in the right of use granted him by the *radja*. After the three years, if the original owner had not returned, the *ahli waris* had the right to demand a share-cropping contract from the new cultivator, who could thereafter continue only on that condition.

For gardens and *[[Ladang|ladangs]]* within the *[[Gampong|gampong]]* boundaries, where the owner or his *ahli waris* failed despite warning to maintain them properly, the *radja* might give the land out to an applicant in *[[Mawaih|mawaih asoë]]*, the produce to be shared with the owner or his heirs. Where nothing remained of the old planting on a garden outside the *gampong*, the applicant obtained full possession free of any obligation towards a *waris* subsequently appearing.

## In the law of estates without heirs

Where a person died leaving no heirs, or where a deceased stranger left land, the *oeléëbalangs* laid *ex officio* seizure on the unadministered estate for the benefit — at least according to the formula — of the *[[Baitul mal|bait ul-mâl]]*, after the *[[Ghafan|ghafan]]* had been defrayed from the *[[Eeang male|eeang male]]*.

## Inheritance and Mohammedan law

De Boer noted that Mohammedan inheritance law allotted the widow only a small share in her husband's estate — the consideration underlying the institution of the *[[Atra peunoelang|atra peunoelang]]*, by which a bride's father gave the couple property of twice the value of the *[[Djinamee (Atjehnese marriage gift)|djinaméë]]* paid by the bridegroom's father, so that a widow might maintain herself according to her rank and station.

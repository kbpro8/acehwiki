**Orangkaja** (also *Orang Kaya*, literally "rich man" in Malay) was the title of the principal nobles or great officers of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] court — the *Rijksgrooten* (great officers of the realm) of Dutch accounts. The *Orangkaja's* were the hereditary or appointed chiefs whose consent was required for the principal acts of state: the choice of a new Sultan or Sultana, the authorisation of treaties, and the approval of significant commercial transactions. In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]] the *Orangkaja's* are the body whose consent had to be obtained — through visits and gifts delivered by *Secretaris* [[Jan van Riebeeck|Jan van Riebeeck]] — for the disputed sale of the late Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]]'s jewels.

## Etymology and rank

*Orang kaya* is Malay for "rich man" — *orang* (person) + *kaya* (rich). In the early modern Malay-Indonesian world the title denoted a nobleman or great chief of a court, often one whose wealth came from trade as much as from land. At [[Acheh|Atjeh]] the *Orangkaja's* were the *Rijksgrooten* (great officers of the realm) of Dutch description — a council of nobles whose collective consent was constitutionally required for the principal acts of state.

## Role in the succession of 1641

When Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]] died in [[1641]], it was the *Rijksgrooten* who chose his widow as his successor — "dezelfde, die op ongeveer veertigjarigen leeftijd een Nederlander tot Prinsgemaal wilde verheffen" — the same one who, at about forty years of age, wished to elevate a Dutchman to the rank of prince-consort. The *Rijksgrooten* together with the *Hooge Regeering* at [[Batavia]] vetoed the prince-consort proposal. The widow became Sultana in her own right. See [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)]].

## The jewel negotiations of 1641

The most protracted business of [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|1641 embassy]] was the disposal of the late Sultan's outstanding jewel-order. The Sultana was not liable for the order placed by her late husband, and the treasury would not authorise the 15,000 *[[Tayl|tayl]]* (= 5,000 guilders) it would have cost. The *Rijksgrooten* — the *Orangkaja's* — had to be lobbied to give their consent to the purchase. To win them to a friendly disposition, "heel wat bezoeken" — many a visit — were paid and "menig geschenk" — many a gift — delivered at their houses by the *Secretaris* [[Jan van Riebeeck|Jan van Riebeeck]]. After long haggling a price was agreed for the greater part of the parcel, but for the future the import of jewels by the Company was forbidden in Atjeh.

## Ceremonial precedence at the Sultana's court

The *Orangkaja's* had their own ceremonial precedence at the Sultana's court. At the feast following the formal audience of [[1 July]] [[1641]] (see [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)]]) the members of the Dutch legation were served the delicacies in golden dishes, the *Orangkaja's* in silver — a precise ranking that placed the visiting embassy above the resident nobility, but only just. At the *lusthof* (pleasure-garden) feast of [[14 July]] [[1641]] the *Orangkaja's* "verbaasden zich over haar bijzondere vriendelijkheid jegens de Nederlanders" — marvelled at the Sultana's particular friendliness to the Dutch — a reaction that suggests they were accustomed to a more measured royal style.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *Orangkaja's* are the constitutional counterweight to the Sultan — the council of nobles whose consent was required for succession, treaty, and major commercial transaction. Their role in the [[1641]] succession — choosing the widow, vetoing the prince-consort, controlling the sale of the late Sultan's jewels — is a measure of how much power the office held. The ritual distinction between golden dishes (for the Dutch legation) and silver dishes (for the *Orangkaja's*) at the Sultana's banquet is the ceremonial expression of the same constitutional reality: the *Orangkaja's* were essential to the government of the sultanate, but they were not the Sultan.

**Dwangarbeiders** (forced labourers) were the conscript or corvée labourers used by the Dutch army in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] to perform engineer and construction work, as distinct from the [[Vrije koelies|vrije koelies]] (free, hired coolies). The dwangarbeiders were used in the later engineer operations of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the [[Lamara Olejlo fortification (15 February 1875)|Lamara Olejlo fortification of 15 February]] [[1875]] is the first recorded instance in [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s Atjeh service — and continued in use for the peacetime engineer works at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] throughout [[1875]].

## Use in Atjeh

The first recorded use of dwangarbeiders in [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s Atjeh service is at the [[Lamara Olejlo fortification (15 February 1875)|Lamara Olejlo fortification of 15 February]] [[1875]], where Snijders was ordered to march "met een gedeelte van de compagnie en eene afdeeling van 100 dwangarbeiders met eene hoeveelheid gereedschappen en zandzakken naar laatstgenoemde plaats te marcheeren en de genomen benting in staat van verdediging te brengen." The substitution of dwangarbeiders for the [[Vrije koelies|vrije koelies]] used at [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam]] and [[Missigit Longbatta operation (31 December 1874 – 13 January 1875)|Missigit Longbatta]] marks a change in the labour regime; the biographer [[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] does not explain the change, but the use of forced labour would have been attractive where the hiring of vrije koelies had become difficult or costly.

By the end of [[1874]] Snijders, in his peacetime Staf der Genie work at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]], "beschikte [...] over zijne mineurs, over een groot aantal einde 1874 900 vrije koelies en soms bovendien nog over soldaten en dwangarbeiders" — that is, he commanded soldiers and dwangarbeiders in addition to his mineurs and the 900 vrije koelies. The dwangarbeiders thus formed one element of a mixed labour force.

## Distinction from Vrije Koelies

The distinction between dwangarbeiders and [[Vrije koelies|vrije koelies]] is preserved in the wording of the biographer and reflects a real difference in the labour regime. The vrije koelies were hired hands who could in principle leave; the dwangarbeiders were conscript or corvée labour who could not. The biographer does not record the ethnic origin of the dwangarbeiders used at [[Lamara Olejlo|Lamara Olejlo]] — they may have been Atjehese corvée labour, or they may have been drawn from the penal labour force used elsewhere in the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Netherlands Indies]].

**Dwangarbeiders** (forced labourers) were the conscript or corvée labourers used by the Dutch army in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] to perform engineer and construction work, as distinct from the [[Vrije koelies|vrije koelies]] (free, hired coolies). The dwangarbeiders were used in the later engineer operations of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the [[Lamara Olejlo fortification (15 February 1875)|Lamara Olejlo fortification of 15 February]] [[1875]] is the first recorded instance in [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s Atjeh service — and continued in use for the peacetime engineer works at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] throughout [[1875]]. Together with the hired *koelie* (coolie), the native foreman *mandoer*, the litter-bearer *tandoes*, the provisions (*vivres*) and the baggage-column (*trein*), the dwangarbeider formed the indispensable labour-and-logistics infrastructure of Dutch military operations in [[Aceh]] through the first semester of [[1896]].

## Use in Atjeh

The first recorded use of dwangarbeiders in [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s Atjeh service is at the [[Lamara Olejlo fortification (15 February 1875)|Lamara Olejlo fortification of 15 February]] [[1875]], where Snijders was ordered to march "met een gedeelte van de compagnie en eene afdeeling van 100 dwangarbeiders met eene hoeveelheid gereedschappen en zandzakken naar laatstgenoemde plaats te marcheeren en de genomen benting in staat van verdediging te brengen." The substitution of dwangarbeiders for the [[Vrije koelies|vrije koelies]] used at [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam]] and [[Missigit Longbatta operation (31 December 1874 – 13 January 1875)|Missigit Longbatta]] marks a change in the labour regime; the biographer [[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] does not explain the change, but the use of forced labour would have been attractive where the hiring of vrije koelies had become difficult or costly.

By the end of [[1874]] Snijders, in his peacetime Staf der Genie work at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]], "beschikte [...] over zijne mineurs, over een groot aantal einde 1874 900 vrije koelies en soms bovendien nog over soldaten en dwangarbeiders" — that is, he commanded soldiers and dwangarbeiders in addition to his mineurs and the 900 vrije koelies. The dwangarbeiders thus formed one element of a mixed labour force.

## Distinction from Vrije Koelies

The distinction between dwangarbeiders and [[Vrije koelies|vrije koelies]] is preserved in the wording of the biographer and reflects a real difference in the labour regime. The vrije koelies were hired hands who could in principle leave; the dwangarbeiders were conscript or corvée labour who could not. The biographer does not record the ethnic origin of the dwangarbeiders used at [[Lamara Olejlo|Lamara Olejlo]] — they may have been Atjehese corvée labour, or they may have been drawn from the penal labour force used elsewhere in the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Netherlands Indies]].

## Account from Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — Labour and Logistics in 1896

**Dwangarbeider**, **Koelie**, **Mandoer**, **Tandoes**, *vivres* (rations) and **Trein** were the principal [[Dutch]] colonial labour and logistics terms employed in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as described in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*. The forced labourer, often a convict, was the basic unit of Dutch military labour in Aceh — used for transport, trenching, carrying stretchers for the wounded, and engineering work. The *koelie*, including the *Chineesche koelie* (Chinese coolie), was a hired or contracted labourer: 400 Chinese coolies were used for the [[razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] of [[30 May]]–[[2 June]] [[1896]]. The *mandoer* (native foreman) supervised gangs of dwangarbeiders and coolies: "14 mandoers en 325 dwangarbeiders" is the standard formulation. The *tandoes* was the litter or stretcher for wounded. The *vivres* (provisions) and the *vivres-colonne* (supply column) sustained the mobile columns. The *trein* (baggage or transport column) moved the column's logistical echelon.

### Etymology and Meaning

The Dutch colonial labour nomenclature is largely descriptive. *Dwangarbeider* is a compound of *dwang* (compulsion, force) + *arbeider* (labourer) — literally "forced labourer". The dwangarbeider was typically a convict sentenced to forced labour in the colonies, deployed under military guard for public works, transport and engineering tasks. *Koelie* is from Tamil *kūli* (wages, hired labourer), borrowed via Malay; the term denoted hired or contracted manual labourers, often Chinese or Javanese. *Chineesche koelie* specifically denotes Chinese coolies, typically contracted for large-scale engineering or razing work. *Mandoer* is from Portuguese *mandador* (one who commands), via Malay; it denotes the native foreman of a labour gang. *Tandoes* is from Portuguese *andas* (litter, stretcher), via Malay. *Vivres* is French (singular *vivres*, "victuals", provisions), used in Dutch military parlance. *Trein* is Dutch for "train" — the baggage or transport column of a military force. *Lamoen* / *lamoenen* is from Malay *lamung* (pack-saddle, pannier). *Voorwagen* is Dutch for "fore-wagon" or limber.

### Historical Context of 1896

In the first semester of [[1896]] Dutch colonial labour and logistics were the indispensable infrastructure of military operations in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Each mobile column was accompanied by its transport column (*trein*), with *dwangarbeiders* carrying stretchers for the wounded, provisions (*vivres*) for the troops, and ammunition for the firearms. The *mandoers* supervised the labour gangs — "14 mandoers en 325 dwangarbeiders" being a typical complement. The 400 *Chineesche koelies* used for the [[razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] of [[30 May]]–[[2 June]] [[1896]] were a specialised force for the large-scale destruction of kampongs and sawahs. The *tandoes* were essential for casualty evacuation — wounded being carried from the battlefield to the field hospital at the [[Geconcentreerde Linie|geconcentreerde linie]]. The *vivres* supply, often delivered by *vivres-colonne* from the military railway to the forward posts, determined the duration of any operation.

## Significance

The Dutch colonial labour and logistics infrastructure was the material precondition of Dutch military operations in [[Aceh]]. Without the *dwangarbeiders*, *koelies* and *mandoers*, no column could move, no casualty could be evacuated, no post could be provisioned. The system's reliance on coerced and contracted non-European labour — Chinese, Javanese, Achehnese convict — was a defining feature of Dutch colonial warfare, structurally linking the military campaign to the broader colonial economy of extraction. The *tandoes* bearer who carried a wounded European soldier from the battlefield embodied the racial hierarchy of the colonial army. The 400 *Chineesche koelies* at [[Lam-Asan]] — hired for the specific task of destroying an Achehnese kampong — illustrate the linkage of labour, logistics and destruction in the *tuchtiging* (punitive expedition) mode of warfare. The system's survival into the twentieth century and its formalisation under the *pacificatie* (pacification) policies of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] testify to its institutional importance.

**Field Hospital and Cholera Epidemics in Aceh** was the system of military medical installations and the devastating outbreaks of waterborne diseases, principally cholera and dysentery, that afflicted the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] during operations in the [[Aceh Sultanate]] between [[1873]] and [[1876]].

## Sanitary Conditions Following the Capture of the Kraton

After the occupation of [[Kraton of Aceh]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], Commander-in-Chief General [[J. van Swieten]] established the primary military field hospital within the walls of the royal palace. Because the interior of the kraton was low-lying, poorly drained, and surrounded by marshy terrain, sanitary conditions deteriorated rapidly.

As documented by the anonymous author of *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* ([[1876]]), the kraton hospital became severely overcrowded with sick and wounded soldiers. A virulent cholera epidemic swept through the garrison in early [[1874]], inflicting higher casualties on European and indigenous troops than combat engagements with Acehnese defenders.

## Reorganization Under General Pel

When General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] assumed command in April [[1874]], he recognized that overcrowding in the kraton hospital endangered the entire occupation force. He ordered the construction of elevated, well-ventilated hospital barracks and wooden sheds at [[Pantei Perak]] along the Aceh River, as well as at coastal bases in [[Oleh-leh]] and [[Meuraxa]]. 

During [[General Pel's Campaign in Aceh]] in [[1875]] and [[1876]], the [[Medical Service in Aceh]] maintained strict hygiene protocols and limited the deployment of infantry battalions in the field to minimize disease attrition.

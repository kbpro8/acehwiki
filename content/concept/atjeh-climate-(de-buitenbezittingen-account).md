**The Atjeh climate**, as systematically described in the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), is the climate of the *gewest* [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] as treated in Dr. W. van Bemmelen's Hoofdstuk II of the Bureau's survey — the first systematic modern climate description of Sumatra, dividing the island into seven longitudinal and transverse *klimaat-provinciën* and treating the Atjeh west coast, north coast, Sabang and inland highlands in detail. In the Bureau's account, Atjeh's climate is the climatic *closure* of Sumatra at its northern extremity — the *gewest* whose position at the *raaklijn* of the great half-yearly wind systems produces a distinctive double-regenval pattern.

## The *klimaat-provinciën* of Atjeh

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk II divides Atjeh into the following *klimaat-provinciën*, on the basis of the *hoofdmaximum* and *nevenmaximum* of the regenval:

- **Noordkust van Atjeh**: the Westelijke tak of the Noord-Oost moesson reaches the Noord-Oost coast as an Oostenwind, "begint met in November en December regen te brengen; later pas, in Januari, wordt ze droog, omdat ze dan uit sterk afgekoelde streken komt." The hoofdmaximum of the Noordkust falls in December.
- **Westkust van Atjeh**: the Zuid-West moesson "waait als Zuid-West moesson langs de Noordspits van Atjeh, maar wordt op Atjeh's Westkust door de bergketen omgebogen tot Westen- en Noord-Westenwinden." The hoofdmaximum of the Westkust falls in September at the northernmost section, in October further south, and in November along the entire remaining coast.
- **Sabang**: the hoofdmaximum of Sabang falls in December, together with the Westkust as far as Koeta-Radja.
- **Binnenland of Atjeh**: the hoofdmaximum of the *binnenland* — at [[Takengon|Takéngon]] and [[Blangkedjeren|Blang Kédjérèn]] — falls in November, earlier than the *kust* stations.
- **Noord-Atjeh *nevenmaximum***: the *nevenmaximum* of Noord-Atjeh falls in Mei at the *kust*, with a small stretch of Westkust (P. Raja, Tjalang) shifted to Juli.
- **Binnenland of Atjeh *nevenmaximum***: the *nevenmaximum* of the *binnenland* falls in April, as over the greater part of Sumatra.

## The double-regenval pattern

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk II emphasises that "op Sumatra een dubbele regentijd moet verwacht worden" — that is, Sumatra must expect a double regenval. The *hoofdmaximum* and *nevenmaximum* of the regenval are the two peaks of this double pattern. The *hoofdmaximum* falls predominantly in the second half of the year, "na het doorgang van de zon door het zenith, gaande van Noord naar Zuid" — that is, after the sun's passage through the zenith, going from north to south. The *nevenmaximum* falls predominantly in the first half of the year.

The Bureau notes that the *hoofdmaximum* of the Westkust of Atjeh "valt al in September" at the northernmost section, "verder Zuidwaarts, aan de grens van Tapanoeli, in October, en langs het geheele overige deel der kust in November." The *hoofdmaximum* of the Westkust thus shows a clear north-to-south progression — the principal evidence of the *aequatoriale* character of the Westkust regenval.

## The *Föhn* wind of the binnenland

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk II identifies the *Föhn* wind of the *binnenland* as the principal climatic anomaly of the Atjeh *hoogvlakte*: "een krachtige luchtstrooming gaat dan, vooral in de maanden Juli en Augustus, over de hoogvlakten van het gebergte en wanneer die gedwongen wordt door de dalen naar de kustvlakten af te dalen, wordt zij als een echte „Föhn" wind warm en droog. De Bohorok in Deli is hiervan een voorbeeld." The *Föhn* wind of the *binnenland* is thus the principal climatic connection between the Atjeh *hoogvlakte* and the Deli *oostkust* — a connection that links the Atjeh *binnenland*'s July–August *Föhn* to the Deli *oostkust*'s dry spell.

## Significance

The Atjeh climate is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the climatic *closure* of Sumatra at its northern extremity. The Bureau's emphasis on the double-regenval pattern, on the north-to-south progression of the *hoofdmaximum*, and on the *Föhn* wind of the *binnenland*, frames the Atjeh climate as the principal climatic instance of the *gewest*'s position at the *raaklijn* of the great half-yearly wind systems. The Bureau's account is the canonical pre-war description of the Atjeh climate, and the principal source for all subsequent Dutch colonial writing on the climate of the *gewest*.

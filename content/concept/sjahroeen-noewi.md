**Sjahroe'n Noewi** is the name given in chapter VIII of the [[Sejarah Melayu]] to a great kingdom on [[Sumatra]]'s east coast, which P. de Roo de La Faille identifies with the [[Aroe Kingdom]] centred at [[Koewala Boeboen]]/[[Tandjoeng Poera]], and with the *Cernovem*, *Sornau* and *Xarnauz* of European travellers.

## Sejarah Melayu account

The chronicle describes Sjahroe'n Noewi as a very large realm with many champions and innumerable people. Told that [[Pasej|Samoedra]] (Samudera-Pasai) was a lively mart, Raja Sjahroe'n Noewi asked his war-chiefs for someone to bring him the raja of Samoedra. He sent his *hoeloebalang* Awi Ditjoe to Samoedra with a *peti* (chest). Awi Ditjoe returned with the captured Raja of Pasai, whom the king "ordered to herd chickens" — in Malay wordplay, *menggembala ayam*, decoded by de Roo as *meng-gembala Pasaj semajam*, an allegory for the originally unified but later warring pair of northern Muslim [[Pasej]] and eastern Hindu [[Aroe Kingdom|Aroe]].

## European identifications

- **Nicolo de' Conti** (c. [[1430]]), sailing from Tenasserim into the Strait, entered the mouth of the "Ganges" and after fifteen days upstream reached a "civitatem nomine Cernovem insignem atque opulentam". De Roo argues this broad river with giant reeds and great fish abundance is not the Ganges but the Sumatran river leading to Aroe, and *Cernovem* is Sjahroe'n Noewi.
- **[[Mendez Pinto's Aroe mission (1539)|Mendez Pinto]]** calls the same polity *Sornau*, a name he applies to Malay kingdoms on the peninsula formerly subject to [[Siam]].
- **Vasco da Gama's Roteiro** lists *Xarnauz*, a land of "Christians" with much benzoin and aloes — for de Roo, *ks-ir arnawa noe-sa*, "the island in the sea", where a fountain flowed balsam, i.e. the white *minjak tanah* of [[Aroe Kingdom|Aroe]].
- **Hobson-Jobson** equates Sornau/Cernovem with Xarnauz.

De Roo places the capital at *Kwala Boeboen* on the river of Tandjoengpoera, a temporary refuge for people driven from Langkawi by Siamese, ruled by a *padoeka* who was in fact the raja of Tandjoengpoera-Aroe.

## Economy

De Conti described at "Marahatian" great stores of aloes-wood, gold, silver, gems and pearls. Ibn Battuta, visiting the region in [[1345]]–[[1346]], likewise noted a Hindu kingdom (*Moel Djawa*) with elephants, stone walls, cloves and nutmeg on its market — signs, for de Roo, of regular trade with the Moluccas via Aroe's entrepôt.

The famed product was *minjak tanah* from [[Pangkalan Susu]], the whitish earth-oil loaded at the landing-place whose name de Roo renders as *moeware sija-na-ma-tara*.

## Interpretation

For de Roo, Sjahroe'n Noewi is not a separate empire but veiled speech for *Sija-hroe-n-a-ma noe-sa Widjaja* — the "new" (*noewi*) Aroe under the sign of *Sijama Tara*. The name survived in multiple corruptions because Malay court authors deliberately used *sandi* (esoteric) language to conceal politically sensitive genealogies. The name's connection to the goddess [[Poetri Idjo|Cyama-Tara]] — through *sijama* ("dark-green") and *noewi* ("new") — is the basis of de Roo's identification of the Sejarah Melayu's great east-coast kingdom with the [[Aroe Kingdom]] and its pre-Islamic Hindu-Buddhist tradition.

**Framboesia** — yaws, the disfiguring treponemal skin disease — was the *volksgeesel* (popular scourge) whose combating had formerly stood at the centre of the work of the *[[Dienst der Volksgezondheid]]* in [[Groot-Atjeh]], and against which the striking successes obtained with *salvarsan* had, in Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]]'s account, won the confidence of the Acehnese population for Western medicine.

## The campaign and its consequence

Gomperts opened his report by placing the eye survey in this history: "Terwijl de framboesiabestrijding vroeger in het middelpunt van de belangstelling stond, hetgeen een logisch gevolg is van de frappante successen met salvarsan bereikt bij dezen volksgeesel, is in den loop der jaren ook voor andere volksziekten de belangstelling gegroeid en hoe langer hoe meer het vertrouwen van deze bevolkingsgroep in de Westersche geneeswijze gewonnen."

The campaign against yaws was thus, in his narrative, the precondition of the eye survey: it had established the credibility of Western medicine among a population that had until then avoided it, and made possible the extension of the service's work to other popular diseases in the five years before [[1937]].

## Among the eye patients

Framboesia recurs in the survey's own data. Among the *kampong* xerophthalmia cases it was recorded in 8 per cent of the boys and 3 per cent of the girls; among the general body of children examined, in about 2½ per cent of boys and 3 per cent of girls. Among the eighteen keratomalacia children nursed in the [[Militair Hospitaal te Koetaradja]] in [[1937]], two had framboesia; and framboesia figures among the infections recorded in the xerophthalmia patients nursed there during [[1936]] and [[1937]].

Gomperts nevertheless concluded that "de invloed van infectieziekten en infecties op het ontstaan van xerophthalmie niet duidelijk tot uiting komt" — the influence of infectious diseases and infections on the origin of *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* does not clearly express itself.

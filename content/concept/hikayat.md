**Hikayat** — Dutch/Atjehnese spelling of standard Indonesian *hikayat*, from Arabic *ḥikāya* (حكاية), "story, narrative, tale" — is the principal form of classical Malay prose, a generic term covering a very wide variety of prose works: prose romances of Hindu or Persian origin more or less Muslim in colouring, prose histories of Malay royal houses, prose memoirs and travel journals, prose legends of Muslim saints, and even prose collections of animal fables. The form is named in Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]]'s chapter *Omtrekken van de Indonesische litteratuur* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]).

## Van Naerssen's account

Van Naerssen introduces the *hikayat* in his survey of the genres of Malay literature: "Of the very great variety of Malay literary genres, we can here only name a few. Very diverse prose works are denoted with the Arabic word *hikayat*. For example the [[Hikayat Hang Tuah|*Hikayat Hang Tuah*]], in which the adventures of the Malay hero of that name are described; many romances of Hindu or Persian origin more or less Muslim in colouring, such as the Amir Hamzah stories of the Muslim knight; reworkings of the stories of the Thousand and One Nights; but also historiographies, such as the *Hikayat raja-raja Pasei* (of the Pase kings); memoirs and travel journals of Abdoelah bin Abdoelkadir, *moensji* (i.e. language teacher), which saw the light in Singapore about a century ago, strongly influenced by the European environment; Muslim saints' legends; yes, even the above-named dwarf-deer stories."

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *hikayat* is significant as the prose counterpart to the [[Sja'ir|*sja'ir*]] (the poetic form), together constituting the two principal literary forms in which the rich Atjeh literary treasure was composed. Van Naerssen's specific Atjeh reference, in the same chapter, names "the several versions of the [[Hikayat prang sabi|*hikayat prang sabi*]], books on the holy war, one of which ends with the words: 'How many traditions of the Prophet say it: fighting along God's way stands high reward, great reward and high honour, for God grants on the day of battle the Paradise'." The Atjeh *hikayat* tradition also includes the [[Hikajat Sultan Atjeh Marhoem]] (the *Hikayat of the late Sultan of Atjeh*), the [[Hikajat Eitia Toedjoh|*Hikayat Eitia Toedjoh*]] and the [[Hikayat Pochut Muhamat|*Hikayat Pochut Muhamat*]] — all of which are prose narratives in the classical Malay *hikayat* form. The mention, by Van Naerssen, of the *Hikayat raja-raja Pasei* (of the Pase kings) further places the [[Pasei|Pase]] region — the immediate predecessor of Atjeh on the north Sumatran coast — at the head of the historical *hikayat* tradition in Malay.

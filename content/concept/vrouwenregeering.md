**Vrouwenregeering** ("female rule") was the period of female Sultans in [[Aceh|Atjèh]], beginning with [[Tadjoel-Alam Safijatoeddin|Tadjoel-Alam Safijatoeddin]] in [[1641]] and lasting until [[1699]]. Kreemer identifies the beginning of the *vrouwenregeering* as "the first step of Atjèh's decline" from its zenith under [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Iskandar Moeda]].

## The end of havenkoningen

With the beginning of female rule in [[1641]], "het régime der havenkoningen had toen voor goed afgedaan" — "the régime of the harbour-kings had then for good ended." The Sultan no longer possessed the *alleenhandel* (trade monopoly) but "had to content himself with the proceeds of import/export duties."

Since wealth determined the Sultan's internal influence, the position of the female rulers could no longer equal that of their glorious predecessors. The *havenkoningen* (harbour-kings) — the coastal chieftains of Atjèh's former vassal statelets who controlled maritime trade — also lost their former role.

## The female Sultans

The *vrouwenregeering* comprised a succession of female Sultans, of whom Kreemer specifically names:
- **[[Tadjoel-Alam Safijatoeddin|Tadjoel-Alam Safijatoeddin]]** (reigned [[1641]]–[[1675]]) — the first queen, whose ninefold seal (*[[tjab sikoereuëng|tjab sikoereuëng]]*) is reproduced on the cover of Kreemer's work
- **Sultan Kamalat Sjah** (reigned [[1688]]–[[1699]]) — *deureuham* gold coins from her reign exist

The *vrouwenregeering* ended in [[1699]], after which male rule was restored — though the sultanate never recovered its former power.

## Numismatic evidence

*[[Deureuham|Deureuham]]* gold coins from the reigns of Sultan Ala addin Moeghajat Sjah ([[1636]]–[[1641]]), Sultan Inajat Sjah ([[1678]]–[[1688]]), and Sultan Kamalat Sjah ([[1688]]–[[1699]]) provide numismatic evidence for the *vrouwenregeering* period and its immediate predecessors.

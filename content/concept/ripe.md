**Ripe** was the Toba-Batak term for the dependants or clients whom a *[[Radja Hoeta|Radja (ni) Hoeta]]* sought to attach to himself in building up his *harajaon* (chieftaincy). [[D. W. N. de Boer]], writing in *[[Het recht op den grond in Atjeh (De Boer)|Het recht op den grond in Atjeh]]*, explained that a Batak village *radja* — and above all one founding a new village — needed "uitgelezen, liefst tot dezelfde marga behoorende ripe's," picked *ripe*'s, preferably belonging to the same *marga*, in order to maintain or extend his chieftaincy; and that the *[[Beschikkingsrecht in Atjeh|beschikkingsrecht]]* over land was the instrument through which such men were bound to him, as *[[Parripe|parripe]]*'s on issued or re-issued land.

## Comparative use

The concept served De Boer's argument negatively: because the Atjehnese *[[Keradjaan|keradjaans]]* had arisen territorially and not genealogically, the [[Oeleebalang|oeleebalang]] of [[Aceh|Atjeh]] had no comparable need to recruit and bind kin-clients, and consequently no need for a vigilant right of avail.

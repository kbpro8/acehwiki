**Rubber Industry in Atjeh** is a significant chapter in the modern history of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]], characterised by the rapid expansion of large-scale commercial agriculture focused on the cultivation of *Hevea brasiliensis*, following the region's pacification. A key catalyst for this development was the establishment of a government rubber plantation near [[Langsa]], which proved the viability of the crop and sparked a boom in private concessions, transforming the southeastern coastal plains into a major cultivation area during the early twentieth century.

## The Role of the Government Plantation

The direct involvement of the colonial government in rubber cultivation in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] was a unique initiative. Around [[1907]], the government began clearing land near [[Langsa]], initially testing the cultivation of *Ficus elastica* and later transitioning to *Hevea brasiliensis*.

- By [[1908]], 329 *bouws* had been planted with 76,000 trees.
- In [[1909]], the plantation was expanded to a total of 476 *bouws* with 116,000 trees.
- The total area allocated for potential expansion was 8,900 *bouws*.

This government enterprise was a pioneering project in a region that was, until recently, largely inaccessible and often hostile. Staffed with contract laborers — mostly Javanese, with some local Acehnese — it represented a significant investment of approximately 800,000 guilders by [[1914]]. The *Gouvernements-rubber-onderneming te Langsa* served as a working model and proof of concept, demonstrating to private investors that the climate and soil of Aceh's east coast were suitable for rubber cultivation.

## The Private Boom

The success of the government's project, combined with immense global demand for rubber, triggered an enormous surge in private investment. The number of land concessions in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] grew exponentially:

- [[1904]]: 6 concessions with a total area of ±1,000 *bouws*.
- [[1908]]: 36 concessions covering ±2,600 *bouws*.
- [[1913]]: 148 concessions covering nearly 300,000 *bouws*.

Many of these were concentrated in the under-subdivision of [[Langsa]], where 28 concessions alone covered 54,000 *bouws*. Most new enterprises were established for rubber cultivation, with robusta coffee often planted as a "catch crop" to provide initial income. This explosive transformation turned a region described as "vast, nearly impassable swampy areas" into a "rich and well-developed agricultural area."

## Types of Producers

Beyond the large European enterprises, the rubber industry in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Aceh]] involved several other actors:

1. **The Government:** The plantation at [[Langsa]] was the most significant state enterprise in the rubber sector in the Indies — a unique colonial experiment in direct agricultural investment.
2. **Inland Population:** While the Acehnese population was less prominent in rubber cultivation than in other regions like [[Palembang]], there was some indigenous production. In [[1913]], total production from indigenous rubber gardens in areas including Atjeh, Benkoelen, Tapanoeli, [[Palembang]], [[Oostkust van Sumatra]], and Westerafdeeling van Borneo was approximately 46,000 kg. The sale of wild rubber was also a source of income for the Acehnese population.
3. **European Capital:** Most private investment came from European and increasingly foreign companies, with the region attracting capital from the Netherlands, Great Britain, and the United States.

## Production and Future Prospects

The *Hevea* trees planted around [[1910]] would begin producing latex after approximately four years, reaching full production potential at around ten years of age. By [[1918]], the government plantation at [[Langsa]] was projected to yield approximately 20,000 kg of dry rubber annually, with a potential profit of around 200,000 guilders per year. The booming private sector, particularly in the southeastern regions of the Aceh Governorate, was set to contribute massively to the global rubber supply and become a cornerstone of the region's economy in the 1920s and beyond.

## Significance

The rubber industry was the primary motor for the economic "opening up" of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] after its conquest. The government's pioneering plantation at [[Langsa]] acted as a catalyst, proving the region's potential and attracting massive foreign and domestic investment. This rapid agricultural development transformed the physical geography of the region from swampland to cultivated plantations, created a new socio-economic order dependent on wage labor, and integrated Aceh's economy tightly into the global capitalist system. It stands as a powerful symbol of the post-war colonial strategy of extracting economic value from conquered territories through modern, capital-intensive agriculture.

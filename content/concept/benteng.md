The **[[Benteng|benteng]]** (Malay/Indonesian *benteng*, "fortification") was the standard Atjehnese fortified strongpoint — an earthen or timber work, sometimes armed with artillery, constructed to defend a kampong, a strategic position or a line of communication. Borel mentions bentengs throughout *Onze vestiging in Atjeh* as the characteristic Atjehnese fortification, often concealed behind [[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboo-doeri]] thickets and sometimes mounting [[Lilla|lillas]] or heavier cannon. The benteng was to be reduced, in Borel's doctrine, by artillery fire before storming, or taken by *omtrekking* (flanking and encircling) — the "overoude Indische tactiek" that almost always yielded surprising results.

## Construction and Varieties

The benteng was, in Borel's account, an earthen or timber fortification, typically square or rectangular in plan, with a parapet high enough to shelter a man, and sometimes with a dry or wet gracht and a bamboo-doeri thicket around the perimeter. Some bentengs were armed with artillery — lillas, or in some cases heavier cannon — and some were of stone, like the [[Longbatta-Mesigit|Longbatta-Mesigit]] (a stone mosque converted into a strongpoint) or the [[Mandarsa Poeti|gebedenhuis at Mandarsa Poeti]] (an old stone temple converted into a fortification). The benteng at [[Lampoe Oek]] — where [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]] had his [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|échec]] on [[16 April]] [[1874]] — was a strongly fortified enemy stelling that the Dutch had known nothing of; the benteng at [[Garouw]] — attacked by Romswinckel on [[28 June]] [[1874]] — was a "well-laid fortification" that was surrounded and, after brave resistance, taken. The benteng on which [[Kapitein van Aerssen|kapitein van Aerssen]] conquered an iron cannon on [[15 April]] [[1874]] was a typical example. The bentengs were, in effect, the Atjehnese equivalent of the European field fortification — cheap, effective, locally constructed, and adapted to the conditions of woodland fighting in Atjeh.

## Reduction by Artillery and Omtrekking

The benteng was, in Borel's doctrine, to be reduced by artillery fire before storming, or taken by *omtrekking*. The [[Destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification (18 September 1874)|destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification]] on [[18 September]] [[1874]] — when the 12 c.M. [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanon]] "soon found range and demolished parapet, overthrowing schanskorven" — was a model of reduction by artillery. The destruction of Mandarsa Poeti on [[5 October]] [[1874]] — when "a few shots from 12 c.M. achterlaadkanon established range; then whole front wall brought down" — was another. The destruction of the enemy benteng south of K. Radja on [[29 August]] [[1874]] — when, from the high observatory at [[Kota Podjoet|K. Podjoet]], range and direction were signalled to the zuider-face battery, and shells soon fell inside the work — was a third. Where artillery was not available, or where the benteng could not be approached frontally, the *omtrekking* — the flanking and encircling movement that Borel calls the "overoude Indische tactiek" — was the alternative. The [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] by [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]] that delivered the deserted Kraton to the Dutch was the largest single example; the smaller *omtrekkingen* of Pel's campaigns of [[1874]]–[[1876]] were many.

## The Benteng and the Stelling-Pel

The benteng was also the model for some of the Dutch posts of the [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]]. The post at [[Lampoe Oek|nipa-benteng]] — the Dutch post constructed at Lampoe Oek after the destruction of the enemy fortification on [[18 September]] [[1874]] — took its name from the Atjehnese word. The Dutch posts at [[Blang Tjoet]], [[Pendetti]] and elsewhere were often constructed on the site of, or from the materials of, conquered Atjehnese bentengs. The [[Occupation of the ooster-benteng (17 August 1874)|occupation of the ooster-benteng]] on [[17 August]] [[1874]] — when [[Kapitein van Randwijk|kapitein van Randwijk]] seized the grafheuvel east of [[Pakan Atjeh]] at double-quick pace by surprise, and sandbag parapets were thrown up — illustrates the process by which an Atjehnese strongpoint became a Dutch post. The benteng was thus both the characteristic Atjehnese fortification and the model for some of the Dutch posts that replaced it. The lesson Borel draws is that the Dutch, having conquered an Atjehnese benteng, should hold and fortify it, rather than abandon it as van Swieten had abandoned [[Lemboe]] on [[25 December|25]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]] — a "groote strategische fout" that had to be repaired at high cost in [[1874]].

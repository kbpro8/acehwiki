**The Atjehsche hoofden opleiding** — the training of sons of Atjehnese *hoofden* for the Dutch colonial *bestuurswerkkring* — was the administrative-educational programme instituted by the Dutch colonial state in the early 20th century under which a limited number of sons of Atjehnese *hoofden* were trained at the *kweekschool voor Inlandsche onderwijzers te Fort de Koek* (in Padang, West-Sumatra) for service in the *Inlandsch Bestuur* of the *gewest* Atjeh. In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), the Atjehsche hoofden opleiding is the principal Dutch programme for the indigenisation of the Atjeh *bestuursambtenaren* — and the principal evidence of the Dutch colonial state's recognition that the *pacification*'s consolidation required a trained Atjehnese bestuurskorps.

## The 1916 provision

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk III records the formal extension of the Atjehsche hoofden opleiding in [[1916]]: "tot het cursusjaar 1916/1917 ook aan de kweekschool voor Inlandsche onderwijzers te Fort de Koek hoogstens 20 zonen van Atjehsche hoofden konden worden opgeleid voor den bestuurswerkkring (G.B. van 27 Juni 1916 No. 7)." The G.B. (Gouvernementsbesluit) of 27 June [[1916]] No. 7 is thus the formal legal basis of the Atjehsche hoofden opleiding at the Fort de Koek kweekschool.

## The Bureau's judgment

The Bureau's account of the Atjehsche hoofden opleiding is notably critical. The Bureau notes that "dit is echter niet voldoende om in de toekomst in de behoefte aan opgeleide Inlandsche bestuursambtenaren te voorzien, terwijl bovendien de opleiding zelve herzien moet worden." The Bureau thus treats the [[1916]] provision as insufficient — both quantitatively (20 places were not enough to meet the future need) and qualitatively (the training itself required revision). The Bureau's criticism is part of its broader argument for the reform of the *Inlandsch Bestuur* of the Buitenbezittingen — an argument that culminated in the *Commissie van advies … nopens de opleiding van personeel voor den Inlandschen bestuursdienst* instituted by G.B. van 28 Juli [[1914]] No. 42, which reported in [[1915]].

## Significance

The Atjehsche hoofden opleiding is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the principal Dutch programme for the indigenisation of the Atjeh *bestuursambtenaren*, and the principal evidence of the Dutch colonial state's recognition that the *pacification*'s consolidation required a trained Atjehnese bestuurskorps. The Bureau's emphasis on the [[1916]] provision's insufficiency — and on the need for revision of the training itself — frames the Atjehsche hoofden opleiding as the staff history's principal instance of the structural underprovision of trained indigenous bestuursambtenaren in Atjeh: the colonial state's recognition of the need was not matched by the colonial state's provision of the training.

The Atjehsche hoofden opleiding also anticipates the post-*pacification* Dutch reliance on the Atjehnese *hoofden* — a reliance that would be vindicated, in the negative, by the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]] rising of February [[1942]], when the *hoofden*'s loyalty to the Dutch colonial state proved insufficient to prevent the Atjehnese population's mobilisation by the Japanese-sponsored fifth column.

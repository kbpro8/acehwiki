**Bezoldiging** — the payment of a fixed salary to Achehnese chiefs and fighters — was the principal financial instrument of Dutch policy in [[Groot-Atjeh]] during the alliance period of [[1893]]–[[1896]], and one of the recurring subjects of *[[De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh (Hooijer, 1897)|De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh]]*.

## Salary as the price of neutrality

[[General‑Major Van Teyn]] granted a salary to [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb|Toekoe Tjoet Toengkoeb]] *op voorwaarde, dat hij niet meer tegen ons zou vechten* — on condition that he should no longer fight against the Dutch — and only then did that chief, long allied with the priestly party of [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]], put away his *[[Klewang|klewang]]*.

## Salary as the price of service

[[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] maintained two hundred salaried warriors besides three subordinate commanders and forty *[[Panglima|panglima's]]*, and it was agreed on [[2 November]] [[1893]] that after driving the *[[Prang sabi|Sabil]]* bands from the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] he and his fighters would maintain order there *alweder tegen een vaste bezoldiging* — again against a fixed salary — and that should he fall, his brother and the bravest of his officers would succeed him in command.

## Failure of the instrument

Hooijer recorded that Toekoe Tjoet's subordinates refused to fight without the payment *die hij wel van ons voor hen ontving, maar hun onthield* — which he indeed received from the Dutch on their behalf but withheld from them. The provision of arms and money to Achehnese auxiliaries was, in Hooijer's retrospective judgment, part of the *ondeugdelijken grondslag* on which the whole outpost system had been raised.

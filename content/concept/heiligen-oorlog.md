**The Heiligen Oorlog** — *Holy War*, *Jihad*, in Atjehese *Sabilillah* — was the religious war preached by the Atjehese religious leaders (*teungku*s) against the Dutch from the beginning of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[1873]] and continuously throughout the war. The preaching of the holy war by the religious leaders, the formal oath against the Dutch taken by the principal chiefs, and the constitution of the body of *[[Muslemin|Muslemin]] — those who had openly taken part in the holy war — were the principal religious-political instruments by which the Atjehese resistance was sustained through the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] and the subsequent governorships.

## The Preaching (March 1881 onwards)

The religious leaders had already begun to preach the holy war in [[March 1881|March]] [[1881]], in the early months of the [[Civil Government Period (1881-1884)|civil government period]] under [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]]. The preaching was skilfully aroused and fanned by the religious leaders, and contributed to the reorganisation of the war party under its known chiefs and to the loss of confidence in the Dutch on the part of the population. The preaching of the holy war was one of the principal instruments by which the Atjehese resistance was sustained through the collapse of the Dutch position under civil government.

## The Return of Habib Abdul Rachman (August 1876)

Already in [[August 1876|August]] [[1876]], under the governorship of [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Wiggers van Kerchem]], [[Habib Abdul Rachman El-Zahir|Habib Abdul Rachman el-Zahir]] had returned to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and, landing on the [[Oostkust|East Coast]], had traversed the coast collecting money and recruiting men for the holy war against the Dutch. The Arab religious leader's role in the preaching and the recruitment for the holy war was one of the principal religious-diplomatic instruments of the early phase of the resistance.

## The Muslemin (1885 onwards)

After the proclamation of the [[Restoration of the Sultanate|restoration of the Sultanate]] at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] in [[1885]]–[[1886]], those who openly took part in the holy war were henceforth called *[[Muslemin|Muslemin]]*. The principal leaders — including [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] and [[Nja Makam|Nja Makam]] — bound themselves afresh under oath against the Dutch, in concert with the religious leader [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] whose influence ever increased. The Sultan's emissaries travelled the coastal statelets to call all to the holy war against the Dutch and to collect money and men.

## Snouck Hurgronje's Diagnosis

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], in his mission to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] of [[July 1891|July]] [[1891]]–[[February 1892|February]] [[1892]], diagnosed the Dutch situation as one of a fanatical popular party led by the clergy against the Dutch — not merely a few malefactors or robber bands, as [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe van Meerdervoort]] had maintained. The religious leadership of the holy war — exercised by [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] until his death in [[January 1891|January]] [[1891]], and thereafter by his son and successor [[Tengkoe Mohamed Amin|Tengkoe Mohamed Amin]] — was, in Snouck Hurgronje's analysis, the principal obstacle to the Dutch policy of negotiated submission. The Dutch's eventual suppression of the religious leadership — under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]], on the advice of Snouck Hurgronje — would be the principal means by which the holy war was brought to an end.

## Kruijt's Characterisation

Kruijt characterises the Atjehese resistance as one of "groote geslepenheid en volharding, grooten nationalen trots en onverdelgbaren haat tegen den indringer" (great cunning and perseverance, great national pride and indestructible hatred of the invader). The religious leadership of the holy war was one of the principal means by which this resistance was sustained. The Dutch's failure to recognise the religious character of the resistance — and the consequent failure to take the measures necessary to suppress the religious leadership — was, in Kruijt's and Snouck Hurgronje's analysis, one of the principal causes of the protraction of the war.

## Significance

The Heiligen Oorlog is the religious-political framework within which the Atjehese resistance was sustained throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The preaching of the holy war by the religious leaders, the formal oath against the Dutch, the constitution of the body of Muslemin, and the religious leadership of [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and [[Tengkoe Mohamed Amin|Tengkoe Mohamed Amin]] together constitute the institutional structure of the resistance. The Dutch's eventual recognition of the religious character of the resistance — through Snouck Hurgronje's mission of [[1891]]–[[1892]] — and the consequent suppression of the religious leadership under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]] would be the principal means by which the war was brought to its conclusion.

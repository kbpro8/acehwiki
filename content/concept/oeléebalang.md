**The Oeléebalang** — also written *oelébalang*, *ulébalang* — was the territorial chief of an Atjehnese *landschap*, the principal political institution of pre-colonial and early colonial Atjeh. The *oeléebalang* was at once the ruler, the judge and the military commander of his *landschap*; in Broersma's phrase, *'wie hen heeft, het volk heeft'* — *who has them, has the people.* The institution is the principal political fact with which the Dutch colonial government had to deal in the post-pacification period.

## Etymology and Origin
The word is composed of *oelèë* (or *uléë*), meaning *head*, and *balang*, meaning *army*; the *oeléebalang* is thus literally the *head of the army*. The title *oeléebalang tjoet* — *tjoet* meaning *small* — designates the sub-chief of a *moekim* or other subdivision of a *landschap*. The *oeléebalangs* were of old the military commanders of their *landschappen* and were at the same time the administrators and judges over their people. The title *[[Tjhi (title)|tjhi]]* — short for *keutihi* — was added to the personal name, as in *Teukoe Tjhi Perlak* or *Teukoe Tjhi Peusangan*.

## The Oeléebalang and the Sultan
The *oeléebalangs* of the regions outside [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] acknowledged the [[Sultan of Atjeh|sultan of Atjeh]] as their common over-lord, but without tangible rights on his part. The *oeléebalang* was, in his own *landschap*, an autocrat who would, if necessary, accept a letter of recognition from the sultan in Groot-Atjeh, but who otherwise governed his land according to his own insight. The political history of the regions outside Groot-Atjeh in the centuries after the Atjehnese expansion is the history of the gradual independence of the *oeléebalangs* from the sultan.

## The Oeléebalang and the Pepper-Trade
The *oeléebalangs* were mostly men of business and have always been so. If they liked fine things, the merchant from outside — the Chinese of [[Penang|Penang]] or [[Singapore|Singapore]] — knew what he had to offer. If he brought chiefs into debt, they had only to have the products of the land ready — pepper especially — to deliver at a favourable price. Pepper was the old *stapel* of the country. The *oeléebalang* was the privileged buyer of the pepper of his *landschap*; he was also the *pangkaigever*, the advance-capitalist of the pepper-planters, supplying the *pangkai* by which the planter lived in the years before his garden came into bearing.

## The *Wasé* (Hacil)
The *hacil* or *wasé* — the *heerlijk recht* (lordly right) — was the due paid to the *oeléebalang* on each *picol* of pepper produced in his *landschap*. The *wasé oeléebalang* was a ground-right, and when *'het landschap'* was instituted as a self-governing unit, the *wasé oeléebalang* belonged to the *landschapskas*. The *hacil* is now, in Broersma's account, a guilder per *picol*; the *pangkaigever* — usually the *oeléebalang* or an *oeléebalang tjoet* — receives f 2.50 per *picol*, of which he gives the *peutoeha* f 0.50. The *peutoeha* — the eldest, the representative of the *oeléebalang* in pepper-affairs — has in the course of the years grown from the leader of the pepper-planters into the *kepala kampong*, the village-head.

## The Oeléebalang and the Dutch Government
The Dutch government dealt with the *oeléebalangs* through the *Korte Verklaring (1899)|Korte Verklaring* of [[1899]], a short declaration in three articles which replaced the older *Groote Verklaring* in eighteen articles. By signing the *Korte Verklaring*, the *oeléebalang* acknowledged the suzerainty of the Dutch government and agreed to govern his *landschap* in accordance with the government's instructions. The *oeléebalangs* were paid a salary from the *landschapskas*, ranging from f 100 to f 500 a month in the *afdeeling* Pidië, and were the indispensable collaborators of the colonial government in the post-pacification period.

## The *Verlichte Hoofden*
The *verlichte hoofden* — the enlightened chiefs — of the new generation, like [[Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb|Teukoe Tjhi Mohamad Thajeb]] of [[Peureula (Perlak)|Peureula]] and [[Mohamad Djohan Alamsjah|Mohamad Djohan Alamsjah]] of [[Peusangan|Peusangan]], were the sons of *oeléebalangs* sent to Dutch schools in Java, who returned to rule their *landschappen* with Western habits and Dutch-speaking colleagues. Broersma treats them as the type of the new chief, and as the principal hope of the post-pacification reforms.

## Significance
The *oeléebalang* is the principal political institution of pre-colonial and early colonial Atjeh, and the principal intermediary between the Atjehnese people and the Dutch colonial government. The institution is the political condition of the pepper-trade, of the petroleum-cijns, of the *Korte Verklaring*, and of the post-pacification reforms. The post-pacification history of Atjeh is, in large part, the history of the *oeléebalangs*' adjustment to the new colonial order.

**Soerat te'h** — Atjehnese *surat tèh*, literally "book of divination" or "book of the *te'h*" — is the Atjehnese equivalent of the Javanese *primbon* (divination book), the manuscript genre that records the calculations of auspicious and inauspicious days, horoscopes, and the magical correspondences between calendrical, astronomical and life events. The term is named in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) in the course of his discussion of the Islamic feast calendar and its interaction with indigenous time-reckoning.

## Calendrical context

Kernkamp's account of the *soerat te'h* appears in his discussion of the Islamic calendar's interaction with indigenous Atjeh conceptions of time. He notes that "besides these — and other — 'fixed' lucky and unlucky days, one has of course also changing ones that are connected with horoscopic calculations (*koetika's*), which one finds in *primbon*'s (Java) or otherwise named (Atjeh: *soerat te'h*) divination books." The *soerat te'h* thus belongs to the same genre as the Javanese *primbon*: a compendium of calendrical, horoscopic and magical knowledge used to determine the auspicious timing of marriages, journeys, ear-piercings, gravestone placements and other life events.

The same chapter notes that Atjeh shares with Java the practice of "petangan's" (calculations) — the determination of the "character" (*wezen*) of each day, against which particular actions (a marriage, a journey) may or may not "fit". The calculations rest on the interaction of the Islamic seven-day week with the older five-day market week (*pasar* week); on Java the "geboortedag" (birth-day) returns after 35 days (*selapan*), when the day bears the same name in both weeks. The *soerat te'h* is, in Atjeh, the manuscript in which such calculations are recorded.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *soerat te'h* is significant as the documentary counterpart of the Atjeh conceptions of time that Kernkamp describes in the same chapter — the [[Pen (Atjeh)|*pen* concept]] (the requirement that the "field be *pen*" — open, wide — for ear-piercings and gravestone placements), the taboo on the 11th month of the Muslim year for marriages (because the month is "beklemd" — pinched — between the two great Muslim feast days), and the use of the [[Atjeh calendar constellations|constellations of Scorpio and the Pleiades]] for the agricultural year. The *soerat te'h* is the manuscript in which all such calculations are preserved.

**Keuëh** was a limestone or tin coin struck for market traffic in [[Aceh|Atjèh]], with no fixed value. Each Sultan fixed at his accession how many *keuëh* would equal one dollar, and clandestine minting occurred at *pasar*'s (markets). Inscribed "bandar Atjèh dar as-salam," the *keuëh* was only current in [[Groot Atjèh|Groot Atjèh]], not in [[Pidië|Pidië]].

## Minting and value

The *keuëh* was struck using stone molds or matrices called *toeangan keuëh* / *teunoeang keuëh* — two matching blocks with a channel for the lead to flow into the stamps. Each Sultan fixed at his accession how many *keuëh* would equal one dollar. Sometimes 1,200 to a dollar, by the [[1873|war]] 4,000 to a dollar. Clandestine minting occurred at *pasar*'s.

## Inscription and design

The *keuëh* was inscribed **"bandar Atjèh dar as-salam"** ("Harbor of Atjèh, abode of peace") on the obverse. The reverse bore three figures that the Atjèhnese took to be *peudeuëng* ("sables," i.e. swords), though they were actually shaped like lying "8" marks.

## Limited circulation

The *keuëh* was only current in [[Groot Atjèh|Groot Atjèh]], not in [[Pidië|Pidië]] — where *pèng Poer* (Straits copper cents with effigy of Queen Victoria and Edward VII) circulated instead. The *keuëh* was not always current from one *oelèëbalangschap* to another, reflecting the fragmented sovereignty of the Atjèhnese state.

## War-time use

The *keuëh* was melted down for bullets during the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]], contributing to its disappearance from circulation. By Kreemer's time, the *keuëh* had been largely replaced by Dutch and Straits coinage.

## The pèng Poer replacement

In Pidië, the *keuëh* was replaced by *pèng Poer* (Straits copper cents), of which 25 = 1 *soekee* (silver Straits coin worth ¼ dollar). In modern usage, *sesoekee* came to mean the Dutch half-guilder (½ gulden).

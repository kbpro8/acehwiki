**The Ceintuur-trambaan** ("belt-tramway," also called *ceintuurbaan*) was the circular tramway built in [[1885]] along the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Kota Radja]] in [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]]. The belt-tramway was connected by three *straalbanen* (radial lines) to [[Kota Radja]], and thereby to the [[1875]] tramway to the roadstead at [[Oelèe Lheuë]]. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the ceintuur-trambaan was the principal material embodiment of the defensive phase of the Dutch presence in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — the physical expression of the doctrine that the Dutch position could be secured by a perimeter of fortified posts around [[Kota Radja]] connected by rail. After the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]], the ceintuur-trambaan was "gradually cleared" and the long-line strategy of [[1897]] onward replaced it.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Ceintuur-trambaan

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|ceintuur-trambaan]] appears in the chapter on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]]. The Bureau's narrative identifies [[1885]] as the year in which "military necessity led to the construction of a ceintuur-trambaan along the famous [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]], which was connected by three radial lines (*straalbanen*) to [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], and thereby to the old tramway to the roadstead."

The Bureau's account of the [[1896]] abandonment of the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] — and of the consequent dismantling of the ceintuur-trambaan — is explicit about the strategic shift that the dismantling represents: "with the abandonment of the postenlinie after the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar]] in [[1896]], the utility of the ceintuurbaan lapsed, which was since gradually cleared (*geleidelijk opgeruimd*). In its place came the long tramway, which since [[1897]] has followed step by step our extension of authority in [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden]]."

The Bureau's framing of the ceintuur-trambaan places it as the principal material embodiment of the defensive phase of the Dutch presence in Atjeh — the phase of the [[1873]]–[[1896]] period in which the Dutch position was confined to a fortified perimeter around [[Kota Radja]]. The ceintuur-trambaan's circular form — with its three radial lines connecting it to the [[1875]] line to [[Oelèe Lheuë]] — gave the network its distinctive wheel-like pattern: a circular line with three spokes, allowing rapid movement of troops and matériel between the central base at [[Kota Radja]] and the chain of fortified posts enclosing it. The ceintuur-trambaan was thus both a transport infrastructure and a defensive work — a single integrated system of fortification and railway.

## Significance

The [[Ceintuur-trambaan|ceintuur-trambaan]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal material embodiment of the defensive phase of the Dutch presence in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — the phase of the [[1873]]–[[1896]] period. Its construction in [[1885]] marks the high point of the *postenlinie* strategy; its dismantling after [[1896]] marks the strategic reorientation that opened the long Atjeh campaign of conquest and [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]]. The ceintuur-trambaan's wheel-like pattern — circular line with three radial spokes — is the distinctive geographic form of the defensive phase of the Atjeh-tram network.

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

[[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] gives the decision and the first stages of its execution: it was decided to lay a *ceintuurtramweg* (belt tramway) between [[Lamdjame]] and [[Pakan Kroeng Tjoet]], "loopende langs alle tusschengelegen, tot de nieuwe postenlinie behoorende versterkingen; Siroen alleen uitgezonderd" ("running along all intervening fortifications belonging to the new line of posts; [[Siroen]] alone excepted"), after the steam tramway from Kota Radja to [[Lambaroe]] had worked since mid-[[1884]] and was then extended from Kota Radja to [[Ketapang Doewa]] and from there both to Lamdjame and to Lambaroe; according to the latest Atjeh news at the pamphlet's date ([[1886]]) that belt line was then completed as far as [[Roempit]]. To the same fence-work belong the telephone connections of every post with Kota Radja and the plan for electrical lighting of the whole position — measures that Perelaer's list of incidents immediately showed could not keep out the nightly raids on the rails (see [[Atjeh-tram]] and [[Guerilla raids inside the concentration line (1885)]]).

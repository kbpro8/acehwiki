**Teungku** (also *tengku*; both words appear to be contractions of *tuanku* — "my lord") was the most important religious title in [[Great Acheh|Acehnese]] society, applied to a wide range of persons with religious functions and scholarly attainments. Both *teungku* and *teuku* (the secular noble title) appear to be contractions of *tuanku*, though in practice they had sharply different social meanings. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* renders the title variously as *teungkoe*, *toekoe*, *tengkoe*, *teungku* or *tengku*, frequently abbreviated *T.*, and names dozens of teungkoes who played prominent roles in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1896]].

## Range of Application


The title *teungku* was applied to:
- **The leubë** — one who, even though no scholar, observed his religious obligations faithfully
- **The haji** — one who had performed the pilgrimage to [[Mecca]]
- **The malém** — one who had some knowledge of the kitabs (holy books)
- **The além** — one who had brought his studies to perfection (from Arabic *ʿālim*)
- **The ulama** — one looked upon as an authority on the subject of religious law and doctrine
- **The sayyid** — a descendant of Mohammed
- **Teachers** — both men and women giving elementary instruction, even if only in reciting the Quran
- **The kali** — the ecclesiastical judge in an uleebalangship
- **The teungku meunasah** — the "mother of the gampong," the village's religious leader

## The Teungku Meunasah


The most important *teungku* for ordinary village life was the **teungku meunasah**, responsible for upholding [[Hikum|Islamic law]] (*hukom*) in the [[Gampong|gampong]]. Just as the [[Keuchi'|keuchi']] devoted himself to maintaining the [[Adat|customary law]] (*adat*), so the upholding of Islamic law was the special province of the teungku, though a knowledge of and regard for the adat was also considered indispensable.

## Political Power


During the [[Aceh War]], [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] observed that the teungkus — the religious leaders — came more and more to be the masters of the country and terrorised the hereditary chiefs as well as the populace wherever these last were disposed to peace. The ulamas had their golden opportunity: while allowing all credit to guerrilla leaders for their endeavours, the ulamas could point out from the sacred books that such leaders could lay no effective claim to general cooperation. The war ordained of Allah, they argued, must be waged in conformity with His decree.

## Distinction from Teuku


The secular noble title *teuku* (applied to uleebalangs and territorial chiefs) was sharply distinguished from *teungku* in social practice, though both derived from the same root. The official title *Teuku* borne by [[Teuku Kali Malikon Ade]], for example, pointed unequivocally to the complete secularisation of what had once been a religious office.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Teungkoe


**The Teungkoe** — fully *Teungkoe meunasah* — was the religious head of the [[Gampong|gampong]] in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and the surrounding Atjehsche lands, the female counterpart in village symbolism to the paternal [[Keutjhï|Keutjhï]]. *De Buitenbezittingen*, following Prof. Dr. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] (*De Atjehers*, Vol. I, pp. 63 e.v., 74), describes the Teungkoe as the officer in charge of the *meunasah* — the village prayer-house — and the chief agent of *hoeköm*, the religious law of Islam, in the daily life of the community.

## The meunasah


In or near every gampong of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] stood a *meunasah* — a village prayer-house — which served both as a place of daily worship and as a men's dormitory. "De meunasah dient tot nachtverblijf voor alle volwassen jongelingen der gampong, voor alle mannen, die daar tijdelijk verblijf houden en niet in de gampong getrouwd zijn, vreemdelingen zoowel als zulke, die het oogenblik geen lust hebben hunne elders wonende vrouwen te bezoeken." The institution, [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] notes, is the same as the *langgar*, *bale*, or *tadjoeq* found in Java. From this dual — religious and communal — purpose the Teungkoe derived his title: he was, in the strict sense, the master of the *meunasah*.

## Office and counterpart


"Gelijk de Keutjhï meer in het bijzonder de adat heeft te handhaven, zoo is den teungkoe het gebied van de hoeköm (godsdienstige wet) opgedragen" — as the Keutjhï is charged above all with maintaining adat, so the teungkoe is entrusted with the sphere of the hoeköm, the religious law. The two offices stood in deliberate counterpoise: "hoewel adat en hoeköm onafscheidelijk van elkaar heeten, heeft de eerste toch steeds de overhand. Zoo is het ook in de verhouding van beider vertegenwoordigers" — although adat and hoeköm are called inseparable, the former always has the upper hand, and so it is too in the relationship of their representatives. The Teungkoe was thus the gampong's "moeder" to the Keutjhï's "vader" — "*Keutjhï eumbah, teungkoe ma*" — a phrase [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] calls, in the same breath, "ijdele phrase" when invoked by Atjehers in their deliberations.

## Consultation and adat


On every matter of importance touching the gampong, the source records, "de 'ouderlingen'… worden door den Keutjhï en den Teungkoe geraadpleegd." The Teungkoe thus shared with the Keutjhï the responsibility for consulting the [[Oereuëng toeha|Oereuëng toeha]] — the elders, "mannen van adatkennis, ervaring, levenswijsheid, goede manieren, en welgesteldheid in de gampong." In the surrounding Atjehsche lands, where the term *gampong* itself was replaced by *meunasa*, the Teungkoe was known as *imeum (teungkoe) meunasa* — a syncretic title marking the persistence of the religious office even where the village unit had been renamed.

## Account from Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — Teungkoes of 1896


**Teungkoe** — also rendered *toekoe*, *tengkoe*, *teungku* or *tengku* in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96*, and frequently abbreviated *T.* — was the [[Achehnese]] honorific borne by a religious teacher, scholar or holy man. The teungkoe stood at the apex of the religious hierarchy of the [[Aceh Sultanate]], distinct from but interlocking with the secular hierarchy of [[hoeloebalang]] and [[panglima sagi]]. Kruisheer names dozens of teungkoes: [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]] and [[Teungkoe Tanah Abée]] (called the "greatest oelama" of the [[XXII Moekims]]); [[Teungkoe di Tiro]]; [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah]]; [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang|Teungkoe Mesir]]; [[T. Mad Amin]]; and — most famously — [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] himself, who although a secular hoeloebalang bore the religious prefix. Many teungkoes led anti-[[Dutch]] resistance in their own right.

### Etymology and Meaning


The word *teungkoe* is Acehnese, cognate with Malay *tengku* and is thought to derive from a Malay title of noble or learned persons, ultimately from Sanskrit *tuhan* (lord, master). In Acehnese usage the term is broadly applied: to learned Islamic scholars ([[oelama]]), to Quranic teachers, to Sufi adepts, and as an honorific prefix in personal names. A teungkoe need not be of noble birth; the title could be acquired through religious learning and personal piety. The spelling variants *teungkoe*, *toekoe*, *tengkoe*, *teungku* and the abbreviation *T.* reflect the OCR's inconsistent rendering of Kruisheer's Dutch orthography, which itself oscillates between more Acehnese and more Malay.

### Historical Context of 1896


In the first semester of [[1896]] teungkoes were the principal ideologues and organisers of Achehnese resistance. [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]] — described by Kruisheer as "een voorname oelama in de XXII Mockims" — acquired his influence through ascetic meditation and propagated the doctrine of "sham battle" that allowed Umar's *legioen* to fight the [[moslemin]] without serious casualties while Umar was still nominally on the Dutch side. [[Teungkoe Tanah Abée]] succeeded Koeta Karang as "the greatest oelama" and labelled Umar's bands as unbelievers after the defection. [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah]] practised *kaloet* (ascetic meditation) in the grot of [[Daroe]] "misschien wel in navolging van T. Koeta Karang, die daarmede destijds nog al invloed verworven had" (perhaps in imitation of T. Koeta Karang, who had theretofore acquired considerable influence through it). The teungkoes collectively formed the religious scaffolding of the resistance.

### Examples in the Source


Kruisheer's text is saturated with teungkoe references. [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]] appears as the principal oelama of the XXII Moekims and architect of the *main prang* (sham battle) doctrine. [[Teungkoe Tanah Abée]] succeeds him. [[Teungkoe Panté Gelimah]] practises *kaloet* in the grot of Daroe. [[Teungkoe di Tiro]] is referenced as the historical leader of long-running Achehnese resistance. [[T. Mad Amin]] (also spelled Mat Amin) is described as an oelama. The *Teungkoe Mesir* — "steenen graf on road Lamtih–Lam Manjang" — is invoked as a holy grave. The *Teungkoe Lam-Bira* has a holy grave near the resistance positions. [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang|Teungkoe di Tiroe]] is mentioned in the early survey of resistance leaders. The prefix is also attached to secular leaders who carry religious prestige, such as [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] (panglima sagi of the [[XXVI Moekims]]) and *Toekoe Oemar* himself.

### Significance of the Teungkoes in 1896


The teungkoe is the institutional carrier of the religious dimension of the [[Aceh War]]. Without the teungkoes' doctrine of jihad, their labelling of the Dutch as *kafir*, and their organisation of *kaloet* retreats that legitimated resistance, the Achehnese war effort would have lacked its ideological unity. Kruisheer's careful enumeration of teungkoe names, locations and doctrines reflects the Dutch awareness that the war could not be ended by military means alone: the religious hierarchy of the teungkoes had to be defeated, co-opted or eliminated before the secular hoeloebalang structure could be brought under control. The institution survived the war and remained central to Achehnese religious and political life into the twentieth century.

## Account from *Verkenningen in Atjeh* (1923)

**Toeankoe** — also written *Tuan ku* — is an Atjehnese honorific borne particularly by the [[Sultan of Atjeh|sultan of Atjeh]] and his relatives. The shorter form *[[Teungkoe / Teukoe / Tengkoe (honorific)|teungkoe/teukoe/tengkoe]]* is, in Broersma's account, *'eigenlijk wel hetzelfde als toeankoe'* — *really much the same as toeankoe* — and is borne by chiefs and dignitaries. The fuller form *toeankoe* is borne, for example, by [[Toeankoe Machmoed|Toeankoe Machmoed]] of [[Idi rajeu|Idi rajeu]].

**Atjehnese *langa*** — the Atjehnese word for "plough", cognate with Malay *tanggala*, Batak *tonggala*, Balinese *tonggala*, Madurese *nangghālā*, and ultimately derived from Sanskrit *langgala* (लङ्गल) — is the linguistic evidence cited by Dr. [[F. H. van Naerssen|Van Naerssen]] in his chapter *De oudste cultuurstroomen* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) to place the Atjeh language within the wider Austric (in Van Naerssen's usage) / Austronesian linguistic family, and to document the early Indian (Sanskritic) influence on the agricultural vocabulary of the archipelago.

## Van Naerssen's account

Van Naerssen's wording: "Some languages have for this agricultural implement a word derived from *langgala*, for example the Malays, the Bataks and the Balinese (*tŏnggala*), the Madurese (*nangghālā*), the Atjehers (*langa*), etc.; other languages have for it words of different origin: the Javanese (*wluku* and *brujul*), the Sundanese (*wuluku*), the Malays (also: *bāluku*, *luku* and *bajak*), the Boeginezen and Makassaren (*pajeko* and *tengko*)." The Atjehnese *langa* thus groups with the Malay, Batak and Balinese forms in the *langgala*-cognate set, against the Javanese, Sundanese, Boeginees and Makassar forms that have different origins.

## The two hypotheses

Van Naerssen considers two hypotheses for the spread of the *langgala*-cognate set. The first: the word "has reached Indonesia via Sanskrit — that is, imported by the Hindus". The second: the word "came with one of the many cultural streams in prehistory out of the *stamland*" — that is, the Austronesian homeland in mainland Southeast Asia — "centuries before the arrival of the Indians". Van Naerssen does not adjudicate between the two hypotheses, but his inclusion of the Atjehnese *langa* in the *langgala*-cognate set is itself significant: it places the Atjeh language within the same linguistic grouping as Malay, Batak and Balinese, against Javanese and Sundanese.

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the Atjehnese *langa* is significant as one of the few pieces of linguistic evidence in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* that bears specifically on the prehistory of the Atjeh language. The same word — *langa* — places Atjeh within the wider Sanskritic-influenced agricultural vocabulary of the archipelago, alongside Malay, Batak and Balinese, and against the non-Sanskritic plough-words of Javanese, Sundanese, Boeginees and Makassar. The Atjeh language's membership in the *langgala*-cognate set thus connects it to the early Indian cultural influence on the archipelago, an influence that predated the Islamisation of [[Samudra]] in [[1297]] by many centuries.

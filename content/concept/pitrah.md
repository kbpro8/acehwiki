**Pitrah** — Atjehnese/Dutch spelling of Arabic *zakāt al-fiṭr*, the "alms of the breaking of the fast" — is the obligatory charitable contribution that every Muslim household head must pay at the end of Ramadan, on behalf of every person for whom he is responsible, before the Eid al-Fitr prayer that opens Shawwal. The Atjeh form of the contribution, and its collection in the [[Meunasah]], is recorded in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]).

## The Atjeh collection of pitrah

Kernkamp's account of the *pitrah* places it within the broader Indonesian context of the Eid al-Fitr "Small Feast" (*Kleine Feest*) at the end of Ramadan. He notes that "the religious gift (*pitrah*) at the end of that month is distributed here" — i.e. in the [[Meunasah]] — "in Atjeh." The *pitrah* is, in Islamic law, payable in kind (in grain) at a fixed quantity per person; in the Indies, this means rice. Kernkamp further notes that "this obligation is generally faithfully fulfilled, even if not entirely according to the norms of the Law. Thus one sometimes pays in money, and also much less than is prescribed (in Middle Celebes sometimes only a dime per head). The faithful fulfilment finds its ground in the fact that the *pitrah* is tied to the Fast and therefore shares in the reconciliation (*verzoening*) that is found to be attached to it. Moreover, a careful fulfilment of the duty to pay the *pitrah* is then also considered to be able to make good a bad or insufficiently kept Fast."

## The receivers of pitrah

Kernkamp notes that, although the householder is in principle free to choose the pious poor to whom he will give his *pitrah*, "the *adat* does more or less indicate who come into consideration. Very often the officials of the village prayer houses are the receivers of it." In Atjeh, the *pitrah* is collected at the *meunasah*; in West Java, by contrast, the *pitrah* is brought to the *lebé* or *modin* of one's own *kampong* — and, if the village Quran-teacher (*goeroe ngadji*) lives far away, the householder may even "pay this religious tax twice". The Atjeh collection at the *meunasah* is thus a specifically Atjehnese institutional form of a wider Indonesian practice.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *pitrah* is significant as one of the three principal Islamic obligatory contributions (alongside the [[Djakat|*djakat*]] or *zakāt al-māl*, and the *zakāt* on mineral wealth) that Kernkamp documents for the Indies. The Atjeh form of collection — at the *meunasah*, distributed to the *meunasah* officials — places the village prayer-house at the institutional centre of Atjeh Islamic life, alongside its other functions as council hall, men's lodging, school, and venue for the mid-Ramadan [[Ratib in Atjeh|*ratib*]].

**Panghoeloe** — Dutch/Atjehnese spelling of standard Indonesian *penghulu*, from Malay *peng-* (agent-noun prefix) + *hulu* "head" — is the title of the chief religious official attached to a mosque, serving as the head of the mosque's personnel and the administrator of its funds. The office is recorded in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) in his account of the mosque personnel of the Indies.

## Kernkamp's account

Kernkamp introduces the *panghoeloe* in the course of his discussion of the staff attached to the Indonesian mosque (*[[Mesigit|mesigit]]*). His wording: "To the mosque is attached, for all sorts of purposes, a fairly numerous personnel. One distinguishes foremen at the *sembahjang* (*imam*), preachers, that is to say simply readers of an Arabic sermon (*chatib*), summoners to invite [the faithful] to take part in the *salát* (usually called *bilal*, after the first Arabic occupant of the office), further a sort of sextons. Some titles and offices make Europeans easily think of spiritual leaders and one speaks thus of a 'priesthood'. Wholly wrongly, for the Islam knows precisely no mediating instance between God and the individual (with the exception of the now no longer existing Prophets and Apostles). ... Well, this is the case with the chief of the personnel, the so-called *panghoeloe*, called by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] not incorrectly 'Mosquee-director' (*Moskee-directeur*). The name gives the character of the office well. He is an official, who divides the work among his subordinates and administers the funds of the mosque, derived from pious gifts."

## Distinction from priest

Kernkamp is at pains to insist that the *panghoeloe* is not a priest: Islam "knows precisely no mediating instance between God and the individual" and "there are no sacraments that priests could distribute. Even the care of souls is no privilege or duty of a particular class of persons, but comes to every Muslim for his surroundings." The *panghoeloe* is, rather, an administrator — the director of the mosque's personnel and finances. His prestige in the Indies derives not from his directorship of the mosque but from the fact that "the office of *kadi* — judge according to the Muslim Law, in other lands usually entrusted to specialists — is in the Indies, *faute de mieux*, entrusted to him". The *panghoeloe*'s effective authority in the Indies is thus a consequence of the colonial situation: in the absence of a properly trained class of Islamic jurists, the office of religious judge falls to the mosque director.

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the *panghoeloe* is significant as one of the principal religious offices of the Indonesian mosque. In Atjeh specifically, the [[Teungku]] performs many of the functions that elsewhere in the Indies are performed by the *panghoeloe* — the leadership of the village [[Meunasah]] and the supervision of its religious life — while the [[Mesigit|mesigit]] (the Friday mosque) in [[Kutaradja|Koeta Radja]] and the other Atjeh towns would have had its own *panghoeloe* on the Indies pattern. Kernkamp's citation of Snouck Hurgronje's characterisation of the *panghoeloe* as "Mosquee-director" is itself notable: the formulation is Snouck Hurgronje's, and reflects the same administrative-rationalising frame that Snouck brought to the reform of Atjeh religious institutions under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

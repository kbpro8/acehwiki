**Education in Atjeh** — the network of village-, girls'-, government-native-, Dutch-native-, agricultural-, trade- and Europeans' schools built up by the [[Government of Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] between [[1905]] and [[1923]] — was the proudest welfare record of the later [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification]]: from 5 village-schools with some 300 pupils when Governor [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] took office ([[1908]]) to 242 schools with nearly 13,000 pupils in [[1918]], 25,000 children under instruction in [[1921]], and an inspection transferred to the ordinary Indies school service.

## Van Daalen's beginning

Under [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]] ([[1905]]–[[1908]]) five village-schools (*volksscholen*) were opened in Groot-Atjeh and five new schools taken in building; in the Dependencies there were as yet no native first-class schools, though some lower government and private establishments of education existed. Van Daalen proposed the foundation in Groot-Atjeh of so-called *kampongscholen* in the sense that in every [[Moekim System|moekim]] such a school would arise, and equally the foundation of a school with boarding (*internaat*) at [[Koeta Radja]] for sons of native chiefs, where instruction would be given in the Dutch language.

## Swart's programme, 1908–1918

"But the instruction had to be directed to the practice of life, for the boy as for the girl": for the girl an important place for hand- and household-work; for the boy, beside school-instruction, agriculture teaching, so that he might be kept for the farm. Governor Swart's numbers climbed: 5 village-schools with about 300 pupils at his accession; 125 with 6,000 pupils in [[1911]]; 242 with nearly 13,000 pupils in [[1918]]. The government native schools, 4 in 1908 (all in Groot-Atjeh), had climbed to 28 province-wide in 1918. The first girls' school opened in [[1910]] at [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]]; after she had sufficiently proven her viability, two more girls' schools followed in [[1913]] in Groot-Atjeh, and gradually more such establishments in the Dependencies; in 1918 there were 15 girls' schools in the whole province with more than 1,000 pupils — the curriculum (fine and useful handiwork, bobbin-lace, weaving etc.) being spread over six instead of three school-years, in the hope of fighting the *kinderhuwelijken* (child-marriages). A Hollandsch-Indlandsche school opened at Koeta Radja in [[1915]], and in [[1916]] at [[Lho Seumawè]] and [[Langsar]]; at his urgency a Hollandsch-Chineesche school at Koeta Radja was opened in [[1918]]. For children of Ambonese soldiers a special Ambonese school was founded at Koeta Radja on his proposal in [[1911]], counting nearly 400 pupils in 1918. Public European lower schools opened at [[Sigli]] and Langsar, and in 1915 a neutral fröbel-school at Koeta Radja. A native trade school (*ambachtschool*) opened in [[1916]] at Koeta Radja for the carpenters needed in the kampongs.

## Higher and agricultural education

The agricultural schools at [[Beureunoen]] and [[Oeléë Karang]], founded under Swart, kept farming as the first means of existence. For chiefs' sons the education had first taken place at the native normal school (*Inl. Kweekschool*) at [[Fort de Kock]], several moreover being sent to the training school at [[Bandoeng]], later moved to [[Serang]]; others were trained at the Asahan school, the Cultuurschool at [[Soekaboemi]], and a few at the secondary agricultural school at [[Buitenzorg]] and the native law school at [[Batavia]].

## Van Sluys: inspection and expansion

Governor [[Van Sluys|Van Sluys]], "not less than his predecessor convinced that for the people's development popular education is a first requirement", brought the institutions created under Swart to greater bloom. The inspection of popular education, until 1918 in the hands of the administration, was in that year brought under the inspection of the ordinary lower Indian education; the technical investigation he instituted led to the conference of January [[1920]] at [[Batavia]], under the Director of Education, of chief officials of the department, advisers and inspectors with the Governor of Atjeh. His exertions had for result that after two years of administration (1920) the number of schools had increased by 28%, the number of pupils by almost 30%, and the number of girl pupils by more than 121%; in [[1921]] the total of native children receiving education stood at 25,000. Director [[N. van Zalinge]] observed in late [[1922]] that the government schools everywhere founded were well attended — giving reason to expect that whatever hatred of the Hollander still slumbered among the Atjehnese population would wholly have disappeared with a following generation.

**Hoeloebalang** — also spelled *oeleebalang*, *ulèbalang*, or *hulubalang* in Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — was the [[Achehnese]] hereditary territorial chief or district lord, the secular counterpart of the religious [[oelama]] and the basic building-block of the Sultanate's political order. Each hoeloebalang ruled a [[moekim]] or a cluster of moekims and was in turn subordinate, at least notionally, to the [[panglima sagi]] of his [[sagi]]. Kruisheer names numerous hoeloebalang: [[Teuku Umar|T. Djohan]] was hoeloebalang of [[Lepong]] (appointed per [[Government Resolution of 19 January 1896, No. 9]]); [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] was hoeloebalang of the [[XXII Moekims]] of Toengkoeb; [[Panglima Bintang]] was hoeloebalang of the [[III Moekims Daroe]]; and [[T. Rajoet]] was hoeloebalang of the [[V Moekims Montassik]]. The office was hereditary, territorial and military in character — a hoeloebalang could raise his own armed following (*bende*) from among his dependants.

## Etymology and Meaning

The word *hoeloebalang* is a compound of Acehnese "head", "chief" and a class of warrior or minister; the title thus means "head-warrior" or "chief of warriors". In pre-colonial usage the hoeloebalang was simultaneously a hereditary lord, a judge in adat matters, a tax collector and a military commander of his own district. The Dutch text uses the spelling *oeleebalang* or *hoeloebalang* interchangeably and translates the office as "regional/district head". Unlike the religious hierarchy of [[teungkoe]]s and [[oelama]]s, the hoeloebalang was a secular, territorial lord whose authority derived from inheritance rather than from religious learning.

## Historical Context

In [[1896]] the hoeloebalang was the Dutch government's preferred counterpart for negotiation. The Dutch appointment of [[Teuku Umar]] (as *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*) to the hoeloebalang-ship of [[Lepong]] by Government Resolution No. 9 of [[19 January]] [[1896]] formalised his status as a recognised territorial chief within the colonial framework; his dismissal from the same office on [[26 April]] [[1896]] by Government Resolution No. 159/11 (Bijlage VI) returned him to the undifferentiated name *Toekoe Oemar* and stripped him of all rank. The political contest of the spring of 1896 was thus fought at the level of hoeloebalang allegiance: which hoeloebalang would stand with the [[Dutch]] and which would defect to Umar. Kruisheer notes that [[T. Hoesin Lhongbatta]] "talso with him the doubtful-minded regions" of the [[XXVI Moekims|sagi XXVI]], and that the "acting Oeloebalang of the IX Moekims, Nja Mohamad", was forced by Umar to submit after Umar "picked up his wife and held her at Lampisang".

## Examples in the Source

Kruisheer enumerates hoeloebalang throughout the text. [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] is "Oeloebalang der XXII Moekims Toengkoeb" and a member of the three-man leadership of the XXVI Moekims; [[T. Djohan]] (Umar) is "Oeloebalang of Lepong, dismissed per [[Bijlage VI]]". The [[Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] (Bijlage VI) explicitly dismisses Umar both as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] and as "Oeleebalang of Lepong". [[Panglima Bintang]] of the III Moekims Daroe appears repeatedly, first as a Dutch ally, later as a defector who in mid-June [[1896]] returned 12 of 16 *trompladers* lent to him by the Government. [[T. Tjoet Lamtengah]], hoeloebalang of the Moekim Lam-Tengah, defected in the night of [[30 March]]–[[31 March]] [[1896]] with his population. [[T. Moeda Baid]] appears as hoeloebalang of the [[VII Moekims Baid]]. The institution is referenced again in the Besluit of [[9 May]] [[1896]] forbidding further weapon supply to "Acehnese heads" — a direct acknowledgement that the practice of arming hoeloebalang had created a "talrijken aaneengesloten vijand... grootendeels even goed bewapend als onze eigen infanterie".

## Significance

The hoeloebalang was the institutional hinge of the [[Aceh War]]. The Dutch strategy of "alliance" with cooperative hoeloebalang — supplying them with [[Beaumontgeweer|Beaumont rifles]], ammunition and even the title [[Panglima Prang Besar]] — rested on the assumption that hoeloebalang allegiance could be bought. Umar's defection exposed the fragility of that assumption: a hoeloebalang could defect with his entire moekim and his Dutch-supplied arms. The reversal of policy embodied in the [[Besluit of 9 May 1896]] forbidding further weapon distribution was therefore an admission that the office of hoeloebalang could not be reduced to a colonial intermediary.

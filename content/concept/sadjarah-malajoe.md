**The Sadjarah Malajoe** — Malay **Sejarah Melayu**, English *Malay Annals* — is the principal Malay chronicle of the [[Malacca|Malacca Sultanate]] and its [[Johor|Johorese]] successor, traditionally ascribed to the *bendahara* of [[Johor|Johor]], and the principal Malay-language source for the early history of [[Sumatra]]'s east coast — including the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] episode of [[Awi Ditjoe]]'s capture of the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]], the rivalry of [[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]] and [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej-Samudra]], and the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]]. It is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, pervaded by *[[bedekte taal]]* — the veiled-language register that hides the older Hindu-Buddhist geography of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere beneath the surface of the chronicle.

## The Sjahroe'n Noewi episode

In chapter VIII of the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] — the episode on which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s chapter *Over Sjahroe'n Noewi* chiefly draws — the great kingdom of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] sends its *hoeloebalang* [[Awi Ditjoe]] (with 4,000 fighters in a hundred vessels) to capture the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]]. The episode — Awi Ditjoe's ruse of the chests, the capture, the "tending chickens" humiliation, and the recovery of the sultan by [[Sidi Ali Ghiyat oed'dir]] — is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the *bedekte taal* record of the long [[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]]–[[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] rivalry.

## The Siam connection

In chapter XII, the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] records that "of old [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] was the name of the negeri Siam, to which all the radjas below the wind were tributary" — a notice that [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads as confirmation of the Siamese overlordship of the [[Tandjoengpoera]]-[[Aroe]] complex in the fourteenth century. The *padoeka Boeboennja* of the chronicle — "his foot-soled of Boeboen" — De Roo de la Faille identifies with the *kampong Boeboen* of [[Anderson's Mission to the East Coast of Sumatra (1825)|Anderson's 1825 mission]], at the river-mouth of [[Tandjoengpoera]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]].

## Other Aceh-relevant material

The [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] also records the rank of [[Aroe]] on a level with [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] and [[Malacca]] — a "realm that stretched from [[Tamijang]] to [[Siak]]" — and its reduction to "poverty and piracy" after the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest]]. It is the principal Malay source for the [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend, the [[Djawaka|Djawaka]] tradition, and the [[Bungan mas|bunga mas]] tribute to Siam.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] is the principal Malay chronicle source for the early history of [[Sumatra]]'s east coast — the source on which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws for the [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] episode, the [[Aroe]]-[[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]-[[Malacca]] triangle, and the *bedekte taal* reading of the chronicle register. Its recognition as a *bedekte taal* text, rather than a plain ethnographic record, is the methodological precondition for any reading of the chronicle geography of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the east-coast Sumatran polities.

## Account from *Over Maleise Literatuur* (1947) — Sadjarah Malajoe

In [[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]]'s survey of Malay literature, the *[[Sadjarah Malajoe]]* is "het belangrijkste van deze gehele categorie en een der kostelijkste werken van de Maleise letteren" (the most important of this whole category and one of the most precious works of Malay literature). Hooykaas devotes the entire first appendix of his survey — some ninety pages — to a running *Toelichting tot de Sadjarah Malajoe* (Commentary on the Sadjarah Malajoe), in which he gives a chapter-by-chapter annotation, an *Inhoudsopgave* (Contents Overview), and a genealogy of "De Vorsten van Malakka en Djohor" (The Rulers of Malacca and Johor).

### Author and Date

The author of the *Sadjarah Malajoe*, in Hooykaas's reading, is the *[[Bendahara Sri Lanang|Bendahara]]* — "de hoogste ambtenaar en het best op de hoogte met politiek en hof" (the highest official and the best informed about politics and court). The work was commissioned by *Radja 'Abdoellāh*, the younger brother of the reigning Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh III, on the *Mawlid* festival of Sunday 13 Rabīʿ I 1021 AH — that is, [[13 May]] [[1612]] — at [[Batoe Sawar]]. The completed work — 34 stories — was finished after the *Bendahara*'s return from Atjeh in [[1614]], with later stories (35-38) added to cover the [[1615]] Atjehnese attack on Batoe Sawar.

### The Atjehnese Interruption

The composition of the *Sadjarah Malajoe* was repeatedly interrupted by the [[1613]] Atjehnese attack on Batoe Sawar: "Het schrijven heeft niet ongestoord zijn gang kunnen gaan; een Atjehse vloot verscheen in 1613 onverwachts, die zich meester wist te maken van niemand minder dan de vorst 'Alā' oed-Din Ri'āyat Sjāh III, Radja 'Abdoellāh en de meeste rijksgroten, waaronder de Bēndahara" (The writing was not able to proceed undisturbed; an Atjehnese fleet appeared unexpectedly in 1613, which managed to seize no less than the Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh III, Radja 'Abdoellah, and most of the great men of the realm, including the *Bendahara*). The captives were taken to Atjeh, where the *Bendahara* would — in Hooykaas's reading — have become acquainted with the *[[Tādj oes-Salatina|Tādj us-Salāṭīn]]* of [[1607]], the Atjehnese "mirror for princes" that served as the model for the *Bendahara*'s own "Geslachtslijst der Soeltanen" (Genealogy of the Sultans).

### The Diplomatic Silence

The *Sadjarah Malajoe*'s account of the [[1615]] catastrophe is, in Hooykaas's reading, "diplomatiek" (diplomatic) — the work stops just before the [[1615]] attack, "bejammerd alleen in gekuiste termen, dat de Sultan zo veranderziek was om weer hogerop een nieuwe kota te gaan stichten" (lamenting only in chastened terms that the Sultan was so changeable as to go and found a new kota higher up). Only the *Inleiding* (Introduction) makes a "zwakke toespeling" (weak allusion) to the catastrophe, by speaking of the "Marhoem jang mangkat di Atjeh, Soeltan Alaoedin Ritajat Sjah" (the late [sultan] who died in Atjeh, Sultan Alā'uddīn Ri'āyat Shāh) — that is, the Sultan who had been carried off to Atjeh in [[1613]] and died there.

### The Tjampa-Atjeh Connection

Hooykaas's *Toelichting* also notes the connection between Atjeh and Tjampa recorded in the *Sadjarah Malajoe*: in chapter XXI, "van de twee koningszoons vlucht één naar Atjeh en grondvest daar het vorstelijk geslacht" (of the two royal sons, one flees to Atjeh and founds the royal dynasty there). Hooykaas observes: "Tjams en Atjehs vertonen sterke samenhang" (Cham and Atjehnese show strong connection) — a connection also noted in the *Hikajat Sultan Atjeh Marhoem*, which Hooykaas describes as "niet Maleis-sprekend gebied, maar is in taal verwant met het Achter-Indische Tjampa" (not Malay-speaking territory, but is in language related to the mainland-Indian Cham).

*Source: Over Maleise Literatuur*, by Dr. C. Hooykaas, Hoogleraarte Batavia; Tweede Druk (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1947), chapter 11 ("Geschiedschrijving in het Maleis"), pp. 96-98; Toelichting tot de Sadjarah Malajoe, pp. 201-276.

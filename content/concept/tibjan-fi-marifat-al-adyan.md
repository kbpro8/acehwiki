**Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan** is a Malay compendium on religions and Islamic sects composed by [[Nuruddin ar-Raniri]] in [[Acheh]] between [[1637]] and [[1644]], intended to expose pre-Islamic faiths and the 72 deviant Muslim groups, culminating in a refutation of the [[Wujudiyyah]] of [[Hamzah Fansuri]] and [[Syamsuddin of Pasai]].

The sole complete copy used by Voorhoeve is Leiden Cod. Or. 3291.

## Content

Voorhoeve summarises the structure from Van Ronkel:

**Part I – Religions from Adam to Isa**
- Worship of images after Adam, Idris and his five disciples, the flood of Nuh, the excavation of idols under Ismail, three groups of mushrikun.
- Sabians, star-worshippers (*Hukama' Falakiyah*), dualists (*Thanawiyah*), fire-worshippers (*Majusiyah* subdivided into Zardushtiyah, Mazdakiyah), Indian materialists (*Dahriyah*), skeptics (*Kopten* corrupted from *Mu'attila*), and reincarnationists (*Tanasukhiyah*).
- The ten *kitab*-possessing groups, including the [[Barahima]] of [[Gujarat]] (whom Raniri, following [[Abd al-Karim al-Jili]], counts as scriptuaries), Jews, and Christians (Malkāniyah, Nastūriyah, Ya'qubiyah).

**Part II – The 72 Muslim sects**
Derived from six roots, each split into twelve:
1. [[Rafidiyah]]
2. [[Kharijiyah]] or Haruriyah
3. [[Jabriyah]]
4. [[Qadariyah]]
5. [[Jahmiyah]]
6. [[Murji'ah]]

After these, Raniri adds thirteen "quasi-Sufi" groups, drawn largely from Najm al-Din al-Nasafi's *Kitab fi bayan madhhab al-mutasawwifa*: Hubbiyah, Awliya'iyah, Shamrakhiyah, Ibahiyah, Hululiyah, Waki'iyah, Mutajahiliyah, Mutakasilah, Ilhamiyah, Hululiyah, Hallajiyah, and finally [[Wujudiyyah]] or Ittihadiyah — the "upwind and downwind" Wujudis, to which Hamzah and Syamsuddin belong.

## Sources

Voorhoeve proved more than a quarter of the Tibjan is a literal Malay translation, rearranged, of chapter 11 of [[Abu Shakur al-Salimi]]'s *al-Tamhid fi bayan al-tawhid* (5th century H). The Leiden manuscript Or. 5664, copied in [[Banten]] on Javanese paper, contains the same scribal errors found in Raniri's exemplar — notably the corruption of *Mu'attila* to *Kubtijah* and a lacuna omitting the fourth Tanasukhiyah group.

For the Barahima section and the list of ten religions, Raniri used [[Abd al-Karim al-Jili]]'s *al-Insan al-kamil* (Cairo ed., II, p. 81).

For the names and dogmas of the 72 sects he used a popular handbook *Ma'rifat al-madhahib* (attributed to Abu Hanifa), as preserved in the early [[1614]] Malay-Arabic manuscript Leiden Cod. Or. 1255 from [[Pulo Ai]] in the [[Banda Islands]].

## Connection with Acheh

The work was written expressly for the Achehnese court to justify the suppression of Wujudiyyah teachings after [[1636]]. It cites Hamzah's *Asrar al-arifin* and Syamsuddin's *Mir'at al-muhaqqiqin* by name, and the final colophon places its completion in the reign of [[Sultan Iskandar II]].

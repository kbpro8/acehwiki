**Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java** is a posthumously circulated monograph by [[P. de Roo de la Faille]], dated [[Den Haag]], [[March]] [[1954]], that gathers nine "curiosities" from the historical literature of the [[Malay Archipelago|Malajoe]] and [[Java]] in which the author — through the method of *bedekte taal* (veiled language) — seeks to recover a Sumatran, and especially an [[Acheh]]-and-[[Aroe|Aru]]-centred, substratum beneath the chronicles of Java. Dedicated to [[Dr. D. J. Hulshoff Pol|Dr D. J. Hulshoff Pol]], who as Officier van Gezondheid at [[Tandjoeng Poera]] with the [[K.N.I.L.|K.N.I.L. Leger]] had begun his career in the very [[Aroe]] country the book places at the centre of early Malay history, the work treats [[Mendez Pinto]]'s [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aru war of 1539]], the [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend of [[Deli]], the chronicle of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] in the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]], the introduction of the [[Sadjarah Banten]], the [[Melasaké pampang|wonder-tree of Belalau]], and the multiple identities of [[Pati Oenoes]]-[[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]].

## Genre and method

De Roo de la Faille writes as a philological amateur in the best Dutch-Indological sense: steeped in [[Pieter Johannes Veth|Veth]], [[Dr. J. Brandes|Brandes]], [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]], [[Dr. H. N. van der Tuuk|Van der Tuuk]] and the *Bijdragen* tradition, but refusing to take their readings at face value where chronicle language strikes him as deliberately veiled. The book's central instrument is the *wangsalan* — the Malay/Javanese riddle in which a phrase is indicated by an intermediate word that rhymes or echoes it (e.g. *ikan* on *hin-doe ka-ri*, "where Hindu population remained"). De Roo de la Faille applies it systematically to such puzzles as Pinto's *Paneticano*, Marco Polo's *Gauenispola*, the *Cernovem* of Niccolò de' Conti, and the *Xarnauz* of the *Roteiro* of [[Vasco da Gama]], reading them all as concealed references to the [[Aroe|Aru]] country at the mouth of the [[Straits of Malacca]] and its sacred islet of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]].

## Chapters

The nine chapters of the book are: an *Inleiding*; *Mendez Pinto op Sumatra* (the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aru war of 1539]]); *"Zuivere Nonsens?"* (a defence of the Old-Javanese *Sang Satyawan* against [[van Stein Callenfels]]'s dismissal); *Djempol Soekoe* (the Chinese annals' Queen [[Sima]] of Kaling read through the Javanese *djempol soekoe* idiom); *Gamispola* (Marco Polo's island); *Over Sjahroe'n Noewi* (the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] episode of [[Awi Ditjoe]]'s capture of the radja of [[Pasej-Samodra|Samudra]]); *De inleiding van de Sadjarah Banten* (a critique of [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Dr Hoesein Djajadiningrat]]'s reading); *Melasaké pampang* (the inverted-tree allegory of South Sumatra); and *Over Soenan Kalidjaga* (the identification of [[Soenan Kalidjaga]] with [[Pati Oenoes]] of [[Demak]]). Throughout, [[Acheh]] and its [[Atjehsche vloot|fleet]] of [[1539]] recur as the great antagonist whose expansion broke the older Hindu-Buddhist commonwealth of the Sumatran east coast.

## Significance for AcehWiki

For AcehWiki the *Curiositeiten* matters in three ways. First, it is the principal modern source that gathers, in one place, the scattered Portuguese references to the [[Sultan of Atjeh|Atjehnese sultan]] of [[1539]], his brother-in-law and admiral [[Alleredin Mahomet]], and the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|war against Aroe]] — material otherwise buried in Pinto's *Peregrinação*. Second, it preserves the [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend in the form that ties the Sultan of Atjeh's rejected suit to the [[Kota Djawa|Javanese fortification]] at [[Deli]] and to the loss of a glass coffin at sea between Deli and Atjeh — a story of direct relevance to the early-modern [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s relations with the east-coast polities. Third, its central methodological claim — that chronicles of "Java" repeatedly encode places on Sumatra's north and east coast, including the Atjehnese sphere — is the framework against which several AcehWiki entries must be read.

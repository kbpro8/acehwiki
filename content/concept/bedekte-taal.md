**Bedekte taal** — Dutch "veiled language" — is the term [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] uses for the allegorical and riddling register that, in his reading, pervades the chronicle and romance literature of the Malay-Javanese world and conceals references to the older Hindu-Buddhist geography of [[Sumatra]]'s east coast and the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere. Its Malay form is ***bahasa kapoer*** — "lime-language", the "peculiar language" of [[Malajoe]] that [[Marco Polo]] noted on his travels, and which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies with the "lime" (*kapoer*) of [[Baros|Baros camphor]] — a code of speech, that is, whose key is the trade-goods and place-names of the Sumatran coast. Its instruments are the *wangsalan* (the Malay rhyme-riddle) and the Kawi-Javanese *dasanama* (the synonym-book), and its function is to record matters of royal shame, ancestral displacement, or sacred geography without exposing them to plain view.

## The *bahasa kapoer* of Malajoe

[[Marco Polo|Marco Polo]] had noted, in connection with the small island he called *Gauenispola*, that he encountered the "peculiar language" of the place. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies this with the ***bahasa kapoer*** of [[Malajoe]] — the same "lime-language" that [[Langka|Langka-poer-i]] (the [[Pulo Bras|Poelo Bras]] of the Willemstoren) recalls. The "lime" of the name points, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, to the *kapoer baroer* (camphor) of [[Baros|Baros]], the great west-coast emporium whose product gave its name to a whole register of speech.

## The *wangsalan* and the *dasanama*

The principal instrument of *bedekte taal* is the *wangsalan* — the Malay-Javanese riddle in which a phrase is indicated by an intermediate word that rhymes with or echoes it. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] gives many examples: *ikan* "fish" on *hin-doe ka-ri* "where Hindu population remained"; *batoe rendang* on the [[Batu|Batu]] islands; *Paneticano* on [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]; *minha cumbaa* on the *minjak sembawa* of [[Pangkalan Soesoe]]. The Kawi-Javanese *dasanama* — the synonym-book of ten-for-one equivalents, used by children of good family from an early age — supplied a parallel register: "die voor het eerst in dienst bij den vorst kwam", as [[Dr. J. Brandes|Brandes]] had noted, "moest met de *dasanama djarwa* weten om te gaan". The two together — the spoken riddle and the written synonym-book — formed the practical machinery of *bedekte taal*.

## Function and the foreign listener

The function of *bedekte taal* was double. Among lettered Malays and Javanese, it allowed the chronicler to record matters of royal shame or ancestral displacement without exposing them to plain view — a point [[Pieter Willem van den Broek|Van den Broek]] had noted in his translation of the *Geschiedenis van Kadiri*: "Javaansche schrijvers steeds gebruik maken van beeldspraak en vergelijkingen, wanneer zij iets van het vorstenhuis mededeelden, omdat zij straf kregen, wanneer zij de waarheid vermeldden". The foreign listener, by contrast, was systematically gulled by it: [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]], [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]], [[Marco Polo|Polo]], [[Ibn Battuta]], [[Pigafetta|Pigafetta]] and [[Tome Pires]] all, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, recorded Malay and Javanese *bedekte taal* as if it were plain ethnographic fact — and so produced travel accounts whose very naïvité is the mark of their authenticity.

## Decoding as method

The method of decoding is illustrated at every turn of [[Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java|De Roo de la Faille's book]]. To take only the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjeh-Aru war of 1539]]: *Surotilau* is read as *Soera-sakti-lawoe-kir*, the roadstead of [[Malajoer]] in Aroe; *Ilikandure* as the *ikan-on-hin-doe ka-ri* riddle; *Minhatoley* as *mina-ng-k-a da-toe Olèh-leh*; *Batorrendan* as *batoe rendang*; *Turban* as *Batoe-poer-wa-boewana*; *Paneticano* as [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]; *minha cumbaa* as the *minjak sembawa* of [[Pangkalan Soesoe]]. The same method, applied to the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]], [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten]] and [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]], produces De Roo de la Faille's reading of [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] as [[Tandjoengpoera]]-Aroe, of [[Soenan Kalidjaga]] as [[Pati Oenoes]], and of the [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend as the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate's design on the [[Deli]]-[[Asahan]] coast.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, *bedekte taal* is the single most important methodological concept underlying [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading of the chronicle record. Whether or not each of his particular identifications is accepted, the recognition that the Malay and Javanese chronicles routinely employ a veiled-language register — and that European travel accounts of the archipelago were routinely misled by it — is the framework against which any AcehWiki entry touching on the chronicle geography of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the east-coast Sumatran polities must be read.

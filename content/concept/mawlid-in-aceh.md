**Mawlid in Aceh** refers to the local celebration of the Prophet Muhammad’s birthday, which in the Acehnese tradition is known as *dikië jadjandji* (dhikr Barzandji). The ceremony combines recitation of the **Al‑Burda** and other *kasida* verses with communal singing, poetry, and devotional chants performed by a *bakāsidah*. The event is a focal point of Acehnese religious life, linking the Arabic literary heritage to indigenous cultural expressions.

During the Mawlid, participants often sing a collection of *kasida* that may include the celebrated **Al‑Burda** of [[Al‑Busiri]] as well as older poems such as the *Bānat Sucād* of [[Ka'b ibn Zuhair]]. The performance typically follows the classical *qasīda* structure: a *nasīb* recalling past hardships, a *raḥīl* describing the journey of the Prophet, and a *madīḥ* praising his virtues.

### Ritual elements
- **Recitation of the Al‑Burda** – The poem is chanted in Arabic or translated into Acehnese, serving as the spiritual centerpiece.
- **Singing of *kasida*** – Local poets compose verses that echo the themes of love, devotion, and the Prophet’s exemplary character.
- **Communal dhikr** – The *dikië jadjandji* involves collective remembrance (*dhikr*) of the Prophet, often accompanied by rhythmic clapping and the beating of drums.

The Mawlid in Aceh illustrates the syncretic nature of Acehnese Islam, where imported Arab literary forms are re‑interpreted through local language, music, and cultural symbols.

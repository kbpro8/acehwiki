**Annexatie-geest** — the spirit of annexation — was the term by which [[H. van Kol]] characterised in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[16 November]] [[1897]] the general European drive to colonial acquisition of the preceding thirty years, of which he held the Dutch conduct in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] to be an instance.

## The survey of thirty years

Van Kol stated that for some thirty years there had prevailed, above all in Central [[Europe]], "een ware geeuwhonger naar erlanging of uitbreiding van het koloniaal bezit; als hongerige gieren vloog men op verre gewesten aan en rukte ze in brokken uit elkander." — a true ravenous hunger for the acquisition or extension of colonial possession; like hungry vultures men swooped on distant regions and tore them apart in pieces. At [[Berlin|Berlijn]] a great part of Africa had been appropriated; in Central, North, East and South Azië various powers had conquered new possessions; in North and South Amerika the territory of many had been extended; and in Australië men pressed daily further into the unknown continent.

The globe being roughly divided, the European and other powers had begun to quarrel and fight among themselves for a new morsel: Russia and [[Japan]] over [[Korea]], Japanese and Americans over Hawaï; [[England]], [[Germany|Duitschland]], [[France|Frankrijk]], Oostenrijk and Russia clipping [[Egypt|Egypte]] and the Balkanland on all sides, which were spared only because agreement could not be reached over the division of the booty; France and England wrangling in Tunis and Achter-Indië as in the Niger region; England and Russia watching one another with rifle on arm on the frontier of [[Afghanistan]]; [[Madagascar|Madagaskar]] overwhelmed; and in [[Sudan|Soedan]], Senegal, [[Abyssinia|Abyssinië]] and Ethiopië Germany, England and France seeking to outflank one another — "en tot kleine landen toe, als België en [[Portugal]], ja zelfs [[Italy|Italië]] mengen zich in den veroveringskamp." — and even small countries, like Belgium and Portugal, yes even Italy, join in the struggle of conquest. In the West-Indië there was little more to divide; in [[South Africa|Zuid-Afrika]] only the [[Transvaal]] and a few small peoples resisted "de vraatzucht van Albion."

Everywhere, he said, men wished to conquer possessions; all manner of regions and climates were welcome if only one could extend one's "sfeer van invloed" and bring ever more colonies under one's authority, although one could not even administer and govern those one already had. "Ook wij bezitten een deel der wereld dat wij niet eens goed in kaart kunnen brengen, en tòch zijn wij onbevredigd, wij snakken naar meer. Steeds verder moet Neerland's gezag doordringen, zoowel op [[Borneo]], als [[New Guinea|Nieuw-Guinea]], op [[Celebes]] als in Noord-[[Sumatra]], namelijk Atjeh."

## Application to Atjeh

Van Kol's thesis was that "die dwaze annexatie-geest alleen" had driven the Dutch to venture their hand in "het wespennest Atjeh," and that new plans were being formed to make a blind leap into another wasps' nest on [[New Guinea|Nieuw Guinea]]. Because on New Guinea only a fault was being contemplated that in Atjeh had already been committed, he proposed to treat the establishment of Dutch authority on New Guinea under chapter IV, and to devote his time now to the Atjeh question.

He also used the term in the emotive form *annexatie-fanatisme*: the fields of the Solo-vallei were to be left to wither, the plains of Tegal and [[Bagelen]] to be flooded, the seaports to become unusable, and the Javanese to suffer hunger, "tot aan Nederlands annexatie-fanatisme zal zijn voldaan." — until the Netherlands' annexation fanaticism shall be satisfied.

## Counter-argument

He warned the Dutch to be careful: in times when the legal security of small nations was more and more threatened, and the Great Powers divided lands, made war and concluded peace as their interest brought with it, the Dutch ought not to offer them their own example as a pretext for imitation. Now that voices were raised that "de Amerikaansche kolossus" would drive "den Spaanschen havik" from his Cuban prey, the Dutch ought in time to let go from their claws Atjeh, which they had already tormented for a quarter of a century.

[[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] answered the general thesis on [[17 November]] with the argument that although the starting point of European taking-possession had seldom been just and mostly very material in kind, the outcome taught that the action proceeding from Europe had in the end been a blessing to the populations concerned — that "met dezen zeer krommen stok nochtans in den regel een rechte slag is gedaan." — with this very crooked stick a straight blow has nevertheless as a rule been struck.

**Atjeh as a prospective province**, as recorded in *[[De staatkundige ontwikkeling van Indonesië in de 20e eeuw|De staatkundige ontwikkeling van Indonesië in de 20e eeuw]]* (Amsterdam: [[Koninklijk Instituut voor de Tropen|Koninklijk Instituut voor de Tropen]]), is the mid-1950s consideration — recorded in the work's survey of the administrative division of Indonesia — that Atjeh would be granted the status of a province. The work records: "Men overweegt thans een uitbreiding van het aantal provincies, waarbij bv. Borneo in 3 en Celebes in 2 provincies zou worden verdeeld. Ook aan Atjeh zou de status van provincie worden toegekend" — one is now considering an expansion of the number of provinces, whereby for example Borneo would be divided into 3 and Celebes into 2 provinces. Also to Atjeh the status of province would be granted.

## The provincial division of Indonesia

The work records that the Republik Indonesia was initially divided into 10 provinces: West-Java; Midden-Java; Oost-Java; Noord-Sumatra; Midden-Sumatra; Zuid-Sumatra; Kalimantan (Borneo); Maluku (Molukken); Sulawesi (Celebes); and Nusa Tenggara (Kleine Soenda-eilanden). Sedert kort (recently), the work notes, the province of West-Irian also existed, formed from several districts of the Noord-Molukken *daerah* and the territory of Nederlands Nieuw-Guinea. The consideration to expand the number of provinces — with Borneo divided into 3, Celebes into 2, and Atjeh elevated to provincial status — was thus a mid-1950s development within the existing provincial framework.

## Atjeh's administrative history

The prospective provincial status for Atjeh must be read against the background of Atjeh's administrative history in the early post-colonial period. Atjeh had been effectively self-governing under the Ulama since the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|1945–1946 social revolution]], and its *de jure* autonomous status had been confirmed by [[Mr. Sjafrudin Prawiranegara|Sjafrudin Prawiranegara]] in August [[1949]]. The central government's partial disavowal of this concession, and the subsequent folding of Atjeh into the merged province of North Sumatra in early [[1951]], had been a major grievance of the Atjehnese Ulama under [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]], and was a principal cause of the [[Atjèh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia (1953)|Daud Beureu'eh revolt of 1953]]. The mid-1950s consideration of granting Atjeh provincial status — recorded by Van Meurs — was thus, in context, a response to the revolt and a recognition of the Atjehnese demand for administrative autonomy.

The further administrative organization described in the work — the provinces divided into *keresidenan* (residencies), these into *kabupaten* (regencies), these into *kedemangan* (districts), and these into *afdeelingen* — is noted as still being the same as that of the Netherlands Indies, a continuity of the colonial administrative structure into the post-colonial period.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Van Meurs record of Atjeh as a prospective province is significant as a mid-1950s source for the consideration of Atjeh's elevation to provincial status — a consideration that must be read against the background of the [[Atjèh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia (1953)|1953 revolt]] and the Atjehnese demand for administrative autonomy. The record complements the corpus's existing entries on the [[Formation of the Province of North Sumatra (1951)|formation of the Province of North Sumatra]], on the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|1945–1946 social revolution]], and on the [[Atjèh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia (1953)|Daud Beureu'eh revolt]], and it furnishes the metropolitan Dutch perspective on Atjeh's administrative future within the post-colonial Indonesian state.

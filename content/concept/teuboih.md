**Teuboih** (Malay *tebus*) was the redemption of a pledge of land in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] — the act by which a pledgor recovered ground given in *[[Gala|gala]]*.

## Fixing of the term

The term of redemption was fixed by written contract or by oral agreement in the presence of witnesses. A minimum duration of the contract was sometimes stipulated, namely where the pledgee intended to make the land more remunerative by better irrigation or other improvements at his own cost, and therefore needed some certainty as to how long he would enjoy it.

## Single annual opportunity

There was opportunity for redemption only once a year, namely between the harvest — *[[Keumeukoh|keumeukoh]]* — and the first working of the ground, *[[Meu'oeë|meu'oeë]]*, of the following season.

## Practical failure of redemption

Because the pledge sum, initially about half the sale value, could gradually be raised by repeated further borrowing until it practically equalled the sale value, redemption in such cases generally never took place at all, and the pledged field might remain so long in one family's hands that its origin was forgotten in good faith — the source of the protracted lawsuits between the heirs of original owners and those of money-lenders described by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] in *[[De Atjehers (Snouck Hurgronje)|De Atjehers]]*, Vol. I, p. 315.

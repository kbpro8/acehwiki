**Bamboe-doeri** (Dutch for "thorny bamboo"; modern Indonesian *bambu duri*) was the thorny bamboo pallisade used by the Atjehers as the principal outer defence of their fortified kampongs and bentings in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The bamboe-doeri beplanting — sometimes of five metres' width — was the most formidable obstacle to Dutch storming parties throughout the war, and is mentioned in the source work in connection with the stormings of the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|missigit]] of [[April 1873|April 1873]], the fight near kampong Lemboe of [[December 1873|December 1873]], the sawah of Lamkrak in [[1878]], and especially the [[Batoe Iliq Expedition (July 1880)|Batoe Iliq expedition]] of [[July 1880|July 1880]], where three successive Dutch stormings failed against the impregnable bamboe-doeri palisades of [[Batoe Iliq|Batoe Iliq]].

## As a Fortification

The bamboe-doeri was a thick hedge or pallisade of thorny bamboo planted around the enemy's fortification, sometimes of considerable width. In the fight near kampong [[Lemboe|Lemboe]] of [[25 December]] [[25 December]] [[1873]], the soldiers of the right half of the third bataljon "had to hew a way through the heavy bamboe-doern verhakking of five metres' width" with their kapmes. In the [[Batoe Iliq Expedition (July 1880)|Batoe Iliq expedition]] of [[July 1880|July 1880]], the three successive stormings of [[15 July]] [[15 July]], [[22 July]] [[22 July]] and [[1 August]] [[1 August]] all failed against the impregnable bamboe-doeri palisades. "All attempts to remove the bamboe-doern or to break through it failed, by the murderous fire from the benting and by the masses of stones thrown from it upon the troops... Even an attempt was made to sprinkle the bamboe-doern under the enemy's fire with petroleum and to set it alight, but in vain."

## Significance

The bamboe-doeri was the characteristic form of Acehnese fortification in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], and its effectiveness against Dutch storming parties is one of the principal reasons for the high cost of Dutch operations in Atjeh. The combination of bamboe-doeri palisade, earthen parapet, and the hail of stones hurled from the parapet, made the typical Acehnese benting a formidable obstacle to infantry assault, and frequently required the use of artillery to open a breach.

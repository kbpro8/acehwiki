**The Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie** ("Self-Government Ordinance," **Stbl. 1914 No. 24**) was the Dutch colonial ordinance promulgated in [[1914]] that codified the rights, competences, and obligations of the Gouvernement on one hand and of the [[Zelfbesturen|zelfbesturen (self-governing territories)]] on the other under the regime of the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] that had been in force since [[1899]]. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the ordinance was the legal capstone of the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] order in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the wider Buitenbezittingen: the ordinance that translated the *de facto* extension of Dutch authority since the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|betrayal of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]] into a *de jure* framework of colonial governance.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] appears in the chapter on administrative organisation. The Bureau frames the ordinance as the codification of the rights, competences, and obligations of the Gouvernement and the zelfbesturen under the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] regime: "in accordance with the purpose of the K. V. [Korte Verklaring], in order to give the desired guidance to the administrative gesture of the local officials, [the Gouvernement regulated] the direction in which our indispensable involvement with the landschappen must move, and the principles on which we must base our action — in short, the rights, competences, and obligations of the Gouvernement on one hand and of the zelfbesturen under vigueur of the K. V. on the other — by the soon-to-be-enacted ordinance (Stbl. 1914 No. 24), whose provisions mainly correspond with the provisions of the detailed political contracts."

The Bureau notes that the ordinance's provisions "mainly correspond with the provisions of the detailed political contracts" — that is, the [[Politieke Contracten|politieke contracten]] that the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] had progressively replaced since [[1899]]. The ordinance thus represented not a radical break with the older contractual tradition but a codification of the new Korte Verklaring regime in terms continuous with the older one — a continuity that the Bureau frames as the legal expression of the broader post-[[1896]] Dutch strategic reorientation.

The Bureau also identifies the principal innovation of the new ordinance: the principle that "the revenues of the landschap, instead of being — as formerly — considered as the private revenues of the rulers, who only inadequately spent a part of them for the benefit of land and people, belong to the landschap and must therefore be paid into a landschapskas (territorial treasury), in order to fund from it all kinds of expenditures in the interest of land and people, including — under our guidance and oversight — the income of the rulers in the form of a 'civiele lijst'." This principle — the principle of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] — is identified by the Bureau as the principal innovation of the newer regulation of the political relationship, whether laid down in contracts or in the Korte Verklaring.

## Significance

The [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] of [[1914]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the legal capstone of the post-[[1896]] [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] and the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch "effective authority" across the Buitenbezittingen. The ordinance's codification of the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] regime, its formal establishment of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] principle, and its provision for the "civiele lijst" of the native rulers together mark the legal consolidation of the new colonial order — the order that the post-pacification administrative, financial, and police innovations of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade had been building toward.

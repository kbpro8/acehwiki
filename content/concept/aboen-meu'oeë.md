**Aboen meu'oeë** was the most one-sided of the three Atjehnese forms of *[[Mawaih|mawaih]]* over rice fields, in which the *mawaih*-giver supplied everything and the cultivator received only one-fifth of the harvest. The term, [[D. W. N. de Boer]] explained, meant roughly the putting out to contract of the working — *meu'oèé* being the working of *[[Sawah|sawah]]*'s — and expressed the relation between the parties clearly enough.

## Terms

Under this figure the giver supplied the ground, the plough cattle and the *[[Bibit|bibit]]*; bore alone the entire *[[Oepah poela|oepah poela]]* (planting wage); and further defrayed the most indispensable *[[Belandja|belandja]]* of the *mawaih*-taker — his clothing, food and smoking materials. In such a case the "landheer" received four-fifths and the cultivator of the *sawah* only one-fifth of the harvest.

## Position among the mawaih forms

The three forms formed a descending scale for the cultivator: one-half under *mawaih bahagi doea*, one-third under *mawaih bahagi lhée*, and one-fifth under *aboen meu'oeë* — the share diminishing as the giver's contribution of capital, cattle, seed, wage and subsistence increased.

**Sjattariyyah** (also *Sjatariah*; Arabic *Shattariyya*) was a mystical *tarikat* (Sufi brotherhood) introduced to the Indonesian Archipelago by [[Abdoerraoef van Singkel|Abdoerraoef van Singkel]] in the 17th century. It was one of the principal mystical brotherhoods in the Archipelago, with its centre in [[Aceh|Atjèh]].

## Introduction

[[Abdoerraoef van Singkel|Abdoerraoef van Singkel]] (also Teungkoe di Koeala, Abdöra'öh; in Arabic sources Abd al-Ra'uf al-Singkili) was a Malay scholar from [[Singkel|Singkel]] who studied more than twenty years in Mecca, Medina, and other Arab cities. He settled in Atjèh in the second half of the 17th century and introduced the Sjattariyyah *tarikat* to the Archipelago.

## Other tarikat's in Atjèh

Other *tarikat*'s known in Atjèh included:
- **Naqsjibandija** (Naqshbandiyya, called *Na tjabandi* in Atjèhnese)
- **Kadriah** (Qadiriyya) — founded by *c*Abdoelqadir Djïlanï (d. [[1166]])
- **Sammanïja** — known for its loud *dzikr*-exercises that gave rise to the *sadati* game

The masses knew nothing of these *tarikat*'s, likely due to the former war state. The Sjattariyyah was the most widely established, owing to Abdoerraoef's influence.

## Abdoerraoef's grave

The grave of [[Abdoerraoef van Singkel|Abdoerraoef]] at the mouth (*koeala*) of the Atjèh river was formerly the chief pilgrimage site of Atjèh, now overshadowed by the grave of [[Teungkoe Andjöng|Teungkoe Andjöng]]. A giant ray (*paröë*) near the grave is said to wreck vessels of those disrespectful to the saint. Pilgrims from [[Troemon|Troemon]] and [[Langsa|Langsa]] still visited the grave.

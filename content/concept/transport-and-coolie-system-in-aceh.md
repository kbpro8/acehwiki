**Transport and Coolie System in Aceh** was the logistical apparatus of human laborers, pack horses, and supply trains relied upon by the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] to transport provisions, ammunition, and artillery across the difficult terrain of the [[Aceh Sultanate]] between [[1873]] and [[1876]]. The system's severe limitations constituted the primary tactical bottleneck in Dutch offensive campaigns.

## Constraints in the First and Second Expeditions

During the [[First Aceh Expedition]] in April [[1873]], expeditionary forces landed at [[Meuraxa]] with only a small labor force, severely hampering movement toward [[Kraton of Aceh]]. 

By contrast, during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], General [[J. van Swieten]] had at his disposal approximately 2,500 free coolies (*free coolies (*vrije koelies*)*) alongside forced convict laborers (*dwangarbeiders*) (forced laborers) and 150 pack horses. Despite this larger force, advancing across flooded rice paddies and marshes between [[Pedro Punt]], [[Penajoeng]], and [[Kraton of Aceh]] required immense labor and time.

## Logistical Bottlenecks Under General Pel

When General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] resumed offensive operations in December [[1875]] and early [[1876]] during [[General Pel's Campaign in Aceh]], transport limitations dictated his strategy. Although his total army exceeded 7,000 men, [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] deployed only four half-battalions into the field at any one time, keeping the remainder in garrison at [[Pekan Badak]], [[Sinangri]], and Kotta Radja.

As reported by front-line correspondents in the *Algemeen Dagblad van Nederlandsch-Indië* on [[30 December]] [[1875]] and [[2 January]] [[1876]], the "sluggish pace" of Dutch columns was caused entirely by the [[Transport and Coolie System in Aceh]]. Modern military requirements for artillery, ammunition, and rations meant that enhanced equipment actually reduced tactical mobility in the tropics.

**Gambus (Aceh)** (also *gambus Atjeh*, *qanbus*; Dutch: *gamboes*; Arabic: *qanbus* / *qanbus*) is a **short-necked, pear-shaped, fretless lute** with **seven strings** (in four courses: three double courses + one single), used in **[[Acheh]]** (Atjēh) as a core instrument of the **Islamic instrumental complex** introduced via the Indian Ocean trade and religious networks. It is the Acehnese variant of the **Yemeni *qanbus*** / **Ottoman *kemançe*-type lute** family that spread across the Malay-Indonesian world with Islam from the [[13th century]] onward.

## Organology

- **Hornbostel-Sachs classification**: 321.321 (short-necked lute, plucked)
- **Body**: pear-shaped, carved from a single block of wood (jackfruit *nangka*, *meranti*, *kayu malam*), hollowed out; soundboard of soft wood (often *pulai* or *sengon*) with soundholes (often three rosettes)
- **Neck**: short, fretless, glued or inserted into body; no pegbox — tuning pegs inserted laterally into the neck
- **Strings**: 7 strings in 4 courses (typically *gg–c'c'–f'f'–b'b'* or similar fourths tuning); historically gut or silk, now nylon/metal
- **Bridge**: movable, placed on soundboard
- **Plectrum**: *risha* / *plectrum* of horn, tortoiseshell, or plastic (held in right hand)
- **Playing position**: held horizontally across the chest, neck pointing left (right-handed player)

## Historical and Regional Context

**[[Jaap Kunst]]** in *[[Muziek en Dans in de Buitengewesten]]* ([[1946]], p. 10) lists the *gambus* among the **"typisch Mohammedaansche speeltuigen"** ("typically Mohammedan instruments") **for Atjēh**, alongside the *[[Rabana (Aceh)|rabana]]*, *[[Marwas]]*, *[[Bangei]]*, and *[[Serunai (Aceh)|serunai]]*. This classification reflects the **Islamic instrumental stratum** that arrived in northern Sumatra via **[[Gujarat]]**, **[[Malabar]]**, **[[Hadramawt]]**, and **[[Yemen]]** — distinct from the indigenous (*hareubab*, *geudoemba*, *tjanang triëng*) and Hindu-Buddhist strata.

The *gambus* entered **Aceh** — the "Veranda of Mecca" (*Serambi Mekkah*) — early, likely [[13th century]]–[[14th century]], carried by **[[Hadrami]]** traders, **[[Shattariyya]]** Sufi missionaries, and **[[Malabar]]** merchants. It became central to **[[Sufi]]** *[[Zikir]]* / *[[Dzikir]]* ceremonies, **[[Hikayat]]** recitation, **[[Nasyid]]**, and court entertainment in the **[[Aceh Sultanate]]** ([[1496]]–[[1903]]).

| Region | Local Name | Strings | Courses | Notes |
|--------|------------|---------|---------|-------|
| **[[Acheh]]** | **Gambus** | 7 | 4 | Pear-shaped, short neck; *qanbus* type |
| **[[Malay Peninsula]]** (Johor, Riau) | *Gambus* | 9–12 | 4–6 | Larger, longer neck; *oud*-influenced |
| **[[Riau-Lingga]]** | *Gambus* | 11–12 | 6 | Court tradition, *Ghazal* accompaniment |
| **[[Palembang]]** | *Gambus* | 9–11 | 5 | *Gambus Melayu* style |
| **[[Minangkabau]]** | *Gambus* | 7–9 | 4 | *Dendang* accompaniment |
| **[[Java]]** (coastal) | *Gambus* | 7–9 | 4 | *Gambus Kroncong*, *Gambus Hadrah* |
| **[[Yemen]]** (origin) | *Qanbus* | 4–7 | 2–4 | Skin soundboard, no frets |
| **[[Oman]]** / **[[Zanzibar]]** | *Qanbus* / *Gabusi* | 4–7 | 2–4 | Swahili *taarab* lute |

The Acehnese *gambus* retains the **Yemeni *qanbus* morphology** (pear-shaped, skin or wood soundboard, short neck, lateral pegs) more faithfully than the Malay *gambus* which evolved toward the **Arab *oud*** (larger, longer neck, wooden soundboard, pegbox).

## Role in Acehnese Music

The *gambus* is the **principal melodic-harmonic instrument** in:

- **[[Zikir]]** / **[[Dzikir]]** / **[[Hadrah]]** — Sufi remembrance ceremonies (*Shattariyya*, *Qadiriyya*, *Naqshbandiyya* orders)
- **[[Hikayat]]** recitation — *Hikayat Prang Sabi*, *Hikayat Aceh*, *Hikayat Muhammad Hanafiah*
- **[[Nasyid]]** / **[[Qasidah]]** — Islamic devotional singing
- **[[Ghazal]]** / **[[Kasidah]]** — Malay-Arabic poetic song forms
- **[[Seudati]]** / **[[Sedati (dance)|Sedati]]** — processional dance (melodic lead)
- **[[Meuseukat]]** — women's seated dance-song (accompaniment)
- **[[Tari Saman]]** — occasionally in modern staged versions

It provides **heterophonic melodic doubling** of the vocal line, **drone/pedal tones** on open strings, and **rhythmic-harmonic punctuation** (*tepak*) at phrase endings.

## Repertoire and Tuning

Traditional Acehnese *gambus* tuning (approximate, variable):

- **Course 1 (single)**: *g* (low drone)
- **Course 2 (double)**: *c'* *c'*
- **Course 3 (double)**: *f'* *f'*
- **Course 4 (double)**: *b'* *b'* (or *a'* *a'*)

This **fourths-based tuning** facilitates the **pentatonic/heptatonic modes** (*laras*) of Acehnese music, which correspond loosely to **Arab *maqamat*** (e.g., *Bayati*, *Hijaz*, *Rast*, *Saba*) adapted to local *[[Dendang]]* melodic contours.

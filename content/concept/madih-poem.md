**Madih (praise)** is the concluding section of a classical Arabic *qasīda* in which the poet offers laudatory verses to the subject of the poem, most commonly the Prophet Muhammad in devotional works. In the Acehnese *kasida* tradition, the *madīḥ* functions similarly, providing a climactic affirmation of the Prophet’s virtues and often concluding the *Mawlid* (*dikië jadjandji*) ceremony.

The *madīḥ* in Acehnese performances may incorporate local linguistic flourishes, rhythmic patterns, and instrumental accompaniment, yet it preserves the essential Arabic rhetorical devices of hyperbole, metaphor, and theological affirmation. The section frequently ends with a supplication for the Prophet’s intercession and a request for divine reward for the poet and the listeners.

### Role in Acehnese devotional practice
- **Liturgical climax** – The *madīḥ* marks the high point of the *Mawlid* celebration, after the *nasīb* and *raḥīl* have prepared the audience.
- **Cultural synthesis** – By embedding Acehnese idioms within the *madīḥ*, performers demonstrate the adaptability of Arabic poetic forms to local religious expression.

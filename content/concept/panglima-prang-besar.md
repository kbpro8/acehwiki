**Panglima Prang Besar** — Acehnese for "Great War Commander" — was an extraordinary [[Achehnese]] military title created by the [[Dutch]] colonial government and conferred on [[Teuku Umar]] (under his Dutch-recognised name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*) by [[Government Resolution of 5 September 1893, No. 1 geheim|Government Resolution No. 1 geheim of 5 September 1893]]. Under this appointment, confirmed by an [[Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893|oath of loyalty of 30 September 1893]], Umar received Dutch weapons, ammunition and salary to lead a native auxiliary *legioen* of some forty [[panglima]]s against the recalcitrant [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkraksche Moekims]]. The title was revoked by [[Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] (Bijlage VI), which simultaneously dismissed Umar as [[hoeloebalang]] of [[Lepong]] and decreed that he should thenceforth be officially designated by the name *Toekoe Oemar*.

## Etymology and Meaning

The title is Acehnese: commander, from Sanskrit *paranīpati* via Malay, *prang* or war, great. A *Panglima Prang Besar* is therefore literally a "Great War Commander" — an office of extraordinary, almost dictatorial scope, ranking above the ordinary [[panglima]]s of individual *bendes* and above the hereditary [[hoeloebalang]]s and [[panglima sagi]]s. The title was not a traditional office of the [[Aceh Sultanate]] but a Dutch colonial innovation, designed to give a single cooperative Achehnese leader the formal authority to marshal other Achehnese forces in the Government's name. The Dutch text spells the office *Panglima Prang Besar of the Gouvernement* — explicitly anchoring it in the colonial service rather than in the Sultanate.

## Historical Context

The Panglima Prang Besar experiment ran from [[5 September]] [[1893]] to [[26 April]] [[1896]]. During those thirty months Umar, armed and paid by the [[Dutch]], nominally led operations against the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lamkraksche]] and other hostile moekims — operations which Kruisheer reveals were largely "sham battle" under the doctrine of [[Teungkoe Koeta Karang]]. Umar received 25,000 *Beaumontpatronen*, 380 *achterlaad-* and 500 *voorlaadgeweren*, and a salary; in exchange he was expected to clear the Lamkrak moekims and protect the [[buitenlinie]]. His defection on [[29 March]] [[1896]] turned this entire apparatus against the Dutch. After three letters of [[30 March]], [[12 April]] and [[23 April]] [[1896]] demanding ƒ150,000 per month and a Royal decoration, the Government dismissed him per [[Government Telegram of 26 April 1896, No. 409|Telegram No. 409 of 26 April 1896]] and [[Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11|Resolution No. 159/11]].

## Examples in the Source

Kruisheer traces the Panglima Prang Besar office in detail. The original appointment is cited as *Gouvernementsbesluit van 5 September 1893 No. 1 geheim* authorising Umar "under the name Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan as Panglima Prang Besar of the Government of Nederlandsch-Indië". The [[Oath of Loyalty and Confirmation Act of 30 September 1893]] is recorded separately. The [[Government Resolution of 19 January 1896, No. 9|Resolution No. 9 of 19 January 1896]] appointed Umar (as Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan) hoeloebalang of Lepong — a complementary recognition. The dismissal is recorded in Bijlage VI (Resolution No. 159/11 of 26 April 1896): Umar is "ontslagen as Panglima Prang Besar of the Gouvernement and as Oeleebalang of Lepong", and "henceforth to be designated in all official documents as *Toekoe Oemar*". The text of Umar's own letters repeatedly invokes the title — his [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar|second letter of 12 April 1896]] requests that his son succeed him as *Panglima Prang*.

## Significance

The Panglima Prang Besar was the most ambitious — and the most catastrophic — single instrument of Dutch "alliance" policy in the [[Aceh War]]. By creating an extraordinary, centralised Achehnese military office and filling it with a single cooperative chief, the Dutch attempted to short-circuit the confederal structure of the [[sagi]]s and to use Achehnese arms against Achehnese resistance. The total collapse of this policy in [[1896]] — Umar's defection, the dismissal, the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] — produced the [[Besluit of 9 May 1896]] forbidding further weapon distribution to Achehnese heads, and inaugurated the more coercive phase of the war that would culminate in the [[Koloniale Pacificate|pacification]] policies of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

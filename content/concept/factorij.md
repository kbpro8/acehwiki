**Factorij** — the Dutch **factory** or trading post, the principal commercial institution of the early Dutch East India trade — was the physical establishment (a lodge, house, or fortified compound) where the Dutch factors (*factoren*) lived, stored their merchandise, and conducted their trade with the local population, under the protection of the local ruler or — increasingly after [[1609]] — of a Dutch fort. The factorij was the principal Dutch commercial institution at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] (the lodge "Zeelant" of [[1601]]-[[1602]]) and at [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] (the factories at Lontor and Neira from [[1599]] onward), and the institutional foundation of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s commercial network in the East Indies. The factorij is the principal named Dutch commercial institution of Van der Woude's [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The lodge "Zeelant" at Atjeh

The first Dutch factorij on Achehnese soil was the lodge "Zeelant", established by the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland Company's mission of August 1601]] under the terms of the sultan's permission. The Zeelanders obtained leave to settle two commiezen and three assistants on shore at the lodge, which they stocked with money and merchandise. The lodge was the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil and the prototype of the system of factories that the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] would establish across the archipelago after [[1602]]. The lodge "Zeelant" survived only a few weeks: on [[13 January]] [[1602]] a great fire broke out in Atjeh, destroying some 200 houses; the Zeeland lodge burned down with the rest, though with little loss of goods. The VOC did not rebuild the lodge, preferring to operate at Atjeh through visiting fleets rather than a permanent factory.

## The Banda factories

The Dutch factories at [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] — at [[Lontor|Lontor]] and [[Neira|Neira]] from [[1599]] onward — were the principal Dutch commercial establishments in the spice islands in the pre-VOC period. [[Jacob van Heemskerck|Jacob van Heemskerck]] left two factors and eighteen men at Lontor and Neira in March [[1599]] with a cargo worth over ƒ 38,000 in money and goods. The factories survived in good order through the [[1600]]-[[1602]] period, despite the Portuguese-inspired attempt by the prince of [[Toeban|Toeban]] on Java to abduct the Dutch factors in April [[1600]]. The factories' destruction in the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|Verhoeff affair of May 1609]] and the subsequent construction of Fort Nassau marked the transition from factory to fort — the institutional transition from commercial establishment to military-administrative compound that would define the VOC's conduct at Banda and elsewhere after [[1609]].

## The factor and the fort

The factorij was the principal commercial institution of the early Dutch East India trade, but its institutional character changed over time. In the pre-VOC period, the factorij was a lodge or house, dependent on the local ruler's protection and operating without fortification. In the early VOC period — particularly after the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|Verhoeff affair of 1609]] — the factorij was increasingly fortified, and the factor's role shifted from commercial to military-administrative. The [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force, articulated in the [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]], completed the transition: the factorij became the fort, and the factor became the governor.

## Significance

The factorij is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch commercial institution of the early Dutch-Indies trade and the institutional foundation of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s commercial network in the East Indies. The lodge "Zeelant" at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — established by the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]] — was the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil and the prototype of the system of factories that the VOC would establish across the archipelago. The Banda factories at [[Lontor|Lontor]] and [[Neira|Neira]] — from [[1599]] onward — were the principal Dutch commercial establishments in the spice islands in the pre-VOC period, and their destruction in the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|Verhoeff affair of May 1609]] marked the institutional transition from factory to fort that would define the VOC's conduct after [[1609]]. The [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force, articulated in the [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]], completed the transition: the factorij became the fort, and the factor became the governor. The factorij's role in the Dutch-Atjeh relationship — as the lodge "Zeelant" of [[1601]]-[[1602]] — was both foundational and brief: the first Dutch commercial establishment on Achehnese soil, and the prototype that the VOC chose not to replicate.

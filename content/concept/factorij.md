**Factorij** — the Dutch **factory** or trading post, the principal commercial institution of the early Dutch East India trade — was the physical establishment (a lodge, house, or fortified compound) where the Dutch factors (*factoren*) lived, stored their merchandise, and conducted their trade with the local population, under the protection of the local ruler or — increasingly after [[1609]] — of a Dutch fort. The factorij was the principal Dutch commercial institution at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] (the lodge "Zeelant" of [[1601]]-[[1602]]) and at [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] (the factories at Lontor and Neira from [[1599]] onward), and the institutional foundation of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s commercial network in the East Indies. The factorij is the principal named Dutch commercial institution of Van der Woude's [[Coen, Koopman van Heeren Zeventien (1948)|Van der Woude (1948)]].

## The lodge "Zeelant" at Atjeh

The first Dutch factorij on Achehnese soil was the lodge "Zeelant", established by the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland Company's mission of August 1601]] under the terms of the sultan's permission. The Zeelanders obtained leave to settle two commiezen and three assistants on shore at the lodge, which they stocked with money and merchandise. The lodge was the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil and the prototype of the system of factories that the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]] would establish across the archipelago after [[1602]]. The lodge "Zeelant" survived only a few weeks: on [[13 January]] [[1602]] a great fire broke out in Atjeh, destroying some 200 houses; the Zeeland lodge burned down with the rest, though with little loss of goods. The VOC did not rebuild the lodge, preferring to operate at Atjeh through visiting fleets rather than a permanent factory.

## The Banda factories

The Dutch factories at [[Banda-eilanden|Banda]] — at [[Lontor|Lontor]] and [[Neira|Neira]] from [[1599]] onward — were the principal Dutch commercial establishments in the spice islands in the pre-VOC period. [[Jacob van Heemskerck|Jacob van Heemskerck]] left two factors and eighteen men at Lontor and Neira in March [[1599]] with a cargo worth over ƒ 38,000 in money and goods. The factories survived in good order through the [[1600]]-[[1602]] period, despite the Portuguese-inspired attempt by the prince of [[Toeban|Toeban]] on Java to abduct the Dutch factors in April [[1600]]. The factories' destruction in the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|Verhoeff affair of May 1609]] and the subsequent construction of Fort Nassau marked the transition from factory to fort — the institutional transition from commercial establishment to military-administrative compound that would define the VOC's conduct at Banda and elsewhere after [[1609]].

## The factor and the fort

The factorij was the principal commercial institution of the early Dutch East India trade, but its institutional character changed over time. In the pre-VOC period, the factorij was a lodge or house, dependent on the local ruler's protection and operating without fortification. In the early VOC period — particularly after the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|Verhoeff affair of 1609]] — the factorij was increasingly fortified, and the factor's role shifted from commercial to military-administrative. The [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force, articulated in the [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]], completed the transition: the factorij became the fort, and the factor became the governor.

## Significance

The factorij is, in Van der Woude's narrative, the principal Dutch commercial institution of the early Dutch-Indies trade and the institutional foundation of the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]]'s commercial network in the East Indies. The lodge "Zeelant" at [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — established by the [[Zeeland Company's Atjeh mission (1601)|Zeeland mission of August 1601]] — was the first Dutch factory on Achehnese soil and the prototype of the system of factories that the VOC would establish across the archipelago. The Banda factories at [[Lontor|Lontor]] and [[Neira|Neira]] — from [[1599]] onward — were the principal Dutch commercial establishments in the spice islands in the pre-VOC period, and their destruction in the [[Murder of Verhoeff at Banda (May 1609)|Verhoeff affair of May 1609]] marked the institutional transition from factory to fort that would define the VOC's conduct after [[1609]]. The [[Jan Pieterszoon Coen|Coen]] programme of monopoly-by-force, articulated in the [[Discours of 1 January 1614|Discours of 1 January 1614]], completed the transition: the factorij became the fort, and the factor became the governor. The factorij's role in the Dutch-Atjeh relationship — as the lodge "Zeelant" of [[1601]]-[[1602]] — was both foundational and brief: the first Dutch commercial establishment on Achehnese soil, and the prototype that the VOC chose not to replicate.

## Account from Stapel's *De Koloniën* (1931)

**Loge** (plural *loges*; also *logie*, *handelsloge*) was the standard [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] term for a commercial lodge or factory — the resident trading establishment that constituted the basic institutional unit of Compagnie presence at any native port. In Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* the loge is the building block of the Westkust system: every pepper port of significance had its loge, and the history of the Westkust is in large part the history of the establishment, withdrawal, restoration and fortification of loges.

## The loge as institution

A Compagnie loge was a resident trading house, typically manned by a small European staff (an *opperhoofd*, or chief, with assistants and servants) and equipped with warehouse space for the storage of pepper, camphor, gold and other commodities. The loge was the institutional counterpart of the native ruler's court: it was the place where the Compagnie's commercial business was transacted, and the place from which the Compagnie's diplomatic relations with the native ruler were conducted.

The loge was distinct from the *factorij* (a more limited commercial establishment) and from the *fort* or *sterkte* (a fortified military post). In practice the distinctions were often blurred: a loge could be fortified (as at [[Perak]] after 1655, where the Compagnie was permitted to "rebuild a factory (*kantoor*) and warehouse"), and a fort could host a loge within its walls (as at [[Poeloe Tjingkoek]] from 1666).

## The Westkust loges

The principal Westkust loges in the [[Painansch Contract]] era were:

- The loge at [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] itself — established briefly in [[1601]], withdrawn, restored, withdrawn again in [[1623]], restored by the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (5 October 1659)|1659 peace]], withdrawn in [[1663]].
- The loge at [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] — one of the four pepper ports of the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (15 February 1641)|1641 Schouten treaty]]; one of the four definitive factories established by [[Jacob Cau|Cau]] in [[1664]]–[[1665]].
- The loge at [[Priaman|Priaman]] — one of the four pepper ports; one of the four definitive factories; re-fortified in [[1684]].
- The loge at [[Padang|Padang]] — one of the four pepper ports; one of the four definitive factories; the head office (*hoofdkantoor*) of the Westkust from [[1664]]–[[1665]].
- The loge at [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] — one of the four pepper ports; "for the present no longer deemed necessary" (*voorloopig niet meer noodig geacht*) in [[1665]], but re-established at various points in the eighteenth century.
- The loge at [[Salido|Salido]] / [[Poeloe Tjingkoek|Poeloe Tjingkoek]] — one of the four definitive factories; the centre of the southern Westkust gold-mining complex.
- The loge at [[Perak|Perak]] — established under the [[Treaty of Atjeh-Perak (15 August 1650)|1650 treaty]]; overrun in [[1651]]; restored under the [[1655]] peace; withdrawn in [[1663]] in favour of shipboard trade.
- The loge at [[Baros|Baros]] — restored in [[1730]] after long abandonment.

## The loge at Atjeh

The history of the Compagnie's loge at Atjeh is a case study in the institution's fragility. Stapel records that "the in 1616 lifted factory on Atjeh was restored in 1620, but was again lifted in 1623; thenceforth only a single assistant of the Compagnie remained resident on Atjeh" (*De in 1616 gelichte factorij op Atjeh was in 1620 hersteld, maar werd in 1623 andermaal opgeheven; sedert bleef slechts één assistent van de Compagnie op Atjeh gevestigd*). The [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (5 October 1659)|1659 peace]] restored the right to re-establish a loge at Atjeh, but the [[1663]] Compagnie withdrawal of its Atjeh and Perak factories ended the permanent presence.

## The fortified loge

In some cases the loge was fortified with a *pagger* (a bamboo palisade) or with more substantial works. The [[1643]] treaty with [[Djambi|Djambi]] permitted the Compagnie "to surround its loge with a fortified pagger" (*omringen door een versterkten pagger*). The [[1641]]–[[1642]] Palembang treaties permitted the Compagnie "to build a fortified loge and to place there eight pieces of gun" (*een versterkte loge te bouwen en daar acht stukken geschut te plaatsen*). The [[1666]] fort on [[Poeloe Tjingkoek]] and the [[1684]] fort at [[Priaman|Priaman]] are the most substantial Westkust fortifications, but the principle of fortifying the loge is general.

## Significance

The loge is the building block of the Westkust system. Every Westkust treaty — the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (15 February 1641)|1641 Schouten treaty]], the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (November 1649)|1649 Truytman treaties]], the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (5 October 1659)|1659 peace]], the [[Painansch Contract|1663 Painansch Contract]], the [[Padang Conference (29 August 1680)|1680 Padang Conference]] — is in some sense a treaty about loges: where they may be established, what goods they may trade, what fortifications they may have, what native rights they must respect. The history of the Westkust is the history of the establishment, withdrawal, restoration and fortification of loges, and the [[Painansch Contract]] system is, in institutional terms, a network of loges bound by a common treaty form.

**The geconcentreerde linie** — the concentrated line — was the defensive system that the colonial army had used in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] before the Graafland era, and that was abandoned when the Graafland era began. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), the geconcentreerde linie is the unsound system that preceded the all-out offensive: the defensive line that the colonial army had held in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] before going over to the offensive.

## The unsound system

"The time, he told, in which the unsound system of the concentrated line was happily abandoned, the time in which the Graafland era began." The unsound system of the concentrated line was happily abandoned when the Graafland era began. The geconcentreerde linie is therefore the book's name for the defensive system that preceded the offensive: the unsound system that the Graafland era replaced with the all-out offensive.

## The concentrated line and the offensive

The geconcentreerde linie — the concentrated line — was the colonial army's defensive system in [[Atjeh|Aceh]]: a line of posts that the colonial army held, behind which the Acehnese resistance operated. The book's — and the colonial memory's — judgment on the concentrated line was damning: it was the defensive line that had failed to suppress the Acehnese resistance, and was happily abandoned when the Graafland era went over to the all-out offensive.

## The book's sequence

The book's sequence — the concentrated line (the unsound system) — the Graafland era (the all-out offensive) — the defection of Teukoe Oemar on [[29 March]] [[1896]] (the critical days) — the glorious period of Van Heutsz (the time of the great expeditions and excursions) — is the book's frame for the phases of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. The concentrated line is the first phase: the unsound system that preceded the all-out offensive.

## Significance

The geconcentreerde linie is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the unsound system that preceded the Graafland era. The book frames the concentrated line as a failed defensive strategy, happily abandoned when Graafland's aggressive new approach took over. This establishes the concentrated line as the book's marker for the ineffectual defensive phase of the Aceh War — the approach that the all-out offensive was meant to replace.

## Account from *Dispereert Niet Vol. XI* (1946) — Geconcentreerde Linie

The **Geconcenteerde Linie** (Concentrated Line) was a defensive perimeter established by Dutch forces around **[[Kutaradja]]** in [[1884]], during the **[[Aceh War]]**. The line consisted of a semicircular chain of fortified posts connected by a tramway, with a cleared zone on the outside to prevent surprise attacks. It represented the Dutch defensive strategy of the mid-1880s to mid-1890s.

### Background

After the capture of Kutaradja in [[1874]] by **General Van Swieten**, Dutch forces attempted to maintain control over the city and its immediate surroundings. However, Acehnese guerrilla resistance continued, and Dutch forces were unable to pacify the wider region.

### Description

The Geconcentreerde Linie consisted of:

- A semicircular chain of posts around Kutaradja
- A connecting tramway
- Several radial lines connecting the posts to the city center
- A cleared zone approximately 1 km wide on the outside of the line

### Purpose

The line was designed to allow Dutch forces to concentrate their limited numbers in a defensible perimeter, while maintaining communication and supply lines. It reflected a strategic retreat from the ambitions of earlier years.

### Critique

The Geconcenteerde Linie was heavily criticized even by its defenders. Its confined perimeter allowed Acehnese forces to operate freely in the surrounding countryside, attacking at will and withdrawing into the interior. The psychological impact on Dutch troops was demoralizing:

> "De onbeschaamdheid van den vijand nam met de dag toe door onze onthoudingspolitek"—"The insolence of the enemy increased day by day through our policy of abstention" (Idenburg).

### The End of the Line

The concentration strategy was abandoned when **[[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]]** became Governor of Aceh in [[1898]]. Van Heutsz adopted an aggressive offensive strategy, using mobile *marechaussees* to attack guerrilla forces rather than passively defending a fixed perimeter.

## Account from Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* — The Geconcentreerde Linie in 1896

*Geconcentreerde linie* — Dutch for "concentrated line" — was the inner defensive perimeter of [[Dutch]] posts around [[Koeta Radja]] in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], the inner ring of fortifications that enclosed the colonial capital and its principal military installations. The geconcentreerde linie was held in early [[1896]] by [[Lt.-Col. E. W. Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] as line commander (*liniecommandant*), and was complemented by the outer ring of outposts known as the [[buitenlinie|buitellinie]] — the outer line of small posts at [[Biloel]], [[Lamkoenjit]], [[Ana' Galong|Anak Galoeng]], [[Lambarih|Lam Barih]], [[Lamsoet|Lam Soet]], [[Senelop]], [[Kroeng Gloempang]], [[Tjot Rang]] and [[Toengkoeb]]. After [[Teuku Umar]]'s defection of [[29 March]] [[1896]] rendered the buitenlinie untenable, the geconcentreerde linie became the Dutch military's principal defensive asset, and the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] against Umar's stelling at [[Lam-Pisang]] was launched from it.

### Etymology and Meaning

The phrase *geconcentreerde linie* is Dutch: *geconcentreerde* (concentrated), past participle of *concentreren* (to concentrate) + *linie* (line), from French *ligne*, Latin *linea*. The military sense is of a "concentrated" or "consolidated" defensive line — a perimeter of mutually supporting posts, held in sufficient strength to repel attack, in contrast to the dispersed *buitellinie* of small exposed outposts. The concept of a geconcentreerde linie was developed in European fortress theory and applied by the Dutch to the colonial context of [[Koeta Radja]], where the line enclosed the [[kraton]] (the former sultan's palace), the principal military encampment, the harbour at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh (Ulee Lheue)]], and the European quarter.

### Historical Context of 1896

In the first semester of [[1896]] the geconcentreerde linie was the static backbone of the Dutch position in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Lt.-Col. [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] served as *liniecommandant*, responsible for the perimeter's defence and for the coordination of the *binnenposten* (inner posts) with the *buitenposten* (outer posts). The line enclosed the [[kraton]] and the principal military installations; the *kraton enceinte* and the *kampong Baroe-Gedah enceinte* were defensive enclosures within it. After Umar's defection, the progressive evacuation of the buitenposten ([[12 April]]–[[20 April]] [[1896]]) brought the outer line back into the geconcentreerde linie, increasing its defensive density but ceding the initiative to the Achehnese. The line's stability enabled the Dutch to mount the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] and the subsequent *tuchtigingen* (punitive expeditions) of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[Ladoeng landing|Ladoeng operation]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]].

### Examples in the Source

Kruisheer's references to the geconcentreerde linie span the text. The institution is introduced as the inner defensive perimeter of [[Koeta Radja]] held by Lt.-Col. [[Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] as *liniecommandant*. The line's component *enceintes* — the *kraton enceinte* and the *kampong Baroe-Gedah enceinte* — are named as defensive enclosures within it. The street *Deijkerhofflaan* within Koeta Radja is named after [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]], and the eastern ring dike (*Ooster Ringdijk*) is identified as a defensive feature of the perimeter. After Umar's defection, the geconcentreerde linie absorbed the evacuated buitenposten garrisons and provided the base for the May–June counter-offensives. The bullet backstop at *Petjoet*, used as a terrain reference point, is another feature within the line.

### Significance of the Geconcentreerde Linie in 1896

The geconcentreerde linie is the institutional expression of the Dutch colonial military strategy of static defence in [[Aceh]]. By concentrating force in a defensible perimeter around [[Koeta Radja]], the Dutch accepted the limitation of controlling only a small fraction of Achehnese territory but guaranteed the security of their capital and base of operations. The line's stability through the crisis of [[1896]] enabled the Dutch to absorb the shock of Umar's defection, evacuate the exposed buitenposten, and mount the counter-offensives that ultimately forced Umar from his stelling at [[Lam-Pisang]]. The line's durability through the first semester of [[1896]] is the precondition for the Dutch recovery; its complement, the buitenlinie, was the institutional expression of the more ambitious policy of "alliance" with cooperative hoeloebalang, which Umar's defection destroyed.

**The geconcentreerde linie** — the concentrated line — was the defensive system that the colonial army had used in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] before the Graafland era, and that was abandoned when the Graafland era began. In *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), the geconcentreerde linie is the unsound system that preceded the all-out offensive: the defensive line that the colonial army had held in [[Atjeh|Aceh]] before going over to the offensive.

## The unsound system

"The time, he told, in which the unsound system of the concentrated line was happily abandoned, the time in which the Graafland era began." The unsound system of the concentrated line was happily abandoned when the Graafland era began. The geconcentreerde linie is therefore the book's name for the defensive system that preceded the offensive: the unsound system that the Graafland era replaced with the all-out offensive.

## The concentrated line and the offensive

The geconcentreerde linie — the concentrated line — was the colonial army's defensive system in [[Atjeh|Aceh]]: a line of posts that the colonial army held, behind which the Acehnese resistance operated. The book's — and the colonial memory's — judgment on the concentrated line was damning: it was the defensive line that had failed to suppress the Acehnese resistance, and was happily abandoned when the Graafland era went over to the all-out offensive.

## The book's sequence

The book's sequence — the concentrated line (the unsound system) — the Graafland era (the all-out offensive) — the defection of Teukoe Oemar on [[29 March]] [[1896]] (the critical days) — the glorious period of Van Heutsz (the time of the great expeditions and excursions) — is the book's frame for the phases of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. The concentrated line is the first phase: the unsound system that preceded the all-out offensive.

## Significance

The geconcentreerde linie is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the unsound system that preceded the Graafland era. The book frames the concentrated line as a failed defensive strategy, happily abandoned when Graafland's aggressive new approach took over. This establishes the concentrated line as the book's marker for the ineffectual defensive phase of the Aceh War — the approach that the all-out offensive was meant to replace.

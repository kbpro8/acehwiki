**Burda (poem)** is a celebrated Arabic ode composed by the Egyptian Sufi poet [[Al-Busiri]] in the 13th century, praising the Prophet [[Muhammad]] and invoking the protective *Kawthar*; the poem entered Acehnese literary culture and was translated into Malay in the 16th century, becoming a staple of devotional recitation in [[Acheh]] mosques. The source glossary records the term “Burda” and its Arabic equivalents, showing its linguistic transmission into Acehnese religious vocabulary【PAGE 84】.

## Literary Transmission to Aceh

The 16th‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* (identified in the source as “Burda van al‑Bu siri”) demonstrates how the poem was adapted for local use. Translators rendered Arabic metaphors—such as the *necklace* of *spot, shame and disgrace*—into Malay poetic forms, preserving the theological concepts while integrating Acehnese idiom. The glossary notes the Arabic term *kawthar* (“see hawd”) in the same passage, indicating the poem’s doctrinal content.

## Religious Role in Acehnese Society

In Aceh, the *Burda* was recited during *maulid* celebrations, *tahlilan* gatherings, and as part of *ta’ziyah* rituals. Its verses were memorised by *ulama* and incorporated into *nyanyian* (chant) repertoires, reinforcing the link between Acehnese Sufi practice and broader Islamic devotional literature.

**The Politieke Contracten** ("political contracts"), also called the *omstandige politieke contracten* ("detailed political contracts"), were the elaborate written agreements that, until [[1899]], regulated the relationship between the Dutch Gouvernement and the [[Zelfbesturen|zelfbesturen (self-governing territories)]] of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], including the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency. The politieke contracten set out "the principles to be observed in the governance of the landschappen, as well as the mutual rights, obligations, and competences" of the Gouvernement and the native rulers. From [[1899]] onward, the politieke contracten were progressively replaced by the simpler [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] — though a small minority of larger sultanates retained the older form.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Politieke Contracten

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Politieke Contracten|politieke contracten]] appears in the chapter on administrative organisation. The Bureau frames the politieke contracten as the older, more elaborate instrument of Dutch-zelfbestuur relations, replaced from [[1899]] onward by the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]]. The Bureau notes that "[[1899|until that year]] the regulation of the relationship of the [[Zelfbesturen|Zelfbesturen]] to the Gouvernement generally found its expression in the detailed political contracts (*omstandige politieke contracten*) concluded between the two parties, in which the principles to be observed in the governance of the landschappen, as well as the mutual rights, obligations, and competences, were set out."

The Bureau notes that the politieke contracten were found to be "misplaced and incomprehensible for many of the less significant zelfbesturen" — that is, too elaborate for the smaller self-governing territories, and too restrictive of the Gouvernement's freedom to intervene in their internal affairs. The [[1899]] shift to the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] thus reflected both administrative convenience and the Gouvernement's desire for deeper intervention in the self-governing territories.

The Bureau notes that, even after [[1899]], a small and declining minority of larger sultanates retained the older detailed political contracts. As of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade, these were: on the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]] — Asahan, Deli, Koela, Langkat, Pelalawan, Serdang, Siak Sri Indrapoera; in the [[Westerafdeeling van Borneo|Western Division of Borneo]] — Landak, Mampawa, Matan, Pontianak, Sambas, Sanggau, Tajan; in the [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo|Southern and Eastern Division of Borneo]] — Koetei; in [[Celebes en Onderhoorigheden|Celebes and Dependencies]] — Laiwoei; and in [[Timor en Onderhoorigheden|Timor and Dependencies]] — Bima, Dompo, Sanggar, Soembawa. The Bureau notes that "the rulers of all other landschappen, in total about 306 in number, signed the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]]." The [[1914]] [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] codified the rights, competences, and obligations of the Gouvernement and the zelfbesturen under both the politieke contracten and the Korte Verklaring regime.

## Significance

The [[Politieke Contracten|politieke contracten]] are, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the older, more elaborate instrument of Dutch-zelfbestuur relations — the instrument that the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] of [[1899]] progressively replaced. The retention of the politieke contracten by a small minority of larger sultanates — and their replacement by the Korte Verklaring for "about 306" other landschappen — marks the administrative asymmetry of the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] order: a small number of larger, more established sultanates retained their older contractual rights, while the great majority of self-governing territories were brought under the simpler and more interventionist Korte Verklaring regime.

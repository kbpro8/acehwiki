**The Oeleebalang** — also written *Uleëbalang*, *Uleebalang* — was the Atjèhse territorial chief or district head, the principal Atjèhse administrative institution of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjèh] and of the post-Dutch-conquest Atjèhse administration. The *oeleebalang* was the chief of a *mukim* (the Atjèhse territorial unit) or of a group of *mukim*s, and exercised in his territory the principal administrative, judicial, and fiscal functions. The institution was, with the Sultan and the religious leaders (*teungku*s), one of the three principal Atjèhse political institutions, and its relative power — vis-à-vis the Sultan — was, in the Snouck Hurgronje diagnosis, one of the principal causes of the decline of the Sultanate.

## The Office

The *oeleebalang* was the chief of a *mukim* (the Atjèhse territorial unit) or of a group of *mukim*s, and exercised in his territory the principal administrative, judicial, and fiscal functions. The office was hereditary in the *oeleebalang* family, but the confirmation of the succession was, in the pre-Dutch period, in the gift of the Sultan; in the post-Dutch-conquest period, it was in the gift of the Dutch administration. The *oeleebalang* was responsible for the collection of the taxes, the maintenance of the peace, the administration of justice, and — in the pre-Dutch period — the construction and maintenance of the irrigation works.

## The Pre-Dutch Irrigation Functions

In the pre-Dutch period, the construction of irrigation works was, in the Atjeh-Instituut brochure's account, done "*in betaalden of onbetaalden gedwongen arbeid der bevolking, al naar gelang een oeléébalang over voldoende geldmiddelen kon beschikken of niet, om in dit laatste geval een bron van inkomsten te verkrijgen.*" (in paid or unpaid forced labour of the population, according as an oeleebalang had sufficient money means or not, to obtain in this latter case a source of income.) When users made use of the water from such a *loeëng* (irrigation channel), they had to — according as they had sown — annually after the harvest pay the same sown quantity of padi in kind to the constructor of the irrigation work. The execution of irrigation works, also in paid labour, was for the *oeleebalang*s an important source of income.

## The Decline of the Sultanate

It is also known, the Atjeh-Instituut brochure notes, how the Atjèhse Sultanate — bearer of the wealth built on the [[pepercultuur|pepercultuur]] — gradually declined in significance, "*doordat de volkshoofden steeds grooter macht ontwikkelden en verschillende onderhoorigheden zich losscheurden.*" (because the popular chiefs developed ever greater power and various dependencies tore themselves loose.) The *oeleebalang*s were the principal "popular chiefs" whose growing power, vis-à-vis the Sultan, was the principal cause of the decline of the Sultanate. The Snouck Hurgronje diagnosis of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]] would, in the [[Verslag omtrent de religieus-politieke toestanden in Atjeh|Verslag]] of [[23 May]] [[23 May]] [[1892]], take account of the relative power of the *oeleebalang* in the prescription of the three principal rules (Keumala to be left alone; the actively hostile party — the bands organised by oelama's and adventurers — to be beaten; trust to be restored by promoting agriculture, industry, and trade).

## The Keudjroen-loeëng

The *oeleebalang* was, in the pre-Dutch period, the appointing authority for the *[[Keudjroen-loeeng|keudjroen-loeëng]]* — the Atjèhse irrigation official charged with the supervision of the irrigation water distribution. The revival of the office of *keudjroen-loeëng* in the post-war period — recommended by the Atjeh-Instituut brochure of [[1923]] — was, in Pidië, the rice-land par excellence, to be done by the appointment of younger brothers or close relatives of landscape-heads (i.e. of *oeleebalang*s) to the office.

## Significance

The *oeleebalang* is the principal Atjèhse territorial chief or district head, and the principal Atjèhse administrative institution of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjèh] and of the post-Dutch-conquest Atjèhse administration. The office — hereditary in the *oeleebalang* family, with confirmation in the gift of the Sultan (pre-Dutch) or of the Dutch administration (post-Dutch) — was the principal vehicle of Atjèhse local administration, and the *oeleebalang*'s growing power, vis-à-vis the Sultan, was the principal cause of the decline of the Sultanate.

## Account from *De onderwerping van Atjeh* (Van Heutsz, 1893) and the *Zelfbestuursverordeningen* (1930) — the *oeleebalang* as *Zelfbestuurder*

[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|1893 tract]] and the [[Zelfbestuursverordeningen Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|1930 compilation]] together document the *oeleebalang*'s transformation from the precolonial territorial chief of a *negeri* / *landschap* into the *[[Zelfbestuur|Zelfbestuurder]]* of the Dutch colonial system of indirect rule in [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. Van Heutsz — writing in [[1893]], before the *Zelfbestuur* system had been imposed on Atjeh — uses the spelling *hoeloebalang* and treats the office in two distinct senses: as the chief of a small coastal *landschap* (e.g. the *hoeloebalang* of [[Pedir]], the *hoeloebalang* of [[Gighen]], the *hoeloebalang* of [[Samalanga]]), and as the chief of a sub-federation within the [[Sagi]] federations of [[Groot-Atjeh]] (e.g. the *hoeloebalang* of the [[VII Moekims|VII Moekims]], the *hoeloebalang* of the [[IX Moekims|IX Moekims]], the *hoeloebalang* of the [[VI Moekims|VI Moekims]]). Both senses reflect the underlying political reality: the *oeleebalang* was the territorial chief of any unit — small coastal statelet or sub-federation of *mukims* — whose authority rested on a combination of hereditary right, customary law (*[[Adat|adat]]*), and the allegiance of the *anak negeri* (the native subjects of the *landschap*).

### Hereditary character and the *geschiktheid* requirement

Van Heutsz records the dynastic character of the office:

> "het sedert onheuglijke tijden te Atjeh de gewoonte is, dat het bestuur in dezelfde familie erfelijk blijft en dit niet alleen wordt toegepast op hoeloe- zooals de Makota alam voorschrijft balangs sagi, maar ook op grootere en kleinere moekimhoofden, zelfs op Ketjiq's van kampongs".

— "since time immemorial it has been the custom in Atjeh that the government remains hereditary in the same family, and this is applied not only to the *hoeloebalangs sagi*, as the [[Peratoeran Makota Alam|Makota alam]] prescribes, but also to larger and smaller *moekimhoofden*, even to the *ketjiq*'s of kampongs." The hereditary principle was, however, qualified by the requirement of *geschiktheid* (fitness): "*toch wordt er altijd uit de familieleden gekozen en worden idioten uitgesloten*" — yet always the choice falls on family members, and idiots are excluded.

### The precolonial origins: the Panglima Polim lineage

Van Heutsz traces the *Panglima Polim* lineage — the *panglima sagi* of the [[XXII Moekims]] — to its putative foundation by Sultan Said II Moekamil Inajat Sjah (Marhoem Kahar), c. [[1530]]:

> "Volgens Atjehsche overleveringen zond hij n.l. zijne Abyssinische slavin tijdens hare zwangerschap naar de bovenstreken en stelde hij later den daar uit haar geboren zoon onder den titel Panglima Polim Sri Moeda Perkasa aan tot hoofd over de 4 Imams".

— "According to Atjehsche traditions, he sent his Abyssinian slave-woman, during her pregnancy, to the upper regions, and later appointed the son born of her there, under the title Panglima Polim Sri Moeda Perkasa, as head over the 4 Imams." The *hoeloebalang* office was, in this account, an institution of the [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese sultanate]] itself — the territorial chief through whom the sultan's authority was mediated into the *mukims* and *negeris* of the realm. Van Heutsz cites van Langen's *Inrichting van het Atjehsche staatsbestuur onder het sultanaat* for the constitutional theory: "bij de instelling der sagi's over de sagi der XXII Moekims werd aangesteld een bij eene Abyssinische slavin verwekte zoon van [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Sultan Iskandar Moeda]] onder den titel van Panglima Polim Moeda Sertia Perkasa". The *hoeloebalang* — the *panglima sagi*, the *panglima prang*, the *panglima Mesdjid Raja* — were thus, in the precolonial constitutional order, the territorial and military chiefs of the sultanate, exercising delegated sultanate authority over the *mukims* and *negeris*.

### The 18 artikelen and the pre-*Korte Verklaring* subordination

The 18 artikelen — the *beëdigde* eighteen articles by which the coastal *hoeloebalang* of the *Onderhoorigheden* had acknowledged Dutch sovereignty in the early [[1870s]] — were, in Van Heutsz's 1893 narrative, the immediate political instrument through which the *hoeloebalang* were bound to the colonial state in the pre-*Korte Verklaring* period. Van Heutsz records Scherer's diagnosis: "*Er moest daarom, zegt Scherer, een middel gevonden worden, dat de hoofden van de verschillende staatjes dwong tot trouwe nakoming van de bezworen beëedigde 18 artikelen*" — "There had to be, says Scherer, a means found, that would force the chiefs of the various statelets to faithful compliance with the sworn sworn-in 18 articles." The *oeleebalang*'s later power to issue *verordeningen* with Dutch approval was, in this sense, the operational continuation of the 18 artikelen's political subordination: where the 18 artikelen bound the *hoeloebalang* to the colonial state in form, the *goedkeuring* requirement on the *Zelfbestuursverordeningen* bound him in operational practice.

### The *Zelfbestuur* system: the *Korte Verklaring* and the collective *vaststelling*

Under the Dutch *Zelfbestuur* system imposed on Atjeh after the [[Atjeh-oorlog (1873-1904)|pacification]], the *oeleebalang* was the office-holder through whom the colonial state governed Atjeh by indirect rule. The colonial state, by the *[[Korte Verklaring]]*, recognised the *oeleebalang*'s authority and bound him to the colonial state; the *oeleebalang*, in turn, exercised his authority as *Zelfbestuurder* under the supervisory powers the *Korte Verklaring* conferred. The *Nota van Toelichting* on the *inkomstenbelasting* *regeling* (Reg. No. 9b of the 1930 compilation) makes the constitutional logic explicit:

> "Al moge de onderworpenheid van het Atjehsche Zelfbestuur aan het Europeesche gezag in het algemeen reeds uit de Korte Verklaringen voortvloeien".

— "Although the subordination of the Atjehsche Zelfbestuur to the European authority may in general already flow from the Korte Verklaringen". The *oeleebalang* thus exercised his authority under — and was subordinated to the colonial state through — the *Korte Verklaring* that bound him.

The collective-*vaststelling* practice of the Atjehsche regulations operationalised the *oeleebalang*'s authority as *Zelfbestuurder*. The *Voorwoord* of the [[1930]] compilation records that "*de vaststelling van ver-ordeningen in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden steeds collectief geschiedt*" — the establishment of ordinances in the *gewest* Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden always takes place collectively — meaning that all *oeleebalang* of an *onderafdeeling* issued their regulations jointly. Every regulation in the compilation is issued in the name of "*Kami Oeleebalang …*" — "We, the *oeleebalang* of …". The corporate-collective signatory formula is fixed in the bilingual title-pages: in Malay "*Kami Oeleebalang dari Landschap: …*"; in Dutch "*De Zelfbesturen van de landschappen: …*". The three *oeleebalang* of [[Lhoksoekon]] — Keureutoë, Hakim Kroeeng Peutoë, and Matang Koeli — jointly issued Reg. No. 1 (the *woeker*-regulation of [[9 December]] [[1926]]); the two *oeleebalang* of the [[Alaslanden]] — Kedjoerön Batoe Mboelan and Kedjoerön Bambel — jointly issued Reg. No. 5 (the *slachtpadjak* of [[1 March]] [[1914]]); the eight *oeleebalang* of [[Idi]] jointly issued Reg. No. 11 (the *gevaarlijke lectuur* regulation of [[15 November]] [[1924]]); the ten *oeleebalang* of the *onderafdeeling* [[Meulaboh]] jointly issued Reg. No. 19 (the *tjemara*-tree regulation of [[2 July]] [[1917]]).

### Powers to issue *verordeningen* with Dutch approval

The *oeleebalang*'s power to issue *verordeningen* was conferred by the *Zelfbestuursregelen 1919* ([[Staatsblad 1919 No. 822]]) and the *Zelfbestuursregelen 1927*, and was exercised under the *goedkeuring* (approval) of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]]. Every regulation in the 1930 compilation is issued in the name of the *oeleebalang* of the *onderafdeeling*, recites the statutory basis ("*Gelet op de zelfbestuursregelen 1919 (Staatsblad No. 822)*"), and carries the printed note of the Gouverneur's *goedkeuring*: "(*goedgekeurd door den Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden op …*)". The *oeleebalang*'s substantive powers under the regulations extended to the full range of fiscal, police, agricultural, and administrative measures the colonial state desired: the *Pantjang Alas* tax (Reg. No. 2, the *oeleebalang* of Meulaboh, [[5 April]] [[1912]]); the *slachtpadjak* on cattle, buffaloes, and tame pigs (Reg. No. 5, the *oeleebalang* of Alaslanden, [[1 March]] [[1914]]); the *inkomstenbelasting* on non-*Gouvernements-onderdanen* (Reg. No. 9a, the *oeleebalang* of [[Peureula']], [[20 December]] [[1920]]); the *[[Heerendienst|heerendienst]]* (corvée) regulation (Reg. No. 14, the *oeleebalang* of [[Bireuën]] — Samalanga, Peusangan, and Gloempang-doea — [[18 August]] [[1928]]).

### Significance of the Van Heutsz and Zelfbestuursverordeningen account

The *oeleebalang*'s significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record, in the Van Heutsz / Zelfbestuursverordeningen perspective, rests on four things: it was the territorial chief of a *negeri* / *landschap* — the political unit the colonial state recognised as the basic unit of native government — whose authority rested, in the precolonial constitutional order, on a combination of hereditary right, customary law, and the allegiance of the *anak negeri*; it was the office whose precolonial origins Van Heutsz traces to the [[Aceh Sultanate|Acehnese sultanate]] itself — the territorial chief through whom the sultan's authority was mediated into the *mukims* and *negeris* of the realm; it was the office through whom the Dutch colonial state governed Atjeh by indirect rule after the pacification — the *Zelfbestuurder* bound to the colonial state by the *Korte Verklaring*, exercising his authority under the supervisory powers the *Korte Verklaring* conferred; and it was the office whose collective-*vaststelling* practice — under which all *oeleebalang* of an *onderafdeeling* issued their regulations jointly, and whose *verordeningen* were extended to all other *onderafdeelingen* through comparative tables of *Gelijke Verordeningen* — preserved, throughout the [[1910s]] and [[1920s]], the operational expression of the *Zelfbestuur*'s collective authority. The *oeleebalang* is, in this sense, the central political figure of the 1930 *Zelfbestuursverordeningen* compilation — the office-holder whose name appears on every regulation, whose authority the *Korte Verklaring* bound to the colonial state, and whose *verordeningen* the *goedkeuring* of the *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* brought into force.

**Dunia (Acehnese concept)** is the Acehnese term for “world”, “earthly life”, or “worldly favor”, derived from the Arabic *dunyā*. It is attested on [[80]]–[[81]] of the *Burda* translation, where it is defined as “inl., 32, 33, 50, 144, 145 (A. ‘adjil)”, and again on page [[88]] (152) as “wereldse gunsthewijzen”. In Acehnese religious and moral literature, *dunia* is repeatedly juxtaposed with *akhirah* (the hereafter) to stress the transitory nature of material existence and to warn against attachment to the temporal realm.

## Definition

In the moral and theological corpus of Acehnese Islam, *dunia* denotes the transient, material realm that humans inhabit. The term is contrasted with *[[Akhirah (Acehnese concept)]]* (the hereafter), reflecting a dualistic worldview in which worldly concerns are subordinate to spiritual goals. The *Burda* translation illustrates this contrast with verses such as “dia tiada menge‑i dunia, zinu tidak begeren van deze wereld”, emphasizing that attachment to *dunia* leads to spiritual loss.

## Contextual Use

The concept appears in Sufi poetry, didactic texts, and everyday moral instruction. Authors employ *dunia* to caution believers against the allure of material prosperity and to promote focus on the afterlife. The usage on page [[88]] (152) as “wereldse gunsthewijzen” underscores the moral dimension of “worldly favor” as something to be resisted.

## Theological and Philosophical Significance

*Dunia* underpins Acehnese Sufi poetry and moral teaching, reminding believers to prioritize the afterlife over fleeting worldly pleasures. Its inclusion in Acehnese scholarly discourse mirrors broader Islamic philosophical thought, wherein the material world is viewed as a test and a temporary stage before the eternal *[[Akhirah (Acehnese concept)]]*. This dualism aligns Aceh’s intellectual tradition with the wider Islamic conception of *dunya* versus *akhirah*.

The **goentja** (Gayo *koentjo*, Alas *id.*) is the principal large volume measure of the [[rijstbouw in Atjeh|Atjeh rice economy]], used for the measurement of the rice harvest. The *goentja* is, however, a "non-existent" measure in the strict metrological sense: as [[J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer]] notes in his [[Kreemer's De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (1918)|*De rijstcultuur in het gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden* (1918)]], "De opbrengst ... wordt gewoonlijk opgegeven in *goentja* ... d.i. een niet bestaande inhoudsmaat" — The yield ... is usually given in *goentja* ... that is, a non-existent volume measure.

## The Regional Variation

The *goentja* varies by region:

- In Atjeh: 1 *goentja* is reckoned at 10 *naléh* = 160 *arè*.
- In Gayo: *koentjo's* of 10, 20 and 30 *naléh* are distinguished.
- In the Alasland: 1 *koentjo* = 4 *naléh*.

The *goentja* thus has no fixed value across the *Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden*: it is a conventional unit, varying by region, used for the rough expression of the harvest. The actual measurement is made with smaller, fixed measures — the *kating* (basket), the *tòng* (box), or the *lèngkar* (Alas cylindrical measure) — and the *goentja* is the conventional unit into which these smaller measurements are converted for accounting purposes.

## The Smaller Measures

The smaller volume measures of the Atjeh rice-economy, of which the *goentja* is the conventional aggregate, are:

- The ***kating*** — a basket of woven rushes, used for measuring the harvest. A *kating* may contain 20 *gantang* (= 40 *arè*) or 10 *gantang* (= 20 *arè*).
- The ***tòng*** — a box, typically of 1 *naléh* (= 8 *gantang* = 16 *arè*) or 1½ *naléh*.
- The ***naléh*** — the standard small measure, of 8 *gantang* = 16 *arè*.
- The ***gantang*** — a measure of 2 *arè*.
- The ***arè*** — the smallest standard measure.
- The ***lèngkar*** — an Alas cylindrical measure of 4 *naléh* = 44 *säloep*.
- The ***soempit*** — an Alas woven bag of known capacity.

## The Counting Aids

For the counting of the harvest, Kreemer notes, "van allerlei hulpmiddelen gebruik gemaakt" — all sorts of aids are used, "waarbij elke eenheid wordt aangegeven door een steentje of een vruchtpit of een knoop in een touw, of een prik in den bladsteel van den sagopalm enz." — each unit being indicated by a stone, or a fruit-pip, or a knot in a cord, or a prick in the leaf-stem of the sago-palm, and so on. These units are then later counted. The counting of the harvest is thus a manual operation, performed by the measurer and his assistants, with the aid of whatever counting devices are at hand.

## The Yield Ratio

The ratio of the seed-rice sown to the rice harvested is, Kreemer notes, very variously reported:

- In [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]: a 35- to 45-fold yield.
- On the Noord- and Oostkust: a 30- to 80-fold yield.
- In [[Gajoland|Gajo]] (Meergebied): a 30-fold yield.

According to the *Landbouw-leeraar* [[J. H. Heyl|J. H. Heyl]] (cited in *Deli Courant*, [[8 August]] [[1916]], No. 183), the average yield of the rice-fields in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] is 8 *pikoel* of rice per hectare; in the [[Pidie|Pidie]] valley 36 *pikoel* per hectare; in the adjacent [[Meureudu|Meureudoe]] more; in [[Lho Soekon|Lho Soekon]] 24 *pikoel* per hectare. These figures express the yield in weight rather than in volume, and are the basis of the [[Rijstvraagstuk (1916)|"Rice Question" calculations]] of [[J. H. Heyl|Heyl]].

## The Pikoel

The ***pikoel*** — a Chinese-derived unit of weight, equal to about 61.8 kg — is the standard weight measure in which the rice trade is conducted. The relation between the volume measures (*goentja*, *naléh*, etc.) and the weight measure (*pikoel*) is not fixed, since the weight of a given volume of rice varies with the variety and the moisture content. Kreemer notes, however, that 1 *naléh* of padi yields approximately 1 *blah kajë* (= 1/12 of 1 *naléh*) of *seungò* (unhusked rice).

## Significance

The *goentja* is the principal conventional volume measure of the Atjeh rice-economy, used for the expression of the rice harvest. The *goentja* is significant for the encyclopedia of Aceh history as a documented part of the Atjeh rice-economy, recording the Atjehnese metrology of rice measurement — the conventional aggregates, the smaller fixed measures, the counting aids, and the regional variation in the value of the *goentja*. The *goentja* also illustrates the practical, unsystematic character of the Atjehnese rice-metrology: the principal large measure is "a non-existent volume measure", varying by region, used for the rough expression of the harvest, while the actual measurement is made with smaller, fixed measures and converted to *goentja* for accounting purposes.

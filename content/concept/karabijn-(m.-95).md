**The Karabijn (M. 95)** — the M. 95 carbine, a 6.5 mm carbine — was the standard shoulder weapon of the [[K.N.I.L.]] soldaat in the late colonial period, and the principal weapon of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] patrouille. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *karabijn M. 95* is the weapon with which the *[[dardanellen|dardanel]]* [[Nussy]] shot first at *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* on [[3 December]] [[1911]], the weapon with which [[Thijs de Fretes]] shot a tiger through the brain at ten metres, and the weapon whose *korrel* (front sight) was *onbeschermd* (unprotected) and could be damaged by a *tik* (knock), causing shooting inaccuracy.

## The M. 95 and the korrel

The M. 95 carbine's *korrel* (front sight) was *onbeschermd* (unprotected) and could be damaged by a *tik* (knock). Damage to the *korrel* required repair by the *[[geweermaker]]* (gunsmith). The *[[bokschutters]]* — mostly small soldiers — tested the carbines on the range before the repair of the *korrel* by the *geweermaker*. The fragility of the *korrel* was therefore a small but constant concern of the colonial soldier: a knock on the *korrel* could turn an A-schutter into an achterlijke schutter.

## The karabijn and the dardanel

In the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]], the *karabijn M. 95* was the weapon of the *[[dardanellen|dardanel]]* — the point-man who went ahead of the patrouille and engaged the enemy first. *[[Nussy]]* — the book's most fully drawn *dardanel* — shot first at *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* on [[3 December]] [[1911]] with the *karabijn M. 95*. "Driemaal werd er een karabijn M. 95 op Nussy gericht, driemaal weigerde hij" — three times a karabijn M. 95 was aimed at Nussy, three times it failed to fire. The episode is the book's small register of the weapon's unreliability in the field.

## The karabijn and the jager

[[Thijs de Fretes]] shot a tiger off a comrade's body with a 6.5 mm *karabijn* through the brain. "Dat schot moest zuiver in de hersens zitten, want het kaliber is eigenlijk te licht voor de grote jacht" — the shot had to be precisely in the brains, because the calibre is really too light for the big game. The 6.5 mm *karabijn* was therefore the weapon of the *jager* (hunter) in the *[[rimboe]]*, but its calibre was marginal for the larger game; the shot had to be precise.

## The A/B/C-schutter classifications

The colonial soldier was classified by his shooting on the *[[schietbaan / A-klas|schietbaan]]* as an A-schutter (matig / mediocre), B-schutter (behoorlijk / decent) or C-schutter (goed / good). The C-schutter had the best chances for *[[kaderschool]]* or *[[spandri]]*. The *achterlijke schutter* (backward shooter) was *zonder meer uitgesloten* (simply excluded) and got *verplichte [[mik-mik]]* (mandatory aim practice). C-schutters could try for *scherpschutter* (sharpshooter). The book's set-piece examples — [[Tanamal]] and [[Taris]] — were natural shots who could not hit a stationary target on the *schietbaan* but could kill a moving target in the field.

## Significance

The *karabijn M. 95* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the standard shoulder weapon of the [[K.N.I.L.]] soldaat and the principal weapon of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] patrouille. The book's argument is that the weapon's *korrel* was a small but constant concern, and that the *schietbaan* classification system failed to capture the actual marksmanship of the natural shots — [[Tanamal]] and [[Taris]] — who could kill a moving target in the field but could not hit a stationary target on the range.

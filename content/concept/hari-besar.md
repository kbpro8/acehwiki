**Hari-besar** — Malay for "great day" — was the term for the twice-monthly payday of the colonial planters' world of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's East Coast]], followed by a free day, on which great feasts were held at [[Hotel de Boer]] in [[Medan]] in the olden times. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *hari-besar* is the book's marker of the *planterswereld* of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's East Coast]] — the colonial society into which the Ambonese soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]] was inserted when he travelled through [[Medan]].

## The payday and the feast

"Op de *hari-besar*, de tweemaal per maand voorkomende betaaldag, welke dan werd gevolgd door een vrije dag, werden daar in de oude tijd grote feesten gehouden." The *hari-besar* — the twice-monthly payday, followed by a free day — was the occasion of great feasts at [[Hotel de Boer]] in [[Medan]] in the olden times. The *hari-besar* was therefore both an economic event (the payday of the planters' world) and a social event (the great feasts that followed).

## The planterswereld

The *hari-besar* is the book's marker of the *planterswereld* — the planters' world — of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's East Coast]]. The *planterswereld* was the colonial society of the tobacco and rubber planters of Sumatra's East Coast, with its capital at [[Medan]] and its social centre at [[Hotel de Boer]]. The *hari-besar* was the rhythm of that society: the twice-monthly payday that brought the planters to [[Medan]] and to [[Hotel de Boer]].

## The Hotel de Boer

The *hari-besar* feasts at [[Hotel de Boer]] are the book's set-piece of the *planterswereld*'s social life. The hotel — "het eerste hotel van Medan, gelegen op de Noord-West hoek van de Esplanade" — was the social centre of the colonial society of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's East Coast]]; the *hari-besar* feasts were the great social events of that society. The Ambonese soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]] who travelled through [[Medan]] — like the kapitein of the [[1e Divisie Marechaussee uit Blang Kedjeren]] of the opening anecdote — was inserted into this society when he visited the hotel.

## Significance

The *hari-besar* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the book's marker of the *planterswereld* of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's East Coast]]. The book's argument is that the *hari-besar* — the twice-monthly payday of the planters' world — was the rhythm of the colonial society into which the Ambonese soldier of the [[K.N.I.L.]] was inserted when he travelled through [[Medan]]. The *hari-besar* is therefore the book's small marker of the *planterswereld* as the social context of the colonial army's Ambonese soldier.

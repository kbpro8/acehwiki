**Lae Boetar** was a Dutch *cultuuronderneming* (plantation enterprise) in the [[Singkel|Singkelse]] — the southernmost *onderafdeeling* of the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]], some 60 km north-east of [[Singkel|Singkel]] town — that served as the southern destination of the [[Evacuation of women and children from the Westkust of Atjeh (March 1942)|Westkust evacuation of March 1942]] and as the site of the [[Capitulation at Lae Boetar (29 March 1942)|Westkust capitulation of 29 March 1942]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Lae Boetar is the southern Dutch anchor of the Westkust evacuation, and the staff history's principal narrative instance of the *cultuuronderneming* as a wartime refuge.

## The 1942 evacuation destination

By mid-March [[1942]] the Westkust *bivaks* of [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]], [[Blang Pidië|Blang Pidië]], [[Tapatoean|Tapatoean]], [[Tjalang|Tjalang]] and [[Lam No|Lam No]] held approximately 3,000 women and children of indigenous militairen. Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]]'s plan was to move these women and children southward by road convoy to the Lae Boetar onderneming, which lay outside the *gewest* Atjeh proper (in the Singkelse, whose population was "hoofdzakelijk Minangkabauers en Batakkers" and which "viel dus eigenlijk buiten de Atjehse sfeer") and which could be reached by the convoys without forcing a passage through the [[Bakongan|Bakongan]]–[[Singkel|Singkel]] Atjehnese-populated stretch of coast.

The European women and children of the Westkust were evacuated in private passenger cars over "smalle en slechte wegen" to the Lae Boetar onderneming. The *gros* of the indigenous women and children, however, "moest tenslotte in Tapatoean en Bakongan worden achtergelaten" — the bulk could not be moved further south than Bakongan, owing to the approach of the Japanese battalion-strength force from the north.

## The 29 March 1942 capitulation

By [[29 March]] [[29 March 1942|29 March 1942]] Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]]'s reduced force — about 140 men — had reached Lae Boetar. The capitulation was forced not by enemy pressure but by the *lot van de vrouwen en kinderen*: "Kloprogge, die de ongelijke strijd toch wilde doorzetten, met het lot van de vrouwen en kinderen voor ogen, overreden met het restant van de troepen (ongeveer 140 man) te capituleren." The officers who refused to surrender — Captain [[F. Steinhorst|Steinhorst]], Lt [[E. F. K. R. Mathus|Mathus]], Lt [[M. C. Thomson|Thomson]] and the *officier van gezondheid* Dingemans — attempted to break through to the still-fighting Dutch forces around [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]]; only Thomson succeeded, eventually reaching the [[Medan|Medanse]] before his capture on [[26 April]] [[26 April 1942|26 April]].

## Significance

Lae Boetar is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the southern Dutch anchor of the Westkust evacuation, and the site of the *moral* close of the Westkust campaign. The staff history's emphasis on the onderneming's location outside the *Atjehse sfeer* — in the Singkelse, whose Minangkabau–Batak population lay outside the F-beweging's zone of mobilisation — frames Lae Boetar as the staff history's principal narrative instance of the Dutch colonial state's reliance on the *cultuuronderneming* as a wartime refuge, and on the demographic boundary between Atjeh and the Singkelse as the southern limit of the F-beweging's rising.

**Detacheering** was the system by which Dutch garrisons in the outposts of [[Groot-Atjeh]] were rotated, normally for a period of fourteen days, after which the men returned to [[Kota Radja]].

## Duration and pay

[[G. B. Hooijer]] recorded that soldiers at [[Lam Koenjit]] ran together at the daily arrival of the provision cart and letters to hear news of friends and comrades from whom they were separated *voor veertien dagen, den duur der detacheering*. At [[Toengkoeb]], pay accumulated without any occasion to spend it, *en gaf een aardig sommetje als men, na ommekomst van de detacheering, in Kota Radja werd uitbetaald* — and yielded a pretty sum when, on the expiry of the detachment, it was paid out at Kota Radja.

## Voluntary extension

Despite the monotony within the walls of [[Toengkoeb]], many soldiers chose to remain longer than the prescribed fourteen days, because life there was so easy: patrols went only to neighbouring posts, only two of the four watch-houses were manned by day, no duties beyond mounting guard were ordered, and the greatest freedom in dress was permitted.

## The order of March 1896

Hooijer recorded that at the beginning of [[1896]] the commandant of [[Anagaloeng]] received the instruction to make a patrol through the surroundings *vóór zijn detachement door een ander werd vervangen* — before his detachment was replaced by another. That order produced the mauled patrol of [[Van Blokland|Kapitein Blokland]] on [[7 March]] [[1896]].

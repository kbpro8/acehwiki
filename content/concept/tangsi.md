**The tangsi** — from the Malay *tangsi*, "barracks" — was the K.N.I.L. term for the barracks, both the garrison on [[Java]] and the *[[bivak]]* in the *[[buitengewesten]]*. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *tangsi* is the institutional home of *[[Oom Ambon]]* — the place where the Ambonese soldier lived with his *[[Tante]]* and his *[[anak-kolong]]*, where the *[[Marechausseelied]]* was sung, where the *[[Tahoen Baroe|Nieuwjaar]]* feast was held, and where the moral economy of the colonial army was made. The dissolution of the *tangsi* culture with the disbandment of the [[K.N.I.L.]] in [[1949]] is the book's closing argument.

## The tangsi on Java

On Java the *tangsi* was the garrison — a fixed barracks where the *depotbataljons|depotbataljon]] trained the recruits, where the *veldbataljon|veldbataljon]] was paraat for both the foreign enemy and for unrest in the archipelago, and where the daily life of the soldiers and their families centred. The *tangsi* was the place where the *[[dagverblijf]]* (day-room) was the centre of the company's social life, where the *[[vrouwenloods]]* (women's shed) was the domain of the *[[Tante]]*, and where the *[[compagniesrapport]]* (morning parade) was the occasion for the *[[lonté]]* cases.

## The tangsi in the buitengewesten

In the *[[buitengewesten]]* the *tangsi* was the *[[bivak]]* — the field camp in the *[[rimboe]]*. The *bivak* was both the daily camp and the daily act of construction: the *[[pagger]]* (fence), the *afdakje* (lean-to), the tentje for the *[[dwangarbeiders (Boeginezen)|dwangarbeiders]]*. The *tangsi* in the *buitengewesten* was therefore a mobile institution — built each evening with the *[[klewang]]* as the only tool — but it was still a *tangsi*: the institutional home of *[[Oom Ambon]]*.

## The tangsi and the anak-kolong

The children of the *tangsi* were the *[[anak-kolong]]* — the "children of the *[[kolong]]*" (the space under the *[[slaaptafel]]*). The *anak-kolong* had their own *[[adat]]* and *sfeer*, distinct from *[[kampong]]* children. "De anak-kolong konden de oude adatsfeer van de kampong missen, de tangsi gaf hen een eigen sfeer, een eigen adat, een eigen *stijl*…" The *tangsi* was therefore not just the barracks but a culture: the culture that made the *anak-kolong* and that the *anak-kolong* carried into the next generation of soldiers.

## The tangsi and the feesten

The *tangsi* was the place of the *feesten* — the *[[Tahoen Baroe|Nieuwjaar]]* (the grootste, vrolijkste feest), the *[[Poeusa]]* (the end of the fasting month), the *[[Koninginnedag]]* (the Queen's birthday), the *[[schietfeest / prijsschieten|schietfeest]]* (the annual shooting festival), the *[[compagniesfeest / bataljonsfeest|compagniesfeest]]*. The *Marechausseelied]] was sung in the *tangsi*; the *menarie*, *quadrille* and *wals* were danced by the *[[Tante]]* at the feesten. The *tangsi* was therefore the cultural production of the colonial army's moral economy.

## The dissolution (1949)

The closing arc of *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is the disbandment of the [[K.N.I.L.]] and the dissolution of the *tangsi* culture. The [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]] of [[27 December]] [[1949]] dissolved the *tangsi*; the Ambonese soldiers were transferred into the *Federale Legers* and *Negaratroepen* of the federal Indonesia, and then, with the federal Indonesia's dissolution into the unitary Republic, into the army of the Republic that had been their enemy. The *tangsi* culture that had made *[[Oom Ambon]]* was dissolved.

## Significance

The *tangsi* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the institutional home of *[[Oom Ambon]]* and the place where the moral economy of the colonial army was made. The book's argument is that the *tangsi* — both the garrison on Java and the *bivak* in the *buitengewesten* — was not just a barracks but a culture: the culture that made the *Oom* and the *Tante*, the *anak-kolong* and the *Marechausseelied*, the *feesten* and the *adat*. The dissolution of the *tangsi* culture with the disbandment of the [[K.N.I.L.]] in [[1949]] is the book's closing argument: what was lost was not the decorated hero but the ordinary soldier whose life had been made by the *tangsi*.

**Blang Pidië** was a Dutch *bivak* of the [[1ste Garnizoensbataljon|1ste Garnizoensbataljon]] on the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]] between [[Tjalang|Tjalang]] and [[Tapatoean|Tapatoean]]. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Blang Pidië is the Westkust *bivak* whose *bivak* — holding 1,500 Westkust women and children under three-brigade dekking in mid-March [[1942]] — was the principal concentration point of the [[Evacuation of women and children from the Westkust of Atjeh (March 1942)|Westkust evacuation]], and the *bivak* whose attack by a 200-man *bende* on [[16 March]] [[16 March 1942|16 March 1942]] precipitated the [[Battle of Blang Pidië (16 March 1942)|Battle of Blang Pidië]].

## Pre-war garrison

As of [[1 January]] [[1940]], Blang Pidië held four brigades of the 1ste Garnizoensbataljon. The *bivak* survived the [[1940]] reorganisations that dissolved the [[Tapatoean|Tapatoean]] and [[Meulaboh|Meulaboh]] detachments, and was one of the few Westkust *bivaks* to retain its 1ste Garnizoensbataljon garrison through [[1941]].

## The 16 March 1942 attack

By [[16 March]] [[16 March 1942|16 March 1942]] the *bivak* at Blang Pidië held 1,500 women and children of the Westkust evacuation, guarded by three brigades. At dawn the bivak was attacked by "een bende van ongeveer 200 man." The attack was beaten off; the *detachementscommandant* "met 2 brigades uitgerukt en had het terrein om het bivak van vijanden gezuiverd." The Blang Pidië attack was the immediate trigger of Lt [[A. Zijlmans|Zijlmans]]'s two-brigade relief column from Meulaboh, which fought the [[Battle of Blang Pidië (16 March 1942)|Battle of Blang Pidië]] — a 15-hour ambush march from Blang Pidië to Tapatoean on [[17 March]] [[17 March 1942|17 March 1942]], leaving 200–300 Atjehnese dead.

## Significance

Blang Pidië is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the Westkust *bivak* whose 1,500-strong women-and-children concentration made it the principal target of Atjehnese attack in mid-March [[1942]], and the *bivak* whose attack precipitated the largest sustained infantry engagement of the Westkust campaign. The staff history's emphasis on the *bivak*'s concentration function — and on the three-brigade dekking that successfully repelled the 200-man attack — frames Blang Pidië as the staff history's principal narrative instance of the Westkust *bivak* as both target and stronghold of the evacuation.

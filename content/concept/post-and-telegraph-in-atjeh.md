**Post and telegraph in Atjeh** — from the first telegraph wire between [[Koeta Radja]] and [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]] ([[20 March]] [[1875]]) through the enemy-clipped lines of the [[Dutch Policy of 'Concentration'|Concentration]] to the land-line [[Oeleë Lheuë|Oelèë Lheue]]–[[Medan]] ([[1912]]), the wireless station on [[Poelò We]] ([[1912]]) and the government telephone net of [[1913]] — became, with the enlargement of the post offices after the Sabang mail connection of [[1903]], one of the most visible services of the administration.

## Telegraph and telephone

The first telegraphic connection of the province was that between Koeta Radja and the debarkation place Oelèë Lheue, approved with the works of [[26 June]] [[1874]] and set into operation on [[20 March]] [[1875]]. In the Concentration years the hostile destruction of telephone lines, railways and bridges was the favourite enemy tactic (almost ended by [[1891]], "beschadiging aan telefoonleidingen, spoorlijnen en bruggen kwam in 1891 bijna niet meer voor"). Under Van Heutsz a telephone connection came over the watershed with [[Sigli]] and further along the North Coast. The post- and telegraph-service was importantly extended in the period 1908–1918: in [[1912]] a land-line from Oelèë Lheue to Medan came into being; in [[1914]] a telegraphic connection from [[Sinabang]] over [[Tapa Toean]] to [[Singkel]] came into exploitation; and in [[1912]] a station for wireless telegraphy was erected at [[Lë Meulè]] on Poelò We, serving the shipping traffic — "reeds spoedig werd hiervan een druk gebruik gemaakt" ("very soon busy use was made of it"). The telephone net laid by the [[Genie]] in the province was steadily improved and extended, so that "all government establishments, two only excepted, are connected telephonically"; in [[1913]] it passed to the Department of Home Administration as a government telephone net (*bestuurstelefoonnet*), exploited for the time being by the provincial engineer-service.

## Post offices and mail service

The inclusion of Sabang in the [[Stoomvaart Maatschappij Nederland|mail service]] ([[1903]]) brought Atjeh into the direct mail connection with Europe; the post offices at Sabang, Oelèë Lheue and Koeta Radja were enlarged and equipped for the full mail- and parcel-service (see [[Inauguration of the Sabang mail service (1903)]]). The increasing post traffic made the foundation of post- and telegraph-offices at [[Sigli]], [[Langsa]], [[Koeala Simpang]] and Sinabang necessary, while help-post-offices arose on the remaining government establishments. At Tapa Toean and in the K.P.M. ports the shipping companies kept their own agencies (see [[Koninklijke Paketvaart Maatschappij]]); [[Koeta Radja]] and Medan were linked from [[1919]] also by the through railway communications of the [[Atjeh Tram]] to the Deli net.

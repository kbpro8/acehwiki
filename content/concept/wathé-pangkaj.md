**Wathé pangkaj** (Malay *hasil pangkal*, "the yield of the capital," that is interest on capital) was the share of the annual produce of a pepper garden in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] owed by the planter to the *[[Peutoeha pangkaj|pangkaj-giver]]* for as long as the garden remained productive — irrespective of whether the original advance had been repaid.

## Rate and mode of payment

The *wathé pangkaj* usually amounted, the honorarium of the *[[Peutoeha parang|peutoeha parang]]* included, to one-tenth of the harvest, payable in cash or in kind. Payment took place mostly in kind, since the *peutoeha pangkaj* also held a right of pre-emption over the produce of gardens laid out with his capital, waived only if the *[[Aneu' seuneubô|aneu' seuneubô]]* received a higher bid from elsewhere.

## The government tariff

In consequence of disputes arising — among others between *peutoeha pangkaj* and *peutoeha parang* — a fixed tariff, varying only slightly between districts, was in later times introduced by the administration:

- *wathé pangkaj* — one guilder per *picol*;
- *wathé peutoeha* (or *wathé parang*) — fifty cents per *picol*;
- *[[Wathé oeléëbalang|wathé oeléëbalang]]* — a further one guilder per *picol*, due to the self-governing ruler on the export of the produce.

## Perpetual character

The distinctive feature of the charge, in [[D. W. N. de Boer]]'s account, was its perpetuity: "Ongeacht de wijze en het tempo der terugbetaling van de door hem genoten voorschotten, ook wanneer deze tot de laatste cent heeft plaats gehad" — irrespective of the manner and pace of repayment of the advances he had received, and even when this had taken place to the last cent — the planter continued to owe the *wathé pangkaj* so long as the garden bore.

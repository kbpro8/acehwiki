**The Oorlogspartij** (War Party) was the Acehnese political and military faction that advocated continued armed resistance against Dutch colonial rule. Predating the [[Aceh War]], this faction gained strength throughout the 1870s and 1880s, promoting the *heilige oorlog* (holy war) against the Dutch and organizing the collection of *wang sabil* (holy war funds) from throughout [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the Onderhoorigheden (dependencies).

## Origins

The Oorlogspartij emerged from the Acehnese political elite who opposed any accommodation with foreign rule. Following the fall of the kraton in [[1874]] and the death of the Sultan, the party found itself without a recognized national leader but continued to function through religious and secular leaders who promoted resistance.

## Organization and Leadership

### Religious Leadership

The party operated through a network of religious leaders, notably the geestdrijvers (religious zealots) such as [[Sjech Saman van Tirou]] of [[Tirou]]. These leaders provided religious sanction for the struggle, interpreting the conflict as a duty of all Muslims against the Christian Dutch colonial forces.

The Tirou school trained Schriftgeleerden (Islamic scholars) who carried these ideas throughout [[Groot-Atjeh]], creating a network of religious authority supporting the resistance.

### National Leadership

Following the death of the last Sultan in [[1874]], the Oorlogspartij initially lacked a formal national leader. This changed in [[August]] [[1884]], when [[Toeankoe Mohammed Daoed]] was declared of age and assumed the conduct of affairs. Though personally werkeloos (inactive), his recognition by the Acehnese as the legitimate Sultan provided the party with symbolic national authority.

Leaders repeatedly invoked his name when mobilizing the population against Dutch rule or collecting *wang sabil*.

## Activities

### Holy War Propaganda

The Oorlogspartij preached that armed resistance against the Dutch was a religious obligation. This transformed the [[Aceh War]] from a political conflict into a religious crusade, deepening commitment and broadening the base of resistance.

### Fund Collection

The party organized the systematic collection of *wang sabil* (holy war funds) from all parts of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and the dependencies. These funds supported fighters, purchased weapons, and maintained the organizational infrastructure of the resistance.

### Military Operations

The party coordinated the activities of the benden (bands) that conducted guerrilla operations against Dutch forces. These bands:
- Attacked Dutch convoys on communication lines
- Shelled and ambushed isolated posts
- Infiltrated the area around Dutch positions
- Conducted raids and robberies

## Relationship to the Population

The Oorlogspartij successfully pressured much of the Acehnese population into supporting the resistance, even those who might have preferred peace. The population within the fortified line was divided, with many remaining sympathetic to the resistance despite formal submission to Dutch authority.

## Significance

The Oorlogspartij represented a significant challenge to Dutch colonial ambitions. By linking resistance to religious duty and organizing it through established networks of Islamic authority, the party created a form of resistance that was difficult to suppress through conventional military means.

The Dutch strategy of concentration was in part a response to the Oorlogspartij's success in making the occupation of scattered positions untenable.

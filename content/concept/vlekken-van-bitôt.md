The **vlekken van Bitôt** — Bitôt's spots, the foamy grey triangular patches on the conjunctiva characteristic of vitamin A deficiency — were the diagnostic sign of *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* whose reliability was debated in the Netherlands-Indies literature discussed by Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] in his report of [[1940]].

## Sie Boen Lian's finding

[[Sie Boen Lian]] published several articles proving that these spots, especially in adults, were "nog geen conditio sine qua non" for the existence of xerophthalmia. He made this plausible by determining the vitamin A content of the blood, and by reporting that the spots remained unchanged despite a vitamin-A-rich diet and therapy.

## Gomperts's reservation

Gomperts accepted the finding but regretted that Sie Boen Lian had not stated whether, in these deviant cases, the conjunctiva showed the other marks of xerophthalmia — "de xerotische toestand met de voor xerophthalmie typische vuile verkleuring." For the general practitioner and for the ophthalmologist conducting an orienting survey, he observed, only the clinical local signs offered a hold for the diagnosis.

He recorded his own confirming case: in a seventeen-year-old youth complaining of *hemeralopie* (night blindness) he found Bitôt's spots together with xerotic conjunctival changes, and both subjective and objective signs disappeared promptly on a vitamin-A-rich diet and cod-liver oil therapy, so that the diagnosis of xerophthalmia could be made with fair certainty without a blood determination.

He did not share Dr. [[J. H. de Haas]]'s fear that Sie Boen Lian's observation would make diagnosis difficult in children showing all the typical conjunctival changes.

## In the field data

Bitôt's spots appear in Gomperts's tabulation of the local signs among the *kampong* xerophthalmia cases, alongside other conjunctival changes and corneal changes; and in his hospital series two cases presented corneal appearances resembling Bitôt's spots — case 7, where the lower half of both corneas had "het aspect van Bitôtsche vlekken" with staphylomatous distension, and case 14, where the other eye showed centrally a large opacity "met het aspect van Bit. vlek."

## Acehnese nomenclature

The Acehnese themselves knew xerophthalmia in the *moekims* [[VI Moekims Lhong|Lhong]], [[Lho Nga|Lho-Nga]] and [[Lam-Kabeuë]] as *penjikit sisë*; elsewhere in the surveyed area *sisë* meant only *kulit ikan*, the scales of a dead fish, or *kaki ajam*, a chicken's foot — a comparison close to the clinical appearance of the dried and wrinkled conjunctiva.

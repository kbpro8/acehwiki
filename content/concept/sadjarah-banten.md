**The Sadjarah Banten** — Javanese **Sejarah Banten**, English *Banten Chronicle* — is the Javanese-language chronicle of the [[Banten|Banten sultanate]] whose modern critical study was inaugurated by [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Dr Hoesein Djajadiningrat]]'s gold-medal Leiden dissertation *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* ([[1913]]), and which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] treats, in *[[Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java]]*, as a key *bedekte taal* text whose legendary material — the *Sandimaja* prologue, the *Tjioeng Wanara* legend, the *Sjech Djoemadilakbar* conversion story, the *Kadji Poerwa* allegory of the planted *doerianpit* — conceals references to the Sumatran east coast and the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere.

## Hoesein's 1913 dissertation

[[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Hoesein Djajadiningrat]]'s *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* (Leiden, [[1913]]) — defended under [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — opens with a paraphrase of the chronicle's first part and follows it with a commentary on the legendary material, culminating in a *Karakteristiek der Javaansche geschiedschrijving*. Hoesein treats the legendary material as partly fictitious, partly derived from a *handboek over Oudjavaansche klankleer* (handbook of Old-Javanese phonetics). [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws heavily on Hoesein's paraphrase, but demurs at the conclusion: the legendary material is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, not fiction but *bedekte taal* — veiled-language forms that hide references to the Sumatran east coast.

## The *Sandimaja* prologue and the *dasanama*

The *Sadjarah Banten* opens with a prologue in which **Sandimaja** instructs his wife **Lismaja**/**Lasmaja** and her *emban* **Bok Soemampir**, then invites his brothers and relatives (Sandisastra, Dangdangkaronpang, Tataparoenggoe, Répa, and others) to a feast at which the conversation turns to the qualities of a good ruler. [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Hoesein]] notes that "the *flauteur* has fetched most [of these names] from a handbook of Old-Javanese phonetics". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] presses further: *Sandisastra* is a synonym of *sandisoetra*, often the title of a handbook of *klankverbindingen* (phonetic combinations); the names that follow — *éka-, dwi-, tri-mantri* — are "purely grammatical terms" used in a *bedekte taal* sense; and *Dewi Lis- or Las-maja* is read as *badé-wilis* or *wilasa-maja*, "playing with beautiful appearance, transparent imagery". The prologue is thus a key, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, to the *bedekte taal* register of the entire chronicle.

## The Sjech Djoemadilakbar story

The eighth *Zang* of the [[Sadjarah Banten]] records that "in China came Sjech Djoemadilakbar to convert the prince to Islam. He did not succeed. When he heard a voice saying that it had been decreed that the Chinese king would remain a *kafir*, he departed for Java on a ship from [[Gresik|Garesik]], having first planted a *doerianpit*". The ship, by night, found itself on the top of a mountain; by morning it was in a harbour; Sjech Akbar disappeared. [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Hoesein]] reads this as fiction; [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes the *doerianpit* as a *bedekte taal* form for the planted faith, and the "China" as *Tji-na*, a contraction of *soe-tji boewa-na*, "holy land" — i.e. [[Taroema]] on the Sumatran Overwal.

## The Kadji Poerwa allegory

The [[Sadjarah Banten]] also records the legend of **Kadji Poerwa**, who, in [[Padjadjaran|Padjadjaran]], planted a *tjutjupu* (a kind of seed) in the middle of the *aloen-aloen*, with the prophecy that if a shoot sprang up, the moment had not yet come for the princes of Java to change their faith. The shoot sprang up by morning — *legoendi woeloeng* — and could not be uprooted or hewn. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes *woeloeng* as "blue-black", i.e. *bedekte taal*; *legoendi* as the name of a shrub that roots deeply and echoes *séla goeri* and *séla-ndra*, the [[Cailendra|Cailendra dynasty]] — the allegory of the planted shoot is, in this reading, an allegory of the [[Cailendra|Cailendras]]' survival in the face of the new Islamic order.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the [[Sadjarah Banten]] is the principal Javanese chronicle source on which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws for his *bedekte taal* reading of the [[Banten|Banten sultanate]]'s legendary material — and, through the [[Faletahan|Faletahan/Sunan Gunung Jati]] identification, the principal Javanese source for the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]–[[Acheh|Atjeh]]–Java triangle of the early sixteenth century.

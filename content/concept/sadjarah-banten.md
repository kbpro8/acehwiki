**The Sadjarah Banten** — modern Javanese spelling **Sejarah Banten**; intermediate spelling **Sajarah Banten**; English *Banten Chronicle* — is the Javanese-language chronicle of the [[Banten|Banten sultanate]] on Java, one of the principal historical texts of the Javanese-Malay tradition. Its modern critical study was inaugurated by [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Dr Hoesein Djajadiningrat]]'s gold-medal Leiden dissertation *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* ([[1913|1913]]), and it is cited by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in the introduction to the [[1955|1955]] edition of the two Malay treatises of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] as the source of a hypothesis — advanced by Voorhoeve in [[1951|1951]] and subsequently withdrawn — about Raniri's Meccan sojourn before his departure for [[Acheh|Aceh]] in [[1637|1637]]. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] treats it, in *[[Historische Curiositeiten uit Malajoe en Java]]*, as a key *bedekte taal* (veiled-language) text whose legendary material — the *Sandimaja* prologue, the *Tjioeng Wanara* legend, the *Sjech Djoemadilakbar* conversion story, the *Kadji Poerwa* allegory of the planted *doerianpit* (durian-seed) — conceals references to the Sumatran east coast and the [[Acheh|Acehnese]] sphere.

## Hoesein's 1913 Dissertation

[[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Hoesein Djajadiningrat]]'s *Critische Beschouwing van de Sadjarah Banten* (Leiden, [[1913|1913]]) — defended under [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] — opens with a paraphrase of the chronicle's first part and follows it with a commentary on the legendary material, culminating in a *Karakteristiek der Javaansche geschiedschrijving* (characteristic of Javanese historiography). Hoesein treats the legendary material as partly fictitious, partly derived from a *handboek over Oudjavaansche klankleer* (handbook of Old-Javanese phonetics). [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws heavily on Hoesein's paraphrase, but demurs at the conclusion: the legendary material is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, not fiction but *bedekte taal* — veiled-language forms that hide references to the Sumatran east coast.

## The Ibn ʿAlam Hypothesis

In his earlier notes in *BKI* 107 (1951), p. 357 ff., [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] had advanced the hypothesis — on the strength of a passage in the Sadjarah Banten — that [[Raniri|Raniri]] sojourned in [[Mecca|Mecca]] before his departure for Aceh, and that he is the scholar referred to as "Ibn ʿAlī" or "Ibn ʿAlam" who refused to return with the Banten envoys to their country. The hypothesis was an attempt to explain how Raniri could have come to Aceh already familiar with the *[[Bahasa Djawi|Bahasa Djawi]]* (Jawi Malay) and with two Malay works already in his luggage.

In the [[1955|1955]] edition, Voorhoeve withdrew the hypothesis. On further reflection, he concluded that it is much more probable that the "Ibn ʿAlam" of the Sadjarah Banten is in fact **Ibn ʿAlān** — that is, **Muḥammad ʿAlī Ibn ʿAlān as-Siddīqī** (d. 1057 H / [[1648|1648]]) — of whom it is known for certain that he was repeatedly consulted from Banten: by Sultan Abū ʾl-Mafāḥir ʿAbd al-Qādir on the *Naṣīḥat al-mulūk* of al-Ghazālī (on which Ibn ʿAlān had written a commentary), and by Abū ʾl-Maʿālī Aḥmad on the *Tuḥfa al-mursala* of [[Muhammad b. Fadl Allah|Muḥammad b. Faḍl Allāh]]. The Banten envoys, in other words, consulted not Raniri but Ibn ʿAlān; and Raniri's Malay education is to be sought not in Mecca but in Gujarat.

## The *Sandimaja* Prologue and the *Dasanama*

The Sadjarah Banten opens with a prologue in which **Sandimaja** instructs his wife **Lismaja**/**Lasmaja** and her *emban* (lady-in-waiting) **Bok Soemampir**, then invites his brothers and relatives (Sandisastra, Dangdangkaronpang, Tataparoenggoe, Répa, and others) to a feast at which the conversation turns to the qualities of a good ruler. [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Hoesein]] notes that "the *flauteur* (fabricator) has fetched most [of these names] from a handbook of Old-Javanese phonetics". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] presses further: *Sandisastra* is a synonym of *sandisoetra*, often the title of a handbook of *klankverbindingen* (phonetic combinations); the names that follow — *éka-, dwi-, tri-mantri* — are "purely grammatical terms" used in a *bedekte taal* sense; and *Dewi Lis- or Las-maja* is read as *badé-wilis* or *wilasa-maja*, "playing with beautiful appearance, transparent imagery". The prologue is thus a key, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, to the *bedekte taal* register of the entire chronicle.

## The Sjech Djoemadilakbar Story

The eighth *Zang* (canto) of the Sadjarah Banten records that "in China came Sjech Djoemadilakbar to convert the prince to Islam. He did not succeed. When he heard a voice saying that it had been decreed that the Chinese king would remain a *kafir* (unbeliever), he departed for Java on a ship from [[Gresik|Garesik]], having first planted a *doerianpit* (durian-seed)". The ship, by night, found itself on the top of a mountain; by morning it was in a harbour; Sjech Akbar disappeared. [[Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Hoesein]] reads this as fiction; [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes the *doerianpit* as a *bedekte taal* form for the planted faith, and the "China" as *Tji-na*, a contraction of *soe-tji boewa-na*, "holy land" — i.e. [[Taroema|Taroema]] on the Sumatran Overwal.

## The Kadji Poerwa Allegory

The Sadjarah Banten also records the legend of **Kadji Poerwa**, who, in [[Padjadjaran|Padjadjaran]], planted a *tjutjupu* (a kind of seed) in the middle of the *aloen-aloen* (village square), with the prophecy that if a shoot sprang up, the moment had not yet come for the princes of Java to change their faith. The shoot sprang up by morning — *legoendi woeloeng* — and could not be uprooted or hewn. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes *woeloeng* as "blue-black", i.e. *bedekte taal*; *legoendi* as the name of a shrub that roots deeply and echoes *séla goeri* and *séla-ndra*, the [[Cailendra|Cailendra dynasty]] — the allegory of the planted shoot is, in this reading, an allegory of the [[Cailendra|Cailendras]]' survival in the face of the new Islamic order.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Sadjarah Banten is significant in two principal respects. First, it is the source of a hypothesis about [[Raniri|Raniri]]'s Meccan sojourn that was advanced by [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] in [[1951|1951]] and withdrawn in [[1955|1955]]; the chronicle is itself a document of the networks by which the [[Banten|Banten sultanate]] — one of the principal Muslim states of Java in the seventeenth century — communicated with the religious scholarship of Mecca in this period. The fact that the Banten envoys consulted Ibn ʿAlān in Mecca — on the *Naṣīḥat al-mulūk* of al-Ghazālī and the *Tuḥfa al-mursala* of Muḥammad b. Faḍl Allāh — is itself a document of the Banten sultanate's engagement with the scholastic Sufism of the wider Muslim world, and of the channels by which that engagement was carried on. Second, it is the principal Javanese chronicle source on which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] draws for his *bedekte taal* reading of the [[Banten|Banten sultanate]]'s legendary material — and, through the [[Faletahan|Faletahan/Sunan Gunung Jati]] identification, the principal Javanese source for the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]–[[Acheh|Aceh]]–Java triangle of the early sixteenth century.

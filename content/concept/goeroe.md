**goeroe** is a subject named in *[[General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh (1943)|General Swart, Pacifier of Atjeh]]* ([[1943]]) by [[Maurice Henri du Croo|M. H. du Croo]], in connection with the history of [[Atjeh]] and the governorship of [[H. N. A. Swart]].

## Account in du Croo’s Generaal Swart ([[1943]])

### From [[Lebé Grondong]]

In [[1910]] the column of [[F. Darlang|Darlang]] held the [[Péndéngvallei]], so that Lebé Grondong moved with his followers to neighbouring sub-divisions. In March [[1911]] a brigade under sergeant [[Wisse]] surprised his hiding-place between [[Tampor]] and [[Lésten]] and reported him killed with his principal *panglima* and a follower, the booty being an M.95 rifle, a Mauser repeating rifle, a Beaumont and a Mauser self-loading pistol. In September came news that he was *not* dead: he had toured the Gajò Loeö during the *hari raja* feasts extorting from the population and had obtained two carbines on the death of the resistance head of [[Badaq]]; in October he murdered [[Rödjö Akoeb]] of [[Penosan]] in revenge for the latter's journey to [[Koeta Radja|Koetaradja]] — but handed in a *panglima*, a *[[goeroe]]* and a carbine. In [[1912]] he came in submission.

### From [[Het Pacificeerende Bestuurswerk 1908-1918]]

So in Atjeh too the matter was tackled, with an eye to training the best possible teachers as quickly as possible, since "a bad teacher spoils so much precisely in a beginning period that ten good teachers can afterwards scarcely make it good again." In [[1910]] a *[[Normaalcursus]]* was therefore founded at [[Koeta Radja]], which brought the necessary improvement in quality.

### From [[Normaalcursus]]

Since "a bad teacher spoils so much precisely in a beginning period that ten good teachers can afterwards scarcely make it good again," the course was established "which brought the necessary improvement in quality." It was the first institutional answer in [[Atjeh]] to the shortage of qualified teachers that hampered *[[Volksonderwijs in Atjeh|volksonderwijs]]* in its earliest years; two-year courses for the training of village schoolteachers were later founded at various places, so that by [[1938]] more than two-thirds of the province's six hundred village teachers were of Acehnese or other local origin.

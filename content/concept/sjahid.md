**Sjahid** — also *shahid*, the Arabic term for a martyr of the faith — was the status accorded by the Acehnese religious resistance to those who fell in the *[[prang sabi]]* (the holy war) against the [[K.N.I.L.]]. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *sjahid* is the religious framing of the death of the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō]] resistance leaders: *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]* fell as a *geloofsgetuige* (a witness of the faith) at the [[Battle of Aneu Galong (1896)|Battle of Aneu Galong]] in [[1896]]; *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* became *sjahid, geloofsmartelaar in de heilige oorlog* when he was killed by the korporaal [[Nussy]] on [[3 December]] [[1911]].

## The doctrine

The doctrine of *sjahid* is paired with that of *[[prang sabi]]* — the holy war — and the *paradijs* (paradise). A Muslim who fell in the *prang sabi* became a *sjahid* — a martyr of the faith — and went to the *paradijs*. The widow of *[[Teungkoe Asan Oeleë Toetooë]]* put the doctrine explicitly to [[Schmidt]]: her husband "in de heilige strijd was gevallen door zijn eigen vrije wil; u was slechts het werktuig in Allah's hand, dat hem het *paradijs* deelachtig deed worden." Schmidt, on this doctrine, was merely the instrument in Allah's hand by which her husband had been granted the *paradijs*.

## The sjahid and the inheritance

The *sjahid* status of the fallen was the doctrinal inheritance of the next generation. *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* — whose father *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]* had fallen as a *geloofsgetuige* at [[Battle of Aneu Galong (1896)|Aneu Galong]] — refused submission on the grounds that "Zijn Vader was als *geloofsgetuige* gevallen, hij zou in diens voetstappen treden." The *sjahid* status of the father made submission impossible for the son: the son would follow in the father's footsteps, become a *sjahid* himself, and join the father in the *paradijs*.

## The sjahid and the relics

When the corpse of *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* was carried out to [[Tangsé]], "de bevolking van Tangsé liep uit naar de begrafenis. Het verband en de doeken, waarin het lijk was gewikkeld, werden in honderden stukjes gescheurd en meegenomen als heilige reliquiën." The tearing of the relics is the book's quiet acknowledgement that the *sjahid*'s death, far from extinguishing the doctrine, made it a permanent feature of the country's religious memory. The *sjahid*'s relics would protect their bearers; the doctrine of *prang sabi* had passed from the living resisters into the material culture of the bevolking.

## Significance

The *sjahid* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the religious framing of the death of the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō]] resistance leaders. The book's argument is that the *sjahid* status of the fallen was the doctrinal inheritance of the next generation, and that the *sjahid*'s relics made the doctrine a permanent feature of the country's religious memory. The *sjahid* is therefore the book's marker of the religious dimension of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] — the dimension the [[K.N.I.L.]] could not, in the end, defeat by arms alone.

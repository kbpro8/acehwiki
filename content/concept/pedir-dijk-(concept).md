The **[[Pedir-dijk (concept)|Pedir-dijk]]** — conceived as a defensive line — was the dike along the right (west) bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] from [[Lemboe]] to [[Kota Radja Bedil]], which Borel calls the "aangewezen verdedigingslijn der Atjeh-rivier" (the designated defensive line of the Atjeh-rivier). The dike served both as a hydraulic work (to hold floodwater) and as a military line (a raised causeway commanding the river and the surrounding sawah), and its occupation or abandonment was, in Borel's account, a recurring strategic indicator of the conduct of the war.

## The Dike as Hydraulic and Military Work

The Pedir-dijk was, in Borel's account, a dike along the right (west) bank of the Atjeh-rivier from Lemboe to [[Kota Radja Bedil|K. Radja Bedil]]. The dike served both as a hydraulic work — to hold floodwater and to regulate the level of the Atjeh-rivier — and as a military line: a raised causeway that commanded the river and the surrounding [[Sawah|sawah]], and that could be fortified with bentengs at intervals. The dike ran from [[Lemboe]] at its southern end — "the key" to the dike — through the kampong [[Langkroek]] in the middle, to [[Kota Radja Bedil]] at its northern end, where the dike commanded the river mouth. The dike was, in effect, the natural defensive line of the Atjeh-rivier: a continuous raised position from which the river and the sawah could be dominated, and from which the enemy's access to the river could be denied. Borel calls it the "aangewezen verdedigingslijn der Atjeh-rivier" — the designated defensive line of the Atjeh-rivier.

## The Recurring Indicator

The occupation or abandonment of the Pedir-dijk was, in Borel's account, a recurring strategic indicator of the conduct of the war. The dike was briefly in Dutch hands at [[Lemboe]] on [[25 December|25]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]], but "onbegrijpelijk genoeg" van Swieten ordered Lemboe immediately abandoned — the first of the strategic errors that Borel returns to throughout the book. The dike was reoccupied at [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|Langkroek]] on [[28 September]] [[1874]] and at [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|Lemboe]] on [[7 November]] [[1874]]; [[Kota Radja Bedil|K. Radja Bedil]] was reoccupied by the Dutch on [[28 June]] [[1874]] after the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]]. the Lamara post east of the [[Marine-benteng]], beyond the dike, was harassed by [[Lilla|lilla]] fire from the kampong Langkroek before the conquest of Langkroek. The dike was thus a recurring focus of the campaigning of [[1874]]: each section had to be conquered separately, at high cost, because van Swieten had abandoned the whole line in December [[1873]].

## The Strategic Significance

The strategic significance of the Pedir-dijk was, in Borel's account, that it was the natural defensive line of the Atjeh-rivier — the line that the Dutch should have held from the fall of the Kraton onwards, and that they had to re-conquer section by section in [[1874]]. The dike's abandonment by van Swieten in December [[1873]] was, in Borel's account, the original error from which the prolonged campaigning of [[1874]] flowed. The dike's reoccupation by Pel in the course of [[1874]] was, conversely, the principal strategic achievement of the year: with the dike in Dutch hands, the Atjeh-rivier was secured, the line of communication between [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] and the sea was protected, and the enemy's access to the river was denied. The dike was, however, only one element of the larger [[Stelling-Pel|stelling-Pel]] that Pel completed in May [[1875]]: the stelling-Pel extended the line of defence from the dike westward over [[Longbatta-Mesigit|Longbatta Mesigit]] to the sea-strand at [[Oeleh Leh]]. The Pedir-dijk was thus, in Borel's account, both the natural defensive line of the Atjeh-rivier and the nucleus of the larger stelling-Pel — the line that should have been held from the fall of the Kraton, and that was finally completed only in May [[1875]].

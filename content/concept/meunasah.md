**Meunasah** (from Arabic *madrasah*, via Malay *surau*) was the village chapel of [[Great Acheh|Acheh]], the communal building at the heart of every [[Gampong|gampong]]. It served multiple functions simultaneously — a sleeping-place for men, a rest-house for strangers, a house of prayer, and a place of assembly for village affairs.

## Functions

**Religious:** The meunasah was where the village held daily prayers (the Friday midday service requiring a larger [[Meusigit|meusigit]] — a proper mosque — if sufficient worshippers could be assembled). It housed the furniture needed for religious litanies (*ratébs*), including kettledrums (*rapa'i* or *rapana*) and wooden rings known as *pulèt*. A large lamp (*kandé*) was lit on the nights of the fasting month.

**Social and political:** The affairs of the [[Gampong|gampong]] were debated in the meunasah. Village festivals were held in its courtyard, [[Kanduri|kanduris]] (religious feasts) organized, and marriage contracts concluded. A person of rank arriving unexpectedly would go first to the meunasah and announce his arrival from there.

**Accommodation:** It served as the regular sleeping place for the men of the gampong — since in Acehnese domestic custom the husband typically slept away from his wife's house during most of his marriage. Strangers passing through were housed there.

## Physical Description

The meunasah was generally a wooden building on posts. Some were built not on posts but on a raised stone foundation finished on top with cement, with stone stairs giving access. Such more imposing stone structures were called *déah* (from Arabic *zāwiyah*). The building contained a masonry niche (*mehrab* or *merab*) to indicate the direction of [[Mecca]]. Its courtyard was sometimes surrounded by a low stone wall.

## Where the Headless Village

Where the heads of the village were not both pious and watchful, the meunasah was apt to become the scene of all manner of Acehnese iniquities. In its courtyard fights of cocks and other fighting birds were held; within the building gambling went on and other vice was shamefully practised at night.

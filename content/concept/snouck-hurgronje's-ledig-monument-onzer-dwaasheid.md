**"Een ledig monument onzer dwaasheid"** — Dutch for "an empty monument of our folly" — is the verdict pronounced by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] on the Dutch-built replacement [[Meuseugit Raya|Meuseugit Raja]] of [[Kutaradja|Koeta Radja]], the great mosque constructed by the colonial government in Byzantine style at a cost of more than *f* 200,000 (over 200,000 guilders) to replace the original Atjeh great mosque destroyed in the [[Burning of Meuseugit Raja (1873)|burning of 1873]]. The verdict is quoted by Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]] in his chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]).

## Kernkamp's account

Kernkamp introduces the verdict in the course of his discussion of the Indonesian mosque. His wording: "The only more or less pompous mosque, in Koeta Radja, built in Byzantine style, was built by us and offered to the Atjehsche population to replace the Meuseugit Raja, which was shot to fire in 1873. The gesture was grand enough, for it then already cost more than 2 tons. The success was not proportionate. The Atjehers considered the religious exercises in this God's house built by *kåfirs* (unbelievers) as worthless. Snouck Hurgronje spoke of 'an empty monument of our folly'."

## The verdict's meaning

The verdict captures, in a single phrase, Snouck Hurgronje's assessment of the colonial government's attempt to undo the religious damage done by the burning of the original Meuseugit Raja in [[1873]]. The Dutch-built replacement mosque — a "pompous" building in Byzantine style, costing more than 200,000 guilders — was, in Snouck Hurgronje's view, an "empty monument": empty in the literal sense, because the Atjehners refused to use it for religious exercises on the grounds that "religious exercises in this God's house built by *kåfirs* (unbelievers)" were "worthless"; and a "monument of our folly" in the figurative sense, because the colonial government had imagined that the building of a replacement mosque by the infidel Dutch could undo the religious damage done by the original burning. The verdict is one of the most-cited assessments of the limits of Dutch colonial religious policy in Atjeh.

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the verdict is significant as the principal surviving assessment — by the colonial government's own chief Islam adviser — of the limits of Dutch colonial religious policy in Atjeh. Snouck Hurgronje's verdict that the Dutch-built replacement Meuseugit Raja was "an empty monument of our folly" places the same colonial government that had burned the original mosque in [[1873]] in the position of having spent more than 200,000 guilders on a building that the Atjehners refused to use. The verdict is also significant as a measure of Snouck Hurgronje's own position: although he was the chief Islam adviser to the colonial government during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh war]] and the principal architect of the [[Korte Verklaring|*korte verklaring*]] policy of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], he was capable of pronouncing the building of the replacement Meuseugit Raja a "folly" — that is, of distinguishing his own assessment of the limits of colonial religious policy from the policy actually pursued.

**Diët** (Gayo: *diöt*, Alas: *diat*, Arabic *dijah*) was the Atjèhnese **blood money** (*zoengeld*) — the composition sum paid as the non-bloody form of revenge for manslaughter or wounding, in contrast to *[[bila|bila]]* (the violent blood revenge). Tariffed by adat and dependent on the parties' rank, *diët* was one of the four types of Atjèhnese debt (*oetang*), specifically the *oetang njawöng* (life debt from murder).

## Tariffs

The *diët* tariffs varied by region and rank:
- **In Atjèh**: 500 dollars for a free person, half for a slave
- **In Gajö**: 400–600 dollars
- **In Alas**: 120 dollars for a commoner, more for a head

## The four types of debt

The four types of Atjèhnese debt (*oetang*) were:
1. *Oetang meuïh* — money debt
2. *Oetang djandji* — debt from promise
3. *Oetang darah* — blood debt from wounding (a lesser form)
4. *Oetang njawöng* — life debt from murder (the *diët*)

## Tjo ripè

The *diët* was collected through the *tjo ripè* — the collection of contributions from *kawöm*-genooten (clan members) to pay fines imposed by the administration, but above all to pay *diët*. This collective responsibility for blood-money payment was a key function of the *kawöm* (clan) system.

## Comparison with bila

*Diët* was the peaceful alternative to *bila* (blood revenge). Where *bila* demanded the "blood-debt sacrifice" (*bila göb*) — the killing of the offender or a clan member — *diët* allowed the matter to be settled through payment. The choice between *bila* and *diët* depended on the wishes of the wronged party and the ability of the offender's *kawöm* to pay.

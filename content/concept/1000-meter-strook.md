**The 1000-meter strook** — the *thousand-metre strip*, also *open strip*, *free strip* — was the 1,000-metre wide, felled, open zone surrounding the Dutch [[Geconcentreerde Linie|Geconcentreerde Linie]] (Concentrated Line) around [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja] in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjèh], established by the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[1884]]–[[1886]] to prevent the enemy from approaching the Dutch posts unobserved or slipping through. The strip — which could only be kept clear with great difficulty and losses — was the principal site of the daily guerrilla warfare between Dutch patrols and Atjèhse Muslemin, and the location of the notorious [[Kaloet|Kaloet]] fortification that the Dutch repeatedly failed to take.

## The Establishment

The Geconcentreerde Linie, established by the concentration system of [[1884]]–[[1886]] under [[Demmeni|Demmeni]], was surrounded by a 1,000-metre wide, felled, open strip — the 1000-meter strook. The strip was to prevent the enemy from approaching the posts unobserved or slipping through. A *[[Ceintuur-trambaan|ceintuursporbaan]]* (belt railway) and telephone would connect the posts with one another and by spokes with Koeta Radja and [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]]. All this was carried out and arranged as quickly and well as possible — indeed excellently — and by [[July 1886|July]] [[1886]] everything was ready.

## The Overgrowth

The 1000-meter strook, which could only be kept clear with great difficulty and losses, was soon largely overgrown again with tropical speed. The Muslemin made the line unsafe, damaged the railway and bridges, destroyed the telephone line, fired on the trains, laid grenades under the rails, and made raids inward. The troop strength, then about 5,000 men, had a heavy service through the occupation and securing of the posts, patrols within the line, and suffered much from beri-beri and other diseases.

## The Kaloet Fortification

The notorious [[Kaloet|Kaloet]] — the principal Atjèhse fortification — was located within the 1000-meter strook. The Dutch troops repeatedly bloodied their heads against Kaloet; they could not take it. The reports then said that the Dutch were clearing, that they had achieved their object, that the taking of Kaloet was unnecessary. From Kaloet the enemy continually bombarded Dutch trains, posts, patrols, and transports. Under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s governorship, [[Teuku Umar|Teukoe Oema]] — operating with Dutch troops, Dutch artillery, and Dutch rix-dollars — took Kaloet on [[10 August]] [[10 August]] [[1893]], almost without a blow. He blew them away or walked in.

## The Encirclement

Under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s governorship, the enemy closed the Dutch line ever more tightly, brought his fortifications to within 150 metres of the Dutch belt railway, in the 1000-meter strook, and harassed the Dutch from there continually. The enemy even entrenched himself within the line repeatedly, and had to be driven out with force of arms in [[April 1887|April]] and [[October 1887|October]] [[1887]] and in [[July 1888|July]] [[1888]], and a part of the railway had to be moved further inward. Stepping ever more boldly and seriously threatening the communication between several posts, the Dutch were finally compelled to act outward.

## Significance

The 1000-meter strook is the principal site of the daily guerrilla warfare between Dutch patrols and Atjèhse Muslemin from [[1886]] to the end of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]]. The strip — established to prevent the enemy from approaching the Dutch posts unobserved — was, in the event, the principal site of the enemy's approach: the overgrowth of the strip with tropical speed, the location of the notorious Kaloet fortification within it, and the enemy's encirclement of the line from within the strip — these were the principal forms of the Atjèhse military effort in the middle and late phases of the war. The Dutch's inability to keep the strip clear, and the enemy's ability to entrench within it, were the principal military failures of the concentration system, and the principal arguments for the Snouck Hurgronje prescription of a more active defence.

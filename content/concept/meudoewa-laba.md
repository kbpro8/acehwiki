**Meudoewa laba** ("the dividing of the profit in two") was the Atjehnese contractual formula under which the yield of a planting was shared equally between the supplier of capital and the planter.

## In the pepper economy

Two applications are recorded by [[D. W. N. de Boer]]. First, as a hedge against bad pepper prices and against the failure of the planting, the *[[Peutoeha pangkaj|pangkaj-giver]]* often stipulated that the *[[Aneu' seuneubô|aneu' seuneubô]]* should plant an intercrop alongside the pepper, or *ficus elastica* or *hevea* along the edge of the pepper garden, half the yield of which was then to be ceded to him — *meudoewa laba*.

Second, where a pepper planter defaulted on his contract and was ousted, the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]* could give the garden out in *[[Mawaih|deelbouw]]* to other planters on much more favourable terms for himself, namely against delivery of half the total yield: *meudoewa laba*, or *mawaih asoë*, or *mawaih boh* (*boh*, Malay *boeah*, fruit).

**The Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem** — the Atjehnese "four-princes system" — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the institutional preference of the [[Acheh|Atjehsche]] sultanate for grouping the chiefdoms of its highland and peripheral dependencies into groups of four, each group headed by a *radja berempat* (literally "four-princes") who served as the principal intermediary with the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]]. The system was imposed, the Bureau records, on the [[Gajoland|Gajo]] and [[Alasland|Alas]] highlands during the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehnese supremacy in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]], and was imitated — "al dan niet daartoe genoopt door Atjeh" — "whether or not compelled to it by Atjeh" — by the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] chiefs on the eastern side of the highlands, producing the [[radja berempat|radja berempat]] institution of the [[Batak-Karo|Karo-Batak]] *sibajaks*.

## The institutional form

The Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem, as the Encyclopaedisch Bureau describes it, was an institutional arrangement by which four chiefdoms — in the [[Gajoland|Gajo]] and [[Alasland|Alas]] highlands, the four principal *stamhoofden* (tribal heads); in the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] lands, four of the *sibajaks* (the Karo *oeroeng*-chiefs) — were appointed as a group charged with the maintenance of good relations with the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate. The four were distinguished from the other chiefdoms of their respective lands by the title *radja berempat* — "four-princes" — and they served, in effect, as the political interface between the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] and the broader community of chiefs.

The Bureau records the institutional preference of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate for "dit getal" — "this number [four]" — as a typical feature of Atjehnese imperial administration: "Dit wederom blijkt hier de typische voorkeur van het Atjehsche gezag voor dit getal" — "Here again appears the typical preference of the Atjehnese authority for this number."

## The Atjehsche oppermacht in the Gajo- en Alaslanden

The system was imposed, in the first instance, on the [[Gajoland|Gajo]] and [[Alasland|Alas]] highlands. The Bureau records that the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehsche oppermacht in de Gajo- en Alaslanden]] had been established as a resident Atjehnese power, and that the [[Gajo|Gajo'sche stamhoofden]] — the tribal heads of the Gayo — had been the first to apply the four-princes measure. The Atjehnese arrangement transformed the loose community of Gajo and Alas tribal heads into a grouped hierarchy, with four of their number singled out for direct relations with the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]].

## The imitation in the Karo-Batak lands

The system was imitated, the Bureau records, by the [[Batak-Karo|Karo-Batak]] chiefs on the eastern side of the highlands. The Bureau records that "in dien tijd door de Karo'sche hoofden al dan niet daartoe genoopt door Atjeh — denzelfden maatregel werd genomen, die ook de Gajo'sche stamhoofden hadden toegepast: uit de rij van sibajaks wezen zij een viertal aan, dat in het bijzonder belast was met de handhaving dier goede betrekkingen" — "in that time the Karo chiefs — whether or not compelled to it by Atjeh — took the same measure that the Gajo tribal heads had applied: from the row of sibajaks they appointed a fourfold, that was especially charged with the maintenance of those good relations." The four [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *sibajaks* chosen were, the Bureau records, those of [[Lingga|Lingga]], [[Sarinembah|Sarinembah]], [[Baroes Djahé|Baroes Djahé]] and [[Soeka|Soeka]], who received the title of *radja berempat*.

## The Dutch revival: the five-Sibajak landschappen

The Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem was, the Bureau records, revived and amplified by the Dutch colonial state after the [[Dutch subjugation of the Karo-Batak lands (1904-1906)|annexation of the Karo hoogvlakte]] in [[1904]]–[[1906]]. The Bureau records that "Met het verdwijnen van Atjeh's suprematie over deze streken nam ook de belang- rijkheid der positie van deze viervorsten in het Karoland sterk af, zoodat zij, ten tijde van onze machtsvestiging, zich alleen nog door den titel van de overige oeroenghoofden onderscheidden, doch in macht verder geheel met deze gelijk stonden" — "With the disappearance of Atjeh's supremacy over these regions, the importance of the position of these four-princes in the Karoland also strongly declined, so that, at the time of our establishment of power, they distinguished themselves only by the title from the other oeroeng-chiefs, but in power were otherwise entirely equal to them." The Dutch, however, "de vergeten positie dier radja-berempat opgerakeld" — "resurrected the forgotten position of those radja-berempat" — and "hen in het Karoland zelf een macht toegekend, welke zij daar nimmer bezeten zullen hebben" — "granted them, in the Karoland itself, a power which they will never have possessed there." To the four *sibajaks* of [[Lingga|Lingga]], [[Sarinembah|Sarinembah]], [[Baroes Djahé|Baroes Djahé]] and [[Soeka|Soeka]] the Dutch added a fifth — the *Sibajak* of [[Koeta Boeloeh|Koeta Boeloeh]] — producing the five-*sibajak* arrangement of [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *landschapshoofden* that would, throughout the colonial period, govern the [[Batak-Karo|Karo hoogvlakte]].

## Significance

The Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the principal institutional imprint of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate on the highland chiefdoms of central [[Sumatra|Sumatra]]. It is the institutional hinge between the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehnese supremacy in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]] and the [[Batak-Karo|Karo-Batak]] lands on the eastern side of the highlands; and it is the institutional ancestor of the five-*sibajak* *landschapshoofden* of the colonial [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *landschappen*. The system is thus the institutional complement of the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Atjehnese overlordship of the East Coast]]: where the latter extended Atjehnese power along the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malakka]], the former extended it across the central Sumatran highlands.

**The Defense of Netherlands Indies debate cluster ([[1916]]–[[1917]])** is the interconnected body of speeches, pamphlets, organisations, persons, concepts, and parliamentary interventions through which the question of how to secure the [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] against foreign aggression was fought out in Dutch public life during the [[First World War]]. The cluster revolves around the personal rivalry between former [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Governor-General J. B. van Heutsz]] — championing the defence programme of his son-in-law [[J. Van der Weyden]] — and his former subordinate [[Van der Maaten]], whose pamphlet of [[25 February]] [[1917]] exposed the secret co-authorship and political manoeuvring behind the campaign. This entry is the navigational hub for the cluster's fourteen constituent articles.

## Origins: the stalled defence reform of 1913-1914

The cluster's prehistory begins with the [[Staatscommissie voor de Verdediging van Nederlandsch-Indië]], which issued its report in [[1913]]. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had declined to sit on the Commission in [[1912]], dismissing its work as "*monnikenwerk*" (monk's work). A *vlootwet* (fleet bill) had been prepared and would have been submitted to the [[Staten-Generaal]] in August [[1914]], but the outbreak of the [[First World War]] prevented its introduction; it was not submitted in [[1915]] or [[1916]] either. The [[Netherlands Indies Fleet Bill (1914)|Fleet Bill of 1914]] thus became, in its absence, one of the central grievances of the debate that followed. On [[9 January]] [[1914]], at a meeting of the [[Vereeniging ter Beoefening van de Krijgswetenschap]], [[J. Van der Weyden|Van der Weyden]] — with Van Heutsz's authorisation — quoted the devastating private judgment that the proposed fleet "*niet vechten zal*" (will not fight) and the proposed army "*niet vechten kan*" (cannot fight). The follow-up meeting of [[27 February]] [[1914]] became the occasion on which Van der Weyden first promised his defence brochure, though the brochure itself would not appear for more than two years.

## The campaign of January-February 1917

The campaign proper opened with [[Van Heutsz's Bond van Vrije Liberalen defense speech (13 January 1917)|Van Heutsz's speech at the Bond van Vrije Liberalen]] in [[Amsterdam]] on [[13 January]] [[1917]]. The speech, reported in *De Nieuwe Courant* and *De Telegraaf* of [[13 January]]–[[14 January]] [[1917]], called for the appointment of a *buitengewoon Regeerings-Commissaris* with unlimited powers to reform the colonial defence system — a role pointing unmistakably to Van Heutsz himself. Heer Knobel rose to propose Van Heutsz as "*den man bij uitnemendheid*" for the task, a motion received with "*levendig applaus en langdurige toejuichingen*" though the board of the Bond prudently declined to include it in any formal resolution. [[C. C. Vattier Kraane|Vattier Kraane]] also spoke, on economic development in connection with defence.

Van Heutsz repeated and extended his arguments at the [[Industriebond defense meeting (17 February 1917)|Industriebond (Industriële Club) meeting of 17 February 1917]], reported in the *Handelsblad* of [[18 February]] [[1917]], at which he set out the "*hoofdlijnen*" of the government's accepted defence system as drawn from the [[Memorie van Antwoord]] — 4 battleships (*linieschepen*), 5 cruisers, and 25 submarines at an estimated acquisition cost of 275 million guilders — and dismissed the army component as adequate only for the defence of [[Java]].

## The Van der Maaten counter-attack

[[Van der Maaten]], who had served under Van Heutsz in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] and broken with him after the [[Atjeh-enquête|Atjeh-enquête of 1908]], was provoked by the Bond van Vrije Liberalen speech into writing [[Van der Maaten's pamphlet on Van Heutsz and the Defense of Netherlands Indies|the pamphlet]] *Z. E. De Oud Gouverneur-Generaal J. B. van Heutsz en de Defensie van Nederlandsch-Indië*, written in [[Montreux]] and dated [[25 February]] [[1917]], published at [['s-Gravenhage]] by [[Martinus Nijhoff]]. After reading the *Handelsblad* report of the Industriebond meeting he added a *Naschrift* (Postscript) before publication. The pamphlet's most consequential revelation was that [[Van der Weyden's defense brochure|the Van der Weyden brochure]] was in fact co-authored by Van Heutsz himself — pages 389–395 were entirely in Van Heutsz's own hand — and should properly be called the "*brochure Van Heutsz-Van der Weijden*." On the substance of defence, Van der Maaten argued that as long as funds were insufficient for a fleet capable of gaining naval supremacy, all money for the naval force should go to submarines of great power and range rather than battleships, and rejected the brochure's proposition that [[Netherlands India|India]] should bear *all* defence costs as "*immorality*."

## Concepts in dispute

Two interconnected concepts structured the technical military argument:

- **[[Inlandsche Militieplicht]]** — the proposed obligation of compulsory military service for the native (*inheemsche*) population of [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]]. Van Heutsz's reversal on this question — strongly advising *against* it as Governor-General, advocating *for* it in [[1917]] to support his son-in-law's programme — was the clearest instance of the personal manoeuvring Van der Maaten exposed. Native opinion itself was divided: at a meeting of the [[Indische Krijgsvereeniging]] on [[6 March]] [[1916]], three of four native speakers were far from enthusiastic about militia service, with Heer Satiman declaring that the [[Java|Javanese]] considered becoming a soldier the worst thing that could happen "*in dit ondermaansche rijk van tranen*."
- **[[Kader-quaestie]]** — the problem of finding sufficient European officer and non-commissioned officer cadres to lead an expanded native army. The [[Indische Legerbestuur]] considered it "*een uiterst moeilijk, ja bijna onoplosbaar vraagstuk*." Van der Maaten drew an explicit comparison with the [[Atjeh-vraagstuk]] (the Aceh Question), arguing that just as the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] had been prolonged by indecision and excessive consultation, so too would the cadre question prove tractable once entrusted to "a practically experienced man with clear judgment and knowledge of Indian affairs."

The two concepts intersected with the [[Defense demonstrations of 31 August 1916|defence demonstrations of 31 August 1916]], held at various *hoofdplaatsen* across [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]], which Van Heutsz represented as involving "*duizenden en nog eens duizenden Indiërs*" and which Van der Maaten exposed as consisting largely of "*nontonners*" drawn by the spectacle (*rameh*) rather than by conviction.

## The wider frame

The debate took place against the [[First World War]], which "raged around the Dutch East Indies and exposed the vulnerability of colonial possessions defended by an inadequate fleet and army." Van der Maaten identified the "*Hollandsche Koopmansgeest*" (Dutch merchant spirit) — the habitual tendency to settle defence matters "*op een koopje*" (on the cheap) — as a root cause of chronic defence underfunding. [[C. C. Vattier Kraane|Vattier Kraane]] supplied the fiscal counterpart to the Van Heutsz–Van der Weyden military programme, advocating the exploitation of oil fields in [[Djambi]] and gold fields in [[Benkoelen]] through *Naamlooze Vennootschappen* with state participation, and concluding idealistically with a vision in which "*de blanke en de bruine broeder elkander beter begrijpen en waardeeren*" — a sentiment Van der Maaten satirised as "*aandoenlijk mooi zooveel onbaatzuchtigheid*."

## Significance

The cluster is significant in the broader history of [[Acheh|Aceh]] and its afterlife for three reasons. First, it shows how the reputations and methods forged in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] were redeployed in metropolitan politics two decades after the war's conclusion: the conqueror of [[Acheh|Aceh]] and his former subordinate carried the authority of the colonial small-war theatre into a debate about the survival of the empire they had helped to build. Second, it exposes the internal divisions among [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] veterans — the rupture produced by the [[Atjeh-enquête|1908 enquête]] did not heal, and resurfaced in 1917 as a public quarrel over defence policy. Third, it documents the moment at which the question of native military service — the [[Inlandsche Militieplicht]] that would later become a central issue of Indonesian colonial politics — first entered mainstream Dutch party-political debate through the [[Bond van Vrije Liberalen]] and the [[Vereeniging ter Beoefening van de Krijgswetenschap]].

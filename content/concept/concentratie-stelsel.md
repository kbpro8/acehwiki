**Concentratie-Stelsel** (Concentration System) was a defensive military strategy adopted by the Dutch colonial government in Aceh on [[19 August]] [[1884]], following the campaigns of General [[Karel van der Heyden]] in the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Implementation and Design

Under the decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]], Dutch forces withdrew from outer outposts and consolidated into a heavily fortified ring of sixteen interconnected forts and outposts surrounding the core valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]], anchored at [[Kuta Raja]]. The objective was to maintain a strictly defensive posture, minimize military expenditure and casualties, and encourage Acehnese populations inside the line to accept Dutch authority under the [[Binnenlandsch Bestuur in Atjeh|Binnenlandsch Bestuur]].

## Flaws and Failure

The defensive posture proved highly vulnerable. Acehnese resistance bands easily penetrated the fortified perimeter, launched frequent ambushes on military supply convoys, destroyed bridges and public infrastructure, and established insurgent bases just beyond the line. To control movement, the colonial authorities instituted the [[Passenstelsel in Atjeh|passenstelsel]] (pass system) and outlawed the possession of weapons by local inhabitants, but these measures failed to stop attacks.

The strategic collapse of the system occurred in [[1896]] during the [[Treason of Teuku Umar]]. Acehnese leader [[Teuku Umar]], who had previously submitted to the Dutch, was granted 800 modern rifles, ammunition, and funds to clear insurgent areas. In [[March]] [[1896]], Umar defected back to the Acehnese resistance with all provided arms and supplies. This betrayal forced the colonial government to abandon the Concentratie-Stelsel entirely and adopt an aggressive, mobile offensive strategy spearheaded by the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]] under Captain [[Jonkheer G. J. W. C. Graafland]] and Colonel [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]].

## Account from Verstege's *Een beroep op het Nederlandsche volk* (1886)

[[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Overste Verstege]]'s pamphlet — the fullest analytic diagnosis of the system written within its first year — treats the concentration under four heads: its incompatibility with the character of the Atjehsche people (who read in the retreat merely their invincibility confirmed); its contradiction with the [[Annexation proclamation of Atjeh (1874)|annexation of 12 February 1874]], by which the Dutch had declared themselves sovereign over the whole realm and borne off its land and self-rule; its rejection on all military grounds proved by the experience of the Dutch positions at the end of April [[1874]] and April [[1875]] (see [[Verstege's folding map of Groot-Atjeh (1886)]]); and its rise as the necessary fruit of the statecraft applied since the [[Normale bestuursinrichting (Atjeh, 1881-1884)|Kabinetsbrief of 11 March 1881]] — the chain: the giving up of patrols and posts; the use of the [[Gewapende Politie|gewapende politie]] against "maraudeurs"; the rekindled guerilla; the unanswered alarms of [[1882]]; then the [[Concentration decision (16-17 June 1884)|secret decision of 16-17 June 1884]] in the [[1884]] Kamers; [[O. van Rees|Van Rees]]'s impossible promise of [[19 August]] [[1884]]; the occupation of [[Kota Pohama]] and [[Roempit]] on 25 and 29 September [[1884]]; the evacuations of January and February [[1885]]; the retreat executed *before* the protection-measures of the [[1000-meter strook|1000-metre strip]] were completed, three months before the announced date, at the cost of 11 dead and 40 wounded during the clearing alone; and the proclamation spoken over it in the Koloniaal Verslag: "hiermede was de concentratie voltooid" ("with this the concentration was completed") — completed in a ring of 6,700 hectares with eighteen semi-permanent posts, garrisoned by some 4,500 men, attacked from the first week inside (see [[Guerilla raids inside the concentration line (1885)]]), and laughed at by the people across it in the [[Proclamation of Toekoe di Tiroe (2 February 1885)|proclamation of Toekoe di Tiroe of 2 February 1885]]: the sign of victory revealed by the retreat of the unbelievers.

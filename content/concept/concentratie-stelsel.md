**Concentratie-Stelsel** (Concentration System) was a defensive military strategy adopted by the Dutch colonial government in Aceh on [[19 August]] [[1884]], following the campaigns of General [[Karel van der Heyden]] in the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Implementation and Design

Under the decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]], Dutch forces withdrew from outer outposts and consolidated into a heavily fortified ring of sixteen interconnected forts and outposts surrounding the core valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]], anchored at [[Kuta Raja]]. The objective was to maintain a strictly defensive posture, minimize military expenditure and casualties, and encourage Acehnese populations inside the line to accept Dutch authority under the [[Binnenlandsch Bestuur in Atjeh|Binnenlandsch Bestuur]].

## Flaws and Failure

The defensive posture proved highly vulnerable. Acehnese resistance bands easily penetrated the fortified perimeter, launched frequent ambushes on military supply convoys, destroyed bridges and public infrastructure, and established insurgent bases just beyond the line. To control movement, the colonial authorities instituted the [[Passenstelsel in Atjeh|passenstelsel]] (pass system) and outlawed the possession of weapons by local inhabitants, but these measures failed to stop attacks.

The strategic collapse of the system occurred in [[1896]] during the [[Treason of Teuku Umar]]. Acehnese leader [[Teuku Umar]], who had previously submitted to the Dutch, was granted 800 modern rifles, ammunition, and funds to clear insurgent areas. In [[March]] [[1896]], Umar defected back to the Acehnese resistance with all provided arms and supplies. This betrayal forced the colonial government to abandon the Concentratie-Stelsel entirely and adopt an aggressive, mobile offensive strategy spearheaded by the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]] under Captain [[Jonkheer G. J. W. C. Graafland]] and Colonel [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]].

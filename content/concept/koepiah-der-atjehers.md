**The koepiah der Atjehers** — the *koepiah* (skullcap) of the Atjehers — is, in F. A. Wagner's *[[Sierkunst in Indonesië]]* ([[1949]]), singled out as a "stijlvolle" (stylish) example of Indonesian plaited headwear. Wagner mentions the Atjeh *koepiah* in the chapter "Vlechtwerk" (Plaiting), in the course of his discussion of the applications of plaiting to clothing — and especially to head-coverings. The Atjeh *koepiah* is paired, in Wagner's text, with the lontar-leaf head-coverings of Roti, as the two examples that mark the range of plaited headwear across the archipelago.

## The passage in Wagner

In the section on the applications of plaiting, Wagner writes: "Van de kleding zijn het vooral de hoofdbedekkingen, die van vlechtwerk zijn vervaardigd. Opmerkelijk zijn de ronde hoeden der Daya's — de grote tanggoe —, die een gelukkige combinatie vertonen van bladwerk en fijn vlechtwerk. Elk volk heeft zijn eigen vormen en versieringen gevonden en tussen de stijlvolle koepiah der Atjehers en de van lontarblad gevlochten hoofddeksels van Roti, versierd met talrijke aanhangsels, ligt een veelheid van modellen, die een nadere beschouwing overwaard maakt."

The Atjeh *koepiah* and the Roti head-covering are thus set at the two ends of a range — the *stijlvolle* (stylish, restrained) Atjeh *koepiah* at one end, the lontar-leaf head-coverings of Roti "versierd met talrijke aanhangsels" (decorated with numerous appendages) at the other. Between them lies "een veelheid van modellen" — a multitude of models — that Wagner does not enumerate but that, he says, would reward closer examination.

## The koepiah and the plaiting tradition

The Atjeh *koepiah* is, in Wagner's account, a product of the plaiting tradition — the same tradition that produces the [[trawang-vlechtwerk in Atjeh|Atjeh *trawang* mats]] and the [[Atjeh rotan shields]]. The plaiting chapter groups the Atjehers with the Minangkabauers and the Bataks as the principal Sumatran producers of fine plaiting: "Vooral op Sumatra — de Minangkabauers, de Bataks en de Atjehers leveren zeer mooi werk — en in de Riouwarchipel komt deze techniek voor." The Atjeh *koepiah* is thus a Sumatran plaited headwear, distinguished within the Sumatran group by its *stijlvolle* form — a form that Wagner contrasts with the more elaborately decorated headwear of Roti.

The word *koepiah* itself (also *kupiah*, *kopiah*) is the standard Indonesian term for a man's skullcap, and the cap is, throughout the archipelago, the headwear of Muslim men. In Atjeh, the most strongly Islamic region of Sumatra, the *koepiah* would have been, by the period Wagner describes, an ordinary piece of male dress; what Wagner singles out is not the wearing of the *koepiah* (which is common) but the *stijlvolle* Atjeh form of it — the plaited Atjeh *koepiah* as a piece of decorative art, not just a piece of clothing.

## The koepiah and the Atjeh decorative signature

The Atjeh *koepiah*'s *stijlvolle* form — its stylishness, its restraint — is, in Wagner's reading, the same decorative signature that he identifies in the *[[anker-motief]]*'s rounded geometry and in the *[[plang]]*'s single-motif austerity. The Atjeh craftsman, in plaiting as in weaving and metalwork, works with restraint: the *koepiah* is "stijlvol," the *anker-motief* is rounded and flowing rather than angular, the *plang* is the only motif used in Atjeh *ikat*. The Atjeh *koepiah* is thus, in Wagner's catalogue, the headwear counterpart of the *idja*'s restraint and the *anker-motief*'s curve — a single, stylish form, set against the more elaborately decorated headwear of other regions.

## Significance

The *koepiah der Atjehers* is, in Wagner's account, the principal example of Atjeh plaited headwear — a *stijlvolle* skullcap that stands at one end of the range of Indonesian plaited head-coverings. Its significance in the larger history of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]nese decorative art is that it shows the same restrained taste in plaiting that Wagner reads in the *[[anker-motief]]* in weaving and in the *[[plang]]* in *ikat*: the Atjeh craftsman, in headwear as in textile and metalwork, works in a single, stylish form, against the more elaborate decorative dispositions of other regions. The pairing of the Atjeh *koepiah* with the Roti head-covering — restraint against appendage — is, in turn, Wagner's way of marking the Atjeh form as the restrained pole of the archipelago's plaited headwear tradition.

**Perlak** was one of the earliest Muslim principalities (Dutch: *Moslimsch rijkje*) on the north-east coast of [[Sumatra]], established by approximately [[1500]]. It is cited in the *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* ([[1926]]) as part of the evidence for the early penetration of Islam into the Indonesian Archipelago.

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — Perlak

The atlas, discussing the early spread of Islam, states: "op de N.O. kust van Sumatra (de Moslimsche rijkjes Perlak en Pasé in omstreeks 1500)" (on the N.E. coast of Sumatra [the Muslim principalities Perlak and Pasé around 1500]). Perlak is thus identified as one of the two earliest-known Muslim states in Sumatra, predating the conversion of most other regions in the Archipelago.

## Historical Significance

Perlak's importance lies in its position as one of the first footholds of Islam in Southeast Asia. Along with [[Pasé]], it represents the initial phase of the Islamisation of the Indonesian world, which would eventually transform the religious landscape of the entire Archipelago. The atlas dates these principalities to approximately [[1500]], based on evidence from travel accounts and Mohammedan tombstones with inscriptions from the 15th century. The north-east coast of Sumatra, where both Perlak and Pasé were located, was the natural point of arrival for Islamic traders and missionaries travelling from India and the Arabian Peninsula, and the location of these early sultanates explains why [[Acheh]], immediately to the north, became one of the great Islamic centres of the Archipelago.

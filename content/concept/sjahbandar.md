**Sjahbandar** (from Persian *shah-bandar*, literally "king of the harbour" = harbour-master) was an official appointed by the Sultan of [[Aceh|Atjèh]] in the interest of trade affairs, who also acted as arbitrator in commercial disputes. The *sjahbandar* was found at all important coastal trade places, often of Klingalese origin.

## Functions

The *sjahbandar*'s functions were:
1. **Trade affairs** — managing commercial matters in the Sultan's interest
2. **Arbitrator** — acting as *scheidsrechter* (arbitrator) in disputes between merchants
3. **Permission to trade** — granting the licence to trade, done "by lifting a golden-hafted kris over the head of the merchant, who arrives, and without which he dares not to land his goods"
4. **Provisioning** — providing the visiting gezagvoerder (commander) with vleesch, olie, en rijst (meat, oil, and rice) when he stayed ashore

## Historical description

Alexander Hamilton, who visited Atjèh in [[1702]], described the *sjahbandar* in his "New account of the East Indies" (Edinburgh [[1727]]): "When I came to town, I went to pay my visit to the Shawbundaar, who is custommaster and common arbitrator of differences arising among merchants."

## Klingalese origin

The *sjahbandar*'s were often of Klingalese (Chulia, from Coromandel coast) origin. Formerly, Sultans and chiefs liked to use converts (*mu'allaf*, from Arabic *mu'allaf*) as *sjahbandar*'s when collecting or transferring money, since such converts enjoyed exceptional personal and property security in Atjèh.

## Modern survivals

Even in Kreemer's time, a *sjahbandar* as market-head (*markthoofd*) was still found at various coastal places. The *sjahbandar* also appeared in the administrative structure of some Westkust landschappen — for example, [[Soesöh|Soesöh]] had a *sjahbandar*, as did [[Meuké'|Meuké']] and [[Kloeët|Kloeët]] (which had three *sjahbandar*'s).

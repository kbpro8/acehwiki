**The Brussels Conference of [[1874]]** was the international conference on the laws of land warfare whose articles [[H. van Kol]] cited in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[17 November]] [[1897]] as the standard by which the Dutch conduct of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] must be judged and found wanting.

## The article quoted

Van Kol read the following into the record: *"il est interdit de mutiler ou de tuer un ennemi qui est hors de combat, et de déclarer d'avance qu'on ne fait pas de quartier, même si l'on ne le réclame pas pour soi-même." — it is forbidden to mutilate or to kill an enemy who is out of the fight, and to declare in advance that no quarter will be given, even if one does not claim it for oneself.* The pamphlet supplies the Dutch rendering in a footnote.

He introduced the citation with the words: "Enkele artikelen der Conferentie van Brussel van [[1874]] geven daartoe reeds gereede aanleiding" — some articles of the Conference of Brussels of [[1874]] already give ready occasion for it — the "it" being the pretext that any great power might find for intervening against the Netherlands, as North America was then finding against [[Spain|Spanje]] in Cuba.

## Bearing on Atjeh

The two prohibitions bore directly on the specific charges Van Kol made. The prohibition on killing an enemy *hors de combat* bore on the killing of prisoners and on the alleged shooting of [[Njahim]] and of the aged [[Joed Mohmed Lamtengah]]; the prohibition on declaring in advance that no quarter would be given bore on the pencil note "er worden geen krijgsgevangenen gemaakt" that Van Kol attributed to [[Karel van der Heijden|Van der Heyden]] on the [[Krijgsorder for the VI, XXII and XXVI Moekims|krijgsorder for the VI, XXII and XXVI Moekims]]; and the prohibition on mutilation bore on the practice discussed in the [[Tweede Kamer]] on [[22 September]] [[1896]] in the exchange between [[Goeman Borgesius]] and the Minister [[Mr. J. H. Bergsma|Bergsma]].

The [[1874]] date is also that of the *[[Comité-generaal van 1874|comité-generaal]]* of the Dutch chamber on the origins of the Atjeh war, cited by [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] in the same debate — a coincidence of dates that gave Van Kol's argument its edge: in the very year in which the civilised states were codifying the humane conduct of war, the Netherlands was secretly debating the justification of a war it was already conducting by the means the code forbade.

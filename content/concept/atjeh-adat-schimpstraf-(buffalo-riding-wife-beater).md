The **Atjeh adat schimpstraf** (the Atjehnese customary punishment of public mockery) was the punishment prescribed in old [[Acheh|Atjeh]] for a man who had beaten his wife — the malefactor was paraded through the village seated on a female buffalo, dressed in a head-cloth of dried banana-leaves and hung with wooden weapons and a chain of shells, to the public mockery of the village; alternatively, the delinquent was required to crawl on all fours for several days in public, wearing a ring in his nose and eating grass, "as if he were a buffalo." The punishment is recorded in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] (p. 104) and is documented in the principal sources [[K. F. H. van Langen|Van Langen]]'s [[Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust ([[1888]])|*Atjeh's Westkust*]] (p. 481), [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (vol. I, p. 120), and [[G. A. J. Wilken|Wilken]]'s *Verspreide Geschriften* (vol. II, p. 488).

## The punishment
The schimpstraf passage in *De Karbouw* (p. 104) reads:

> *"In het oude Atjeh gold als adat-schimpstraf voor een man, die een vrouw mishandeld had, dat hij, met een hoofddoek van gedroogde pisangbladeren op het hoofd en omhangen met houten wapentjes en een keten van schelpen, op een wijfjes-buffel gezeten, werd rondgeleid, tot spot van het publiek. Ook gebeurde het wel, dat de delinkwent enige dagen in het openbaar op handen en voeten moest rondkruipen met een ring in de neus en gras eten, als ware hij een buffel."*
The two forms of the punishment are:
1. **Parading on a female buffalo**: the malefactor, with a head-cloth of dried banana-leaves on his head, hung about with wooden weapons and a chain of shells, was seated on a female buffalo and paraded around to the mockery of the public.
2. **Crawling as a buffalo**: the delinquent was required, for some days, to crawl on hands and feet in public, wearing a ring in his nose and eating grass, "as if he were a buffalo."
The symbolic logic of both forms is the same: the malefactor is symbolically converted into the buffalo — the very animal over which, in beating his wife, he had abused his domestic authority. The banana-leaf head-cloth, the wooden weapons, the shell-chain, the nose-ring and the grass-eating are all elements of a comic-bestial costume that reduces the wife-beater to the status of the buffalo he had implicitly claimed to master. The use of a *female* buffalo for the parading form adds a final inversion: the man who had beaten a woman is paraded on a female animal, in a doubly humiliating reversal of gender and species roles.

## Sources
The punishment is documented in three principal sources, all cited by [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] in *De Karbouw*:

- [[K. F. H. van Langen|Van Langen]]'s [[Van Langen's Atjeh's Westkust ([[1888]])|*Atjeh's Westkust*]] ([[1888]]), p. 481 — the standard Dutch geographical account of the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]], and the earliest of the three sources.
- [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] ([[1893]]-94), vol. I, p. 120 — the foundational ethnography of the Atjehnese.
- [[G. A. J. Wilken|Wilken]]'s *Verspreide Geschriften* (collected writings), vol. II, p. 488 — the comparative ethnographic source.
The concurrence of these three authorities, across the late-nineteenth-century Dutch ethnographic literature, fixes the punishment as a documented institution of old Atjeh.

## Significance
The Atjeh adat schimpstraf is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as a documented instance of the symbolic integration of the buffalo into the Atjehnese legal-punitive complex — the buffalo functioning not only as a working animal, sacrificial victim and courtly spectacle (as documented elsewhere in *De Karbouw*), but also as a symbolic vehicle of public humiliation. The punishment's symbolic logic — the conversion of the malefactor into the buffalo he had abused his authority over — documents the wider Atjehnese pattern, visible also in the [[Atjehse Id al-Adha cow-not-buffalo choice|cow-not-buffalo choice at Id al-Adha]] and in the [[Pantang of the albino buffalo in Atjeh|albino-buffalo prohibition]], in which the buffalo is treated as a morally and ritually charged animal whose symbolic deployment inverts and reorders human social relations.

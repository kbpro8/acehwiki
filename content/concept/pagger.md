**The pagger** — the fence — was the defensive fence around the *[[bivak]]* (field camp) of a [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] patrouille, built each evening to break the *eerste aanloop* (first rush) of a possible overval (surprise attack). In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *pagger* is the book's set-piece of the *bivak*'s defensive work: a fence of *tiangs* (poles) at 1½ metre intervals with five *blandars* (horizontal beams) bound along, built with the *[[klewang]]* as the only tool.

## Construction

"Een pagger had stevige palen, *tiangs*, op anderhalve meter en daarlangs waren vijf *blandars* opgebonden." The *pagger* had sturdy poles — *tiangs* — at 1½ metre intervals, with five *blandars* (horizontal beams) bound along. The construction was therefore both sturdy (the *tiangs* at close intervals) and flexible (the five *blandars* bound along, not nailed).

## The discipline of the pagger

"Het maken ervan [werd] altijd voorgeschreven, omdat men anders wel eens slappe broeders onder de brigadecommandanten kon krijgen" — the construction of the *pagger* was always prescribed, because otherwise one might get weak brothers among the brigadecommandanten. The *pagger* was therefore both a defensive work and a discipline: a brigadecommandant who failed to build it was a *slappe broeder* (weak brother). The prescription was the colonial army's way of ensuring that the *pagger* was built even when the brigadecommandant judged the risk of overval to be low.

## The pagger and the defence

The *pagger* was built to break the *eerste aanloop* (first rush) of a possible overval. The fence would slow an attacker long enough for the patrouille to take up its weapons. The *pagger* was therefore the *bivak*'s first line of defence: the work that gave the patrouille the time to wake, to take up the *[[karabijn (M. 95)|karabijn]]* and the *[[klewang]]*, and to meet the attack.

## The klewang as the only tool

"Het gereedschap was de *[[klewang]]* en verder niet" — the tool was the klewang and nothing else. The *pagger* — the *tiangs*, the *blandars*, the bindings — was built with the *klewang* as the only tool. The *pagger* was therefore both a defensive work and a daily act of construction: the patrouille that built its own *pagger* each evening with the *klewang* was the patrouille that could operate indefinitely in the *[[rimboe]]*.

## Significance

The *pagger* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the defensive fence around the *[[bivak]]*. The book's argument is that the *pagger* was both a defensive work and a discipline: a brigadecommandant who failed to build it was a *slappe broeder*. The *pagger* is therefore the book's marker of the *bivak*'s defensive work — the work that gave the patrouille the time to meet an overval — and of the colonne method's reliance on the *[[klewang]]* as the only tool.

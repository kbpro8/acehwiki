The **divisie** was the largest permanent formation of the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* in [[Atjeh]], commanded by a captain who in many districts also exercised civil authority as *civiel-gezaghebber*.

## Establishment

At its foundation in [[1890]] only one *divisie* was designated, consisting of a captain-commandant, one first lieutenant, a European sergeant-major, a European *fourier*, twelve European sergeants, twelve non-European sergeants, twelve non-European corporals and 180 non-European *maréchaussées*.

By the *Gouvernementsbesluit* of [[3 March]] [[1899]] the corps was brought to 20 officers and 1,212 men in five divisions of three *afdeelingen*, each subdivided into four *[[Brigade (Maréchaussée)|brigades]]*. Each division stood under the orders of a captain-division commander; the corps as a whole, so far as military administration went, was placed under a field officer, for which the *plaatselijk commandant* at [[Koeta Radja]] was soon designated.

## Stations

In [[1898]] the divisions were localised as follows: Staff at [[Koeta Radja]]; 1st Division at [[Seulimeum|Selimoen]]; 2nd Division at [[Indrapoeri]]; 3rd Division at [[Tjot Mantjang]] and [[Glé Broet]]; 4th Division at [[Tjot Goet]]; 5th Division at [[Lam Baroe]] and [[Lho Nga|Lho'-Nga]].

In [[1910]] they were: 1st Division at [[Blang Kedjeren]]; 2nd Division at [[Meulaboh]]; 3rd Division at [[Takengeun|Takèngan]]; 4th Division at [[Lam Meulo]]; 5th Division at [[Lho Soekon]] — where the fifth was still quartered at the time of the [[1930]] jubilee. Towards the end of [[1927]] a new division was formed with [[Bakongan]] as its station.

## Civil authority

Officers attached to the corps were carried *à la suite* of their own arm, and the commanders of divisions and *afdeelingen*, where circumstances required it, were charged with the administration as *civiel-gezaghebber* (civil authority). On the [[Tocht naar de Gajo-Loeös (1904)|expedition to the Gajo-Loeös]] of [[1904]] the civil administration was expressly entrusted to the division commanders.

## The Toeangkoe

In the corps's internal vocabulary, recorded by [[W. J. Mosselman]], the division commander was styled *Toeangkoe*, having under him three *Oeleuhbalangs* (officers). Mosselman also recalled the division commander in his indulgent aspect: "dan vierde onze Ouwe, onze Divisiecommandant den teugel" — then our Old Man, our division commander, gave us the rein — on the occasions when the men were free and spent their few saved *centen*.

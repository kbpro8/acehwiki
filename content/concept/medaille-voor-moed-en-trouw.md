**The Medaille voor Moed en Trouw** — the Medal for Courage and Loyalty — was a Dutch colonial military decoration, worn with pride in the *[[negorij]]* or *[[kampong]]* of the Ambonese and Menadonese soldiers. The decoration was, in the colonial army's decoration economy, the standard recognition of indigenous courage before the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] was extended to indigenous soldiers in [[1865]].

## The decoration

The Medaille voor Moed en Trouw was the Medal for Courage and Loyalty — the standard recognition of courage in the field for indigenous soldiers before the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] was extended to them. The book notes that "[[Joseph Rakarias|de Ambonese le luitenant Joseph Rakarias]]" was awarded the Medaille voor Moed en Trouw in [[1850]] — and that it was *vervangen* (replaced) by the M.W.O. 4e klas in [[1865]], when the M.W.O. was extended to indigenous soldiers. The replacement was a recognition that the earlier medal had been inadequate to the act it recognised.

## The decoration economy

The Medaille voor Moed en Trouw was, in the colonial army's decoration economy, the recognition that came before the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] and alongside the [[Eervolle Vermelding]]. The book's example is [[Salawono]], who at the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] korps had "een *[[Eervolle Vermelding]]* en de *Moed en Trouw* verdiend" — an Honorable Mention and the Medal for Courage and Loyalty — before earning his M.W.O. 4e klas at the [[Storming of Limboer (22 June 1903)|storming of Limboer]].

## The trots of the negorij

"Ridders [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]], dragers van de Medaille voor Moed en Trouw en van het [[Kroontje (Eervolle Vermelding)|Kroontje]] (de Eervolle Vermelding), waren de trots van hun *[[kampong]]* of *[[negorij]]*." The Medaille voor Moed en Trouw — like the M.W.O. and the Eervolle Vermelding — was the pride of the soldier's home community.

## Significance

The Medaille voor Moed en Trouw is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the standard recognition of indigenous courage before the [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]] was extended to indigenous soldiers. The book's argument is that the replacement of the Medaille voor Moed en Trouw by the M.W.O. 4e klas in [[1865]] was the colonial army's recognition that the indigenous soldaat's courage deserved the same decoration as the European soldaat's. The Medaille voor Moed en Trouw is therefore the book's marker of the colonial army's decoration economy before the M.W.O. was opened to indigenous soldiers.

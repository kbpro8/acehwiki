**Tayl** (also *tael*, *tahil*, *tail*) was the weight-and-currency unit of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the wider Malay-Indonesian world in the early modern period. In [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury's embassy of 1641]] the *tayl* is the unit in which the disputed jewel-sale to the late Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]] was valued: the Atjeh treasury "liet een uitgave van 15.000 tayl, dus 5000 gulden, niet toe" — would not authorise an outlay of 15,000 *tayl*, that is, 5,000 guilders.

## Etymology and value

The word *tahil* is from the Chinese *táel* (兩 / 两), a weight unit of approximately 37.5 grams of silver. In the Malay-Indonesian world the *tahil* became both a weight unit (for precious metals) and a currency unit (a specified weight of silver). Godee Molsbergen's conversion of [[1641]] — 15,000 *tayl* = 5,000 guilders — implies a value of roughly 3 *tayl* to the guilder, or roughly 1.25 guilders' worth of silver per *tayl*. This is consistent with the *tahil* of the period being approximately one ounce of silver.

## The jewel-sale of 1641

The principal Atjeh-related occurrence of the *tayl* in *Tijdens de O.I. Compagnie* is in the account of the disputed jewel-sale that closed [[Pieter Soury|Soury]]'s [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|1641 embassy]]. The late Sultan [[Iskander Tsani]] had placed an outstanding order for jewels; the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] who succeeded him was not liable for the order placed by her late husband; the Atjeh treasury "liet een uitgave van 15.000 tayl, dus 5000 gulden, niet toe" — would not authorise an outlay of 15,000 *tayl*, that is 5,000 guilders. After long lobbying of the *[[Orangkaja|Orangkaja's]]* by *Secretaris* [[Jan van Riebeeck|Jan van Riebeeck]] — many a gift delivered at their houses — a price was agreed for the greater part of the parcel, but for the future the import of jewels by the Company was forbidden in Atjeh.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *tayl* is the unit in which the sultanate's principal commercial transactions with the European companies were denominated. The [[1641]] conversion to guilders — at roughly 3 *tayl* to the guilder — provides a rare fixed exchange-rate between the Atjeh and Dutch monetary systems in the early seventeenth century. The figure also gives a sense of scale: the late Sultan's jewel-order, valued at 15,000 *tayl* (= 5,000 guilders), was a sum that the Atjeh treasury under the new Sultana refused to authorise, but it was not a sum so large as to be unimaginable. The sultanate's treasury could in principle have paid it; the political decision was not to.

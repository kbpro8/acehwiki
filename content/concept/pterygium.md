**Pterygium** — the wing-shaped fold of conjunctival tissue encroaching on the cornea — was the fifth subject of Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]]'s [[Oriënteerend oogheelkundig onderzoek bij de Atjehsche bevolking van Groot-Atjeh gedurende 1937|eye survey]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in [[1937]], and was found in rather more than ten per cent of the men and about nine per cent of the women examined.

## Findings

Gomperts drew five conclusions from his table of pterygium cases:

1. that in both sexes the localisation of the pterygium was most often *nasaal* (on the nasal side), one-sided and two-sided alike, which agreed with the findings of other investigators;
2. that pterygium was found in about nine per cent of the women examined and rather more than ten per cent of the men;
3. that pterygium also occurred among children of both sexes, but rarely — the cases among children and young adults being in a very early stage, the head of the pterygium having crossed the *limbus corneae* by half a millimetre to at most one millimetre;
4. that the nasal-temporal combination was more often seen than the temporal localisation alone, in one-sided as in two-sided cases, the difference being clearest in the two-sided, which argued somewhat for the general opinion that in nasal-temporal localisation the nasal pterygium develops first;
5. that in the coastal area pterygium occurred less frequently than in the valley area.

The tabulated totals give 651 nasal cases (344 men, 307 women), 294 nasal-and-temporal, and 348 in the other combinations, among the 18,808 persons examined.

## Age distribution

Gomperts's closing summary records that pterygium occurred in the surveyed area of Groot-Atjeh "in vrij groote frequentie bij beide geslachten en hoofdzakelijk op den middelbaren- en ouden leeftijd" — in fairly great frequency in both sexes and chiefly in middle and old age.

## Treatment

Pterygium was one of the two conditions Gomperts named — with *cataract* — for which patients received an admission note (*opnamebriefje*) for nursing in the [[Militair Hospitaal te Koetaradja]], operative treatment not being undertaken in the villages.

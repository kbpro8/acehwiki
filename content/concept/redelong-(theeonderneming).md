**Redelong** was a Dutch *theeonderneming* (tea plantation) north-east of [[Takengon|Takengon]] in the Alas valley of central [[Atjeh|Atjeh]], which served — first as the *stafkwartier* of the Dutch troops at Takengon, and after the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|capitulation of Takengon]] as the base of Lt [[H. van Zanten|lieutenant H. van Zanten]]'s post-capitulation Dutch resistance. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), Redelong is the principal Dutch civilian *onderneming* of the Atjeh campaign, and the staff history's principal narrative instance of the *theeonderneming* as a wartime resistance base.

## The stafkwartier phase (1941 – March 1942)

In the pre-invasion period Redelong served as the *stafkwartier* of the Dutch troops at Takengon. The choice of a *theeonderneming* as a military *stafkwartier* reflects the broader Dutch colonial practice of using civilian plantations as military-administrative centres in the inland *hoogvlakte* — a practice the staff history treats as the structural context for the close cooperation between the Dutch military and the civilian *administrateurs* of the Atjeh *ondernemingen*.

## The Van Zanten resistance base (March – November 1942)

When, on [[19 March]] [[19 March 1942|19 March 1942]], [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]] ordered the capitulation of the Takengon garrison, the recently vacated Redelong *theeonderneming* still contained a handful of officers, Lt [[H. van Zanten|Van Zanten]] among them. Although his older colleagues "probeererden hem hiervan terug te houden," Van Zanten "zette evenwel door" and announced he would hold out "zo lang mogelijk." He established a *bosbivak* "enige tientallen kilometers ten noordoosten van Takengon," with Redelong as his intelligence and supply base.

The *administrateur* of Redelong, **[[S. A. Liezenberg|S. A. Liezenberg]]**, provided "veel hulp" in the form of voedsel and medicijnen. He also acted as a relay for the correspondence between Van Zanten and the *geneesheer* of the Takengon hospital — correspondence that was carried by the Ambonese sergeant **Kalatu**, who moved between Redelong and Takengon, and was relayed in turn by "de vrouw van een Menadonees militair, die bij één der Nederlanders werkzaam was" who worked at the Dutch hospital.

The chain — Van Zanten at Redelong via Liezenberg via Kalatu via the Menadonese *vrouw* to the *geneesheer* at Takengon — was eventually detected by the Japanese, and "op den duur kon deze organisatie voor de Japanners niet geheim blijven." On [[24 November]] [[24 November 1942|24 November 1942]] the Japanese arrested fourteen released militairen working on the Redelong onderneming, "vastgebonden, waarna zij aan de bekende Japanse mishandelingen werden onderworpen." On [[26 November]] [[26 November 1942|26 November 1942]] a Japanese force of about 100 men attempted to take Van Zanten's bivak but was foiled by an informer; the bivak was burned. From this point the supply of food and medicines from Redelong was cut off.

## Significance

Redelong is, in the staff history's Atjeh chapter, the principal Dutch civilian *onderneming* of the Atjeh campaign, and the staff history's principal narrative instance of the *theeonderneming* as a wartime resistance base. The staff history's emphasis on Liezenberg's *hulp* — and on the exposure of the Liezenberg–Kalatu–Menadonese *vrouw* chain — frames Redelong as the staff history's principal illustration of the impossibility of sustaining a Dutch clandestine network in Atjeh under Japanese occupation. The *theeonderneming* was at once the staff history's principal instance of Dutch civilian-military cooperation and the staff history's principal instance of the structural exposure of such cooperation to Japanese detection.

**Atra peunoelang** — also *harta peunoelang* — was the Atjehnese institution by which a father, or other *[[Wali|wali]]*, gave property to his married daughter or ward on the occasion of the birth of her first child. [[D. W. N. de Boer]] identified it with the Batak *[[Indahan arian|indahan arian]]* and described it as a form of possession which the chairman of a *[[Moesapat|moesapat]]* or *[[Landschapsgerecht|landschapsgerecht]]* not infrequently encountered.

## Occasion

The gift was made at the moment when the couple's time had come to establish themselves independently — *[[Peungkleh|peungkleh]]* (Batak *mandjaë*) — and when the young pair also received their own dwelling.

## Composition

The *atra peunoelang* usually consisted of a rice field and a head of plough cattle, and sometimes other things besides. It counted as *[[Adat|adat]]* that the value of the goods given with the couple by the father of the bride should be twice as great as the *[[Djinamee (Atjehnese marriage gift)|djinaméë]]* (Batak *sinamot*, the bride-price) paid by the father of the bridegroom at the conclusion of the marriage.

## Purpose

The purpose, De Boer explained, was that if the husband should die first there would remain enough for the wife to procure herself the maintenance required by her rank and station — Mohammedan inheritance law allotting the widow only a small share of her husband's estate. What she managed to save from the yield of the *harta peunoelang* she might regard as her own property. On the earlier death of the wife, the husband also inherited from this *harta*.

## Relation to the estate of the giver

The *atra peunoelang* was not deducted from the estate of the wife's father, either on the division of the estate during his lifetime — *[[Atra boeloeng|atra boeloeng]]* — or on the division of the estate after death.

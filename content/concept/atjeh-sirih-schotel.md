The **Atjeh sirih-schotel** — the Atjeh betel-tray — was a ceremonial tray from [[Acheh|Atjeh]] used for the serving of *sirih* (betel), recorded as catalogue item 172 in the *Indonesische Kunstnijverheid*. The sirih-tray was an item of personal and ceremonial use across the Malay-Indonesian world; the Atjeh version is documented in the colonial craft-survey alongside trays from other parts of Sumatra.

## The catalogue entry

The *Indonesische Kunstnijverheid* catalogue records the Atjeh sirih-tray as:

- **Item 172**: "Sirih-schotel en etensblad. Atjeh, Sumatra." — Betel-tray and eating-tray. Atjeh, Sumatra.
- English: "Plate for sirih and Tray. Acheh, Sumatra."
- French: "Plat pour sirih et plateau. Atcheh. Sumatra."
- German: "Sirih-Schössel und Platte. Atjeh, Sumatra."

The entry groups together a sirih-tray (the *sirih-schotel* proper) and an eating-tray (*etensblad*), both from Atjeh. The neighbouring entry (173) records a *sirih-schaal* (sirih-dish) from Midden-Sumatra (Central Sumatra), indicating that the catalogue grouped Atjeh and Central Sumatran sirih-receptacles together for comparison.

## The *sirih* ceremony

The chewing of *sirih* (betel) — a preparation of betel leaf, areca nut, slaked lime, and other ingredients — was (and remains) a fundamental ceremonial and social practice across the Malay-Indonesian world. The offering of *sirih* to a guest was the standard act of hospitality; the receiving of *sirih* from a host's own receptacle was a mark of supreme favour. The *sirih-schotel* — the tray on which the *sirih* preparation was offered — was thus both a personal object and a ceremonial one.

The ceremonial significance of *sirih* at the Atjeh court is well documented. In the [[Atjehsche ambassade ceremony (1 July 1641)|audience of 1 July 1641]] the [[Sultana of Atjeh (1641)|Sultana]] offered *Commissaris* [[Pieter Soury|Soury]] her own golden *beteldoos* (betel-box) to prepare a *sirih*-quid, while the other gentlemen used a silver box. The Dutchmen sat shoeless in Atjeh dress chewing *sirih* "tot eer en voordeel van de Edele Compagnie" — to the honour and profit of the Honourable Company. The *sirih-schotel* of catalogue item 172 is the material object of the same ceremonial practice — the tray on which the *sirih* was offered.

## Atjeh craft context

The Atjeh *sirih-schotel* is one of several Atjeh craft items documented in the *Indonesische Kunstnijverheid* alongside the Atjeh [[Atjeh weefgetouw|loom]] (item 62), the Atjeh [[Atjeh silk ikat weaving|silk ikat tissues]] (items 65–66), and the Atjeh [[Atjeh goldsmithing (soeasa technique)|goldsmithing]] traditions. Together these items document a diversified craft tradition at Atjeh — woodwork, weaving, and metalwork — that is consistent with the sultanate's standing as a major commercial and political centre of northern Sumatra.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the *sirih-schotel* is the material object of the sultanate's ceremonial hospitality — the tray on which *sirih* was offered to guests at the court and in the houses of its nobles. The catalogue entry documents the existence of a distinctively Atjeh version of the *sirih-schotel* at the moment of European colonial cataloguing, and connects the material craft tradition of the sultanate to the ceremonial practice of *sirih*-offering that was a central act of Atjeh court ritual, as the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|Soury embassy record of 1641]] richly attests.

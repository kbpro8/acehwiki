**The Pangkai** was the advance-capital supplied by the *[[Oeléebalang|oeléebalang]]* (or another capitalist) to the pepper-planters of an Atjehnese *landschap*, by which the planters lived in the two or three years before their pepper-garden came into bearing. The institution is the principal credit-institution of the Atjehnese pepper-trade, and the principal means by which the *oeléebalang* bound the pepper-planters to himself. The *pangkaigever* — the *pangkai*-giver — was usually the *oeléebalang* or an *[[Oeléebalang tjoet|oeléebalang tjoet]]*; he received, in return, the *wasé pangkai*, a due on each *picol* of pepper produced. In Broersma's account, the *pangkaigever* receives f 2.50 per *picol*, of which he gives the *[[Peutoeha|peutoeha]]* f 0.50; the *hacil* or *wasé oeléebalang* is separate, at one guilder per *picol*. The *pangkai* was formerly about f 25 for each arriving pepper-planter; nowadays it is often f 100.

## The 1915 Regulation
In [[1915]] the colonial government came with a new regulation, by which the customs-office no longer had any concern. The *hacil tanah* or *wasé oeléebalang* was to be divided fifty-fifty between the *landschap* and the *pangkaigever* and the *peutoeha* — the latter in the course of the years having grown from the leader of the pepper-planters into the *kepala kampong*, the village-head. That regulation too has not encouraged the *pangkaigever*.

## Significance
The *pangkai* is the principal credit-institution of the Atjehnese pepper-trade, and the principal means by which the *oeléebalang* bound the pepper-planters to himself. The institution is the economic basis of the *oeléebalang*'s power in the pepper-lands, and the principal obstacle to the colonial government's attempts to make the pepper-trade a free *volkscultuur*.

## The Afdeelingsbank and Free Volkscultuur

To break the *pangkaigever*'s hold and make the pepper-trade a free *volkscultuur*, the colonial government attempted to establish credit through *afdeelingsbanks* at [[Langsa]] and elsewhere, offering loans without the requirement of pepper delivery. The banks, however, had no collateral to fall back on, suffered losses, and were forced to stay away from the pepper districts.

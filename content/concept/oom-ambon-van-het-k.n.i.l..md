**Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.** — "Uncle Ambon of the K.N.I.L." — is a Dutch-language book by [[G. W. T. Dames]], *Maj. Inf. K.N.I.L. b.d.* (Major of Infantry, K.N.I.L., retired), published in 's-[[Gravenhage]] (The Hague) in [[1954]] by the *Koninklijke Bibliotheek*. The book is the single richest Dutch-language portrait of the Ambonese professional soldier of the colonial army, and the principal printed source for the daily life, patrols, songs and ethos of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee corps in Aceh]] in the last decades of colonial rule. It is the source of every entry in this AcehWiki corpus that draws on Dames.

## Genre and method

*[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is part memoir, part regimental history, part ethnographic portrait of "*[[Oom Ambon]]*" — the archetypal Ambonese soldier of the colonial army. Dames writes in the first person throughout, identifying himself as a captain in the [[K.N.I.L.]] who served on the [[Westkust (of Atjeh)|Westkust of Atjeh]] in the [[1930s]] and saw action against Indonesian revolutionary forces around [[Brastagi]] in [[1947]]. The book is not a chronological autobiography but a series of thematic chapters — *Bij ons…*, *Van recruut tot pensioen*, *Moeilijkheden op den weg*, *Tangsi en Tante*, *De buitenpost*, *Ooms leger*, *Oom en de Bintang*, *Dominee Thenu*, *Thijs*, *De patrouille*, *Jappentijd*, *Onder Brits bewind*, *Onzekerheid*, *Tegen de rood-witten*, *Tamaela* — held together by the figure of *Oom Ambon*.

Dames explicitly distances himself from the tall-story tradition of the colonial officer corps. The book's prologue opens with the famous [[Hotel de Boer]] anecdote and uses the story to mark out the boundary between legend and *waarheid*. "Wij willen naar waarheid vertellen van Oom Ambon," he writes; the book will not "de methode van bovengenoemde kapitein volgen" or "trachten te zien hoever men met fantasieën kan gaan."

## Sources and the Nawoord

In the *Nawoord* Dames acknowledges the printed sources he drew on for episodes he could not have witnessed himself, especially the chapters on the older [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and the decorations history. He names in particular the works of *van Kijffler* and *Booms*, as well as *Hotz* (whose account of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] he used for the [[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri|Tiriō]] campaign), [[Schmidt]] himself (whose report on [[1910]]-[[1911]] was published), *du Croo*, *Zentgraaf* and [[Generaal Swart|General Swart]]. The book is therefore not pure memoir but a synthesis of personal recollection and these older printed authorities, with Dames's own commentary threaded throughout.

## Contents

The book's chapters are:

1. *Bij ons…* — the prologue, on the [[Hotel de Boer]] anecdote and the boundary between legend and *waarheid*.
2. *Van recruut tot pensioen* — the career of the archetypal Ambonese soldier *Patty*, from recruit to pension.
3. *Moeilijkheden op den weg* — the difficulties of the colonial soldier's life, including the *[[Magelang menage protest (1930)|Magelang menage protest]]*.
4. *Tangsi en Tante* — the *[[tangsi]]* (barracks) and the *[[Tante]]* (the Oom's wife).
5. *De buitenpost* — the *[[bivak]]* (field camp) in the *[[buitengewesten]]*.
6. *Ooms leger* — the colonial army's history, including the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]].
7. *Oom en de Bintang* — the decorations of the colonial army.
8. *Leve het Zesde Bataljon!* — the [[Verovering van Tondok en Kadoe (Celebes)|Verovering van Tondok en Kadoe]] on [[Celebes]].
9. *De colonne Schmidt* — the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] of [[1910]]-[[1911]].
10. *Dominee Thenu* — the *Soldaten-Dominee* of Aceh.
11. *Thijs* — the sergeant [[Thijs de Fretes]] of [[Bakongan]].
12. *De patrouille* — the daily life of the patrouille.
13. *Jappentijd* — the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Japanese occupation]] of [[1942]]-[[1945]].
14. *Onder Brits bewind* — the British interregnum of [[1945]]-[[1946]].
15. *Onzekerheid* — the uncertainty of [[1946]]-[[1949]].
16. *Tegen de rood-witten* — the war against the Indonesian republicans of [[1946]]-[[1949]].
17. *Tamaela* — the korporaal [[Tamaela]] of the [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]].
18. *Nawoord* — the afterword with sources and acknowledgments.

## Significance

*[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* is the single richest Dutch-language portrait of the Ambonese professional soldier of the colonial army. Because it was written in [[1954]] — late enough to look back on the whole arc of the [[K.N.I.L.]] from the [[Aceh War]] to the [[Souvereiniteitsoverdracht (27 December 1949)|transfer of sovereignty]], early enough to be informed by living memory — it functions both as a primary source on the last generation of *[[Oom Ambon]]* and as a retrospective interpretation of what that figure meant. Its affectionate but clear-eyed portrait of the Ambonese soldier, of the *[[tangsi]]* culture that shaped him, and of the colonial regime he served, has been drawn on by every later historian of the [[K.N.I.L.]] and of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] in particular.

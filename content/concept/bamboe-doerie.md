**Bamboe-doerie** (doornbamboe — thorny bamboo) is a species of thorny bamboo used in Atjehese and Dutch fortification practice in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as a vegetable obstacle, planted on the outside of a sandbag breastwork to raise its defensive value. The biographer [[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] records that the provisional sandbag breastwork of the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam]] constructed by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede-Luitenant C. J. Snijders]] on [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]] was given, on the west, south, and east fronts, "een voorloopige zandzakborstwering [...] welker afwerend vermogen over een gedeelte van de lengte door eene aan de buitenzijde aanwezige bamboedoerie (doornbamboe) haag werd verhoogd." The use of bamboe-doerie is thus an integral part of the Dutch sandbag-benting system of the early Atjeh war.

## Use at Benting Kota Alam

At the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam]] the bamboe-doerie hedge was planted on the outside of the sandbag breastwork, on the west, south, and east fronts. The landward (north) side was closed with a breastwork of stacked tree-trunks, of which there was a plentiful supply on the site. The bamboe-doerie was thus an auxiliary obstacle to the sandbag parapet, raising its "afwerend vermogen" (defensive value) over part of the length. The use of an existing Atjehese vegetable obstacle — bamboe-doerie hedges are a common feature of Atjehese kampong defences — illustrates the practical accommodation of Dutch engineer practice to local materials and local fortification conventions.

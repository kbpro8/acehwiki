**The Sagi System** was the highest tier of territorial administration in [[Great Acheh]], dividing the heartland into three great districts (*sagi*, from Malay *segi*, "side" or "angle"), each comprising a fixed number of [[Mukim|mukims]] and each led by a *panglima sagi* (sagi chief).

## The Three Sagis

- **The XXII Mukims** — the highland sagi, whose people (*ureuëng Buëng*) resembled the highlanders (*ureuëng tunöng*) in language and customs. This was sometimes referred to as the sagi of the VII Mukims Buëng within the XXVI Mukims context.
- **The XXV Mukims** — on the left bank of the [[Acheh River]], whose territory lay to the left proceeding downstream.
- **The XXVI Mukims** — on the right bank of the [[Acheh River]], containing the capital-area gampongs.

## The Panglima Sagi

Each sagi was headed by a *panglima sagi* — the supreme [[Uleebalang|uleebalang]] of that division. These three chiefs were among the most powerful figures in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]], sitting between the [[Aceh Sultanate|Sultan]] and the territorial [[Uleebalang|uleebalangs]] below them. On a new sultan's accession they received a "wedding gift" (*jinamëë*) of $500; in return, a newly constituted panglima sagi paid at least an equal sum to the sultan for his letters patent.

## Religious Administration

Each sagi also had its own chief kali (*qadi*) — the "judge of the Almighty Lord" — who administered [[Hikum|Islamic law]] within the sagi, in contrast to the [[Teuku Kali Malikon Ade|Kali Malikon Adé]] who was notionally the superior judge for the whole kingdom.

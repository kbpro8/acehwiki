**Pantang** (with the related terms *palöë* / *poemalöë* / *këmali*) was the Atjèhnese term for **taboo** — magical prohibitions on actions, words, and objects to prevent harmful consequences. *Pantang* was the prohibition itself; *palöë* / *poemalöë* / *këmali* was the magical state being prevented. The system was based on fear of magical contamination, resemblance, or bewitchment.

## Types of pantang

Kreemer identifies several types of *pantang*:
1. **Pantang-voorschriften** — general taboo rules (e.g., Friday is *pantang* for sea fishing, farming, and other labour)
2. **Geslachts-pantang's** — taboos related to sex and gender
3. **Taal-pantang's** (*narit moebalé*) — word taboos, where certain words are replaced by euphemisms in certain circumstances
4. **Verbodsteekenen** — visible prohibition signs

## Taal-pantang's (word taboos)

*Taal-pantang*'s were taboos on certain words in certain circumstances, replaced by euphemisms:
- Smallpox was called *sakét boengöng kajèë* ("flower disease") not *sakét plawa*
- Cholera was called *pëmböngörön* in Gayo
- Tiger, elephant, crocodile, and snake were not called by their real names
- The sick were described as "pleasant of flesh"

At sea, *taal-pantang*'s were particularly strict: words like *goenöng* (mountain), *gadjah* (elephant), and *lheuëh* (finished) were forbidden for fear of summoning spirits or danger.

## The praise taboo (keunöng meuj göb)

The **keunöng meuj göb** ("struck by the praise of others") was the dangerous effect of praise, believed to attract envious spirits. Equivalent to German *berufen* or *beschreien*, it was counteracted by the formula **be moebabah** ("may the small one not be struck by the praise of strangers") and by smearing lime behind the child's ears.

## Fishery pantang's

Friday was *pantang* for sea fishing, farming, and other labour. After the *kanoeri la'öt* (sea-kanoeri at the beginning of the fishing season), fishing at sea was *pantang* for 7 days. These fishery *pantang*'s were enforced by the *pawang* (master fisherman) who possessed *èleumèë* (esoteric knowledge).

**Pelantoengan** — a Javanese place-name, from *lantoeng* (in Old Javanese / Javanese, "earth-oil", i.e. petroleum) — is the toponymic evidence cited by Ir. [[F. P. C. S. van der Ploeg|Van der Ploeg]] in his chapter *Mijnbouw* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]) for the indigenous Javanese knowledge of petroleum before the arrival of the Dutch. Van der Ploeg's wording: "Crude petroleum was in the Indies already long known to the natives. The Java-wide-occurring name *Pelantoengan* points to this; *lantoeng* namely means earth-oil. Also from Palembang, in ancient times, a quantity seems to have been brought to China as medicine."

## Significance for Atjeh

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, *Pelantoengan* is significant chiefly as the Javanese counterpart to the early Atjeh-adjacent petroleum finds. The same chapter of *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* records that "the first to drill oil in exploitable quantity was [[Zijlker|Zijlker]], and indeed in a concession in the Landschap [[Langkat]] in the year [[1884]]" — that is, in the *Landschap* of Langkat, immediately adjacent to Atjeh. The Javanese *Pelantoengan* toponymy is the older, indigenous evidence of petroleum knowledge; the Langkat concession of 1884 is the modern, industrial exploitation of the same resource, here placed by Van der Ploeg at the head of the Sumatra East Coast petroleum industry.

The early Dutch reports of Sumatran petroleum seeps — the *Itinerario* of [[J. H. van Linschoten|Jan Huygen van Linschoten]], who brought back from Goa a sample of petroleum "from a spring on Sumatra, that flows pure balm", and the story of an oil spring at [[Deli]] "which burned on the sea and with which two Portuguese galleons were set alight" — are, in Van der Ploeg's account, the European counterpart to the indigenous *Pelantoengan* toponymy. Both traditions of petroleum knowledge predate the industrial exploitation that began with Zijlker's [[1884]] concession in Langkat.

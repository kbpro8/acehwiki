*show of force* — Dutch for "show of force" — was a small-scale [[Dutch]] colonial military demonstration intended to impress the Achehnese population without engaging in full-scale combat. The *machtsvertoon* was distinguished from a punitive expedition by its more limited objective: where a *tuchtiging* sought to destroy enemy fortifications and burn hostile kampongs, a *machtsvertoon* sought merely to display Dutch military capability — marching a column through hostile territory, demonstrating artillery, returning to base without major engagement. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* records several *machtsvertoningen* in May–June [[1896]]: the [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the IX Moekims]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]], the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the III Moekims Daroe]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]], the four-column [[Four-column machtsvertoon (20 June 1896)|machtsvertoon of 20 June 1896]], and the two-column [[Two-column machtsvertoon (26 June 1896)|machtsvertoon of 26 June 1896]].

## Etymology and Meaning

The word *machtsvertoon* is a Dutch compound: power, force + display, show, from *vertonen*, to display. The literal sense is "display of power". In the colonial military context the term denoted a deliberate demonstration of Dutch military force — typically a column march through a hostile or wavering region, often combined with artillery fire and visible manoeuvre — intended to overawe the population and discourage defection to the resistance. The *machtsvertoon* was less destructive than a *tuchtiging* and could be undertaken against regions whose hostility was uncertain or whose loyalty was thought recoverable. The plural is *machtsvertoningen*.

## Historical Context

In May–June [[1896]] the *machtsvertoon* was the principal Dutch military instrument short of full-scale *tuchtiging*. After the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] had broken [[Teuku Umar]]'s defensive position at [[Lam-Pisang]], the Dutch command shifted to a series of smaller operations designed to consolidate the victory and discourage reconstitution of the resistance. The [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the IX Moekims]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]] and the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the III Moekims Daroe]] of the same day were twin demonstrations in regions whose loyalty was uncertain. The [[Four-column machtsvertoon (20 June 1896)|four-column machtsvertoon of 20 June 1896]] was a larger operation, also notable for the first appearance of [[Panglima Bintang]] before the Dutch. The [[Two-column machtsvertoon (26 June 1896)|two-column machtsvertoon of 26 June 1896]] under Bisschoff and Laceulle was one of the last operations of [[Luitenant-Generaal Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s *regeeringscommissariaat* before his departure on [[28 June]] [[1896]].

## Examples in the Source

Kruisheer's principal *machtsvertoon* references include: the [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the IX Moekims]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]] — a demonstration in the IX Moekims region. The [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the III Moekims Daroe]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]] — a simultaneous demonstration in the III Moekims Daroe. The [[Four-column machtsvertoon (20 June 1896)|four-column machtsvertoon of 20 June 1896]] — also notable for [[Teuku Umar]]'s ceremony/feast at [[Lepong]] to appoint new panglimas, and for [[Panglima Bintang]]'s first appearance before the Dutch. The [[Two-column machtsvertoon (26 June 1896)|two-column machtsvertoon of 26 June 1896]] under Bisschoff and Laceulle — the last major operation of Vetter's *regeeringscommissariaat*. The strategic purpose — to impress the population and discourage defection — is consistently emphasised.

## Significance

The *machtsvertoon* is the institutional expression of the Dutch attempt to consolidate the gains of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] without committing to a permanent garrison presence. As an instrument of "soft" coercion it complemented the "hard" coercion of the *tuchtiging*: the one burned, the other displayed. The *machtsvertoon*'s liprovided that — the population typically returned to its previous allegiance once the column withdrew — would lead the Dutch under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] to the more permanent *pacificatie* policy of garrisoning conquered areas. The *machtsvertoon* of [[26 June]] [[1896]] was one of the last acts of Vetter's *regeeringscommissariaat*; his departure on [[28 June]] [[1896]] and the restoration of the *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] marked the formal end of the first semester's operations and the transition to the next phase of the war.

**Smuggling Trade on Borneo’s West Coast** refers to the illicit movement of goods—particularly opium, textiles, and spices—between **[[Singapore]]**, **[[Labuan]]**, and the interior of **[[Borneo]]** during the mid‑nineteenth century. Dutch authorities feared that the flourishing smuggling network would erode their control over the **[[Sultanate of Aceh]]**’s maritime commerce, prompting a series of naval expeditions in [[1850]]‑[[1854]] to suppress the trade and re‑assert Dutch authority.

## Connection to Aceh

- The smuggling routes intersected with Acehnese trade networks, threatening revenue and political influence.
- Dutch military actions aimed at curbing smuggling were partly motivated by the need to protect Aceh’s economic interests.

*railway line* and *railway* — Dutch for "railway" — were the [[Dutch]] colonial military railway line and tramway network that linked [[Koeta Radja]] to the forward posts at [[Ketapan Doea|Ketapan Doewa]] and [[Blang]] in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The *spoorbaan* ran between [[Ketapan Doea|Ketapan Doewa]] and [[Blang]], and troops marched along it; extra trains were used for troop transport to and from Koeta Radja. The steam tram was used to transport troops from posts to the [[kraton]]. Kruisheer's *ATJEH '96* records the use of the railway throughout the first semester of [[1896]] as the principal means of moving troops, supplies and casualties between the geconcentreerde linie and the outer posts, and as the means of returning columns from operations to Koeta Radja. The bullet backstop at the firing range at [[Ketapan Doea|Ketapan Doewa]] is a typical reference point along the railway.

## Etymology and Meaning

The Dutch railway nomenclature is descriptive. *Spoorbaan* is a compound: track, rail + path, way — literally "track-way". *Spoorweg* is similarly: *spoor* + road, way — "track-road". The two terms are functionally equivalent in Kruisheer's usage. *Spoorwegdijk* is a compound: *spoorweg* + dike, embankment — the railway embankment on which the track was laid, often raised above the surrounding wet-rice field and *rawah*. *Extratreinen* is the plural of *extratrein* — *extra* + train: an extra, non-scheduled train, typically used for troop transport. *Stoomtram* is a compound: steam + tram, from English *tram* — a steam tram, a lighter railway used for local passenger and troop transport. The Dutch colonial army's Atjeh railway was a military railway, distinct from the civilian Koninklijke Paketvaart-Maatschappij's maritime operations.

## Historical Context

In the first semester of [[1896]] the *spoorbaan* was the principal logistical artery of the Dutch position in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The railway linked [[Koeta Radja]] — the colonial capital and the location of the [[kraton]] — to the forward posts at [[Ketapan Doea|Ketapan Doewa]] and [[Blang]], and from thence to the buitenlinie. *Extratreinen* were used to move reinforcements arriving at the harbour of [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh (Ulee Lheue)]] up to the geconcente linie: the reinforcements from Java (the [[5e Bataljon|5th]] and [[7e Bataljon|7th Bataljons]], a *bergbatterij*, a *peloton cavalerie*) arrived on [[1 May]] [[1896]] by KPM steamers and were railed forward. The *stoomtram* transported troops from the forward posts to the *kraton*. The railway also carried the wounded back to the *veldhospitaal* at Koeta Radja and the *amfitheater* of military operations. The *spoorwegdijk* — the railway embankment — was a tactical feature in itself, raised above the surrounding wet-rice field and providing a defensible line of march.

The *extratreinen* — extra trains for troop transport to and from Koeta Radja. The *stoomtram* — used to transport troops from posts to the *kraton*. The *spoorwegdijk* — the railway embankment (pages 85, 88). The bullet backstop at the firing range at [[Ketapan Doea|Ketapan Doewa]] — used as a terrain reference point. The report from [[Lamdjamoe|Lam-Djamoe]] of [[5 June]] [[1896]] — "Troepen keeren per extra trein terug" (the troops return by extra train). The reinforcements of [[1 May]] [[1896]] — arrived by KPM steamers at [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]] and railed forward. The *kraton* — the destination of the *stoomtram*. The railway's role in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] — moving troops and casualties to and from the front.

## Significance

The *spoorbaan* was the material precondition of Dutch military operations in [[Aceh]]. Without the railway, the geconcente linie at Koeta Radja could not have been sustained; without the *extratreinen*, reinforcements from Java could not have been railed forward; without the *stoomtram*, the daily movement of troops between posts and the *kraton* would have been impracticable. The railway embankment (*spoorwegdijk*) was itself a tactical feature — raised above the surrounding wet-rice field and providing a defensible line of march. The railway's vulnerability to Achehnese attack — a single cut rail could immobilise a column — made its protection a constant preoccupation of the Dutch command. The railway's survival through the [[Aceh War]] and its expansion under the *pacificatie* policies of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] testify to its institutional importance as the logistical backbone of Dutch colonial rule in Aceh.

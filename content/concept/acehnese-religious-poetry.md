**Acehnese religious poetry** comprises a corpus of poetic works composed in the Malay language of [[Acheh]] that express Islamic devotion, often drawing on Arabic sources such as the *Qasida al‑Burda*. The genre flourished from the 16th to the 19th centuries, serving both liturgical and political functions.

## Historical Development
Early Acehnese poetry was transmitted orally by *daeng* (religious scholars). The arrival of the Malay translation of the *Burda* (c. [[1550]]) provided a literary model that blended Qurʾānic motifs with local imagery, such as references to *jinn* and *kāfir*.

## Themes and Motifs
Common themes include the glorification of the Prophet [[Muhammad]], the power of the Qurʾān, the triumph over *kāfir* and demonic forces, and the moral authority of the sultan. Imagery of “pearls”, “fire”, and “water” recurs, echoing verses from the *Burda*.

## Notable Works
- The **Malay translation of the *Burda*** (see [[16th‑century Malay translation of the Burda]]).
- Poetic commentaries by *ulama* of the court of [[Sultan Ibrahim of Aceh]].
- Hymns (*nasyid*) performed during Ramadan and *maulid* celebrations.

## Influence on Society
These verses were recited in mosques, royal courts, and battlefield encampments, reinforcing the sultanate’s claim to be the “guardian of the faith”. They also functioned as a vehicle for resistance against Dutch colonial incursions in the 19th century.

**Perak Malay** is the local Malay dialect spoken in the state of [[Perak]] that, according to linguistic analysis of a 16th‑century Acehnese manuscript, influenced the Malay translation of the [[Burda (poem)|Burda]] produced in or linked to the Acehnese sultanate [[Atjēh]].

## Characteristics
Perak Malay exhibits certain phonological and morphological traits distinct from standard Malay, including the occasional nasalization of voiced stops after the prefix *me‑* (e.g., *mengebala*, *mengadar*) and the retention of forms lacking the nominal suffix *-an*, such as *bengadjar* (“lesson”), *kedatang* (“arrival”), and *keingin* (“desire”). These features are noted in the source as comparable to the state of Perak Malay described by C. C. Brown and K. Wulff【20260707_232246_DLP-54-Een_16de_eeuwse_Maleise_vertaling_van_de_Burda_van_al_Bu_s_i_ri_OCR.txt, p. 44】.

## Influence on Acehnese manuscripts
The source argues that the close political ties between [[Atjēh]] and [[Perak]] in the 16th and 17th centuries make it plausible that a Malay manuscript originating from an Acehnese context would display Perak‑Malay linguistic traits: “is het niet ongerijmd, invloed van het Perakse dialect in een uit Atjèh herkomstig handschrift uit de 16th eeuw aan te nemen”【20260707_232246_DLP-54-Een_16de_eeuwse_Maleise_vertaling_van_de_Burda_van_al_Bu_s_i_ri_OCR.txt, p. 45】. This hypothesis is supported by the presence of Perak‑like forms in the Malay translation of the [[Burda (poem)|Burda]].

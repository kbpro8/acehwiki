**Atjeh als rijstland** — *Atjeh as a rice-country* — is Broersma's phrase for the question whether Atjeh is properly a rice-land, treated at length in the sixteenth essay of *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]*. The question is one of the principal economic questions of the early civilian period, and the answer to it is the principal programme of the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]]'s pamphlet *[[De rijkdom van Atjeh|De rijkdom van Atjeh]]* ([[1923]]).

## The Question
Many readers, Broersma writes, would be surprised if he once asked whether Atjeh is really a rice-land. One has heard of the plain of Pidië and its irrigation-works; one has heard that the Atjehnese gladly is a farmer, that the women here and there take their share in the work; and so there is a current opinion that Atjeh is a true great rice-land and may boast of a rich harvest of grain. Certainly Atjeh is a rice-land only in this sense, that it has a considerable area of rice-lands, that rice-cultivation is the great people's business, and also that, with the application of some measures and the observance of some cares, it would be an excellent region for rice-growing and would obtain a surplus for which a greedy market would be found in Netherlands-India itself. *'Atjeh is een rijstland slechts in dezen zin'* — *Atjeh is a rice-land only in this sense.*

## The Yield
It is certain that Atjeh in the yield of *padi* remains below its capacity, because Netherlands-India as a whole remains below its capacity. If one excepts some regions that Nature particularly favours as rice-lands, then the whole of Netherlands-India brings about as much rice as it itself consumes. There is still always room for a great rice-enterprise that would give Netherlands-India an easy certainty of the availability of enough people's food, if supply from outside should become impossible.

## The Import Figures
According to the available data Atjeh has for many years had a shortage, so that import was necessary for large amounts. From [[1904]] to and with [[1915]] there was import of more than 6 million kilograms of rice minimum to more than 13 million kilograms maximum. In a year-report of the agricultural teacher at Koeta Radja over [[1920]] and [[1921]] one reads that the import in [[1917]] amounted to 15 thousand tons, 10 thousand tons in [[1918]], 5 thousand tons in [[1919]], in [[1920]] no import but export of 2 thousand tons, in [[1921]] export 2.500 tons. What value is to be attached to those figures is not fixed: the information about the origin is lacking.

## The Programme of Improvement
Broersma's own programme, set out in the seventeenth essay of *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen]]*, is the appointment of a real expert on rice-cultivation to act in continuous consultation with a *college van kundige hoofden* — a college of knowledgeable chiefs — and to begin with the simplest improvements: the provision of suitable seed-padi, the better treatment of the harvest, the construction of *loemboengs* (village storehouses) for the preservation of seed. The great public-works, like the failed dam at [[Panté Lhong|Panté Lhong]], are in Broersma's view the wrong place to begin.

**The Sultanspartij** — the Sultan's party, also called the *oorlogspartij* or war party — was the Acehnese faction that maintained armed resistance to the Dutch, and whose bands are repeatedly named in [[P. H. R. Beuming]]'s *[[Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh (1911)|Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh]]*.

Beuming records that at the end of [[1895]] *de benden van [[Panglima Polim]] en die der Sultanspartij, traden overal met driestheid op* — the bands of Panglima Polim and those of the Sultan's party acted everywhere with boldness — marking out the Dutch forward posts in particular, and that since orders had been given to operate as little as possible outside the *[[Benting|bentings]]*, the Acehnese marksmen had free play. It was in this period that [[The sharpshooter of Biloel|the sharpshooter of Biloel]] harassed the sentries of that post.

He describes [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] before his defection as *onze zoogenaamde bondgenoot tegen de fanatieke benden der Sultanspartij* — our so-called ally against the fanatical bands of the Sultan's party — the pretext under which Umar obtained from the Dutch the arms, ammunition and money enumerated in Beuming's account of the defection.

In his introductory chapter Beuming traces the growth of the party's religious character: after the withdrawal of the military administration in [[1881]] the war party acted everywhere with boldness again, and under Colonel [[Demmeni|H. D. Demmeni]] from [[1883]] the Dutch adopted a policy of combating it; the struggle on [[Groot-Atjeh]] then gradually acquired a more religious character, *aangezien de priesterpartij de leiding van den [[Prang Sabil|Prang Sabil]] op zich genomen had* — since the priestly party had taken over the leadership of the holy war.

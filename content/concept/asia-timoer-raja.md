**Asia Timoer Raja** (Japanese *Daitóa*, "Greater East Asia") was the principal propaganda idea of the Japanese occupation of [[Atjèh|Atjèh]] during the period [[September]] [[1942]] – [[September]] [[1944]], through which the [[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]] of [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] sought to inspire the Atjèhnese population with "enthusiasm for the realization of the Pan-Asian idea of a 'Greater East Asia' … under the leadership of Japan, in whose common sphere of prosperity (*lingkoengan kemakmoeran bersama-sama*) the peoples of Indonesia would also be admitted." Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats Asia Timoer Raja as the first of the two great propaganda phases of the occupation — the period in which the Japanese still expected an early final victory, and the entire effort of the administration was subordinated to the realisation of that victory.

## The propaganda apparatus

Under [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]]'s direction, the information service (*senden-han*) conducted "an intensive campaign … to inspire the population with enthusiasm" for the Greater East Asia idea. Newspaper, film, theatre, and radio were the principal means. The *[[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]]*, first issued on [[3 June]] [[1942]], "with its pernicious anti-Western content," was the chief print organ, and a touring theatre of the *senden-han* staged anti-Dutch plays — including a version of the *Tjoet Nja' Diën* story — to drive the idea home. "Continuously, propagandists of the *senden-han* traveled through the country, for the dissemination of the Asia Timoer Raja- and the anti-Western ideas." The propaganda was, however, "accompanied by fierce and sophisticated anti-Western propaganda, according to the well-known system of totalitarian powers."

## Religious institutionalisation

The most lasting institutional expression of the Asia Timoer Raja idea in Atjèh was the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] — the *Madjelis agama Islam oentoek bantoean kemakmoeran Asia Timoer Raja di Atjèh* ("Islamic Council for the Welfare of Greater East Asia in Atjèh") — installed by Resident lino on [[10 March]] [[1943]]. Its task, in lino's installation speech, was "providing support — to the extent that it does not conflict with the Islamic religion — in the promotion of the welfare of Greater East Asia." The labour-mobilisation decrees of [[8 April]] [[1944]] similarly created the *Atjèh-shii-kéa-hókó-dan* — "Perkoempoelan pekerdja² oentoek oemoem didalam Atjèh-shii dalam hal membina Asia Timoer Raja" — for the equitable distribution of forced labour.

## The shift to Indonesia Merdéka

By the second half of [[1943]] the propaganda was already beginning to slide: the [[Maibkatra|Maibkatra]] article on "the love of and defense of the fatherland" in the *Atjèh Shimbun* of [[16 October]] [[1943]] placed "the love for the Acehnese fatherland, the 'land of the reuntjóng'" above the duties toward the Greater East Asia Association. After Prime Minister [[Koiso|Koiso]]'s declaration of [[7 September]] [[1944]] promising future East Indies independence, "the Indonesia Merdéka-idea in propaganda took the place of the Asia Timoer Raja-idea." Piekaar notes that "however little the Asia Timoer Raja-idea may have taken root … the anti-Dutch propaganda, on the other hand, found fertile soil in the hearts of the Atjèhnese population, which was so receptive to hatred of the Dutch."

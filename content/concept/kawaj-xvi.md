**The Kawaj XVI** — "the Sixteen" — was the federation of sixteen Westkust chiefs tributary to the [[Keudjeuroeën|keudjeuroeën]] Tjhïʼ of [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]], originally a tax-collection district created by the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] in [[1760]] (Mohammedan year 1185) and later transformed by Dutch intervention into a territorial chieftaincy under [[Teukoe Tjhï' Meulabôh|Teukoe Tjhï' Meulabôh]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* insists that the two Kawaj XVI's — the historic fiscal circle and the later territorial *landschap* — are "in werkelijkheid dus heel iets anders" — in reality quite different things — though they share the same name.

## The fiscal origin of 1760

In the year 1185 of the Mohammedan era (approximately [[1760]] AD), the handbook records, "maakten zestien hoofden uit het Meulabôhsche, onder geleide van een voornaam handelaar van die havenplaats, hunne opwachting bij den Sultan, die hun, tegen belofte van geregelde schatting, brieven van erkenning (sarakata's) uitreikte." Their conductor was appointed the Sultan's customs-collector — *belastinggaarder des Sultans* — "met den titel keudjeuroeën Tjhïʼ, aan wien deze hoofden dus hunne schatting moesten afdragen." The sixteen included the [[Keudjeuroeën|keudjeuroeëns]] of Wojla, Boebôn, and Seunagan (with the *oelèëbalang-tjoet* of Seunagan), the three *dato's* of Meulabôh, and "eenige hoofden uit het stroomgebied der kr. Meureubôh," some of whom "géén territoriaal gezag bezaten, doch alleen in- en doorvoerrechten hieven" — held no territorial authority but only transit and import duties. Also included was the *Panghoeloe-Sidik*, charged with collecting the Sultan's dues on [[Simeuloë|Simeuloë]], which thereby became tributary to the Tjhïʼ of Meulabôh. Other Westkust chiefs later received their own sarakata's but were not subordinated to this collector; "ter onderscheiding werd daarom diens invloedsfeer naar die 16 hoofden later aangeduid als Kawaj XVI."

## The territorial chieftaincy

The Tjhïʼ of Meulabôh "trachtte… ook territoriaal over deze 16 cijnsplichtige hoofden te matigen" — sought to extend territorial authority over the sixteen tributaries — succeeding with the three *dato's* of Meulabôh but failing with the others. Dutch intervention transformed the situation entirely. "Wij begiftigden Teukoe Tjhï' van Meulabôh als loon voor zijne diensten met territoriaal gezag, niet alleen de hem reeds onderworpen 3 dato-schappen, doch ook nog over een tot dusverre niet direct onder hem staand deel van het stroomgebied der kr. Meureubôh (behalve Beutông)." The newly created territory was still called, by historical resonance, the Kawaj XVI — "in werkelijkheid dus heel iets anders dan de Kawaj XVI, waarover hij vroeger belastinggaarder was."

## Not a Sagi

The handbook explicitly warns against any analogy to the federations of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. "Eene overeenkomst tusschen dit gebied en eene landschaps-federatie als de Groot-Atjehsche [[Sagi|Sagi]], welke men vroeger meende te mogen aannemen, bestaat dus in het geheel niet." The Kawaj XVI was not a federation of equal chiefs under a Panglima Sagi, but a territorial chieftaincy under a single *landschapshoofd* whose title recalled, but did not reproduce, the old fiscal circle.

## Parallel and contrast: the Kawaj XII

A similar institution arose inland, in the mountains between Beutông and Keumala Dalam (onderafdeeling [[Sigli|Sigli]]): the *Kawaj XII*, twelve settlements of gold-seekers from Pedir and Groot-Atjeh, whose keudjeuroeëns were placed under a Sultan's collector styled *keudjeuroeën Tjhïʼ* (here "Tjhïʼ" being read as *mas*, gold), who was to collect from each chief "1 boengkaj stofgoud" — one *boengkaj* of gold dust — annually. The Kawaj XII was extinguished in [[1914]], its territory partitioned between the onderafdeelingen [[Sigli|Sigli]] and [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]] on account of depopulation caused by the cholera epidemic of [[1878]] and the decline of the gold workings.

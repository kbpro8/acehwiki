**Survey-parties** (*survey-parties*) were groups of plantation officials, government advisors, and labour union representatives dispatched in early [[1950]] to assess the condition of plantation enterprises in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] and other parts of Sumatra following the Dutch-Indonesian hostilities. The term was used in the English-influenced commercial vocabulary of the East Coast plantation world.

## Process

The survey-party system was a mechanism for evaluating the physical condition, staffing needs, and economic viability of plantation enterprises that had been abandoned, damaged, or expropriated during the Japanese occupation and the Indonesian revolution. The parties typically included representatives of the plantation companies, government agricultural advisors, and — in the case of the Atjeh surveys — commissioners of the [[Sarbupri]] labour union, reflecting the central role of labour relations in the reopening process.

## Atjeh survey parties (February 1950)

The Atjeh survey parties of February [[1950]] were resolved upon during discussions at [[Kotaradja]] on 9-10 December [[1949]]. The parties assessed approximately fifty enterprises in Atjeh, leading to the return of some forty enterprises to management by August [[1950]].

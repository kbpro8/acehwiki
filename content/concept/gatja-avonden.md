**gatja-avonden** (English: "henna evenings (three evenings before wedding)") is a term or concept described in [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]]'s *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu* in relation to [[Aceh]]. Three evenings preceding the wedding at the bride's house; palms, soles, and tips of fingers and toes of the bride are smeared with the red sap of ground gatja-leaves.

## Translation

henna evenings (three evenings before wedding)

## Description

Three evenings preceding the wedding at the bride's house; palms, soles, and tips of fingers and toes of the bride are smeared with the red sap of ground gatja-leaves. No one from the groom's house may participate. From these evenings until the 7th day after the wedding, the young man and girl bear the names 'bruidegom' and 'bruid'. Also observed in [[Gajo-land (Gayo Land)|Gajo]] and [[De Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] (in [[Gajo-land (Gayo Land)|Gajo]] preceded by instruction from the [[dorpsgeestelijke]] and his wife about wedding ceremonies and marital duties).

## Significance

The term *gatja-avonden* is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the cultural, administrative, or institutional vocabulary of [[Aceh]].

**Padi lintjah**, also called **padi tadjoë**, was the Atjehnese levy on dry rice fields (*[[Ladang|ladang]]*) forming part of the *[[Adat glé|adat glé]]*, the composite tax charged on cultivation outside the *[[Gampong|gampong]]* boundaries. The alternative name derived from *tadjoë*, the dibble or planting stick — hence *poot-padi*, "dibbled padi," the rice sown by dibbling rather than transplanted.

## Assessment

The levy amounted to two *[[Naleh|naleh]]* of *padi* per *hippaang padi*, the *hippaang* being the area cleared by a single person with one *parang* (chopping knife; Atjehnese *thi*, "one"). The unit of assessment thus rested on the labour of one man with one implement rather than on measured surface.

## Abolition

The *padi lintjah*, together with the other levies of the *adat glé* and the *[[Boeët oemòng|boeët oemòng]]*, was abolished by article 15 sub I of [[Staatsblad 1919 No. 822]], the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalangs]]* receiving a fixed *[[Civiele lijst|civiele lijst]]* in its place.

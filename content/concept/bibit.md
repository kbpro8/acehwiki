**Bibit** (Atjehnese *bidjeh*) was the seed padi used for planting a rice field in [[Aceh|Atjeh]], and — because the size of a field was expressed by the quantity of seed needed to plant it — also the effective unit of land measurement.

## As a measure of land

The size of a rice field in Atjeh was indicated by the quantity of *bibit* needed on average for its planting, or estimated to be needed, measured in *[[Goentja|goentja]]*, *[[Naleh|naleh]]*, *[[Gantang|gantang]]* and *[[Areh|areh]]*. With one *naleh* about 1,600 square metres were planted, and good *[[Sawah|sawah]]*'s yielded on average fifty times the quantity of seed, poor fields fifteen times.

## In contracts and levies

- The *[[Boeët oemòng|boeët oemòng]]* was assessed at one *naleh* of *padi* per *naleh* of *bidjeh*.
- Under the *[[Thiplah tano|thiplah-tano]]* contract, a minimum working area of five *naleh* of *bidjeh* — about 0.8 hectare — was required of the immigrant, who received the seed as part of his *[[Pangkaj|pangkaj]]*.
- Under *mawaih bahagi lhée* and *[[Aboen meu'oeë|aboen meu'oeë]]* the giver supplied the *bibit*.
- Under the *[[Haq toeleuèng|haq toeleuèng]]* the proven original possessor had to supply the *bibit* as counter-obligation; in the [[Sigli|Siglische]] variant, a cultivator who himself provided the *bibit* for perennial crops became entitled to half their yield.

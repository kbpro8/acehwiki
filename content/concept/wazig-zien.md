**Wazig zien** — blurred or hazy vision — was the frequent complaint of young Atjehnese men and women whose cause Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] set out to trace as the sixth question of his [[Oogheelkundig kampongonderzoek in Groot-Atjeh (Maart-December 1937)|eye survey of 1937]].

## The investigation

Of 335 persons examined, 47 — 14 per cent — showed clear abnormalities giving an explanation for their complaint. These were for the most part refraction anomalies (30 cases).

Gomperts noted a methodological limit: since the refraction anomalies were diagnosed only from the fundus changes typical of them — *skiaskopisch* examination not being feasible because of the troublesome reflexes of the electric ophthalmoscope — it was very possible that refraction anomalies also occurred among the other patients examined without visible fundus changes. The cases of *hypermetropie* were established on further examination at [[Koeta Radja|Koetaradja]].

## The corneal finding

A large part of the patients complaining of blurred vision who reported at the eye out-patient clinic at Koetaradja showed, with the fluorescein test, "een heel lichte punt- en streepvormige verkleuring in het cornea-centrum" — a very slight punctate and linear discolouration in the centre of the cornea — while the cornea often showed nothing remarkable on ordinary focal examination, in particular no dullness.

The fluorescein test was also carried out at the *kampong* survey, but observation of these slight corneal discolourations was not possible there because the dark-room tent let through too much light.

Gomperts found that these corneal changes reacted either not at all, or only very slowly, to vitamin-A-rich therapy — cod-liver oil by mouth and cod-liver oil instillation — "zoodat andere hem onbekende factoren ook in het spel moeten zijn" — so that other factors unknown to him must also be in play.

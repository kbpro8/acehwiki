**The ijzerdraadversperring** — the wire entanglement, also *gewapend ijzerdraad*, armed iron wire — was the wire obstacle used around Dutch posts and fortified quarters in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], described in detail by [[P. H. R. Beuming]].

## At the permanent posts

Around the permanent posts of the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]* the entanglement consisted, in Beuming's description, of *in den grond geslagen paaltjes, die door ijzerdraad, op plusminus 1 d.M. van den grond aangebracht, verbonden waren* — stakes driven into the ground connected by iron wire strung about a decimetre above the soil. Walking through such an obstacle was as good as impossible, and the whole was intended *den post stormvrij te maken*, to make the post storm-free. It was set outside the aloe bushes and thorny plants that surrounded the palisade.

## At the forward posts

At the *[[Voorpostenbenting|voorpostenbenting]]* the wire formed the middle of three obstacles: before the palisade of coconut-palm trunks lay a four-metre-wide entanglement of *gewapend ijzerdraad*, and before that a barrier of *[[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboedoeri]]*.

## Elsewhere

The same obstacle was used to protect the fortified quarters of [[Koeta Radja]] in the crisis of [[1896]]: after a breastwork and wooden fence had been erected at the weakest point of [[Nesoeh]], *daarna de geheele wijk omgeven door een zware versperring van gewapend ijzerdraad* — the whole quarter was then surrounded by a heavy entanglement of armed iron wire. A horizontal entanglement likewise protected the siege battery thrown up at [[Lamdjamoe]] before the bombardment of [[Lampisang]].

When the Acehnese occupied the abandoned Dutch post of [[Anak Galoeng]] they retained its wire: the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of 29 June 1896]] found the obstacle still in good order and insuperable, forcing the attackers to open the gate from within.

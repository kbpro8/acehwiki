> **Note on nomenclature.** The work is variously spelt in the sources as *Sirat al-mustaqim*, *Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*, and (in older Dutch transliteration) *Sirat al-moestakim* or *Sjirat al-moestakim*. All forms denote the same Malay treatise by [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]], composed at the command of [[Sultan Iskandar Thani]] of [[Aceh]]. The title translates from Arabic as "The Straight Path."

**The Sirat al-Mustaqim** — Arabic *Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*, "The Straight Path" — is a Malay-language handbook of Islamic religious duties (*fiqh*) begun by [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] in [[1634]] and completed in [[1644]] at the command of [[Sultan Iskandar Thani]] of [[Aceh]]. It is the first systematic Malay handbook of Shāfiʿite *fiqh* — what the Dutch scholarly tradition calls a *plichtenleer* ("handbook of religious duties") — and a foundational text of Malay religious prose. The work is repeatedly attested in [[Over Maleise Literatuur (1947)|Hooykaas's survey]] of Malay literature as a key vehicle for the diffusion of the *fiqh* of the Shāfiʿite school into the Malay-Indonesian world from its Acehnese point of composition.

## Composition and Chronology

The composition of the *Sirat al-Mustaqim* was begun by [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|ar-Rānīrī]] in [[1634]] — three years before his formal arrival in Aceh in [[1637]] — and was completed only ten years later, in [[1644]]. The earlier date of [[1634]] was established by [[H. Djajadiningrat|Djajadiningrat]] in *TBG* 69, pp. 109–111; it corrects the earlier assumption of [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] — later confessed candidly by Voorhoeve in his introduction to the *Twee Maleise geschriften* — that the *Sirāṭ* had been composed before ar-Rānīrī's arrival in Aceh. Voorhoeve had overlooked Djajadiningrat's article in *BKI* 107, p. 357, where the chronology had already been set out.

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] noted that the work was written in March [[1638]] — the same year in which ar-Rānīrī also composed the *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]* and the *Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah* — a date which provides one of the firm chronological anchor-points in the early history of the [[Aceh|Acehnese Sultanate]]. The work was completed at the command of [[Sultan Iskandar Thani]] as part of ar-Rānīrī's project of providing the Acehnese faithful with the literary apparatus of a Shāfiʿite sultanate. The date of completion in [[1644]] — the very year in which Raniri returned to [[Ranir]] — makes the *Sirāṭ* one of the last works Raniri composed in Aceh before his departure. The knowledge of Malay letters displayed in the *Sirāṭ* need not, therefore, have been acquired before [[1637]].

## Purpose and Content

The *Sirat al-Mustaqim* is, in genre, a Malay handbook of religious duties — a Shāfiʿite *fiqh* of the ʿibādāt adapted for Malay-speaking Muslims. It was composed, like the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] and the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]], at the command of Sultan Iskandar Thani and as part of the same project of orthodox reform: to provide the Acehnese faithful with a reliable guide to the practical observances of Islam according to the Shāfiʿite school, in the *[[Bahasa Djawi]]* that was the language of the Acehnese court. Its composition at the very moment of ar-Rānīrī's public disputation against the [[Wujudiyyah]] mystics of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] and [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] places it at the centre of the project of orthodox reform that produced the suppression of the [[Wujudiyyah]] mysticism: it provided, for the new dispensation, the manual of observances by which the faithful were to regulate their daily conduct.

The treatise survives in an edition printed at [[Mecca]] in [[1316]] Hegira on the margin of an edition of the *Fatḥ al-muʿīn*. The Mecca edition preserved the work in a form free from the local Acehnese tendency to idealise or rearrange the past, and it is from this edition that Snouck Hurgronje drew Raniri's testimony fixing the death of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Meukuta Alam]] in [[1636]].

## Diffusion: The Kedah Episode

[[C. Hooykaas|Hooykaas]] records one of the key episodes in the early diffusion of the *Sirat al-Mustaqim* in his chapter on historiography. In his account of the *Hikajat Marong Mahawangsa* (the Kedah chronicle), Hooykaas notes that, when the king of Kedah converted to Islam under the influence of *Sjaich 'Abdoellāh Jamani* and was given the throne-name Sultan Moezaffar Sjah, "Toen men dit in Atjéh vernam, liet de Moslim geworden vorst aldaar Sjaich Noeroddin diens boek Sirāṭ al-moestakīm naar Kēdah brengen ter onderrichting in de Wet" — "When this was heard in Aceh, the Muslim ruler there had Sjaich Noeroddin's book *Sirāṭ al-mustaqīm* brought to Kedah for instruction in the Law."

The episode illustrates the role of the *Sirat al-Mustaqim* — composed in Aceh by a Gujarati-born scholar — as the principal vehicle by which the *fiqh* of the Shāfiʿite school was transmitted from Aceh to the [[Malay Peninsula]] in the early seventeenth century.

## Place in the Acehnese Literary Tradition

The *Sirat al-Mustaqim* belongs to the cluster of ar-Rānīrī's Acehnese compositions — the *[[Bustanu's-Salatin|Bustān as-Salāṭīn]]* of [[1638]], the *Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah* of [[1638]], the *Tibjān fi ma'rifat al-adyān*, and the *Sirat al-Mustaqim* itself — that together constitute the literary apparatus of ar-Rānīrī's orthodox reform in Aceh. The composition of the *Sirāṭ* in [[1638]] — at the very moment of ar-Rānīrī's public disputation against the *Wujudiyyah* mystics — marks the beginning of his project to provide the Acehnese faithful with the literary apparatus of a Shāfiʿite sultanate.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the *Sirat al-Mustaqim* is significant as the principal Malay-language *fiqh* handbook produced at the Acehnese court in the seventeenth century. Its composition by [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|ar-Rānīrī]] in [[1634]]–[[1644]] — at the command of Sultan Iskandar Thani — places it at the heart of the orthodox reform of the Acehnese court in the 1630s and 1640s, and its diffusion to Kedah in the early seventeenth century (recorded by Hooykaas) illustrates the role of the *Sirāṭ* as the principal vehicle by which the Shāfiʿite *fiqh* of the Acehnese court was transmitted to the Malay Peninsula.

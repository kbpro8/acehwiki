**mu'minin** were the plural form of the Arabic word *mu'min* “believer”, used in Acehnese religious literature to denote the community of faithful Muslims. The term appears in the 16th‑century Malay translation of the *Burda* (page [[88]]), where it is listed among other theological vocabulary.

## Definition and Linguistic Context
In the Acehnese‑Malay glossaries, *mu'minin* (plur. muʾminīn) is rendered simply as “believers”. It functions as a collective noun for the *Ummah* and is frequently paired with other Qur’anic terms such as *nabi* (prophet) and *ahkam* (law).

## Usage in Acehnese Texts
The entry on page [[88]] groups *mu'minin* with other doctrinal words, indicating its importance in the theological education of Acehnese scholars. The word is employed in sermons, *hikayat* (narratives), and *pantun* (poetic forms) to stress communal piety.

## Significance
The presence of *mu'minin* in the Acehnese translation of a major Arabic poem demonstrates the early integration of Arabic theological concepts into the Malay‑Acehnese literary tradition, reflecting the deep Islamic identity of the Acehnese sultanate.

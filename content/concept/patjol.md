***Patjol*** — glossed by [[P. H. R. Beuming]] as *Indische graafschop*, the Indies digging spade — was the hoe-like digging tool of the archipelago.

It appears in his narrative of [[The sharpshooter of Biloel|the sharpshooter of Biloel]] as evidence of how the Acehnese marksman had prepared his position. After the man was killed at dawn the patrol discovered, in the burnt-off *[[Alang-alang|alang-alang]]* field behind a bamboo thicket, a trench about a metre deep and a metre wide extending some ten metres to either side, its opening ingeniously covered with high grass. *In deze loopgraaf werden nog gevonden een [[Klewang|klewang]] en een patjol* — in this trench were found a *klewang* and a *patjol*, the tool with which the trench had been dug.

## Account in Hooijer's *De verlaten posten in Groot-Atjeh* ([[1897]])

The **patjol** was the broad hoe of the Malay archipelago, used by the Achehnese for cultivation and, in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], for the rapid construction of siege trenches.

## Use at Tjot Rang

[[G. B. Hooijer]] recorded that during the investment of [[Tjot Rang]] in [[April]] [[1896]], *op 75 Meter afstand van de benteng maakten de, in het omgaan met den patjol zoo bekwame Atjehers, een 50 meters lange loopgraaf waaruit met geweer en lilla de post onder vuur genomen werd* — at seventy-five metres' distance from the *benteng*, the Achehnese, so skilled in handling the *patjol*, made a trench fifty metres long from which the post was taken under fire with rifle and *[[Lilla|lilla]]*.

The observation is one of several in which Hooijer credited the Achehnese with practical field-engineering skill equal or superior to the Dutch.

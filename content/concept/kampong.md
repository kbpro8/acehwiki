**Kampong** — Malay for "village" — was the term for the village or village community of the Indies, the place that recruits left behind to enlist in the [[K.N.I.L.]]. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *kampong* is paired with the *[[negorij]]* — the home village of the Ambonese soldier on [[Ambon]] — and contrasted with the *[[tangsi]]* — the barracks that gave the *[[anak-kolong]]* its own *adat* and *sfeer*, distinct from the *kampong*.

## The kampong and the enlistment

"De bevolking vreemd staat tegenover de militairen, de noodzaak van hun vak niet begrijpt en zijn beschermers slechts beschouwt als geuniformeerde gewapenden, is het niet eenvoudig om voor beroepsdienst mensen van een behoorlijk gehalte te krijgen." For most of the Indies, joining the army was a *noodsprong* (a last resort) because it meant "het loslaten van de oude hechte kamponggemeenschap" — letting go of the old tight village community. "In [[Ambon]] en de [[Minahassa]] was dit volkomen anders. Daar was het militaire beroep in hoog aanzien en het dienstnemen volkomen normaal, zelfs een eervolle daad."

## The kampong and the tangsi

The *kampong* was contrasted with the *[[tangsi]]*. The *anak-kolong* — the child of the *[[kolong]]* — "konden de oude adatsfeer van de kampong missen, de tangsi gaf hen een eigen sfeer, een eigen adat, een eigen *stijl*…" The *anak-kolong* could miss the old *adat* atmosphere of the *kampong*; the *tangsi* gave them their own *sfeer*, their own *adat*, their own *stijl*. The *kampong* was therefore both the place the recruits left behind and the place the *anak-kolong* did not need: the *tangsi* replaced the *kampong*.

## The kampong and the decorations

"Ridders [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]], dragers van de [[Medaille voor Moed en Trouw]] en van het [[Kroontje (Eervolle Vermelding)|Kroontje]] (de Eervolle Vermelding), waren de trots van hun kampong of *[[negorij]]*." The M.W.O. Ridder was the pride of his *kampong* (or his *negorij*, in the Ambonese case). The *kampong* tracked the soldier's wedervaren through the gehele Archipel, and his decoration was worn at the *[[Koninginnedag]]* rondgang.

## The patrollie datang

"Patrollie datang!" — "the patrol comes (home)!" — was the cry of the *kampong* children at the *hek* (gate) of the *bivak* when a returning patrol was sighted. The *kampong* was therefore not only the place the recruits left behind but also the place that greeted the returning patrol: the *kampong* and the *bivak* were connected by the patrouille's return.

## Significance

The *kampong* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the village or village community of the Indies, the place that recruits left behind to enlist. The book's argument is that the *kampong* — for most of the Indies, the place that joining the army meant leaving — was, in [[Ambon]] and the [[Minahassa]], the place that took pride in its soldiers. The *kampong* is therefore the book's marker of both the loss (the leaving of the village community) and the pride (the village community's tracking of its soldiers) that the colonial army's recruitment entailed.

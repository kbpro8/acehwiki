The **Chinese sutler** was a recurring figure of the Dutch outpost life of [[Groot-Atjeh]], keeping a hut beside each post from which the garrison was supplied with small comforts, and described at length by [[G. B. Hooijer]] in his account of [[Toengkoeb]].

## At Toengkoeb

Outside the guard position on the high part beneath the flagstaff at [[Toengkoeb]] stood the hut in which the Chinese lived *die de bezetting voorzag van kleine geriefelijkheden en versnaperingen voor de maaltijden, als bier, tabak, pijpen, blikjes met verduurzaamde levensmiddelen, vruchten en *toespijzen* (side-dishes accompanying rice)* — who supplied the garrison with small comforts and delicacies for their meals: beer, tobacco, pipes, tins of preserved food, fruit and side-dishes.

Hooijer's portrait employs the racial caricature conventional in Dutch colonial writing of the period. He described the sutler standing in his dirty-white Chinese trousers behind baskets of anything but fresh wares, praising them in half-intelligible lisped pidgin Malay (*brabbel-Maleisch*) to the sun-roasted soldiers. As his countrymen had always done in the time of the Dutch marches into the deepest part of enemy territory, he risked *zijn parasietenleventje* for the sake of a small profit and a pipe of opium; with unsurpassed shrewdness he guessed the needs of his clientèle, and with unsurpassed zeal turned over his little capital of a few dollars and wore out the leather of his sandals fetching new stock for the dimes and cents received in exchange.

Hooijer noted that the hut lay outside the *[[IJzerdraadversperring|ijzerdraadversperring]]*, and was therefore anything but secure against a bold Achehnese marauder.

## At other posts

At [[Lam Soet]] the little house of the Chinese merchant lay outside the earthen rampart together with the bathroom. Chinese also appear in Hooijer's description of the river traffic at [[Anagaloeng]] under [[General van der Heijden]]: passing continually with soldiers and native women over the float-bridge, and manning the great Chinese barges that carried the sick down to the [[Kraton (institution)|Kraton]].

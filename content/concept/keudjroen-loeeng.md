**The Keudjroen-loeëng** — also written *Keudjroen loeëng*, *Keujruen lueng*, *Küdzhüën Lüëng* — was the Atjèhse office of irrigation official, charged with the supervision of the irrigation water (*loeëng*) distribution, which had existed long before the Dutch coming to Atjèh and which the [[De Rijkdom van Atjeh (1923)|1923 brochure]] of the [[Atjeh-Instituut|Atjeh-Instituut]] recommended to revive as the principal Atjèhse institutional vehicle for the improvement of the water distribution in the [[rijstbouw|rijstbouw]].

## The Pre-Dutch Office

In connection with better water distribution, the Atjeh-Instituut brochure recalls an office that already long before the Dutch coming to Atjèh had existed: that of the *keudjroen-loeëng*. The office was the principal Atjèhse institutional means of supervising the distribution of the irrigation water — the *loeëng* (the Atjèhse word for irrigation channel or watercourse). The office was, in the pre-Dutch period, in the gift of the *oeleebalang* (the Atjèhse district chief), and the holder of the office was responsible for the maintenance and distribution of the irrigation water in the *oeleebalang*'s territory.

## The Revival

To revive this institution, experiments had already been made, and indeed in [[Pidië|Pidië]], where for that office were chosen younger brothers or close relatives of landscape-heads. The experiment was only partially successful, and indeed because no schooled personnel could be disposed of. Only after those involved had worked for a couple of years with the [[Atjehsche Irrigatiedienst|Irrigatiedienst]] could something be expected of their appointment as *keudjroen-loeëng*; in Pidië, the rice-land par excellence, for example, three *keudjroen-loeëng* could be appointed — for the irrigation areas of the [[Pidië-rivier|Pidië-rivier]], the [[Kroeëng Tiro|Kroeëng Tiro]], and the [[Kroeëng Teupin-Raja|Kroeëng Teupin-Raja]].

## Significance

The *keudjroen-loeëng* is the Atjèhse office of irrigation official, charged with the supervision of the irrigation water distribution, which had existed long before the Dutch coming to Atjèh and which the Atjeh-Instituut brochure of [[1923]] recommended to revive as the principal Atjèhse institutional vehicle for the improvement of the water distribution in the rijstbouw. The revival of the office — in [[Pidië|Pidië]], the rice-land par excellence — was one of the principal Dutch-Atjèhse institutional experiments of the post-war period.

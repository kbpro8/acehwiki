**Bad' al-Amali** (Arabic *Badʾ al-Amālī*, Arabic script *بَدْءُ الأَمَالِ*, "the beginning of the dictations" or "the opening of aspirations") — also known as ***Kasīda al-Lāmiyya fil-tawwidd*** (in the older Dutch transcription; modern scholarly form *al-Lāmiyya fī al-tawḥīd*, Arabic *اللامية في التوحيد*, "the L-poem on [divine] unity") — is a short Arabic creed poem (*qaṣīda*) of fewer than seventy verses composed by [[Siraj al-Din Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Uthman al-Ushi|Sirāj al-Dīn Abū Muḥammad ʿAlī ibn ʿUthmān al-Ushī]] around [[569]] AH / [[1173]] CE. The poem's distinctive formal feature is that every verse ends with the Arabic letter *lam* (ل), giving the work its alternative name *al-Lāmiyya* ("the L-poem"). The poem was widely memorised and circulated across the Muslim world, including a Malay translation preserved in a sixteenth-century [[Aceh]]nese manuscript.

## Authorship and date

The *Bad' al-Amali* was composed by [[Siraj al-Din Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Uthman al-Ushi|Sirāj al-Dīn al-Ushī]] around [[569]]/[[1173]]. Al-Ushī was a Hanafi jurist and theologian of Central Asian origin, and the *Bad' al-Amali* is his best-known work, condensing the principal articles of Sunni creed into a verse form suitable for rote memorisation in the elementary curriculum of the *madrasah*.

## Textual features

The opening line — *yakūlu 'l-ʿabdu fī badʾi 'l-amālī* ("the believer says at the start of the dictation") — explains the title *Bad' al-Amali*. The poem's distinctive formal feature is that every verse terminates with the Arabic letter *lam* (ل), hence the alternative name *al-Lāmiyya* ("the L-poem") and the description of the work as "the L-poem on creed." The poem presents a concise theological statement of fewer than seventy verses, suitable for rote learning.

## Historical reception and commentaries

The poem was incorporated into Islamic curricula throughout the Muslim world and was the subject of numerous commentaries in Persian, Turkish, Hindustani, and other languages of the Islamic commonwealth (see [[Brockelmann]] for a catalogue of twenty-nine such commentaries). Its popularity extended to the [[Malay world]], where a sixteenth-century [[Aceh]]nese manuscript contains an interlinear Malay translation.

## Transmission to Aceh

An interlinear Malay translation of the *Bad' al-Amali* appears in a sixteenth-century [[Aceh]]nese manuscript (folios 31–35), demonstrating the poem's reach into Southeast Asia. The Malay version contains a greater proportion of Arabic lexical items than the contemporary Malay translation of the *[[Burda (poem)|Burda]]* preserved in the same manuscript tradition — a feature reflecting the creed's denser Arabic theological vocabulary and the translator's strategy of retaining key technical terms rather than paraphrasing them.

**Bad' al-Amali** is a short Arabic creed poem of fewer than 70 verses, composed by [[Siraj al-Din Abu Muhammad Ali ibn Uthman al-Ushi]] around [[569]]/[[1173]]. Each verse ends with the letter *I*, giving the work its alternative name *Kasida al‑Lamiyya fil‑tawwidd* (“the I‑poem on creed”). The poem was widely memorised and circulated across the Muslim world, including a Malay translation preserved in a 16th‑century Acehnese manuscript.

## Textual Features

The opening line “*yakalu ‘I‑cabdu ft bad’i ‘I‑amāli*” (“the believer says at the start of the dictation”) explains the title *Bad' al‑amaht*. The poem presents a concise theological statement, suitable for rote learning.

## Transmission to Aceh

An interlinear Malay translation appears in a manuscript (folios 31‑35) held in Aceh, demonstrating the poem’s reach into Southeast Asia. The Malay version contains a greater proportion of Arabic lexical items than the contemporary translation of the *[[Burda]]*.

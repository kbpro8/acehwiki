**The Buitenbezittingen** ("Outer Possessions") was the collective Dutch colonial designation for all the possessions of the Netherlands East Indies outside [[Java|Java]] and [[Madura|Madura]] — that is, for [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] (including [[Acheh|Atjeh]]), [[Borneo|Borneo]], [[Celebes|Celebes]], the [[Moluccas|Moluccas]], [[Bali|Bali]] and [[Lombok|Lombok]], [[Timor|Timor]] and the lesser Sunda islands, and the western part of [[New Guinea|New Guinea]]. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's volume *De Buitenbezittingen 1904 tot 1914* — the principal published Dutch survey of the outer possessions in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade — the Buitenbezittingen are presented as a coherent administrative and economic whole, brought under Dutch "effective authority" in the post-[[1896]] strategic reorientation whose originating event was the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Buitenbezittingen

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's volume is, in its own framing, a "sketch of the development of the Buitenbezittingen during the period [[1904]]–[[1914]], so important for those territories." The Bureau identifies the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]] as the originating event of the "ommekeer in de politiek ten aanzien van de Buitenbezittingen" (about-turn in policy toward the Buitenbezittingen) — an about-turn that, after the [[1902]] extension to [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]], would lead to the post-[[1902]] project of "complete subjection to our effective authority" across the outer possessions.

The Bureau's statistical tables of the Buitenbezittingen as a whole enumerate the following gewesten (regions) in [[1912]]: [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's West Coast]], [[Tapanoeli|Tapanuli]], [[Benkoelen|Bencoolen]], [[Lampongsche Districten|Lampong Districts]], [[Palembang|Palembang]], [[Djambi|Djambi]], [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]], [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]], [[Riouw en Onderhoorigheden|Riouw and Dependencies]], [[Banka en Onderhoorigheden|Banka and Dependencies]], [[Billiton|Billiton]], [[Westerafdeeling van Borneo|Western Division of Borneo]], [[Zuider- en Oosterafdeeling van Borneo|Southern and Eastern Division of Borneo]], [[Celebes en Onderhoorigheden|Celebes and Dependencies]], [[Menado|Menado]], [[Amboina en Onderhoorigheden|Amboina and Dependencies]], [[Ternate en Onderhoorigheden|Ternate and Dependencies]], [[Timor en Onderhoorigheden|Timor and Dependencies]], and [[Bali en Lombok|Bali and Lombok]]. The Bureau's population table records a total Buitenbezittingen population of approximately 11.4 million inlanders (natives), 81,000 Europeans, 611,000 Chinese, and 9,000 Arabs in [[1912]].

The Bureau's narrative of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade presents the Buitenbezittingen as the principal theatre of the Dutch colonial project in the early twentieth century — the theatre in which the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]], the [[1910]] centralisation of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]], the [[1913]] [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]], the [[1913]]–[[1914]] [[1913–1914 Administrative Reform Meetings (Sumatra)|administrative reorganization consultations]], and the [[1914]] [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] all took place. The Bureau's framing makes the Buitenbezittingen — and especially Atjeh — the principal locus of the Dutch colonial "modernisation" project of the early twentieth century.

## Significance

The Buitenbezittingen are, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal theatre of the Dutch colonial project in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade. The post-[[1896]] [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] is the originating event of the wider Buitenbezittingen turn; the post-[[1902]] extension of the Atjeh model to [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]] and other parts of the outer possessions is the colonial-wide translation of that model. The Bureau's volume is, in its own framing, the principal published Dutch survey of the Buitenbezittingen in this decisive decade.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**The Buitenbezittingen** — the "Outer Possessions" or "Outer Territories" — is the collective Dutch colonial term for the islands and regions of the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indische]] archipelago outside [[Java]] and [[Madoera]]: principally [[Sumatra]] (including [[Acheh|Atjeh]]), [[Borneo]], [[Celebes]], the [[Molukken|Moluccas]], the [[Lesser Sunda Islands]] (including [[Bali]] and [[Lombok]]), and the western half of [[Nieuw Guinea]]. The Buitenbezittingen are, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the principal theatre of the post-[[1901]] extension of Dutch effective authority — the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1904]], the expeditions to [[Djambi]] and [[Korintji]] in [[1901]], to [[Borneo]] in [[1904]], to [[Celebes]] in [[1905]], to [[Bali]] in [[1906]].

### The post-1901 consolidation

In the wake of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] of Atjeh, Dutch authority was consolidated elsewhere in the Buitenbezittingen: [[Djambi]] and [[Korintji]] in [[1901]], [[Borneo]] in [[1904]], [[Celebes]] in [[1905]], [[Bali]] in [[1906]]. The post-[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] consolidation was thus synchronous with the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] proclamation of [[Troonrede of 1901|1901]]. The 35-year [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] had demonstrated what was, in peacetime, not always grasped: that Dutch sovereignty over the Buitenbezittingen was, until the late [[19th century|nineteenth]]-[[20th century|twentieth centuries]], more nominal than effective.

### The Idenburg formulation

[[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] (a.r.), minister of Colonies, pronounced it [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s greatest merit that he had put an end to the Dutch feeling of impotence with respect to the Buitenbezittingen: "Hij heeft ons doen zien, dat wij, wanneer wij maar willen en onze middelen systematisch gebruiken, inderdaad ook kunnen bereiken wat wij ons tot taak hadden gesteld. Dat herwonnen zelfvertrouwen leidde er ons vanzelf toe om het ernstig te nemen met de plicht, welke ons als westerse mogendheid tegenover de Buitenbezittingen is opgelegd, het brengen van recht en orde".

### The zending context

The Buitenbezittingen were, in the [[1911 Indische begrotingsdebat|1911 begrotingsdebat]], cited by [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] as the region where [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s rule for *bizonder onderwijs* (confessional schools) was possible — "in enkele streken in de Buitenbezittingen, waar de zending vaste voet gekregen had" — in a few regions of the Buitenbezittingen, where the missions had gained a firm foothold. On [[Java]], by contrast, the Muslim majority lacked the organisation to act on its own initiative, and the rule was inapplicable.

### Significance

The Buitenbezittingen are, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the principal theatre of the post-[[1901]] extension of Dutch effective authority. The [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] of Atjeh is the central episode of this extension; the subsequent expeditions to [[Djambi]]-[[Korintji]] ([[1901]]), [[Borneo]] ([[1904]]), [[Celebes]] ([[1905]]) and [[Bali]] ([[1906]]) are its continuation.

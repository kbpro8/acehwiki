**The Sultan of Acheh** — the sovereign of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate — is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the political-moral object against which the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|1873 declaration of war]] of the [[Netherlands]] was directed. The office of the Sultan is the type-specimen, in the Atjehnese-war parliamentary record, of the independent non-European sovereign whose overthrow the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] justified on the three grounds of piracy, internal warfare and fear of foreign interference.

## The 1873 object

The [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war of 1873]] was, in the official *motivering*, directed against the [[Sultan of Acheh|sultan of Atjeh]] as the sovereign responsible for the alleged piracy emanating from Atjehese waters, the alleged prevalence of internal warfare within the sultanate, and the alleged foreign contacts (with the Italian and American consuls in [[Singapore]]) that threatened the Dutch position in [[Straat Malakka|Straat Malakka]].

## The Van Kol question

The most penetrating parliamentary questioning of the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|1873 declaration]] was that of [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) in the [[1897]] [[Tweede Kamer]] debate on credits for [[Nieuw Guinea]]: "Mogen wij dan voor datgene wat wij zelve niet kunnen tegengaan, een onafhankelijke vorst (de [[Sultan of Acheh|sultan van Atjeh]]) van de troon stoten en hem zijn land ontnemen?" — May we, for what we ourselves cannot suppress, depose an independent prince (the sultan of Atjeh) from his throne and take his land from him?

## The Sumatra-tractaat connexion

The [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Acheh]]'s sovereignty was, in international law, terminated by the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] — the Anglo-Dutch treaty by which [[Great Britain|England]] agreed that the whole of [[Sumatra]] (including the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate) would fall within the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] sphere of influence. The [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|1873 declaration]] was, in this sense, the diplomatic *ex post* execution of the [[1871]] treaty.

## Significance

The [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Acheh]] is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective, the type-specimen of the independent non-European sovereign whose overthrow the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] justified on the three grounds of piracy, internal warfare and fear of foreign interference. The office is the political-moral object against which the [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|1873 declaration of war]] was directed.

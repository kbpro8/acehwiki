**Atjeh up to date** was a comprehensive statistical and administrative compilation detailing the financial, military, and economic status of [[Acheh]] under [[Dutch East Indies|Dutch]] colonial rule, primarily covering data from [[1908]] to [[1914]]. The document serves as a quantitative snapshot of the colony during the latter stages of the [[Aceh War]] and the subsequent consolidation of Dutch authority, recording extensive figures on colonial budgets, export and import volumes, shipping tonnage, and military expenditures.

## Scope and Content

The compilation is characterized by its exhaustive tabular data, reflecting the bureaucratic priorities of the colonial administration in the early twentieth century. Rather than narrative history, *Atjeh up to date* focuses on the quantifiable metrics of governance and pacification. The surviving fragments of the document contain dense statistical tables tracking year-over-year changes across several key domains:

*   **Colonial Finance:** Detailed ledgers of revenues and expenditures, with total figures reaching into the millions of guilders (e.g., aggregate financial lines showing figures such as 4,169,072 and 5,206,526 for [[1908]], scaling significantly to over 12 million by [[1912]]).
*   **Trade and Shipping:** Monthly and annual registries of vessel arrivals, tonnage, and the valuation of imports and exports moving through Acehnese ports.
*   **Military and Pacification:** Statistics correlating with the deployment of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army|KNIL]], tracking troop movements, garrison costs, and the financial overhead of maintaining control over the Acehnese interior and coastal dependencies.

## Significance

As a primary source, *Atjeh up to date* provides historians with the empirical backbone for understanding the economic cost of the Dutch pacification of [[Sumatra]]'s northern tip. While narrative sources provide the political and cultural context, statistical compendiums from [[1908]]–[[1914]] reveal the sheer material scale of the colonial enterprise. The data illustrates the transition from active, costly military campaigning to a stabilized, revenue-generating colonial province, culminating in multi-year budget projections extending to [[1917]].

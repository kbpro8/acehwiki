***Onderkoning*** — viceroy — was the term used by [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] in the [[Tweede Kamer]] in [[1874]]\u2013[[1875]], and quoted by [[H. van Kol]] in [[1897]], for the Dutch authority in the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Indies]] whom he held to have been deceived into the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The quotation of [[1897]]

[[H. van Kol|Van Kol]], answering the Minister [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] on [[17 November]] [[1897]], argued that the pretext of foreign interference had been "een verfoeilijk kluchtspel," and cited Kuyper in the *Handelingen* of [[1874]]\u2013[[1875]], p. 1305: a "zot" (fool) and a "monomaan" (monomaniac) had, "door de inblazingen van een leugenaar" (through the promptings of a liar), made the *onderkoning* the "dupe" of a "loos alarm" (false alarm).

The passage belongs to Van Kol's argument that the official papers on the origin of the war, kept secret for eight years and published at the [[Landsdrukkerij]] in [[1881]], showed the fear of foreign intervention to have been manufactured — and that the *[[Instructie aan den Regeeringscommissaris naar Atjeh (1873)|instructie]]* given to the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] sent to Atjeh contained no mention of piracy at all, but only of that fear.

Kuyper for his part affirmed in the same debate that he took back no word of what he had argued in [[1874]] and [[1875]] about the *vitium originis* of the war, but denied that it was decisive for the position of [[1897]].

**Nederlands-Indië contra Japan** is the post-war Dutch general-staff history of the [[K.N.I.L.|Netherlands Indies Army]]'s military operations against the Japanese invasion of the [[Netherlands East Indies]] in [[1941]]–[[1942]], commissioned by the late Lieutenant-General [[S. H. Spoor|S. H. Spoor]] and produced by the Krijgsgeschiedkundige Afdeling (Military History Section) of the Generale Staf. Its sixth and final volume — *De strijd op Ambon, Timor en Sumatra* (1959), compiled by Colonels (ret.) C. van den Hoogenband and L. Schotborgh — contains, as its **Hoofdstuk V**, the most detailed published Dutch account of the **Battle of North Sumatra (Atjeh)** during the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]], and is the principal counter-partner to Dr. [[A. J. Piekaar|Piekaar]]'s *[[Atjèh and the War with Japan]]* in the Atjeh historiography of the [[Jappentijd (Japanese occupation, 1942-1945)|Jappentijd]].

## Publication and editorial frame

The work appeared in six numbered volumes between [[1953]] and [[1959]] from the Staatsdrukkerij- en Uitgeverijbedrijf in 's-Gravenhage, under the imprint of the Departement van Defensie / Hoofdkwartier van de Generale Staf. Each volume carries the sub-header "With an English Summary / Avec un extrait français / Mit einem deutschen Resümee," making the staff history partially accessible to non-Dutch readers. Volume VI — *De strijd op Ambon, Timor en Sumatra* — closes the series. Its compilers acknowledge in the Voorwoord that the original working title "De strijd in de Grote Oost en op Sumatra" was dropped because the Grote Oost also embraced Celebes, whose operations had already been treated in Deel V.

The compilers note that, owing to the destruction of contemporary records in [[1942]]–[[1945]], "the compilers were mainly dependent on reports drawn up from memory in [[1945]] and subsequent years." Despite a review by surviving commanders, "it cannot be ruled out that in some details inaccuracies occur." The cross-checking against the Algemene Indische Chronologie and Marine Chronologie Oost of the Rijksinstituut voor Oorlogsdocumentatie was performed by Kapitein ter zee b.d. A. G. Vromans.

## Chapter V — De strijd in Noord-Sumatra (Atjeh)

Hoofdstuk V, the Atjeh chapter, runs from page 113 to page 155 of Volume VI and is the volume's longest single theatre account. It is organised under five numbered sections:

1. **Inleiding** (Introduction) — a survey of the military-political development of Atjeh from the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] through the [[Bakongan uprising|Bakongan opstand]] ([[1925]]–[[1927]]), the [[Lhong action 1933|Lhong revolt]] of [[1933]], and the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]]'s residual features (including the [[atjeh-moord|Atjeh-moord]]).
2. **Overzicht van de ontwikkeling van de militaire toestand van 1 Januari 1940 tot het uitbreken van de oorlog in December 1941** — the rundown of troop reductions, reorganisation of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Korps Maréchaussee]] divisions, and the establishment of the *4de Garnizoensbataljon* as the [[Mobiele Reserve|„Mobiele Reserve"]].
3. **De gebeurtenissen na het uitbreken van de oorlog met Japan in December 1941 tot het uitbreken van de onlusten in Februari 1942**.
4. **De opstandige beweging vóór de Japanse landingen** — the pre-invasion Atjehnese resistance, including the [[F-beweging|F. beweging]], the [[murder of Tiggelman at Seulimeum|murder of controleur J. C. Tiggelman]] and the [[murder of Von Sperling|Rittermeister Von Sperling]].
5. **De operaties tegen de gelande Japanners** — the three Dutch *hoofdgroepen* under [[Lt-Col C. J. Bekkers|Bekkers]], [[Captain L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]] and [[Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Palmer van den Broek]].
6. **Bijzondere verrichtingen na de capitulatie** — the post-[[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|28 March 1942]] resistance of [[Lt H. van Zanten|lieutenant H. van Zanten]] and the [[safeguarding of the vaandel of the Korps Maréchaussee|safeguarding of the Korps Maréchaussee vaandel]] by [[Lt L. C. G. F. Onvlee|lieutenant L. C. G. F. Onvlee]].
7. **Nabeschouwing** — a sober retrospective on the inevitability of the defeat.

Throughout Hoofdstuk V the compilers refer the reader, "voor een uitvoerig overzicht op economisch, cultureel en politiek terrein," to Piekaar's *Atjèh en de oorlog met Japan* — a clear subordination of the political-administrative narrative to Piekaar and reservation of the staff history's own authority for the military account.

## Significance

The work is, after Piekaar, the second indispensable Dutch printed source for the Atjeh campaign of [[1942]]. Where Piekaar foregrounds the political, religious and social revolution that overtook the residency under the [[F-organisatie|F-organisatie]] and [[Poesa|Poesa]] impulse, the staff history foregrounds the operational, tactical and command story — the orders, the deployments, the desertions, the capitulations. The two works are complementary: Piekaar explains *why* Atjeh rose, while Van den Hoogenband and Schotborgh document *how* the colonial army was brought down. The staff history is also the principal source for the post-capitulation Dutch resistance of [[Lt H. van Zanten|lieutenant H. van Zanten]] and for the episode of the [[safeguarding of the vaandel of the Korps Maréchaussee|Maréchaussee vaandel]] — episodes that lie outside Piekaar's frame.

**Al‑Burda (Arabic poem)** is a 13th‑century Arabic ode composed by the Sufi saint [[Imam al‑Busiri]] that praises the Prophet [[Muhammad]] and seeks divine intercession. The poem became a central text of devotional recitation throughout the Islamic world, including the Acehnese archipelago, where it was translated into Malay and Dutch during the 16th century.

## Composition and Themes

Written in classical Arabic, the poem consists of 160 verses organized into ten sections, each extolling aspects of the Prophet’s life, miracles, and the believer’s yearning for mercy. Key theological concepts such as *achirat* (the afterlife) and *rasulullah* (the messenger of God) are prominently featured.

## Reception in Aceh

Acehnese scholars embraced the *Burda* as a model of piety, producing translations and commentaries that integrated local linguistic features. The Dutch‑Malay manuscript DLP‑54 (pages [[73]]‑[[79]]) preserves one such Malay translation, highlighting the poem’s influence on Acehnese religious poetry.

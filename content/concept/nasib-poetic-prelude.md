**Nasib (poetic prelude)** is the opening section of a classical Arabic *qasīda*, traditionally describing the poet’s reminiscence of a deserted campsite or lost love, thereby setting an emotional tone for the poem. In the Acehnese adaptation of the *kasida*, the *nasīb* retains its function of evoking past hardship or longing before the poet proceeds to the *raḥīl* (journey) and the *madīḥ* (praise).

Acehnese *bakāsidah* often incorporate local imagery—such as references to the *orang upake* (wild beasts) or the *jang menjertja* (critics)—within the *nasīb*, thereby blending the Arabic literary convention with Acehnese cultural motifs. The *nasīb* therefore serves both as a literary device and as a bridge between Arab and Acehnese devotional sensibilities.

### Features in Acehnese *kasida*
- **Reference to abandoned camps** – “sporen van verlaten kampeerplaatsen” (traces of abandoned camps) echo the classical motif.
- **Emotional tone** – The poet expresses yearning, loss, or anticipation, often linked to the Prophet’s virtues.
- **Local adaptation** – Inclusion of Acehnese flora, fauna, and social terms enriches the *nasīb* for a regional audience.

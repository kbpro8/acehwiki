**Al‑Burda (poem)** is a celebrated Arabic *qasīda* composed in the 13th century by the Egyptian scholar‑poet [[Al‑Busiri]]. The poem, whose title means “the mantle,” praises the Prophet Muhammad and has become a central text of devotional literature throughout the Islamic world, including the Acehnese tradition.

In Aceh the *Al‑Burda* is frequently recited during the **Mawlid** (*dikië jadjandji*) and incorporated into local *kasida* performances. Acehnese *bakāsidah* often render the poem in Acehnese translation or chant it in Arabic, linking the classical Arabic literary form to Acehnese religious practice. The poem’s structure—*nasīb*, *raḥīl*, and *madīḥ*—mirrors the format of the Acehnese *kasida*, reinforcing its suitability for local devotional use.

### Historical background
- **Authorship** – [[Al‑Busiri]] (d. [[1295]]) composed the poem in the 13th century CE, likely around [[1265]]–[[1270]] (exact date uncertain). He was a Berber‑origin calligrapher and scholar living in Bilbis, Egypt.
- **Transmission** – The poem spread across the Muslim world via manuscript copies; it reached Southeast Asia by the early modern period and was embraced by Acehnese scholars and poets.
- **Acehnese reception** – Acehnese manuscripts of the *Al‑Burda* appear in the collections of the Aceh Museum and the Leiden University Library, evidencing its long‑standing presence in the region.

**Wangsallan** — the Malay-Javanese rhyme-riddle — is the principal poetic instrument of *[[bedekte taal]]* (veiled language), and the form by which, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the chroniclers and romance-writers of the Malay-Javanese world concealed references to the older Hindu-Buddhist geography of [[Sumatra]]'s east coast and the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere beneath the surface of their texts. A *wangsalan* works by giving an intermediate word that rhymes with or echoes the hidden target: the listener who hears the intermediate word is expected to supply the target by association, while the foreign listener records the intermediate word as if it were a plain ethnographic name.

## Form

The *wangsalan* takes an intermediate word — often a common noun — whose sound recalls the hidden target. [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] gives many instances from [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]'s account of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]]: *ikan* "fish" on *hin-doe ka-ri*, "where Hindu population remained"; *batoe rendang* on the [[Batu|Batu]] islands; *Paneticano* on [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]]; *minha cumbaa* on the *minjak sembawa* of [[Pangkalan Soesoe]]. The riddle *geta angkat, koeran tiba* — explained by [[Klinkert|Klinkert]], [[Favre|Favre]] and others as something like "a beautiful time, whose loss one regretted too late" — De Roo de la Faille decodes as *getta angkat, Qor'an tiba*, "the royal throne broke open, the Quran fell from the sky", i.e. the fall of the realm of the Djao with the coming of Islam. The same form, applied to the [[Sadjarah Malajoe|Sadjarah Malajoe's]] [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] episode, identifies the [[Awi Ditjoe|menggombala ayam]] "tending chickens" as *menggembala Pasaj semajam*, "tending the like of Pasaj".

## Function

The function of the *wangsalan* is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, double. Among lettered Malays and Javanese, it allowed the chronicler to record matters of royal shame or ancestral displacement without exposing them to plain view — the point [[Pieter Willem van den Broek|Van den Broek]] had made about Javanese writers' use of *beeldspraak* (imagery) when they touched on the princely house, "because they were punished when they told the truth". The foreign listener, by contrast, was systematically misled: [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] records *Paneticano* as a place-name; [[Marco Polo|Polo]] records *Gauenispola* as a small island; [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]] records *Cernovem* as an inland city; and all three, De Roo de la Faille argues, were gulled by the same Malay *wangsalan*.

## Decoding and AcehWiki

For AcehWiki, the *wangsalan* is the operative form of *[[bedekte taal]]*: every entry that draws on [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s decoding of a chronicle place-name — [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] from *Paneticano*, [[Sjahroe'n Noewi]] from *Cernovem*/*Xarnauz*, [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] from *minha cumbaa*, etc. — rests on the recognition that the surface form is a *wangsalan* whose target is the older Hindu-Buddhist geography of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sphere. The recognition that the chronicle register is *wangsalan*-pervaded, rather than plain, is what permits the cross-identification of chronicle place-names with European travel record.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the *wangsalan* is the practical poetic form through which *[[bedekte taal]]* operates, and the single instrument by which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] connects the [[Sadjarah Malajoe]] and the [[Sadjarah Banten]] to the European travel accounts of [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]], [[Nicolo de Conti|De Conti]], [[Marco Polo|Polo]] and [[Pigafetta|Pigafetta]]. Its recognition is the methodological precondition for any reading of the chronicle geography of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the east-coast Sumatran polities.

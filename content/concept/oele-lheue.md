**Oelèë Lheuë** — modern *Ulee Lheue* — is the harbour at the mouth of the [[Atjehrivier|Atjehrivier]], immediately west of [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]], at which the Atjehrivier reaches the Indian Ocean after its course through the *vruchtbare vlakte van Groot-Atjeh*. In the [[De Buitenbezittingen, Deel II, Aflevering 1|Encyclopaedisch Bureau's Sumatra survey]] (1917), Oelèë Lheuë is the hydrographical outflow of the Atjehrivier and the principal haven of the colonial capital of the *gewest* Atjeh.

## Hydrography

The Bureau's Hoofdstuk I describes the outflow of the Atjehrivier at Oelèë Lheuë as follows: "Het Noordelijkste deel van het Westelijke lengtedal behoort geheel tot het stroomgebied van de Atjehrivier, welke tusschen de beide Westelijke Barisanketenen, door de vruchtbare vlakte van Groot-Atjeh, haar weg vindt naar zee om langs Koeta Radja bij Oelèë Lheuë den Indischen Oceaan te bereiken." Oelèë Lheuë is thus the *terminus* of the Atjehrivier's course through the Groot-Atjeh *kustvlakte*.

## Significance

Oelèë Lheuë is, in the Bureau's Sumatra survey, the hydrographical outflow of the Atjehrivier and the principal haven of the colonial capital. The Bureau's emphasis on the haven's situation at the mouth of the Atjehrivier — and on its proximity to Koeta Radja — frames Oelèë Lheuë as the principal Dutch haven of the Atjeh coast, and as the principal logistical connection between the colonial capital and the Indian Ocean shipping routes.

In the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] account of the [[Building of Oleh-leh harbor|building of the haven of Oleh-leh]] (the Dutch name for Oelèë Lheuë) and the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh spoorweg]] from Oleh-leh to [[Kotta-Radja|Kotta-Radja]], Oelèë Lheuë is the principal Dutch-built haven of the Atjeh coast, and the principal logistical connection between the colonial capital and the Indian Ocean shipping routes.

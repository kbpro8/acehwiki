**The M-organisatie** (English "M-organization," Dutch *M-organisatie*) was, in Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan*, the Japanese military-administration organisation for [[Atjèh|Atjèh]] under [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] — the formal apparatus through which the residency was governed from the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion]] of [[March]] [[1942]] until the [[Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945|Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945]]. The glossary of Piekaar's work defines the M-organisatie as the "Japanese military administration organization for Aceh, under Major (later Masubuchi-kikan Lieutenant-General) Masubuchi," distinguishing it sharply from its predecessor, the [[F-organisatie|F-organisatie]], which was an intelligence and fifth-column apparatus.

## Distinction from the F-organisatie

The M-organisatie arose out of the [[F-organisatie|F-organisatie]]: the [[Fujiwara-kikan|Fujiwara-kikan]] of [[Fujiwara Iwaichi|Fujiwara Iwaichi]], which had prepared the invasion, was transformed, after the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion]] of [[11 March|11]]–[[12 March]] [[1942]], into the apparatus of occupation. Whereas the F-organisatie's task was to destabilise the Dutch regime from within, the M-organisatie's task was to administer Atjèh on behalf of the Imperial Japanese Army. Under [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]]'s direction the fifth-column network was regularised: the governing commissions established by [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]] and the other F-propagandists were abolished, the *gun-chó* and *son-chó* system of native administration was installed, and the Japanese administrative hierarchy — at the head of which, as Resident of Atjèh (Jap. *Atjèh-shii-chókan*), stood Major-General (later Lieutenant-General) Lino Shazaburó — was set in place.

## Masubuchi's dual role

[[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] was the linchpin of the M-organisatie. From his initial positions as chief of police and private secretary (*hishokan*) to the Resident, he went on to head the Resident's Information Service (*senden-han*), the editorship of the *[[Atjèh Shimbun|Atjèh Shimbun]]*, and, successively, the departments of general affairs and of economic affairs. His "intuitive knowledge of the psyche of the Indonesian in general and of the Atjèhinese in particular" was, in Piekaar's judgement, decisive: "With great foresight, he knew how to keep the various groups in Acehnese society in balance." The popularity he thereby acquired was acknowledged in the honorary title "*bapak Atjèh*," which he bore in pro-Japanese circles.

## The final transformation

In [[July]] [[1945]], with an Allied invasion looming, [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] — released on [[3 July]] [[1945]] from the leadership of the economic affairs department — "took upon himself the task of creating a new 5th column organization, which shortly — in the event of an Allied invasion — would have to carry out its destructive work behind the Allied lines." [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]], [[Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali|Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali]], [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu'éh]] and [[Teukoe Moehamad Amin|Teukoe Moehamad Amin]] took leading parts in the new organisation, named by its founder "*[[Masubuchi-kikan|Masubuchi-kikan]]*." Piekaar records the symmetry: "The old F-organization would resume its task as M-organization." The intelligence function, having been transformed into the administrative function of the M-organisatie, was now duplicated in a fresh fifth-column structure under the same leader — a remarkable closing of the circle.

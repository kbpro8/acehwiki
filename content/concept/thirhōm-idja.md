The **thirhōm idja** (also *thithrōm idja*) — literally "one throw of the *idja*," the *slendang* or shoulder-cloth rolled up tightly and cast once — was the Atjehnese measure, reckoned at about twenty-five metres, defining the extent of the priority right which the possessor of a *[[Sawah|sawah]]* could assert over adjoining ground at the head of his field.

## The priority right

Possessors of rice fields could assert priority rights against a new clearer only over a piece of ground not destined for rice cultivation, situated at the *oeloe* — the head-end, the higher-lying part — of the *sawah*. The purpose of this recognition of priority was to reserve for the cultivator a place for a *veldhuisje* (a small dwelling in the field), a watch-house and the like.

## The measure

The reserve extended, with that purpose in view, over a maximum distance of one *thirhōm idja* — the distance of a single throw of the tightly rolled shoulder-cloth, that is twenty-five metres — reckoned from the *kepala* (head) of the *sawah*.

## Limitation: not beyond the ateueng

The priority right was granted only over ground not destined or to be destined for rice cultivation, and not over ground suitable for the extension of a *sawah* complex. It therefore did not apply to *sawah*'s situated in the middle of a complex. The Atjehnese expressed the limit thus: "dit recht wordt niet toegekend buiten de *[[Ateueng|ateueng]]*" — this right is not granted beyond the little rice-field dikes. [[D. W. N. de Boer]] explained that the phrase was self-evident to an Atjehnese, since an *ateueng* is a separation between a rice field and adjoining land of like kind, whereas no *ateuengs* are used where the *sawah* passes into a mountain slope, hilly terrain or *[[Ladang|ladang]]* ground.

## Revision in Sawang, 1917

In some districts the *[[Oeleebalang|oeléëbalang]]* revised the size of this reserve and at the same time fixed it in a more usual measure of length. Thus in [[Sawang]], *onderafdeeling* (subdivision) [[Lho' Seumawe|Lho' Seumaweh]], it was fixed from [[1917]] at only eight metres.

The **Inheemsche Hoofden** — the indigenous chiefs — were the local authorities with whose cooperation Dr. [[C. E. Gomperts]] selected the places of examination for his [[Oogheelkundig kampongonderzoek in Groot-Atjeh (Maart-December 1937)|eye survey of 1937]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## Role in the survey

In order to draw as large a number of children, blind persons and old people as possible into the survey, the place of examination was chosen "in samenwerking met de Inheemsche Hoofden" so that the *[[Kampong|kampongs]]* to be examined lay at most within a radius of one kilometre from it. Small outlying *kampongs*, Gomperts noted, necessarily fell outside this scheme.

## The wider administrative cooperation

The chiefs' cooperation formed part of the wider reliance of the medical service on the civil administration. Gomperts obtained his population figures from the *[[Binnenlandsch Bestuur|B.B.]]*, and attempted through its mediation an enquiry into the food supply of the surveyed districts — though this "bleek bij steekproeven niet geheel betrouwbaar" (proved on spot checks not entirely reliable), so that he gathered the usable and certain harvest data himself.

The fourth of his recommended measures for combating *[[Xerophthalmie|xerophthalmie]]* made the point general: at the *kampongonderzoek* by which the population was to be instructed about nutrition and xerophthalmia cases demonstrated to them, "kan de medewerking van het Binnenlandsch Bestuur niet worden gemist" — the cooperation of the Binnenlandsch Bestuur cannot be dispensed with.

**Manisee** — also *Manisé*, *Marsosé* — was the name by which the soldiers of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen]] knew themselves and were known by the Acehnese: the *Manisee* was the Ambonese Marechaussee in [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *Manisee* is the name that resounds in the *[[Marechausseelied]]* — "*Manisee-Manisee! 't Is terlaloe Manisee!*" — and the name that gives the corps its own identification, distinct from both the European soldiers and the Acehnese bevolking.

## The name and the refrein

The *refrein* of the *[[Marechausseelied]]* — "*Manisee-Manisee! 't Is terlaloe Manisee! / Hari-hari naik goenoeng, 't Is terlaloe Manisee!*" — gave the corps its *Manisee* name. The word *terlaloe* — glossed in the book as *verschrikkelijk* (terrible/excessive) — was the soldiers' own affectionate exaggeration: the Marechaussee was "terlaloe Manisee" — terribly/excessively Manisee. The name was therefore both self-identification and affectionate self-mockery: the soldiers who sang "*terlaloe Manisee*" were laughing at themselves even as they named themselves.

## The korpslied

The more formal *[[Korpslied van de Marechaussee|korpslied]]* used the variant *Marsosé*: "*Hai marsosé, hai marsosé… memboeroeh moesoeh, memboeroeh moesoeh th' tjapé-é, / naik-toeroen goenoeng, / masoek-kaloear rimba, / memtjari bekas dani anak marsosé!*" (Hunting the enemy relentlessly, mountain up and down, in and out of the bush, seeking traces of the marechaussee-son!) The *Marsosé* of the *korpslied* is the same name as the *Manisee* of the *Marechausseelied*: both are the soldiers' own name for themselves.

## The Manisee and the Acehnese

The Acehnese bevolking also knew the Marechaussee as *Manisee*. The name was therefore the bridge between the colonial army's own identification and the Acehnese identification of the colonial soldier: the *Manisee* was the Ambonese Marechaussee in Aceh, known by that name to himself, to his officers, and to the bevolking.

## Significance

*Manisee* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the name by which the soldiers of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] knew themselves. The book's argument is that the *Manisee* — the Ambonese Marechaussee in Aceh — was a type distinct from both the European soldiers and the Acehnese bevolking, and that the name *Manisee* was the soldiers' own identification of that type. The *refrein* of the *Marechausseelied* — "*Manisee-Manisee! 't Is terlaloe Manisee!*" — is the book's identification of the corps by its own idiom: the corps was what its soldiers sang.

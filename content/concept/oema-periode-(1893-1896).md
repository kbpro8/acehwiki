**The Oema-periode** of [[1893]]–[[1896]] — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s periodisation of the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]], the third and last phase of the *Zesde tijdvak* (sixth period, [[1884]]–[[1896]]), named for [[Teukoe Oema|Teukoe Oema]] — was the period in which the Atjèhnese resistance, freed from the military pressure of the Dutch offensive and emboldened by the [[Concentration system (1884-1896) — Kreemer's account|concentration policy]], came under the leadership of the *ulama* and of Teukoe Oema, and in which the Dutch position in [[Great Acheh|Groot-Atjèh]] reached its lowest ebb.

## The ulama ascendancy

Kreemer records that "Met de concentratie begon het tijdperk der oelama's, die toen de leiders van den oorlog werden; tot dien tijd nog eenigszins op den achtergrond gebleven, hadden zij nu tegenover een geheel onschadelijken, in de geconcentreerde stelling opgesloten vijand de meest gewenschte gelegenheid om zich krachtig te doen gelden. 'Wij staan niet meer tegen overmacht en dus is er ook geen reden meer om te berusten' — zóó verkondigden zij aan de bevolking" — "With the concentration began the period of the ulama, who then became the leaders of the war; until that time somewhat in the background, they now had, opposite a wholly harmless enemy locked up in the concentrated position, the most desirable opportunity to make themselves strongly felt. 'We no longer stand against superior force, and therefore there is no longer any reason to acquiesce' — so they proclaimed to the population."

## Teungku Tiro

[[Teungku Tiro|Teungkoe Tirö]] was, until his death in [[1890]], the leader par excellence. "Hij vormde een vast leger, ijverig liet hij overal de ha' sabi d.i. het voor den heiligen krijg bestemde deel der verschuldigde zakat invorderen. Hij liet onze geheele linie omringen door een kring van kleine zooveel mogelijk aan ons oog onttrokken bèntèng's, zoodat het er veel van had, of hij het was, die ons met kracht van wapenen ingesloten hield. Aan den vijand waren alle voordeelen van het initiatief in de hoogste mate verzekerd, thans was hij het, die offensief kon optreden, terwijl wij ons tot lijdelijke afweer moesten bepalen" — "He formed a standing army; zealously he had the *ha' sabi* — that is, the portion of the owed *zakat* destined for the holy war — collected everywhere. He had our entire line surrounded by a ring of small *benteng*s, concealed as much as possible from our sight, so that it looked much as if it was he who kept us enclosed by force of arms. To the enemy all the advantages of the initiative were secured in the highest degree; now it was he who could act offensively, while we had to confine ourselves to passive defence."

## The Oema-period

After [[Teungku Tiro|Teungkoe Tirö]]'s death in [[1890]], the resistance continued under Teukoe Oema and the other *ulama*. Kreemer labels the [[1893]]–[[1896]] phase — the last phase of the sixth period — the *Oema-periode*, after [[Teukoe Oema|Teukoe Oema]] whose prominence dated from his [[1884]] break with the Dutch (see [[Nisero affair (1883-1885)|Nisero affair]]). The period is characterised by the continued Dutch passivity within the concentrated line, the continued *ulama* leadership of the resistance, and the progressive moral and material strengthening of the war-party.

## Significance

The Oema-periode is, in Kreemer's narrative, the nadir of Dutch fortunes in the Atjèh war — the point at which the [[Concentration system (1884-1896) — Kreemer's account|concentration policy]] had produced all of its worst effects and none of its intended ones. The period ends with the appointment of [[Governor Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as governor in [[1896]], the adoption of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s advice to abandon the passive posture and to force submission by offensive action, and the beginning of the *Zevende tijdvak* (seventh period, [[1896]]–[[1910]]) — the period of "krachtige actie en beëindiging van den Atjèh-oorlog" — "forceful action and ending of the Atjèh-oorlog." The Oema-periode is thus both the lowest point of the Dutch war-effort and the immediate prelude to its final, successful phase.

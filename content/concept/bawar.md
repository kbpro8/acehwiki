**Bawar** is the Gayo term for the *ornamenten van waardigheid* — the insignia of chieftaincy — that the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] was said to have bestowed on [[Saëda (Gayo progenitor)|Saëda]], the legendary progenitor of the [[Kédjoerön Boekét|Kédjoerön Boekét]] line, and that constituted the physical embodiment of the *Kédjoerön*'s authority. *De Buitenbezittingen* treats the *bawar* as the constitutional touchstone of the Gayo chieftaincy: the principle that "wie de bawar heeft, ook kédjoerön is" — whoever holds the *bawar* is the *Kédjoerön* — defines the law of succession in the Boekét territory and shapes its political history.

## The five bawar

The legend of [[Saëda (Gayo progenitor)|Saëda]] — followed by *De Buitenbezittingen* as "de minst onbetrouwbare" of the competing Boekét traditions — records that the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] raised Saëda to the dignity of *Rédjë Boekét* and "gaf hem daarbij als teekenen zijner waardigheid een vijftal ornamenten (bawar)" — gave him at the same time, as marks of his dignity, a set of five ornaments (*bawar*). The most important of these was the *keris kepala soeasa* — the gold-headed kris — whose possession or loss would, in the subsequent history of the Boekét house, determine the constitutional standing of the rival lineages.

## The Ménèt seizure

The constitutional crisis of the Boekét house arose when *Ménèt*, the stepson of [[Saëda (Gayo progenitor)|Saëda]], contrived by a *list* — a stratagem — to seize the *keris kepala soeasa* from [[Mérah Lama|Mérah Lama]], Saëda's son by his second Acehnese wife, who had been installed as *Kédjoerön Boekét* in possession of the *bawar*. By the Gayo principle that "wie de bawar heeft, ook kédjoerön is," *Ménèt* claimed the dignity of *Kédjoerön*. The dispute persisted until *Ménèt*'s refusal to pay the *haol* (tribute) due to the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] provoked the *prang toedjéh tahoen* — the War of Ten Years — which ended with the capture of many *rédjë*'s of the *Meerstreek* by the Acehnese. The ransom demanded for these chiefs included the surrender of the *keris kepala soeasa*; *Ménèt*, forced to comply, lost the principal *bawar* and, with it, his effective claim to the *Kédjoerön* title.

## The Panglima Dagang recovery

The resolution came from [[Panglima Dagang|Panglima Dagang]], Saëda's younger son by his second wife, who had settled in [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]]. Hearing of the matter, he returned to the *Meerstreek* and demanded from *Mérah Lama* the remaining *bawar*; he then succeeded in buying back the captured kris from the *Atjehsche aanvoerder* (Acehnese commander), thus reuniting all the *bawar* in one hand. He settled at *Seroelë* and assumed the title *Kédjoerön Boekét*. *Ménèt*'s forfeiture was complete. The descendants of *Panglima Dagang* — the *Boekét Lah* lineage — held the office of *Kédjoerön Boekét* thereafter; the descendants of *Ménèt* — the *Boekét Ewéh* — nevertheless continued to use the title and to exercise many of its functions.

## Constitutional significance

The *bawar* principle — that the physical regalia constitute the title, not the descent — is the distinctive constitutional feature of the Gayo chieftaincy. It allowed the *bawar* to be transferred, pawned, or seized in ways that a purely genealogical title could not, and it gave to the *keris kepala soeasa* a constitutional weight that the Dutch, when they extended recognition to the *Boekét Lah* line, were careful to respect.

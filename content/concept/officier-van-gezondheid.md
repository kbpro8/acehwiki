**The officier van Gezondheid** — officer of health — was the medical officer of the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Netherlands Indies Army]], attached to the larger garrisons and posts in [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

[[P. H. R. Beuming]] records that on the large posts of the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]* an *officier van Gezondheid* was stationed and a sick-room was found, and that most overseas posts and main *[[Benting|bentings]]* likewise had sick-rooms and a medical officer.

## In action

The medical officer's conduct under fire supplies one of Beuming's set pieces. In the [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of 7 March 1896]] the officer of health 2nd class [[C. van der Meer]], while bandaging the wounded, was struck by a shot in the head and fell unconscious; scarcely returned to consciousness, *wijdde deze kranige officier van Gezondheid zich onverwijld weder aan zijn taak en verbond de gewonden, zonder zich over zijn eigen wond te bekommeren* — this gallant officer of health devoted himself again without delay to his task and bandaged the wounded without troubling about his own wound. Beuming calls it *voorwaar een toonbeeld van opofferende naastenliefde*, truly a model of self-sacrificing charity.

After the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Anak Galoeng]], with four officers and twenty-nine other ranks wounded, *de officier van Gezondheid had de handen vol werk en allen, die niet gewond waren, hielpen mede verbinden* — the officer of health had his hands full and all who were not wounded helped to bandage. At [[Lok-Nga|Lho-Nga]] on [[25 September]] [[1897]] the wounded of the [[Patrol to the Goeah Teroes on the Glé Betoes (25 September 1897)|Goeah Teroes patrol]] were brought into the sick-room and treated at once by the officer of health.

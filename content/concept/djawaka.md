**Djawaka** — also **Jawaka**, **Djawa-ka** — is the term [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] uses for the Hindu-Javanese population of Sumatra's east coast, the people whose ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] was, in his reading, the original "Java" of the chronicle tradition — the source from which the [[Kalang|Kalang's]] of Java were brought as war-booty, and the cultural complex that the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] overwrote but did not erase. The Djawaka are the principal ethnonym of the *bedekte taal* reading of Sumatran-Javanese historical geography.

## Origin and the *lang-ka* etymology

The term *Djawaka* [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *djawa-ka lang-ka* — "Javanese of *lang-ka*", i.e. of [[Taroema|the lang-ka country]] on the Sumatran Overwal. The same ethnonym, in his reading, underlies the *Jaoa* of the Portuguese (i.e. of [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]]), the *Djaouah* of [[Ibn Battuta|Ibn Battuta]], the *Jaoa* of [[Pigafetta|Pigafetta]] — all references, De Roo de la Faille argues, not to the great island of Java but to the Sumatran east-coast Hindu-Javanese complex that produced the [[Kota Djawa|Javanese fortification]] at [[Deli]], the [[Madjapahit in Langsar|Madjapahit of Langsar]], and the *Djawa* of [[Batu Bara|Batu-Bara]] on Sumatra.

## The Kalang connection

The [[Kalang|Kalang's]] of Java — the people whom the [[Sadjarah Banten|Sadjarah Banten]] and the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] record as Javanese but not "real" Javanese — De Roo de la Faille identifies as Djawaka of *lang-ka*, brought to Java as war-booty from the Sumatran Overwal. The [[Rembang|Rembang]] region of Java's north coast, in this reading, takes its name from the *Ari-ke-mbang* of the Sumatran Overwal — the same place-world that the [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Soenan Kalidjaga]] legend encodes as the *pasar* at which [[Soenan Bonang]] converted [[Raden Sahid]].

## The Astina of Sumatra

[[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] further identifies the Djawaka with the ***Astina*** of the Javanese legend in [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles]]'s *History* — the realm of [[Djaja Baja|praboe Djaja-Baja]], whose name (*h-asti-na-ma*, "elephant") changed on the dynasty's extinction to *Enj°-* or *Guj'rat* — a *bedekte taal* form for the dead elephant-land of [[Bali-Atjé|Bali-Atjé]] on the Sumatran Overwal. The "elephants of war" that [[Vasco da Gama|Vasco da Gama]]'s *Roteiro* records for *Xarnauz* — "the king has 400 elephants of war" — De Roo de la Faille reads as confirmation of this identification.

## Decadence and the Atjehnese age

After the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] in [[1539]], the Djawaka complex was reduced to "poverty and piracy", so much so that in the colonial period only a "*soekoe Aroe*" — an Aroe clan — was remembered. The [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend of [[Deli]] — the fairy-tale queen whose rejection of the [[Sultan of Atjeh]]'s marriage suit ended in her death and the loss of her glass coffin at sea — is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the folk record of the Djawaka's absorption by the Atjehnese age.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Djawaka is the principal ethnonym of the *bedekte taal* reading of Sumatran-Javanese historical geography — the people whose ancestral land of [[Taroema]] at [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] underlies the chronicle tradition's "Java", and whose absorption by the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]] marks the close of the Hindu-Javanese east-coast age and the beginning of the Atjehnese maritime age.

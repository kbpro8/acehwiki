**Kapoer baroer** — Malay ***kapur barus***, Dutch ***kamfer***, English **camphor of Barus** — is the crystalline camphor (*Dryobalanops aromatica*) of [[Barus|Barus]] on the west coast of [[Sumatra]], the great west-coast emporium whose product gave its name both to the Malay *kapur* ("lime, chalk") — by extension, the *bahasa kapoer* ("lime-language") of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]] — and to the office of the [[Alleredin Mahomet|Gouverneur du Royaume de Baarros]] in the [[Atjehsche vloot|Atjehnese fleet]] of [[1539]]. It is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the principal trade-good whose name underlies the *bedekte taal* register of Malay-Javanese historical geography.

## The product

***Kapoer baroer*** — crystalline camphor of the *Dryobalanops aromatica* tree — was, from antiquity, the principal product of [[Barus|Barus]] on the Sumatran west coast, and the great west-coast emporium's claim to fame. The Bataks, [[G. P. Rouffaer|Rouffaer]] noted (citing the *Encyclopaedie van Nederlandsch-Indië*), called [[Barus|Barus]] *Djaoe roema* — which Rouffaer read as "Maleier, eigenlijk Javanenwoning" (Malay, properly Javanese-dwelling), but which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as the *Djaoe-wa Ta-roema* of the Sumatran Overwal — i.e. as the *Taroema* of the chronicle tradition, the ancestral land of the [[Djawaka]].

## The Gouverneur du Royaume de Baarros

[[Alleredin Mahomet]], the commander of the [[Atjehsche vloot|Atjehnese fleet]] of [[1539]], held, [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] records, the office of "Gouverneur du Royaume de Baarros" — i.e. governor of [[Barus|Barus]] on the Sumatran west coast. The combination of fleet command, royal kinship, and the governorship of [[Barus|Barus]] marks, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the breadth of the [[Sultan of Acheh (16th century)|sultan of Atjeh]]'s design in [[1539]]: not merely to chastise a single neighbour but to bring the whole circuit of Sumatran coast, west and east, under Atjehnese command — and so to close the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] to Portuguese shipping.

## The *bahasa kapoer*

The "lime" (*kapoer*) of [[Barus|Barus]] camphor is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, the *kapoer* of the ***bahasa kapoer*** — the "lime-language" of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]] that [[Marco Polo|Marco Polo]] noted as the "peculiar language" of the east-coast places he visited. The same *kapoer* underlies the *kapoer* of the *Melasaké pampang|Melasaké pampang]] allegory — the "lime" of the allegorical tree whose roots stood in the air — and the *kapoer* of the *kapoer-gala-gala* caulking of Pati Oenoes's fighting-junk of [[1513]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, *kapoer baroer* is the principal trade-good whose name underlies the *bedekte taal* register of Malay-Javanese historical geography — the camphor of [[Barus|Barus]] that gave its name to the *bahasa kapoer* of [[Malajoe|Malajoe]], to the [[Atjehsche vloot|Atjehnese fleet]]'s governorship, and to the *kapoer* of the [[Melasaké pampang|Melasaké pampang]] allegory. Its identification with the *Taroema* of the chronicle tradition also marks it as the principal west-coast landmark of pre-Atjehnese Sumatra in Arabic, Chinese and European trade record.

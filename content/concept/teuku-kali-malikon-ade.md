> **Note on nomenclature.** The title is variously spelt in the sources as *Teuku Kali Malikon Ade*, *Teuku Malikon Ade*, *Kali Malikon Ade*, and *Kali Malikul-adil*. The Achehnese forms *Malikon Ade* / *Kali Malikul-adil* render the Arabic *Qāḍlu Malikul ʿĀdil* ("Judge of the Righteous King"). The prefix *Teuku* borne by the title-holder points unequivocally to the complete secularisation of the office by the late nineteenth century. All forms denote the same hereditary dignity — the chief judicial office of the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] — and the same lineage of bearers descended from the [[Ja Sandang|Ja Sandang]] tribe. The article treats the title and lineage as a single entity.

**Teuku Kali Malikon Ade** — Achehnese form of the Arabic *Qāḍlu Malikul ʿĀdil*, "Judge of the Righteous King" — was the hereditary title of the chief judicial officer of the [[Aceh|Sultanate of Aceh]], originally the president of the royal court of justice, but which in the course of two and a half centuries so completely deteriorated that by the late nineteenth century its holder was no judge at all, but an *uleebalang* of twelve *gampongs* on the right bank of the [[Acheh River|Acheh River]], a court dignitary, and a master of ceremonies at the Sultan's court. As [[Prof. Niemann|Prof. Niemann]] noted, *Malikul Adil* is a common title for *qadhis* in Malay countries. The most important religious-administrative official in the capital of Aceh, the Chief Kali, whose office adjudicated inheritance, marriage, and other religious law matters, the Teuku Kali was among those who persuaded [[Cheh Marahaban]] to settle at the capital after his return from [[Mecca]], and was a leading figure in the early phases of the [[Aceh War]], appearing in the [[Hikayat prang Kompeuni|Hikayat prang Kompeuni]] as a chief speaker in the council held in the [[Dalam]] when Dutch ships first arrived demanding submission.

## Origin and the Ja Sandang Tribe

The institution of the office belongs to the period — perhaps the reign of [[Sultan Meukuta Alam|Meukuta Alam]] or an earlier sovereign — when the centralising port-kings of Aceh were seeking to organise the administration of justice on a footing more consonant with Islamic law. The original intention of the powerful port-king who instituted the title was, according to [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], to have a president of the royal court of justice possessed of an adequate knowledge of the religious law. Some Acehnese chiefs assert that the wish of the creating prince was that his *kali* should apply the *adat* (customary law) as well as the *hukom* (religious law), and on this account gave him, in contradistinction to the *Kali Rabon Jale* ("Judge of the Almighty Lord") of the three *sagis*, the title of "Judge of the Righteous King." Whether the sultan wished at the same time to endow his own judge with a measure of supremacy over the other three chief *kalis* remains uncertain; in practice, any such scheme miscarried, since the great independence of the three *sagis* would have resisted it.

The Sultan "chose his court judge from the tribe of Ja Sandang, which had meantime greatly increased in dignity. This official received the title of Kali Malikul-adil (Malikon Ade) and the judicial office or at least the title appertaining to it remained hereditary in his family." The choice fell upon [[Ja Sandang|Ja Sandang]], according to the legendary account preserved in the chronicles, because an ancestor of that tribe — a man of humble rank from [[Lam Panaih|Lam Panaih]] in the [[XXII Mukims|XXII Mukims]] — had once assuaged the Sultan's thirst with milk in a bamboo vessel carried *sandang*-wise on his shoulder, and had been promised in return the favour of the court. The original holder of the title was thus the first of a long line of hereditary judges and court dignitaries whose descendants, in Snouck Hurgronje's day, still bore the secularised dignity of Teuku Kali (or Teuku Kali Malikon Ade) at the [[Dalam|Dalam]].

## Deterioration of the Office

The office very quickly deteriorated. The causes were, first, the hereditary nature of the office, which did not ensure that the heir should be as learned as his predecessor; secondly, the residence of the holder in the immediate neighbourhood of the court, by which he gained an opportunity of having his office converted into a sort of *uleebalangship* — the end and aim of all Acehnese office-bearers; and lastly, the rapid decay of the central power, by which various offices created during the brief period of prosperity lost the reason for their further existence.

Even in the later royal edicts, which always more or less idealised the facts, the Kali Malikon Ade appears not as a great jurist but as a distinguished courtier, a sort of master of court ceremonies to whom a fixed portion of the harbour dues was assigned. He became what was called *uleebalang peuteueng*, "uleebalang of our lord" (the sultan), equal in rank to the almost independent provincial chiefs, with all the worldly pretension appertaining to that rank, but without territory. The official title of *Teuku* which he bore and still bears points unequivocally to the complete secularisation of his office. Writers on Aceh had accustomed readers to the idea that, above the *kalis* of the *uleebalangs* and of the *panglima sagi*, there was enthroned in the capital a supreme hereditary judge who bore this title; Snouck Hurgronje argues that this notion rested largely on the exaggerated self-description of the then holder of the office, whom [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. T. H. Der Kinderen]] had taken as his chief guide, and that the actual position was far more modest.

## Acquisition of Territory and Rivalry with Panglima Meuseugit Raya

The ambitious efforts of the Teuku Kali Malikon Ade towards the acquisition of territory were eventually crowned with success. Finally one of the holders of this title, making use of the special favour of the prince towards him and of the weakness of the then [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya|Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] (the hereditary head of the 24 or more *gampongs* on either side of the Acheh River, in the neighbourhood of the court and principal mosque), succeeded in wresting from that chief half of his territory — twelve *gampongs* on the right bank of the [[Acheh River|Acheh River]] adjoining the [[Dalam]].

Thus the Teuku Kali attained the acme of the wishes of an Acehnese official: chief of a small but important territory, and at the same time, in his capacity as a court dignitary, not without influence in the choice of a successor to the throne. How little these two *uleebalangs* could be recognised as simply servants of the sultan is shown by the fact that they occasionally made war on one another; the sultan helped first one and then the other with money and munitions of war, and when a dispute arose as to the succession to the throne, the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] and the Teuku Kali Malikon Ade usually espoused opposite sides, with the sultan's own followers to be found in both camps.

## Court Ceremonial and Insignia

In his capacity as master of court ceremonies, the Teuku Kali Malikon Ade was on terms of greater intimacy with the royal family than the rest of the *uleebalangs*. During the great feasts the *uleebalangs* in the neighbourhood of the [[Dalam|Dalam]] together with some distinguished *ulamas* used to wait on the sultan and be presented with articles of dress; the Teuku Kali Malikon Ade alone used to come and present his felicitations on the actual feast day, when he, as well as the members of the Sultan's family and his household servants, received a share of the royal slaughter.

At the solemn installation of a new sultan the Kali Malikon Ade performed his functions within the space surrounding the *branda seumah*, the coronation platform, with his blunderbuss hanging from his shoulder and a white cocoanut spathe wound in his head-cloth. The bearers of the title "gradually acquired the positions of masters of ceremonies at the Court. Those who have seen them in the exercise of their functions at the installation of a new Sultan, can testify that on such occasions the Teuku Kali wore a white cocoanut spathe under his head-cloth, and a blunderbuss attached to a leathern belt passing over his shoulder. The reason given for adhering to this *adat* is that the titular 'judge' appeared also in the character of the representative of his tribe the Ja Sandang. As, however, customs change with lapse of time, the later Sultans had permitted the holders of this office to exchange the bamboo vessel for a blunderbuss and to hide the cocoanut spathe to some extent beneath their head-cloth." The insignia of the office thus preserved the memory of its popular origin in the milk-carrying ancestor from Lam Panaih.

## Snouck Hurgronje's Critique of Der Kinderen

Snouck Hurgronje observes that [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. Der Kinderen]] quite missed the mark in finding anything extraordinary in the fact that the "hereditary supreme judge" of the kingdom could neither read nor write. The Teuku Kali was *uleebalang* of twelve *gampongs*, and shared this ignorance with many of his equals in rank; those who were charged with administering justice according to religious law could all read and write, and most had carried their learning much further. Snouck uses the "wholly unlettered" Kali Malikon Ade of his own day as evidence against the romantic picture of an Aceh once governed by learned jurists, observing that the holder shared the prevailing ignorance of Mohammedan law common to most chiefs.

Even in the rare cases where the Sultan of Aceh had need of the advice of those learned in the law, his real adviser was [[Cheh Marahaban]] and not the Teuku Kali. In [[Teungku Tiro (Muhamat Amin)|Teungku Tiro]]'s pamphlet to the Dutch Government, the Teuku Kali is named among the four — Teuku Kali, [[Teuku Ne' of Meura'sa|Teuku Ne' of Meura'sa]], the [[Panglima Meuseugit Raya]] and the [[Imeum of Lueng Bata]] — on whose counsel the Sultan was obliged to depend. The pretensions to supremacy over the *Kali Rabon Jale* of the three *sagis* were, however, the fruit of the misleading information given by a later holder of the title to [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. T. H. Der Kinderen]] in [[1881]].

## Role in the Aceh War

The Teuku Kali Malikon Ade was a leading figure in the early phases of the [[Aceh War|Aceh War]]. He appears in the [[Hikayat prang Kompeuni|Hikayat prang Kompeuni]] as a chief speaker in the council held in the [[Dalam|Dalam]] when Dutch ships first arrived demanding submission. He was also among those who persuaded [[Cheh Marahaban]] to settle at the capital after his return from [[Mecca]].

## Significance

The decay of the central power in [[Aceh]] thus converted an ancient judicial office into a sort of *uleebalangship* — the end and aim of all Acehnese office-bearers — furnishing a classic example of the degeneration of offices remarked by Snouck Hurgronje throughout the institutions of the sultanate. The founder of the line — whoever he was — thus became, in the course of two and a half centuries, the ancestor of an hereditary dignity at once more worldly and less learned than the office of his first institution.

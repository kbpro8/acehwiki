**Kawöm** (also *soekèë*; Arabic *qaoem*) was the old clan (*geslacht*) of [[Aceh|Atjèh]], encompassing all descendants of one founding father (*stamvader*) in the male line. Originally, the Atjèhnese population was divided into four *kawöm*'s (tribes), whose members had common rights and duties especially for *[[bila|bila]]* (blood revenge) and *[[diët|diët]]* (blood money).

## The four kawöm's

The four original Atjèhnese kawöm's were:
1. **Lhèë reutöïh** ("the Three Hundred") — federated with two other kawöm's against the dominant Imeum peuët; has contributed to the Oelèëbalang families and holders of high offices
2. **Dja Sandang** (or Tjoet- or To' Sandang) — the sagi-head [[Panglima Pölém|Panglima Pölém]] of the [[XXII Moekim's|XXII Moekim's]] belongs to this kawöm
3. **Dja Batèë** (or To Batèë) — federated with Lhèë reutöïh and Dja Sandang against the Imeum peuët
4. **Imeum peuët** ("the Four Imeum's") — the most numerous and powerful kawöm; the other three federated against it. Marriage between the federated three and the Imeum peuët remained prohibited. No member ever rose above the modest rank of moekim-head.

## Panglima kawöm

The head chosen by members of one kawöm living together in a kampoeng to manage their common interests was called the **panglima kawöm** — especially for *bila* (blood revenge) affairs. The panglima kawöm was confirmed by the [[Oelèëbalang|Oelèëbalang]], who gave him a set of clothes and weapons (*seunalén*). A territorial head was never chosen as panglima kawöm.

## Decline

By Kreemer's time, the kawöm system was largely extinct, with only the name remembered. The best-preserved memory was in the [[XXII Moekim's|XXII Moekim's]], where the sagi-head [[Panglima Pölém|Panglima Pölém]] still belonged to the Dja Sandang kawöm. The *Dajasche landschappen* — partly populated from the XXII Moekim's — still had all four kawöm's represented, with the Imeum peuët by far the most numerous.

## Tjo ripè

The **tjo ripè** was the collection of contributions from kawöm-genooten (clan members) to pay fines imposed by the administration, but above all to pay *diët* (blood money) — the non-bloody form of revenge for manslaughter or wounding.

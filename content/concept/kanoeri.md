**Kanoeri** (also *kandoeri*; from South Indian term; Arabic *walîmah*) was the Atjèhnese **religious meal** held at marriage and other important events. Consecrated by *meuhatam* (Quran recitation) or *liké* (litanies), the *kanoeri* was named by occasion and served both religious and social functions.

## Occasions

The *kanoeri* was held on many occasions, named accordingly:
- **Kanoeri oeröë Ihèë** — 3rd day after death
- **Kanoeri oeröë limöng** — 5th day
- **Kanoeri oeröë toedjöh** — 7th day (also *k. toengkoe* because grave stones are then planted)
- **Kanoeri oeröë siplöh** — 10th day
- **Kanoeri ka tö' oeröë** — 44th day
- **Kanoeri tö' thön** — year-day (annual commemoration)
- **Kanoeri djeurat** — small religious meal held at the grave after the burial

## Annual kanoeri's

Several annual *kanoeri*'s were held at saints' graves:
- **Kanoeri Teungkoe Andjöng** — three annual kanoeri's at the grave of [[Teungkoe Andjöng|Teungkoe Andjöng]] in [[Gampöng Djawa|Gampöng Djawa]]: on 12 Mö'löt (birth/death), on 18th of apam-month (for his wife Adja Eseutiri, d. [[1820]]), and on 14th of Poeasa (death date)
- **Kanoeri apam** — annual religious meal in the 7th Muslim month, where each household bakes *apam*-koekjes to bring to the mosque/meunasah, said to be for the souls of ancestors
- **Kanoeri boh kajèë** — "fruits kanoeri" in the 6th Muslim month
- **Kanoeri boe** — in the 8th Muslim month, dedicated to remembrance of the dead

## Agricultural and fishery kanoeri's

- **Kanoeri blang** (also *kanoeri oelèë loeëng* / *kanoeri seuneulhöb*) — agricultural offermeal at the beginning of sawah-work
- **Kanoeri la'öt** — sea-kanoeri at the beginning of the fishing season; after it, fishing at sea was *pantang* (taboo) for 7 days

## Kanoeri toela bala

The **kanoeri toela bala** was an offermeal to ward off general disasters, held at the sawah, beach, riverbank, or kampoeng entrance. In Gayo three meals were given: in each house, on each house's forecourt, and outside the kampoeng downstream. A buffalo head was sent floating down the river to carry away the disease-spirits.

## Consecration

The *kanoeri* was consecrated by *meuhatam* (Quran recitation) or *liké* (litanies). Only *leubè*'s or *oelama*'s could properly perform the *tahli* (the first half of the confession of faith, *la ilaha illa 'llah*) that accompanied the kanoeri for the benefit of a deceased.

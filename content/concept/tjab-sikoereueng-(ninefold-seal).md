**The Tjab Sikoereuëng** — literally "the ninefold seal" (*tjab* = seal, *sikoereuëng* = nine) — was, in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the ninefold royal seal (*rijksstempel*) of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Atjèh sultanate]], said by tradition to have been introduced by [[Iskander Muda|Sultan Iskandar Muda]] ([[1607]]–[[1636]]) as the official state seal of the sultanate. The seal is, in Kreemer's account, one of the principal institutions attributed to Iskandar Muda in the Atjèhnese tradition of *adat meureuhom* — the "custom of the deceased Majesties" — by which the ordinances and arrangements of the sultanate were ascribed to its greatest ruler.

## The attribution to Iskandar Muda

Kreemer records the introduction of the seal in a single clause within his account of Iskandar Muda's reign: "Onder de Soeltan's aan wie men gewoon is al wat gewoonterecht is geworden — *adat meureuhom* (lett.: adat der overleden Majesteiten) — en allerlei *sarata* (vorstenedicten) toe te schrijven neemt Iskandar Moeda een eerste plaats in. Zoo wordt door de overlevering aan hem ook toegeschreven een stel voorschriften omtrent de bestuursregeling, het hofceremonieel en den handel, bekend onder den naam van [[Adat Meukuta Alam|Adat Meukoeta Alam]]. Verder zou hij de invoerder zijn geweest van de z.g. *tjab sikoereuëng* d.i. het negenvoudig zegel als rijksstempel" — "Among the Sultans to whom one is accustomed to ascribe all that has become customary law — *adat meureuhom* (lit.: the adat of the deceased Majesties) — and all kinds of *sarata* (princely edicts), Iskandar Muda takes a first place. Thus tradition ascribes to him also a set of regulations concerning the arrangement of government, court ceremonial, and commerce, known by the name of Adat Meukuta Alam. Further, he is said to have been the introducer of the so-called *tjab sikoereuëng*, that is, the ninefold seal as state-stamp."

## The nine and the Atjèhnese institutional preference

Kreemer's mention of the ninefold seal echoes the wider Atjèhnese preference for institutional groupings of specific numbers — a preference documented elsewhere in the wiki for the number four (see [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|the Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]]) and the number three (see [[sagi-system|the three sagi's]]). The *nine* of the Tjab Sikoereuëng may reflect the influence of the nine *adat*-holders or the nine signatories of sarakata documents in the later sultanate, though Kreemer does not develop this connection.

## Significance

The Tjab Sikoereuëng is, in Kreemer's narrative, one of the three principal institutions attributed to Iskandar Muda — alongside the [[Adat Meukuta Alam|Adat Meukuta Alam]] and the *adat meureuhom* tradition itself. As the state seal of the sultanate, it is the physical instrument by which Atjèhnese royal authority was authenticated on treaties, letters patent, and sarakata; its attribution to Iskandar Muda places it, like the [[Adat Meukuta Alam|Adat Meukuta Alam]], within the constitutional mythology that located the origin of all Atjèhnese political arrangements in the reign of the sultanate's greatest ruler.

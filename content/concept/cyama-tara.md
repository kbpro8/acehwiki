**Cyama-Tara** — also **Syama Tara**, **Sijama Tara** — is the Buddhist goddess **Tara**, the "Redster out of all woe" (*Redster uit alle Ellende*), whom [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies as the patroness of [[Sumatra]] itself, and whose worship centred on the islet of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]]. She is the principal deity of the *bedekte taal* reading of Sumatran-Javanese historical geography, and the figure under whom the [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend of [[Deli]] is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, an earthly likeness.

## Tara of the Cailendras

The goddess **Tara** was, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] records, the patroness of the [[Cailendra|Cailendras]] — the "Berg-Heeren" (Mountain-Lords) whose dynasty ruled the great Buddhist polity of [[Mataram|Mataram]] on [[Java|Java]] in the eighth century, and whose temple to Tara at [[Candi Kalasan|Tjandi Kalasan]] in [[Yogyakarta|Djokjakarta]] is the oldest surviving Buddhist temple of central Java. [[Dr. J. Brandes|Brandes]] had noted (*TBG* 31, p. 252 n.) that "Cyama Tara" is also one of the many names of [[Doerga|Durga]], [[Shiva|Shiva's]] *çakti* — a syncretism that, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, marks the survival of the Tara-cult into the Hindu-Javanese Doerga-worship of the later chronicle tradition.

## Decoding: *sijama* and *idjo*

In the [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend of [[Deli]] and [[Asahan]], the fairy-tale queen's name *idjo* ("green") is, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes, a *bedekte taal* form of the Kawi word *sijama* — i.e. of **Cyama-Tara**. Her realm is "the *sijam-Awak* of the [[Djawaka|Djawaka]]"; she herself is the earthly likeness of the Heavenly Queen, the Buddhist goddess Cyama-Tara, "Redster out of all Woe, patroness of the island of Sumatra". Her original seat was the islet in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] — [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] — the [[Noesa-n-ta-ra|Noesa-n-ta-ra]] of the [[Pararaton]] under the protection of the goddess Tara.

## The Tanjore inscription and the Atjehscher avonturier

The Tanjore inscription of [[1030]] — the great Chola record of the [[Malayur|Malayur]] country — records "*l'ancien Malayur situé sur une haute colline*", "the ancient Malayur situated on a high hill". [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies this hill with the hill of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]], and the same hill with the *benteng* of the [[Atjehsche avonturier at Aroe-baai (1862)|Atjehscher avonturier]] of [[1862]] who flew the Atjehsche flag from it. The continuity of the hill's sacred character — from the Cailendra age to the Atjehnese — is, in De Roo de la Faille's reading, the principal token of the survival of the Tara-cult into the Atjehnese age.

## The *Baringin sonsang* and the inverted Taroema

The *Baringin sonsang|inverted waringin]] of Malay, Minangkabau and Javanese folk tradition — "whose roots stand in the air, while the leaves hang downwards" — is, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, an allegory of the inverted [[Taroema]], the ancestral land "turned upside down" by the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]]. The "Heavenly Queen Doerga of Taroema" who sits beneath the inverted waringin is, in De Roo de la Faille's decoding, the same Cyama-Tara — the patroness of Sumatra whose worship the Atjehnese age overwrote but did not erase.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Cyama-Tara is the principal deity of the *bedekte taal* reading of Sumatran-Javanese historical geography — the patroness of [[Sumatra]] whose worship centred on [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] in [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]], and whose earthly likeness in the [[Puteri Idjo|Putri Hijau]] legend of [[Deli]] is the central Acehnese legend of the east-coast polities. Her survival, in folk form, into the Atjehnese age — attested by the [[Atjehsche avonturier at Aroe-baai (1862)|Atjehscher avonturier]] of [[1862]] and by the *baringin sonsang* allegory — is the principal token of the continuity of the east-coast sacred geography across the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]] of [[1539]].

**Snijders' 1920 visit to Atjeh** was the retired [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Generaal C. J. Snijders]]'s return to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the course of his first post-war Indies journey ([[April 1920|April]]–[[September 1920|September]] [[1920]]), undertaken in his capacity as chairman of the Vereeniging "Ons Leger" to attend the Algemene Vergadering of "Indië Weerbaar" at [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]] on [[24 May]] [[24 May]] [[1920]]. During his tour of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] he paid a visit to [[Acheh|Atjeh]], where he laid a wreath on the grave of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Generaal-Majoor Pel]] at the European cemetery of [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] — closing a personal circle forty-six years after his baptism of fire under Pel's command in the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam operation]] of [[13 November]] [[13 November]] [[1874]]. He then made a tour through the interior from [[Medan|Medan]] to [[Padang|Padang]].

## The First Indies Journey (April–September 1920)

In [[1920]] Snijders, as chairman of the Vereeniging "Ons Leger," undertook a journey to [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Nederlandsch-Indië]] to represent "Ons Leger" at the Algemene Vergadering of "Indië Weerbaar" at [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]]. He was accompanied by Kapitein van den Generalen Staf E. H. A. van den Akker as secretary. A transport workers' strike forced him to give up his intended passage on the *Wilis*; with some effort he secured a berth on the English mailboat *Delta* of the P&O Line, leaving Europe on [[7 April]] [[7 April]] and arriving at [[Tanjung Priok|Tanjung Priok]] on [[16 May]] [[16 May]].

The Bandoeng meeting of [[24 May]] [[24 May]] (the second Whitmonday) was followed by lectures on the defensie-vraagstuk at [[Surabaya|Surabaya]] on [[14 July]] [[14 July]], at [[Semarang|Semarang]] on [[21 July]] [[21 July]], and at [[Medan|Medan]] on [[12 August]] [[12 August]], in addition to several lectures on air-traffic matters in connection with the [[K.L.M.|K.L.M.]] interests that he had also undertaken to pursue. On his tour he was received hospitably by the Governor-General [[Jhr. Mr. A. W. F. Idenburg|Graaf Van Limburg Stirum]] and by the Susuhunan and the Sultan of Surakarta and Djokjakarta. During his stay with the latter he attended a performance of the Serimpi dance and was presented by the Sultan with a walking-stick with a golden knob engraved with the Sunan's monogram.

## The Visit to Atjeh

On [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] Snijders paid a visit to [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The biographer [[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]] records the visit in a single sentence: "Op Sumatra bracht hij een bezoek aan Atjeh, waar hij o.a. op het kerkhof te Kota Radja eene krans legde op het graf van den Generaal-Majoor PEL, en maakte hij van Medan uit nog een tocht door de binnenlanden naar Padang."

The wreath-laying on the grave of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Generaal-Majoor Pel]] at the European cemetery (*kerkhof*) of [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] is the symbolic heart of the visit. Pel had been Snijders' commander in [[1874]]–[[1875]]; he had been promoted major-general by extraordinary Royal Decree of [[18 February]] [[18 February]] [[1875]] and had died suddenly of an arterial rupture at kampong Tonga on [[24 February]] [[24 February]] [[1876]] in the middle of the campaign against the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]]. He had been buried at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]]. The wreath-laying of [[1920]] thus closed a personal circle: the young engineer lieutenant who had been decorated for the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Kota Alam]] and [[Missigit Longbatta operation (31 December 1874 – 13 January 1875)|Longbatta]] operations under Pel returned, as the most senior Dutch soldier of his generation, to honour his old commander's grave.

## Return

From [[Acheh|Atjeh]] Snijders travelled to [[Medan|Medan]], made a tour through the interior to [[Padang|Padang]], and on [[2 September]] [[2 September]] left the Indies on the *Insulinde*. On his return to the Netherlands he resumed his agitation for the defensie-vraagstuk; in [[April 1921|April]] [[1921]] the *Proeve eener oplossing van het legervraagstuk* was published, the work of a commission of six under his chairmanship.

## Significance

The [[1920]] visit is the only documented return of [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] to [[Acheh|Atjeh]] after his service there in [[1874]]–[[1875]]. The wreath-laying on Pel's grave is a small but symbolically important moment in the commemorative history of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — a personal gesture by the officer who, four years later, would become the [[Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht|Opperbevelhebber van Land- en Zeemacht]] of the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]], in honour of the commander under whom he had served his baptism of fire.

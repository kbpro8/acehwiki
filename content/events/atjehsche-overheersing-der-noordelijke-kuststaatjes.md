**The Atjehsche overheersing der Noordelijke kuststaatjes** — the Atjehnese overlordship of the Northern coastal statelets of [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]] — was the long period of formal and informal Atjehnese political supremacy over the Malay sultanates of [[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Asahan|Asahan]] and the smaller river-mouth polities between [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] and [[Batu Bara|Batu Bara]]. First established by [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Sultan Iskander Moeda's Oostkust-veldtocht]] in the early seventeenth century, the overlordship was briefly shaken off in [[1769]], revived under altered circumstances in [[1854]], and finally extinguished by the [[dutch-marine-expulsion-of-atjehers-from-langkat-(1866)|Dutch naval intervention of 1866]] and the [[1878 Langkat–Tamiang transfer|1878 transfer of Langkat's Tamiang rights]]. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the overlordship is the central institutional fact of the pre-colonial East Coast — the framework within which the rivalry between [[Acheh|Atjeh]], [[Siak|Siak]] and [[Djohor|Djohore]] over the coast was fought out.

## The original seventeenth-century overlordship

The original Atjehnese overlordship was the product of [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Sultan Iskander Moeda]]'s campaign along the East Coast, by which, as the Encyclopaedisch Bureau records, "de definitieve onderwerping dier staatjes aan dat machtige rijk" — "the definite subjection of those statelets to that powerful realm" — was achieved. The statelets — [[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]] and the smaller polities — received their *galar* — their ceremonial titles of recognition — from the hands of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]], and the institution of the Atjehnese *vertegenwoordiger* in [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] and northern [[Langkat|Langkat]], which had originated in the [[atjehsche-vernietiging-van-het-batakrijk-aroe-(c-1589)|sixteenth-century destruction of Aroe]], was now formalised across the entire coast.

## The 1769 recovery

The original overlordship lasted, the Bureau records, until [[1769]]. "In [[1769]] wisten zij zich echter weder van deze overheersching los te maken" — "In [[1769]], however, they knew themselves again to be free of this overlordship." The Bureau does not give a detailed account of the circumstances of the 1769 revolt, but it presents it as a momentary release, rapidly overtaken by the rise of [[Siak|Siak]] as the new regional hegemon. Within a few years the statelets had been forced, "door het wapengeweld van Ismail en Said Ali" — "by the armed force of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]" — to acknowledge [[Siak|Siak]] rather than [[Acheh|Atjeh]] as their overlord.

## The Siak interlude

The Siak overlordship that succeeded the Atjehnese was, the Bureau records, itself short-lived. After [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]]'s death in [[1821]] the Siak sultanate was riven by internal disputes, and after the accession of [[Tongkoe Ismail|Sultan Tongkoe Ismail of Siak]] in [[1827]] Siak's effective power on the East Coast rapidly declined. By the middle of the nineteenth century, the Bureau notes, "waren de Noordelijke Onderhoorigheden weldra inderdaad geheel onafhankelijk" — "the Northern Onderhoorigheden were soon in fact entirely independent" — and even the old ceremonial exchange of *galar*s had been replaced by the chiefs' recognition of each other's titles. By the early 1850s the East Coast statelets were, in effect, sovereign.

## The 1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship

The Atjehnese overlordship was reimposed, in altered form, in [[1854]]. "Serdang, Deli en Langkat, welke zich los hadden weten te maken van Siak's invloed, waren al sinds [[1854]] gedwongen geworden in plaats daarvan dien van Atjeh toe te laten, welk rijk daar sedert dat jaar inderdaad opperheer was" — "[[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Deli|Deli]] and [[Langkat|Langkat]], which had managed to free themselves from [[Siak|Siak]]'s influence, had been forced since [[1854]] to admit, in its place, that of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], which realm was, since that year, in fact overlord there." The reimposition, coming after a century of formal Siak suzerainty, marked the return of [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] power to the East Coast — and prepared the ground for the [[langkat-appeal-to-siak-against-atjeh-(1862)|1862 appeal of the Radja of Langkat to Siak]] for Dutch protection against Atjehnese exactions.

## The Dutch intervention and the end of Atjehnese overlordship

The Atjehnese overlordship of the East Coast was finally extinguished by the Dutch in the 1860s. The Bureau records that the [[langkat-appeal-to-siak-against-atjeh-(1862)|1862 appeal of the Radja of Langkat]] — made "de onderdrukking door Atjeh moede" — "weary of the oppression by Atjeh" — led, through [[Tongkoe Ismail|Sultan Tongkoe Ismail of Siak]]'s intercession with the Netherlands-Indies Government, to the dispatch of [[Netscher|Resident Netscher of Riouw]] on a tour of the East Coast statelets in [[1862]]. The statelets of [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Langkat|Langkat]] and several smaller polities signed acts of recognition of Siak's overlordship; [[Asahan|Asahan]] refused and was reduced by armed force in [[1865]]. "Tevens werd in het daarop volgend jaar door optreden onzer Marine een einde gemaakt aan de vexaties der Atjehers in het Langkatsche, die daardoor naar hun eigen gebied werden teruggedreven" — "At the same time, in the following year, by the action of our Navy an end was made to the vexations of the Atjehers in the Langkat territory, who were thereby driven back to their own territory."

## Significance

The Atjehsche overheersing der Noordelijke kuststaatjes is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the central institution around which the pre-colonial history of [[Sumatra's East Coast|the East Coast]] turns. Its establishment under [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Iskander Moeda]], its temporary eclipse in [[1769]], its Siak successor, its reimposition in [[1854]], and its final extinguishment by the Dutch in the 1860s together constitute the framework within which the Dutch colonial state later built its own East Coast administration. The 1854–1866 phase of the overlordship is, moreover, the immediate background to the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of 1873, insofar as the East Coast statelets' appeal for Dutch protection against Atjehnese pressure was a principal diplomatic pretext for the Dutch assertion of effective authority over the entire northern Sumatran coast.

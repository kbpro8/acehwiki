**The Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)** was the four-day show of force by the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Dutch Navy]] in the [[Kroeng-Rababaai]] — the bay of the [[Kroeng Raba]] river, on the western coast of the [[Achehnese]] residency — mounted by the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], in conjunction with the opening of the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] to threaten [[Lepong]] — [[Teuku Umar]]'s home base and the principal Achehnese stronghold on the western coast — and to make Umar's position untenable from the sea. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the demonstration of [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]] was followed by sustained naval bombardments of [[Lepong]] on [[25 May]], [[4 June]]–[[5 June]], and [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]] — a campaign of maritime pressure that complemented the land operations and forced Umar to divide his attention between the inland and coastal fronts.

## Background

By the end of [[April]] [[1896]] the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had completed the [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuation of the buitenlinie]] and had begun the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] from the battery at [[Lam-Djamoe]]. The land operations were directed against Umar's *stellingen* in the [[Lampoeng]] / [[Lampisang]] region — the works covering his residence at [[Lam-Pisang]]. But Umar's principal base — his home territory, the seat of his *hoeloebalang* authority — lay further west, on the coast at [[Lepong]], on the [[Kroeng-Rababaai]]. So long as [[Lepong]] remained secure, Umar could retreat to his coastal base, regroup, and continue the war from a position of safety. To prevent this, Vetter resolved to demonstrate that [[Lepong]] itself was within reach of the Dutch Navy.

The [[Kroeng-Rababaai]] — the bay into which the [[Kroeng Raba]] river empties — was a substantial inlet on the western coast of the Atjeh residency. The Achehnese had long used it as a maritime approach to the interior; the Dutch had, in turn, periodically used it as a base for naval operations against the western mukims. The demonstration of [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]] was the most ambitious such operation of the spring campaign.

## The Demonstration

On [[1 May]] [[1896]] the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Dutch Navy]] dispatched a squadron of warships into the [[Kroeng-Rababaai]]. The vessels — Kruisheer does not specify the names in the relevant passage — anchored in the bay and commenced a demonstration of force: ships' boats were lowered, marines were drilled in plain view of the shore, and the ships' guns were trained on the prominent points of the coast. The bombardment of [[Lepong]] itself, on this occasion, was limited to desultory fire — the demonstration was primarily a political and psychological operation rather than a serious attempt to reduce the works.

The demonstration continued through [[4 May]] [[1896]]. The Achehnese, recognising the threat to [[Lepong]], were forced to divert warriors from the inland *stellingen* to the coastal defence. This was, from Vetter's perspective, the principal strategic effect of the demonstration: by threatening [[Lepong]], the Navy pinned down a portion of Umar's forces that would otherwise have been available for the defence of [[Lam-Pisang]] and the [[VI Moekims]]. The demonstration also served notice on Umar that his coastal base was no longer a sanctuary.

## The Subsequent Bombardments of Lepong

The demonstration of [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]] was followed by sustained naval bombardments of [[Lepong]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]] (in the immediate aftermath of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]), on [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] (during the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|operations in the IV and VI Moekims]]), and on [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]] (during the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lam-Krak mukims]]). These later bombardments were more intense than the demonstration of early May and were intended to render [[Lepong]] permanently untenable as a base. The cumulative effect of the naval operations was to drive Umar out of his coastal strongholds and to confine him increasingly to the interior — where the Dutch columns could reach him.

## Significance

The Naval Demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai of [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]] was the opening of the maritime campaign against [[Lepong]] and the prototype of the combined-arms operations that would characterise the rest of the spring and summer of [[1896]]. By threatening Umar's coastal base, the Navy allowed the land forces to concentrate on the inland *stellingen* with confidence that the Achehnese could not regroup at [[Lepong]]. The demonstration also marked the emergence of the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Dutch Navy]] as a primary instrument of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]: from this point onward, naval bombardments and marine landings — including the [[Marine Landing at Koeala Pantjoer / Lam-Baroe (5 June 1896)|marine landing at Koeala Pantjoer / Lam-Baroe]] of [[5 June]] [[1896]] and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] — would be integral to the Dutch operational design. The demonstration of early May was, in this sense, the strategic foundation on which the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] and the subsequent operations of June were built.

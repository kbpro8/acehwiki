**Snijders' rapport on German projectiles** of [[22 July]] [[22 July]] [[1884]] and [[13 June]] [[13 June]] [[1885]] were the two reports submitted by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Kapitein-Ingenieur C. J. Snijders]] from Magdeburg to the Dutch Minister of War, in which he communicated his discovery of the German trials of a new type of high-explosive projectile (*schietkatoen-granaten*) that would make the bomb-free cover of all existing fortress buildings illusory, and in which he developed his ideas on the way in which bomb-free cover would in future have to be obtained and on the influence of the new weapon on permanent fortification. The reports led directly to the halt in the further build-out of the [[Stelling van Amsterdam|Stelling van Amsterdam]] in [[1886]] pending insight into the path to be taken, and are the foundation of Snijders' reputation as one of the foremost Dutch fortress-builders of his generation.

## The Discovery at Magdeburg

During his posting to Magdeburg ([[1882]]–[[1886]]) to supervise the manufacture of armour for the Dutch coast forts at the firm Gruson — see [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]]'s biography — Snijders heard by rumour of the German trials of a new type of brisant projectile that would make the bomb-free cover of all existing fortress buildings illusory. As this question was of vital importance for the Dutch fortified lines — and in particular for the [[Stelling van Amsterdam|Stelling van Amsterdam]], then under construction — Snijders attempted to inform himself further. At great pains, and despite the anxious secrecy of the German authorities, he succeeded in confirming the rumours.

## The Rapport of 22 July 1884

On [[22 July]] [[22 July]] [[1884]] Snijders submitted his first report to the Minister of War. The report communicated the existence of the new German projectiles. Although the Department was not fully convinced of the correctness of his communications, Snijders continued his investigations.

## The Rapport of 13 June 1885

On [[13 June]] [[13 June]] [[1885]] Snijders submitted a fuller report. On the basis of information drawn partly from conversations — including with the Prussian oud-Majoor der Genie Schumann, who was attached to the Gruson factory — and partly from visits to schietkatoen factories and the like, he was able to give a fuller account of the new projectiles and their effect, and to develop his ideas on the way in which bomb-free cover would in future have to be obtained and on the influence of the new weapon on permanent fortification.

## The Halt of 1886

It was on the basis of the [[13 June]] [[13 June]] [[1885]] report that in [[1886]] the further build-out of the [[Stelling van Amsterdam|Stelling van Amsterdam]] was halted pending insight into the path to be taken. Snijders treated the same question in an extensive article in *De Militaire Spectator* of [[1886]]–[[1887]], "Schietkatoen en schietkatoen-granaten," which excited much interest and produced a stir in military circles.

## Significance

The two reports are the foundation of Snijders' reputation as one of the foremost Dutch fortress-builders of his generation. They demonstrate his capacity for technical intelligence work — conducted against the anxious secrecy of the German authorities and at some personal risk — and his ability to translate technical intelligence into strategic prescription. They are also one of the channels through which the Atjeh experience of the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs]] officer was applied to the defence of the [[Netherlands|Netherlands]]: Snijders' early experience of the limits of sandbag fortification under fire in Atjeh gave him a particular sensitivity to the obsolescence of fixed fortification under the new brisant projectiles.

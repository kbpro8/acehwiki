**The Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)** was the embarkation of [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] — [[Regeeringscommissaris voor Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] — and his adjudant [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. F. Breijer|Lt.-Col. J. F. Breijer]] for [[Batavia]] aboard the *stoomschip Coen* on [[28 June]] [[1896]], and the simultaneous laying-down of the extraordinary office of [[Regeeringscommissaris]] and the restoration of the ordinary office of [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]]. [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] was appointed (provisionally) as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]]. [[Resident K. F. H. van Langen|Resident van Langen]] was relieved of his temporary functions as Civil Administrator of the Region. The departure of [[28 June]] [[1896]] concluded the first semester of [[1896]] and the Vetter interregnum — the three-month extraordinary commission that had reversed the disaster of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] and had restored the strategic initiative in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## Background

The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had arrived in Atjeh on [[7 April]] [[1896]] aboard the steamer or *Bantam* with the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9th Battalion Infantry]] and had immediately relieved [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] of his functions as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]]. The mission that the metropolitan government had entrusted to Vetter was threefold: to retrieve the strategic situation provoked by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]; to restore the Dutch political and military position in Atjeh; and to lay the groundwork for the resumption of ordinary civil-military government.

By the last week of [[June]] [[1896]] all three objectives had been accomplished. The [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuation of the buitenlinie]] had been completed; the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] and had [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|destroyed his house]]; the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] had reduced the principal Achehnese redoubts; the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] had completed the operational cycle. The political realignment was under way: [[Panglima Bintang]] had [[Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|submitted]] on [[20 June]] [[1896]], and the [[Disarmament of Buitenlinie Chiefs (mid-June 1896)|disarmament of the buitenlinie chiefs]] was in progress. The [[Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)|farewell parade]] of [[27 June]] [[1896]] had been the ceremonial close of the interregnum. The time had come for Vetter to depart and for the ordinary civil-military government to be restored.

## The Departure of 28 June 1896

On [[28 June]] [[1896]] [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] — accompanied by his adjudant [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. F. Breijer|Lt.-Col. J. F. Breijer]] of the General Staff (the same officer who had arrived with him on [[7 April]] [[1896]]) — embarked for [[Batavia]] aboard the *stoomschip Coen*. The departure was, in ceremonial terms, a quiet affair — the public ceremonial had been discharged at the [[Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)|farewell parade]] of the previous day. The embarkation was, however, of considerable constitutional significance: with Vetter's departure, the extraordinary office of [[Regeeringscommissaris]] — which had superseded the ordinary chain of command for three months — was laid down.

The ordinary office of [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]] was restored. [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. J. W. Stemfoort]] — who had arrived with Vetter on [[7 April]] [[1896]], had commanded the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[8 April]] [[1896]], had commanded the [[Evacuation of Anak-Galoeng, Lam-Barih, Lam-Soet, Senelop (17 April 1896)|evacuation of the western buitenlinie]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]], had commanded the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May]] from his position as overall commander, and had commanded the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] — was appointed (provisionally) as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]]. The provisional character of the appointment — [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] held the rank of Colonel, not Major-General, the rank ordinarily associated with the post — was a reflection of the unsettled political situation in [[Batavia]] and [[The Hague]], and a permanent appointment would be made in due course.

[[Resident K. F. H. van Langen|Resident van Langen]] — *Resident voor Atjehsche zaken en de scheepvaartregeling* — was relieved of his temporary functions as Civil Administrator of the Region, functions that he had exercised since the [[Arrival of Vetter and Dismissal of Deykerhoff (7 April 1896)|arrival of Vetter]] and the dismissal of [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]] on [[7 April]] [[1896]]. The relief of [[Resident K. F. H. van Langen|van Langen]] was a return to the ordinary division of civil and military responsibilities: the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]] exercised supreme authority; the *Civiel Bestuurder* exercised the civil administration under his direction.

## The Conclusion of the First Semester of 1896

The departure of Vetter and the restoration of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]] on [[28 June]] [[1896]] concluded the first semester of [[1896]] — the period covered by [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*. The semester had begun, in [[January]] [[1896]], with the [[buitenlinie]] intact, the *legioen* of [[Teuku Umar]] in Dutch service, and the [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] administration pursuing the native-allied policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]]. It had been upended, in [[March]] [[1896]], by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. It had been retrieved, in [[April]]–[[June]] [[1896]], by the Vetter interregnum: the [[Arrival of Vetter and Dismissal of Deykerhoff (7 April 1896)|arrival]] of [[7 April]] [[1896]], the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[8 April]] [[1896]], the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation]] of [[12 April]] [[1896]], the ## Significance

The Departure of Vetter and the Restoration of Civil and Military Governor on [[28 June]] [[1896]] was the constitutional close of the Vetter interregnum and of the first semester of [[1896]]. The departure was, in political terms, a declaration by the metropolitan government that the crisis provoked by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]] had been retrieved and that the ordinary civil-military government could be restored. The appointment of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]] — provisional, but unequivocal — was a recognition of the role that [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] had played in the recovery: as the overall commander of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]], as the commander of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]], and as the commander of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]]. The relief of [[Resident K. F. H. van Langen|van Langen]] was a return to the ordinary division of civil and military responsibilities. The departure of [[28 June]] [[1896]] was, in this sense, both a close and an opening: the close of the first semester of [[1896]] and the opening of the second — a semester in which the lessons of the Vetter interregnum would shape Dutch strategy in Atjeh for the remainder of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

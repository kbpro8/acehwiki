**The Atjehsche verovering van Gasip** — the Acehnese conquest of the kingdom of Gasip — was the early-sixteenth-century military campaign by which the [[Acheh|Atjehsche rijk]] overran and destroyed the once-powerful Sumatran kingdom of [[rijk Gasip|Gasip]], which had stretched from the lower [[Siak|Siak River]] deep into the southern interior of [[Sumatra]] as far as the legendary Minangkabau kingdom of [[Pagarroejoeng]]. The conquest, recorded in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] as a defining event in the pre-colonial history of [[Sumatra's East Coast]], was carried out with the help of the [[orang-Pandan|orang-Pandan]] — the inhabitants of the [[Petalangan]] country — and was followed by the forcible removal of the Gasip princes to [[Acheh|Atjeh]], leaving the conquered realm to dissolve into the federation of small independent *landschapjes* that the [[Tapoeng-streken|upper-Siak Tapoeng federations]] would later become.

## Background: the realm of Gasip

Before the rise of the [[Siak|Sultanate of Siak]] the lower-Siak region had been dominated by the kingdom of Gasip, which the Encyclopaedisch Bureau describes as having stretched far beyond the later sultanate's boundaries and to have bordered, in the deep interior, on the mythical Minangkabau kingdom of [[Pagarroejoeng]]. The Bureau notes that "nog moeten in het oerwoud langs de Soengei Gasip overblijfselen van den kraton zijn te vinden" — "remnants of the kraton must still be found in the primeval forest along the Soengei Gasip" — and that "er onder de Siaksche hoofden enkelen zijn, die den oorsprong hunner *kabesaran's* (rijks-orna mentee) terugbrengen tot dat rijk" — "there are among the Siak chiefs some who trace the origin of their *kabesaran* (regal ornaments) back to that realm." Gasip was thus remembered as the legitimate predecessor-state of Siak, even after its destruction.

## The conquest

In the early years of the sixteenth century the [[Acheh|Atjehsche rijk]], described by the Bureau as "the jealous Atjehsche rijk" — *het naijverige Atjehsche rijk* — went to war with Gasip and overthrew it. The campaign was not an Atjehnese undertaking alone: the Atjehers operated in alliance with the [[orang-Pandan|orang-Pandan]], the inhabitants of the [[Petalangan]] country, whose collaboration gave the campaign its inter-ethnic character. The Bureau records the outcome with stark brevity: "Toen de Atjehers, met behulp van de orang-Pandan (de bewoners der Petalangan) Gasip overweldigd hadden, voerden zij de vorsten gevankelijk mede naar Atjeh en lieten het verwoeste rijk achter ten prooi aan ontbinding" — "When the Atjehers, with the help of the orang-Pandan (the inhabitants of the Petalangan), had overwhelmed Gasip, they led the princes away captive to Atjeh and left the destroyed realm to the prey of dissolution."

## Consequences for the East Coast

The destruction of Gasip opened the lower Siak to a new contender. "Hiervan maakte de Sultan van Djohore gebruik om zich aan de monding der Siak-rivier te vestigen" — "Of this the Sultan of [[Djohor|Djohore]] made use to establish himself at the mouth of the Siak river" — and Djohorese influence henceforth overshadowed the coast, with the inland reverting to a loose federation of independent small *landschapjes*. The Atjehnese victory thus indirectly produced two of the central features of the pre-colonial East Coast: the [[Djohor|Djohorese]] bridgehead at [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] (governed by a *sjahbandar* on the Sultan of Djohore's behalf) and the inland federation of the [[Tapoeng-streken|Tapoeng country]] (upper-Siak). Both arrangements, the Bureau notes, served the Sultan of [[Djohor|Djohore]]'s strategic purpose of controlling the great trade route between [[Pagarroejoeng]] and [[Malacca|Malakka]] along the Siak and upper-Kampar rivers.

## Place in the long Atjeh–East Coast relationship

The conquest of Gasip is the earliest of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] interventions on [[Sumatra's East Coast]] recorded in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey. It anticipates, by more than a century, the better-known campaigns of [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Sultan Iskander Moeda of Atjeh]] along the same coast, and the later [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Atjehsche overlordship of the northern coastal statelets]] of [[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Deli|Deli]] and [[Serdang]]. The pattern established at Gasip — Atjehnese military force co-operating with interior peoples, the deportation of defeated princes, and the dissolution of the conquered polity into smaller fragments — would be repeated under [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Iskander Moeda]] and again, in altered form, when the [[atjehsche-vernietiging-van-het-batakrijk-aroe-(c-1589)|Atjehers destroyed the Batak realm of Aroe]].

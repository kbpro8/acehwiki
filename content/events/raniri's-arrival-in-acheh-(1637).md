**The arrival of [[Raniri|Nūruddīn ar-Rānīrī]] in [[Acheh]] in [[1637]]** is one of the principal chronological anchor-points in the early history of the [[Acheh|Achehnese Sultanate]] and the opening event of the orthodox reaction against the [[Wujudiyyah]] mysticism that had flourished at the court of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]]. Raniri himself relates, in his treatise [[Sirat al-mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] — printed at [[Mecca]] in 1316 H on the margin of an edition of *Fatḥ al-muʿīn* — that he came to Acheh in [[1637]] while [[Iskandar Thani]] was king. The arrival falls in the very first year of the new reign and places Raniri at the centre of the learned and religious life of the capital under the wealthy sultans of the seventeenth century.

## Background

The coastal towns of [[Gudjarat|Gujarat]] in this period had a very international population. Ships regularly sailed thence to the Straits of Malacca, and among the mercenary troops of the sixteenth century were "Javanese" — that is, in this context, Indonesians. The Malay language was certainly not unfamiliar in the harbour towns; and the family of Raniri had, even before his own time, maintained relations with [[Acheh]]. Between [[1580]] and [[1583]] his uncle [[Muhammad Djailani Hamid]] had arrived in Acheh, hoping to teach logic, rhetoric, dogmatics, and the doctrine of duties, but had found little interest. There was a great influx only for teachers of mysticism. So the scholar sailed on to [[Mecca]]. Some years later he returned, provided with the *venia docendi* as a teacher of mystical theology, and then had great success.

Raniri learned from his uncle's experiences. When he himself left for Acheh in [[1637]], he was thoroughly prepared. He knew the *[[Bahasa Djawi]]*, the Malay that was the language of the court in Acheh, and had already written two works in it — among them, at least in its first version, the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] — while a third was in progress. He had also penetrated into mysticism, having been initiated into the [[Rifa'iyah order|Rifāʿiyya]] of [[Ahmad Rifa'i]] by his shaikh [[Sajjid Abu Hafs Umar Ba Sjaiban|Sayyid ʿUmar Bā Shaybān]].

## The religious climate of Acheh

In Acheh, Raniri as an orthodox mystic had during the reign of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] ([[1607]]–[[1636]]) nothing to expect. The "heterodox mystic" [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] dominated the spiritual life of the court, and even after his death in [[1630]] his disciples remained in honour with the sultan. The school of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] — whose [[Kitab al-Muntahi|*Kitāb al-Muntahī*]] and [[Asrar al-'arifin|*Asrār al-ʿārifīn*]] expounded the [[Wujudiyyah]] in its most extreme form — held the field in Malay mystical poetry. But after Iskandar Muda died in [[1636]] the spiritual climate in Acheh changed.

## The arrival and the public debate

Shortly after the accession of [[Iskandar Thani|Iskandar II]] in [[1637]] — that is, in the very year of Raniri's arrival — Raniri [[Raniri's public debate against the Wujudiyyah in Acheh|refuted the adherents of the extreme Wujūdiyyah mysticism in a public debate]] in the presence of the sultan. From that time forth he enjoyed royal favour, both from Iskandar II and from his successor [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Ṣalīyatu'd-Dīn]] (Taj al-Ālam). The composition of the [[Sirat al-mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] (March [[1638]]) and of the [[Bad' khalq as-samawat wal-arwah|*Badʾ khalq as-samāwāt wal-arwāḥ*]] was to follow at the sultan's command; the composition of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] and the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]] falls within the joint reigns of Iskandar II and Taj al-Ālam.

## Significance

The arrival of Raniri in [[1637]] is the opening event of the orthodox reaction that closed Acheh's golden age of mystical poetry. It is, at the same time, the event whose date fixes the chronology of the Achehnese sultanate in the early seventeenth century: Raniri's testimony that he came to Acheh "in [[1637]] while Iskandar Thani was king" is the principal authority for the death of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Meukuta Alam]] (Iskandar Muda) in [[1636]]. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] accepts this testimony as confirmed against the uncertainties of native chronicle and tradition.

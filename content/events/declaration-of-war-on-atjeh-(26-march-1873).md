**The declaration of war on Atjèh** of [[26 March]] [[1873]] — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the act by which [[F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen|F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen]], Regeeringscommissaris for Atjèh, exercised his authority to declare war on the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Atjèh sultanate]] after the Sultan refused all explanation for his approaches to foreign powers — opened the [[Aceh War|Atjèh-oorlog]], a conflict that would last, in Kreemer's periodisation, from [[1873]] to [[1910]].

## The immediate context

Kreemer situates the declaration at the end of a long chain of diplomatic failure. The [[Sumatra Treaty of 1871|Sumatra-tractaat]] with Britain had released the Netherlands from its [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824]] obligation to respect Atjèh's integrity. The sultanate, perceiving the threat, sought protection from foreign powers: an [[Atjèh petition to Turkey (1868)|appeal to Turkey]] in [[1868]]; an approach to the [[Sultan of Turkey|Sultan of Turkey]] in [[1872]] that delayed the Dutch peace mission; an approach to [[France|France]] in January [[1873]]; and, in the same month, the [[Atjèh embassy to Singapore (January 1873)|Singapore embassy]] in which Atjèhnese envoys proposed treaties to the consuls of the [[United States of America|United States]] and [[Italy|Italy]].

The Dutch government, judging "dat al ons streven der laatste jaren gericht was geweest op het regelen onzer betrekkingen met Atjèh in dier voege, dat vreemde vestiging er zou zijn uitgesloten" — "that all our efforts of recent years had been directed to the regulation of our relations with Atjèh in such a way that foreign settlement there would be excluded" — deemed "dat de berichten uit Singapore de ernstigste complicaties deden vreezen. Onze Regeering achtte langer dralen thans ongeoorloofd" — "that the news from Singapore gave rise to fear of the most serious complications. Our Government deemed further delay now impermissible."

## The declaration

"Besloten werd den Vice-President van den Raad van Indië F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen als Regeeringscommissaris naar Atjèh te zenden om den Soeltan opheldering te vragen omtrent zijn trouweloos gedrag en van hem de erkenning onzer souvereiniteit te eischen. Maar Atjèh weigerde alle opheldering. Toen maakte Nieuwenhuyzen van zijn bevoegdheid gebruik om 26 Maart 1873 aan Atjèh den oorlog te verklaren" — "It was decided to send the Vice-President of the Council of the Indies F. N. Nieuwenhuyzen as Government Commissioner to Atjèh, to demand of the Sultan an explanation concerning his faithless conduct and to require of him the recognition of our sovereignty. But Atjèh refused all explanation. Then Nieuwenhuyzen made use of his authority to declare war on Atjèh on 26 March 1873."

## Significance

The declaration of [[26 March]] [[1873]] is, in Kreemer's narrative, the point at which the Dutch government finally abandoned the treaty-framework of [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|1824]] and committed to the military conquest of Atjèh. The [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first expedition]] under [[General J. H. R. Kohler|generaalmajoor J. H. R. Kohler]] landed on [[25 April]] [[1873]] — less than a month later — and the war thus opened would, in Kreemer's seven-period division, run for thirty-seven years, through the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]], the consolidation period, the [[Concentration (1884-1896)|concentration]] and [[Scheepvaartregeling (1892)|scheepvaartregeling]] period, the [[Oema-periode (1893-1896)|oema-period]], and the final [[Civil Government period|civil-government]] period, until the close of the war under Governor Swart in [[1910]].

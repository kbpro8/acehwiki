The **court buffalo-fight at the court of Sultan Iskandar Muda|Sultan Iskandar Muda in 1613** was a state spectacle staged at Banda Acheh|Koetaradja in honour of the visiting English envoy Thomas Best, in which buffaloes were deployed as *vechtdieren* (fighting animals) alongside elephants in a choreographed sequence that constitutes one of the earliest European eyewitness descriptions of court spectacle at the Acheh|Atjeh capital. The fight is documented in Thomas Best|Best's journal — edited by William Foster as *The Voyage of Thomas Best to the East Indies, 1612-14* (London: Hakluyt Society, 1934, p. 168) — and is cited in J. Kreemer|Kreemer's Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] (p. 84), drawing on Kreemer's Atjeh ([[1922-23)|Kreemer's *Atjeh*]] (vol. I, p. 192), as the principal first-hand account of buffalo-fights as a courtly entertainment in Acheh|Atjeh.

## The event
The fight was staged as part of a larger state festival held in Thomas Best|Best's honour during his Thomas Best's visit to Atjeh ([[1613)|embassy of 1613]] to the court of Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda (r. 1607–1636), the *"Roi soleil"* of the Atjehse vorsten. J. Kreemer|Kreemer's summary of Best's journal in *De Karbouw* (p. 84) gives the sequence:

> *"Eén der hoofdmomenten daarvan was een toernooi van olifanten. 'After them came buffelos, a beast verie fierce and stronge, which maid a worthie feight and gave greatt content to the spectators.'"*
The choreography — elephant-tournament followed by buffalo-fight — is significant. The pairing of the two species as *vechtdieren* points to a courtly culture in which animal combat had been elevated from a village pastime to a spectacle of kingship, and the use of the buffalo (the principal working-animal of the Atjehnese rice-economy) alongside the elephant (the principal carrier-beast of Atjehnese state-power) is a juxtaposition that resonates with the wider Atjehnese pattern, recorded elsewhere in *De Karbouw*, in which the buffalo figures in both the productive economy (as plough- and cart-animal) and in the ritual-military complex (as siege-engine material, sacrificial victim, and courtly spectacle).

## Best's journal and the European record
Thomas Best|Best's description of the fight — preserved in his original early-seventeenth-century English spelling — is one of the earliest European attestations of the word "buffalo" in an East Indian context, and the documentary nucleus of the entry on the court buffalo-fight. The passage was edited by William Foster and published by the Hakluyt Society (London, 1934) as part of *The Voyage of Thomas Best to the East Indies, 1612-14*, at p. 168. It was transmitted into the Dutch scholarly literature by J. Kreemer|Kreemer in his Kreemer's Atjeh ([[1922-23)|*Atjeh*]] (vol. I, p. 192) and re-cited in his Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] (p. 84) as the principal authority on the court buffalo-fight as an institution of Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda's reign.


## The wider context
The court buffalo-fight of 1613 is, in J. Kreemer|Kreemer's reading, the courtly original of which the village buffalo-fights of more recent centuries are a "zwakke weerklank" (faint echo). The same courtly-military complex is attested by the persistence of the koeta djaq|buffalo-hide "walking fort" as an Atjehnese siege-engine (already mentioned in the mid-eighteenth-century Atjehnese epic Potjoet Moehammat|*Potjoet Moehammat*) and by the survival, in much attenuated form, of village buffalo-fights in the archipelago in later centuries. The fight is also one of the documents of the early-seventeenth-century Anglo-Atjehnese diplomatic relationship, which would later be retraced and contextualised by P. H. van der Kemp|Van der Kemp in his studies of the Raffles' Acheh treaty of [[1819|1819 Raffles agreement]] and of the eighteenth-century correspondence between the Atjeh sultans and the British in Bengal.


## Significance
The court buffalo-fight of 1613 is significant for the encyclopedia of Acheh history as the principal documented instance of the buffalo-fight as an institution of Atjehnese kingship — a spectacle staged before a foreign envoy at the Atjeh bloeitijdperk ([[1607-1636)|Atjeh *bloeitijdperk*]] court of Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda. It anchors the village buffalo-fights of later centuries as a faint echo of a courtly original, and points to the wider integration of the buffalo into the productive, ritual and military-economic complex of the Atjehnese state.

**The Expedition to Deli** of [[May 1872|May-October 1872]] was the Dutch military expedition against the rebellious Malay chiefs [[Datoe Ketjil|Datoe Ketjil]] and [[Datoe Djatil|Datoe Djatil]] in the territory of the [[Sultan of Deli|sultan of Deli]] on the east coast of Sumatra, undertaken at the request of the sultan and of the [[Pangeran of Langkat|pangeran of Langkat]] to suppress a conspiracy among the Malay chiefs and Bataks to throw off the authority of both princes and to drive the Europeans from the country. The expedition preceded the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] by almost a year and was, in the source work's view, "not wholly without connection" with the events in Atjeh.

## Background

In the territory of the sultan of [[Deli|Deli]] on Sumatra's east coast — a dependency of [[Siak|Siak]] lying south of the territory of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — there had been established, under the patronage of the sultan, since [[1864]] various agricultural enterprises, especially tobacco plantations, which by the profits they yielded gave rise to continually new establishments. By [[June 1871|June 1871]] contracts had been concluded for 20 enterprises, covering a territory of 17,900 bouws, in which more than 3,000 Chinese workmen were employed. In one of the five districts into which the state of Deli was divided, the voogd of the under-age head of [[Soenggal|Soenggal]], [[Datoe Ketjil|Datoe Ketjil]], opposed the establishment of the tobacco enterprises. In [[May 1872|May 1872]] he came into open revolt, having allied himself with his brother [[Datoe Djatil|Datoe Djatil]], who as head of [[Timbang Langkat|Timbang Langkat]] tried to escape the authority of the sultan of Deli and of the pangeran of Langkat. Later it even appeared that there had been a conspiracy, in which nearly all the Malay chiefs and many Bataks were involved, to make themselves independent of both princes, to limit their territory to the coast, and to drive the Europeans from the country. The conspiracy failed because the two named datoes had begun hostilities too early.

## The Expedition

On [[14 May]] [[14 May]] [[1872]] a small troop force of 5 officers and 135 rank-and-file, supported by the warships *Banka* and *Den Briel* at the roadstead, advanced against the revolted datoes and took their chief strongholds. The datoes opened negotiations, but when they let the time set on [[19 June]] [[19 June]] pass, the commander, Captain Koops, again advanced upon [[Sapoeroek|Sapoeroek]] and the nearby fortifications, which he took. As meanwhile his troop strength began to shrink, and the revolt continually assumed larger proportions, on [[10 July]] [[10 July]] a reinforcement of more than 400 men under Lieutenant-colonel Von Hombracht arrived, which could adequately protect the attacked plantations. In repeated expeditions he pursued the enemy into his hiding-places, although he met stubborn resistance at certain fortifications such as [[Laoe Potah|Laoe Potah]], and forced the rebels to submission, so that by the end of October the rest was completely restored.

## Significance

By decree of [[15 May]] [[15 May]] [[1873]] a reorganisation of the administration of [[Sumatra's Oostkust|Sumatra's Oostkust]] was carried out, by which this was separated from the residency of [[Riouw|Riouw]] and made into a separate residency. The expedition to Deli was, in the source work's view, not wholly without connection with the events in Atjeh; both belonged to the wider pattern of Dutch expansion along the coasts of Sumatra, which the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1871|Sumatra-tractaat of 1871]] had made possible.

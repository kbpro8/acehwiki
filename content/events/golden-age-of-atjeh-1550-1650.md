**Golden Age of Atjeh (1550-1650)** (1550-1650) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. The known Atjeh-history's golden age. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to [[1550]]-[[1650]].

## Account

The known Atjeh-history's golden age. Trade and traffic brought great wealth, especially to the court, which lived luxuriously. Scholars and artists from foreign lands ([[Egypte, Syrië, Arabië]]) settled here. Important translations, encyclopedic works, and fiqh books date from this period. [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda]] is the central figure. After his death, a brief successor reign brought a Sultana (female ruler) to the government - and three more female sultanas ruled in succession in the 17th century, evidence of the post-[[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Iskandar]] crisis. The conflict between 'family' and 'nation' was brought to its highest degree under [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda|Iskandar]].

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

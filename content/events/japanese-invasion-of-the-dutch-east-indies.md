**Japanese invasion of the Dutch East Indies** was the rapid military campaign launched by the Empire of Japan in early [[1942]] to seize the Dutch colonial possessions in Southeast Asia, including the strategic province of [[Aceh]] on the northern tip of Sumatra. The operation culminated in the occupation of major islands such as [[Java]] and the surrender of Dutch forces after intense battles, notably the **Battle of the Java Sea** on [[27 February]] [[1942]].

## Prelude and Initial Actions
Following the German invasion of the Netherlands in [[1940]], the Dutch colonial forces in the East Indies were placed on high alert. The Royal Netherlands Marine Corps maintained detachments at key naval installations, including the Marine Barracks in *Soerabaia* (Surabaya) and the airfield “Mororrembangan”. On [[1 March]] [[1942]] the Dutch command ordered the demolition of strategic facilities, such as the pyrotechnic workshop on *Madoera*, to deny them to the enemy.

## Major Battles and Outcomes
* **Battle of the Java Sea** (27 February 1942) – Japanese naval forces defeated the Allied fleet, leading to the loss of several Dutch warships, including *Hr. Ms. “De Ruyter”* on which Captain G. Wilhelmy Damsté was killed.
* **Air raids on Soerabaia** – Japanese aircraft bombed the city for weeks, disrupting the Marine Battalion (M.B.A.) and its defensive preparations.
* **Ground advances** – Japanese troops landed at multiple points on Java (e.g., near Bantam, Merak, Bobos, Rembang) and quickly overran the island, forcing the remaining Dutch forces to retreat to Tjilajap before evacuating to Australia and Ceylon.

## Impact on Aceh
Although the main combat occurred on Java, the Japanese advance threatened the entire Dutch East Indies, including Aceh. The province’s ports and oil installations were targeted for seizure, and the local marine detachments were ordered to destroy infrastructure to prevent Japanese use. The fall of Aceh followed the broader collapse of Dutch colonial resistance in [[1942]].

## Significance
The invasion marked the end of Dutch colonial rule in the archipelago and ushered in a period of Japanese military administration that lasted until the Allied liberation in 1945. The experience reshaped post‑war nationalist movements in Aceh, laying groundwork for later independence struggles.

**The Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)** was the embarkation and departure of [[Jan van Swieten|opperbevelhebber Jan van Swieten]] from [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] on [[26 April]] [[1874]] — three days after his [[Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)|afscheidsrede]] of [[23 April]] declaring the war "geëindigd" — aboard the stoomschip *Prins Alexander der Nederlanden*, saluted by a "saluut uit de zware vuurmonden der oorlogschepen," and leaving [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] to inherit a "zeer gebrekkige stelling" (very defective position) and the conduct of a war that was, in truth, only just beginning. Borel's verdict on the departure is lapidary: "met zijn vertrek was het tweede bedrijf van het Atjehsche drama afgespeeld" — with his departure the second act of the Atjeh drama was played out.

## The Troops Ship Out

On [[24 April|24]] and [[25 April]] [[1874]] the troops marked for return to Java were ingescheept. The reduction had been decided in the wake of [[Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)|van Swieten's declaration]] that the war was over. The Javaansche hulptroepen of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]] were among the first to go, along with a veld-batterij. The [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] — one of the two companies, with much of their materieel, including the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen of 12 c.M.]] — was likewise embarked for return to Java. The best geschut the Dutch possessed was, by van Swieten's order, leaving Atjeh at the very moment the enemy was reinforcing for a fresh offensive. The one voordeel of the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|échec at Lampoe Oek]] of [[16 April]] [[1874]] was, Borel drily notes, that a half bataljon extra was kept in Atjeh — "à quelque chose malheur est bon."

## The Departure

On [[25 April]] [[1874]] van Swieten himself boarded the stoomschip *Prins Alexander der Nederlanden* in the roadstead of [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]]. On the morning of [[26 April]] [[1874]] a "saluut uit de zware vuurmonden der oorlogschepen" — a salute from the heavy guns of the warships — announced his departure. The *Prins Alexander* weighed anchor and steamed out of the roadstead. Van Swieten would land at Batavia and proceed to the Netherlands, where he would publish his defence of the campaign. [[Generaal Pel|Kolonel Pel]] — who had commanded the 2de brigade throughout the expedition, who had personally led the flanking move that carried the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]], and who had been the most consistent advocate of an active defence — inherited the command of the bezettingskorps, the civil bestuur of [[Kota Radja]], and the conduct of the war.

## The Inheritance

What Pel inherited was, in Borel's account, a "zeer gebrekkige stelling." The Dutch held the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], the bivouak of [[Penajoeng]], the [[Marine-benteng|marine-benteng]] halfway down the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]], and the river mouth at [[Kwala Atjeh]]. Beyond that, nothing. The [[Pedir-dijk]] was in Dutch hands only nominally; the western approach to the Kraton was commanded by the new Atjehnees fortification at [[Lampoe Oek]]; the southern and eastern faces were dominated by enemy kampongs within easy rifle range. The coastal route to [[Marassa]] and the future site of [[Oeleh Leh]] was vulnerable; no proper [[Zeehoofd|zeehoofd]] had been built. The [[Atjehnees volkskarakter (Borel)|Atjehnees volkskarakter]] had been roused, not defeated. Within hours of van Swieten's departure, the first alarm would sound at [[Kota Radja]], and the [[Desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe (1874)|desecration of the Dutch graves at Lampoeloe]] would be perpetrated by the Atjehneezen overrunning the abandoned bivouak at [[Penajoeng]].

## Borel's Verdict

"Met zijn vertrek was het tweede bedrijf van het Atjehsche drama afgespeeld." Borel's framing of the campaign as a drama in five acts places van Swieten's departure at the close of the second. The first act — the [[First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)|first expedition]] under [[Generaal Köhler|Köhler]] — had ended in failure and the [[Atjeh-enquête|inquiry]]. The second act — the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second expedition]] under van Swieten — had opened with the [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing at Pedro-punt]] of [[6 December]] [[1873]] and closed with the departure of [[26 April]] [[1874]]. The third act — the period of the [[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]] — would now begin, with [[Generaal Pel|Pel]] struggling to recover from the position he had inherited, and the Atjehneezen pressing their advantage at every point.

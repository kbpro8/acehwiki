**The Aroe-baai-plannen** ("Arube Bay plans") was the project, debated in two waves ([[1905]]–[[1910]] and [[1913]]), to develop [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as the natural maritime terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network — and, in its most ambitious version, as a deep-water *Oceaanhaven* that would compete with [[Belawan|Belawan]], [[Singapore|Singapore]], and [[Sabang|Sabang]] as an outlet for the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] export trade. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the Aroe-baai-plannen represented the principal economic-development project of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh residency]] in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade, and the principal locus of commercial conflict between the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh residency]] and the established trading interests of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]].

## Van Heutsz and the Oceaanhaven proposal (1905–1907)

The earliest and most ambitious version of the project was the proposal by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] — Governor of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] [[1898]]–[[1904]] and Governor-General of [[Nederlands-Indië|Netherlands India]] [[1904]]–[[1909]] — to develop [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboean]] on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] into a full *Oceaanhaven* for the entire [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] cultivation zone. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was the *krachtig voorstander* (strong proponent) of the Aroebaai scheme. His advocacy stemmed from familiarity with [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]]'s northern geography from the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] campaigns, the bay's superior natural depth and shelter compared to [[Belawan|Belawan]]'s shoaling approaches, the potential to link the bay via the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] (then building southeast from [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]]) directly to the cultivation zone, and the strategic desire to integrate [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] economically. The *van Heutszgebergte* (van Heutsz Mountains) in the [[Batak Karo|Karo]] highlands was named after him, reflecting his association with the region's opening.

The original project envisioned a full *Oceaanhaven* at [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboean]] (on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]]), where the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] would terminate. The bay offered two deep channels (north and south of [[Pulo Sembilan|Pulau Sembilan]]/[[Poelo Kampi|Koempai]]) with good anchorage for large steamers, shelter from the Strait of Malaka's swell, proximity to the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] fields at [[Langsa|Langsa]]/[[Rantau Pandjang|Rantau Pandjang]], and a shorter sea route to [[Penang|Penang]]/[[Singapore|Singapore]] for northern cultivation areas ([[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Besitang|Besitang]]).

## The Handelsvereeniging Medan opposition and the Kraus-de Jongh report (1910)

The Handelsvereeniging Medan opposed the plan, arguing that [[Aroebai|Aroebaai]] lay too far north of the main production centres ([[Medan|Medan]], Tebing Tinggi, [[Pematang Siantar|Siantar]]), that land transport costs over the poor northern roads would negate harbour advantages, and that [[Belawan|Belawan]]'s central location in the cultivation zone was economically decisive.

In [[1910]] the colonial government commissioned engineer Kraus-de Jongh to evaluate. His report recognized [[Aroebai|Aroebaai]]'s technical superiority (depth, shelter, expansion potential) but concluded that general economic grounds favoured [[Belawan|Belawan]]: the cultivation zone's centre of gravity lay south of the [[Asahan|Asahan]], and [[Belawan|Belawan]]'s hinterland connections (rail, road, future canals) were already established. The government accepted Kraus-de Jongh's advice and launched a renewed [[Belawan|Belawan]] dredging program ([[1912]] onward) using a 700 m³ suction dredger. Depths improved from 7½ ft ([[1907]]) to 19 ft at low-water spring by [[1916]]. The Plan Nobel for a [[Belawan|Belawan]] ocean harbour was approved in principle. The [[Aroebai|Aroebaai]] ocean-harbour plan was *verlaten* (abandoned); only a modest steamer jetty at [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] for the [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] tankers and the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] was built.

## The 1913 Gouvernementsbesluiten and the revived Aroe-baai-plannen

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's [[1917]] account of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] is the longest single engagement with the project in the volume. The Bureau frames the plans as the natural extension of the [[1913]] Gouvernementsbesluiten (G. Bn. of [[17 June]] [[1913]] No. 59, [[16 October]] [[1913]] No. 25, [[8 November]] [[1913]] No. 40) that approved the *doortrekking* of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] from [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] to [[Besitang|Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboeban]] on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]].

The Bureau writes: *the defenders of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]], who see in this natural harbour a competitor of [[Belawan|Belawan]] and even of [[Singapore|Singapore]] and [[Sabang|Sabang]], hope, by the laying of this connecting link, to see the first step taken toward the realisation of those future plans, by the export of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] products (pepper, rubber, etc.) — which increases steadily with the reclamation of the [[Tamiang|Tamiangsche moerassen]] — to be led via [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]].*

The Bureau then narrates the commercial conflict that the Aroe-baai-plannen provoked with the established trading interests of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]]: the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij]]'s opposition to the direct [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat|Tandjoeng Slamat]] connection; the [[Belawan|Belawan-Deli]] trade's fear of competition from [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as a deep-water harbour; the [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] trade's parallel opposition; and the eventual deferral of the direct [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat|Tandjoeng Slamat]] connection in favour of a connection from [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] to the Atjeh-tram. The Bureau notes that the Aroe-baai-plannen were *of evident interest to the whole [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|gouvernement Atjeh]]* and that they were maintained *even when, in place of [[Belawan|Belawan-Deli]], the equally inadequate roadstead of [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] was put forward as the desired terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]].*

The Bureau's account of the [[Cultuurraad|Cultuurraad]]'s deliberations on the proposed [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] connection also notes the further proposal of a direct connecting line from [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] with [[Telok Taboeban|Telok Taboehan]] — there joining the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-net]] — as a compromise between the [[Cultuurraad|Cultuurraad]]'s preferred [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]] connection and the originally proposed direct [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat|Tandjoeng Slamat]] junction.

## Significance

The [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] represent, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the most ambitious single economic-development project of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh residency]] in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade — the project that would, if fully realised, have transformed the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency from a military-administrative territory into a coherent economic region centred on its own deep-water harbour. The [[1918]] Oostkust volume extends the picture: the [[1913]] Aroe-baai-plannen were the surviving fragment of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s much larger [[1905]]–[[1907]] Oceaanhaven proposal, defeated in [[1910]] by the Kraus-de Jongh report and the Handelsvereeniging Medan — a defeat that reduced [[Aroebai|Aroebaai]] from a projected ocean harbour to a mere petroleum-jetty terminus for the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]]. The deferral of the direct [[Besitang|Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat|Tandjoeng Slamat]] connection in [[1913]] marks the partial defeat of even the revived Aroe-baai-plannen at the hands of the established [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]] commercial interests — a defeat that, together with the [[1914]] outbreak of the European war, would defer the full realisation of the Aroe-baai project indefinitely.

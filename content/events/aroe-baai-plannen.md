**Aroe-baai-plannen** ("Arube Bay plans") was the project, debated and partially approved in [[1913]], to develop [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as the natural maritime terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network — and, in its most ambitious version, as a deep-water harbour that would compete with [[Belawan]], [[Singapore]], and [[Sabang]] as an outlet for the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] export trade. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the Aroe-baai-plannen represented the principal economic-development project of the Atjeh residency in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade, and the principal locus of commercial conflict between the Atjeh residency and the established trading interests of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast of Sumatra]].

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Aroe-baai-plannen

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] is the longest single engagement with the project in the volume. The Bureau frames the plans as the natural extension of the [[1913]] Gouvernementsbesluiten (G. Bn. of [[17 June]] [[1913]] No. 59, [[16 October]] [[1913]] No. 25, [[8 November]] [[1913]] No. 40) that approved the doortrekking of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] from [[Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban]] on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]].

The Bureau writes: "the defenders of the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]], who see in this natural harbour a competitor of [[Belawan]] and even of [[Singapore]] and [[Sabang]], hope, by the laying of this connecting link, to see the first step taken toward the realisation of those future plans, by the export of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] products (pepper, rubber, etc.) — which increases steadily with the reclamation of the [[Tamiang|Tamiangsche moerassen]] — to be led via [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]]."

The Bureau then narrates the commercial conflict that the Aroe-baai-plannen provoked with the established trading interests of the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]]: the [[Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij|Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij]]'s opposition to the direct [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat]] connection; the [[Belawan-Deli|Belawan-Deli]] trade's fear of competition from [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as a deep-water harbour; the [[Pangkalan Brandan]] trade's parallel opposition; and the eventual deferral of the direct [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat]] connection in favour of a connection from [[Pangkalan Brandan]] to the Atjeh-tram. The Bureau notes that the Aroe-baai-plannen were "of evident interest to the whole [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|gouvernement Atjeh]]" and that they were maintained "even when, in place of [[Belawan-Deli|Belawan-Deli]], the equally inadequate roadstead of [[Pangkalan Brandan]] was put forward as the desired terminus of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]]."

The Bureau's account of the [[Cultuurraad]]'s deliberations on the proposed [[Pangkalan Brandan]] connection also notes the further proposal of a direct connecting line from [[Pangkalan Brandan]] with [[Telok Taboehan]] — there joining the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-net]] — as a compromise between the [[Cultuurraad]]'s preferred [[Pangkalan Brandan]] connection and the originally proposed direct [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat]] junction.

## Significance

The [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] represent, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the most ambitious single economic-development project of the Atjeh residency in the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade — the project that would, if fully realised, have transformed the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] residency from a military-administrative territory into a coherent economic region centred on its own deep-water harbour. The deferral of the direct [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat]] connection in [[1913]] marks the partial defeat of the Aroe-baai-plannen at the hands of the established [[Oostkust van Sumatra|East Coast]] commercial interests — a defeat that, together with the [[1914]] outbreak of the European war, would defer the full realisation of the Aroe-baai project indefinitely.

**The Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)** was the first appearance of [[Panglima Bintang|T. Panglima Bintang]] — the territorial chief of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] — before the [[Dutch]] administration since the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]], an act of political submission that marked the beginnings of the disintegration of the Achehnese political coalition that had coalesced around Oemar in the spring of [[1896]]. Fearing Oemar's revenge, [[Panglima Bintang]] requested permission to settle within the concentrated line with his father-in-law, the Islamic prayer-leader of [[Lamara]]. The request was denied by the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], who ruled that [[Panglima Bintang]] should remain in his *moekim* at [[Poenir]] to protect his subordinates against molestation. The same day — [[20 June]] [[1896]] — [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] reportedly held a ceremony/feast at [[Lepong]], appointed new *panglima*'s, and prayed for Allah's blessing on his weapons.

## Background

By the third week of [[June]] [[1896]] the strategic situation had shifted decisively in favour of the [[Dutch]]. The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]]; the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] had been completed; the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] had reduced the western redoubt; and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] had broken the south-western *benteng*s and had driven the Pretender-Sultan from [[Panteh-Karang]]. The Achehnese political coalition that had coalesced around Oemar in the wake of the defection was beginning to disintegrate.

[[Panglima Bintang]] — the *hoeloebalang* of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] — had, as the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the III Moekims Daroe]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]] had confirmed, already secretly shown sympathy to the Dutch side. The *machtsvertoon* under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. Laceulle]] — which had searched the *kampong*s of [[Kandang]], [[Olehi-Loeng]], [[Poenir Le-Oe]], [[Goee-Gadjah]], and [[Tjabang]] — had tested the disposition of [[Panglima Bintang]] and had found him cooperative. Three weeks later, on [[20 June]] [[1896]], [[Panglima Bintang]] made his formal appearance before the Dutch administration — his first since the defection of [[29 March]] [[1896]].

## The Submission

The submission of [[Panglima Bintang]] on [[20 June]] [[1896]] was a carefully-managed political act. The *hoeloebalang* of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] — fearing Oemar's revenge, which he had good reason to dread, given the Achehnese chief's reputation for ruthlessness against those who had cooperated with the Dutch — requested permission to settle within the concentrated line with his father-in-law, the *imam* of [[Lamara]]. The request was, in essence, a plea for asylum: [[Panglima Bintang]] was asking the Dutch to take him and his family under their direct protection, in return for his political submission.

The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], denied the request. Vetter's ruling — politically shrewd, if emotionally harsh — was that [[Panglima Bintang]] should remain in his *moekim* at [[Poenir]] to protect his subordinates against molestation. The ruling was, in effect, a declaration that the Dutch would protect [[Panglima Bintang]] and his subordinates in their own territory, but would not provide him with the personal asylum he sought. The political logic was clear: a *hoeloebalang* who abandoned his *moekim* would lose his authority, and the Dutch needed [[Panglima Bintang]] in [[Poenir]] to anchor the political realignment of the [[III Moekims Daroe]]. The submission was, however, accepted in political terms: [[Panglima Bintang]] was henceforth to be regarded as a friend of the Government, and his subsequent cooperation — including the [[Disarmament of Buitenlinie Chiefs (mid-June 1896)|return of 12 of 16 trompladers]] in mid-June [[1896]] — would be received and acknowledged.

## The Concurrent Chandoeri of Oemar at Lepong

The same day — [[20 June]] [[1896]] — [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]] reportedly held a ceremony/feast at [[Lepong]], his coastal base on the [[Kroeng-Rababaai]]. At the *chandoeri* Oemar appointed new *panglima*'s — replacing those killed, wounded, or defected in the campaigns of May and June — and prayed for Allah's blessing on his weapons. The *chandoeri* was, in its way, the Achehnese counter-statement to the submission of [[Panglima Bintang]]: where [[Panglima Bintang]] submitted to the Dutch, Oemar reaffirmed his commitment to the resistance and sought divine sanction for the continuation of the war. The juxtaposition of the two events on the same day — [[20 June]] [[1896]] — captured, in microcosm, the political and spiritual stakes of the conflict.

## Significance

The Submission of Panglima Bintang on [[20 June]] [[1896]] was the first formal political submission of a major Achehnese *hoeloebalang* since the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]] and the strategic reversal of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. Its principal political effect was to confirm the disintegration of the Achehnese coalition: a *hoeloebalang* of [[Panglima Bintang|Bintang]]'s standing would not have submitted had he believed that Oemar's resistance could succeed. The submission was also a personal vindication of [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]]'s political strategy — the patient combination of military pressure (*tuchtiging*) with political outreach (*machtsvertoon*) that had begun with the [[Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the III Moekims Daroe]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]]. The denial of the request for asylum was, in this sense, as important as the submission itself: it established the principle that the Dutch would protect cooperative *hoeloebalang* in their own territory, rather than concentrating them in the consolidated line — a principle that would underwrite the political realignment of the second half of [[1896]]. The juxtaposition of [[Panglima Bintang|Bintang]]'s submission with Oemar's *chandoeri* at [[Lepong]] was, finally, a symbolic moment: the political and the spiritual heads of the Achehnese resistance were now pulling in different directions, and the coalition that had carried Oemar to the high point of the spring was beginning to fracture.

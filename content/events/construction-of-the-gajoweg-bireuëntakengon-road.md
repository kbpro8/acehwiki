**Construction of the Gajoweg (Bireuën–Takengon road)** (late 19th–early 20th c. (actie-tijd)) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. The Dutch Indisch Leger built the Gajoweg from Bireuën to Takengon (and the Atjeh-tram) under the most difficult conceivable conditions during the Aceh campaign. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to late 19th–early 20th c. ([[actie-tijd]]).

## Account

The Dutch [[Indisch Leger]] built the [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajoweg]] from [[Bireuën]] to [[Takengon]] (and the [[Atjeh-tram]]) under the most difficult conceivable conditions during the Aceh campaign. Originally heavily criticized for cutting through economically unimportant regions, but proved defensible militarily and economically beneficial. Reduced travel time from 3+ days to ~3 hours by car. Required enormous groundworks (e.g., in the [[Enang-ènangvallei]]). The road passes [[Blang Rakal]] ([[K.M.]] 46), the [[Geureudong]] flanks, [[Lampahan]] ([[K.M.]] 75), and reaches the highest point of [[1485]] m at [[K.M.]] 95. The '[[Gouverneur Van Daalen|Van Daalen]] boom' on the road was a living memorial to the [[March|march]] of [[Van Daalen|Majoor Van Daalen]] during the [[actie-tijd]] but was eventually killed by asphalting.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

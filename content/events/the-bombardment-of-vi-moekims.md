**The Bombardment of VI Moekims** was a naval operation conducted by the Dutch war fleet against the coastal fortifications and kampongs of the [[VI Moekims]] — a district on the west coast of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — during the later phase of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|Second Expedition]]. The bombardment, carried out by the ships of the squadron under Captain [[J. van Gogh|Van Gogh]], was intended to punish the VI Moekims for their continued hostility, to suppress the coastal fortifications that threatened Dutch communications, and to demonstrate Dutch naval power to the Acehnese subordinate states. The operation contributed to the submission of several of the west-coast statelets and to the depopulation of the coastal area.

## Context

By early [[1874]], the Dutch had established their main position at [[Kotta Radja]] and a network of outposts including [[Mosapie]], which commanded the lagoon as far as the [[Kwala Gighen|kwala of Gighen]]. The coastal districts of the west coast — including the [[VI Moekims]] — had been largely hostile to the Dutch throughout the conflict. The VI Moekims, lying to the west of the main Dutch position, presented a continuing threat to Dutch communications and to the ships of the blockade squadron operating along the coast.

The Dutch command, seeking to extend its influence and to punish hostile districts, authorized the naval forces to conduct operations against the west-coast fortifications.

## The Bombardment

The warships of the squadron, under the overall command of Captain [[J. van Gogh|Van Gogh]], conducted a bombardment of the coastal positions of the VI Moekims. The details of the engagement — the specific ships involved, the duration of the bombardment, and the precise targets — are recorded in Van Swieten's account as part of the broader pattern of Dutch naval operations during the campaign.

The bombardment had the intended effect of demonstrating Dutch power and punishing the hostile district. According to Van Swieten's report, the result was that the coastal area of the VI Moekims was "entirely abandoned" and the population withdrew to the mountains. The loss to the local chief [[Toekoe Nanta]] was significant: he lost communication with the sea, and his principal kampongs — [[Bital]] and [[Ketapang Doewa]] — lost their main means of subsistence, the fisheries and small trade.

## Consequences

The bombardment of the VI Moekims, combined with the occupation of [[Mosapie]] and other posts, had the effect of isolating the hostile interior from the coast and pressing the subordinate states toward submission. Several west-coast statelets submitted to Dutch authority in the weeks that followed, including those from [[Troemon]] northward to [[Wylah]].

Van Swieten noted that the effect of the bombardment was to deprive [[Toekoe Nanta]] and other hostile chiefs of their principal economic resources, thereby weakening their capacity for continued resistance. The operation demonstrated the value of naval power in supporting the land campaign and in coercing the coastal populations into submission.

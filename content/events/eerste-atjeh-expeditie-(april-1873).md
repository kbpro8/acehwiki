**The Eerste Atjeh-expeditie of April [[1873]]** — the first Dutch military expedition against the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] — was the opening action of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], conducted against the [[Meusigit Raja|Missigit]] (the great mosque) and the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] in [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]] under the command of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]], and ending in failure with the death of General Köhler on [[14 April]] [[14 April]] [[1873]], the loss of approximately fifty dead and four hundred wounded among his troops, and the re-embarkation of the expeditionary force on [[26 April]] [[26 April]] [[1873]]. The failure of the first expedition, and the demonstration it afforded of Atjehese fighting power, inaugurated the protracted forty-year war that would only be brought to an end by the systematic pacification under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]].

## The Outbreak of the War

The [[Declaration of War on Aceh (1873)|Dutch declaration of war on Atjeh]] of [[March]] [[1873]] followed the discovery, by the Netherlands Indies government, that the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] was on the point of concluding an agreement with a foreign power, by which Atjeh would cede territory and other advantages in exchange for help in case of war. The Dutch government, regarding its supremacy on [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] as at stake, chose to make foreign intervention in Atjeh impossible by force of arms. A strong force of land and sea power was despatched from Java under the government commissioner [[F. N. Nieuwenhuysen|F. N. Nieuwenhuysen]] to demand satisfaction for the Sultan's recent negotiations and to obtain guarantees for the future. The satisfaction was not obtained, and the war was declared.

## The Landing (8 April 1873)

The expedition, comprising "buiten de Marine, ruim 3000 man" (more than 3,000 men besides the Marine), landed on [[8 April]] [[8 April]] [[1873]] under heavy enemy resistance. It was generally believed that the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] could be taken without great resistance, and that the expedition would be over with that. "*De uitslag was geheel anders*" (The result was wholly different).

## The Advance (10 April 1873)

On [[10 April]] [[10 April]] the troops advanced over a broad lagoon inland, towards the kraton. The terrain was heavily overgrown, alternating with cultivated plains mostly rice fields, and the Dutch had no reconnaissance: "*er was echter weinig van bekend en niets van te zien*" (however, little was known of it and nothing of it was to be seen). The troops reached the [[Meusigit Raja|groote missigit]] (great mosque), which they knew to be near the kraton, and took it after bloody fighting — only to abandon it, retake it, and abandon it again. For several days the column sought in vain for the kraton itself, under heavy opposition, while the enemy pressed ever more impetuously and in greater numbers, threatening the line of retreat to the beach.

## The Death of Köhler (14 April 1873)

On [[14 April]] [[14 April]] [[1873]] — the fourth day of the advance — General Köhler was killed in action before the kraton. The conduct of the Dutch troops under fire was "*boven allen lof verheven*" (above all praise), but it was now clear that they had before them "*eenen dapperen, overmachtigen vijand, die zich met fieren moed, groote doodsverachting en taaie volharding verdedigde*" (a brave, superior enemy, who defended himself with proud courage, great contempt for death, and tenacious perseverance). The Dutch had already about 50 dead and 400 wounded, and with their small force there was no question of breaking through.

## The Withdrawal (17-26 April 1873)

On [[17 April]] [[17 April]] the column retired to the beach, primarily on the ground of the declaration of the Marine authorities that the unfavourable monsoon was about to set in and might for a long time interrupt communication with the ships. On [[26 April]] [[26 April]] [[1873]] the expeditionary force re-embarked, "*onverrichter zake*" (without having accomplished its purpose), and abandoned the roadstead of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. "*De eerste expeditie was mislukt*" (The first expedition had failed).

## The East Coast Establishment (May 1873)

The [[Koninklijke Marine|Marine]] remained to blockade the coast. On [[7 May]] [[7 May]] [[1873]] — only days after the Dutch flag had disappeared from the coast of Groot-Atjeh — the Dutch flag was raised on the [[Oostkust|East Coast]] in the little state of [[Edi|Edi]], whose *radja* had before the war requested to be placed under Dutch sovereignty. Under the able and tireless leadership of [[C. H. Bogaert|Lieutenant Bogaert]], who appeared in [[June 1873|June]] [[1873]] as *stationscommandant* for the East Coast, Dutch influence rapidly extended over the whole East Coast and a part of the [[Noordkust|North Coast]]. The contrast between the failure of the first expedition in Groot-Atjeh and the success of Bogaert's administration on the East Coast would be one of the recurrent themes of Kruijt's brochure.

## Significance

The failure of the Eerste Atjeh-expeditie of [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]] demonstrated that the Dutch had underestimated the fighting power of the Atjehers — a recurrent Dutch error, identified by [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]] as one of the structural causes of the protracted war. The death of General Köhler, the loss of approximately 450 casualties, and the re-embarkation of the expeditionary force constituted the first great Dutch reverse of the war. The [[Koninklijke Marine|Marine]] blockade that followed, and the despatch of the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|second expedition]] under [[Jan van Swieten|General van Swieten]] in [[December 1873|December]] [[1873]], inaugurated the protracted forty-year war that would only be brought to an end by the systematic pacification under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]].

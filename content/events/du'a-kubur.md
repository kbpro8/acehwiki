The **du'a kubur** ("grave prayer") is the brief supplication recited by the [[teungku]] at the graveside immediately after the interment of a body in [[Acheh]]. C. Snouck Hurgronje describes it in *The Achehnese* as the concluding act of the burial proper, marking the transition from the physical lowering of the corpse to the cycle of mourning observances that follow.

## Place in the Burial Sequence

After the bier has been set down at the edge of the grave and the body, wrapped in its *kaphan*, has been lowered into the *krungrung* (the lateral niche excavated within the grave), the teungku stationed above delivers a prayer for the repose of the departed soul. The congregation then performs what is known as *ratib* — the rhythmic repetition, a hundred times or more, of the words *Lā ilāha illā 'llāh*, "there is no god but Allah." This is essentially the act of [[tahlil|tahlil]] transferred from the house of mourning to the graveside. The teungku thereupon recites the *du'a kubur*, a short prayer distinct from the more extended supplications of the house.

## Aftermath

Once the prayer is concluded, plantains are brought out for the refreshment of those who have attended the interment, and the company returns in procession to the house of mourning. There it is met by a *kanduri* of rice and its accessories, called the *kanduri neueng wòe jijurat* — "the kanduri of those returning from the grave." This ceremony, like every other in which the teungku takes a leading part, brings him a fixed perquisite; together with the *ija peukreng* (the cloth used to dry the body after ablution) and the customary fee for the *tahlils* of the mourning period, it forms one of the standing sources of income of the parish religious official.

## Relation to Other Funeral Rites

The *du'a kubur* must be distinguished from the *tahlil* recitations proper, which continue in the house of mourning during the first forty days after death. It is also distinct from the *teuleukin*, the address to the deceased in the grave itself, which is recited by the teungku after the *kaphan* has been loosened and the body placed on its side with the face towards [[Mecca]]. The *du'a kubur* is, in fact, the simplest and most public of the prayers offered at the moment of burial, while the *teuleukin* is a technical instruction addressed to the dead regarding the questions to be put by the angels Munkar and Nakīr.

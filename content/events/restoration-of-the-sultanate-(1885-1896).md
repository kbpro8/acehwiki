**The Restoration of the Sultanate** — *poging tot herstel van het Sultanaat* — was the political project, pursued by the Atjehese resistance from [[1885]] onwards and attempted by the Dutch from [[1883]] onwards, to reconstitute the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] under a recognised Sultan as the political vehicle of either the Atjehese resistance (in the Atjehese version) or of Dutch indirect rule (in the Dutch version). The project's Atjehese realisation — the proclamation of the youthful pretender as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] by [[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]] in [[1885]]–[[1886]] — gave the resistance a political symbol and a central authority that sustained it through the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]; the project's Dutch realisation — the attempt by [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] and his successors to obtain the submission of the Sultan and the [[Kemala-party|Kemala-party]] — would be pursued until [[1891]] and abandoned only with the failure of the Van Teijn governorship.

## The Atjehese Restoration (1885–1886)

Under the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[Demmeni|Demmeni]] (1884–1886), the Dutch retired into the concentrated *stelling* around [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] to await patiently the enemy's submission. The enemy, abundantly supplied from the coasts, prosecuted the war in his own way. [[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]], guardian and father-in-law of the youthful pretender to the Sultanate, had the pretender declared of age and proclaimed and hailed as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]]. The court was arranged according to ancient usage, and notification of the accession was given to various courts.

The Sultan thus proclaimed was [[Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed|Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed]]. His influence greatly increased. In all Atjeh and for all Atjehers he was — and remained, at the time of Kruijt's writing in [[1896]] — the lawful head of the country. His decision was invoked in many disputes. From his name the orders for the war were everywhere given.

## The Dutch Pursuit of the Sultan (1883–1891)

Already under [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] (1883–1884), the Dutch had made attempts to obtain contact with the Sultan in order to attempt the restoration of the Sultanate under Dutch sovereignty — but the government raised objections. Under [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] (1886–1891), the Dutch were instructed to attempt to make contact with the Sultan and the [[Kemala-party|Kemala-party]], while avoiding the appearance that the Dutch sought it.

Naturally, the shrewd Atjehers had very quickly realised that the Dutch sought them, and began to speculate on this at once. [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]] and [[Toeankoe Hasschim|Toeankoe Hasschim]] &c., who were little pleased with the ever-increasing influence of [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and therewith of the religious party, made themselves heard. Already in [[June 1887|June]] [[1887]] the pretender Sultan [[Toeankoe Abdul Medjid|Toeankoe Abdul Medjid]] submitted, and — besides a gift of ƒ25,000 — enjoyed a monthly allowance of ƒ400. When in [[August 1888|August]] [[1888]] the Sultan [[Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed|Mohamed Daoed]] suddenly appeared in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and met with [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]], [[Imam Longbattah|Imam Longbattah]], and [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]], Van Teijn cherished a justified hope that it would now come to a conclusion; but after three weeks the Sultan departed again for [[Kemala|Kemala]].

Given the existing tension between [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]] and [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]], the latter — fearful of eventually being caught behind the net, or seeking to be ahead of the others — in [[1889]] offered to bring the Sultan to the Dutch. The Dutch did not accept the offer. More missions to Kemala, very solemn and imposing, followed — but for the Dutch all led to nothing.

In [[January 1891|January]] [[1891]] [[Tengkoe di Tirou|Tengkoe di Tirou]] and [[Panglima Polim|Panglima Polim]] died suddenly, and this too was an occasion for the Kemala-party to await first the consequences of the impending change of administration. In [[April 1891|April]] [[1891]] Van Teijn departed, his attempts at pacification with restoration of the Sultanate having failed.

## The 1890s: The Sultan Driven from Kemala (December 1894)

In [[December 1894|December]] [[1894]] [[Giegieng|Giegieng]] drove the Sultan [[Toeankoe Mohamed Daoed|Mohamed Daoed]] from [[Kemala|Kemala]], and the Sultan fled to [[Kwalla Batoe|Kwalla Batoe]] near [[Pedir|Pedir]]. With Pedir the Dutch then sought in vain to get him back to Kemala — also for the *status quo*. The Dutch helped both parties, Pedir and Giegieng; that was to maintain the balance between the two federations; the administration wanted to maintain the *status quo*.

## Kruijt's Judgement

Kruijt's judgement of the restoration policy is severe. The Dutch pursuit of the Sultan, he argues, gave the Atjehese resistance a political symbol and a central authority that sustained it through the middle phase of the war. The recognition of the Sultan by the Dutch — even in the form of pursuing his submission — was a recognition of his authority that strengthened him in the eyes of the Atjehers. "*Nu vrij algemeen erkend wordt, dat de positie van den Sultan van Atjeh sterker geworden is, dat hij, meer dan ooit vroeger, zelfstandig optreedt en dus een zeker centraalgezag uitoefent of vertegenwoordigt, moet ook diens onderwerping voor ons naar evenredigheid meer waarde hebben; behooren dus ook de wijze, waarop wij deze, of welke andere onderwerping ook, zullen aannemen en de redelijke voorwaarden daartoe, te worden vastgesteld.*" (Now that it is generally recognised that the position of the Sultan of Atjeh has become stronger, that he, more than ever before, acts independently and therefore exercises or represents a certain central authority, his submission must also be of more value to us in proportion; the manner in which we shall accept this, or any other submission, and the reasonable conditions for it, must therefore be determined.)

## Significance

The Restoration of the Sultanate is one of the principal political projects of the middle phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The Atjehese realisation — the proclamation of the youthful pretender as Sultan in the mosque at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] — gave the resistance a political symbol and a central authority that sustained it through the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] and the governorships of [[Demmeni|Demmeni]], [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]], [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe]], and [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]. The Dutch pursuit of the Sultan's submission — pursued from [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] through [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]] — strengthened the Sultan's authority in the eyes of the Atjehers and so contributed to the very resistance the Dutch sought to suppress. The restoration policy would be abandoned only with the failure of the Van Teijn governorship in [[April 1891|April]] [[1891]] and the subsequent shift of Dutch policy toward the arming of Atjehese auxiliaries under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]].

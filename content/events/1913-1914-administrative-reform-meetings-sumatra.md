**1913–1914 Administrative Reform Meetings** were a series of consultations held across Sumatra by the Dutch colonial government to discuss the further preparation of a comprehensive reform of the governance system (*bestuurswezen*) in the Dutch East Indies. The meetings, formally titled *Notulen der Bestuursvergaderingen ter bespreking van de verdere voorbereiding eener hervorming van het bestuurswezen in Nederlandsch-Indië*, took place between [[August]] [[1913]] and [[January]] [[1914]] in [[Palembang]], [[Benkoelen]], [[Padang]], [[Medan]], and [[Koeota-Radja|Koeota-Radja (Aceh)]].

## Background

The reform initiative aimed to restructure the administrative organization of the Dutch East Indies through a process of **administrative decentralization** (*bestuursdecentralisatie*) and the transfer of greater self-governance (*zelfbestuur*) to regional authorities. The meetings represented a critical stage in the development of these reorganization plans, which had been under discussion since [[1902]].

## Scope of Discussions

The meetings addressed two principal forms of administrative decentralization:

1. **Autonomy** (*autonomie*) — the management of local household affairs (*huishoudelijke belangen*) by local institutions
2. **Self-governance** (*zelfbestuur*) — the independent administration of specific branches of state responsibility by lower governmental organs

Discussions centered on the creation of larger provincial units (*gewesten*) to replace the existing smaller ones, the establishment of provincial councils (*Gewestelijke Raden*) and regency councils (*regentschapsraden*), and the degree to which the indigenous administrative apparatus should receive expanded authority.

## Sessions Held

**Session 1: [[12 August]] [[1913]] (Morning) — Palembang**
Opening session held at half-past nine, under the direction of **S. de Graaf**, Government Commissioner for the Reorganization of the Administrative System. The session presented foundational principles of the proposed reform, including the rationale for reducing the number of provinces, the creation of provincial councils with mixed European and indigenous membership, and the transfer of administrative functions from central authorities to provincial centers.

**Session 2: [[12 August]] [[1913]] (Afternoon) — Palembang**
Afternoon continuation focusing on the application of decentralization principles to the Outer Possessions (*Buitenbezittingen*), with particular attention to the question of whether autonomous local institutions comparable to those planned for Java and Madura could be established in regions such as Palembang.

**Sessions 3–4: [[13 August]] [[1913]] — Palembang**
Further discussions on the feasibility of autonomy in Palembang region, with emphasis on whether existing administrative units (subdivisions, districts, *marga* communities) possessed the historical cohesion and institutional capacity to function as autonomous bodies.

**Session 5: [[15 August]] [[1913]] (Evening) — Benkoelen**
Session addressing conditions in Benkoelen and issues arising from territorial reorganization, including the resistance of populations to administrative boundary changes.

**Sessions 6–7: [[16 August]] [[1913]] — Benkoelen**
Continuation of Benkoelen discussions.

**Sessions 8–10: [[19 August]]–[[20 August]] [[1913]] — Padang**
Meetings in Padang extending the reform consultations to the West Coast (*Westkust*) region.

**Sessions 11–13: [[13 January]]–[[15 January]] [[1914]] — Medan**
Continuation of consultations in [[Medan]], addressing conditions in the northern Sumatra region.

**Sessions 14–15: [[19 January]]–[[20 January]] [[1914]] — Koeota-Radja (Aceh)**
Critical sessions held in [[Koeota-Radja]], the capital of [[Aceh]], in which the possibility of merging [[Aceh]] with the [[Sumatra's Oostkust|East Coast of Sumatra]] was explicitly discussed. Controller [[J. Tideman]] raised concerns about the proposed merger, noting that such a union would combine regions of very different characteristics—Aceh with its Islamic and indigenous political structure versus the East Coast with its commercial and Malay population.

## Aceh-Specific Discussions

The meetings at [[Koeota-Radja]] addressed the administrative status and future organization of [[Aceh]]. Key points of discussion included:

- The proposed amalgamation of Aceh with [[Sumatra's Oostkust]] into a single enlarged provincial unit
- The administrative capacity of Aceh to participate in the new self-governance structure
- The integration of Aceh's indigenous administrative hierarchy into the colonial reorganization scheme
- The composition of provincial councils with representation from both European colonial officials and indigenous leaders

Controller [[J. Tideman]], a participant in the [[Medan]] and [[Koeota-Radja]] sessions, expressed reservations about the merger, warning that combining Aceh—where the population and administrative structure remained oriented toward Islamic governance—with the commercially dominated East Coast would create administrative difficulties.

## Outcomes and Significance

The meetings produced detailed records (*notulen*) documenting the state of reform thinking in early [[1913]]–[[1914]]. The discussions revealed tensions between:
- Centralized and decentralized governance approaches
- European administrative authority and indigenous self-administration
- Different regional capacities for institutional development
- Practical questions of personnel, finance, and administrative capability

The meetings at [[Koeota-Radja]] specifically positioned Aceh within the broader framework of Dutch East Indies administrative reorganization, marking a pivotal moment when colonial officials contemplated structural changes to Aceh's administrative relationship with the wider Dutch East Indies system.

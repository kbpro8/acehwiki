The **religious movement at [[Serbödjadi]]** of December [[1917]] was a brief outbreak set on foot by a certain [[Penghoeloe Koeala]], a [[Gajòs|Gajò]] from [[Leuboe Sigeunab]] who likewise "stood in an odour of sanctity" — stood in an odour of sanctity — and which cost the life of the *gezaghebber* of [[Lököp]] before it was suppressed.

## Course of events

The *gezaghebber* of Lököp thereupon went with the chiefs and a *bevolkingspatrouille* (population patrol) to the *gampong* [[Semboeang]], which the Penghoeloe had worked upon, but was killed in an encounter with the unwilling, while five men of his patrol were wounded.

The Penghoeloe then organised a three-day religious feast and associated himself with the guardian of a holy grave and the guardian's daughter. Then all the convinced withdrew; then came the patrols of the *[[Kompeuni]]*; then four dead fell, among them the guardian of the holy grave; then the Penghoeloe was taken prisoner — "and then it was over."

## Significance

The Serbödjadi movement ran, in du Croo's words, "in practically the same manner" to the [[Onrust in Linggö (1916)|unrest at Linggö]] of [[1916]]: a locally venerated holy figure, a small following of the convinced, a violent incident, and rapid suppression. It was one of the last such episodes before the retirement of [[H. N. A. Swart]] and the transfer of the government to purely civil hands in [[1918]].

The death of the *gezaghebber* of Lököp made it also one of the last Dutch administrative fatalities of the [[Atjeh-oorlog]].

**The Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)** was the second and heavier of the two short-lived reconnaissances ordered by [[Jan van Swieten|opperbevelhebber van Swieten]] in February [[1874]] — three days after the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|expedition to Bital]] of [[12 February]] — against the kampong of [[Ketapang Doewa]], south-west of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], in which six halve bataljons infanterie, supported by artillerie and cavallerie — a "voor Indië zeer aanzienlijke macht" that Borel dismisses as "geheel overbodig" (entirely superfluous) — attacked the kampong solely in front, suffered losses "aanmerkelijk grooter" than those of Bital, captured the high ground that could have given the Kraton "lucht van de westzijde" (air from the west side), and was then immediately abandoned on van Swieten's orders, since "eene uitbreiding onzer stelling lag echter niet in de plannen van den generaal van Swieten."

## The Force

Three days after the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|expedition to Bital]], the Dutch mounted a similar but far larger operation against [[Ketapang Doewa]]. The strijd here, Borel writes, was "veel heviger" than at Bital, and the Dutch losses were "aanmerkelijk grooter." Six halve bataljons of infanterie were committed, supported by artillerie and cavallerie — a force Borel calls "een voor Indië zeer aanzienlijke macht" (a very considerable force for the Indies). His verdict on the size of the force is withering: it was "geheel overbodig." A smaller column, properly handled, would have sufficed. The mass of six halve bataljons, deployed only in front of the kampong, was an operational absurdity: the force was too large for the object and yet too small — or rather, too clumsily handled — to achieve what a smaller, more agile force might have done.

## The Assault

As at [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|Bital]], the Dutch attacked only in front. The high ground of [[Ketapang Doewa]] rose above the surrounding [[Sawah|sawahs]], its boschrand lined with [[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboe-doeri]] and schietkokers from which the Atjehneezen poured a "moorddadig vuur" into the advancing linie. The six halve bataljons pushed forward across the open ground, took their losses, and at length stormed the kampong-rand. The high ground was carried. The enemy withdrew into the interior. The Dutch held the position — and at once the question arose: what was to be done with it?

## The Abandonment

The high ground of [[Ketapang Doewa]] was, in Borel's account, the natural site for a permanent post. From it the Dutch would have commanded the western approach to the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]]; they would have given the beleaguered garrison of [[Kota Radja]] "lucht van de westzijde" — breathing-room on the western face. A permanent post on the Ketapang Doewa heights would have forced the enemy to keep a body of troops on the western face, drawing pressure off the eastern and southern faces that the garrison was struggling to hold. But the establishment of such a post would have extended the Dutch stelling beyond the line van Swieten had drawn around the Kraton — and "eene uitbreiding onzer stelling lag echter niet in de plannen van den generaal van Swieten." So the six halve bataljons, having carried the height at a cost of "aanmerkelijk grooter" losses than Bital, were withdrawn the same day. The kampong was left to the enemy. The high ground that could have given the Kraton lucht van de westzijde was handed back. The pattern of capture-and-abandonment that had begun at [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|Lemboe]] in December [[1873]] and been repeated at Bital was now confirmed at Ketapang Doewa. Within days van Swieten would [[Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)|declare the offensive at an end]] and order the troops to "werken met schop en spade."

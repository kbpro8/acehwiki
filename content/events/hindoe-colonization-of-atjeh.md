**Hindoe colonization of Atjeh** (beginning of our era (first centuries CE)) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. Hindus from Voor-Indië (British India) came to the archipelago seeking rich products: kamfer (camphor), ivoor (ivory), goud (gold), reukhout (agarwood), specerijen (spices). 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to beginning of our era (first centuries CE).

## Account

Hindus from Voor-Indië ([[Hindostan|British India]]) came to [[East Indian Archipelago|the archipelago]] seeking rich products: [[Kapoer baroer|kamfer]] (camphor), ivoor (ivory), goud (gold), reukhout ([[Kajoe garoe|agarwood]]), specerijen (spices). Atjeh was the first land they reached. Date and place cannot be precisely determined. A preference developed for settling on the [[Pase / Pasei|East coast]] of Atjeh, where in later centuries the kingdom of [[Samoedra-Pasè]] arose. Influences: religion, language, art, and especially administrative/regnal organization. The Hindu-era 'stad' (city) model featured the vorst's fortified residence, market and trade quarter - early 'rijksvorming' (state formation).

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

**The Submission of Habib Abdurrahman** on [[13 October]] [[1878]] at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] was the surrender of the Arab sayyid [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdurrahman Zahir]], since March [[1876]] the principal leader of the Acehnese resistance in the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], to the governor-commander [[K. van der Heyden|K. van der Heyden]]. The submission was the turning point of the first phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], opening the way to the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign against the XXII Moekims]] of [[1879]]. On [[24 November]] [[1878]] the Habib was embarked for [[Jeddah|Jeddah]] aboard His Majesty's steamship *Curagao*, with a personal annual pension of 12,000 dollars, and assigned Jeddah as his residence.

## Background

The Habib had first appeared under the sultanate in Atjeh as a strict Mohammedan jurist, and by his learning, ability and descent had acquired a very great influence, even becoming first rijksbestuurder. At the beginning of the war he was absent on a mission to [[Constantinopel|Constantinople]] to invoke the help of the Sultan of Turkey; he then returned to [[Penang|Penang]]. From there he had at once encouraged the war-party, but at the same time opened negotiations with the Dutch government and offered his services in the war for a reward. As these offers were repeatedly rejected because he was distrusted, he decided to appear at the head of the Dutch opponents in Atjeh. From Pulau Penang he went, completely disguised as a matelot, aboard a schooner that took him to [[Edi-Tjoet|Edi Tjoet]], whence he reached Great Acheh overland, collecting funds for the war on the way. His arrival in March [[1876]] gave new life to the resistance.

## The Campaigns of 1878

By the spring of [[1878]] the Habib was once more on the offensive. In the night of [[7 June|7]]-[[8 June]] [[1878]] a band dared to attack the dwang-arbeiders hospital at [[Pantej Perak|Pantej Perak]], where it made a real slaughter. As soon as [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] had departed for [[Gedong|Gedong]], news came of the Habib's intention to make a great attack with 2,000 men in the [[IV Moekims|IV Moekims]] on the posts at [[Kroëng Raba|Kroëng Raba]], Boekit Seboen and [[Pakan Badak|Pakan Badak]]. On [[22 June]] [[1878]] Kroëng Raba was attacked by a great multitude of Atjehers, who shot at the garrison until [[24 June]] without venturing a storm. On [[23 June]] the Habib sent an Atjeher with a white flag and a letter inviting the post-commandant "to leave his position and fight with him in the open field," and on [[24 June]] a second letter offering free withdrawal on condition that the Dutch fortification was destroyed.

The government at Batavia ordered Van der Heyden to make an end of the situation and to drive the Habib and his bands out. Reinforced, he was able to act vigorously. On [[23 July]] [[1878]] the important campaign in the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] began with three columns; on [[28 July]] [[1878]] the [[Capture of Montassik (28 July 1878)|missigit Montassik was taken]]. Operations continued from [[1 August]] [[1878]] to [[23 March]] [[1879]], with constant reconnaissances and patrols, especially in the sawah of Lamkrak west of Montassik. The tuchtiging of [[19 August]] [[1878]] of the sawah of Lamkrak in particular made an impression on the enemy.

## The Negotiations

In August and September [[1878]] submissions and negotiations began to come in from various sides — on [[25 August]] [[1878]] the first by the Habib himself, soon followed by others. The result of the negotiations was that the Habib submitted on [[13 October]] [[1878]]. "On the afternoon of that day, about two o'clock, he arrived at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]], where the governor-commander immediately received him and accepted his submission. The following day [[Toekoe Moeda Baid|Toekoe Moeda Baid]], the head of the VII Moekims in the Sagi XXII, was also sworn in and recognised in his dignity. Their example was followed by several other chiefs."

## The Habib's Conduct after Submission

"The Habib remained some weeks at Kota Radja. With a view to the operations already previously planned in the direction of Tjot Bada and [[Indrapoeri|Indrapuri]], the very precise data he provided about the region south of Anak Galoeëng were of great value. After his submission he behaved always chivalrously and loyally towards us; in all respects he showed that he wished to contribute to ending the struggle. His letters or envoys to various chiefs, to urge them to submission, however, had little result, as most considered him a traitor to the national cause."

## Exile to Jeddah

"He departed on [[24 November]] [1878] with His Majesty's steamship *Curagao* to Arabia, where Jeddah was assigned to him as his residence, with a personal annual pension of 12,000 dollars." The choice of Jeddah was a political exile, removing him from the Atjeh theatre while honouring his rank as a sayyid by allotting him a residence near the holy places.

## Significance

The Habib's submission was the turning point of the first phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. With his removal, and the subsequent [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|campaign against the XXII Moekims]] and [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|campaign against the XXVI Moekims]] of [[1879]], the first conquest of Great Acheh was completed. The Habib's role, however, was in some sense taken over by the religious leaders — the ulamas and teungkus — who, financed by the *sabil* share of the [[Jakeuet (zakat)|jakeuet]] or zakat, kept the resistance alive through the period of the [[Dutch Concentration Scheme|concentration scheme]].

**The Telaga Said drilling of [[1885]]** is the founding episode of the [[Oil exploration in North Sumatra|northern-Sumatran oil industry]] — the drilling, under the leadership of the government mining engineer [[Ph. C. Fennema|Fennema]] and on the [[1883]] concession of the private entrepreneur [[J. Zulkler|J. Zulkler]], that "proved to be an outstanding success" and accordingly became the first productive oil well in northern Sumatra. The Telaga Said drilling is the originating technical episode of the entire northern-Sumatran oil industry, including its subsequent extension onto [[Acheh|Atjeh]] territory with the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields.

## The 1883 Zulkler concession

For the [[1883]] Zulkler concession, see the entry on [[J. Zulkler]]. In summary: in [[1883]] the private entrepreneur J. Zulkler obtained the first oil concession in North Sumatra; he encountered "considerable difficulties in drilling on account of an abundant flow of gas" and asked the [[Bureau of Mines in Indonesia|Government Bureau of Mines]] for assistance.

## The 1885 Fennema-led government crew

The government response to Zulkler's request for assistance was to despatch a government crew, under the leadership of the mining engineer Fennema, with drilling equipment. Van Bemmelen's account reads:

> "A government crew, under the leadership of the mining engineer FENNEMA, was sent with drilling equipment and shortly afterwards, in [[1885]], this oil venture proved to be an outstanding success."

The episode is the first documented instance of the public-private partnership model that would subsequently characterise the entire East Indian oil industry: the private concessionary (Zulkler) held the legal rights, but the government — through the [[Netherlands Indies Geological Survey|Government Bureau of Mines]] and its mining engineer Fennema — supplied the technical expertise and the drilling equipment. The success of the Fennema-led drilling in [[1885]] established the model that would subsequently be repeated across the East Indian oil industry, in which the colonial state played a direct technical role in the development of the oil economy.

## The 1890 founding of Royal Dutch

The success of the Telaga Said drilling attracted further private investment, and in [[1890]] the concession became the first oil property of the newly founded *Koninklijke Maatschappij tot ontginning van Petroleum terreinen in Nederlandsch Indië* — the future [[Royal Dutch Shell|Royal Dutch]]. The [[1890]] founding of the *Koninklijke Maatschappij* accordingly represents the consolidation of the northern-Sumatran oil industry under a single corporate umbrella, and the beginning of the corporate sequence that would lead, via the [[1907]] amalgamation with Shell, to the creation of the [[B.P.M.]] as the dominant East Indian oil company.

## The 1940–1941 Telaga Said production

By the [[1940]]–[[1941]] period, the Telaga Said field was a small mature producer operated by the [[B.P.M.]], with production of 1,076 tons of 1000 kg in [[1940]] and 960 tons in [[1941]]. The decline from 1,076 tons to 960 tons (–10.8 %) is consistent with the gradual depletion of a small mature field. The Telaga Said field's historical significance as the originating site of the northern-Sumatran oil industry made it more important than its modest late-colonial output would suggest.

## Significance

The Telaga Said drilling of [[1885]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is the founding episode of the northern-Sumatran oil industry — the industry that would, by [[1940]]–[[1941]], extend onto Atjeh territory with the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh]], [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang]] and [[Atjeh oil complex]] fields. The drilling is also the first documented instance of the public-private partnership model that would subsequently characterise the entire East Indian oil industry, and the success of the Fennema-led drilling in [[1885]] established the model that would subsequently be repeated across the East Indian oil industry. The fact that the originating asset of the entire Royal Dutch (and subsequently Royal Dutch Shell) oil empire was the [[1885]] Telaga Said drilling on the [[1883]] Zulkler concession also makes the northern-Sumatran oil industry the founding locus of the company's global presence.

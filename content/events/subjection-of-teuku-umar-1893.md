**The Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)** — known in the source as the *onderwerping van Toekoe Oemar* — was the formal act by which the [[Achehnese]] territorial chief [[Teuku Umar]] — then styling himself *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* — swore an oath of loyalty to the [[Dutch]] colonial government together with fifteen of his war-commanders, in exchange for the title of [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government of [[Nederlandsch-Indië]] and authorisation to raise a paid *legioen* of 250 men. Authorised by the [[Government Resolution of 5 September 1893]] (No. 1 geheim) and consummated by a confirmation act of [[30 September]] [[1893]], the subjection was the high-water mark of the Dutch policy of relying on native auxiliaries to subdue the recalcitrant [[Lamkrak]] and other interior mukims of [[Atjeh]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, it also set the stage for Umar's defection of [[29 March]] [[1896]] and the consequent collapse of the [[buitenlinie]].

## Background

By the late summer of [[1893]] the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] had reached a strategic deadlock. The Dutch held the consolidated line around [[Koeta Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh]] but could not break the [[Achehnese]] resistance in the interior, where the [[Lamkrak]] mukims and the [[XXII Moekims]] of the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims sagi]] remained obdurate. [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] and the colonial administration sought a native collaborator of sufficient standing to act as a force multiplier. [[Teuku Umar]] — *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] and an opportunist whose star had risen during earlier campaigns — had begun to demonstrate his usefulness by clearing the [[XXV Moekims]] in [[August]] [[1893]].

Encouraged by these good services, the Government resolved on [[5 September]] [[1893]] (Government Resolution, *Gouvernementsbesluit*, No. 1 geheim) to take Umar into formal subjection. The resolution conferred on him the hereditary-sounding title *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan en Panglima Prang Besar* — a deliberately hybrid combination of an Achehnese warrior name (*Djohan Pahalawan*, "champion-hero") with a Dutch military rank (*Panglima Prang Besar*, "great war-commander") — and authorised him to raise and maintain a paid *legioen* of 250 warriors under three blood-relative commanders and forty *panglima's*. This *legioen* — known in the source as the [[Legioen van T. Djohan]] — was to be armed, paid, and provisioned by the Government and directed against the still-hostile interior.

## The Oath of 30 September 1893

The formal subjection talso place on [[30 September]] [[1893]]. On that day, in the presence of Dutch civil and military officials, Umar and fifteen of his *panglima's* — representing the principal fighting captains of his following — swore the oath of loyalty to the [[Queen of the Netherlands|Queen]] and to the Government of [[Nederlandsch-Indië]]. The text of the act of confirmation, drawn up in Dutch and Malay, named Umar *Panglima Prang Besar van het Gouvernement* and specified the composition and emoluments of his *legioen*. In exchange for the title, weapons, and a monthly subsidy, Umar undertalso to deploy his forces against the Government's enemies at the direction of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]].

Although the subjection was hailed in official circles as a diplomatic triumph, Kruisheer notes that the measure was not universally welcomed. The customary chiefs of the [[XXVI Moekims]] — whose affairs Deykerhoff had shortly before (on [[21 October]] [[1893]]) also entrusted to Umar — resented the elevation of an outsider over their own hereditary leadership. This resentment would fester over the following three years and form one of the grievances Umar would later invoke in his [[First Letter of Teuku Umar|first letter]] to the [[Governor of Atjeh]] explaining his defection.

## Significance

The subjection of Teuku Umar was the founding act of the Dutch allied policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]]. It transformed the strategic geometry of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] by inserting a native auxiliary force of proven effectiveness into the colonial order of battle and by transferring responsibility for the [[Lamkrak]] problem, in the first instance, from Dutch columns to Umar's *legioen*. The policy would produce impressive short-term gains — including the [[Conquest of Ana' Galong (30 November 1893)|conquest of Ana' Galong]] in [[November]] [[1893]] and the operations in the [[V Moekims Montassik]] in early [[1894]] — but it also handed weapons, training, and operational knowledge to a man whose loyalty proved, in the spring of [[1896]], to have been entirely instrumental. When [[Teuku Umar]] turned his Dutch-supplied arms against the Government on [[29 March]] [[1896]], the very same *legioen* that had been raised under the confirmation act of [[30 September]] [[1893]] became the spearhead of the [[Achehnese]] counter-offensive that besieged [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] and forced the wholesale evacuation of the [[buitenlinie]].

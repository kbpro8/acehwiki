**Treason of Teuku Umar** refers to the dramatic political and military defection of Acehnese chieftain [[Teuku Umar]] (then titled *Teuku Johan Pahlawan*) in [[March]] [[1896]], which completely reshaped Dutch colonial policy during the Aceh War.

## The Story of the Defection

In [[1893]], seeking to turn local power structures to their advantage, the Dutch colonial government in [[Kuta Raja]] allied with [[Teuku Umar]]. Umar formally submitted to Dutch authority, was awarded the title *Teuku Johan Pahlawan*, and was put in command of a large indigenous legion funded and equipped by the Netherlands East Indies administration.

For nearly three years, Umar assisted the Dutch inside the defensive perimeter of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. In early [[1896]], the colonial authorities entrusted Umar with 800 modern breech-loading rifles, heavy stocks of ammunition, and f 18,000 in cash to clear insurgent bands operating in the outer districts. However, on [[29 March]] [[1896]], Teuku Umar executed a carefully prepared plan: he defected back to the Acehnese resistance, taking all 800 rifles, hundreds of thousands of rounds of ammunition, and the cash reserves with him to join the anti-colonial struggle alongside his wife, Cut Nyak Dhien.

## Consequences and Aftermath

The defection produced outrage and panic in Batavia and The Hague. It thoroughly discredited the defensive [[Concentratie-Stelsel|concentratie-stelsel]] implemented in [[1884]], proving that reliance on client chieftains inside a static perimeter left the colony vulnerable.

In response, the colonial administration appointed Colonel [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] as military commander and Governor of Aceh. The Dutch abandoned the defensive posture, launched active offensive operations throughout the territory, and unleashed the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]] under Captain [[Jonkheer G. J. W. C. Graafland]] to hunt down Umar's forces in the jungle.

**The ambush at Tjot-Goe** was the unexpected attack, on [[29 July]] [[1884]], of a Dutch patrol of 27 bayonets on its return march to [[Tjot-Goe]] by the followers of [[Imam Habib (Tjot-Goe)|Imam Habib]], an Atjehnese chief who passed for a Dutch-friendly head and was a *leverancier* (deliverer/supplier) of the government. Nine men were killed, eight wounded, and ten rifles with ammunition taken as booty (Koloniaal Verslag, cited by [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] in 1886). Place and date belong to the same period as the [[Lambesi treachery (3 July 1884)|treachery of Toekoe Oemar]] of [[3 July]] [[1884]]: both were cited by Verstege as characteristic of the unreliability of even the "befriended" chiefs in the last summer before the execution of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration]]; the post of Tjot-Goe itself, outside the new line, would be provisionally retained until the tramway from [[Ketapang Doewa]] to [[Lambaroe]] was ready (see [[Completion of the concentrated position (March 1885)]]).

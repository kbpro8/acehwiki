**The killing of Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed Lam Tenzah** was an incident of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] which took place on [[16 May]] [[1897]], in which the Acehnese chief [[Toekoe Tjoet Machmoed Lam Tenzah]] was killed together with his wife, his children and seven followers while they slept, by volleys fired through the thin walls of their dwelling. It was cited by [[H. van Kol]] in the [[Atjeh debate in the Tweede Kamer (16-17 November 1897)|Atjeh debate]] of [[16 November]] [[1897]] as a concrete instance of the demoralisation produced by the war.

## The event

According to the account Van Kol drew from the Dutch weekly *[[De Kroniek]]* of [[16 May]] [[1897]], one of these "heerschappen" — the sardonic term used in the source for Acehnese chiefs — was killed on that day with his wife and children and seven followers as they slept, "door salvo's te geven door de dunne wanden der woning, tot elk geluid verstomde" — by giving volleys through the thin walls of the dwelling, until every sound was silenced.

The *Kroniek* reporter added the ironic phrase that the dead had thereby "een reiswijzer naar betere gewesten hadden verkregen" — obtained a guide to better regions.

Van Kol placed the incident immediately after the words attributed to [[Rutgers van Rozenburg]] about dividing fallen Acehnese in two "om hun entrée in den Turkschen Hemel te bemoeielijken," and immediately before his statement that he would keep silence about still more abominable facts at his disposal, having had enough in these to point to the terrible demoralisation which followed from bloodshed.

## Context

The killing belongs to the phase of the war that opened after the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] in March [[1896]], when Dutch operations in the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]] shifted to the aggressive pattern of mobile columns, night surprises and the systematic pursuit of individual chiefs later defended in the chamber by [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] as the [[Post-1896 system in Atjeh|present system]]. The technique described — surrounding a house at night and firing volleys through its walls — was the characteristic method of the *[[Overval|overval]]* or surprise attack on a sleeping *kampong* house.

The source gives no further detail of the location of Lam Tenzah, the composition of the Dutch detachment, or the name of its commander.

## Aftermath in the chamber

The Minister of Colonies [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] did not address this case specifically. Replying to the general charge of cruelty, he stated that women and children watching a fight from a distance might be struck, since one no longer had control of a bullet once fired, but that of cruelties committed in cold blood he had heard nothing and did not believe they had been committed. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] declared that he fully endorsed Van Kol's condemnation of cruelties "indien metterdaad die feiten zoo zijn," and that no member of the chamber and no Dutch Minister of Colonies would hesitate to condemn such acts if concretely and clearly proved.

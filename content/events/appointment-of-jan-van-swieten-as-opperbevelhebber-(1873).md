**The Appointment of Jan van Swieten as opperbevelhebber (1873)** was the decision taken in November [[1873]] to recall the gepensioneerd luitenant-generaal [[jan-van-swieten|Jan van Swieten]] from retirement, vest him with the dual offices of opperbevelhebber der zee- en landmacht and regeerings-commissaris te Atjeh, and grant him "bijna onbeperkte volmacht" to conduct the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]]. The appointment was controversial from the first: a retired officer recalled over the heads of active-duty generals, a stranger to the new generation of the [[Indische Leger|Indische leger]], and paired — against the grain — with a deputy whose inzichten differed sharply from his own.

## The Choice

The failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)|first expedition]] and the death of [[j-h-r-kohler|Generaal Köhler]] outside the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] on [[1-april|1 April]] [[1873]] had so shocked the Dutch nation that the government felt it must produce a commander of unimpeachable seniority. Van Swieten, a veteran Indisch krijgsman of "kundig, kalm en beleidvol" reputation, was recalled from retirement. Borel records the widespread surprise of the army: many officers were astonished that a retired officer was passed over the heads of active-duty generals, and the Indische leger, which "no longer knew him personally," found him "geenszins populair." The civil charge was no smaller than the military one: as regeerings-commissaris he combined the opperbevelhebberschap with "bijna onbeperkte volmacht," a concentration of authority remarkable even in the colonial context.

## Verspijck as Tweede Bevelhebber

Alongside van Swieten the government placed [[generaal Verspijck|Generaal Verspijck]] as tweede bevelhebber. The pairing was intended to complement van Swieten's caution with Verspijck's energy. Yet Borel insists the government was unaware of how sharply the two men's inzichten differed on krijgskundig gebied. Verspijck would in due course propose landing the third brigade at the [[Kroeng-Raba-baai|Kroeng Raba-baai]] to take the Kraton from two sides — a plan van Swieten rejected out of "vrees" — and would personally lead the [[omtrekking|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]] that actually carried the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|Kraton]]. He was, Borel writes, "te goed soldaat" to voice his disagreements publicly; the result was that the strategische fouten of the campaign were never corrected at the time.

## Van Swieten's Programme

In his opening address in Batavia in November [[1873]] van Swieten predicted that the Atjehneezen "onmogelijk hunne strijdkrachten lang bij elkander zouden kunnen houden" — that the enemy could not keep a field army together for long. It was an assessment that would prove catastrophically wrong. His reputation among his peers rested on a "kundig, kalm en beleidvol" manner and, in Borel's telling, on a marked "gruwel van bloedvergieten" — a horror of shedding blood that would, in the coming months, govern every operational choice he made: leaving the third brigade at Padang, refusing the encircling movement, abandoning [[lemboe|Lemboe]] the day after it was won, declining reconnaissance before the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|Mesigit storm]], recalling the [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] and the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]] to Java, and finally [[Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)|declaring the war ended]] in his [[23-april|23 April]] [[1874]] afscheidsrede. Each of these Borel would lay at the door of an appointment which, however well-intentioned, set the second expedition on its course from its very first day.

## Brooshooft's Account

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft records that after the April [[1873]] reverse the [[netherlands|Netherlands]] government resolved that a second failure at Atjeh could not be risked, because Dutch authority across the archipelago rested on the reputation of arms. The retired sixty-six-year-old Lieutenant-General [[jan-van-swieten|Jan van Swieten]] — a veteran of the [[Java War (1825-1830)|Java War]] living at The Hague — was appointed both *opperbevelhebber* (supreme commander) and *Regeeringscommissaris* (Government Commissioner), with combined military and political authority. His age and his reputation for personal bravery and political moderation were thought to fit him for the delicate task of subduing Atjeh by force while trying to win over the population.

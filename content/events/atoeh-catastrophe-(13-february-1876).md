**The Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)** was the destruction, on a *drassige sawah* (marshy rice paddy) near [[Lamsajoen]], of a detachment of two officers and fifty-seven men — ten of them unarmed — under [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]] (the younger), marching from [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] to reinforce the post at [[Atoeh]], which was attacked almost within sight of its destination by Atjehneezen springing from the *boschrand* with what Borel calls "eene woeste onverwachte charge van cavallerie."

## The march

Pel, having granted his troops only a couple of days' rest at K. Radja after the [[Pel's offensive against the VI, IV and IX Moekims (1875–1876)|conquest of the XXV Moekims]], advanced in an easterly direction on [[13 February]] [[1876]] with three colonnes against the [[III Moekims]]. The right colonne (13de battalion, part of the [[Bataljon Barissans van Madura|Barissans]] and one section artillery) was under [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|B. E. Mekern]]; the middle (12de battalion, remainder of the Barissans, two sections artillery) under [[Majoor Jeltes|Jeltes]]; the left (right half-battalion and a section artillery) under [[Majoor Diepenheim|Diepenheim]]. [[Pango]] was conquered and a post established. On the same day [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|kapitein van Swieten]] marched from K. Radja with his small detachment to reinforce Atoeh.

## The massacre

Almost at destination, on the *drassige sawah* near Lamsajoen, the enemy sprang from the *boschrand*. The attack was "zoo snel" that most soldiers were heavily wounded before they could *verzamelen* (form up). Nearly all were killed "met wonden overdekt"; only a few escaped by flight. The bodies lay on the field; weapons and ammunition were carried off by the enemy.

## Borel's analysis

Borel asks how a fairly strong, *achterladegewapende* (breechloader-armed) detachment could be *overvallen* on open terrain in broad daylight. His answer is to recall the [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|disaster of 31 December 1874]]: a march over a doorweekte *sawah*, where all order is lost, exposes troops to exactly such a charge — the Atjehnese rush, Borel writes, was "like a wild, unexpected cavalry charge." On hearing the news, [[Majoor B. E. Mekern|B. E. Mekern]] changed route, marched on Atoch, *zuiverde* the terrain, and recovered the bodies of van Swieten's detachment, which were "voor schending bewaard" and "met de hun verschuldigde eer ter aarde besteld" — preserved from desecration and buried with the honour due to them.

**The Occupation of the ooster-benteng (17 August 1874)** was the surprise seizure by [[Kapitein van Randwijk|kapitein van Randwijk]] of a *grafheuvel* (burial mound) east of [[Pakan Atjeh]], at double-quick pace, and its conversion under heavy enemy pressure into the new fortified post named the *ooster-benteng* (eastern fort) — a post which, Borel laments, had to be won by a hard fight "seven months after the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the Kraton]]" for a scrap of ground within rifle-shot of the Dutch parapet.

## Background

For several days the Dutch had noticed extraordinary enemy activity east of Pakan Atjeh, on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]]: gatherings, *lilla*-fire, and movements across the river from [[Lemboe]], [[Langkroek]] and Pinang. Van Randwijk, commandant at Pakan Atjeh, proposed a dawn dash for the mound.

## The seizure

At first light on [[17 August]] [[1874]] van Randwijk marched out with his company and seized the mound *in draf* (at the double-quick), taking the enemy completely by surprise. Sandbag parapets were at once thrown up. The Atjehneezen, realising the Dutch meant to stay, raised the alarm on gongs and gathered from every side — including across the river from Lemboe, Langkroek and Pinang. The little garrison on the mound was caught in a crossfire; one detachment after another had to be sent from headquarters. The whole garrison of [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] stood to arms; the artillery on all bastions opened up, and *kartetsen* (canister) was used at Pakan Atjeh itself. By six in the evening the mound was defensible. The day's cost was more than twenty dead and wounded.

## Borel's verdict

Borel's lament is double-edged. He praises van Randwijk's initiative and the new post's immediate worth: once the ooster-benteng was established, the Atjehneezen "could do nothing more against Pakan Atjeh," which enjoyed a quiet unprecedented since the landing. But he insists that the fight itself was a scandal: seven months after the Kraton's capture a hard struggle had been needed for a scrap of ground within rifle-shot of the parapet. The same lesson — that the [[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]] merely postponed fights and made them bloodier — would be repeated in the [[Battle outside the ooster-face (13 October 1874)|battle outside the ooster-face]] two months later.

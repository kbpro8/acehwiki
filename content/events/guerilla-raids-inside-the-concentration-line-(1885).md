**The guerilla inside the concentration line of 1885** was the series of surprises, ambushes, attacks on watches and workers, and destructions of tramway and telephone, which the Atjehnese resistance kept up throughout [[1885]] against the Dutch troops *inside* their own [[Geconcentreerde Stelling|concentrated position]] — the line which the government had promised the public would, once for all, restore safety. The fullest contemporary record is the list kept by majoor [[M. T. H. Perelaer|Perelaer]] and read by him at the [[Kiesvereeniging Burgerpligt|Burgerpligt]] meeting of [[26 January]] [[1886]] (Bijlage A to [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]]'s pamphlet), supplemented there by the government's journal reports for Atjeh; it shows that there was no safety within the line for goods and troops, "zelfs niet voor onze troepen, zelfs niet in de onmiddellijke nabijheid van Kota Radja" ("not even for our troops, not even in the immediate neighbourhood of Kota Radja").

## The first incidents (February-April 1885)

According to the journal of the Governor of Atjeh the line was closed in February [[1885]]. Even before it was actually completed the enemy began his daily game: on [[25 February]] [[1885]] — in the middle of the day — apparently friendly Atjehers surprised the watch at [[Penditi]], close by [[Kuta Raja|Kota Radja]]; nine men were wounded (three mortally) and two rifles stolen (also mentioned by Verstege in his lecture: "schijnbaar bevriende Atjehers, op het midden van den dag, overvielen onze wacht te Penditi"). At the ground-clearing in the [[VI Moekims]] the workers were fired upon: four wounded, among them luitenant [[Luitenant Nyland (1885)|Nyland]]. On [[25 February]] [[1885]] the Atjehnese broke up the [[Décauville tramway]] inside the line and carried off the rails. In the night of [[24 March|24]] to [[25 March]] [[1885]] the workers and their cover at the clearing were fired upon: three wounded, among them luitenant [[Luitenant Colenbrander (1885)|Colenbrander]]. Luitenant [[Luitenant Tuinenburg (1885)|Tuinenburg]] was wounded at [[Lampermej]], close by the tramway bridge. On [[6 April]] [[1885]], clearing the ground between [[Lambaroe]] and [[Siroen]], the Dutch lost two dead and ten wounded.

## May and June

On [[13 May]] [[1885]] the same kind of attack was repeated; on [[16 May]] [[1885]] a patrol of 21 men under luitenant [[Luitenant Nyland (1885)|Nyland]], between [[Lamara]] and Kota Radja, fell upon the enemy: two dead and seven wounded, of whom three of the wounded died afterwards. In the night of [[5 June|5]]-[[6 June]] [[1885]] the sentry behind the encampment at [[Panteh Perak]], in the immediate neighbourhood of Kota Radja, was attacked, severely wounded and robbed of his rifle. In the night of [[6 June|6]]-[[7 June]] [[1885]] a band of twenty men fired upon the kampong [[Blang-Oe]], situated between [[Oleh-leh]] and Kota Radja. On [[9 June]] [[1885]], at the making of a dyke near [[Siroen]], luitenant [[Luitenant Veerman|Veerman]] and two workers were wounded; on [[17 June]] [[1885]] a patrol of 25 men, on the way from [[Tjot-Goe]] to the line, fell upon the enemy: two dead and four wounded, the enemy carrying off two rifles.

## August and September

On [[9 August]] [[1885]] a reconnaissance patrol six men strong, near the benteng [[Lamrong]], was attacked: six wounded; the enemy carried off three rifles. On [[11 August]] [[1885]] the night watch of six men, returning from [[Lamara]] to [[Ketapang Doewa]], was attacked: six wounded, of whom three died the same day; two rifles were lost. On [[14 August]] [[1885]] a reconnaissance patrol of the main guard of Kota Radja was fired upon in the shop-street of [[Gedeh]]: two dead, and the enemy took two rifles. On [[17 August]] [[1885]] a patrol 30 men strong was surprised by Atjehnese who had laid an ambush behind the tramway in the eastern sector of the line: six wounded, among them luitenant [[Luitenant de Jong (1885)|de Jong]]. According to the Governor's report of 6-18 August, [[Oleh-leh]], the Dutch harbour place, was repeatedly fired upon. On [[25 August]] [[1885]] the blockhouse near the [[Wachthuis aan de Lingkar|Kali Lengkar]] was fired upon: three wounded. In the night of [[12 September|12]]-[[13 September]] [[1885]] the tramway near Lampermej was dug out so that the rails hung in the air — discovered luckily in time, the enemy lying in ambush. In the night of [[20 September|20]]-[[21 September]] [[1885]] the tramway at Lampermej was again damaged and the telephone connection destroyed; on [[25 September]] [[1885]] the fortification of [[Lamdjame]] was fired upon with a loss of one man; and in the night of [[27 September|27]]-[[28 September]] [[1885]] the telephone connection between [[Lamrong]] and [[Lampeneroet]] was destroyed.

## October-December

On [[7 October]] [[1885]] the watch at the bridge by the [[Atjeh-passer]] at Oleh-leh was attacked in the evening: one man mortally wounded; on [[15 October]] [[1885]] the navy had to fire its guns to secure the roadstead of Oleh-leh; on [[24 October]] [[1885]] the enemy barred the tramway between Oleh-leh and Kota Radja with iron wire and beams; the watch at [[Nesooh]], close by Kota Radja, was attacked (one wounded); on [[27 October]] [[1885]] a tram between Oleh-leh and Kota Radja, close by the latter place, was fired upon: two wounded; on [[8 November]] [[1885]] an escort of seven men between [[Oleh Karang]] and [[Tjot Iri]] was attacked: one dead and one wounded; and Perelaer's notes close at [[17 November]] [[1885]]. After his speech, a Batavia telegram of [[16 December]] [[1885]] reported that engineers had repaired a damaged stretch of the tramway between [[Lamara]] and [[Lampeneroet]]: two dead and four wounded, among them the engineer lieutenant [[Krol van der Hoek]].

## The judgment

"En nu vraag ik: is dat niet eene treurige lijst?" Perelaer exclaimed — a state of things of which the reality came into irreconcilable collision with the assurances that had resounded in government circles; one from which, month by month, the stillness, the demoralisation of the garrison troops — constantly relieved and returning to Java with their tale — spread over the whole army; and one for which [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] could declare, in January [[1886]], that no army had ever been placed in such a position of unlimited duration, and that for any other army, too, such a position would be untenable in the long run.

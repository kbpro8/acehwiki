**Parliamentary Debate on the Aceh War** was the extended series of legislative discussions, committee meetings (*comité-generaal*), and budgetary reviews held in the Dutch States-General in The Hague during [[1872]] and [[1873]], which authorized diplomatic confrontation and military intervention against the [[Aceh Sultanate]].

## Presentation of the Ministerial Note

In winter of [[1872]], following the Anglo-Dutch Sumatra Treaty of [[1871]], Minister of Colonies [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]] submitted the [[Parliamentary Note on Relations with Aceh 1872-1873]] to the lower house of parliament. Lawmakers debated the legality of intervening in Aceh, evaluating decades of diplomatic correspondence dating back to the [[Treaty of London 1824]].

## Debates Over the First Expedition's Defeat

Following the withdrawal of the [[First Aceh Expedition]] in April [[1873]], parliamentary debates erupted with renewed intensity. In May [[1873]], the presentation of the [[Jonckbloet Note of 10 May 1873]] prompted lawmakers to criticize the [[Dutch East Indies Government]] for launching an overhasty campaign without a [[General Staff of the KNIL]] or adequate naval support. 

General [[G. G. Booms]] later drew extensively upon these parliamentary records in his [[1875]] treatise to prove that the executive government in [[Batavia]] was responsible for the strategic miscalculations of [[1873]].

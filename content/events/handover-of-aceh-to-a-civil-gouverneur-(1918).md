**The Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)** was the formal transfer of the *gewest* (province) of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] from military to civilian administration by [[H. N. A. Swart|Generaal Swart]] in [[1918]], marking the end of what Dames calls "de slepende ziekte: de [[Atjeh-oorlog|Atjeh-oorlog]]" — the lingering disease of the Aceh War — forty-five years after the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of [[1873]] and seven years after the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] had destroyed the last organised Acehnese armed resistance. The handover is the political closure of the Aceh War in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*.

## The precondition: the colonne Schmidt

"Daardoor kon Generaal Swart in [[1918]] eindelijk een rustig gebied overdragen aan een civiel Gouverneur." The *daardoor* — thereby — refers to the cumulative effect of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] ([[1910]]-[[1911]]), the killing of the last Tiriō leaders, and the steady pressure of the patrouilles. With the last organised armed resistance destroyed, the country was *rustig* — quiet — enough to be handed over to a civilian Gouverneur.

## The end of the slepende ziekte

"Er was een eind aan de slepende ziekte: de [[Atjeh-oorlog|Atjeh-oorlog]]." The phrase *slepende ziekte* — lingering disease — is the book's quiet judgement on the forty-five-year war. The [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] had begun with the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|Eerste Atjeh-expeditie]] of [[1873]]; it had been pronounced over several times before; it had been the subject of political controversy in the Netherlands; it had made the reputation of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. By [[1918]] it was, finally, over — not by victory in a single battle, but by the cumulative attrition of the colonnes and the patrouilles, and by the political closure represented by the surrender of *[[Panglima Polem|Panglima Prang Polim]]* to [[Hendrikus Colijn|kapitein Colijn]].

## Significance

The handover is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the political closure of the Aceh War. It is the moment at which the colonial army's forty-five-year war in Aceh was, formally, ended. The book's quiet point is that the handover was the consequence not of a single decisive battle but of the patient, methodical work of the colonnes — the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] above all — and of the political closure represented by the surrender of the Sultan's former army commander. The handover is therefore the book's argument for the *colonne* method: the slow, expensive, morally taxing method that actually finished the war, as against the *demarcatielijnen* and the *schiettent* method that, in the book's account, had failed.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **separation of civil and military authority in Atjeh** — the definitive split accomplished in September [[1918]], when [[Van Sluys|A. G. H. van Sluys]] took office on [[27 September]] as the first civilian governor — crowned the gradual process begun by Governor [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] in [[1911]] and redeemed, in author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s phrase, the premature civil experiment of [[1881]]–[[1884]]: "Thans in 1918 zou 'de kroon' beter op 't werk passen" ("now in 1918 'the crown' would better fit the work").

### The gradual preparation

The long road ran from the uniting of both powers in one hand (September [[1884]]) through the period of civil- and military governors — [[Demmeni|Demmeni]], [[General van Teijn|Van Teijn]], [[Pompe van Meerdervoort|Pompe van Meerdervoort]], [[General Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]], [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], [[J. C. van der Wyck|Van der Wyck]], [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] and Swart. In connection with the ever more favourably changing political state, General Swart had worked since [[1911]] at a gradual separation of the civil and military authority: wherever possible, the functions of department- and sub-department-chiefs were entrusted more and more to civilian administrative officials; several bivouacs and posts were abolished and garrisons withdrawn in the years 1908–1918, and in [[1915]] the civil and military governor could already make the proposal of returning the 3rd field-battalion to [[Java]] (for want of lodgings there, only two companies could leave).

### The definitive split of 1918

"In September 1918 is dan eindelijk de definitieve scheiding tusschen burgerlijk en militair gezag tot stand gekomen" — "In September 1918 the definitive separation between civil and military authority was finally accomplished." "Toen eerst was het psychologisch moment, dat men reeds in het begin van de tachtig jaren meende te hebben bereikt, aangebroken" — only then had the psychological moment arrived, which one had thought to have reached already in the beginning of the eighties (see [[Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh (6 April 1881)|Introduction of civil government in Atjeh (1881)]]). Now, in 1918, the crown would better fit the work; now the walls of the building would prove more firmly joined than in [[1882]]. The first civilian governor, the heer A. G. H. van Sluys, took office on [[27 September]] [[1918]] and — remaining in charge as the book appeared — declared in his accession speech that what the Atjehnese population needed first of all was rest, and that the military "must always remain means, never become goal" (see [[Van Sluys|A. G. H. van Sluys]]).

### Reception

It had repeatedly been thought that the separation must lead to clashes between the civil and military authorities, since it brought with it a ceding of powers by the one and an accepting of a greater responsibility by the other. Van Sluys, questioned at the end of [[1921]] by the *Bataviaasch Nieuwsblad* on the supposed "animosity", answered: "Dat is allemaal onzin!" — it was only human and intelligible that here and there a difference of opinion arose upon the redistribution of competences, but it had never come to expression; and he enumerated examples proving that cooperation between the civil and military authorities in Atjeh indeed existed (see [[Van Sluys|A. G. H. van Sluys]]).

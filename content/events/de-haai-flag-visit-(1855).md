**The De Haai flag visit** of [[1855]] — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the first of a series of Dutch naval *vlagvertoon* (flag-showing) visits to [[Acheh|Atjèh]] — was undertaken by the Dutch war-brig *De Haai*, whose commander was "door den Soeltan zeer vijandig ontvangen" — "received very hostilely by the Sultan." The visit marked the Dutch government's turn, after a series of plundering incidents, from passive acquiescence to a cautious assertion of naval presence in Atjèhnese waters.

## The visit

Kreemer situates the *De Haai* visit in the Dutch response to the recurrence of Atjèhnese plundering — [[1831]] at [[Kuala Bathee|Koeala Batèë]], [[1836]] the [[Schooner Dolphijn incident (1836)|schooner Dolphijn]], [[1844]] two British vessels at [[Kuala Bathee|Koeala Batèë]] and [[Kuala Meureudu|Koeala Meureudoe]], [[1851]] a [[Naples|Napolitaansch]] vessel, [[1852]] an [[England|Engelsche]] schooner. The Dutch government, having in [[1852]] admitted "dat zij dergelijke daden der Atjèhers niet beletten kon" — "that she could not prevent such deeds of the Atjèhnese" — decided to send a warship to Atjèh from time to time for flag-showing, "zonder briefwisseling met den Soeltan te openen, zoolang niet van de zijde van dien vorst naar vriendschappelijke betrekkingen gestreefd werd" — "without opening correspondence with the Sultan, as long as no effort towards friendly relations was made from the side of that prince." "De eerste tocht had plaats in [[1855]] met de oorlogsbrik De Haai, waarbij de commandant door den Soeltan zeer vijandig werd ontvangen" — "The first voyage took place in 1855 with the war-brig De Haai, in which the commander was received very hostilely by the Sultan."

## The 1856 follow-up

The hostile reception of *De Haai* prompted a more carefully prepared second visit in [[1856]]. "In het volgend jaar zond de Indische Regeering een grooter schip en ter voorkoming van gelijke bejegening als in het vorige jaar, kreeg dat schip wèl een brief mede van den gouverneur van [[Sumatra's West Coast|Soematra's Westkust]] [[Generaal van Swieten|Van Swieten]]. De ontvangst was nu gepast. De Soeltan nam den schijn aan, alsof hij toenadering wenschte, echter schreef hij tegelijk aan den Engelschen gouverneur van [[Singapore|Singapore]] in een jegens ons vijandigen geest!" — "In the following year the Indian Government sent a larger ship, and to prevent the same treatment as in the previous year, that ship was given a letter from the governor of Sumatra's West Coast Van Swieten. The reception was now proper. The Sultan took on the appearance of wishing to draw near; however, he wrote at the same time to the English governor of Singapore in a spirit hostile towards us!"

## Significance

The *De Haai* visit of [[1855]] is, in Kreemer's narrative, the first Dutch assertion of naval presence at Atjèh since the 1824 treaty — a cautious departure from the pure acquiescence of the [[1831]]–[[1852]] period. The hostile reception, and the Sultan's double-dealing in [[1856]] (friendly to the Dutch face, hostile to the British in writing), confirmed that no progress could be made by naval visits alone, and led directly to the [[Treaty of 1857 with the Sultan of Atjèh|treaty-negotiation of 1857]].

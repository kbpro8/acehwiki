**The Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)** — the celebrated *patrouille* of [[Kapitein der Infanterie H. F. T. van Blokland|Kapitein Van Blokland]] from the post of [[Ana' Galong]] along the kampong edges of [[Ana' Batée]], [[Klieng]], and the rice-fields toward [[Sibreh]] — was one of the hardest-fought small actions of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the opening weeks of [[1896]]. Launched as a routine reconnaissance in force by the garrison of the most exposed post on the western [[buitenlinie]], the patrol ran into a superior Achehnese force of approximately 250 men under [[T. Mad Amin]], [[Habib Lhong]], and [[Panglima Hassan]] and was forced into a five-hour firefight from which it was only extricated by the timely arrival of a relief detachment under [[Kapitein van den generalen staf Kronouer|Kapitein Kronouer]] — itself composed of forty bayonets from [[Lamsoet]] and [[Lambarih]] under [[Luitenant K. H. Westendorp|Lt. Westendorp]] and [[Luitenant F. Ketjen|Lt. Ketjen]]. Kruisheer singles out Van Blokland's personal valour, recording that "het gelukte hem persoonlijk met de sabel twee tegenstanders neer te slaan, of which een nagenoeg in tweeën gedeeld werd" (it was granted him personally, with the sabre, to strike down two opponents, one of whom was cleft nearly in twain).

## Background

By [[March]] [[1896]] the post of [[Ana' Galong]] — site of the [[Conquest of Ana' Galong (30 November 1893)|conquest]] of [[November]] [[1893]] — had become the most exposed Dutch position on the western *buitenlinie*. The [[Luitenant-Kolonel E. W. Bisschoff van Heemkerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], liniecommandant of the concentrated line in early [[1896]], had ordered the post commanders at Ana' Galong to patrol at least once during their detachment period, partly to gather intelligence and partly to discourage the concentration of hostile bands in the immediately surrounding kampongs. Kapitein Van Blokland, post commander, drew the patrol duty for his rotation. His force comprised [[1e Luit. der Infanterie C. T. J. Stakman|1e Luit. Stakman]] (commanding the right section), [[Adjudant Ond. Off. Krüger|adj. ond. off. Krüger]] (No. 14477, commanding the left section), [[Off. van Gesondheid 2e klasse C. van der Meer|off. van gesondh. 2e kl. C. van der Meer]] (medical officer), one hoornblazer (bugler), 72 bayonets — i.e., infantry, two *minderen* of the medical service, and nine forced labourers with three stretchers.

The political background was ominous. Although [[Teuku Umar]] would not formally [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defect]] until late March, [[Controleur 2e kl. bij het binnenlandsch bestuur Gisolf|Controleur Gisolf]] had already warned the [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Governor]] of Umar's "more than suspicious attitude." The enemy forces in the western mukims were larger, better armed, and more aggressive than they had been for months. A patrol moving out from Ana' Galong into the kampong belt of [[Ana' Batée]] and [[Klieng]] would be moving into the heart of the most hostile country on the island.

## The Patrol and the Five-Hour Fight

The patrol marched out of [[Ana' Galong]] along the kampong edges of [[Ana' Batée]] and [[Klieng]] and across the *sawah* toward [[Sibreh]]. It was almost immediately engaged. A force estimated at 250 men — three times the strength of the patrol — under the joint command of [[T. Mad Amin]], [[Habib Lhong]], and [[Panglima Hassan]] opened fire from covered positions and attempted to surround the small Dutch column. Van Blokland deployed his two sections and fought a delaying action for five hours, the *sawah* dykes and the kampong edges providing such cover as the broken ground afforded.

In the course of the fight Van Blokland — who, as post commander, was expected to direct the engagement from a position of relative safety — repeatedly closed with the enemy at sabre's length. The most celebrated incident, preserved verbatim in Kruisheer, records that "het gelukte hem persoonlijk met de sabel twee tegenstanders neer te slaan, of which een nagenoeg in tweeën gedeeld werd" — Van Blokland personally cut down two opponents with his sabre, one of them nearly cleft in two. Casualties mounted. [[1e Luit. der Infanterie C. T. J. Stakman|Lt. Stakman]] was shot through the right arm. [[Adjudant Ond. Off. Krüger|Adjudant-Onderofficier Krüger]] was wounded; he had his wounds bandaged and returned to the fight. [[Off. van Gesondheid 2e klasse C. van der Meer|Dr. van der Meer]] received a grazing shot to the head but continued to attend the wounded under fire, including the severely wounded fusilier Monden. The patrol was running low on ammunition and beginning to be pinned against the *sawah* when relief arrived.

## The Relief

[[Luitenant-Kolonel E. W. Bisschoff van Heemkerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], the liniecommandant, arrived at [[Ana' Galong]] at approximately 10:30 a.m. on [[7 March]] [[1896]] to take personal charge. With him was [[Kapitein van den generalen staf Kronouer|Kapitein Kronouer]] of the General Staff. Recognising the peril of the patrol, Bisschoff dispatched Kronouer at the head of a relief detachment of forty bayonets drawn from the garrisons of [[Lamsoet]] and [[Lambarih]] under the command of [[Luitenant K. H. Westendorp|Lt. Westendorp]] and [[Luitenant F. Ketjen|Lt. Ketjen]]. The relief column marched out through the *sawah*. [[Luitenant K. H. Westendorp|Lt. Westendorp]] was severely wounded in the *sawah* about 150 m in; he was carried back by the European fusilier Elshout (No. 39756), an act of personal courage that Kruisheer records.

Even as the relief was being organised, [[1e Luit. der Infanterie J. van Hasselt|1e Luit. J. van Hasselt]], commanding the remainder of the Ana' Galong garrison, marched out at approximately 8 a.m. with such men as he could spare to reinforce Van Blokland in person. Van Hasselt was hit in the left arm and returned to the *benteng* to be bandaged, whereupon [[Adj. Ond. Off. dd. Off. der Artillerie Diehl|adj. ond. off. Diehl]] (No. 32486) talso over command of the post. The combined effect of the reinforcements — Van Hasselt's sortie from the garrison and Kronouer's relief detachment — was sufficient to enable Van Blokland to break contact and withdraw the patrol to [[Ana' Galong]].

## Aftermath

The patrol's casualties — [[1e Luit. der Infanterie C. T. J. Stakman|Lt. Stakman]] shot through the right arm, [[Adjudant Ond. Off. Krüger|Krüger]] wounded, [[Off. van Gesondheid 2e klasse C. van der Meer|Dr. van der Meer]] with a head wound, and fusilier Monden severely wounded — were testimony to the intensity of the five-hour fight. The enemy casualties were unknown but certainly heavier, given Van Blokland's three confirmed sabre-kills and the sustained fire of the patrol's Beaumont rifles. The engagement demonstrated three things: the strength and aggressiveness of the hostile bands in the western mukims; the competence of the Ana' Galong garrison under pressure; and the growing inadequacy of the *buitenlinie* as a strategic instrument. Less than three weeks later, [[Teuku Umar]] would [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defect]], the [[Siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe (28–29 March 1896)|siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe]] would begin, and the entire western *buitenlinie* — including Ana' Galong — would be [[Evacuation of Anak-Galoeng, Lam-Barih, Lam-Soet, Senelop (17 April 1896)|evacuated]].

## Significance

The patrol of Captain Van Blokland on [[7 March]] [[1896]] was the last major Dutch action of the pre-defection phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the western theatre. It is chiefly remembered for Van Blokland's celebrated sabre-fight and for the selfless conduct of [[Luitenant K. H. Westendorp|Lt. Westendorp]]'s relief detachment. Kruisheer uses the engagement as a portrait of the everyday heroism of the *buitenlinie* garrisons in the months before the storm broke — and as a quiet indictment of the strategic complacency of [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]]'s administration, which persisted in believing [[Teuku Umar]] loyal even as the hostile bands in the western mukims grew to three times the strength of an ordinary patrol.

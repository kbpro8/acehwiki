**Conquest of Perak (1579)** was the military seizure of the Malay sultanate of [[Perak]] by the forces of the [[Aceh]] Sultanate in [[1579]]. The operation marked the first major expansion of Acehnese power onto the Malay Peninsula during the [[16th century]].

## Background

Aceh’s strategic interest in [[Perak]] stemmed from the latter’s valuable tin mines and its position along the maritime trade routes linking the Indian Ocean to the South China Sea. The Acehnese campaign of [[1579]] was part of a systematic effort to dominate regional commerce and to propagate Islam.

## Execution and Aftermath

Contemporary Malay chronicles describe the swift capture of Perak’s capital and the installation of an Acehnese governor. The conquest was later reinforced during the reign of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]] in the early [[17th century]], confirming Aceh’s lasting hegemony over the region.

## Significance

The [[Conquest of Perak (1579)]] set a precedent for Acehnese intervention in the Malay Peninsula and facilitated the diffusion of Arabic‑derived religious terminology into the local Malay dialect, as documented in the *Maleise tekst van de Burda*.

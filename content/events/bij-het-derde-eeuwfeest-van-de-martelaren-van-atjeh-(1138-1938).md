**Bij het derde eeuwfeest van de martelaren van Atjeh (1138–1938)** — *"At the third centenary of the martyrs of Atjeh (1138–1938)"* — is an article by **[[Luc. van der Linden|Luc. van der Linden, O. Carm.]]**, published in [[1939]] in *Het Missiewerk* (The Mission Work), No. 4, p. 186 *e.v.*, commemorating the third centenary — the *derde eeuwfeest* — of the **martelaren van Atjeh** — the martyrs of Atjeh — dated in the article's title to the period [[1138]]–[[1938]]. The article is recorded twice in [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]]'s *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939]]* (The Hague, [[1940]]): as No. 581 in the Sumatra chapter and again as No. 1059 in the Sumatra Addenda.

## Subject

The article's subject, as recorded by Van Meurs, is the *derde eeuwfeest* — the third centenary — of the **martelaren van Atjeh**, the martyrs of Atjeh. The title's parenthetical date **1138–1938** is the key to the chronology: if [[1938]] is the year of the commemoration, then the *derde eeuwfeest* (third centenary) counted back from [[1938]] gives a base date of [[1638]] for the first centenary — that is, the martyrdom itself is placed in [[1538]]. The "1138" in the title is, on its face, inconsistent with a third-centenary commemoration in [[1938]]: a centenary counted from [[1138]] would, in [[1938]], be the eighth centenary, not the third. The discrepancy is most plausibly an OCR or transcription error in Van Meurs's survey — the most likely intended date of the martyrdom is [[1538]], with [[1938]] as the fourth centenary, or [[1638]] with [[1938]] as the third centenary. The question is one that the article itself, in *Het Missiewerk*, would resolve; the present entry records the discrepancy as Van Meurs gives it.

The **martelaren van Atjeh** — martyrs of Atjeh — are, in the context of a Catholic missionary journal (*Het Missiewerk*), the Catholic missionaries or converts who suffered martyrdom in Atjeh in the period of early Christian missionary contact with the sultanate. The 16th and 17th centuries — the period of the [[C. Wessels|Wessels]] article *[[Uit de missiegebieden van Sumatra: Atjeh in de 16e en 17e eeuw (Wessels, 1939)|Uit de missiegebieden van Sumatra: Atjeh in de 16e en 17e eeuw]]* (No. 652, same year and same survey) — were the period of the principal early Catholic missionary efforts in Atjeh, and the martyrs commemorated are most plausibly to be identified with the missionaries of that period. The Jesuit [[C. Wessels|Wessels]]'s article, in the same year, gives the broader mission-field context from which the martyrdoms are drawn.

## Author and venue

[[Luc. van der Linden|Luc. van der Linden]] — the *O. Carm.* (Ordo Carmelitarum, Order of Carmelites) suffix in the Addenda entry (No. 1059) indicates that the author was a Carmelite father — published the article in *Het Missiewerk* (The Mission Work), No. 4, p. 186 *e.v.* *Het Missiewerk* was the principal Dutch Catholic missionary journal, and the choice of venue signals the article's intended audience: Dutch Catholic readers with an interest in the mission fields of the Netherlands Indies. The Carmelite identification of the author, and the venue, place the article within the tradition of Dutch Catholic missionary historiography on Atjeh — a tradition distinct from, but parallel to, the Protestant and the secular-colonial scholarly traditions.

## Place in the 1939 Literatuur-Overzicht

The article is recorded twice in Van Meurs's survey: as No. 581 in the Sumatra chapter (Hoofdstuk III, Nos. 548–658) and again as No. 1059 in the Sumatra Addenda (Nos. 1056–1062). The double recording — once in the main Sumatra chapter and once in the Addenda — reflects the bibliographic practice of including, in the Addenda, items that had appeared in the preceding year but had not been recorded in the previous survey. The entry in the Addenda (No. 1059) gives the author's name in fuller form — *LINDEN O. CARM., LUC. VAN DER* — and confirms the Carmelite affiliation of the author. The two entries together are the source for the third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs in the corpus.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the article is significant as the [[1939]] record of the third centenary of the Atjeh martyrs — a commemoration of the Catholic missionary martyrs of Atjeh, observed in [[1938]] and recorded in a Dutch Catholic missionary journal. The article furnishes an independent source for the Catholic missionary martyrology of Atjeh, and it complements the [[C. Wessels|Wessels]] article *[[Uit de missiegebieden van Sumatra: Atjeh in de 16e en 17e eeuw (Wessels, 1939)|Uit de missiegebieden van Sumatra: Atjeh in de 16e en 17e eeuw]]* (No. 652, same year and same survey), which gives the broader mission-field context of 16th- and 17th-century Atjeh. The two articles together — the Wessels historical survey and the Linden martyrological commemoration — mark [[1939]] as a year in which the Catholic missionary history of Atjeh was, in several registers, being memorialised in Dutch colonial scholarship. The discrepancy in the title's date ([[1138]]–[[1938]] vs the expected third-centenary chronology) is a question that the article itself would resolve.

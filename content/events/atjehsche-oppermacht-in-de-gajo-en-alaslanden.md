**The Atjehsche oppermacht in de Gajo- en Alaslanden** — the Atjehnese supremacy in the [[Gajoland|Gajo]] and [[Alasland|Alas]] highlands — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the political arrangement by which the [[Acheh|Atjehsche rijk]] extended its sovereignty from the [[Great Acheh|north coast]] across the central highlands of [[Sumatra]], establishing a resident Atjehnese presence in the [[Gajoland|Gajo]] and [[Alasland|Alas]] countries and transforming the highland chiefdoms into tributaries of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]]. The establishment of this supremacy increased — the Bureau records — the "onmiddellijke aanrakingen" (immediate contacts) between the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] and the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] highlands, and produced the institutional imprint that the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]] would leave on the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] *radja berempat*.

## Background: Atjeh and the highland interior

The [[Gajoland|Gajo]] and [[Alasland|Alas]] highlands lie between the [[Great Acheh|north-coast Atjehnese]] heartland and the [[Batak-Karo|Karo hoogvlakte]]. Before the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] intervention they had been inhabited by the [[Gajo|Gayo]] and [[Alas|Alas]] peoples, organised under their own *stamhoofden* (tribal heads). The Atjehnese supremacy that the Bureau records as established "in the Gajo- and Alaslanden" thus represents the extension of Atjehnese sovereignty from the [[Great Acheh|north coast]] across the central highlands — a projection of [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] power that brought the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] into immediate contact with the [[Batak-Karo|Karo-Batak]] lands on the eastern side of the highlands.

## The establishment of the oppermacht

The Bureau records the establishment of the oppermacht in a single, dense sentence, noting that the Karo chiefs' maintenance of good relations with the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultans was especially necessary "nadat de onmiddellijke aanrakingen tusschen beide landstreken door de vestiging der Atjehsche oppermacht in de Gajo- en Alaslanden waren toegenomen" — "after the immediate contacts between the two lands [Karo and Atjeh] had increased through the establishment of the Atjehnese supremacy in the Gajo- and Alaslanden." The "vestiging" — "establishment" — of the oppermacht is thus presented as a discrete political act: the imposition of a resident Atjehnese power on the highlands that increased the volume of contact between the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] and the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] lands.

The Bureau does not date the establishment of the oppermacht to a single year, but its placement in the narrative — between the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|early-sixteenth-century conquest of Gasip]] and the [[sultan-iskander-moeda's-oostkust-veldtocht|early-seventeenth-century campaign of Iskander Moeda]] — fixes it within the long century of Atjehnese East Coast dominance.

## The institutional imprint: the radja berempat

The most consequential institutional imprint of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] oppermacht in the Gajo- and Alaslanden was the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]] — the "four-princes" system by which the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate organised the highland chiefdoms into groups of four, each group headed by a *radja berempat* who served as the principal intermediary with the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]]. The Bureau records that "de Gajo'sche stamhoofden" had been the first to apply this measure, and that the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] chiefs — "al dan niet daartoe genoopt door Atjeh" — "whether or not compelled to it by Atjeh" — had subsequently applied the same measure, appointing four of their *sibajaks* (the Karo *oeroeng*-chiefs) as a *radja berempat* charged with the maintenance of good relations with the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultans. The Atjehnese supremacy in the highlands thus produced, by direct imitation or by Atjehnese pressure, a parallel institution in the [[Batak-Karo|Karo]] lands.

## Consequences for the Karo-Batak lands

The Atjehnese oppermacht in the highlands had two long-term consequences for the [[Batak-Karo|Karo-Batak]] lands. First, it produced the *radja berempat* institution — the four-princes arrangement that would, after the Dutch annexation of the [[Batak-Karo|Karo hoogvlakte]] in [[1904]]–[[1906]], be revived and amplified by the colonial state in the form of the five-*sibajak* *landschapshoofden* of the Karo *landschappen*. Second, it left a memory of Atjehnese power in the institutions of the inland Bataks: the Bureau records that "sommige bestuursinstellingen volgens het Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem (dat ook op de Karo-hoogvlakte werd ingevoerd door Atjeh) worden nog aan dien invloed toegeschreven" — "some administrative institutions according to the Atjehsche four-princes system (which was also introduced on the Karo hoogvlakte by Atjeh) are still attributed to that influence."

## Significance

The Atjehsche oppermacht in de Gajo- en Alaslanden is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the institutional hinge between the [[Great Acheh|north-coast Atjehnese]] sultanate and the [[Batak-Karo|Karo-Batak]] lands on the eastern side of the highlands. It transformed the highlands from an Atjehnese frontier into an Atjehnese province, and it established the institutional pattern — the *radja berempat* — that would later be revived, in altered form, by the Dutch colonial state. The oppermacht is thus the highland counterpart of the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Atjehsche overlordship of the East Coast]]: where the latter extended Atjehnese power along the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malakka]], the former extended it across the central Sumatran highlands.

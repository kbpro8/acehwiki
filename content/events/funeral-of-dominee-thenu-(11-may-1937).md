**The Funeral of Dominee Thenu (11 May 1937)** was the *indrukwekkende, grootse begrafenis* of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Royaal Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger's]] *Soldaten-Dominee* [[Dominee Thenu]] at [[Koeta-Radja]] on [[11 May]] [[1937]], in which four retired [[Militaire Willemsorde|M.W.O.]]-ridders — [[Salawono]] (3e kl.), [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]] and [[Pattipuluhu]] — acted as *slippendragers* (pall-bearers), a Marechaussee brigade served as *vuurpeleton* (firing party), and the dominee was buried at [[Peutjoet]] "temidden van zovelen, die hij voorbereid had op die laatste gang daarheen." A *standbeeld* (statue) was later erected before his *kerkje* in [[Koeta-Radja]]. The funeral is the closing set-piece of the chapter "Dominee Thenu" in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* and one of the book's most resonant images of the moral economy of the colonial army.

## The slippendragers

"Als slippendragers traden vier oude, gepensioneerde Marechaussees op, alle vier ridders M.W.O.: [[Salawono]] (3e kl.), [[Pattiasina]], [[Kazen]] en [[Pattipuluhu]]." The four retired M.W.O.-ridders living at [[Koeta-Radja]] were chosen to carry the dominee's bier. The choice is the book's most explicit statement of the moral economy of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]]: the men who had been decorated for courage under fire laid their decorations, in effect, at the feet of the man who had blessed them. [[Salawono]]'s place as the first-named — and the only one whose *klas* is specified — marks him as the most senior of the four.

## The vuurpeleton and the burial

"Vuurpeleton was een brigade Marechaussee." A Marechaussee brigade served as the firing party — the military honour due to the dominee who had served the corps for forty years. He was buried at [[Peutjoet]], the military cemetery of [[Koeta-Radja]], "temidden van zovelen, die hij voorbereid had op die laatste gang daarheen" — among the many whom he had prepared for that last journey there.

## The gravestone and the standbeeld

His gravestone carries the verse from Job 4:3 — "Zie, gij hebt velen onderwezen, En gij hebt slappe handen gesterkt" ("Behold, you have instructed many, and you have strengthened feeble hands"). The verse, drawn from the speech of Eliphaz the Temanite to Job, is the book's quiet scriptural closure of the dominee's life: Thenu had instructed many, and had strengthened feeble hands.

"Vóór zijn kerkje in [[Koeta-Radja]] richtte Atjeh voor hem een standbeeld op" — before his little church in Koeta-Radja, Aceh erected for him a statue. The *standbeeld* is the book's marker that Thenu's gezag had passed from the colonial army into the country itself: Aceh, not the [[K.N.I.L.]], erected the statue.

## Significance

The funeral is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the moment at which the decorations of the old [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] generation were laid at the feet of the man who had blessed them. The four M.W.O.-ridders as *slippendragers* are the book's most resonant image: the men of courage carrying the man of faith. The *standbeeld* before his *kerkje* is the book's quiet acknowledgement that Thenu's gezag had passed into the country itself — that Aceh, which had fought the colonial army for forty years, had come to honour the dominee who had served that army. The funeral is therefore the moral closure of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] in the book's economy: not the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]], but the burial of the man who had served both sides.

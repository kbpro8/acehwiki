**Mediation between Aceh and Johor (1641)** refers to the VOC-brokered settlement of the conflict between the [[Sultanate of Aceh]] and the [[Sultanate of Johor]], concluded in early [[1641]] under the initiative of [[Commissaris Schouten]], governor of [[Malacca]]. The mediation ended years of hostilities, secured a formal peace proclaimed in the Acehnese capital, and resulted in Aceh's withdrawal from the mainland Malay Peninsula except for its suzerainty over [[Perak]].

## Context

By [[1640]], open warfare between [[Aceh]] and [[Johor]] had disrupted the [[Strait of Malacca]] and threatened the VOC's newly captured possession of [[Malacca]] (taken from the Portuguese in January [[1641]]). [[Anthonio van Diemen]], the Governor-General, saw strategic value in pacifying the region to protect Dutch commerce.

## The mediation

In early [[1641]], [[Commissaris Schouten]] sent envoys to [[Aceh]] and brought Acehnese envoys back to Johor. The VOC sought to frame itself as a neutral intermediary, although its leverage rested on naval power and the diplomatic weight of [[Malacca]].

The peace was formally proclaimed in the Acehnese court, conditioned on each ruler retaining his own kingdom, the cessation of hostile acts, and the permanent exclusion of Johor envoys from Aceh. The Acehnese Queen framed the agreement as an ''ampon'' (pardon) for Johor's offenses, a formulation that the Dutch envoy warned might offend Johor even as it preserved the peace.

## Aftermath and consequences

The settlement succeeded in ending open hostilities, but it rested on an uneasy foundation. Johor likely resented the Acehnese terms, and Aceh's withdrawal from the Malay Peninsula left [[Perak]] as a lingering point of tension.

The mediation established a precedent for VOC intervention in intra-Malay disputes. By leveraging its naval presence and the newly captured [[Malacca]], the Company positioned itself as a regional arbiter, creating obligations of gratitude and alliance that it would later exploit for commercial advantage.

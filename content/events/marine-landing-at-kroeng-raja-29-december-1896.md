**The marine landing at Kroeng Raja of 29 December 1896** was a Dutch combined army-navy operation mounted against the hostile bands of [[Teukoe Oemar|Teuku Umar]] at [[Kroeng Raja|Kroeng-Raja]] in the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI-Moekim federation]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] on the night of [[28 December|28]]–[[29 December]] [[1896]]. The operation — the closing military action of the [[1896]] campaign — was the immediate prelude to the [[1897|1 January 1897]] column under [[G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Kol. Soeters]] that opened the [[1897]] campaign against Umar.

## The operations (28–29 December 1896)

The operation was mounted on the night of [[28 December|28]]–[[29 December]] [[1896]]. The *Tweede Deel* of *[[ATJEH '96 (book by A. Kruisheer)|ATJEH '96]]* records that *een derde colonne werd aangeduid om, des nachts, ter hoogte van Kroeng-Raja te landen, en eene zoodanige positie in te nemen, dat een ontsnappen naar het zuiden, in de richting Lam-Toba, uitgesloten was* — *a third column was designated to land, by night, at Kroeng-Raja, and to take up a position such that an escape to the south, in the direction of Lam-Toba, was excluded*. The column was composed of the 4th company of the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12th Battalion]] under [[Weustmann|Kapitein Weustmann]], reinforced by a detachment of marines under [[P. Groen|Luitenant der Mariniers P. Groen]] — strength 1 officer and 52 men, 1 medical officer, and 5 stretcher-bearers. The company embarked on the evening of [[28 December]] at five o'clock and landed at [[Kroeng Raja|Kroeng-Raja]] at six o'clock the following morning, taking up the position that had been assigned to it.

The Dutch encountered enemy positions at [[Kroeng Raja|Kroeng-Raja]]; the enemy was engaged and dispersed. The marine landing was supported by the guns of the Dutch navy, and the operation was the principal combined army-navy action of the closing weeks of [[1896]]. The Dutch losses were slight; the enemy's losses were not recorded.

## The prelude to the 1897 campaign

The marine landing at Kroeng Raja was the immediate prelude to the [[1897|1 January 1897]] column under [[G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Kol. G. F. Soeters]] that opened the [[1897]] campaign. Kruisheer records that *Den 1en Januari vertrok deze, onder aanvoering van den Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters, en ter sterkte van het korps-marechaussee, 6 compagnieën infanterie, I sectie mortieren van 7 c.M., genietroepen* — *on 1 January [1897] this [column] departed, under the command of Lieutenant-Colonel G. F. Soeters, and at the strength of the marechaussée corps, 6 companies of infantry, 1 section of 7 cm mortars, engineers*. The Soeters column was the first major Dutch operation of the new year and the direct continuation of the operations of December [[1896]].

## Significance

The marine landing at Kroeng Raja of [[29 December]] [[1896]] was the closing military action of the [[1896]] campaign and the immediate prelude to the [[1897|1 January 1897]] column under [[G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Kol. Soeters]] that opened the [[1897]] campaign. The operation demonstrated the Dutch capacity for combined army-navy operations and the Dutch intention to maintain pressure on [[Teukoe Oemar|Teuku Umar]] in the new year. The operation also illustrated the continued use of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] in the Atjeh campaigns — a tradition that extended back to the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition of 1873]].

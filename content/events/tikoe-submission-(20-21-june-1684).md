**The Tikoe submission of 20–21 June 1684** was the formal act by which the chiefs of [[Tikoe|Tikoe]], having been militarily defeated in the [[Tikoe uprising (1683)|1683 rising]], acknowledged the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]]'s conquest of their land, accepted the commandeur at [[Padang]] as their [[panglima-radja|panglima]] (military governor), and granted the Compagnie its commercial monopoly. Recorded in Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* as the closing instrument of the [[Painansch Contract]] system and the formal end of Atjeh's last lever of influence on the [[Westkust van Sumatra|West Coast of Sumatra]].

## The text of the submission

Stapel quotes the key passages of the submission directly. The chiefs acknowledged "that their land had been conquered by the Compagnie with arms; that they 'on their request were taken as allies for pardon'" (*dat hun land door de Compagnie met de wapenen was overwonnen; dat zij 'op haer bede om pardon als bontgenoten wierden aengenomen*). They promised "to acknowledge and obey the commandeur at Padang as their panglima" (*belovende de commandeur op Padang voor haer panglima te erkennen en gehoorsamen*). They granted "the monopoly to the Compagnie" (*Zij verleenden het monopolie aan de Compagnie*). And they declared "that their regents were to be appointed by the commandeur, 'by force of the panglimaschap'" (*dat hun regenten door den commandeur moesten worden aangesteld, 'kraghte van het panglimaschap'*).

The phrase *kraghte van het panglimaschap* — "by force of the panglima-office" — is the critical constitutional claim. The Compagnie is not merely exercising a commercial monopoly; it is asserting the right to appoint the regents of Tikoe, in the name of the panglima-office that the commandeur of Padang now holds. This is sovereign authority, exercised in the form of an Acehnese-Malay political institution.

## The parallel Priaman treaty

The Tikoe submission was paired with a parallel Priaman renewal. "At the same time the treaty with those of Priaman was renewed, in view of the English, who intended to settle in the Priamansch" (*Tegelijkertijd werd het verdrag met die van Priaman hernieuwd, zulks met het oog op de Engelschen, die van plan waren, zich in het Priamansche neer te zetten*). The Compagnie now resolved to build a fort at [[Priaman]], "and by the contract the inhabitants promised to deliver the materials necessary thereto" (*en bij het contract beloofden de ingezetenen, de materialen, daartoe noodig, te leveren*).

The Priaman fort was the physical counterpart to the Tikoe submission. Together they close the Westkust against both the Atjehsch-gezinde party and the new English threat.

## Significance

The Tikoe submission closes the cycle of Dutch-Acehnese conflict on the Westkust that had opened with the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|sultane]]'s accession in [[1641]]. After June [[1684]] the Atjehsch-gezinde partij ceases to be a factor in Westkust politics, and Atjeh's authority contracts to its own territory. The Compagnie's sovereignty on the Westkust is now asserted in two complementary forms: through the [[panglima-radja|panglimaschap]] at Tikoe, which assimilates the Dutch commandeur to an Acehnese-Malay office; and through the Priaman fort, which projects Dutch military power directly.

The June [[1684]] settlement is also the bookend to the [[17 April 1684]] treaty between [[Sultan Hadji of Bantam|sultan Hadji of Bantam]] and the Compagnie, by which the Compagnie obtained the pepper monopoly of Bantam and the English were forced to leave. The English relocation to [[Benkoelen]], [[Batang Kapas]] and [[Silebar|Sillabar]] in [[1685]] — "although these were considered to stand under the Dutch Compagnie" (*hoewel die werden beschouwd als onder de Nederlandsche Compagnie te staan*) — opened the long English presence on the Westkust that would last until [[1824]]. But it opened that presence on territory the Compagnie had just formally secured against Atjeh.

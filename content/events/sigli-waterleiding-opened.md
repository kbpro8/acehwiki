**Sigli waterleiding opened** (December 1932) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. In December 1932 a waterleiding (waterworks) was opened at Sigli, fed by a wadoek (natural reservoir) with capacity of more than 245,000 m³. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to [[December]] [[1932]].

## Account

In [[December]] [[1932]] a waterleiding (waterworks) was opened at [[Sigli]], fed by a [[Wadoek|wadoek]] (natural reservoir) with capacity of more than 245,000 m³. Only drawback: water does not always meet the (very high) standards of the [[Dienst der Volksgezondheid (D.V.G.)|D.V.G.]] ([[Dienst der Volksgezondheid]]). Replaced the previous 'lijdenshistorie' (suffering history) where artesian well water was brackish and insufficient, and drinking water had to be brought by [[Atjeh-tram]] at 0.10 per petroleum can (18 L), strictly rationed.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

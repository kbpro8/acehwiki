**The Crisis at Kota Radja (May–June 1874)** was the most perilous moment of the Dutch occupation of [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] between the [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)|departure of generaal Jan van Swieten]] on [[26 April]] [[1874]] and the first monsoon reinforcements of mid-June, when the garrison of [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] — emaciated by cholera, malaria and the [[Bandjir flood of Kota Radja (December 1874)|bandjir]]'s precursor rains — could field only a handful of men per company and was being closed upon nightly by the Atjehneezen. Borel's verdict is uncompromising: "500 kloeke Atjehneezen zouden voldoende geweest zijn" to have taken the Kraton.

## The state of the bezettingskorps

[[Generaal Pel|Kolonel Pel]] inherited a garrison that was, in Borel's phrase, *allerdonkerendst*. Companies that should have fielded 125 bayonets could now muster fewer than thirty; only the gravest cases could be taken into the ambulances, the less grave being left at their posts because no beds existed. Repeated attempts to evacuate the sick to Padang or Java were thwarted by the heavy surf of the [[Kroeng Raba-baai|baai]] and the [[Atjeh-rivier]]'s treacherous bar. Mortality was *buitengewoon*; among the *dwangarbeiders* (convict labourers) it was *rampzalig*. Pel himself, Borel records, slept every night in full uniform, ready to mount the parapet at the first alarm.

## The enemy closes in

The Atjehneezen, scenting weakness, crept closer each night. They feasted openly, with *gamelan* and drumming, three or four hundred paces from the parapet, slaughtering and roasting Dutch slaughter-oxen stolen across the line. On the night of [[16 July|16]]–[[17 July]] [[1874]] — only weeks after the worst of the crisis — seven freshly landed slaughter-oxen were led clean over the parapet of K. Radja, through a gap in the *bamboe-doerie*, without anyone noticing until morning, an episode Borel cites as proof of the *verregaande brutaliteit* of the enemy and of the wretched state of Dutch watchfulness.

## The thread snaps tight

Pel warned the government in Batavia repeatedly that the establishment could not hold out another fortnight. The arrival of the [[Indische Stoomboot-maatschappij]]'s steamer *Baron Sloet van de Beele* on [[11 June]] [[1874]] with roughly 400 men — disembarked on [[12 June]] and *feestelijk ontvangen* by the garrison, with the *Prins Alexander* anchoring the same day with a couple of hundred more — broke the spell. [[Liggelden|Demurrage]] alone on the *Baron Sloet van de Beele* cost the Treasury almost ƒ 17 000. The reinforcements let Pel clear the ambulances, resume offensive operations, and open the [[Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)|first attack on Lampoeloe]] on [[20 June]] [[1874]] — "den eersten stap op een nieuwen weg."

The **Landing at Pante Cermin** occurred on [[8 April]] [[1873]] when the invasion force of the [[First Aceh Expedition]] under Major-General [[Johan Harmen Rudolf Köhler]] established a beachhead on the coast of Great Aceh.

## Detailed Narrative

In the early morning of [[8 April]] [[1873]] at 5:00 AM, Dutch warships anchored off [[Pante Cermin]] opened a heavy artillery bombardment against coastal stockades. Under cover of naval gunfire, four waves of landing craft carrying infantry, mountain guns, and sappers moved toward the shore, flanked by armed naval sloops commanded by Captain-Lieutenant [[J.W. Binkes]].

The 6th Company of the 12th Infantry Battalion landed first at 6:00 AM and was immediately charged by 400 Acehnese warriors armed with traditional sword-blades (*klewang*). Hand-to-hand fighting ensued on the sandbars. The Dutch troops, armed with modern Beaumont breech-loading rifles, repulsed the assault, leaving 82 Acehnese warriors dead on the beach.

By 11:30 AM, Colonel [[Eduard Cornelis van Daalen]] had brought the 2nd landing wave ashore and ordered an assault on [[Kota Mogat]], a stone fortress backed against a coastal lagoon. The initial infantry attack was repulsed by heavy musket fire from the fort's high stone walls. After naval guns bombarded the walls on [[9 April]] [[1873]], Dutch forces secured Kota Mogat and established a fortified shore bivouac.

## Impact

The successful landing secured the initial operational base for the [[First Aceh Expedition]], allowing colonial forces to disembark horses, supply trains, and ammunition before advancing inland toward the [[Mesjid Raya Aceh]].

**Bivouacs on Groot Atjeh** were temporary Dutch marine encampments established in the interior of [[Groot Atjeh]] during the [[Aceh Expedition (1873–1876)|Atjeh expedition (1873–1876)]] and the subsequent [[Aceh Expedition (1873–1876)|Atjeh campaign (26 December 1875 – 4 September 1876)]]. The locations are noted on the *Map of Groot Atjeh* (c. [[1873]]) and described in Cambier’s monographs (see pages 471‑473).  

## Purpose  

The bivouacs served as supply depots, resting points for troops advancing through dense jungle, and bases for reconnaissance missions against Acehnese resistance.  

## Documented Sites  

- **Camp Alpha** – Near the upper reaches of the [[Peusangan River]].  
- **Camp Beta** – On a plateau overlooking the interior road to [[Banda Aceh]].  

## Operational Impact  

These camps enabled the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] to sustain prolonged operations far from the coast, though they also exposed troops to disease and supply difficulties, factors that contributed to the temporary withdrawal on [[7 February 1876]].

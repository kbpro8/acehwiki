**The Murder of Mas Soemo Widikdjo** in [[December 1873|late December 1873]] at [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] was the murder of the native envoy dispatched by Lieutenant-general [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]] to deliver two letters to the [[Sultan of Aceh|sultan]] offering peace on the model of the [[Tractaat with Siak of 1858|Siak-tractaat]] and proposing to wage war "in the manner of civilised nations." Mas Soemo Widikdjo, the son of a British-Indian officer, had repeatedly rendered services as a guide or interpreter on previous occasions, hoping for his children to recover the place in society which he had lost for himself by the early death of his father. His murder hardened the Dutch resolution to take the Kraton by force.

## Background

After the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] had landed east of the Atjeh river on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]] and had pushed forward to the mouth of the Atjeh river by [[15 December]] [[15 December]], Van Swieten decided to make one last attempt to end the war by negotiation. He sent two letters to the sultan. In the one, the sultan was informed on what conditions the Dutch were still willing to make peace — namely, if he declared himself willing to sign a tractaat of about the same content as the Siak-tractaat. In the other, the sultan was proposed to wage war "in the manner of civilised nations." The bearer, Mas Soemo Widikdjo, was the son of a British-Indian officer, "who had repeatedly rendered us services as a guide or interpreter on previous occasions, hoping for his children to recover the place in society which he had lost for himself by the early death of his father."

## The Murder

He departed on [[26 December]] [[26 December]] [1873] but never returned. "Later it became known that, after being held prisoner a couple of days, he was murderously killed at Longbatta." The place of his murder — [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] — was the kampong of the [[Imam of Longbatta|imam of Longbatta]], one of the most embittered enemies of the Dutch, who was named by [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] in [[1875]] as one of the principal leaders of the war-party.

## Significance

The murder of Mas Soemo Widikdjo hardened the Dutch resolution to take the Kraton by force. When he did not return and the enemy began to fire on the bivouac, it was decided on [[25 December]] [1873] to make a reconnaissance in a south-east direction, "with the plan of finding in south-east direction an open plain, which according to the reports collected before the campaign and also according to the assertion of the guides, lay on the right bank of the river opposite the Kraton, and was therefore probably a suitable place for our siege batteries." The reconnaissance led to a fierce fight at a series of enemy fortifications near kampong Lemboe, in which the adjutant-underofficier [[E. C. O. von Bredow|Von Bredow]] planted the oranje-vaandel at the foot of the parapet; the line was carried; on [[6 January]] [[6 January]] [[1874]] the missigit of the Kraton was taken; on [[12 January]] [[12 January]] the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]] was stormed; on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|Kraton itself was found evacuated]].

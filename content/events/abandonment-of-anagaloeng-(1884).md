The **Abandonment of Anagaloeng** in [[1884]] was the Dutch evacuation of the *[[Benteng|benteng]]* held at [[Anagaloeng]] on the [[Acheh River|Atjeh-rivier]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], part of the general withdrawal behind the *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]* that inaugurated the ten-year *concentratie* period.

## The withdrawal

Hooijer recorded that the *benteng* the Dutch had kept occupied after the fighting was decided, when rest and peace seemed to prevail as far as the remotest districts of the [[XXII Moekims]], *was in 1884 verlaten, daar de onverzoenlijke en slechts voor macht bukkende Atjehers opnieuw de wapenen hadden kunnen opnemen en ons noodzaakten achter de geconcentreerde linie te wijken* — was abandoned in 1884, because the irreconcilable Achehnese, who bow only to power, had been able to take up arms again and compelled the Dutch to withdraw behind the concentrated line.

## The death of Teungkoe di Tiro

The man who had then been the Achehnese spiritual leader in the holy war, *Oelama* [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Teungkoe di Tiro]], breathed his last at Anagaloeng, lying on the gravestone erected there for [[Kapitein Miseroy]] in the time of Dutch mastery. His life-aim — to drive the Dutch out of [[Atjeh]] and himself become spiritual supreme head — had failed; the *geconcentreerde linie* had proved too strong for him.

## Later re-occupation

The Dutch returned to Anagaloeng only after [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] took the place on [[21 December]] [[1893]], and built there a *benteng* which Hooijer called a model of neatness. It was lifted and burnt on [[18 April]] [[1896]].

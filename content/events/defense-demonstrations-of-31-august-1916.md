**The defense demonstrations of [[31 August]] [[1916]] were coordinated *defensie-betoogingen* held at various *hoofdplaatsen* (principal towns) across [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]] in support of strengthening the colony's defenses, though their scale and popular character were later exposed as significantly exaggerated.**

## The Demonstrations

On [[31 August]] [[1916]], defense demonstrations took place at multiple *hoofdplaatsen* across [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]]. The gatherings were organized in support of strengthening the defense of the colony and were presented as evidence of broad indigenous and European enthusiasm for the defense cause.

## Van Heutsz's Representation

In his [[Van Heutsz's Bond van Vrije Liberalen defense speech (13 January 1917)|speech of 13 January 1917]] before the [[Bond van Vrije Liberalen]], [[J. B. van Heutsz]] claimed that *duizenden en nog eens duizenden Indiërs* (thousands and again thousands of Indians) had gathered to demonstrate support for defense. This portrayal suggested a mass popular movement behind the defense cause.

## Van der Maaten's Exposure

[[Van der Maaten]], who had served in India and knew conditions intimately, exposed Van Heutsz's representation as misleading. The crowds, Van der Maaten clarified, consisted of a few hundred Europeans and a few hundred intellectually developed Indians, with the remainder being *nontonners* who attended for the spectacle — what locals called *rameh* — without understanding the issue at hand. Van der Maaten compared these onlookers to people who gather to watch races, parades, exhibitions, and processions, drawn by curiosity rather than conviction.

## What the Demonstrations Actually Revealed

Despite the exaggerated claims about their scale, the demonstrations did reveal something significant. They showed that European residents of [[India]] (the Dutch term for Netherlands Indies) had finally recognized that the old approach to defense was unsustainable, breaking with what Van der Maaten called *den oud-Nederlandschen koopmans- en sleurgeest* (the old Dutch mercantile and penny-pinching spirit).

The demonstrations also indicated that a large portion of the [[Java|Javanese]] princes and high nobility — the *vorsten* and the *Javaanschen hoogen adel*, including the [[Regenten]] — along with the more developed natives, recognized that they preferred to keep the [[Netherlands]] as master. Van der Maaten suggested these sentiments were likely shaped by awareness of [[Japan]] and its rising power in the region.

## Native Skepticism

Not all intellectuals supported the defense cause. At a meeting of the [[Indische Krijgsvereeniging]] on [[6 March]] [[1916]], three of four native speakers were far from enthusiastic about the creation of a native militia on [[Java]]. Heer Satiman declared that the Javanese considered becoming a soldier the worst thing that could happen *in dit ondermaansche rijk van tranen* (in this sublunary world of tears). The Oud-Resident [[J. E. Jellesma]] cited from the [[Oetoesan Hindia]] to demonstrate native skepticism about defense contributions. Speaking at the [[Vereeniging Moederland en Koloniën]] on [[2 February]] [[1917]], Heer Walbeehm referred to a *slecht ingelichte pers* (poorly informed press) regarding native sentiment on defense.

## The Ambonese Exception

The [[Ambonese]] were noted as an exception to the general pattern of native indifference. Having become a military people with few or no illiterates, their participation in the demonstrations was considered genuinely significant. Similar conditions existed in [[Menado]], where the population had also developed a strong martial tradition under Dutch colonial military service.

## Significance

The demonstrations of [[31 August]] [[1916]] became a point of contention in the wider debate over the defense of [[Netherlands India|Netherlands Indies]]. While they marked a genuine shift in European colonial opinion, the misrepresentation of their scale by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and others became a target for critics such as [[Van der Maaten]], who used the episode to illustrate the tendency of defense advocates to inflate evidence of popular support.

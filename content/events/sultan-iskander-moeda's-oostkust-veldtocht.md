**Sultan Iskander Moeda's Oostkust-veldtocht** — the campaign of [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Sultan Iskander Moeda of Atjeh]] along [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]] — was the early-seventeenth-century military expedition by which the most powerful of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultans imposed "the definite subjection" (*de definitieve onderwerping*) of the East Coast statelets to the [[Acheh|Atjehsche rijk]]. Recorded by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] as the central event in the long Atjehnese supremacy over the northern coast of Sumatra, the campaign transformed the loose [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] influence that had followed the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|conquest of Gasip]] and the [[atjehsche-vernietiging-van-het-batakrijk-aroe-(c-1589)|destruction of the Batakrijk Aroe]] into formal political subjection — a subjection that would last, in the Bureau's account, until the East Coast statelets "knew themselves again to be free of this overlordship" in [[1769]].

## Background: Atjehnese pressure on the East Coast

By the time [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Iskander Moeda]] came to the throne of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in [[1607]], the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] had already, in the previous century, destroyed the [[rijk Gasip|kingdom of Gasip]] and shattered the [[rijk Aroe|Batak realm of Aroe]]. The coastal statelets of [[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Deli|Deli]] and [[Serdang|Serdang]] had emerged from the debris, but their status remained ambiguous: nominally independent, but subject to repeated Atjehnese pressure and, in the northernmost districts of [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] and [[Langkat|Noordelijk Langkat]], already governed by *vertegenwoordigers* — resident representatives — of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]]. The Bureau's account presents Iskander Moeda's campaign as the definitive imposition of Atjehnese sovereignty, replacing the older, looser arrangement with formal subjection.

## The campaign

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau describes the campaign in a single, dense sentence: "De zegetocht van Sultan Iskander Moeda van Atjeh langs de Oostkust had de definitieve onderwerping dier staatjes aan dat machtige rijk tengevolge gehad" — "The victorious campaign of Sultan Iskander Moeda of Atjeh along the East Coast had the definitive subjection of those statelets to that powerful realm as its consequence." The Bureau does not date the campaign to a single year, but its placement in the narrative — between the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|early-sixteenth-century conquest of Gasip]] and the [[1769|1769]] recovery of independence — fixes it within Iskander Moeda's reign, that is, between [[1607]] and [[1636]].

The "statelets" — *staatjes* — referred to are the northern Malay coastal sultanates that had emerged on the ruins of [[rijk Aroe|Aroe]]: [[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Asahan|Asahan]], and the smaller river-mouth polities of [[Batu Bara|Batoe Bara]], [[Koealoe|Koealoe]], [[Leidong|Leidong]] and [[Panei|Panei]]. The campaign brought all of these under Atjehnese sovereignty, and the *galar* — the ceremonial title of recognition — that the sultans of these statelets would henceforth receive from the hands of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] became, in the Bureau's account, the documentary proof of Atjehnese supremacy.

## The long Atjehnese supremacy (c. 1610s–1769)

The subjection imposed by [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Iskander Moeda]] lasted, the Bureau records, until [[1769]]. "In [[1769]] wisten zij zich echter weder van deze overheersching los te maken" — "In [[1769]], however, they knew themselves again to be free of this overlordship." The recovery of independence was, however, short-lived: "Doch slechts korten tijd genoten zij hunne onafhankelijkheid; weinige jaren later reeds werden zij door het wapengeweld van Ismail en Said Ali gedwongen Siak als opperheer te erkennen" — "But only a short time did they enjoy their independence; a few years later they were already forced, by the armed force of [[Sultan Ismail of Siak|Ismail]] and [[Sultan Said Ali|Said Ali]], to acknowledge Siak as overlord." The Atjehnese yoke was thus replaced, in the closing decades of the eighteenth century, by a Siak hegemony that would itself be superseded in the nineteenth.

## Significance

The Oostkust-veldtocht is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the political watershed of the pre-colonial East Coast. It transformed the loose Atjehnese influence inherited from the sixteenth-century campaigns into a formal Atjehnese overlordship that would last for the better part of two centuries, and it established the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] claim to the East Coast that would be revived — under very different circumstances — in the [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship over Langkat, Deli and Serdang]]. The Bureau's framing of the campaign also explains the long Siak-Atjeh rivalry over the East Coast: it was a rivalry over the inheritance of [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Iskander Moeda]]'s conquest.

**Raid on the Medway** was a bold Dutch naval operation conducted in [[June]] [[1667]] during the [[Second Anglo‑Dutch War]]. A fleet under the overall command of [[Michiel de Ruyter]] sailed up the River Medway, captured the English flagship [[Royal Charles]], and burned or seized numerous warships at the shipyards of [[Chatham]] and [[Rochester]].

## Execution
The raid began on [[19 June]] [[1667]] when Dutch ships passed the fort at [[Sheerness]], which was taken after a brief resistance. Marine forces, including 450 marines under the command of [[François Palm]] and [[Colonel Dolman]], landed on the island of [[Chappey]] (modern [[Chatham]]) and secured the English magazines. The operation culminated in the capture of the [[Royal Charles]] on [[23 June]] [[1667]].

## Strategic impact
The success of the raid forced England to negotiate peace, leading to the Treaty of [[1667]] (Treaty of Breda). It also demonstrated the effectiveness of Dutch amphibious tactics, which later influenced VOC operations against the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] in the Indian Ocean.

**The Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)** — the chastisement, punitive expedition of the long-feared [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak area]] (seven *mukim*s west of [[Ana' Galong]]) — was the major three-colonne operation ordered by the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] under the overall command of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] to reduce the principal Achehnese redoubt in the western *sagi* that had defied Dutch arms since [[1893]]. The 1e colonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] attacked [[Ateuh Raja]] and [[Baha]]; the 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] attacked [[Lam-Soet]] / [[Lam-Tengah]] / [[Lam-Barih]]. The operation's first day, [[9 June]] [[1896]], saw the [[Battle of Ateuh Raja]] — a continuous assault by the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. F. T. Veeren|Lt.-Col. Veeren]], in which European fusilier v. Dongen (No. 28365) was first on the *borstwering* and received a fatal chest shot from the last Achehnese defender, who was in turn shot by European fusilier v. Castricum — and the [[Combat at Baha]], where the *maréchaussée* brigades under [[Luitenant S. v. Hulstijn|Lt. Hulstijn]] and [[Luitenant M. Campioni|Lt. Campioni]] stormed the *benting* "Baha" and a newly-built *koeta* with *missigit*. The second day, [[10 June]] [[1896]], saw the [[Battle of Lam-Soet]] — a sharp engagement on the retreat in which [[Luit.-Adj. Romswinckel|Luit.-Adj. Romswinckel]] rode forward repeating the identification signal "9e bataljon" until he realised the Achehnese were using him as a target, and in which European fusilier Klaassens (No. 40093) was killed, his body not recovered despite a ¾-hour search. The Afghan adventurer [[Habib Koeala van Pedir]] — Said Anajatoellah — was killed in the operation.

## Background

The [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak area]] — a cluster of seven *mukim* lying west of [[Ana' Galong]] — had been the principal Achehnese redoubt in the western *sagi* since the [[Conquest of Ana' Galong (30 November 1893)|conquest of Ana' Galong]] of [[30 November]] [[1893]], when [[Teuku Umar]] — guided by superstition (a prophecy of death in Lamkrak) — had refused to advance further. The Dutch had launched no fewer than three major operations against the Lamkrak area in the spring of [[1896]]: the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]] of [[14 April]] [[1896]] (a punitive expedition against the *bendehoofd* [[Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]], whose residence at [[Lam-Goet]] lay on the eastern approaches to the Lamkrak); the cancelled operation of [[30 March]] [[1896]] (a planned three-colonne descent aborted because of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]); and the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] (which had cost the Dutch a 7 c.M. A. veld-gun, a 12 c.M. mortar, and a *mitrailleur*-chest, but had killed the celebrated [[Habib Lhong]] and [[Panglima Nja Bintang]]). The Lamkrak mukims had remained, however, unsubdued.

By the second week of [[June]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]], the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] was complete, and the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|operations in the IV and VI Moekims]] had cleared the northern coastal stretch. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] now resolved to chastise the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak area]] in force. Three colonnes were assembled under the overall command of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]]: the 1e colonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] (the same officer who had commanded the 1e Colonne at the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]]); the 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] (the same officer who had commanded the Bergcolonne at the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Slag bij Tjot Tjako]]); and a substantial force of *maréchaussée* — 12 brigades under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] (4 officers, 12 European under-officers, 207 *minderen*).

## First Day — 9 June 1896: The Battle of Ateuh Raja

On [[9 June]] [[1896]] the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. F. T. Veeren|Lt.-Col. Veeren]] attacked [[Ateuh Raja]] in one continuous assault. The 1e compagnie under [[Kapitein H. T. de Moulin|Kapt. H. T. de Moulin]] hit the breastwork behind the [[Kroeng Ateuh]] and was attacked by [[2de Luit. W. A. Engelbrecht|2de luit. W. A. Engelbrecht]]. European fusilier v. Dongen (No. 28365) was the first on the *borstwering* and received a fatal chest shot from the last Achehnese defender; that Achehnese was in turn shot by European fusilier v. Castricum — a sequence that captured, in microcosm, the brutal close-quarter character of the fighting. The 2de and 3de compagnieën — the 3de under [[Kapitein J. A. M. M. von Schauroth|Kapt. J. A. M. M. von Schauroth]] — drove the enemy from [[Tjot Lepong]]. The 3e compagnie then attacked two *redoute*s near the northern *rand* of [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak]] ([[Lam-Djamé]]) on von Schauroth's own initiative; both *benting*s were taken. European fusilier Blankenstein (No. 39189) and Kuipers (No. 39193) were first in, shooting the last defenders. Von Schauroth received a light grazing shot along the belly. [[Kapitein Jansen|Kapt. Jansen]]'s compagnie talso three small works in the western *rand* of [[Lam-Oeri]]. The losses of the day: 1 dead, 17 wounded (including [[2e Luit. Boerrigter|2e luit. Boerrigter]]).

## First Day — 9 June 1896: The Combat at Baha

Concurrently, during the 1e colonne's advance into [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak]], the *maréchaussée* brigades under [[Luitenant S. v. Hulstijn|Lt. S. v. Hulstijn]] — commanding the 3e compagnie of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] — advanced with the *maréchaussée*. The right peloton under [[Luitenant M. Campioni|Lt. M. Campioni]] faced the *benting* "Baha"; the left peloton faced a newly-built *koeta* with mosque. After volleys, the works were stormed. The *koeta* "Baha"'s garrison of 5 Achehnese fled into the hands of the *maréchaussée*. One Achehnese fired on Ambonese fusilier Pelitimoe (No. 41233) and Sergeant [[Stemfoort (sergeant)|Stemfoort]] (No. 40785); the Achehnese was killed. The other peloton had 1 killed in action — an Ambonese fusilier — and 1 mortally wounded — a forced labourer. The combat at Baha, though small, was a sharp engagement that demonstrated the continued determination of the Achehnese resistance even in the face of the convergent Dutch colonnes.

## Second Day — 10 June 1896: The Battle of Lam-Soet and the Burning of the Lam-Krak Kampongs

On [[10 June]] [[1896]] the 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — still believing [[Lam-Soet]] to be occupied by the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e Bataljon]] — advanced in march column toward [[Lam-Soet]]. At approximately 100 m from Lam-Soet, shots from the *benteng* hit the foremost troops. [[Luit.-Adj. Romswinckel|Luit.-Adj. Romswinckel]] rode forward repeating the identification signal "9e bataljon" — until he realised that the Achehnese were using him as a target. The spits had 1 *gesneuvelde*; the body was not found despite a ¾-hour search. The dead man was European fusilier Klaassens (No. 40093); his body was not recovered. The enemy from the *rand* of [[Ana' Batée]] and from thickets fired on the colonne, causing more wounded at the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]]. The artillery came into *batterij* on the dyke; repeating carbines were fired. The last shot from [[Lam-Tengah]] came at 3:20 p.m.; half an hour later, the troops returned from [[Lam-Baroe]] to [[Koeta-Radja]]. The 2e colonne losses: 2 European fusiliers killed; 1 second lieutenant, 13 European, 2 Ambonese, 11 native wounded; the battery lost 1 muildier (mule). Ammunition expended: 11,528 Beaumont, 258 *min. kal.*, 46 *Gr.*, 21 *G. K. T.*

After the combat, both colonnes burned the *kampong*s of the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak area]]. The 1e colonne: the [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljon]] and the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] (4 *afdeelingen* to the *Zuidrand* of the area to be *tuchtigated*, [[Blang Miraii]]); the 2e compagnie occupied [[Baha]]; at the signal, the burning proceeded northward. The 2e colonne: marched in combat formation in broad front through [[Lam-Lheuë]] toward [[Ana' Batée]]; burned everything in the area. The Afghan adventurer [[Habib Koeala van Pedir]] — Said Anajatoellah — was killed in the operation, a significant loss for the resistance.

## Significance

The Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims on [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] was the long-delayed reduction of the principal Achehnese redoubt in the western *sagi*. The [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak area]] — which had defied Dutch arms since [[1893]] and which had been the objective of three previous operations — was at last chastised: the *benteng*s were taken, the *kampong*s were burned, the Afghan adventurer [[Habib Koeala van Pedir]] was killed, and the resistance was broken. The operation was also the most costly single engagement of the June campaign: the 2e colonne alone lost 2 European fusiliers killed and 27 wounded. The death of European fusilier Klaassens — whose body was not recovered despite a ¾-hour search — was a particular blow, and the episode was preserved by Kruisheer as an exemplar of the harshness of the fighting and the difficulty of recovering the dead in the broken terrain of the Lamkrak. The *tuchtiging* of Lam-Krak was, in strategic terms, the completion of the work begun at the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]: with the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak area]] reduced and Oemar's *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] broken, the Dutch had recovered the strategic initiative and had established the conditions for the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]].

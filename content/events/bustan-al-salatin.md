**The *Bustān as-Salāṭīn*** ("The Garden of Kings") is a Malay didactic and historical compendium composed by [[Raniri|Nuruddin ar-Raniri]] in [[Acheh]] during the reign of [[Iskandar Thani|Sultan Iskandar Thani]] and his successor [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Taj al-Ālam]]. It is one of the major works of Malay prose of the seventeenth century and, with the [[Sirat al-mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]] and the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]], one of the three principal writings by which Raniri shaped the religious and political literature of the Achehnese sultanate after the suppression of the [[Wujudiyyah]] mysticism of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] and [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]].

## Genre and content

The *Bustān as-Salāṭīn* belongs to the genre of "mirror for princes" (*adab* literature) and at the same time to that of universal history. Its declared purpose is to instruct rulers and their ministers in the principles of just government by exempla drawn from the lives of past kings, prophets, sages, and saints. Its range is encyclopaedic: cosmology, the history of the prophets, the history of the pre-Islamic kings of Persia and of the caliphs, the history of the saints and mystics, the principles of statecraft, the duties of the ruler and the ruled. The work was composed in the *[[Bahasa Djawi]]* of the Achehnese court, drawing on Arabic and Persian sources familiar to Raniri from his Gujarati education, and on Malay sources — including the historical traditions of [[Acheh]] itself.

## Place in Raniri's oeuvre

The *Bustān as-Salāṭīn* is named by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] in *The Achehnese* as one of the principal writings of the pandit, alongside the [[Sirat al-mustaqim|*Ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm*]]. It was composed in [[Acheh]] in the period between Raniri's arrival in [[1637]] and his return to [[Ranir]] in [[1644]]. [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]], in his introduction to the [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises, notes that "of the works that Raniri composed in Acheh, most are directed against the Wujūdiyyah" — but the *Bustān as-Salāṭīn* belongs to a different category: a work of statecraft and universal history rather than of polemic. Its composition in the immediate aftermath of the [[Raniri's public debate against the Wujudiyyah in Acheh|public disputation]] against the Wujūdiyyah suggests that it formed part of the same project of orthodox reform — providing the new Achehnese monarchy with the literary apparatus of a Shāfiʿite sultanate.

## Manuscripts and reception

The *Bustān as-Salāṭīn* survives in numerous Malay manuscripts, of which the most important are preserved in the collections of the Leiden University Library, the [[Royal Asiatic Society]] in London, and the Koninklijk Instituut voor Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde at The Hague. The work remained a standard reference for Malay historians and religious teachers throughout the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; it was used as a source by later Malay chroniclers and is cited in the historical literature of [[Acheh]], [[Johor]], and other Malay sultanates.

## Significance

The *Bustān as-Salāṭīn* is the most ambitious of Raniri's Malay compositions and the work in which his vision of an orthodox, Shāfiʿite Achehnese sultanate finds its fullest literary expression. It is at once a monument of Malay prose and a programme of royal education: a *mirror for princes* composed for the court of [[Iskandar Thani]] and [[Sultan Tajul-alam|Taj al-Ālam]] in the very years when the suppression of the [[Wujudiyyah]] was redrawing the contours of Achehnese religious life.

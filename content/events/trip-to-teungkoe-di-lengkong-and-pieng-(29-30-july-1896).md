**The Trip to Teungkoe di Lengkong and Pièng** — *tocht naar Teungkoe di Lengkong en Pièng op 29 en 30 Juli* [[1896]] — was a two-day Dutch reconnaissance and diplomatic contact with the religious leader [[Habib Lengkong|Teungkoe di Lengkong]] of the [[Oostergebergte]] (Eastern Highlands) of [[Groot-Atjeh]], undertaken on [[29 July|29]]–[[30 July]] [[1896]]. The trip formed part of the broader Dutch strategy of the second semester of [[1896]] — the slow extension of effective Dutch knowledge of, and influence over, the religious leaders of the interior, in preference to the permanent occupation of points beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line). The contact with [[Habib Lengkong]] — a *Habib* (descendant of the Prophet) and a religious leader of the eastern highlands — was a significant diplomatic gesture, reflecting the Dutch recognition that the *pacificatie* (pacification) of Groot-Atjeh would require the cooperation or neutralisation of the religious leadership of the interior.

## The Strategic Context

By the end of July [[1896]], the Dutch, under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s second tenure, had established the [[Occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga) (15 July 1896)|permanent occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga)]] on [[15 July]] [[1896]] and had conducted a [[Reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims (24 July 1896)|reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims]] on [[24 July]] [[1896]]. The trip to Teungkoe di Lengkong and Pièng of [[29 July|29]]–[[30 July]] [[1896]] was the next step in the slow forward movement of the second semester — a diplomatic reconnaissance of the eastern highlands, designed to ascertain the disposition of the religious leaders of the interior and to establish direct contact with the *Habib* (descendant of the Prophet (Habib)) of Lengkong. The choice of Lengkong as the destination reflected the strategic importance of the [[Oostergebergte]] — the eastern highlands which formed the watershed between the [[Aceh River|Atjeh-rivier]] basin and the eastern coast of Atjeh, and which had been a centre of Acehnese religious and military resistance throughout the war.

## The Religious Leadership of the Oostergebergte

The religious leadership of the [[Oostergebergte]] was a complex hierarchy of *Teungkoes*, *Habibs*, and *Ulamas* — the religious scholars and descendants of the Prophet who provided the ideological leadership of the Acehnese resistance. [[Habib Lengkong]] — the *Habib* (descendant of the Prophet (Habib)) of Lengkong, in the eastern highlands — was one of the principal religious figures of the interior, and his disposition towards the Dutch was a critical factor in the *pacificatie* (pacification) of the eastern highlands. The Dutch contact with Habib Lengkong on [[29 July|29]]–[[30 July]] [[1896]] was thus both a reconnaissance and a diplomatic gesture — designed to ascertain the Habib's disposition and to establish a channel of communication which might be used in the future.

## The Trip and the Contact

The trip was undertaken by a Dutch colonne, which marched from the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) into the eastern highlands on [[29 July]] [[1896]] and arrived at the *kampong* (village (kampong)) of Lengkong on the same day. The contact with [[Habib Lengkong]] was made on [[30 July]] [[1896]], and the Dutch officers — acting under Stemfoort's instructions — sought to ascertain the Habib's disposition towards the Dutch and his willingness to cooperate in the *pacificatie* (pacification) of the eastern highlands. The details of the contact are not separately narrated by Kruisheer in the detail given for other operations of the second semester, but the trip is recorded in the *Inhoud* (Table of Contents) of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as one of the principal operations of late July [[1896]].

## Significance

The Trip to Teungkoe di Lengkong and Pièng of [[29 July|29]]–[[30 July]] [[1896]] is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as the principal Dutch diplomatic contact with the religious leadership of the [[Oostergebergte]] in the second semester of [[1896]]. The trip — undertaken in the same period as the [[Occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga) (15 July 1896)|occupation of Kroeng-Raba]] and the [[Reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims (24 July 1896)|reconnaissance of the XXVI Moekims]] — formed part of the broader Dutch strategy of the second semester: the slow extension of effective Dutch knowledge of, and influence over, the religious and political leaders of the interior. The contact with Habib Lengkong — a *Habib* (descendant of the Prophet (Habib)) and a religious leader of the eastern highlands — was a significant diplomatic gesture, reflecting the Dutch recognition that the *pacificatie* (pacification) of Groot-Atjeh would require the cooperation or neutralisation of the religious leadership of the interior.

**The punishment of Marassa** was the vengeance taken by the Atjehnese resistance against the Dutch-friendly populace of the [[Marasa|moekim Marassa]] — allies of the Dutch in both expeditions — on [[27 April]] [[1874]], the day after [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)|van Swieten's departure]] with the so-called main force. The enemy punished the area and laid seven kampongs in ashes; on the same evening of the departure there was already much movement with the enemy south and east of the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]], so close to the eastern face that the garrison stood under arms and some shots were exchanged, while an Arab — one of the most trusted Dutch spies — brought in the morning the report that the enemy, in great numbers, would try to cut the main position off from the sea and so make it untenable; the [[Rivierbenting|Marinebenting]] and other points along the [[Atjeh River|Atjehrivier]] were alarmed and the communication with the sea seriously threatened. The events of that day, described by [[J. J. W. E. Verstege|Verstege]] after [[B. C. P. Kielstra|Kielstra]], showed how critical the inherited position of [[Jacobus Pel|kolonel Pel]] was from its very first hours.

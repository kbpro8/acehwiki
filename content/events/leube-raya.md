The **leubè raya** (Achehnese *leubè raya*, "the great feast"; also called *uròe raya*) is the feast-day that concludes the [[Puasa (Ramadhan fasting month)|Ramadhan fast]], falling on the 1st of Shawwal, the tenth month of the Mohammedan year. It corresponds to the *Hari Raya Puasa* of the Malays and the *'Id al-Fitr* of the Arabs. The feast-day is fixed by calculation like its commencement, and is thus known long beforehand. A number of guns from the [[Dalam]] at sunset on the last day, used in the Sultans' time, conveyed the superfluous announcement that the first day of the new month had begun.

The night before the commencement of the feast is one of general rejoicing. Before every housedoor is set a lighted lamp with seven mouths, or "eyes" as the Achehnese call them. The children let off numerous "little Chinese guns" (*beudé China*, i.e. crackers), and the women are busily employed with the preparation of food, especially **jeumphan**, a kind of small cake made of ground glutinous rice mixed with cocoanut and sugar, rolled or folded into the shape of a cylinder or prism, wrapped in a leaf, and steamed or boiled. The *jeumphan*, also called *limphan*, most closely resembles what the Malays call *lëpok* (in Menangkabau Malay *lënggëk*). Guns used to be fired in the Dalam on the *uròe raya* from 4 A.M. till the afternoon.

Early in the morning all the men go forth and take a **feast-bath** (*manòe uròe raya*). Besides this bath, the law strongly recommends a religious service to celebrate the feast (*seumayang uròe raya*), held in the chapels, great and small, or else without regard to place, in the morning after sunrise, followed by a sermon. Before this service, every householder pays the [[Pitrah (zakat fitrah)|pitrah]] on behalf of himself and all his dependents, in kind — two Achehnese *ares* of rice per head, heaped up so as to rise in a cone at the top. The customary preliminary breakfast on the last day of the fast is a porridge (*kanji*) made from rice and various leaves pounded fine, cooked by some poor old man of the gampong, who receives from the teungku a share of the pitrah for his pains.

The rest of the day is given over to exchanges of congratulation and visits. The imeums are waited on by all their subordinate keuchi's and teungkus and by many of the common people, the latter making obeisance from a respectful distance, just as in an ordinary visit. Sirih is first served to the visitors, followed by jeumphan and other sweetmeats; the drinking of coffee on such occasions is quite a modern custom but is gradually becoming more in vogue. The uleebalangs are visited by few below the rank of teungkus. In the afternoon many visit the family burial-places, an observance in which the Achehnese differ from the Javanese, who assemble for the feasts of all souls at the burial-places; in Acheh these feasts are always held at home. Gambling, despite prohibition, is a feature of the feast-day amusements, as it is of the *kurubeu'en* sacrifices at [[Bitay]].

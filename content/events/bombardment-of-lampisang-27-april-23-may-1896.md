**The Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)** was the sustained [[Dutch]] artillery bombardment of [[Teuku Umar]]'s fortified *stellingen* in the [[Lampoeng]] / [[Lampisang]] region — the principal defensive works covering Umar's residence at [[Lam-Pisang]] and his base at [[Lepong]] — conducted from the principal Dutch battery at [[Lam-Djamoe]] with mortars of 20 c.M., 12 c.M. A., and a 7 c.M. A. veldbatterij, reinforced on [[27 April]] [[1896]] with 5 officers and 105 men of the fortress artillery, and combined throughout with the naval bombardment of [[Lepong]] from the [[Kroeng-Rababaai]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the bombardment was the preparatory phase of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896]]: a month-long softening-up of Umar's positions that, while it did not break the Achehnese defence, fixed the enemy's attention, degraded his works, and set the conditions for the storming of [[Tjot Tjako]] and the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang]].

## Background

By [[27 April]] [[1896]] the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], had completed the [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuation of the buitenlinie]] and was preparing the counter-offensive against [[Teuku Umar]]. The principal obstacle was Umar's *stelling* — a system of fortified works centred on his residence at [[Lam-Pisang]] and extending across the low hills of [[Tjot Tjako]], [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Lam-Tjroeng]], [[Daman]], and [[Lam-Poelau]], covering the approaches to his base at [[Lepong]] on the western coast. The *stelling* was too strong to be carried by a coup de main and too extensive to be invested without prior reduction. Vetter resolved to soften it up by a sustained artillery bombardment from the Dutch post at [[Lam-Djamoe]] — the closest Dutch position to Umar's works — and to combine the land bombardment with a naval bombardment of [[Lepong]] from the [[Kroeng-Rababaai]] (the bay of the Kroeng Raba river).

The principal battery at [[Lam-Djamoe]] was equipped with mortars of 20 c.M. (heavy siege mortars), 12 c.M. A. (mountain howitzers), and a 7 c.M. A. field battery — a combination that allowed both high-angle fire against the interior of the *benteng*s and direct fire against the parapets. On [[27 April]] [[1896]] the battery was reinforced with 5 officers and 105 lower ranks of the fortress artillery — a deliberate augmentation that brought the firepower of a siege train to what had been, until then, a forward post. The bombardment would last, with intervals, for nearly four weeks.

## The Course of the Bombardment

From [[27 April]] [[1896]] the battery at [[Lam-Djamoe]] maintained a steady fire against Umar's *stellingen*. The 20 c.M. mortars — heavy bronze pieces throwing a shell of substantial explosive content — were directed against the interior of the works at [[Lam-Pisang]], [[Tjot Tjako]], and [[Boekit Seboen]], where their plunging fire could reach the defenders behind the parapets. The 12 c.M. A. howitzers were used against the *bamboedoeri* abatis and the earthen walls, attempting to breach the works for assault. The 7 c.M. A. *veldbatterij* — the lightest of the three calibres — was used for counter-battery fire against Achehnese guns and for harassing fire against the *kampong* edges. The rate of fire was sustained throughout the four weeks, with periodic intensifications as targets of opportunity presented themselves.

Concurrently, the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Dutch Navy]] conducted the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai]] of [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]], threatening [[Lepong]] and making Umar's western base untenable. The naval bombardment of Lepong would be repeated on [[25 May]], [[4 June]]–[[5 June]], and [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]]. The combined effect of the land bombardment from [[Lam-Djamoe]] and the naval bombardment from the [[Kroeng-Rababaai]] was to compress Umar's freedom of manoeuvre: his *stellingen* at [[Lam-Pisang]] were under continuous fire; his base at [[Lepong]] was under intermittent naval fire; and the gap between them — the country of the [[VI Moekims]] and the [[XXII Moekims]] — was increasingly dominated by Dutch columns.

## The Intelligence Picture

The bombardment was, however, more a means of shaping the battlespace than of breaking the enemy. Achehnese *benteng*s — earthen walls behind bamboo-thorn abatis, with deep shelters (*kulu*) cut into the interior — were largely proof against anything short of a direct hit by a heavy mortar shell. The defenders could, and did, shelter in their *kulu* during the bombardment and emerge to man the parapets the moment the infantry assault began. The bombardment's principal effects were therefore indirect: it forced Umar to keep his warriors under cover, it degraded his communications between the *stellingen*, it disrupted his supply of food and ammunition, and it imposed a steady drain of casualties from direct hits and from the demoralisation of sustained shellfire. It also allowed the Dutch to gather detailed intelligence on the layout of the *stellingen* — intelligence that would be crucial to the planning of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]].

## The Culmination and the Offensive

The bombardment culminated in the third week of [[May]] [[1896]] as Vetter brought his forces into position for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the [[Bergcolonne]] under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] marched from [[Ketapan Doea]] at 3:30 a.m. to surprise the enemy at [[Tjot Tjako]]; on [[24 May]] [[1896]] the colonne of [[Kapitein C. J. Laceulle|Kapt. Laceulle]] marched from [[Lam-Djamoe]] to [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture Lam-Pisang]]; on [[25 May]] [[1896]] the colonne of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] stormed the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]]. The bombardment had done its work: Umar's warriors were tired, his communications disrupted, and his works degraded. The storming of the *stellingen* would still be hard — the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Slag bij Tjot Tjako]] and the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|bestorming of 25 May]] were among the hardest-fought actions of the war — but the conditions for success had been set.

## Significance

The Bombardment of Lampisang, lasting from [[27 April]] to [[23 May]] [[1896]], was the longest sustained Dutch artillery preparation of the first semester of [[1896]] and the prototype of the combined-arms operations that would characterise the rest of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. It demonstrated the importance of heavy fortress artillery in the reduction of Achehnese *stellingen* — a lesson that [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] would later carry forward in his own command — and it established the operational pattern of sustained bombardment followed by infantry assault that would be repeated at the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]]. The bombardment also marked the beginning of the strategic recovery: from [[27 April]] [[1896]] onward the Dutch were on the offensive, and the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[Teuku Umar]] — though not yet undone — was being answered.

**Operette van Tjaleuë (the underhoofden-action interception)** (c. 1930s) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. In a landschap with serious friction between the head and the under-chiefs (a movement called the 'onderhoofden-actie' that exists in various forms across landschappen), groups of dissatisfied underhoofden-supporters had twice already gone directly to the governor in Koeta Radja, bypassing all instances. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to c. 1930s.

## Account

In a [[landschap]] with serious friction between the head and the under-chiefs (a movement called the 'onderhoofden-actie' that exists in various forms across landschappen), groups of dissatisfied underhoofden-supporters had twice already gone directly to the governor in [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]], bypassing all instances. Both times the governor personally promised a thorough investigation and asked them to wait. A third such trek was organized, but strict orders came from [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] that the governor would receive no more deputations and that they should, if necessary, be stopped by force. The author and a [[Controleur|controleur]] posted themselves at the crossroads [[Tjaleuë]], with a [[Brigade (Maréchaussée)|brigade]] under a kapitein housed in a [[volksschooltje]] nearby. The first groups were held without difficulty until the estimated number (including instigators) was assembled. The author addressed them first with a friendly chat and managed a flat joke that won over the audience. With no other 'violence' than a serious word that hinted it could become bitter earnest, the men were sent home. Within a quarter hour they were busily selling the rice they had brought (at better prices than in their own [[Gampong|gampong]]). When an extra train pulled up to take them home for free, they looked more like a troop of noisy fair-goers in high spirits than a group of the resolute who had sworn to bring down their head.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

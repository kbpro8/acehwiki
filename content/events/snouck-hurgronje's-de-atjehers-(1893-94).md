**Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers* (1893-94)** ("The Atjehnese") is the two-volume foundational ethnography of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]nese by [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]], published in [[1893]]-[[1894]] (Leiden: E. J. Brill), and is, with [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's Atjeh (1922-23)|*Atjeh* (1922-23)]], one of the two principal Dutch scholarly syntheses on Atjeh as a country and people. The English translation by A. W. S. O'Sullivan, with index by R. J. Wilkinson, was published as *The Achehnese* (Leiden: E. J. Brill; London: Luzac & Co., 1906). The work is the most-cited source in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] for Atjehnese ethnographic detail, and is cited throughout the Atjeh-specific entries in this wiki.

## The work

Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers* (1893-94) was the product of the author's years of study of Atjehnese language, religion, custom and history, conducted in part during his advisory role to the Dutch colonial government during the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]]. The work is, in its breadth and depth, the foundational ethnography of the Atjehnese: it treats Atjehnese religious life (Islam as practised in Atjeh, with attention to the Sufi *tarekat* and the saint-veneration or *kramat* cult), Atjehnese adat (customary law, including the institutional structure of the *mukim*, the *teungkoe meunasah*, the *keutjhiq*, and the *kawōm*), Atjehnese ritual practice (the [[maq meugang|*maq meugang*]] slaughter-days, the [[hakikah|*hakikah*]] newborn-sacrifice, the [[Koeroebeuën|*Koeroebeuën*]] Id al-Adha sacrifice, the [[Hakikah and Koeroebeuën pre-Islamic preparation in Atjeh|pre-Islamic preparation rites]]), Atjehnese folk-belief and magic (the [[baris-Laksamana|*baris-Laksamana*]] magic circle, the [[sakét oelèe neuraja|*sakét oelèe neuraja*]] maturation crisis), and Atjehnese buffalo-related custom (the [[Atjehse Id al-Adha cow-not-buffalo choice|cow-not-buffalo choice at Id al-Adha]], the [[poewôê siê|*poewôê siê*]] husband obligation, the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|buffalo-riding *schimpstraf*]], the [[Pantang of the albino buffalo in Atjeh|albino-buffalo prohibition]] for [[kawōm Dja Sandang|*kawōm* Dja Sandang]], the [[Rabò annual seven-day feast|Rabò buffalo-sacrifice feast]]).

## The English translation

The English translation by A. W. S. O'Sullivan, with index by R. J. Wilkinson, was published as *The Achehnese* (Leiden: E. J. Brill; London: Luzac & Co., 1906) in two volumes. The translation is cited in *De Karbouw* (p. 108, footnote) for the illustration of a buffalo-driven sugar-mill at vol. I, p. 274, and is the version by which the work is most widely known in the English-speaking scholarly world.

## Significance

Snouck Hurgronje's *De Atjehers* (1893-94) is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as the foundational ethnography of the Atjehnese — the principal Dutch scholarly source on Atjehnese language, religion, custom and history, and the most-cited source in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw (1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] for Atjehnese ethnographic detail. The work is, with [[Kreemer's Atjeh (1922-23)|Kreemer's *Atjeh* (1922-23)]], one of the two principal Dutch scholarly syntheses on Atjeh as a country and people, and is cited throughout the Atjeh-specific entries in this wiki. Its English translation as *The Achehnese* (1906) made the work accessible to the English-speaking scholarly world and remains the standard English-language reference.

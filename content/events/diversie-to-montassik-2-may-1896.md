**The Diversie to Montassik / Battle of Senelop (2 May 1896)** — the diversion to [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]] mounted by a colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]], with Chef van den Staf [[Kapitein G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapt. G. C. E. van Daalen]], on [[2 May]] [[1896]] — was the second Dutch operation against [[Senelop]] in the spring of [[1896]], conducted fifteen days after the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|first battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] as part of the wider shaping operations preceding the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May]]. The colonne, comprising *maréchaussée* and a peloton of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]], talso [[Senelop]] at 9:10 a.m. — [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] giving the signal for the assault — but found "the birds flown" (the birds flown): the Achehnese had evacuated the post. [[Lamsoet]] was taken without opposition by the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] and burned. The bands of [[T. Brahim Montassik]] and [[Habib Sarih]] suffered 5 killed and 12 wounded each in the day's fighting.

## Background

The [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|first battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] had cost the Dutch a 7 c.M. A. veld-gun, a 12 c.M. mortar, and a *mitrailleur*-chest with barrels, and had cost the Achehnese 75 killed — including the celebrated [[Habib Lhong]] and [[Panglima Nja Bintang]]. The post had been evacuated by the Dutch; but the Achehnese, recognising its strategic value, had re-occupied it. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], required a diversion to draw Achehnese attention away from the preparations for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] against [[Teuku Umar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] and to disrupt the Achehnese concentration in the [[V Moekims Montassik]]. He selected [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] — recently blooded in the first battle of Senelop — to command the diversion, with [[Kapitein G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapt. G. C. E. van Daalen]] of the General Staff as his Chef van den Staf.

The colonne was small, mobile, and aggressive: *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] (the same officer who had commanded the voorhoede at the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|first battle]]), a peloton of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] armed with repeater rifles, and the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]]. The objective was a descent on [[Senelop]] and [[Lamsoet]] — a reprise of the operation of [[17 April]] [[1896]] — combined with a demonstration against [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]], the home territory of the *bendehoofd* [[T. Brahim Montassik]].

## The Taking of Senelop and Lamsoet

The colonne advanced on [[Senelop]] in the early morning of [[2 May]] [[1896]]. The approach was contested in the usual Achehnese fashion — desultory fire from the *kampong* edges, sniping from the *tjots* — but the resistance was lighter than that encountered on [[17 April]] [[1896]]. At 9:10 a.m. [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] — standing where he could be seen by his *maréchaussée* brigades — gave the signal for the assault. The *maréchaussée* and the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] peloton advanced on the post. The Ambonese *maréchaussée* Simin (No. 8502) was the first to enter — but found "the birds flown": the Achehnese had evacuated the post in the hours before the assault, recognising that they could not hold it against a determined Dutch attack and preferring to preserve their forces for the defence of the interior *stellingen*.

[[Lamsoet]] — taken on [[17 April]] [[1896]] at heavy cost — was taken on [[2 May]] [[1896]] without opposition. The [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] advanced on the post, found it empty, and burned it. The destruction of [[Lamsoet]] was, in tactical terms, a small operation; in strategic terms, however, it confirmed that the Achehnese had shifted the weight of their defence from the western *buitenlinie* — now abandoned — to the *stellingen* around [[Lam-Pisang]] and [[Lepong]].

## The Engagement with T. Brahim Montassik and Habib Sarih

The *diversie* element of the operation — the demonstration against [[V Moekims Montassik|Montassik]] — brought the Dutch column into contact with the bands of [[T. Brahim Montassik]] and [[Habib Sarih]]. These two war-leaders — the former the *bendehoofd* of Montassik, the latter a descendant of the Prophet who had taken up arms alongside the Achehnese resistance — commanded substantial followings in the south-western mukims. Their *benden* engaged the Dutch column in a series of sharp firefights in the *kampong* belt around Montassik. The casualties on each side were 5 killed and 12 wounded — modest figures by the standard of the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|first battle of Senelop]], but sufficient to confirm that the *benden* of [[T. Brahim Montassik]] and [[Habib Sarih]] were active, aggressive, and would have to be reckoned with in any future operation against the V Moekims.

## Significance

The Diversie to Montassik of [[2 May]] [[1896]] was a shaping operation rather than a decisive battle. Its principal effects were three. First, it confirmed that the Achehnese had abandoned the western *buitenlinie* and had concentrated their defence in the interior *stellingen* around [[Lam-Pisang]] — a strategic decision that, while it shortened the Achehnese lines, also played into the Dutch plan for a concentrated offensive. Second, it brought the Dutch into contact with the *benden* of [[T. Brahim Montassik]] and [[Habib Sarih]] — intelligence that would be crucial for the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] of [[16 June]]–[[17 June]] [[1896]]. Third, it kept the Achehnese off-balance during the critical weeks of preparation for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. The "the birds flown" episode at [[Senelop]] was, in its way, the strategic turning point of the spring: the Achehnese had stopped trying to hold fixed positions on the western line and had shifted to a defence of the interior — a defence that the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] would, three weeks later, put to the test.

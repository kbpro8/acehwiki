**The Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)** was the two-pronged combined-arms attack on the Achehnese *stelling* of [[Glé Broeë]] east of [[Kroeng-Kali]], commanded overall by [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]], in which a **1e Colonne (sea-landing)** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel A. G. Hansen|Lt.-Col. G. A. Hansen]] — comprising the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] (14 officers, 417 men), Genie, ambulance, and train — embarked at [[Oleh-leh]] at 4 p.m. on [[22 June]] [[1896]] on the [[H. M. Merapi]], the [[Gouvernementsstoomer Condor|Gouvernementsstoomer *Condor*]], and the [[Gouvernementsstoomer Albatros|Gouvernementsstoomer *Albatros*]], anchored off [[Ladoeng]] at 2:30 a.m. on [[23 June]] [[1896]], landed under the command of [[Luitenant ter zee 1e klasse van den Broek|Lt. ter zee 1e kl. van den Broek]] in four échelons, and marched through the mountains via [[Glé Broeë]] to reach the plain of the [[XXVI Moekims]] by 11:30 a.m. — while a **2e Colonne (land)** under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] himself, comprising the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e]], [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e]], and [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljons]], two cavalry pelotons, the [[2e Bergbatterij|2e]] and [[4e Bergbatterij|4e Bergbatterijen]], Genie detachments, and three ambulances, marched from [[Lam-Njong]] at 6:45 a.m. on [[23 June]] [[1896]]. Compagnie Linck stormed the [[Kroeng-Kali benteng|Kroeng-Kali *benteng*]]; 2nd Lt. [[Heyligers]] was first inside (with hoornblazer van Spanje and fusilier Veeges No. 31895); the *benteng* was destroyed. [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] retreated to [[Lam-Tobah]].

## Background

By the fourth week of [[June]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]], the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] had reduced the western redoubt, and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] had broken the south-western *benteng*s and had driven [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] into retreat. The remaining Achehnese strongpoint of consequence was the [[Glé Broeë]] *stelling* east of [[Kroeng-Kali]] — a system of fortified works on the slopes of the eponymous hill, which had been the target of the [[Recapture of bentengs Glé Broë and Koeta T. Rajoet (4 August 1895)|recapture of 4 August 1895]] and which the Achehnese had since re-fortified. The [[Kroeng-Kali benteng|Kroeng-Kali *benteng*]] — a square redoubt 15 m on a side, with walls 1.5 m high and a small swivel gun — formed the eastern anchor of the *stelling* and had been the target of the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] of [[17 June]] [[1896]], when compagnie Linck had stormed it under fire from the *lilla*.

The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] resolved to mount a final, two-pronged operation against the [[Glé Broeë]] *stelling* before his [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|departure]] from Atjeh on [[28 June]] [[1896]]. The plan called for a sea-landing colonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel A. G. Hansen|Lt.-Col. Hansen]] to land at [[Ladoeng]] on the eastern coast, to march through the mountains via [[Glé Broeë]], and to descend on the [[XXVI Moekims]] plain from the east; while a land colonne under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] himself advanced from the west along the established road. The two colonnes would converge on the [[Glé Broeë]] *stelling* and the [[Kroeng-Kali benteng|Kroeng-Kali *benteng*]] from opposite directions.

## The 1e Colonne (Sea-Landing) under Hansen

The 1e Colonne (sea-landing) under [[Luitenant-Kolonel A. G. Hansen|Lt.-Col. G. A. Hansen]] comprised the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] (14 officers, 417 men), Genie (1 officer, 21 men), ambulance under Dr. Rasoux-Kühr (12 men), and train (4 *mandoers*, 112 *dwangarbeiders*). The Chef van den Staf was [[Kapitein G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapt. van Daalen]] — the same officer who had served as Van Heutsz's Chef van den Staf at the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Slag bij Tjot Tjako]]; the Adjudant was [[1e Luit. Onland|1e Luit. Onland]].

The 1e Colonne embarked at [[Oleh-leh]] at 4 p.m. on [[22 June]] [[1896]] on three vessels: the [[H. M. Merapi]] (carrying the staff, one European compagnie, and the train); the [[Gouvernementsstoomer Condor|Gouvernementsstoomer *Condor*]] (carrying one Ambonese compagnie and the train); and the [[Gouvernementsstoomer Albatros|Gouvernementsstoomer *Albatros*]] (carrying one European and one Ambonese compagnie, the train, the Genie, and the ambulance). The three vessels anchored off [[Ladoeng]] at 2:30 a.m. on [[23 June]] [[1896]].

The landing was led by [[Luitenant ter zee 1e klasse van den Broek|Lt. ter zee 1e kl. van den Broek]] in four échelons. The disembarkation was contested: one European sergeant and one fusilier were wounded by an Achehnese (who was killed). The 1e Colonne then marched through the mountains via [[Glé Broeë]], reaching the [[XXVI Moekims]] plain by 11:30 a.m. — emerging east of [[Lambaroe]] rather than opposite the [[Glé Broeë]] *versterkingen*, because the latter were already occupied by the Dutch 2e Colonne. The 1e Colonne crossed the [[Kroeng-Angin]] bridge at 1:45 p.m., rested at [[Kroeng-Kali]], reached [[Boekit Karang]] by 3:40 p.m., and the [[Kraton]] an hour later. The march through the mountains was, by Kruisheer's reckoning, comparable to the earlier columns of Dompseler ([[1877]], 6–7 hours) and Halewijn ([[1879]], 9.5 hours in reverse) — a measure of the difficulty of the terrain.

## The 2e Colonne (Land) under Stemfoort

The 2e Colonne (land) under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] comprised the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e]], [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e]], and [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljons]], two cavalry pelotons (under [[1e Luit. C. W. F. Happé|Happé]] and [[1e Luit. J. P. Gentil|Gentil]]), the [[2e Bergbatterij|2e]] and [[4e Bergbatterij|4e Bergbatterijen]], Genie detachments, and three ambulances. The 2e Colonne marched from [[Lam-Njong]] at 6:45 a.m. on [[23 June]] [[1896]]. The cavalry — pelotons Happé and Gentil — scouted [[Landoero]], [[Lam-Poe-Oe]], [[Lam-Genoi]], [[Masdjid Siem]], and [[Lam-Oedjoeng]]: no resistance was encountered. The *hoofd* of [[Lam-Poe-Oe]] hoisted a white flag. About 50 men under [[Teungkoe Panteh Glima]] were reported in the eastern *stelling*.

The artillery opened fire at 10 a.m. on the old [[Kroeng-Kali benteng|Kroeng-Kali *benteng*]]; the Achehnese fled toward [[Glé Pajong]]. The bridge was repaired; the cavalry passed at 10:47 a.m. By 12:50 p.m. the *versterkingen* on [[Glé Broeë]] were found weakly occupied; a compagnie of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] and the Genie burned the buildings there.

## The Storming of the Kroeng-Kali Benteng

The storming of the [[Kroeng-Kali benteng|Kroeng-Kali *benteng*]] fell to compagnie Linck — the same compagnie that had stormed the *benteng* at the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] on [[17 June]] [[1896]]. The *benteng* was a square redoubt, 15 m on a side, with walls 1.5 m high, defended by a a small swivel gun of the type used by the Achehnese. Compagnie Linck closed under fire from the *lilla*. **2nd Lt. [[Heyligers]] was the first inside the *benteng*** — accompanied by hoornblazer van Spanje and fusilier Veeges (No. 31895). The same three officers and men who had been first into the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* on [[17 June]] [[1896]] were first in again on [[23 June]] [[1896]] — a remarkable distinction. 1st Lt. [[P. van der Molen]] and 2 European fusiliers were wounded. The *benteng* was destroyed. About 10 muzzle-loading rifles were captured.

## The 12e Bataljon's Advance and the Return

The [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] marched via [[Lam-Poe-Oe]] and [[Lepong]] eastward to the swamp, lalsoing for [[Lam-Blang]] (which the guide could not locate), then south. The 12e Bataljon was heavily fired upon from the mountains: 2 European fusiliers were wounded, 1 fatally. The 12e Bataljon returned via [[Kroeng-Kali]] under continuous flank and rear fire; 4 more were wounded near the bridge. The [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] covered the retreat. The last troops reached [[Lam-Njong]] at 7 p.m. The losses of the 2e Colonne on [[23 June]] [[1896]]: 1 European fusilier dead, 2 European and 3 native fusiliers wounded. Ammunition expended: 23 *granaten*, 24 *granaatkartetsen*, 8,784 Beaumont cartridges.

[[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] retreated to [[Lam-Tobah]].

## Significance

The Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] was the largest amphibious operation of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the first semester of [[1896]] and the operational climax of the June campaign. The two-pronged attack — a sea-landing colonne and a land colonne converging on the same objective from opposite directions — demonstrated the full integration of the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Dutch Navy]] and the Dutch Army in a single coordinated operation, and the operational debut of the *marinelandingsdivisie* as a strategic instrument. The storming of the [[Kroeng-Kali benteng|Kroeng-Kali *benteng*]] by compagnie Linck — with the same three men (Heyligers, van Spanje, Veeges) first in for the second time in a week — was one of the most-celebrated small-unit actions of the campaign. The operation also marked the operational close of the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] interregnum: within five days [[Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)|the farewell parade]] would be held, and within six days Vetter would [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|depart]] for Batavia. The Ladoeng Landing was, in this sense, the final military achievement of the first semester of [[1896]] — the closing of the strategic recovery that had begun, in confusion and disaster, with the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]].

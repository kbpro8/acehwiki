**The Jappentijd** — the Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies from [[1942]] to [[1945]] — was the period of Japanese military rule that began with the Japanese invasion of [[January]]-[[March 1942]] and ended with the Japanese surrender of [[August 1945]], during which the [[K.N.I.L.]] was dissolved, its European soldaten interned in prisoner-of-war camps, and its indigenous soldaten — including the Ambonese of the [[Korps Marechaussee van Atjeh en Onderhorigen|Marechaussee]] — demobilised, pressed into Japanese auxiliary formations like the *[[Heiho]]*, or killed. In *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* the *Jappentijd* is the great rupture of the colonial army's history: the moment at which the *[[tangsi]]* culture that had made *[[Oom Ambon]]* was broken, and the moment that conditioned the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution that followed.

## The invasion and the dissolution of the KNIL

The Japanese invasion of the Dutch East Indies began in [[January 1942]] and was complete by [[March 1942]]. The [[K.N.I.L.]] — weakened by the cutbacks of the 1930s and the absence of modern equipment — was unable to mount an effective defence. The European soldaten were interned in prisoner-of-war camps; the indigenous soldaten were demobilised, pressed into Japanese auxiliary formations like the *[[Heiho]]*, or killed. The *[[tangsi]]* — the barracks culture that had made *[[Oom Ambon]]* — was broken.

## The Japanese occupation of Ambon

The Japanese occupation of [[Ambon]] was particularly harsh. The *Kempetei]] — the Japanese military police — conducted the *mass arrest of 23 September 1943|mass arrest of 23 September 1943]] on Ambon, in which large numbers of the Ambonese elite were seized and many executed. The book's named victim of the Japanese occupation is [[Pietje de Fretes]] — the *fourier* and later *onderluitenant*, nephew of [[Thijs de Fretes]] — who "helaas hebben later de Jappen ook deze aardige, vlotte en flinke vent onthoofd" — the Japanese beheaded him.

## The Heiho and the auxiliary formations

The Japanese pressed indigenous soldaten into auxiliary formations like the *[[Heiho]]* — a paramilitary labour corps — and the *PETA]] (Pembela Tanah Air, "Defenders of the Fatherland"). The book registers these formations as part of the Japanese occupation's dissolution of the colonial army: the indigenous soldaten who had served the Dutch were now made to serve the Japanese, in formations that had no continuity with the *[[tangsi]]* culture that had made them.

## The end of the occupation (August 1945)

The Japanese occupation ended with the Japanese surrender of [[August 1945]]. The Allied landing that followed — under British command — was the beginning of the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution. The book's chapter *Onder Brits bewind* describes the brief British interregnum that followed the Japanese surrender, before the Dutch returned in force and the colonial army was reconstituted as the instrument of the [[1945]]-[[1949]] war.

## Significance

The *Jappentijd* is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the great rupture of the colonial army's history. The book's argument is that the *[[tangsi]]* culture that had made *[[Oom Ambon]]* was broken by the Japanese occupation, and that the [[1945]]-[[1949]] Indonesian revolution that followed was therefore a war fought by a reconstituted colonial army, not by the old [[K.N.I.L.]]. The beheading of [[Pietje de Fretes]] is the book's small personal register of the occupation's cost: the Ambonese soldiers who had served the Dutch were killed by the Japanese, and the survivors were then used in the Dutch war against the Republic.

The **dispatch of the *Minto* to Pedir** on [[20 January 1819|20 January 1819]] was a unilateral act by [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles]] during the convoluted preliminaries to the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles–Coombs Acheh Commission]]. It was carried out by [[Lieutenant Criddle|Lieutenant Criddle]] commanding the Honourable Company's ship *Minto*, and forms one of the irregular proceedings for which Raffles was later censured by the Bengal government.

The immediate background was Raffles' decision, after receiving [[Major William Farquhar|Major Farquhar]]'s letter of [[19 January 1819|19 January]], to set the Acheh mission aside and proceed instead on his celebrated [[Raffles' detour to Karimon Islands|detour to the Karimon Islands]] — a project that culminated in the [[Founding of Singapore|founding of Singapore]]. On board ship, after the surprise departure from [[Penang]], it suddenly occurred to him ("It occurred to me for the first time") that the Achehnese chiefs and the pretender [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] were to have been informed of the commission's approach, and that [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] at [[Pedir]] would now be left without notice. Raffles feared that the Dutch might "slaan hun slag bij sultan Djohor" — steal a march on the British with the sultan at Pedir — and to forestall this he ordered Criddle, while still in Penang harbour, to make at once for Pedir.

Criddle, who apparently had no reason to decline the order, sailed to Pedir and there, in accordance with his instructions, communicated to Sultan Jauhar Alum that the commission would appear within three or four weeks and that the sultan could rely on the justice and good intentions of the British government. The sultan replied with characteristic coolness that the Dutch had made him no overture whatsoever, but promised that he would in due course enter into negotiations with them as well. Raffles thus obtained, through Criddle, what the joint commission had not yet secured: a prior contact with the sultan at Pedir.

[[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Governor Bannerman]] and [[Captain Coombs]] both protested the "eigenmachtige wegzending van de Minto". In his defence, addressed to [[O. F. Metcalfe|O. F. Metcalfe]], secretary to the government at Fort William, and dated [[Fort Marlborough]], [[30 September 1819|30 September 1819]], Raffles argued that the suddenness of his departure had precluded any prior communication with Coombs. The Calcutta government was not persuaded. Its report of [[7 October 1820|7 October 1820]] to London observed that the proceeding was "contrary to the spirit of our instructions" and "obviously improper without previous communication with his colleague", adding that the circumstances had never been sufficiently explained and indicated "an undue bias towards the interests of Jowhar ool Aulum". The dispatch of the *Minto* was, in retrospect, the first clear sign that Raffles intended to push Jauhar Alum's claims against those of [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] regardless of his colleague's opinion — a policy that led directly to the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|Treaty of Pedir]] of [[22 April 1819|22 April 1819]].

**The Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)** — the chastisement, punitive expedition of the kampong of [[Lam-Goet]], residence of the band-leader [[Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata]] — was the first offensive operation launched by the [[Dutch]] after the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] and the first Dutch *tuchtiging* of the [[Vetter]] interregnum. Conducted by two colonnes under [[Luitenant-Kolonel E. W. Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] and [[Luitenant-Kolonel H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]], totalling three infantry battalions, eight *maréchaussée* brigades, two mountain batteries and auxiliary arms, the operation stormed the fortification of [[Lam-Goet]] — Kapitein [[A. B. B. B. Jansen|A. B. B. B. Jansen]] leading the assault, against orders but successfully — destroyed an iron 9 c.M. cannon with dynamite, and was marked by heavy casualties on the retreat through the so-called "1000 m vrije stralso" (the thousand-metre free sone) in which [[Luitenant Maarschalk|Lt. Maarschalk]] was killed. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the *tuchtiging* of Lam Goet was a foretaste of the heavy fighting that would characterise the April–June campaign.

## Background

The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], had arrived on [[7 April]] [[1896]], ordered the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] on [[8 April]], and the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation]] of the two posts on [[12 April]]. He was not yet decided on which course to follow with respect to the other *buitenposten* on the western line. Before deciding, he wished to test the provisioning of [[Tjot Rang]] and to use the occasion to chastise [[Lam-Goet]] — the kampong and residence of the *bendehoofd* [[Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]], one of the principal Achehnese war-leaders in the central mukims. The objective was twofold: to demonstrate, in the immediate aftermath of the abandonment of Biloel and Lamkoenjit, that the Government could still strike; and to clear the road to Tjot Rang for the provisioning column.

Two colonnes were formed. The **1e Colonne**, under [[Luitenant-Kolonel E. W. Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] — staffed by [[Kapitein van den generalen staf H. C. Kronouer|Kap. Kronouer]] of the General Staff, [[1e Luit. E. A. van Kappen|1e Luit. van Kappen]], and [[Luitenant der Artillerie F. H. de Bruïne|Luit. der Art. F. H. de Bruïne]] — comprised the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Maj. Okhuizen]] (17 officers, 342 men), the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor G. F. Soeters|Maj. Soeters]] (16 officers, 457 men), the *maréchaussée* under [[1e Luit. H. M. Vis|1e Luit. Vis]], the [[4e Bergbatterij]] under [[Kapitein A. Bangert|Kapt. Bangert]] (six guns), the 4th Squadron of Cavalry under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|Ritmeester Jhr. de Lannoy]], the engineers under [[1e Luit. A. S. Ruzette|1e Luit. Ruzette]], and two ambulances under [[Off. van Gesondheid 2e klasse E. Rasoux-Kühr|Off. v. Gez. Rasoux-Kühr]] and [[Off. van Gesondheid J. L. W. F. van Leent|van Leent]]. Its orders were: march 7 a.m. from [[Roempit]] over [[Lam Batoe]] to [[Lam-Goet]], chastise that kampong, then march south to the [[Tjot Iri]]–[[Tjot Rang]] road; by a westward movement, in conjunction with the 2e Colonne, clear and hold that road with surrounding terrain to enable provisioning and relief of Tjot Rang.

The **2e Colonne**, under [[Luitenant-Kolonel H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]] with [[1e Luit.-Adj. F. J. Kroesen|1e Luit.-Adj. Kroesen]] as adjutant, comprised the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] (18 officers, 294 European, 261 native), the [[1e Bergbatterij]] under [[Kapitein G. J. E. Nauta|Kapt. Nauta]] (with [[J. J. Romswinckel|Romswinckel]]; six guns), the engineers under [[1e Luit. Plantenga|1e Luit. Plantenga]], and one ambulance under [[Off. van Gesondheid 1e klasse L. J. Juda|Off. v. Gez. 1e kl. Juda]]. Its orders were: march 7 a.m. from [[Tjot Iri]] toward [[Tjot Rang]], engage the enemy in the stellingen [[Baba Djoeroeng]]–[[Oleh Tjoet]]–[[Tjap Tanah]] in a dragging firefight until further orders or until the 1e Colonne's flanking movement began, then advance toward Tjot Rang, joining the 1e Colonne to clear and hold the road.

## The March and the Combat

Foot troops were moved by extra trains from [[Koeta Radja]]: the 1e Colonne to [[Roempit]], the 2e Colonne to [[Tjot Iri]]. At 7:18 a.m. on [[14 April]] [[1896]] the Roempit column advanced. The cavalry rode ahead independently, followed by *maréchaussée* and engineers, then the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] with one section, then the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] with the other two sections. The enemy had removed the decking from all bridges and replaced it with one or two tree-trunks. The terrain east of the railway grew ever swampier, becoming wholly impassable at [[Lam Baloi]]. The *kampong*s in it sat on elevations and were hard to approach. The cavalry could keep moving; the infantry could march through; the artillery had to wait for *coupures* or bridge repairs. The engineers were ordered to lift planks behind passing troops and carry them forward — much delay ensued, which the enemy noticed. From the initially unoccupied *randen* of [[Lam Baloi]] and [[Raboes]], the enemy soon opened fire. The cavalry cleared the front for the *maréchaussée*, who fired the first shots at 8:15 a.m.

The 12e Bataljon commander had orders to take two *compagnieën* and one artillery section on a footpath 1,300 m east of and parallel to the railway in a southward direction, to take position against *kampong*s [[Tjap Tanah]] and [[Baba Djoeroeng]] — heavily occupied per intelligence — and so support the 2e Colonne's attack. To save time these *compagnieën* went 600 m east of Roempit onto good terrain, avoiding a bridge. The other two 12e *compagnieën* under [[Kapitein Steup|Kapt. Steup]], with an artillery section, followed the main column. As rifle fire against the *kampong randen* yielded little, the colonne-commander ordered [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Maj. Okhuizen]] to advance as fast as possible. By 8:55 a.m. the *maréchaussée* and the 3e compagnie of the 6e Bataljon reached the covered but dry terrain of [[Raboee]]. To contain the enemy in the south *rand* of [[Lagan]], the 1e compagnie of the 6e Bataljon talso position in the north *rand* of the covered terrain near Raboee. The 4e compagnie covered the artillery.

## The Storming of Lam Goet

The fortification of [[Lam-Goet]] itself — the residence of [[Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]] — was a substantial work with earthen walls, bamboo-thorn abatis, and a parapet pierced for rifle-fire. Kapitein [[A. B. B. B. Jansen|A. B. B. B. Jansen]] of the 6e Bataljon, in defiance of the standing orders of the colonne-commander — which specified a methodical approach with artillery preparation — led his company in a direct assault on the work. The assault, against orders, succeeded: the *benteng* was carried, the defenders driven out, and the work occupied. An iron 9 c.M. cannon found inside the work was destroyed with dynamite by the engineers. The capture of the *benteng* was, in tactical terms, a clear success; in disciplinary terms, however, it was a breach of orders that the colonne-commander could not overlalso, however grateful he may have been for the result.

## The Retreat and the "1000 m vrije stralso"

The hardest fighting of the day came on the retreat. The column had to withdraw through what the source calls the "1000 m vrije stralso" — the thousand-metre free sone — a stretch of open ground between the *benteng* of Lam-Goet and the cover of the next *kampong*, across which the column was exposed to Achehnese fire from multiple directions. The Achehnese, recognising the moment of greatest Dutch vulnerability, pressed hard. The casualties were heavy. [[Luitenant Maarschalk|Lt. Maarschalk]] was killed. The artillery — the [[4e Bergbatterij]] under [[Kapitein A. Bangert|Kapt. Bangert]] — was brought into action repeatedly to break up the Achehnese concentrations, and the *maréchaussée* brigades under [[1e Luit. H. M. Vis|1e Luit. Vis]] fought a series of rear-guard actions to hold the enemy at a distance while the infantry and the wounded were extricated.

By the end of the day the two colonnes had effectually cleared the road to [[Tjot Rang]] and had chastised [[Lam-Goet]]; but the cost — [[Luitenant Maarschalk|Lt. Maarschalk]] killed, an unknown but considerable number of NCOs and men killed and wounded, and a substantial expenditure of ammunition — was heavy. The 2e Colonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]], advancing from [[Tjot Iri]] against the stellingen [[Baba Djoeroeng]]–[[Oleh Tjoet]]–[[Tjap Tanah]], had been engaged in a long firefight that had served its purpose of pinning the enemy while the 1e Colonne executed its flanking movement.

## Significance

The *tuchtiging* of Lam Goet on [[14 April]] [[1896]] was a deliberate demonstration that the [[Dutch]] retained the operational initiative even as they abandoned the western *buitenlinie*. It was also the first major test of the colonne-commanders — [[Luitenant-Kolonel E. W. Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Bisschoff van Heemskerck]], [[Luitenant-Kolonel H. Bendien|Bendien]], [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Okhuizen]], [[Majoor G. F. Soeters|Soeters]], [[Kapitein A. B. B. B. Jansen|Jansen]] — who would carry the principal burden of the fighting through the spring and summer of [[1896]]. The storming of the *benteng* by Jansen against orders was a portent of the aggressive command culture that would, within weeks, produce the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] and the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. The cost of the retreat through the "1000 m vrije stralso," however, was a sober reminder that the Achehnese, armed with Dutch weapons and led by the former allies of the Government, were no longer the disorganised force of the pre-[[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] period.

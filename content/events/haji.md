**Haji** is the Achehnese name for the twelfth month of the Mohammedan year — Arabic **Du'l-ḥijjah** (also written *Dhu al-Hijjah*), known in Achehnese also as **Doy Hijah**. C. Snouck Hurgronje records the name in the calendar of Achehnese months given in *The Achehnese*, where the Arabic names of the months are matched with their Achehnese popular equivalents.

## Place in the Calendar

The Mohammedan year, as observed in Acheh, comprises twelve lunar months, whose Arabic names and Achehnese equivalents are:

1. **Muḥarram** — Achehnese *Asan-Usén* (also the month of the [[Ashura|kanduri Ashura]])
2. **Ṣafar** — Achehnese *Sapha*
3. **Rabī' al-awwal** — Achehnese [[Maulud|Mo'lot]] (month of the [[Feast of the Birthday of Mohammad|birth of the Prophet]])
4. **Rabī' al-ākhir** — Achehnese *Adbe Mo'lot* ("Younger Brother of Mo'lot")
5. **Jumādā al-awwal** — Achehnese *Mo'lot Seumeulheueh* ("Final Mo'lot")
6. **Jumādā al-akhir** — Achehnese [[Kanduri boh kayee|Kanduri boh kayee]] ("kanduri of fruits")
7. **Rajab** — Achehnese [[Kanduri Apam|Kanduri Apam]] (also *Rajab* or *Ra'jab*); includes the [[Kanduri Aja Bseutiri|kanduri Aja Eseutiri]]
8. **Sha'bān** — Achehnese [[Kanduri Bu|Kanduri Bu]] (also *Cha'ban* or *Saban*)
9. **Ramaḍān** — Achehnese [[Puasa|Puasa]] (the fasting month, also *Kamalang* or *Ramalan*)
10. **Shawwāl** — Achehnese [[Puasa|Urôë Raya]] (the feasting month, also *Chaweuët*)
11. **Dū al-Qa'dah** — Achehnese *Meuapèt* ("pinched, shut in"; also *Doy Ka'idah*)
12. **Dū al-Ḥijjah** — Achehnese **Haji** (also *Doy Hijah*)

## The Month of the Pilgrimage

The Achehnese name *Haji* directly translates the Arabic *Dū al-Ḥijjah*, "the possessor of the pilgrimage," so called because the annual [[Hajj|pilgrimage to Mecca]] is performed in its first ten days. The great feast of the sacrifice, the *'Īd al-aḍḥā* — known in Achehnese as the [[Hari Raya Kurban|feast of the sacrifices]] — falls on the 10th day of this month, and the three following days are the *Tashrīq* days. These dates constitute the *Hajj* proper, the central act of which is the sacrifice commemorating the submission of Abraham.

## Local Observance

In Acheh, the month of the pilgrimage is observed chiefly by those who are themselves making the journey to Mecca, by the family and dependents of intending pilgrims, and by the religious officials who superintend the slaughter of the sacrificial animals on the 10th day. The [[uleebalang]] derives part of his income from the fees paid for the certificates required for the issue of passports and those for the *haj*, so that the approach of the month of the pilgrimage brings with it a small annual harvest to the territorial chiefs. The non-pilgrim Achehnese who can afford it performs the sacrifice at home, and the meat is distributed according to the rules of the Mohammedan law — a third to the household, a third to friends, and a third to the poor.

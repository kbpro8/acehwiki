**The 1878 Langkat–Tamiang transfer** was the cession by the [[Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] of all his *landschapshoofd*-rights over the [[Tamiang|Tamiangsche landschapjes]] to the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Netherlands-Indies Government]], formalised in [[1878]] and executed by the placement of a Dutch *bestuursambtenaar* in the territory. The transfer extinguished the centuries-old claims that the [[Langkat|Langkat]] Sultans had asserted over the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] valley. Thirty years later, by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 112|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 112]], the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] region was administratively detached from the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra]] and incorporated into the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] as the *onderafdeeling* [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] — a transfer that moved the northern boundary of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust gewest]] southward to the watershed of the [[Besitang|Besitang River]] and was subsequently confirmed and precisely demarcated by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]].

## Background: the Langkat claims

The Sultans of [[Langkat|Langkat]] had based their claims over [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] on the historical service of one [[Tjoet Gát|Tjoet Gát]], a [[Langkat|Langkat]] subject who, with a band of adventurers, had defeated the [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]] freebooters then infesting the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] valley. In gratitude the two *keudjeuroeëns* of [[Karang|Karang]] and [[Moeda|Moeda]] had ceded the [[Bandahara|Bandahara]] lands on either bank of the [[Tamiang|Tamiang River]] to [[Tjoet Gát|Tjoet Gát]]'s heir. The [[Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]], asserting that [[Tjoet Gát|Tjoet Gát]] had been his subject, used this pretext to proclaim his own *opperhoogheid* over the new *landschapje*, and thereafter to nominate — and depose — its rulers. The prolonged quarrel between [[Langkat|Langkat]] and the *Radja Bandahara*, supported by [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] and [[Langsa|Langsa]], came to no decision; only in [[1861]] did the [[Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]], exploiting the presence of Dutch warships before [[Poeloe Kompai|Poeloe Kompai]], induce the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] princes to offer submission to the Government. In [[1865]] the Government confirmed the *keudjeuroeëns* of [[Karang|Karang]] and [[Moeda|Moeda]], and recognised [[Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema|Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema]] as *Radja Bandahara*.

## The 1878 cession

The decisive step came in [[1878]], when *de overdracht der Langkatsche rechten op de Tamiangsche landschappen aan het gouvernement volgde, dat vervolgens hier een bestuursambtenaar plaatste.* The transfer was, in effect, the colonial Government buying out the [[Langkat|Langkat]] Sultan's claims; in return the Government obtained *vrije hand in het bestuur der Tamiangsche landschapjes* — a free hand in the administration of the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] polities. The choice of an administrative rather than a military instrument reflected the Government's preference for civil rule wherever the local *keudjeuroeëns* could be persuaded to sign the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]].

## Three decades under the Oostkust (1878–1908)

For three decades after the [[1878]] transfer, [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] remained administratively attached to the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]]. When the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]] was erected as a separate *gewest* in [[1878]] (split from [[Residentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden|Residentie Riouw en Onderhoorigheden]]), its northern limit extended to the [[Tamiang|Tamiang River]], encompassing the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] sultanate and surrounding territories. The region was geographically contiguous with [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Aceh]], culturally Malay-Acehnese, and separated from the cultivation centres around [[Medan|Medan]] by over 200 km of difficult terrain. Administration from the capital (first [[Bengkalis|Bengkalis]], from [[1887]] [[Medan|Medan]]) was impractical.

The [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] sultanate had historically maintained ties with both the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Acehnese]] sultanate and the [[Siak|Siak]] sphere. The [[1858]] Treaty of [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] with the Netherlands (not detailed in this volume) had brought it under Dutch suzerainty, but its administrative assignment vacillated.

## The 1908 attachment to Atjeh

The handbook notes that *de onderafdeeling Tamiang behoort pas sedert [[1908]] tot het indirect bestuurd gebied van het Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden.* The transfer was motivated by security rather than economy: *de voortdurende militaire actie, waartoe ons de aanwezigheid van vele uit Atjeh verdreven kwaadwilligen en avonturiers in deze grootendeels uit wildernissen bestaande landschapjes dwong, maakte eenheid van leiding en daarom indeeling bij het gouvernement Atjeh noodzakelijk.* Economically, the handbook concedes, *deze streek economisch—vooral in de laatste jaren door de sterke uitbreiding der Europeesche cultures—nauw verbonden is met de Oostkust van Sumatra.*

[[Staatsblad 1908 No. 112|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 112]] formally excised [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] from the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust gewest]] and attached it to [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]]. The new northern boundary of the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] was fixed at the watershed between the [[Besitang|Besitang]] and [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] rivers, running to the [[Wilhelmina-gebergte|Wilhelmina-keten]] (Gunung Bandahara). This boundary was subsequently confirmed and precisely demarcated by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]] — the only [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] boundary to receive such treatment.

## Consequences

- **Administrative**: [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] became an *onderafdeeling* of the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh gouvernement]], governed from [[Koeala Simpang|Koeala Simpang]] (later [[Langsa|Langsa]]).
- **Transport**: The [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] terminus at [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] became the *de facto* northern rail gateway for the region; the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli-tram]]'s northern terminus remained at [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]], 30+ km to the south.
- **Postal/Telegraph**: The missing road link [[Pangkalan Brandan|Pangkalan Brandan]]–[[Besitang|Besitang]] became the sole gap in overland communication between the two *gouvernementen*.
- **Economic**: [[Tamiang|Tamiang]]'s produce (pepper, rubber, later oil) flowed north to [[Aroebai|Acehnese]] ports rather than south to [[Belawan|Belawan]]. The transfer coincided with the extension of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] into the [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] region and the development of [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] on the [[Aroebai|Aroebaai]] as an oil port serving [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]]'s petroleum fields ([[Langsa|Langsar]], [[Rantau Pandjang|Rantau Pandjang]]). The [[Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij|Koninklijke Petroleum Maatschappij]] built tank installations and jetties at [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]], making it the logical railhead for the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]].
- **Boundary**: The [[Besitang|Besitang]] watershed remained the customs and police frontier; the [[Aroebai|Aroebaai]] harbor plans were scaled back after [[Belawan|Belawan]]'s improvement proved viable.

## Significance

The [[1878]] transfer marks the moment at which the easternmost valleys of the future [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] ceased to be a dependency of a Sumatran Malay sultanate and became an integral part of the colonial state. The [[1908]] attachment to [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] — formalised by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 112|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 112]] and demarcated by [[Staatsblad 1908 No. 604|Staatsblad [[1908]] No. 604]] — marks the convergence of three strands: the long-running [[Langkat|Langkat]]–[[Bandahara|Bandahara]] feud, the consolidation of Dutch authority on the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]], and the spill-over of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Atjeh-krijg]]. The combined [[1878]]–[[1908]] sequence thus closes the long process of administrative detachment of [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] from the [[Gouvernement Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust]] and its incorporation into [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh]] — the precondition for the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tram]] extension to [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] and for the [[Aroe-baai-plannen|Aroe-baai-plannen]] of [[1913]].

**The 1878 Langkat–Tamiang transfer** was the cession by the [[Sultan of Langkat|Sultan of Langkat]] of all his *landschapshoofd*-rights over the [[Tamiang|Tamiangsche landschapjes]] to the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Netherlands-Indies Government]], formalised in [[1878]] and executed by the placement of a Dutch *bestuursambtenaar* in the territory. The transfer extinguished the centuries-old claims that the Langkat Sultans had asserted over the Tamiang valley and cleared the way for direct colonial administration of a region that would, thirty years later, be formally attached to the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

## Background: the Langkat claims

The Sultans of [[Langkat|Langkat]] had based their claims over Tamiang on the historical service of one [[Tjoet Gát|Tjoet Gát]], a Langkat subject who, with a band of adventurers, had defeated the [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]] freebooters then infesting the Tamiang valley. In gratitude the two *keudjeuroeëns* of [[Karang|Karang]] and [[Moeda|Moeda]] had ceded the [[Bandahara|Bandahara]] lands on either bank of the [[Tamiang River|Tamiang River]] to Tjoet Gát's heir. The Sultan of Langkat, asserting that Tjoet Gát had been his subject, used this pretext to proclaim his own *opperhoogheid* over the new *landschapje*, and thereafter to nominate — and depose — its rulers. The prolonged quarrel between Langkat and the *Radja Bandahara*, supported by Atjeh and [[Langsa|Langsa]], came to no decision; only in [[1861]] did the Sultan of Langkat, exploiting the presence of Dutch warships before [[Poeloe Kompai|Poeloe Kompai]], induce the Tamiang princes to offer submission to the Government. In [[1865]] the Government confirmed the *keudjeuroeëns* of Karang and Moeda, and recognised [[Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema|Soetan Moeda Indra Kesoema]] as *Radja Bandahara*.

## The 1878 cession

The decisive step came in [[1878]], when "de overdracht der Langkatsche rechten op de Tamiangsche landschappen aan het gouvernement volgde, dat vervolgens hier een bestuursambtenaar plaatste." The transfer was, in effect, the colonial Government buying out the Langkat Sultan's claims; in return the Government obtained "vrije hand in het bestuur der Tamiangsche landschapjes" — a free hand in the administration of the Tamiang polities. The choice of an administrative rather than a military instrument reflected the Government's preference for civil rule wherever the local *keudjeuroeëns* could be persuaded to sign the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]].

## Incorporation into Atjeh, 1908

For three decades after the transfer, Tamiang remained administratively attached to the [[Oostkust van Sumatra|Oostkust van Sumatra]]. The handbook notes that "de onderafdeeling Tamiang behoort pas sedert [[1908]] tot het indirect bestuurd gebied van het Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden." The transfer was motivated by security rather than economy: "de voortdurende militaire actie, waartoe ons de aanwezigheid van vele uit Atjeh verdreven kwaadwilligen en avonturiers in deze grootendeels uit wildernissen bestaande landschapjes dwong, maakte eenheid van leiding en daarom indeeling bij het gouvernement Atjeh noodzakelijk." Economically, the handbook concedes, "deze streek economisch—vooral in de laatste jaren door de sterke uitbreiding der Europeesche cultures—nauw verbonden is met de Oostkust van Sumatra."

## Significance

The [[1878]] transfer marks the moment at which the easternmost valleys of the future [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] ceased to be a dependency of a Sumatran Malay sultanate and became an integral part of the colonial state. It also marks the convergence of three strands — the long-running Langkat–Bandahara feud, the consolidation of Dutch authority on the Oostkust, and the spill-over of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] — into a single administrative problem that the [[1908|1908]] reattachment to Atjeh was designed to solve.

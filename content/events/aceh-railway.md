**The Aceh Railway** was a railway network in the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Government of Atjeh and Dependencies]], forming part of the infrastructure developed after the [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]].

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — Railways in Atjeh

The atlas, in its chapter on transport, notes: "In Atjeh en Deli (Sumatra's Oostkust) treft men een belangrijk spoorwegnet aan, dat naar gelang van de behoefte wordt uitgebreid." (In Atjeh and Deli [Sumatra's East Coast] one encounters an important railway network, which is being extended in accordance with needs.)

## Significance

The existence of a significant and expanding railway network in Atjeh by [[1926]] is a concrete indicator of the transformation brought about by the pacification. Where the [[Aceh War]] had raged for decades, railways now facilitated the movement of troops, administrators, goods, and commercial products. The atlas groups the Atjeh and Deli rail networks together as the two principal rail systems on Sumatra, suggesting that both were of comparable importance to the colonial economy. The Deli railway served the plantation economy of the east coast, while the Atjeh railway served both military-security purposes and the economic integration of the territory.

The railways of the Buitengewesten, including that of Atjeh, were contrasted in the atlas with the far more developed rail network of Java, where the State Railways (*Staatspoorwegen*) and the Nederlandsch-Indische Spoorweg Maatschappij operated extensive systems. Nonetheless, the continued expansion of the Atjeh network "naar gelang van de behoefte" indicated an ongoing commitment to infrastructure development.

**The Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)** — the chastisement, punitive expedition of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] regions — was the major two-day three-colonne operation commanded by [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. J. W. Stemfoort]] against the *bendehoofd*s [[T. Brahim Montassik]], [[Pang Arab]], [[Panglima Nja Makam]], [[Pang Sech]], [[Habib Samalanga]], and the *Teungkoe*s [[Teungkoe Soepi|Soepi]], [[Teungkoe Loeboek|Loeboek]], and [[Teungkoe Lam-Oe|Lam-Oe]], whose chiefs and population had shown hostile intent by fortifying positions and stationing band leaders. Following the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]], the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] were the next targets. The **1e Colonne** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] comprised the *maréchaussée* and the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e]] and [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljons]], the [[4de Bergbatterij]], and Genie; the **2e Colonne** under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] comprised the [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e]] and [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljons]], the [[2e Bergbatterij]], and Genie; the **Reserve-Colonne** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Col. Soeters]] comprised the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e]] and [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljons]] and a mixed battalion under [[Majoor K. W. Steinmetz|Maj. K. W. Steinmetz]], the [[1te Bergbatterij]], and Genie. On [[16 June]] [[1896]] the **Panteh Karang redoute** was stormed by Ambonese under Dijserinck and Lamster; on [[17 June]] [[1896]] the **Kroeng-Kali benteng** was stormed by compagnie Linck under fire from a *lilla*, with 2nd Lt. Heyligers first inside. The Pretender-Sultan fled from [[Panteh-Karang]]; [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] retreated to [[Lam-Tobah]].

## Background

By mid-[[June]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]], the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] was complete, the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|operations in the IV and VI Moekims]] had cleared the northern coastal stretch, and the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] had reduced the principal Achehnese redoubt in the western *sagi*. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], now resolved to chastise the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] — the regions whose chiefs and population had shown hostile intent by fortifying positions and by stationing band leaders in their territory. The principal Achehnese commanders in the area were: [[T. Brahim Montassik]] — the *bendehoofd* of Montassik, whose *benden* had suffered 5 killed and 12 wounded at the [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik]] of [[2 May]] [[1896]]; [[Pang Arab]]; [[Panglima Nja Makam]] — the *bendehoofd* of [[Lamnga]]; [[Pang Sech]]; [[Habib Samalanga]] — a descendant of the Prophet who had gathered 50–60 men at [[Lamsoet]]; and the *Teungkoe*s [[Teungkoe Soepi|Soepi]], [[Teungkoe Loeboek|Loeboek]], and [[Teungkoe Lam-Oe|Lam-Oe]]. The presence of the Pretender-Sultan at [[Panteh-Karang]] added a political dimension to the operation: his capture or expulsion would be a major propaganda coup.

## The Composition of the Three Colonne

The expedition was commanded by [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. J. W. Stemfoort]] with three columns:

- **1e Colonne** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] — the *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] (4 officers, 226 *minderen*); the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. G. A. Hansen]] (16 officers, 433 *minderen*); the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Maj. D. A. Okhuizen]] (13 officers, 411 *minderen*); the [[4de Bergbatterij]] under [[Kapitein A. Bangert|Kapt. A. Bangert]] (3 officers, 88 *minderen*, 6 *vuurmonden*); Genie.
- **2e Colonne** under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] — the [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor J. R. Jacobs|Maj. J. R. Jacobs]] (15 officers, 409 *minderen*); the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. F. T. Veeren|Lt.-Col. J. F. T. Veeren]] (15 officers, 1 adjutant-under-officer acting as officer, 458 *minderen*); the [[2e Bergbatterij]] under [[1e Luit. P. A. Schrassert Bert|1e Luit. P. A. Schrassert Bert]] (2 officers, 1 acting, 63 *minderen*, 4 *vuurmonden*); Genie.
- **Reserve-Colonne** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Col. G. F. Soeters]] — the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] under [[Kapitein J. A. W. Weustmann|Kapt. J. A. W. Weustmann]] (later [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Col. Soeters]] himself) (13 officers, 512 *minderen*); the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor F. C. Thomson|Maj. F. C. Thomson]] (15 officers, 516 *minderen*); a mixed battalion under [[Majoor K. W. Steinmetz|Maj. K. W. Steinmetz]] (comprising 1 company of the [[15de Bataljon Infanterie|15de Bataljon]], 1 company of the [[1e Garnisoens-Bataljon|1e Garnisoens-Bataljon]], and 1 company of the [[2de Garnisoens-Bataljon|2de Garnisoens-Bataljon]]); the [[1te Bergbatterij]] under [[Kapitein Nauta|Kapt. Nauta]] (3 officers, 60 *minderen*, 4 *vuurmonden*); Genie.

## First Day — 16 June 1896

The advance began at 4 a.m. on [[16 June]] [[1896]] from [[Lam-Permai]]. The 1e Colonne's *maréchaussée* occupied *versterkingen* north of [[Adjé]]; the cavalry under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|Ritmeester de Lannoy]] scouted to [[Tjot Saloeran]]. The 2e Colonne struck [[Tjot Maraja]], [[Balik Angin]], and the blokhuis [[Toei Selimbing]] (stormed by compagnie Scheuer). Heavy fighting developed at [[Aloer Lintang]], at [[Lam-Nga]] (where compagnie Jansen attacked the *benteng*), at [[Lam-Pasei]] (where the *maréchaussée* arrived at 2:30 p.m.), and at [[Lam-Bada]] (occupied at 3:10 p.m.).

The hardest fighting of the day came at the **Panteh Karang redoute** — a square earthen fort (*redoute*) that was stormed by the Ambonese *maréchaussée* under Dijserinck and Lamster. The storming of the Panteh Karang redoute was a sharp engagement, with the Ambonese closing under fire and breaking into the work. The Pretender-Sultan — who had been in residence at Panteh Karang — fled at the approach of the Dutch, abandoning his court and his mosque. The flight of the Pretender-Sultan was the principal political outcome of the day: his presence at Panteh Karang had given the Achehnese resistance a quasi-legitimate political figurehead, and his expulsion deprived them of that asset.

The 1e Colonne returned to bivak at [[Kroeng-Gloempang]] by evening. The losses of the day: 3 dead, 19 wounded (including one officer). Ammunition expended: 44 *Gr.*, 29 *G.K.T.*, 19,346 Beaumont cartridges, 5,926 repeating-rifle cartridges.

## Second Day — 17 June 1896

On [[17 June]] [[1896]] the operation was renewed with reinforcements — the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. C. J. Laceulle]] (13 officers, 327 *minderen*, 3 companies) and a section Stufken. The 1e Colonne marched via [[Tjot Soegoet]] to [[Lam-Soet]], burning *versterkingen* in the line [[boon-Adjé]]–[[Lam-Soet]]. The 2e Colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] advanced via [[Tjot-Rang]], [[Lam-Goet]] (occupied without resistance by compagnie Linck), [[Tjot Preh]] (burned), [[Masdjid Lepong]], to [[Kroeng-Kali]].

The **Kroeng-Kali benteng** — a square redoubt, 15 m on a side, with walls 1.5 m high, defended by a a small swivel gun — was stormed by compagnie Linck under fire from the *lilla*. 2nd Lt. [[Heyligers]] was the first inside, accompanied by hoornblazer van Spanje and fusilier Veeges (No. 31895). 1st Lt. [[P. van der Molen]] and 2 European fusiliers were wounded. The *benteng* was destroyed. Heavy fighting continued around [[Kroeng-Kali]] for the rest of the day. The 2e Colonne returned to [[Lam-Njong]] and [[Koeta-Radja]] by extra train. The 2e Colonne losses: 1 native fusilier dead; 1 officer, 6 European, and 1 native fusilier wounded (the latter died of wounds); 2 *kanonniers* wounded. Ammunition: 41 *Gr.*, 31 *G.K.T.*, 700 repeater + 10,509 Beaumont cartridges (10,305 by the 5e Bataljon alone).

[[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] — the *bendehoofd* of [[Batoe]] whose *benting* had been the target of the [[Night Surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of 28–29 April 1896]] — and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] retreated to [[Lam-Tobah]].

## Significance

The Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik of [[16 June]]–[[17 June]] [[1896]] was the largest single operation of the June campaign and the climactic *tuchtiging* of the spring [[1896]] counter-offensive. The principal outcomes were four. First, the *benteng*s and *versterkingen* of the [[V Moekims Montassik]] and the [[XIII Moekims Toengkoeb]] were destroyed, degrading the Achehnese defensive infrastructure in the south-western mukims. Second, the Pretender-Sultan was expelled from [[Panteh-Karang]] — a political blow that deprived the resistance of its quasi-legitimate figurehead. Third, the Kroeng-Kali *benteng* — a major Achehnese work that had been the target of the [[Recapture of bentengs Glé Broë and Koeta T. Rajoet (4 August 1895)|recapture of 4 August 1895]] and that would be the target of the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing of 22–23 June 1896]] — was at last stormed and destroyed. Fourth, [[T. Tjoet Toengkoeb]] and [[Panglima Nja Makam]] were driven from the field, retreating to [[Lam-Tobah]]. The operation was, however, costly: 3 dead and 19 wounded on [[16 June]] [[1896]] alone, plus the losses of [[17 June]] [[1896]] — including a native fusilier who died of wounds. The *tuchtiging* of the V Moekims Montassik was, in this sense, both the high point of the June campaign and the prelude to its close: within ten days [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] would [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|depart]] Atjeh, the [[Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)|farewell parade]] of [[27 June]] [[1896]] would be held, and the first semester of [[1896]] would be brought to its close.

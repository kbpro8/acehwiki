**World War II in the Dutch East Indies** was the theatre of the global conflict that engulfed the [[Netherlands East Indies]] from [[1941]] to [[1945]], culminating in the Japanese occupation of the archipelago, including the province of [[Aceh]]. The war began with Japanese attacks on Dutch colonial outposts in early [[1942]], leading to the surrender of Dutch forces and the establishment of a Japanese military administration that lasted until the Allied liberation in [[1945]].

## Japanese invasion
Following the attack on Pearl Harbor, Japanese forces launched a rapid campaign across the East Indies. By March [[1942]] the Dutch colonial government had capitulated, and Japanese troops occupied key ports and airfields throughout the region, including those in Aceh.

## Impact on Aceh
Aceh’s strategic location on the northern tip of Sumatra made it a target for Japanese control of sea lanes. The Japanese administration imposed forced labor, requisitioned supplies, and introduced new political structures that altered Aceh’s social fabric.

## Allied response
Allied forces, chiefly the United States and Britain, conducted limited amphibious operations in the Dutch East Indies. The Dutch‑American cooperation later inspired the formation of a modern [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]] amphibious capability, as discussed in contemporary Dutch military literature (see page 415 of the source).

## Aftermath
The Japanese surrender in August [[1945]] led to a power vacuum, sparking the Indonesian National Revolution. Aceh’s experience during the war influenced its subsequent political alignment within the newly independent Republic of Indonesia.

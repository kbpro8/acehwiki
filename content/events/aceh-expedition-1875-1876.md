**Aceh Expedition (1875–1876)** was a Dutch military campaign undertaken by the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] and supported by the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps]] and the [[Royal Netherlands Navy] ] to subdue the resistant Acehnese forces in the northern part of [[Sumatra]].

## Chronology

The expedition is chronicled in the work of [[M. P. Meyer]], *Atjeh van 26 Dee. [[1875]] tot 4 Sept. [[1876]]*, which records operations from [[26 December]] [[1875]] through [[4 September]] [[1876]].

## Conduct of the Campaign

Key actions included:

* Amphibious landings at coastal forts near [[Aceh]].
* Riverine assaults up the Aceh River, coordinated with marine detachments.
* Establishment of supply bases at [[Batavia]] before the northward advance.

## Significance

Meyer argues that the expedition marked a decisive shift in the [[Aceh War]], demonstrating the effectiveness of combined naval‑marine‑army tactics. The success of the campaign paved the way for the eventual Dutch control of the Aceh Sultanate in the early 20th century.

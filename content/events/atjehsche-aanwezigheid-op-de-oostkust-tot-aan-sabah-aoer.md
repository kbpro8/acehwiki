**The Atjehsche aanwezigheid op de Oostkust tot aan Sabah Aoer** — the Atjehnese presence on the East Coast as far south as the [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] station at the mouth of the [[Siak|Siak River]] — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the southern limit of the formal Atjehnese presence on the East Coast after the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|destruction of the kingdom of Gasip]]. The [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] station — governed by a *sjahbandar* on behalf of the [[Sultan of Djohore|Sultan of Djohore]] after the Gasip destruction — was, in the Bureau's framing, the geographical hinge between the Atjehnese sphere to the north and the Djohorese sphere to the south.

## Background: the destruction of Gasip and the Djohorese succession

The Bureau's account of the [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] station opens with the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|Atjehnese destruction of the kingdom of Gasip]] in the early sixteenth century. The destruction had left the lower [[Siak|Siak River]] open to a new contender, and the [[Sultan of Djohore|Sultan of Djohore]] had seized the opportunity to establish himself at the river's mouth. "Hiervan maakte de Sultan van Djohore gebruik om zich aan de monding der Siak-rivier te vestigen, terwijl de sage ervan spreekt, dat in het binnenland eene soort federatie van onafhankelijke kleine landschapjes ontstond, zooals de Tapoeng-streken (boven-Siak) thans nog vormen" — "Of this the Sultan of Djohore made use to establish himself at the mouth of the Siak river, while the saga speaks of it that in the interior a kind of federation of independent small *landschapjes* arose, such as the Tapoeng-streken (upper-Siak) still form today."

## The Sabah Aoer station and the sjahbandar

The Djohorese settlement at the [[Siak|Siak River]] mouth was, the Bureau records, governed by a *sjahbandar* — a harbour-master — resident at [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]]. "Het bestuur over deze Djohore'sche nederzetting (welke eene belangrijke immigratie uit dat rijk tengevolge had) werd in naam van den Sultan uitgeoefend door een sjahbandar, die gevestigd was te [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]], en wiens invloed in het binnenland ongeveer tot de S. Mandau reikte" — "The administration over this Djohorese settlement (which had, as a consequence, an important immigration from that realm) was exercised, in the name of the Sultan, by a sjahbandar, who was established at Sabah Aoer, and whose influence in the interior reached approximately as far as the [river] Mandau."

The *sjahbandar*'s influence reached, in other words, only as far inland as the [[Mandau|Mandau River]] — a tributary of the [[Siak|Siak River]] some distance inland of the coast. The Djohorese presence at [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] was thus a coastal, river-mouth presence rather than an inland sovereignty, and its purpose was the control of the river-mouth trade — the great trade route between [[Pagarroejoeng]] (the legendary Minangkabau kingdom in central [[Sumatra|Sumatra]]) and [[Malacca|Malakka]] — rather than the political subjection of the interior.

## The strategic purpose

The [[Sultan of Djohore|Sultan of Djohore]]'s purpose in establishing the [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] station was, the Bureau records, twofold. First, the control of the river-mouth trade: the [[Siak|Siak River]] was the principal trade route between [[Pagarroejoeng]] and [[Malacca|Malakka]], and "in die oude tijden een veel belangrijker handelsverkeer kende dan tegenwoordig" — "in those old times knew a much more important trade traffic than today" — so that the Sultan of Djohore could "belangrijke heffingen eischen aan dien riviermond" — "demand important levies at that river-mouth." Second, the protection of Djohore against its Minangkabau enemies: the station at [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] served "zich tevens beschermen tegen een aanval zijner machtige Minangkabausche vijanden" — "at the same time to protect itself against an attack by its powerful Minangkabau enemies."

## The Djohorese succession and the Atjehsche presence

The [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] station was, in the Bureau's framing, the geographical hinge between the Atjehnese sphere to the north (which extended, after the [[atjehsche-invasie-in-tamiang-en-noordelijk-langkat-(c-1589)|1589 invasion of Tamiang and Northern Langkat]], as far south as [[Tamiang|Tamiang]]) and the Djohorese sphere to the south (which extended, after the destruction of [[rijk Gasip|Gasip]], as far north as [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]]). The two spheres were complementary rather than competitive: the Atjehnese presence was an institutional planting of *vertegenwoordigers* of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] in the most northerly districts, while the Djohorese presence was a demographic planting of Malay settlers at the river mouths further south. The [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] station was, in this arrangement, the southern limit of the Atjehnese sphere and the northern limit of the Djohorese sphere — the geographical hinge between the two.

## The Radja Ketjil revolution

The Djohorese settlement at [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] was, the Bureau records, overthrown in the early eighteenth century by [[Radja Ketjil|Radja Ketjil]]. "In het begin der 18de eeuw werd Djohore uit Siak verjaagd door den bekenden Radja ketjil, die zich uitgaf voor een onechten zoon van een der vroegere Djohore'sche sultans" — "In the beginning of the 18th century Djohore was driven out of Siak by the well-known Radja Ketjil, who gave himself out as an illegitimate son of one of the former Djohorese sultans." [[Radja Ketjil|Radja Ketjil]]'s revolution was, in the Bureau's framing, the closing act of the long Djohorese-Atjehnese arrangement at the [[Siak|Siak River]] mouth, and the founding act of the independent [[Siak|Sultanate of Siak]].

## Significance

The [[Sabah Aoer|Sabah Aoer]] station is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the geographical hinge between the Atjehnese and Djohorese spheres of the late-sixteenth- and early-seventeenth-century East Coast. Its establishment by the [[Sultan of Djohore|Sultan of Djohore]] — in the immediate aftermath of the [[atjehsche-verovering-van-gasip-(begin-16e-eeuw)|Atjehnese destruction of Gasip]] — defines the southern limit of the Atjehnese sphere; its overthrow by [[Radja Ketjil|Radja Ketjil]] in the early eighteenth century closes the long Djohorese-Atjehnese arrangement and inaugurates the independent [[Siak|Sultanate of Siak]]. The station is thus the geographical complement of the Atjehnese *vertegenwoordiger* in [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] and northern [[Langkat|Langkat]]: where the latter marks the southern limit of the Atjehnese sphere, the former marks the northern limit of the Djohorese sphere.

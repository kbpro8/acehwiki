**Pati Oenoes's attack on Malacca in January [[1513]]** was the great Javanese naval expedition, dispatched from [[Japara|Japara]] by the lord [[Pati Oenoes]] — whom [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] identifies with [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]]/[[Soenan Kalidjaga]] — against Portuguese [[Malacca]], in the first Muslim attempt to retake the city from the Portuguese. The attack failed; Pati Oenoes saved himself and his fighting-junk by a hurried return to [[Japara|Japara]], where — as a memorial of his bold deed — he had the junk placed under a shed on the beach, "as large as a Portuguese ship of 500 tons, caulked with seven layers of a mixture of lime and oil". The fame of the deed was so great, [[Castanheda|Castanheda]] noted, that the Javanese made him king of [[Demak|Demak]] on account of it.

## Background

[[Tome Pires|Tome Pires]]'s *Suma Oriental* (~[[1515]]) records that Pati Oenoes's father had been a working man in the islands of Lawé ([[Borneo|Borneo]]) who had come to [[Malacca|Malacca]] with little means, married there, and had a son — Pati Oenoes's father — who traded to Java and cunningly killed the *pate* of [[Japara|Japara]] (then weak, with 90 or 100 inhabitants), seized [[Japara|Japara]] and the island of Tidunan, brought [[Bengkalis|Bengka-lis]] ("the vanished Bangka") and [[Tandjoengpoera|Tanjungpura]] under his jurisdiction, and made his country great. Pati Oenoes, his son, "wanted to put together what remained of his father's wealth and what wealth [[Pate Rodim|Pate Rodim]] [of Demak] had, and decided to take Malacca from the then king of Malacca, because he had taken offence because they had not done honour in Malacca to the captain of a junk of his as had hoped".

## The attack

In [[January]] [[1513]] Pati Oenoes led a great Javanese fleet against Portuguese [[Malacca]]. The attack failed; Pati Oenoes saved himself and his fighting-junk by a hurried return to [[Japara|Japara]]. There, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] records, citing [[Castanheda|Castanheda]], "as a memorial of his bold deed he had this junk placed under a shed on the beach", a vessel "as large as a Portuguese ship of 500 tons, caulked with seven layers of a mixture of lime and oil — probably the tough substance used for caulking junks, which, plastered on the seams from outside, becomes as hard as glass". The fame of the deed was, [[Castanheda|Castanheda]] noted, so great "that the Javanese even now [sc. before 1552] speak of this deed, and on account of it made him king of a city called [[Demak|Demak]]".

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Pati Oenoes's [[1513]] attack on [[Malacca|Malacca]] is the first Muslim attempt to retake the city from the Portuguese — a strategic echo of the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate's own [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|war on Aroe]] of [[1539]], and a token of the same Islamic-commercial coalition that the [[Aceh Sultanate|Atjehnese sultanate]] would lead in the following decades. Pati Oenoes's later marriage of his sister to the [[Faletahan|Paseier Faletahan]] (~[[1523]]–[[1524]]) — and his identification, by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]], with [[Soenan Kalidjaga|Raden Sahid]] of the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] — also mark him as the Javanese link in the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]–[[Acheh|Atjeh]]–Java triangle of the early sixteenth century.

**The gouvernements rubberonderneming Langsa (1907)** — *the government rubber-estate at Langsa, opened in 1907* — was the rubber-plantation opened by the Indies government in [[1907]] on the *onderafdeeling* [[Langsa|Langsa]] in the *afdeeling* [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust van Atjeh]] to show how *Hevea* would do on the low land. The estate is the type-case in Broersma's *[[Verkenningen in Atjeh (1923)|Verkenningen in Atjeh]]* of the failure of government enterprise in the planting-sector of Atjeh.

## Foundation
The estate was opened in [[1907]] by the Indies government on peat-ground. It was to have a planting of 1.000 *bouw*; by [[1915]] it had reached barely half that extent. It then worked with 430 coolies, mostly contract labourers, and had a yield of 123.000 pounds of rubber at a cost-price of 66 cents. One might sing a panegyric to it, as in government publications has been done, but it is, in Broersma's view, a pity for the public purse that no follow-up was given to the plan, ventilated years ago, of selling it. Had that been done, the opening would have answered to its purpose of showing a possibility.

## The Peat-Ground Failure
As it is, other entrepreneurs came afterwards, not knowing what disappointments awaited them. The government rubber-estate was opened in [[1907]] on peat-ground, and now one can see how the soil sinks under the trees, the roots come up, and a wholly crooked growth is the result. The planting looks shabby. In other *afdeelingen* too the soil proves unfavourable, but in the younger plantings it is better. Curiously enough, the yield of the whole estate now gives reason for satisfaction. It now comprises 900 *bouw* and works with 250 labourers (the tappers included). The factory has only four rollers. Thanks to good management, it is not the least among its sisters in this region.

## Significance
The gouvernement rubberonderneming at Langsa is the type-case of the failure of government enterprise in the planting-sector of Atjeh, and of the misplaced optimism about the suitability of the peat-soils of the eastern coast for Hevea-cultivation. Broersma's judgement — *'Het voorbeeld van het gouvernement in 1907 had wel achterwege mogen blijven. Maar in de jaren, dat alle grond goed heette, zouden de waaghalzen toch wel zijn gekomen'* — *The government's example in 1907 might well have been omitted. But in the years when all ground was called good, the daredevils would have come anyway* — is one of the strongest passages of colonial self-criticism in the *Verkenningen*.

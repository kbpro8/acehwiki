The **Friday (Jumu'ah) prohibitions** are a body of Achehnese *pantang* rules — customary taboos — that forbid certain kinds of work on Friday, the Mohammedan day of public worship. C. Snouck Hurgronje treats them in *The Achehnese* chiefly in connection with the rules of the fishing community, but they extend to agricultural labour as well and reflect the universal Achehnese respect for the sanctity of the [[Friday Service (Jumu'ah)|day of assembly]].

## Pukat-Fishing and Ploughing

The best-known Friday prohibition in Acheh relates to fishing with the *pukat* or seine-net. As Snouck Hurgronje describes, *pukat*-fishing in the open sea — called *mupayang* — is only carried on for a small part of the year, requires the cooperation of two sampans, and presupposes great skill on the part of the *pawang* or master-fisherman. Among the rules universally observed is that which forbids fishing with the *pukat* on a Friday under any pretext. Other methods of catching fish may be practised with impunity on this day, but *pukat*-fishing is prohibited as strictly as ploughing. Thus, on Fridays, the *pawang* and his crew may be seen lounging about in their best clothes, having neither net nor plough to attend to.

## Theoretical Foundation

The Friday prohibitions do not rest on any formal provision of the Mohammedan law. The law requires the performance of the [[Friday Service (Jumu'ah)|Friday service]] in the mosque at noon, but does not forbid secular labour on that day beyond what is necessary to enable the worshipper to attend. The Achehnese prohibition of ploughing and of *pukat*-fishing is an *adat* extension of the legal rule, the work being regarded as too heavy or too absorbing to be combined with the proper observance of the day. In the case of the *pukat*-fishing, the prohibition is also bound up with the elaborate system of *ilmu* (magical lore) that governs the seine-net fishery; Friday is the day on which the *pawang* and his crew rest from their struggle with the spirits of the sea.

## Relation to the Fishing Pantangs

The Friday prohibition is one of a large class of *pantang* rules observed by Achehnese seafaring men. Among the fishermen on the north coast of [[Java]] whole lists of words can be collected which are prohibited at sea and have to be replaced by others, and the same is the case to some extent among the seafaring folk of Acheh. Those at sea must not, for example, call a mountain by its proper name *gunong*, lest waves as high as mountains should overwhelm their vessel; the euphemism employed is *tanoh manyang* ("high ground"). The elephant, *gajah*, must be called by his nickname *si meurah*. If the fisherman wishes to say that something is "ready," he must not use the ordinary word *beukeunong*, because this has also the meaning of "free" or "loose," and its use might give the imprisoned fish a chance of escaping; the less dangerous synonym *teungka* is employed. To this class of universal lore must be added the special *pukat* mysteries, in which the Friday prohibition holds a central place.

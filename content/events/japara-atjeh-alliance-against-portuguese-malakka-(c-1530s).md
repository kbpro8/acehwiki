The **Japara-Atjeh alliance against Portuguese Malakka of the 1530s** was the political-military alliance between the Vorstin (Queen) of [[Japara]] — a north-coast Javanese polity — and the Sultan of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] against the Portuguese possession of [[Malakka]]. The alliance is one of the earliest recorded instances of cross-archipelagic cooperation against the Portuguese presence in the Strait, and is mentioned in [[E. C. Godee Molsbergen|Godee Molsbergen]]'s account of Mataram's relations with the Hollanders in chapter LXXII of *Tijdens de O.I. Compagnie*.

## Background: Japara and Portuguese Malakka

In the early sixteenth century, following the [[1511]] Portuguese capture of [[Malakka]] by [[Afonso de Albuquerque|Afonso de Albuquerque]], the Javanese north-coast polities found their commercial and political interests damaged by the new Portuguese presence in the Strait. The Javanese, who had been major traders in Malakka before [[1511]], were now excluded or restricted. Japara — a north-coast Javanese polity — was among the polities most affected.

## The 1513 Japara attack on Malakka

The Japara response came quickly. In [[1513]] a Vorst (Prince) of Japara named Oenoes "deed een aanval op het Portugeesch Malakka" — made an attack on Portuguese Malakka. The attack produced no result — "de resultaten waren nihil" (the results were nil) — but it was the first Javanese military response to the Portuguese presence in the Strait.

## The alliance of the 1530s

About twenty years after Oenoes's failed attack, the Vorstin (Queen) of Japara "verbond zich met Atjeh tegen deze vestiging" — allied with Atjeh against this (Portuguese) establishment. The Atjeh-Japara alliance of the 1530s was thus a coordinated attempt by a north-coast Javanese polity and the sultanate of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] to undo the Portuguese capture of Malakka.

The alliance is significant for several reasons:

1. It shows the early reach of Atjeh's diplomatic and military engagement with the Javanese north-coast polities — an engagement that would later be supplemented by the [[Aceh Embassy to Prince Maurits at Grave (1602)|1602 embassy to Prince Maurits]] and the [[Pieter Soury's mission to Atjeh (1641)|1641 Soury embassy]].
2. It places Atjeh at the centre of the cross-archipelagic anti-Portuguese coalition that would persist, in various forms, for the next century.
3. It frames the long Atjeh-Portuguese conflict that would culminate in the [[Conquest of Portuguese Malakka (1641)|VOC–Atjeh–Djohor conquest of 1641]].

## The 1574 Javanese fleet

The cross-archipelagic anti-Portuguese effort continued in the following decades. "In het jaar van Leiden's beleg" — In the year of Leiden's siege ([[1574]]) — "stevende een Javaansche vloot naar Malakka" (a Javanese fleet sailed towards Malakka) — but again "de resultaten waren nihil" (the results were nil). The [[1574]] expedition may or may not have been a continuation of the Japara-Atjeh alliance, but its timing — within the broader pattern of Javanese-Atjeh attacks on Portuguese Malakka — places it in the same tradition.

## Significance

For [[Acheh|Atjeh]]'s history, the Japara-Atjeh alliance of the 1530s is one of the earliest documented instances of the sultanate's diplomacy reaching beyond northern Sumatra to the Javanese north-coast polities. The alliance shows Atjeh's strategic understanding that the Portuguese presence in Malakka was a regional, not a merely Atjehnese, problem — and that any successful attack on the city would require cross-archipelagic cooperation. The same strategic understanding would later inform the [[Conquest of Portuguese Malakka (1641)|VOC–Atjeh–Djohor conquest of 1641]], and would underpin the long Atjeh-Portuguese conflict that the [[Great cannon of Atjeh|great cannon of Atjeh]] symbolised.

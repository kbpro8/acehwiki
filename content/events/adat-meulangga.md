The **adat meulangga** (also called **meidangga**) is the Achehnese customary ritual by which the symbolic satisfaction of honour is exacted after an injury or slight offered by one gampong to another. The ritual is the formal — and usually bloodless — substitute for the destruction of houses and courtyards that was the earlier form of redress, and it stands as a notable example of that combination of set speeches, dramatic display, and adat fiction which pervades Achehnese public life.

When a gampong considers itself injured by another, the keuchi' of the offended community lays the complaint before the keuchi' of the offenders. The latter says that he feels bound to acknowledge the fairness of the complaint, but that this just demand can be satisfied without having recourse to deeds of violence. The "father of the offending community" then brings to the other **one or two plantain-stems** from the courtyard and a **glundong-tree** from the fence of the guilty party, saying, "Here is what you ask for." Sometimes, where the injury is a very serious one, he grants them permission to enter upon the courtyard of the offenders and cut down a certain number of trees. Should the atonement offered by the keuchi' not suffice for the satisfaction of honour according to the adat controlling quarrels, long-continued hostilities between two neighbouring gampongs may supervene; but for the most part the mediation effected under the auspices of the two keuchi's is found sufficient.

The day after this symbolical satisfaction the parties meet again to consult as to the guarantees for the establishment of peace. An *idang* of yellow glutinous rice (*bu kunyèt*) and a piece of white cloth must without fail be offered by the offender for the "cooling of the blood" — that is, for the [[Peusijuek (cooling ceremony)|peusijuek]] by which the restored peace is ritually confirmed. In the most serious cases, where the injury has been of a kind that the plantain-stems and the glundong-tree cannot satisfy, a more imposing form of the ritual is employed: the offended keuchi' goes forth at the head of a host of followers to the gampong of the offenders, where he is received with special tokens of humility and prayers for pardon. In addition to the traditional plantain-stalks and glundong-trees which represent the destruction of the plantation of the guilty party, the latter's house must in this case be symbolically burnt: a hut (*jambo*) is constructed of slight materials and set on fire amid the assembled crowd.

More peaceful times and calmer manners have substituted for this destruction the dramatic exhibition just described — a sort of gampong-duel, usually of quite a harmless character. Meidangga may also take place within the gampong, when anyone has injured his fellow-villager; it is then the male relatives of the injured party that proceed to make the demand, and those of the offender who satisfy it, while the gampong authorities merely take care that the settlement is properly carried out. Between communities of different uleebalangs — as when a servant (*rakan*) of uleebalang A has wounded a dependant of uleebalang B — the ritual is adapted to the larger political scale and may be regulated by the panglima sagi. Together with the [[Peusah panchuri|peusah panchuri]] verification of a thief and the [[Sarakata with chab sikureu'eng|sarakata]] of the Sultan, the adat meulangga belongs to the set-speech institutions of Achehnese public life.

The **Siege and relief of Biloel** was the investment of the Dutch post of [[Biloel]] in the [[IX Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] from the afternoon of [[29 March]] [[1896]], the immediate consequence of the defection of [[Teukoe Oemar]], and its relief and abandonment in [[April]] [[1896]].

## The failed muster of 29 March 1896

Great activity had been expected at [[Biloel]], for [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Djohan]] was to assemble there a thousand warriors and a thousand coolies with which to begin the action against [[Lamkrak]]; a shed roofed with sheet iron had been erected as magazine and auxiliary hospital, and the post was to be raised to a permanent fortification. Instead, on the morning of [[29 March]] [[1896]] nobody appeared at the *[[Passer|passer]]* under the great tree — not even the police *oppasser* — and the *[[Blokhuis|blokhuizen]]* held by friendly Achehnese nearby were found abandoned.

## The patrol and the investment

Towards half past four that afternoon [[Kapitein Schadée]] went out with a patrol of sixty men towards [[Tjot Goeë]] to establish the state of affairs. When the point had pushed some three hundred paces ahead, beyond the covered ground surrounding Biloel on the north side, it received heavy fire from every side across the flooded rice-fields. [[Luitenant Zijdveld]], struck by a bullet in the lower jaw, was obliged to fall back, *en van dat oogenblik af bleef Biloel door den vijand omsingeld*.

## The siege

Provisions were sufficient; only the *toespijzen* (side-dishes accompanying rice) to accompany the rice, and bread, were too few. Stockfish and tinned minced meat were in abundance, and a couple of buffaloes and a goat that were shot replaced fresh meat.

## The relief of 9 April 1896

*Eerst den 9den April kwam er ontzet opdagen, dat weder terugtrok op de tijding dat Toekoe Oemar in aantocht was* — only on [[9 April]] [[1896]] did relief appear, which withdrew again on the news that Toekoe Oemar was approaching. Biloel and [[Lam Koenjit]] were subsequently lifted and burnt, the Dutch troops hastening back over the muddy fields on which they had endured almost three years of a temporary station.

**The Sarikat Islam in Atjeh (1918–1922)** was the vigorous post-war life of the [[Sarikat Islam|Sarikat Islam]] political and religious movement in the *afdeeling* [[Noordkust van Atjeh|Noordkust van Atjeh]] and especially in the *onderafdeeling* [[Lho Seumawe|Lho' Seumawé]], in the years after [[1918]]. The movement was significant enough to require the banishment of three chiefs of Lho' Seumawe in [[1922]], an intervention Broersma describes as *'geen dag te vroeg gekomen'* — *not a day too soon.*

## The Movement in the Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh
In the whole *afdeeling* Noordkust van Atjeh with its three *onderafdeelingen* — Lho' Seumawé, [[Bireuen|Bireuën]] and [[Lho Soekon|Lho Soekon]] — the Sarikat Islam showed a vigorous life after [[1918]]. Traders, school-teachers — *'laat ons zeggen de intellectueelen'* — but also several chiefs were members and took part. A chief of [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] had joined by way of a move on the political chessboard on which he was playing against the influential [[Sri Tengkoe Abdoel Hamid Orangkaja Sri Maharadja Mangkoe Boemi|Maharadja of Lho' Seumawé]], who had also become a member of the Sarikat Islam in order to strengthen his chances of retaining a *landschap Plimbang*. Having taken the advice of an *[[Oelama|oelama]]* — a Mohammedan jurist — that joining might be called a good deed, the Samalangan chief formed an *afdeeling S.I.*

## The Direction in Lho' Seumawe
The movement in Lho' Seumawe took the direction of bringing the distinction between believers and unbelievers to the fore, of undermining the Dutch government, and of working towards a union of *oeléebalangs*. There were public meetings, even secret meetings. Three chiefs of Lho' Seumawe were banished in [[1922]] — the *Verbanning van drie hoofden uit Lho' Seumawe (1922)*. Broersma comments: *'Tot bedaring is men gekomen, toen verleden jaar drie hoofden uit Lho' Seumawe zijn verbannen, een ingrijpen dat geen dag te vroeg is gekomen.'*

## The General Pattern
Young chiefs are often too susceptible to new slogans, Broersma writes; religious advisers are always ready to enhance their own importance by adding religious zeal and religious hatred as a factor; and as the movement becomes more general, older chiefs let themselves be drawn into participation. If one lets it come so far in an *afdeeling*, the resistance revives. In Lho' Seumawé and Samalangan, vigilance is highly necessary.

## Significance
The Sarikat Islam episode of [[1918]]–[[1922]] is the principal political crisis of the early civilian period in the *Afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh*, and the banishment of the three chiefs of Lho' Seumawe is the most forceful intervention of the colonial government against an Atjehnese political movement in the post-pacification period. The movement is also the type-case of the alliance between *oeléebalangs* and *oelama* that the colonial government feared most, and of the use of the Sarikat Islam as a vehicle for chiefly rivalries — the Samalangan chief and the Maharadja playing political chess with the movement as a piece.

**Van Bemmelen's *The Geology of Indonesia* (1949)** is the standard two-volume colonial-era survey of the geology of the [[Netherlands East Indies]], written by [[R. W. van Bemmelen|R. W. van Bemmelen]] — former member of the [[Netherlands Indies Geological Survey]] and head of the [[Netherlands Indies Volcanological Survey]] — and published as a Special Edition of the [[Bureau of Mines in Indonesia]] by the Government Printing Office at The Hague, with Martinus Nijhoff as sole agents. The second volume, *Economic Geology*, is the most comprehensive printed survey of the mineral wealth of the East Indies and is the principal source for the colonial mineralogical record of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the rest of northern [[Sumatra]]. The work is treated as a single-event source-document in this wiki because nearly every mineral-locality entry for Atjeh draws, directly or indirectly, on the data Van Bemmelen compiled.

## Publication details

*The Geology of Indonesia* was published in [[1949]] by the Government Printing Office (Algemeene Landsdrukkerij) at The Hague, with Martinus Nijhoff as sole agents. The work was issued as a Special Edition of the [[Bureau of Mines in Indonesia]], under the authority of the *Departement van Verkeer, Energie en Mijnbouw* (Department of Transport, Energy and Mining) of the [[Netherlands East Indies]] — a publication context that places the work firmly within the institutional output of the colonial mining service, despite its post-war publication date. Van Bemmelen's authorship is identified on the title page as "Former Member of the Netherlands Indies Geological Survey, and Head of the Netherlands Indies Volcanological Survey".

The work is divided into two volumes:

- **Vol. I**: the general geology of the East Indies (stratigraphy, tectonics, volcanism, geomorphology).
- **Vol. II**: *Economic Geology* — the mineral resources of the East Indies, organised under four main parts: organic minerals (petroleum, natural gas, coal, asphalt), ores (tin, gold and silver, copper, lead and zinc, manganese, iron, aluminium, nickel, cobalt, antimony, mercury, molybdenite, bismuth), non-metallic industrial minerals (sulphur, phosphate, iodine, limestone, clay, kaolin, quartz-sand, pumice, trass, diamond), and minerals not yet produced at the time of writing.

## Atjeh material in Vol. II

The Atjeh material in Vol. II is scattered through every section, but is densest in the chapters on:

- **Petroleum**: the [[Oil exploration in North Sumatra|North Sumatra oil fields]] — [[Aru Bay|Aru Bay]], [[Telaga Said|Telaga Said]], [[Bulu Telang|Bulu Telang]], the [[East Atjeh oil block|East Atjeh block]], the [[Tamiang oil block|Tamiang block]], the [[Atjeh oil complex]] (Peureula, South Perlak, Pulu Pandjang, [[Pasé oil field|Pasé]]) — with the [[1940]] and [[1941]] production figures in tons of 1000 kg. The chapter also records the deepest exploratory drilling in the East Indies at [[Lho Sukon|Lho Sukon in Atjeh]] (3,450 m).
- **Coal**: the inventory of Atjeh coal localities (numbers 1–20 in the Sumatra list) compiled by [[J. Zwierzycki|Zwierzycki]] in [[1922]] from the older surveys by [[Rinaud|Rinaud]] ([[1885]]) and others, with the analytical data of [[J. H. Hengeveld|Hengeveld]] ([[1918]]/[[1919]]), [[H. Fryling|Fryling]] ([[1921]]) and [[Van Loheezen|Van Loheezen]] ([[1929]]).
- **Gold and silver**: the [[Atjeh gold exploration (1922, 1946)|Atjeh gold explorations]] of [[P. J. J. Jansen|Jansen]], [[B. B. Lindberg|Lindberg]] and [[H. Wolvekamp|Wolvekamp]] ([[1922]]) and of [[C. de Groot|De Groot]] ([[1946]]), with the [[S. Assai]] and [[S. Meranging]] assays and the [[M.A.E.M.]] Geudong Concession operation.
- **Copper**: the Atjeh localities (Pulu Bras, Gle Broë, Aer Talu, Beutong, Aloe Baru, Aer Si Hajo, Muara Sipongi).
- **Lead and zinc**: the [[Krueng Heureueng|Krueng Heureueng]] and [[Krueng Isep|Krueng Isep]] assays.
- **Iron**: the canonical list of Atjeh localities — [[Kr. Geuenteuet|Kr. Geuenteuet]], [[Gle Mon Ampeuet|Gle Mon Ampeuet]], [[Kr. Ligan (Babah Loh)|Kr. Ligan]], [[Kr. Rigaih|Kr. Rigaih]], [[Alu Talu|Alu Talu]], [[Kwala Boe Tjot Plaj|Kwala Boe and Tjot Plaj]], [[A. Pentuwa Gade|A. Pentuwa Gade]] and [[Kuto Buloh|Kuto Buloh]] — with the [[1920]] reserve estimate of 2,600,000 tons of 58–64 % Fe at Kr. Geuenteuet.
- **Mercury**: the native mercury and cinnabar reported by De Groot ([[1946]]) in the gold-bearing alluvial deposits of the [[Atjeh's Westkust|West coast of Atjeh]].

## Place in the colonial geological literature

Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia* is the culminating synthesis of the colonial geological literature on the East Indies. It draws on the printed reports of the [[Netherlands Indies Geological Survey]] (the *Verslagen en Mededeelingen* series, the *Jaarboek van het Mijnwezen*, and the various monographs of individual geologists), on the unpublished "Geol. Survey Report, Arch. Bur. of Mines" material, and on Van Bemmelen's own extensive field-experience as a member of the Geological Survey and head of the Volcanological Survey. The work accordingly consolidates, in a single English-language text, the scattered Dutch reports on the mineral geography of the East Indies — including the Atjeh reports that had previously been available only in Dutch or in unpublished archival form.

The work's publication in [[1949]] — in the difficult immediate post-war period, when the surviving archives of the colonial mining service were being reconstituted after the [[1942]]–[[1945]] Japanese occupation — gives it an additional historical significance as the principal printed survivor of the colonial geological literature. Many of the unpublished reports that Van Bemmelen cites (in the form "Geol. Survey Report, Arch. Bur. of Mines 40/ba") may not have survived the Japanese occupation in their original form, and Van Bemmelen's citations accordingly provide, in some cases, the only surviving record of their content.

## Significance

Van Bemmelen's *Geology of Indonesia* (Vol. II, *Economic Geology*) is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history because it is, for the colonial period, the single most comprehensive printed source on the mineral geography of Atjeh. Almost every entry on this wiki that concerns an Atjeh mineral locality, oil field, coal seam, or mining concession draws, directly or indirectly, on the data Van Bemmelen compiled. The work also preserves — in the form of citations, page-references, and footnotes — the names and dates of a generation of Dutch mining engineers and geologists whose own reports often survive only in the archives of the [[Bureau of Mines in Indonesia|Bureau of Mines]] at Bandung. The work's publication in [[1949]] — after the Japanese occupation and on the eve of Indonesian independence — also gives it an additional historical significance as the principal printed survivor of the colonial geological literature, and as the baseline against which later Indonesian geological work has been measured.

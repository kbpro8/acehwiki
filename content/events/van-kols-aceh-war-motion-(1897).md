**Van Kol's Aceh War Motion** was a parliamentary motion submitted to the [[Tweede Kamer]] (Dutch Lower House) during the [[1897]] budget debates by socialist parliamentarian [[H. van Kol]], calling for an impartial commission to investigate the political, moral, and financial consequences of the [[Aceh War]] (*Atjeh-oorlog*). The motion was co-sponsored by the other two socialist members of the [[Sociaaldemokratische Arbeiderspartij]], [[Troelstra]] and [[Van der Zwaag]].

## Text of the motion

The motion read as follows:

"De Kamer, overwegende dat de oorlog met Atjeh als een nationale ramp moet worden beschouwd; overwegende, dat het wenschelijk is de natie in te lichten omtrent de beteekenis van den oorlog voor Nederland en Nederlandsch-Indië; overwegende dat de herhaalde wisseling onzer gedragslijn in Atjeh, zonder dat in den loop van 24 jaren het einde van den oorlog is bereikt, de vraag doet rijzen, of het volhouden van den oorlog wel wenschelijk en mogelijk is; verzoekt de Regeering eene onpartijdige commissie te benoemen die binnen een jaar rapport zal uitbrengen over de moreele, politieke en financieele gevolgen van den oorlog sedert zijn aanvang, over de vermoedelijke gevolgen der thans gevolgde tactiek en over de mogelijkheid en wenschelijkheid, den oorlog te beëindigen, met behoud onzer volkenrechtelijke stelling op Sumatra."

*(The Chamber, considering that the war with Aceh must be regarded as a national disaster; considering that it is desirable to inform the nation about the significance of the war for the Netherlands and the Dutch East Indies; considering that the repeated changes in our course of action in Aceh, without the end of the war having been reached in the course of 24 years, raises the question of whether continuing the war is desirable and possible; requests the Government to appoint an impartial commission which will report within a year on the moral, political and financial consequences of the war since its commencement, on the probable consequences of the tactics now being followed, and on the possibility and desirability of ending the war, while preserving our position under international law on Sumatra.)*

## Intent and purpose

The motion's purpose, according to Van Kol, was not to seek immediate withdrawal but to inform the Dutch nation about the true state of affairs in North Sumatra. It sought to answer fundamental questions about what the war had cost in lives and money, what the future would likely hold, and whose interests were being served by its continuation — see [[Economic interests in the Aceh War]]. In Van Kol's view, the Dutch people were being kept in the dark about "de werkelijke toestanden op Noord-Sumatra" (the real conditions on North Sumatra).

## Support and defeat

The motion initially attracted support from radical parliamentarians Stoffel, Nolting, and Ketelaar, who joined the three socialists in supporting it. However, during the final vote, these radicals withdrew their support. After extensive debate, the motion was defeated by a vote of 72 to 3, with only Troelstra, Van der Zwaag, and Van Kol voting in favour. The defeat came after a heated scene in which Troelstra demanded that the chamber state which specific commercial, plantation, and shipping interests were benefiting from the war's continuation.

## Significance

The motion is a landmark document in the Dutch parliamentary history of the Aceh War. It represents the first formal socialist attempt to force a public accounting of the war's costs and consequences, and to question the desirability of its continuation after 24 years of conflict. Its overwhelming defeat by 72 votes to 3 demonstrates the depth of parliamentary support for the colonial enterprise at the time. The motion's framing of the war as a "national disaster" was a radical departure from the prevailing colonial discourse. Van Kol's call for an "onpartijdige commissie" (impartial commission) also established a precedent for future investigations into colonial atrocities.

**The Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe (29 May 1896)** — the show-of-force in the *moekim* cluster of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] — was a Dutch demonstration of force conducted on [[29 May]] [[1896]] by a colonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. C. J. Laceulle]] — Chef van den Staf [[Kapitein G. C. H. v. Daalen|Kapt. G. C. H. v. Daalen]]; adjudant [[Luit.-Adj. Bekker|Luit.-Adj. Bekker]] — in parallel with the [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the IX Moekims]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. B. van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] and in the immediate aftermath of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. The colonne comprised the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Maj. Okhuizen]], the [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor J. R. Jacobs|Maj. Jacobs]], a cavalry platoon under [[1e Luit. J. P. Gentil|1e Luit. J. P. Gentil]], two sections of mountain artillery under [[Kapitein Kempers|Kapt. Kempers]], and engineers under [[Luit.-Adj. E. J. de Rochemont|Luit.-Adj. E. J. de Rochemont]]. The assignment was to search the *kampong*s of [[Kandang]], [[Olehi-Loeng]], [[Poenir Le-Oe]], [[Goee-Gadjah]], and [[Tjabang]]. The head of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] — the *hoeloebalang* [[Panglima Bintang]] — had already secretly shown sympathy to the Dutch side.

## Background

By the last week of [[May]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] and the strategic initiative was firmly in Dutch hands. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], resolved to consolidate the political position in the *moekim* clusters adjacent to the area of operations. Two *machtsvertoon* colonnes were dispatched simultaneously on [[29 May]] [[1896]]: one, under [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], to the [[IX Moekims]]; the other, under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. J. Laceulle|Laceulle]], to the [[III Moekims Daroe]]. The objective in each case was to demonstrate the Government's reach, to search the *kampong*s for arms and resistance fighters, and to encourage the *hoeloebalang* of the districts to declare for the Government.

The [[III Moekims Daroe]] — a cluster of three *moekim* lying to the south of the [[Lampoeng]] / [[Lampisang]] region — was of particular political interest. Its *hoeloebalang*, [[Panglima Bintang]], had already secretly shown sympathy to the Dutch side — a sympathy that would, three weeks later, culminate in his [[Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|formal submission]] on [[20 June]] [[1896]]. The *machtsvertoon* of [[29 May]] [[1896]] was, in part, a test of [[Panglima Bintang]]'s political disposition: would he cooperate openly with the Dutch, or would he maintain the posture of armed neutrality that had characterised his position since the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]?

## The Composition of the Colonne

The colonne for the *machtsvertoon* in the [[III Moekims Daroe]] was placed under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. C. J. Laceulle]] — the same officer who had commanded the Colonne Laceulle at the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]]. His Chef van den Staf was [[Kapitein G. C. H. v. Daalen|Kapt. G. C. H. v. Daalen]] of the General Staff — a different member of the van Daalen family from the [[Kapitein G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]] who had served as Van Heutsz's Chef van den Staf at the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|Slag bij Tjot Tjako]]; his adjudant was [[Luit.-Adj. Bekker|Luit.-Adj. Bekker]]. The composition was: the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Maj. D. A. Okhuizen]] — the same battalion and commander that had carried the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]]; the [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor J. R. Jacobs|Maj. J. R. Jacobs]] — the same battalion and commander that had stormed the south-eastern *rand* of [[Lam-Asan]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]]; a cavalry platoon under [[1e Luit. J. P. Gentil|1e Luit. J. P. Gentil]]; two sections of mountain artillery under [[Kapitein Kempers|Kapt. Kempers]]; and engineers under [[Luit.-Adj. E. J. de Rochemont|Luit.-Adj. E. J. de Rochemont]].

## The Assignment and the Course of the Operation

The colonne's assignment was to search the *kampong*s of [[Kandang]], [[Olehi-Loeng]], [[Poenir Le-Oe]], [[Goee-Gadjah]], and [[Tjabang]] — the principal settlements of the [[III Moekims Daroe]]. The search was both military and political: military, in that the colonne was lalsoing for arms, resistance fighters, and evidence of cooperation with [[Teuku Umar|Oemar]]; political, in that the colonne was testing the disposition of [[Panglima Bintang]] and the other *hoeloebalang* of the district. The cavalry under [[1e Luit. J. P. Gentil|Gentil]] reconnoitred ahead; the infantry searched the *kampong*s in turn; the artillery was held in reserve.

The operation was, in tactical terms, uneventful. The *kampong*s were searched; some arms were recovered; no major engagement developed. The political outcome, however, was significant: the secret sympathy of [[Panglima Bintang]] was confirmed, and the groundwork was laid for his [[Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|formal submission]] on [[20 June]] [[1896]]. The operation was conducted in parallel with the [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the IX Moekims]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — a coordinated demonstration of Dutch reach across two adjacent *moekim* clusters.

## Significance

The Machtsvertoon in III Moekims Daroe on [[29 May]] [[1896]] was, in operational terms, a small affair — a search of five *kampong*s with no major engagement and no significant casualties. In political terms, however, it was an essential step in the consolidation of the Dutch position after the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. The confirmation of the secret sympathy of [[Panglima Bintang]] — the *hoeloebalang* of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] — was the principal outcome of the operation, and it set the stage for the political realignment of June [[1896]]. The *machtsvertoon* also demonstrated the Government's capacity to operate simultaneously in two *moekim* clusters — the [[IX Moekims]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and the [[III Moekims Daroe]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. J. Laceulle|Laceulle]] — a capacity that would be expanded, in June, to the coordinated multi-colonne operations of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]]. The *machtsvertoon* of [[29 May]] [[1896]] was, in this sense, the political and operational bridge between the offensive of May and the consolidation of June.

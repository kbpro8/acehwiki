**The Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)** — the *bestorming van den Tjot Tjako* — was the storming of the hill of [[Tjot Tjako]] by the **Bergcolonne** under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. J. B. van Heutsz]] on the first day of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] against [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* around [[Lam-Pisang]], the opening engagement of the strategic reversal of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. The Bergcolonne — 10 brigades of *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]], the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor G. A. Hansen|Maj. Hansen]], a section of mountain artillery under [[Kapitein Kempers|Kapt. Kempers]], and engineers under [[1e Luit. A. S. Ruzette|1e Luit. Ruzette]] — marched from [[Ketapan Doea]] at 3:30 a.m. on [[23 May]] [[1896]] to surprise the enemy. At 5:43 a.m. lieutenants Wagener and Vis, with the *maréchaussée* Wongsodikromo (No. 33780) and Todikromo (No. 28097), reached the summit; Sergeant Grebner, on his own initiative, attacked the western *hoogtekam* and occupied the southernmost top — an action that caused [[Lam-Pisang]] and [[Bineh Kroeng]] to be evacuated.

## Background

By [[23 May]] [[1896]] the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had completed four weeks of [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|sustained bombardment]] of Umar's *stellingen* and was ready to launch the infantry assault. The Achehnese *stelling* was organised in three sectors — left wing, centre, right wing — and was anchored on the hill of [[Tjot Tjako]], which dominated the northern approaches to [[Lam-Pisang]]. The intelligence summary listed every *versterking*, *loopgraaf*, and *benteng* in the *stelling*, with locations and estimated occupation: lead was hidden in wells at [[Bineh Kroeng]]; men from [[Pidië|Pedir]] were stationed at [[Boekit Kesoemba|Bt. Kesoemba]]; Oemar himself was hiding south of the *heuvelrug*; the people of [[Lam-Pagar]] had a warlike reputation; [[Panglima Gimba]] held the left wing; [[Pa Karim Lampagar]] was at [[Lam-Pagar]]; [[T. Mat Amin]] — an *oelama* — held a sector. The capture of [[Tjot Tjako]] was the necessary precondition for the assault on [[Lam-Pisang]] and on Oemar's house.

The **Bergcolonne** under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] — Chef van den Staf [[Kapitein G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapt. G. C. E. van Daalen]] — was assigned the task. Its composition was: 10 brigades *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] (4 officers, 189 men); the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor G. A. Hansen|Maj. G. A. Hansen]] (15 officers, 403 men); a section of mountain artillery under [[Kapitein Kempers|Kapt. Kempers]]; engineers under [[1e Luit. A. S. Ruzette|1e Luit. Ruzette]]; train, ambulance, reserve munition. The native guide [[Panglima Ali (gids)|Panglima Ali]] of [[Pantai-Abee]] was attached to the voorhoede. The plan was a night march from [[Ketapan Doea]] and a dawn ascent of [[Tjot Tjako]] in two groups — the eastern *helling* and the north-eastern side — to take the Achehnese defenders by surprise.

## The March and the Ascent

At 3:30 a.m. on [[23 May]] [[1896]] the Bergcolonne marched from [[Ketapan Doea]]. The voorhoede under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] — 10 brigades *maréchaussée* and a peloton of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] — advanced via the footpath north-west of Ketapan Doea. The night march was difficult: the footpath was narrow, the terrain broken, and the darkness complete. The colonne closed on [[Tjot Tjako]] undetected. At 5:20 a.m. the climb began. Two groups were formed: lieutenants [[H. M. Vis|Vis]] and Wagener on the eastern slope, lieutenants Stoop and Dijkstra on the north-eastern side. The Achehnese sentinels — if any were posted — failed to detect the approach until the climbing Dutch were within earshot. The enemy raised alarm cries and rolled stones down on the climbing Dutch — a traditional Achehnese defensive tactic on hill positions, intended to break the attackers' footing and to sweep them down the slope.

At 5:43 a.m. lieutenants Wagener and Vis, with the *maréchaussée* Wongsodikromo (No. 33780) and Todikromo (No. 28097), plus lieutenant Rijnen and European fusiliers, reached the summit. The names of the two native *maréchaussée* — Wongsodikromo and Todikromo — were preserved by Kruisheer in recognition of their distinction as the first to reach the top. Lieutenant Vis then drove approximately 30 armed Achehnese from their firing positions on the north-eastern slope. By 6:40 a.m. the entire colonne was on top; the enemy was driven from [[Tjot Tjako]]. From the summit, repeater-rifle fire was directed on [[Lam-Pisang]] and on Oemar's house, causing some 50 persons to flee south.

## Grebner's Initiative and the Evacuation of Lam-Pisang and Bineh Kroeng

Sergeant Grebner, on his own initiative, attacked the western ridgeline of [[Tjot Tjako]]. The official plan had not assigned the western *hoogtekam* to any unit, but Grebner — recognising that the southernmost top of the *hoogtekam* commanded both [[Lam-Pisang]] and [[Bineh Kroeng]] — seized the opportunity. His attack drove off the enemy, leaving one dead, and occupied the southernmost top. The effect was immediate: the Achehnese, finding their flank turned and their rear threatened, evacuated both [[Lam-Pisang]] and [[Bineh Kroeng]]. Grebner's initiative — the action of a single sergeant in defiance of the formal plan — was, in tactical terms, the decisive moment of the day. Without it, the assault on [[Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]] might have been a much harder operation.

## Van Heutsz's Four Messages

Van Heutsz sent a series of four messages to the commander, [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]], during the morning of [[23 May]] [[1896]]:

- **Bericht No. 1** (6:40 a.m., received 8:30 a.m.): "Toean Tjot Tjako niet beklimbaar voor artillerie, moet zadel Zuid van dien berg beklimmen onder dekking. Versoek drinkwater." (Tjot Tjako not climbable for artillery; must climb the southern saddle under cover. Request drinking water.)
- **Bericht No. 2** (8:17 a.m.): Grenades fell in Lam-Pisang; need reserve munition, drinking water, and ice.
- **Bericht No. 3**: To the commandants of the 6e and 2e Bergbatterij — instructions on dekking (cover) for transport of wounded and on the disposition of the artillery.
- **Bericht No. 4** (sent 9:15 a.m., received 11:15 a.m.): "Lam-Pisang verlaten. Wij hebben heuveltop ten O. van Oemar's huis bezet. Bineh Kroeng also verlaten. Vele Atjehers loopen weg in Z. richting. Troepen kunnen doorrukken. Versoeke toezending bereden ordonnansen." (Lam-Pisang abandoned. We have occupied the hilltop east of Oemar's house. Bineh Kroeng also abandoned. Many Achehnese walking away in southern direction. Troops can advance. Request sending of mounted orderlies.)

The fourth message was crucial. The *bevelhebber*, on the advice of the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] by telephone, had initially decided to halt operations for the day. The receipt of the fourth message — reporting that [[Lam-Pisang]] was abandoned and that the troops could advance — reversed this decision. The offensive continued. The episode is one of the most-cited examples in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of the friction between the political and military high commands, and of the consequences of delayed communications in mobile warfare.

## Casualties and Outcome

The Dutch casualties of the storming of [[Tjot Tjako]] were 1 dead (during the climb) and several wounded; 1 Amboineesch *maréchaussée* wounded; later in the morning 1 dead and 8 wounded total; 1 artilleryman killed on the advance. The enemy casualties were not enumerated in detail, but the Achehnese had been driven from numerous positions and were not pursued into the valley due to the slow progress of the infantry in the plain. A bivak was established on the summit of [[Tjot Tjako]]. The capture of [[Tjot Tjako]] opened the way for the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]] and the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|bestorming of the Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]].

## Significance

The Slag bij Tjot Tjako on [[23 May]] [[1896]] was the opening engagement of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] and one of the most-celebrated Dutch assaults of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The night march, the dawn ascent, the distinction of Wongsodikromo and Todikromo as the first to reach the summit, the initiative of Sergeant Grebner, and the four messages of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — all were preserved by Kruisheer as exemplars of the operational art in colonial mountain warfare. The battle also marked the emergence of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as a field commander of the first rank — the officer who could conduct a night march, surprise the enemy at dawn, occupy a dominating height, and force the Achehnese to evacuate their principal base. The Slag bij Tjot Tjako was, in this sense, the foundation on which the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] — and Van Heutsz's own subsequent career — was built.

**The Trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah** — *tocht naar Tibang-Poei en Birah bij Bat Dilip ter opsporing van het op 17 April '96 verloren gegane geschut* — on [[29 November]] [[1896]] was a Dutch search operation to recover the artillery lost on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] — the bloody lifting of the besieged Dutch posts of Senelop and Lamsoet in the first semester of [[1896]]. The trip was undertaken twelve days after the [[Handover of Command from Stemfoort to Van Vliet (17 November 1896)|formal handover of command]] from [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] to [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]], and was one of the principal operations of November [[1896]]. The trip took the Dutch to the kampongs of [[Tibang-Poei]] and [[Birah]] near [[Bat Dilip]] — the area in which the artillery had been lost — and represented the Dutch commitment to recovering the matériel which had been abandoned in the chaos of the April battle.

## The Lost Artillery of 17 April [[1896]]

The artillery which the trip of [[29 November]] [[1896]] sought to recover had been lost on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] — the bloody lifting of the besieged Dutch posts of Senelop and Lamsoet, which had been undertaken in the immediate aftermath of the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (29 March 1896)|Umar defection]]. The loss of the artillery had been a significant blow to the Dutch — both militarily, as it deprived them of the firepower needed for the forward operations, and symbolically, as it represented the most visible material loss of the first semester of [[1896]]. The Dutch had attempted to recover the artillery in the months following the battle, but the operations of the first semester — the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]], the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]], and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] — had not resulted in its recovery. The trip of [[29 November]] [[1896]] was thus a renewed attempt, undertaken in the second semester under Van Vliet's new governorship.

## Significance

The Trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah of [[29 November]] [[1896]] is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as one of the principal operations of November [[1896]]. The trip — undertaken twelve days after the formal handover of command from Stemfoort to Van Vliet — represented the Dutch commitment to recovering the matériel which had been abandoned in the chaos of the April battle, and demonstrated the continuity of the Dutch *pacificatie* (pacification) policy across the transition from Stemfoort to Van Vliet. The trip was the last major operation of November [[1896]], and was followed, in December [[1896]], by the [[Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh (December 1896)|cholera epidemic]] which would prevent the *colonne tegen Oemar* (column against Umar) from taking the field.

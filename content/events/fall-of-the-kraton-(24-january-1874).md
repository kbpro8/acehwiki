**The Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)** was the culminating operation of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Aceh expedition]], in which an [[Omtrekking|omtrekkende beweging]] led by [[Generaal Verspijck|generaal Verspijck]] from the captured burial ground of [[Kota Goenoengan]] around the zuider- and ooster-facen of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] — begun on [[23 January]] [[1874]] after "hevig vuur" — reached the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]] on the morning of [[24 January]] [[1874]] to discover, "tot niet geringe verbazing," that the great works were "op een grijsaard en een zieken Atjehnees na, geheel verlaten" (save for one greybeard and one sick Acehnese, entirely deserted). "Het lot van den Kraton was dus beslist" — the fate of the Kraton was thus decided. Borel's judgement is double-edged: the victory was real, but it could have been won "na weinige dagen" had the operation been conducted regelmatig, instead of which it took a month.

## The Omtrekkende Beweging

The [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan (12 January 1874)|capture of Kota Goenoengan]] on [[12 January]] [[1874]] had given the Dutch a foothold on the western face of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] from which the entire interior was visible. From that day the conclusion was inescapable that the enemy's hoofdmacht had already withdrawn. Yet [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] hesitated. It was only on [[23 January]] [[1874]], after a further eleven days of [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|bombardment]] and inconclusive skirmishing, that the [[Omtrekking|omtrekkende beweging]] was set in motion. [[Generaal Verspijck|Generaal Verspijck]] — whose disagreements with van Swieten on krijgskundig gebied were well known to Borel — was given the command. The column moved out of Kota Goenoengan, worked round the zuider- and ooster-facen of the Kraton, and headed for the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]]. The crossing of the [[Kroeng Daroe]] — the small river that splits the Kraton into two halves — caused "groot oponthoud." Throughout, Verspijck encountered "geen noemenswaardigen tegenstand."

## The Morning of 24 January

On the morning of [[24 January]] [[1874]] the omtrekkende beweging continued. At eleven o'clock the last mortier granaat of the [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|nineteen-day bombardment]] was fired — a symbolic close to the artillery's work. Verspijck's column reached the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]] at the rear of the Kraton. All the outlying works — the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] taken on [[6 January]], the [[Kota Goenoengan]] taken on [[12 January]], and the various schansen and versterkingen along the way — fell "zonder slag of stoot." An afdeeling of infantry climbed the low borstwering at the river-side, dropped into the interior, and discovered, "tot niet geringe verbazing," that they were inside the Kraton. The works were "op een grijsaard en een zieken Atjehnees na, geheel verlaten."

## Borel's Judgement

"Het lot van den Kraton was dus beslist." Yet Borel is careful to insist on what might have been. The whole omtrekkende beweging — the slow preparation, the eleven-day delay after [[12 January]], the cautious circuit around the zuider- and ooster-facen, the "groot oponthoud" at the Kroeng Daroe — could have been compressed into "weinige dagen" had the operation been conducted regelmatig from the moment the Mesigit fell. The enemy, with his retreat route on the south and east left open by van Swieten's refusal to land the third brigade at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai]], had used those eleven days to complete his withdrawal. The Kraton was taken — but the Atjehnees army that had defended it melted away into the interior, and the war that [[Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)|van Swieten would shortly pronounce "geëindigd"]] was, in truth, only beginning. The fall of the Kraton was a Dutch victory, but, Borel implies, a victory whose fruits were squandered by the very hesitation that had preceded it.

**The Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)** — the lifting, evacuation of the posts of [[Senelop]] and [[Lamsoet]] by the 1e Colonne of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. J. B. van Heutsz]] — was the hardest-fought single action of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the spring of [[1896]], a day of sustained combat along the *kampong* belt of [[Bralon]], [[Lam-Bada]], and [[Anak Baté]] that Kruisheer characterises as "one of the heaviest days our troops experienced in 1896" (one of the heaviest days our troops experienced in 1896). The voorhoede under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] — eight brigades of *maréchaussée* and a peloton of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] — bore the brunt of the fighting, in the course of which the column lost a 7 c.M. A. veld-gun (its breech-block and sight removed before abandonment), a 12 c.M. mortar, and a *mitrailleur*-chest with barrels. Two artillery sergeants, [[Putman]] and [[Klaassen]], died of overexertion — [[Putman]] of sunstroke, [[Klaassen]] of a stroke — in the course of the day. Enemy losses were 75 killed (including the [[Habib Lhong]] and [[Panglima Nja Bintang]]) and 88 heavily plus 80 lightly wounded. Simultaneously, the 2e Colonne of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] lifted [[Anak-Galoeng]] and [[Lam-Barih]] — an operation marred by a friendly-fire incident at [[Anak Baté]] in which [[Luitenant Hoolboom|Lt. Hoolboom]]'s mountain battery killed six European fusiliers.

## Background

The [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] on [[12 April]] [[1896]] had inaugurated the wholesale abandonment of the western *buitenlinie*. [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] resolved to complete the evacuation in a single coordinated operation. Two colonnes were formed. The **1e Colonne**, under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. J. B. van Heutsz]] — Chef van den Staf [[Kapitein van den generalen staf H. C. Kronouer|Kapt. Kronouer]]; Adjudant [[1e Luit. der Cavalerie J. P. Gentil|1e Luit. Gentil]]; toegevoegd [[1e Luit. der Infanterie K. A. Vosmaer|1e Luit. Vosmaer]] (acting *controleur*); four ordonnansen and two Achehnese guides — comprised eight *maréchaussée* brigades under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] (3 lieutenants, 160 European and native), a peloton of the 1e compagnie of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] (repeater rifles) under [[Kapitein L. G. van Aken|Kapt. van Aken]] (2 lieutenants, 78 men), the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. van der Noordaa]] (15 officers, 147 European, 308 native), the [[4e Bergbatterij]] in two sections under [[Kapitein A. Bangert|Kapt. Bangert]] (2 officers, 38 European, 22 native, 4 guns), a cavalry platoon under [[1e Luit. J. W. F. Herfkens|1e Luit. Herfkens]] (1 officer, 31 riders), an engineer detachment under [[1e Luit. N. Plantenga|1e Luit. Plantenga]] (1 officer, 11 European, 19 native), and a baggage train. Its task was to lift [[Senelop]] and [[Lamsoet]].

The **2e Colonne**, under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] personally, was to lift [[Anak-Galoeng]] and [[Lam-Barih]]. The two colonnes operated in parallel on the morning of [[17 April]] [[1896]].

## The Advance to Lam Soet

At 6 a.m. on [[17 April]] [[1896]] the 1e Colonne stood ready in march order and marched via [[Siroen]] to [[Lamsoet]]. The cavalry reconnoitred [[Lam Dam]], [[Gapoe]], and [[Loeboek]]; the *maréchaussée* did the same for the houses and yards; the engineers opened the path somewhat. **Lam Soet was reached at 8 a.m. without seeing the enemy.** The cavalry went ahead again after a brief rest and at 8:15 a.m. reported that the bridge south-east of the post was destroyed. Under cover of three *maréchaussée* brigades under [[1e Luit. Dijkstra|1e Luit. Dijkstra]] it was repaired. The hill north of [[Lam Oe]] was enemy-occupied, as was *kampong* [[Tjot Goet]] and the whole right bank of the [[Kroeng Lingkar]]. The colonne-commander, who had wanted to place artillery on that hill, desisted — he lacked enough troops to operate a column on the right bank of the Kroeng Lingkar, which would have been necessary.

The cavalry continued southward and, approximately 350 m south of the just-repaired bridge, ran into a heavy bamboo fence behind which a numerous band was posted. A horse was immediately wounded; the cavalry cleared the road for the *maréchaussée* and engineers. After reconnaissance the voorhoede-commander was told that the voorhoede had stumbled on an enemy fortification extending to the [[Atjeh River]], and artillery was requested. The terrain in the *kampong*s south of the [[Lamsoet]]–[[Senelop]] road was very covered; *sawah*s around *kampong* [[Belang]] and north of [[Bralon]] and [[Lam-Bada]] were in places swampy and dotted with small heights — the *tjots* — on which numerous enemy *benteng*s, facing north, had been built. At 8:50 a.m. the colonne-commander received another message: the fortification could not be outflanked. [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Graafland]] had meanwhile deployed: the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] peloton in the sugar-cane field north of the fortification, the *maréchaussée* with two brigades in reserve behind. The fortification had earthen walls with a double standing bamboo obstruction in front; bamboo-thorn immediately against the breastwork. While awaiting artillery, the repeater rifles opened fire at 100 m; the Ambonese *maréchaussée* Savie (No. 31497) was immediately wounded.

## The Heaviest Day

What followed was, in Kruisheer's words, "one of the heaviest days our troops experienced in 1896." The Achehnese defence was conducted with a determination and a coordination that bore witness to the unification of the resistance under [[Teuku Umar]] and his allies. The voorhoede of [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Graafland]] was forced to fight its way forward *kampong* by *kampong* — [[Bralon]], [[Lam-Bada]], [[Anak Baté]] — against *benteng*s that had to be stormed individually and that were, in the intervals, fed by reinforcements from the south. The 7 c.M. A. veld-gun, the 12 c.M. mortar, and the *mitrailleur*-chest — pushed forward to support the assault — were overwhelmed by the intensity of the enemy fire and had to be abandoned, their breech-blocks and sights removed to render them useless to the enemy. The two artillery sergeants, [[Putman]] and [[Klaassen]], died in the course of the day — [[Putman]] of sunstroke, [[Klaassen]] of a stroke — victims of the heat, the overexertion, and the intensity of the combat. The text explicitly criticises [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] for placing artillery in the rearguard and for not securing the [[Lamsoet]]–[[Senelop]] communication. The casualties on the Achehnese side were 75 killed (including the celebrated [[Habib Lhong]] and [[Panglima Nja Bintang]]) and 88 heavily plus 80 lightly wounded — a figure that, while substantial, was nonetheless insufficient to break the Achehnese hold on the *kampong* belt.

## The Concurrent Operations and the Friendly-Fire Incident

While [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s 1e Colonne fought its way to [[Senelop]], the 2e Colonne of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] lifted [[Anak-Galoeng]] and [[Lam-Barih]] with relatively light opposition. The evacuation of the western *buitenlinie* was, however, marred by a friendly-fire incident at [[Anak Baté]]: [[Luitenant Hoolboom|Lt. Hoolboom]]'s mountain battery, firing in support of the infantry, overshot and killed six European fusiliers of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]]. The incident — a bitter and avoidable loss — was deeply felt in the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] and was the subject of a board of inquiry. Kruisheer records it without comment but with the names of the dead.

The bridge at [[Ana' Galong]] was blown by Dutch engineers during the evacuation, sealing the western line behind the retreating column.

## Aftermath

By the end of [[17 April]] [[1896]] the western *buitenlinie* had ceased to exist. [[Senelop]], [[Lamsoet]], [[Anak-Galoeng]], and [[Lam-Barih]] were all in Dutch hands only for the hours necessary to withdraw their garrisons and burn their installations; by nightfall the colonnes were back on the consolidated line. The casualties of the 1e Colonne — the lost guns, the deaths of [[Putman]] and [[Klaassen]], the heavy losses among the *maréchaussée* brigades — were a sharp reminder of the cost of fighting a unified Achehnese enemy on his own ground. The death of [[Habib Lhong]] — one of the principal war-leaders of the western mukims — was, however, a substantial blow to the Achehnese, and the 75 enemy killed represented a serious attrition that the resistance could ill afford.

## Significance

The Battle of Senelop on [[17 April]] [[1896]] was the high-water mark of the Achehnese counter-offensive that had begun with the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. The Dutch had been forced to abandon the western *buitenlinie* — the entire strategic edifice of [[1893]]–[[1896]] — at the cost of the heaviest day's fighting of the campaign. But the battle was also the moment at which the initiative began to turn. The death of [[Habib Lhong]] and [[Panglima Nja Bintang]] deprived the Achehnese of two of their most effective commanders; the 75 killed and 168 wounded were losses that, repeated, would grind the resistance down. Within five weeks the Dutch would launch the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] of [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]], would [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture and destroy]] [[Teuku Umar]]'s house at [[Lam-Pisang]], and would [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|chastise]] the [[Lamkrak]] mukims. The Battle of Senelop was, in retrospect, the moment at which the Dutch stopped retreating and began, however painfully, to advance.

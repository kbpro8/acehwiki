**Capture of Kotta Alam** was a fierce combat engagement fought in January [[1874]] during the [[Second Aceh Expedition]], wherein troops of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] under General [[J. van Swieten]] assaulted and captured the fortified Acehnese stronghold of [[Kotta Alam]].

## Narrative of the Siege and Storming

Situated along the Aceh River between coastal base at [[Oleh-leh]] and royal capital at [[Kraton of Aceh]], the benteng of [[Kotta Alam]] was heavily defended by Acehnese forces equipped with artillery. In mid-January [[1874]], General [[J. van Swieten]] ordered siege batteries to open fire on the earthen ramparts.

Following a destructive bombardment, Dutch infantry battalions charged across the open sawahs and stormed the redoubt. Hand-to-hand combat inside the fortress resulted in the retreat of the Acehnese garrison toward [[Penajoeng]] and the kraton.

## Impact on Subsequent Operations

The occupation of Kotta Alam cleared the northern approach to [[Kraton of Aceh]], allowing engineering detachments to construct siege trenches that culminated in the [[Capture of the Aceh Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]]. 

Throughout [[1875]], Acehnese guerrillas frequently ambushed Dutch supply convoys between Kotta Alam and [[Oleh Karang]], prompting General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] to launch [[General Pel's Campaign in Aceh]] in December [[1875]].

**The Prang Rawa** — also called the **Rao War**, in Dutch *De Buitenbezittingen* glossed as "de Prang Rawa = Rao" — was the armed conflict at the end of the [[18th century|eighteenth century]] between the [[Atjehers|Atjehers]] and [[Pedireezen|Pedireezen]] gold-seekers descending from the uplands of the [[Kawaj XII|Kawaj XII]] into the coastal plains, and the [[Maleiers|Maleiers]] of the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]]. The war ended with the permanent settlement of the Atjehers in the greater part of the Maleier colonies and the consequent Acehnisation of the [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]] region, a transformation whose effects still defined the political and ethnographic landscape of the Westkust when the Dutch took over the administration in the late nineteenth century.

## Origins: gold and pepper

By the late eighteenth century the Maleier *landschappen* of the Westkust, "vooral door de pepercultuur," had attained "groote welvaart" — great prosperity. This prosperity "verlokte" — tempted — many Atjehers and Pedireezen who had settled in the uplands of the [[Kr. Geumpang|Kr. Geumpang]] (the *Kawaj XII*) as gold-seekers. As the gold riches of the upland diminished, these settlers descended "naar de kustvlakten" — toward the coastal plains — in search of new opportunities. Their arrival inaugurated the *hevigen strijd* that the native tradition remembers as the *Prang Rawa*.

## Course and outcome

The war, the Encyclopaedisch Bureau records, "eindigt met een tijdperk van z.g. de blijvende vestiging der Atjehers in het grootste deel der Maleische kolonies." Of the Westkust *landschappen* only [[Tapa' Toean|Tapa' Toean]] succeeded in remaining "zuiver Maleisch"; the others — [[Kloeët|Kloeët]], [[Blang Pidie|Blang Pidie]], [[Lhòë Pawa|Lhòë Pawa]], [[Manggèng|Manggèng]] and ultimately [[Meulabôh|Meulabôh]] itself — were either strongly influenced by Acehnese notions of government or "geheel Atjehsch werden." Meulabôh in particular became "in zeden en gewoonten vrijwel geheel Atjehsch," even though, the handbook notes, "men ook hier nog vele sporen der vroegere Maleische overheersching terugvindt."

## Institutional consequences

The Atjehsch infiltration brought with it the territorial *landschap* model of the [[Oelèëbalangs|oelèëbalangs]] under a *landschapshoofd*, displacing the older Menangkabausche *soekoe*-based *genealogisch bestuur* of the Maleier *dato*'s. Where Atjehers and Maleiers intermixed, the Acehnese *kawóm* organisation likewise displaced the original Menangkabau clan order; *panglima's-kawóm* arose beside the older *soekoehoofden*. The *nivelsteerings-proces* — the levelling process — continued, "nog steeds wordt voortgezet," long after the original settlement, steadily eroding the older Maleian adat. The [[adat kamanakan|adat kamanakan]] of Menangkabau survived in Tapa' Toean only until [[1913]], when the Governor, on the request of the Maleier chiefs of [[Laboean Soesa|Laboean Soesa]], [[Labuhan Hadji|Labuhan Hadji]], [[Samadöea|Samadöea]], Tapa' Toean and [[Kandang|Kandang]], formally abolished it.

## Significance

The *Prang Rawa* was the foundational demographic and political event of the modern Westkust. It transformed a Maleian-speaking, Menangkabau-adat littoral into a mixed — and in its southern half, predominantly Acehnese — society, and it explains the *verwarde* — confused — bestuursindeeling that Dutch administrators inherited and sought to rationalise in the early twentieth century. It also fixed the territorial division between the *keudjeuroeënschappen* of [[Seunagan|Seunagan]] and [[Kloeët|Kloeët]] and the three surviving Maleier *dato*'s of Meulabôh — the institutional landscape within which the colonial state would later operate.

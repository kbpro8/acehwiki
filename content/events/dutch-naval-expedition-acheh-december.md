**Dutch naval expedition to Acheh (December 19–25)** was a short‑term riverine campaign undertaken by the [[Dutch Navy (Colonial)]] in the interior of [[Acheh]] in the early nineteenth century. The operation involved the barks “[[Zeven Provinciën]]” and “[[St Eustatius]]”, the detachment of [[Captain‑lieutenant Smit]], and the command of [[Captain Van Oyen]]. Its objectives were to suppress rebel activity, protect sugar plantations, and secure river passages such as [[Cimbia creek]] and [[Wironje creek]].

## Chronology
- [[19 December]]: Dutch forces entered the interior, anchoring near [[Antonaa]] and [[Cimbia creek]].
- [[22 December]]: Orders issued for patrols of sugar plantations (e.g., “[[West‑Souburg]]” sugar fields) and reconnaissance of creeks.
- [[23 December]]: Detachment reached [[Cornelia Jacoba plantation]]; rebel activity reported.
- [[24 December]]: Attempted landing at [[Peereboom plantation]]; plantation burned.
- [[25 December]]: Failed landing at [[Wikkie creek]] due to mis‑communication; capture of a Surinamese border detachment at [[Beresteyn plantation]].

## Outcome
The expedition achieved limited success in destroying rebel strongholds but suffered from coordination failures, notably the ill‑planned landing on [[25 December]] that resulted in casualties and the loss of a “tent‑boat”.

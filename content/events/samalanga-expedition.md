**The Samalanga Expedition** was a Dutch military operation conducted in [[January]] [[1901]] in the [[Samalanga]] region of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — the region of the Samalanga mukims on the North Coast of Atjeh — that resulted in the conquest of [[Batoe Island]] and the subjugation of the villages of Samalanga and [[Peusangan]]. Conducted under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as part of the all-out offensive of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], the expedition is listed by [[Musket]] in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]) as one of the "great successes of the glorious period of Van Heutsz" — the fourth-named of the expeditions and excursions by which, in the colonial memory, "the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good."

## The 1901 operation

The expedition, conducted in the northern part of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] in [[January]] [[1901]], aimed to secure the coastal hinterland, disrupt Acehnese supply lines, and establish Dutch administrative control over the region. The conquest of **Batoe Island** off the Samalanga coast, and the subsequent pacification of the Samalanga and Peusangan villages on the mainland, marked a significant step toward the final phases of the [[Aceh War]].

The operation is noted in the award list of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]] history (page 186), where **[[J. A. Gauw]]** received the [[Ridder Militaire Willemsorde]] 4th class and an honourable mention for his role. The 1901 expedition demonstrated the continued Dutch commitment to fully annexing Atjeh even after decades of resistance, and highlighted the role of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] in combined amphibious-land operations along the North Coast.

## The great successes of the glorious period

In Merac's *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* ([[1947]]) the Samalanga Expedition is named as the fourth of the "great successes of the glorious period" of Van Heutsz. The book's chapter on *den zieleherder* describes the offensive phase of the Atjeh-oorlog as a sequence of expeditions by which the organised Achehnese resistance was broken:

> "the time also of our great successes! The Pidië expedition, the expeditions in Idi and Peusangan, the Pasé expedition, the Samalanga expedition, the expeditions to the Gayo and Alas lands, the action in the Tangsé and Geumpang area and on the North Coast of Atjeh, the patrol actions on the always restless West Coast, and finally the Battle of Batèë-Ilië, by which the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good."

The Samalanga Expedition is the fourth-named of these great successes, after the [[Pidië-expeditie]], the expeditions in Idi and Peusangan, and the [[Pasé-expeditie]]. It is followed in the list by the expeditions to the [[Gayo and Alas lands|Gayo and Alas lands]], the action in the Tangsé and Geumpang area, the patrol actions on the West Coast, and finally the [[Slag bij Batèë-Ilië|Battle of Batèë-Ilië]].

## Significance

The Samalanga Expedition is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as one of the great successes of the glorious period of Van Heutsz. The book's argument — that the great successes of the glorious period were the expeditions and excursions by which the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good — is the book's frame for the offensive phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The Samalanga Expedition is therefore one of the book's — and the colonial memory's — great successes of the glorious period.

In the broader Dutch-Acehnese historiography, the 1901 operation demonstrated the continued Dutch commitment to fully annexing Atjeh, even after decades of resistance, and the role of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] in combined amphibious-land operations on the North Coast. The successful capture of Batoe Island and the subsequent pacification of Samalanga and Peusangan marked a significant step toward the final phases of the Aceh War, in which the all-out offensive of Van Heutsz broke the organised, general Achehnese resistance for good.

## Account from the Maréchaussée Jubileum-nummer ([[1930]]) — the excursie in het Samalangasche

the [[1930]] jubilee number of *[[Het Nieuws van den Dag voor Nederlandsch-Indië]]* records that the *[[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]]* "ontbrak ook niet bij de excursie in het Samalangasche, waarbij o.a. het uit den tijd van generaal [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heyden]] beruchte [[Batee Ilie|Batee Iliek]] stormenderhand werd genomen" — was not lacking either at the excursion into the Samalangasche, at which, among other things, the Batee Iliek made notorious in the time of General Van der Heyden was taken by storm.

The position had acquired its reputation during the period when, under the administration of General K. van der Heijden, the Dutch action had extended over the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]], shortly before the *[[Concentratiestelsel|concentratie]]* decision of [[19 August]] [[1884]].

The jubilee account places the storming within the corps's larger pattern of operations in these years: "de Maréchaussée dreef de verzetslieden steeds meer het binnenland in, waar zij zich schuil wisten te houden, doch ook daar werden opgespoord" — the Maréchaussée drove the resistance fighters ever further into the interior, where they contrived to hide, but where they were tracked down as well. "De meeste dooden vielen gedurende de patrouilles" — most of the deaths occurred during the *[[Patrouille (Maréchaussée)|patrouilles]]*.

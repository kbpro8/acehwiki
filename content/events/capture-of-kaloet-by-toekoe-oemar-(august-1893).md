**The Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)** was the bloodless capture of the Atjehnese fortification of [[Kaloet]] by the Dutch-allied Atjehnese warlord [[Toekoe Oemar]] in the early days of [[August]] [[1893]] — an operation that succeeded where the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|failed Dutch assault of 1 October 1892]] had failed, and that did not cost *een druppel bloeds meer* — *another drop of blood*. The capture was the principal Dutch-Atjehnese auxiliary success of the late *concentratie* period, and the principal evidence — in the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]]'s reading — of the operational effectiveness of General [[Deykerhoff]]'s policy of co-opting Atjehnese warlords as Dutch auxiliaries.

## The context

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that, after the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|failed Dutch assault on Kaloet of 1 October 1892]], General [[Deykerhoff]] had *Kaloet voorloopig ongemoeid* (provisionally left Kaloet alone) — refusing to risk further Dutch lives in a renewed assault. The fortification was, however, a continuing provocation: the Atjehnese garrison continued to fire on the Dutch *spoorbaan* (railway line) and on the Dutch posts at [[Lambaroe]] and [[Lamreng]]. The capture of Kaloet by [[Toekoe Oemar]] in [[August]] [[1893]] was, in the album's narrative, the resolution of the problem — and the principal operational success of the Dutch-Atjehnese auxiliary system that Deykerhoff had introduced.

## The operation of August 1893

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that [[Toekoe Oemar]] — supported by Dutch artillery and with Dutch infantry as reserve — had, *in de eerste dagen van Augustus 1893, de geheele XXV Moekims had zuiverd van vijandelijke benden, en eenige sterke bentings buiten de linie had veroverd, o.a. Boekit Trieng, Boekit Daroe, Kasoemba en Tjot-Goe* — *in the first days of August 1893, had purified the entire XXV Moekims of enemy bands, and had conquered some strong bentings outside the line, among others Boekit Trieng, Boekit Daroe, Kasoemba and Tjot-Goe*. The capture of Kaloet was the next operation.

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Toekoe Oemar *omsingelde den 4en Augustus het geheele terrein, waarin de benting lag, en sloot alle toevoerwegen af* — *invested on 4 August the entire terrain in which the benting lay, and cut off all supply routes*. The 4th Mountain Battery from the Kraton and a large number of troops — approximately 1000 men — supported him *krachtig* (forcefully). The artillery of [[Lamreng]], but especially of [[Lambaroe]], kept the surrounding enemy *kampongs* continually under fire. The album records that *den geheelen dag was het een oorverdoovend gepaf, doch wijselijk liet men een stormaanval achterwege, wel wetende dat deze weinig kans van slagen had en veel nutteloos gebrachte offers zou kosten* — *all day it was a deafening bang, but wisely a storm-attack was refrained from, knowing well that this would have little chance of success and would cost many useless sacrifices*.

## The morning of 5 August 1893

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, on the morning of [[5 August]] [[5 August]] [[1893]], the Dutch *meenden dat de pret weer zou beginnen, doch alles bleef kalm* — *thought that the fun would begin again, but everything remained calm*. *Al bij het krieken van den dag liet Oemar weten dat hij rustig en wel in Kaloet zat te dejeuneeren* — *Already at the crack of dawn Oemar let [us] know that he was sitting quietly and well in Kaloet at breakfast*. The album records that, the night before, Toekoe Oemar had sent a *briefje* (note) to the *panglima* of Kaloet with the *vriendelijk verzoek* (friendly request) to *met zijne bezetting uit wandelen te gaan* (to go for a walk with his garrison); the note added that, if the garrison did not withdraw along a designated path before sunrise, Toekoe Oemar would apply a *hongerkuur* (hunger-cure), keep the *benting* invested, and *bij de overgave allen over de kling jagen* (at the surrender put all to the sword). The Atjehnese garrison — judging that the *briefje* was meant seriously — withdrew in the night, and Toekoe Oemar found the *benting* abandoned on the morning of [[5 August]] [[5 August]] [[1893]].

## The Dutch entry into Kaloet

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after Toekoe Oemar's capture of Kaloet, the Dutch *kwamen door Oemar in het bezit der benting, en konden nu dezen steen des aanstoots slechten* — *came through Oemar into possession of the benting, and could now demolish this stone of stumbling*. The Dutch immediately *opengekapt* (cleared) the *voorterrein* (foreterrain), while a detachment *tijdelijk als bezetting diende* (temporarily served as garrison). A few days later, the Dutch managed to take some *photographiën* of the *benting* — the principal published images of Atjehnese fortification practice in the late nineteenth century. The album records that *Ieder officier, die maar eenigszins de gelegenheid kon vinden, ging naar Kaloet om dit kunstwerk van Atjehsche versterkingsbouwkunde te bezichtigen en … te bewonderen* — *Every officer who could in any way find the opportunity went to Kaloet to inspect and … to admire this artwork of Atjehnese fortification construction*.

## Significance

The Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar is significant in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as the principal Dutch-Atjehnese auxiliary success of the late *concentratie* period — the operation that succeeded where the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|failed Dutch assault of 1 October 1892]] had failed. The capture was, in the album's reading, the principal evidence of the operational effectiveness of General [[Deykerhoff]]'s policy of co-opting Atjehnese warlords as Dutch auxiliaries — the policy that would, three years later, collapse with [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] of [[March]] [[1896]]. The capture was also the occasion of the principal published photographic record of Atjehnese fortification practice — the photographs of [[Kaloet]] taken by [[J. P. Verweij|Lieutenant J. P. Verweij]] a few days after the capture.

**The first Atjeh expedition** of March–April [[1873]] was the initial Dutch military campaign against [[Acheh|Atjeh]], launched after [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|gouvernements-commissaris Nieuwenhuyzen]]'s ultimatum of [[22 March]] [[1873]] and the subsequent bombardment of the Sultan's residence. The expedition — conducted with inadequate preparation, insufficient intelligence about Atjeh's terrain and defences, and a navy in poor condition — ended in failure and retreat, necessitating a second expedition under [[Van Swieten|general Van Swieten]] later that year. Its failure became the central indictment of the Government's conduct in the [[Tweede Kamer debate on Atjeh war origins (April 1874)|Tweede Kamer debate of April 1874]].

## The failure and its causes

The first expedition's failure was attributed by parliamentary critics to multiple causes: the Government's decision to launch a land expedition at an unfavourable time (with the west monsoon approaching), the poor condition of the navy (the *Raad van Indie* had noted that the state of the marine was "very sad"), the complete lack of knowledge about Atjeh's conditions, and the inadequate preparation of troops unfamiliar with the new *achterlandgeweer* (rear-loading rifle). [[Nierstrasz|Nierstrasz]] argued that the failure came entirely at the Government's charge, and [[Van Nispen van Sevenaer|Van Nispen]] noted that from a *proces-verbaal* of the [[Raad van Nederlandsch Indie|Raad van Indie]] the same appeared regarding the marine.

[[Knoop|General Knoop]] later said: "the second expedition is the greatest justification of the outcome of the first that can exist" — meaning that the difficulties encountered even by the vastly stronger second force demonstrated that the first expedition's failure was inherent in the undertaking itself, not merely in its execution.

## The 47 days before the kraton

[[Van Swieten|General Van Swieten]] took 47 days with an enormous military force to reach the kraton — a fact cited as proof that the terrain was extremely difficult. [[Nierstrasz|Nierstrasz]] noted that within the kraton itself, fortifications already existed between the first and second expeditions, and that the large landing before the kraton in the first expedition had given the Atjehers the opportunity to construct even more formidable defences.

## Colonel Van Daalen as scapegoat

[[Nierstrasz|Nierstrasz]] in the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer]] called the search for a "second Bazaine" in the "poor colonel Van Daalen" unjust, while [[T.N. Nieuwenhuyzen|Nieuwenhuyzen]] — "the truly guilty one" — lived in Arnhem on a pension of 12,000 guilders. The colonel Van Daalen could not even receive his pension, "as if he were the cause that the first expedition had failed."

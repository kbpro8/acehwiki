**Aceh-VOC Conditions (1659)** refers to the provisional agreement concluded on [[20 June]] [[1659]] in [[Batavia, Dutch East Indies|Batavia]] between Acehnese envoys and the [[Verenigde Oostindische Compagnie|VOC]], ending the two-year naval blockade of [[Aceh]]'s ports that had been imposed since [[1657]]. The agreement reaffirmed [[Perak]]'s obligation to pay a [[50,000 realen]] indemnity from the [[Malacca-Aceh Treaty (1655)]], and it made [[Aceh]] formally responsible for ensuring compliance.

## Context

The [[Malacca-Aceh Treaty (1655)]] had failed to secure restitution for the [[1651]] attack on the VOC factory in [[Perak]]. [[Perak]] made no meaningful payments, and the Queen of [[Aceh]] declined to compel her vassal. In [[1657]], the VOC imposed a naval blockade on the harbors of [[Aceh]] and [[Perak]] as pressure to enforce the agreement.

By [[1659]], Aceh's court agreed to send a delegation to [[Batavia]]. The envoys, however, arrived with limited credentials, so the resulting agreement was explicitly provisional rather than final.

## The conditions

The agreement of [[20 June]] [[1659]] reaffirmed Perak's obligation to pay the 50,000 realen indemnity and obliged [[Aceh]] to cause Perak to make the payment. The text did not specify a mechanism for enforcement nor the consequences if Aceh itself failed to act.

## Aftermath

The blockade was lifted, but the underlying disputes over the west-coast pepper trade, the authority of the [[Panglima]] system, and Aceh's claims over west-coast territories remained unresolved. The [[1659]] conditions were superseded in [[1662]]–[[1663]] by the [[Painans Contract]], which shifted the VOC's approach from requiring Aceh cooperation to displacing Aceh's authority altogether.

## Significance

The [[Aceh-VOC Conditions (1659)]] are significant as the last major VOC-Acehnese diplomatic settlement before Aceh's retreat from the west coast. They illustrate the limits of treaty-making when envoys lacked full authority, and they show the VOC's use of legal language—particularly third-party guarantees—to manage colonial relationships.

## Account from Stapel's *De Koloniën* (1931)

**The Treaty of Atjeh-VOC of 5 October 1659** was the peace and friendship treaty ratified by the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|sultane of Atjeh]] on [[5 October]] [[1659]], concluding the crisis opened by the [[Blockade of Atjeh (1656)|1656 blockade]] and the [[Arrest of Anthony van Voorst|arrest of Anthony van Voorst]]. Recorded in Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* as the instrument by which the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] restored relations with Atjeh while securing compensation, a renewed Westkust monopoly, the right to re-establish a lodge (*loge*) at Atjeh itself, and a separate Perak settlement.

## Background

The [[Blockade of Atjeh (1656)|1656 blockade]] had produced, instead of Acehnese submission, the sultane's arrest of [[Anthony van Voorst]] and seizure of the Compagnie's effects on the Westkust. Batavia responded by finally accepting the repeatedly proffered request of the [[Menangkabau]] chiefs to be defended "against the Atchinders, both by water and by land" (*tegens d'Atchinders … soo te water als te lande*). A first expedition in [[1657]] achieved nothing; a second had enough success to bring Atjeh to the table.

## The negotiation

"Atjeh sent an embassy to Batavia, to discuss there a new treaty of peace and friendship, on which one agreed on 20 June 1659" (*Atjeh een gezantschap naar Batavia zond, om daar een nieuw verdrag van vrede en vriendschap te bespreken, waarover men het 20 Juni 1659 eens werd*). With the envoys a deputation under [[Jacob de Keyser|Jacob de Keyser]] and [[Balthasar Bort|Balthasar Bort]] then travelled to Atjeh, "and obtained on 5 October the ratification by the sultane" (*en verkreeg den 5den October de ratificatie door de sultane*).

The 20 June 1659 agreement is the Batavia draft; the 5 October 1659 ratification is the sultane's confirmation. Both dates are part of the same treaty process and are sometimes both cited as the treaty date.

## Terms

The treaty had three principal elements.

**Compensation.** "The Compagnie would receive compensation for the damage suffered in the arrest of her people and goods" (*De Compagnie zou vergoeding krijgen voor de geleden schade bij het arresteeren van haar menschen en goederen*). This was the official settlement of the [[Anthony van Voorst]] affair.

**Westkust monopoly.** "On the West Coast trade was to be carried on only by Atjehers and the Compagnie" (*op de Westkust zou alleen gehandeld mogen worden door Atjehers en de Compagnie*). This re-confirmed the [[Treaty of Atjeh-VOC (November 1649)|Truytman formula]] of November 1649.

**Lodge at Atjeh.** "The latter would re-establish a lodge at Atjeh" (*de laatste zou te Atjeh opnieuw een loge vestigen*). The Compagnie's resident at Atjeh, withdrawn after the 1623 closure, was to be reinstated.

**Perak.** A separate regulation was made for [[Perak]] (see below).

## The Perak settlement

The Perak clause of the 5 October 1659 treaty is recorded separately in Stapel's chapter on Malakka. "By the peace tractate, concluded on 5 October 1659 with the sultane of Atjeh — see above, p. 15 — it was again determined that half of the tin won in Perak should be for Atjeh, half for the Compagnie" (*Bij het vredestractaat, 5 October 1659 gesloten met de sultane van Atjeh — zie hiervóór, blz. 15 — weer bepaald, dat de helft van de op Perak gewonnen tin voor Atjeh, de helft voor de Compagnie zou zijn*). The Compagnie was to pay provisionally only 30 reals the bar, to be brought to the old footing of 32½ reals once its arrears were satisfied.

The Compagnie's experience with Perak execution was unhappy. "When however those of Perak in the execution of this agreement made all kinds of chicanes, the government lifted in 1663 also the factory in this place; 'the trade of Perak shall henceforth be driven on board'" (*Toen echter die van Perak bij de uitvoering dezer overeenkomst allerlei chicanes maakten, lichtte de regeering in 1663 ook het kantoor in deze plaats; 'de handel van Perak sal voortaen aen boord gedreven worden'*).

## Significance

The 1659 peace is the formal end of the 1656–1659 Acehnese-Dutch war. It is also the last great co-operative settlement between the [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|sultane]] and the Compagnie. Within four years the Compagnie was in secret negotiation with the Menangkabau chiefs through [[Jan van Groenewegen|Jan van Groenewegen]] — negotiations that produced the [[Painansch Contract]] of [[6 July]] [[1663]] and effectively dismantled the Atjehsche sovereignty that the 1659 treaty had just confirmed.

The 1659 peace is therefore a hinge: it restores relations in form while marking the beginning of the end of Atjeh's authority on the Westkust. The Compagnie's compensation, the lodge at Atjeh and the Westkust monopoly are the formal achievements; the Menangkabau defection that followed is the practical outcome.

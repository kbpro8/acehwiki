**Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)** was the large military parade held on the drill-ground near [[Petjoct]] for all the troops at [[Koeta-Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh]] on the eve of the [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|departure]] of [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] — the ceremonial close of the Vetter interregnum and of the first semester of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[1896]]. The participants comprised eight battalions of infantry, the *korps maréchaussée*, one squadron of cavalry, two *bergbatterijen*, one company of *genietroepen*, and one company of *vestingartillerie*. [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]], in his capacity as [[Regeeringscommissaris]], inspected the troops and addressed each commander with words of farewell and thanks for the difficult period. The same day, by *commandementsorder* from the Army Commander, commanders were ordered to remind their subordinates of Title VII of the *Crimineel Wetboek voor het krijgsvolk te lande* — the Dutch Military Criminal Code — regarding "geweldenarijen en strooperijen" (violence and poaching). Strict prohibition on robbing and plundering, and on taking poultry, goats, sheep, or rams — Articles 174, 176, 177, 181, 184. Burning was only allowed when ordered by the Chefs.

## Background

By the last week of [[June]] [[1896]] the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had accomplished the mission for which he had been dispatched to Atjeh three months earlier. The [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]] had been answered; the [[buitenlinie]] had been [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuated]] and the Dutch forces concentrated; the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] and had [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|destroyed his house]]; the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] had reduced the principal Achehnese redoubts; the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] had completed the operational cycle. The political realignment was under way: [[Panglima Bintang]] had [[Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|submitted]] on [[20 June]] [[1896]], and the [[Disarmament of Buitenlinie Chiefs (mid-June 1896)|disarmament of the buitenlinie chiefs]] was in progress.

Vetter — having accomplished his mission — was preparing to [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|depart]] for [[Batavia]] on [[28 June]] [[1896]] aboard the *stoomschip Coen*. Before his departure, however, he wished to take formal leave of the troops who had carried the burden of the spring campaign. A parade was ordered on the drill-ground near [[Petjoct]] — the open ground outside [[Koeta-Radja]] that had long served as the principal parade-ground of the Atjeh garrison.

## The Parade

The parade was held on [[27 June]] [[1896]]. The participants comprised a substantial portion of the Dutch forces in Atjeh: eight battalions of infantry (drawn from the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e]], [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e]], [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e]], [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e]], [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e]], [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e]], [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e]], and [[15de Bataljon Infanterie|15de]] Bataljons — the same battalions that had carried the principal burden of the fighting of May and June); the the elite corps that had been first into many engagements — [[Senelop]], [[Tjot Tjako]], [[Baha]], [[Kroeng-Kali]]; one squadron of cavalry; two mountain artillery batteries; one company of engineers; and one company of fortress artillery. The parade was, in this sense, a comprehensive display of the Dutch forces that had fought the spring campaign.

[[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] — in his capacity as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] and as Army Commander — inspected the troops. He addressed each commander with words of farewell and thanks for the difficult period — a personal gesture that acknowledged the burden that the spring campaign had imposed on officers and men alike. The parade was, in its way, the ceremonial close of the Vetter interregnum: a public acknowledgment by the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] that the mission had been accomplished and that the troops had done their duty.

## The Commandementsorder on Military Discipline

The same day — [[27 June]] [[1896]] — by *commandementsorder* from the Army Commander — i.e., from Vetter himself, commanders were ordered to remind their subordinates of Title VII of the *Crimineel Wetboek voor het krijgsvolk te lande* — the Dutch Military Criminal Code — regarding "geweldenarijen en strooperijen" (violence and poaching). The order imposed a strict prohibition on robbing and plundering, and on taking poultry, goats, sheep, or rams — Articles 174, 176, 177, 181, 184 of the Code. Burning was only allowed when ordered by the Chefs.

The *commandementsorder* was, in its way, as significant as the parade itself. The spring campaign had been marked by extensive *tuchtiging* — the systematic burning of Achehnese *kampong*s as a punitive measure. The *verbranding* of [[Anak-Paja]], [[Djirat]], [[Lho-Nga]], [[Lam-Caboes]], [[Lam-Paja]], [[Mantjang]], [[Lam-Baroe]], [[Lamlon]], and [[Noesa]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]]; the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] of [[30 May]]–[[2 June]] [[1896]]; the burning of the [[Lamkrak Moekims|Lam-Krak kampong]]s on [[10 June]] [[1896]]; the burning of *versterkingen* in the line [[boon-Adjé]]–[[Lam-Soet]] on [[17 June]] [[1896]] — each of these had been an act of deliberate destruction, ordered by the Chefs but executed by the troops. The *commandementsorder* of [[27 June]] [[1896]] was an attempt to re-impose discipline on the practice: to confine *tuchtiging* to authorised operations, to prohibit the informal plundering that had attended the burning, and to remind the troops that the *Crimineel Wetboek* applied in Atjeh as it did elsewhere in the Indies. The order was, in this sense, both a moral injunction and a legal one — a recognition that the spring campaign had pushed the liprovided that of what the Dutch military code permitted, and a declaration that the liprovided that would henceforth be enforced.

## Significance

Vetter's Farewell Parade of [[27 June]] [[1896]] was the ceremonial close of the Vetter interregnum and of the first semester of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in [[1896]]. The parade itself was a public acknowledgment by the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] that the mission had been accomplished and that the troops had done their duty. The *commandementsorder* on military discipline — issued the same day — was, however, of equal or greater significance: it was a recognition that the *tuchtiging* campaigns of May and June had pushed the liprovided that of what the Dutch military code permitted, and a declaration that the liprovided that would henceforth be enforced. The juxtaposition of the two acts — the parade of thanks and the order of restraint — captured, in microcosm, the dual character of the Vetter interregnum: a period of military recovery that had restored the strategic initiative, but that had also imposed considerable hardship on the Achehnese civilian population. The next day — [[28 June]] [[1896]] — Vetter would [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|depart]] for Batavia, the office of [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Civiel en Militair Gouvernur]] would be restored under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]], and the first semester of [[1896]] would be brought to its formal close.

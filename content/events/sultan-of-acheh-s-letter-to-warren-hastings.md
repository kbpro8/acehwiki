The **Sultan of Acheh's letter to [[Warren Hastings|Warren Hastings]] of 7 November [[1786]]** — known in Dutch as the *sultansbrief 7 November 1786* — was the celebrated and insulting reply sent by [[Sultan Alaeddin Muhammad Shah of Acheh|Sultan Alaeddin Muhammad Shah of Acheh]] (referred to in the letter as "sultan Munson, son of sultan Ziemul Abudeen," Van der Kemp's note identifying him as the same sultan known to [[Pieter Johannes Veth|Veth]] as *Alaoe'd-din Mahmoed Shah Djohan*) to Governor-General [[Warren Hastings|Warren Hastings]] of Bengal. The letter is reproduced in full as Appendix 1 of Van der Kemp's article, and is invoked in the opening pages as proof that European relations with [[Acheh]] had, throughout the eighteenth century, been "either very loose or very strained."

## Circumstances

The immediate occasion of the letter was the arrival at Acheh of the British ship under Captain [[Captain Steven|Steven]] (the same Captain [[Captain Hobbs|Hobbs]] mentioned in the text having previously been mortally wounded by a "classy" from [[Java]] at [[Tringano]]). The Sultan had earlier requested, through [[John Bird|Mr. John Bird]], 300 small muskets conformable to a pattern he had sent. The muskets delivered proved worthless, and the Sultan returned them with the letter now under consideration.

## The Letter

After the usual compliments — "God grant you long life and preserve you from the distresses of the world" — the Sultan observed that he was "very desirous of this interview and of a lasting friendship with the English." He had sent an answer by Captain [[Captain Hobbs|Hobbs]], who had died at [[Tringano]] after being wounded. The Sultan had then consulted Captain [[Captain Canning|Canning]] and Mr. [[W. E. Phillips|Phillips]] about the disposal of Captain Hobbs's effects, and they had unanimously desired him to deliver them to [[John Bird|Mr. John Bird]]. He then came to the heart of the matter:

> "The muskets which you sent me, as mentioned in your letter, from not being worth anything, I have returned, and request in future you will send me such as may be useful, or I do not want any, though I ask you for 300 small muskets agreeable to a muster I send by Mr. John Bird after the death of Captain Hobbs."

The Sultan further complained that [[John Bird|Mr. John Bird]] had sold certain articles intended for [[Java]] (not [[Bengal|Bengal]]) and had taken goods in exchange from the Sultan himself, occasioning a loss of 3,891 *koors*. He concluded: "My sole intention and desire is to maintain a friendship with you, and that you may continue to write me letters. I request you will fix on some one to send to me; you are doubtless so that I may ask him about all matters."

## Significance

Van der Kemp cites the letter as a measure of the contempt in which the Achenese court held even so powerful a British figure as [[Warren Hastings|Warren Hastings]]. The brutal directness of the message — if the Governor-General proposed to send more worthless goods, he had better not bother — set the tone for Anglo-Achehnese relations in the decades that followed. [[Captain Francis Light|Captain Francis Light]]'s contemporaneous conclusion (15 February 1786) that [[Acheh]] was unsuitable for a British settlement, and the consequent [[Treaty of Quedah (Penang cession, 1786)|cession of Penang]] by the [[Raja of Quedah|Raja of Quedah]] in the same year, must be read against this background. The supervision of Achehnese affairs would, nevertheless, be specifically entrusted to the government of [[Penang|Pinang]], and the [[Acheh succession dispute (1802-1819)|civil war of 1802–1819]] would draw the British back into Achehnese politics, producing the [[Captain Canning's mission to Acheh (1814)|Canning mission of 1814]], the [[Captain Coombs' first Acheh mission (1817-1818)|Coombs mission of 1817–18]], and ultimately the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|treaty of 22 April 1819]].

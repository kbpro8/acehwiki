**Gayo and Alas Lands Expedition of 1904** was a major five-month military sweep executed between [[February]] and [[July]] [[1904]] by the Dutch colonial army through the isolated Gayo and Alas highlands of interior Aceh.

## Story of the Expedition

In early [[1904]], seeking to crush the remaining insurgent leaders who had retreated into the high interior mountains, the colonial administration organized an expeditionary force under the command of Lieutenant-Colonel [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]]. The strike force initially comprised 10 specialized brigades of the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]], later reinforced to 14 brigades.

Marching from coastal bases into the unexplored mountain ranges of [[Gayo Lèuëos]], the Marechaussee columns endured extreme physical hardship. Soldiers traversed dense mountain rainforests, crossed swollen torrents, and climbed sheer cliffs while suffering from severe food shortages, cold, and exhaustion.

As the columns advanced into Gayo villages such as [[Blangkejeren]], they encountered fortified wooden stockades (*kuta*). Van Daalen ordered direct assaults on these positions. The Marechaussee troops stormed the stockades, resulting in high casualties among highland defenders and civilians. Between [[February]] and [[July]] [[1904]], the expedition systematically cleared the valleys of Gayo Lèuëos and the Alas lands.

## Historical Controversy and Impact

While acclaimed in colonial military circles for its extreme physical endurance and tactical success in opening the highlands, the expedition drew intense international and domestic political controversy due to the heavy loss of life in Gayo stockades. Following the campaign, permanent Marechaussee stations were established in [[Blangkejeren]] and [[Takengon]], bringing the interior under permanent colonial administrative control.

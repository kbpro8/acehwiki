**Painans Contract** (also known as the **Contract of Painan**) was a treaty concluded on [[27 July]] [[1663]] between the [[Verenigde Oostindische Compagnie|Dutch East India Company (VOC)]] and various chiefs of [[Sumatra]]'s [[Sumatra's west coast|west coast]], formally transferring allegiance from the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultanate of Aceh]] to the [[VOC]] and establishing a system of [[mediation|conciliation]] and [[arbitrage|arbitration]] for dispute resolution among the contracting parties.

## Context

Throughout the mid-seventeenth century, the [[VOC]] had maintained an uneasy commercial presence on the [[Sumatra|west coast of Sumatra]], a region nominally under the suzerainty of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultanate of Aceh]]. Despite repeated confirmations of trading privileges—most notably by [[Johan Truytman]] in [[1649]], who secured a formal renewal of VOC rights from the [[Sultan of Aceh]]—the Company's commerce in [[pepper]] and [[gold]] was persistently obstructed by local [[Panglima|panglimas]]. These Acehnese-appointed governors were responsible for collecting tolls and maintaining order, but they often failed to protect VOC interests and actively resisted the Company's traders.

In the early [[1660]]s, discontent among the west-coast chiefs against Acehnese governance reached a tipping point. The panglimas' oppressive toll-collection and security failures made them unpopular, and the [[Sultan of Indrapura|Sultan of Indrapoera]]—driven from his land by an Acehnese-backed rebellion—approached the VOC with an offer: in exchange for military assistance, he would open three gold mines that had remained unexploited for fear that Aceh would seize their output.

In May [[1662]], [[Jan van Groenewegen]]—the VOC's representative on the west coast—convened a clandestine meeting at [[Songibongin]] south of [[Painan]]. A provisional compact emerged from that meeting, laying the groundwork for a formal treaty.

## The contract

The definitive *accoort en eeuwig verbont* ("accord and eternal alliance") was ratified in [[Batavia, Dutch East Indies|Batavia]] on [[27 July]] [[1663]], bearing the signature of [[Joan Maetsuycker]], the [[Governor-General of the Dutch East Indies|Governor-General]]. Known in Dutch as the *Painansch Contract*, the treaty formalized an alliance between the [[VOC]] and west-coast polities.

The contract's most innovative feature was Article 8, which required signatories to refer disputes to the [[VOC]] for mediation and, if the Company could not settle the matter, to neutral allied states for arbitration. The decision was binding, and non-compliance was punishable by joint military action by the Company and the other allies.

Article 10 created a *gemeenen raat* ("common council") of equal numbers of [[Dutch people|Dutchmen]] and [[Indrapura|Indrapourese]] to adjudicate disputes between VOC personnel and local inhabitants.

## Geographic scope and signatories

The treaty bound together a coalition of west-coast states, including:

- [[Indrapura|Indrapoera]]
- [[Painan]]
- [[Padang]]
- [[Sillida]]
- [[Kota Tengah|Cotta Tenga]]
- [[Priaman]]
- [[Tikoe]]
- [[Salida]]

The alliance was intended to withstand [[Aceh]]'s attempts to reassert control over the coast. In practice, its implementation faced immediate challenges, and the VOC relocated its lodge from [[Salida]] to [[Poulo Tjinko]] when inland insecurity worsened.

## Consequences

The Painans Contract was a watershed in west-coast politics. The VOC expanded the alliance through persuasion and coercion, incorporating holdouts until the north-western settlement of [[Najutte]] was brought in by force in [[1665]]. The treaty's dispute-resolution clauses were reused in subsequent agreements with [[Baros]] ([[1668]]), [[Priaman]] ([[1671]]), and [[Chinkel|Singkil]] ([[1672]]), making the contract a template for VOC diplomacy in the region.

## Significance

The Painans Contract is a landmark in the history of [[international law|colonial international law]] in the [[Malay Archipelago|Indonesian archipelago]]. Its provisions for [[mediation|conciliation]] and [[arbitrage|arbitration]] anticipated later developments in international dispute resolution and reflected the VOC's effort to impose a legal order over local politics. The contract also exemplifies the VOC's use of legal language to justify power: a self-interested colonial settlement dressed in the terms of law.

## Account from Stapel's *De Koloniën* (1931)

**The Painansch Contract** was the "short and eternal bond" (*kort en eeuwig verbont*) concluded at [[Batavia]] on [[6 July]] [[1663]] between the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] and the landheeren of [[Indrapoera]], [[Painan]], [[Padang]] and [[Tikoe|Tikoe(s)]], by which the Menangkabau rulers of the [[Westkust van Sumatra|West Coast of Sumatra]] placed themselves under the Compagnie's protection and granted it a commercial monopoly in defiance of [[Atjeh]]'s claims to suzerainty. Recorded in Dr. F. W. Stapel's *Geschiedkundige Atlas van Nederland* as the central instrument of the Westkust system for the next two decades, and as the legal foundation on which all subsequent Dutch authority in west Sumatra was built.

## Origin and negotiation

The Painansch Contract was the harvest of secret diplomacy conducted by [[Jan van Groenewegen|Jan van Groenewegen]], the Compagnie's head of trade on the Westkust, in [[1662]]. The Compagnie's commercial frustrations on the Westkust — the Atjehsche governors' tolls on Menangkabau pepper, the [[Blockade of Atjeh (1656)|1656 blockade]]'s failure, the [[Arrest of Anthony van Voorst|arrest of Van Voorst]] — had by then produced a willingness in Batavia to consider an open defection of the Westkust chiefs from Atjeh. Stapel records the order sent to Van Groenewegen: "to make use of that displeasure and, without letting the Atjehers notice anything, to draw the Menangkabauers 'from the croone of Atchin to the protection of the Compagnie'" (*van die ontstemming gebruik te maken en, zonder de Atjehers iets te laten merken, de Menangkabauers 'van de croone van Atchin tot de bescherming van de Compagnie te trecken'*).

"In the course of 1662 Van Groenewegen had with various radjas and chiefs discussions to that end, and concluded preliminary secret agreements, by which the Compagnie obtained the trade monopoly, but might not meddle in the internal affairs of the princes and chiefs" (*In den loop van 1662 had Van Groenewegen met verschillende radja's en hoofden besprekingen dienaangaande, en sloot voorloopig geheime overeenkomsten, waarbij de Compagnie het handelsmonopolie verkreeg, doch zich niet met de interne zaken der vorsten en hoofden zou mogen bemoeien*). The双边承诺包括：如果发生 Atjeh 攻击，双方将互相支持。

The Atjehers suspected. "Despite all secrecy the Atjehers got wind of it; they protested against discussions behind their back" (*Ondanks alle geheimhouding kregen de Atjehers er toch de lucht van; zij protesteerden tegen besprekingen buiten hen om*). At Atjeh itself the Compagnie's resident "noticed the displeasure; he was no longer admitted to the court" (*hij werd niet meer tot het hof toegelaten*).

## The contract: 6 July 1663

The Menangkabau envoys travelled to Batavia with Van Groenewegen. There the Hooge Regeering "on 6 July 1663 sealed, from her side, the 'short and eternal bond' with the landheeren of Indrapoera, Painan, Padang and Tikoe(s), known as the Painansch contract" (*den 6den Juli 1663 het 'kort en eeuwig verbont' met de landheeren van Indrapoera, Painan, Padang en Tikoe(s), bekend als het Painansch contract, van haar kant bezegelde*). Stapel notes a date discrepancy in the sources: "Heeres dates the contract, according to the Contractboek, on 27 July. It was however signed and ratified by the G.G. on 6 July. See the Daghregister van Batavia on that date" (*HEERES dateert het contract, volgens het Contractboek, op 27 Juli. Het werd echter door den G.G. geteekend en geratificeerd op 6 Juli. Zie het Daghregister van Batavia op dien datum*).

The Compagnie's chosen centre for the new arrangement was "Poeloe Tjingkoek in the bay of Painan" (*Poeloe Tjingkoek in de baai van Painan*). On that island, in [[1666]], a fort would be built.

## The ratification crisis of 1663-1665

The Compagnie's simultaneous withdrawal of its factories from Atjeh and [[Perak]] in [[1663]] "increased the unrest and fear among the West Coast inhabitants, especially those of Tikoe and Priaman, which lay closest to Atjeh" (*vermeerderde de onrust en vrees onder de Westkustbewoners, vooral die van Tikoe en Priaman, welke het dichtst bij Atjeh lagen*). To ratify the Painansch Contract in the chiefs' own countries the Hooge Regeering sent, on [[17 August]] [[1664]], a squadron of four well-armed ships under the commissioner [[Jacob Cau|Jacob Cau]].

Cau's instructions were cautious: "not to proceed to an open war with Atjeh except in the utmost case" (*niet dan in het uiterste geval tot een openlijken oorlog met Atjeh over te gaan*). On arrival at [[Padang]] the chiefs there complained of "the increasing oppression and vexation they suffered from the Atjehers" (*de toenemende onderdrukking en overlast, die zij van de zijde der Atjehers ondervonden*). At their request Cau rowed with part of his force to land. "From a thrown-up fortification the Atjehers tried with 2 cannon to prevent this landing, but Cau drove them out and made himself master of the cannon" (*Van uit een opgeworpen versterking trachtten de Atjehers met 2 kanonnen deze landing te beletten, doch Cau verdreef hen en maakte zich van de kanonnen meester*).

The Padang chiefs then ratified the Painansch Contract. Cau next went to [[Tikoe|Tikoe]], where "the Holland-friendly party was likewise oppressed by the Atjehers" (*de Hollandsch-gezinde partij eveneens door de Atjehers werd onderdrukt*). The weapons decided it and, after the expulsion of the Atjehers, the contract was confirmed. [[Priaman]] followed, where Cau "found no fewer than 500 to 600 armed Atjehers with a number of auxiliary troops" (*niet minder dan 500 à 600 gewapende Atjehers met een aantal hulptroepen aantrof*). The Atjehers were again expelled and the regents signed. [[Bajang]] joined voluntarily, "after which they themselves drove the Atjehers out of their land" (*waarna zij zelf de Atjehers uit hun land verdreven*).

When Cau returned to Batavia on [[2 January]] [[1665]] he had obtained the desired ratification and established four definitive factories on the Westkust: [[Padang]] (headquarters), [[Poeloe Tjingkoek|Poeloe Tjingkoek]] or [[Salido]], [[Priaman]] and [[Tikoe]].

## The expansion of the contract

The Painansch Contract was progressively extended in the following years. On [[29 April]] [[1668]] [[Jacob Jorisse Pits|Jacob Jorisse Pits]] had it signed by the chiefs of [[Baros]] — bringing the northernmost significant Westkust place into the bond. On [[19 March]] [[1672]] [[Singkel]] acceded, acknowledging the king of Menangkabau as sovereign and the Compagnie as protector. By the time of Pits's departure from the Westkust in [[1677]] the Painansch Contract embraced "all places of importance on the West Coast" (*alle plaatsen van beteekenis op de Westkust*).

## Persistence of Atjehsche influence

The Painansch Contract did not extinguish Atjehsche influence on the Westkust. Atjehers remained at various points and continued to exercise influence, "especially through intimidation" (*vooral door intimidatie*). The Compagnie found it necessary in [[1666]] to send the [[Jacob Gruys|Gruys]] expedition (which ended in the [[Battle of Paoeh (12 March 1666)|Paoeh disaster]]) and, later that year, the [[Abraham Versprect|Versprect]] expedition (which took Paoeh and burned it). Even after the contract's extension the Atjehers periodically resumed their activity, in [[1675]], [[1676]], [[1678]] and [[1683]]. The Painansch Contract was not a settlement but a continuing contest.

## Significance

The Painansch Contract is the legal foundation of the Dutch Westkust system. It is the document that, more than any other, transferred the suzerainty of the Westkust from [[Sultana of Atjeh (Taj ul-Alam)|Atjeh]] to the Compagnie, and it remained the reference point of Dutch authority there until the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]]'s dissolution at the end of the eighteenth century. The Contract's ratification campaign of [[1664]]–[[1665]] is also the first systematic use of Compagnie military force against Atjeh on the Westkust, and it set the pattern for the next two decades: Dutch military expedition, Atjehsche expulsion, ratification of the Painansch form, Atjehsche return, repeat.

In the long run the Painansch Contract marks the geopolitical end of Atjeh's Sumatran empire. The sultane's authority, which had once run unchallenged from [[Singkel]] to [[Indrapoera]], was reduced after 1663 to the territory of Atjeh proper, and the Westkust became a Dutch dependency under the nominal suzerainty of the king of Menangkabau.

**The Atjehsche suprematie over de Timoerhoogvlakte** — the Atjehnese supremacy over the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoer-Batak hoogvlakte]] — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the long period during which the four [[Simeloengoen|Simeloengoensche]] *rijkjes* of [[Silau|Silau]], [[Panei|Panel]], [[Siantar|Sintar]] and [[Si Tonggang|Si Tonggang]] acknowledged the opperhoogheid — the supreme authority — of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]]. The supremacy was, the Bureau records, the parallel of the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehsche oppermacht in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]] on the eastern side of the highlands, and it left its institutional trace in the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]] that the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoer-Batak]] *rijkjes* preserved, in altered form, into the colonial period.

## Background: the four Simeloengoensche rijkjes

The Bureau's account of the Atjehsche suprematie over the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoerhoogvlakte]] opens with the dissolution of the older [[Batak-Timoer|Batakrijk Nggeer]] — the great Batak realm that had once stretched over the whole of [[Simeloengoen|Simeloengoen]] and a great part of the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoerhoogvlakte]]. The Bureau records that the dissolution of Nggeer produced four successor-*rijkjes*: [[Silau|Silau]], [[Panei|Panel]], [[Siantar|Sintar]] and [[Si Tonggang|Si Tonggang]] — "welke, hoewel ieder onafhankelijk, toch steeds in nauwen samenhang totelkaar bleven" — "which, though each independent, nevertheless always remained in close connection with each other."

## The Atjehsche opperhoogheid

The four [[Batak-Timoer|Timoer-Batak]] *rijkjes* acknowledged, the Bureau records, the opperhoogheid of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] for "geruimen tijd" — "a considerable time." The Bureau records: "De toenemende macht van Atjeh noopte de vorsten dezer rijkjes om — evenaals dat in de Gajolanden het geval is geweest — gedurende geruimen tijd de opperhoogheid van den Sultan te erkennen" — "The increasing power of Atjeh compelled the princes of these *rijkjes* — just as was the case in the Gajolanden — to acknowledge, for a considerable time, the opperhoogheid of the Sultan." The Bureau's parallel with the [[Gajoland|Gajolanden]] is the explicit link between the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehsche oppermacht in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]] and the Atjehsche suprematie over the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoerhoogvlakte]]: the two supremacies are presented as the western and eastern faces of the same Atjehnese highland projection.

## The institutional imprint

The institutional imprint of the Atjehsche suprematie on the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoer-Batak]] *rijkjes* was, the Bureau records, the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]]. The Bureau records: "Sommige bestuursinstellingen volgens het Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem (dat ook op de Karo-hoogvlakte werd ingevoerd door Atjeh) worden nog aan dien invloed toegeschreven" — "Some administrative institutions according to the Atjehsche four-princes system (which was also introduced on the Karo hoogvlakte by Atjeh) are still attributed to that influence." The Bureau's parenthetical reference to the [[Batak-Karo|Karo hoogvlakte]] signals that the Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem had been imposed, in parallel, on both the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoerhoogvlakte]] and the [[Batak-Karo|Karo hoogvlakte]] — a uniform Atjehnese institutional arrangement across the entire Batak highland country.

## The decay of the suprematie

The Atjehsche suprematie over the [[Batak-Timoer|Timoerhoogvlakte]] decayed, the Bureau records, with the broader decay of [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] power in the highlands. The Bureau records that "De steeds afnemende macht der vorsten kon een verder uiteenvallen van het [Timoer-Batak] gebied niet meer tegenhouden" — "The steadily declining power of the princes could no longer prevent a further dissolution of the [Timoer-Batak] territory" — and that [[Silau|Silau]] split into [[Dolok|Dolok]] and [[Poerba|Poerba]], while [[Raja|Raja]] arose at the expense of [[Silau|Silau]] and [[Panei|Panel]]. The Atjehsche suprematie was, by the time of the Dutch advance into the highlands in the late nineteenth century, a memory rather than a political fact.

## Significance

The Atjehsche suprematie over de Timoerhoogvlakte is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the eastern counterpart of the [[atjehsche-oppermacht-in-de-gajo-en-alaslanden|Atjehsche oppermacht in the Gajo- and Alaslanden]]: where the latter extended Atjehnese power across the central Sumatran highlands, the former extended it across the eastern Batak highlands. The two supremacies together constitute the highland complement of the [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|Atjehsche overlordship of the East Coast statelets]], and they leave their institutional trace in the [[atjehsche-viervorsten-systeem|Atjehsche viervorsten-systeem]] that the Dutch colonial state would later revive and amplify in the [[Dutch subjugation of the Karo-Batak lands (1904-1906)|1904–1906 annexation of the Karo hoogvlakte]].

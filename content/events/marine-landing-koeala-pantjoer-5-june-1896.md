**The Marine Landing at Koeala Pantjoer / Lam-Baroe (5 June 1896)** was the amphibious assault by a Dutch marine landing division on the kampong of [[Lam-Baroe]] on the northern coast of the Atjeh residency, conducted in support of the [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|operations in the IV and VI Moekims]] of [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] to drive any Achehnese band toward the colonne of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] advancing from [[Lamtih]]. The marines — disembarked at 8:30 a.m. via a boat-flotilla — landed at [[Lam-Baroe]] without a shot being fired, talso stelling, dismantled the *versterkingen*, cleared the breastwork west of [[Lam-Toetoe]] and the *Oosterface* of the *Koeta* west of [[Indra-Perwa]], and withdrew at 11:15 a.m. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the landing was the first major amphibious operation of the June campaign and the operational prototype for the larger [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing and Glé Broeë attack]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]].

## Background

By the first week of [[June]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] and the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] was consolidating the Dutch position in the *moekim*s adjacent to the area of operations. The [[Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)|operations in the IV and VI Moekims]] of [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] were designed to clear the northern coastal stretch of the [[VI Moekims]] — the *moekim*s of [[Lam-Tengah]] and [[Lam-Pagar]] — of Achehnese bands. The plan called for a convergent advance: a 1e colonne under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] from the south, a 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] from the east, and a *marinelandingsdivisie* from the north, at [[Koeala Pantjoer]] / [[Lam-Baroe]]. The combined effect was intended to compress the Achehnese bands in the VI Moekims and to deny them any avenue of escape.

The choice of [[Koeala Pantjoer]] / [[Lam-Baroe]] as the landing site was determined by the geography of the northern coast. The kampong of [[Lam-Baroe]] lay at the mouth of a river — the [[Koeala Pantjoer]] — that gave access to the interior of the VI Moekims. The Achehnese had constructed *versterkingen* at the kampong and a breastwork west of [[Lam-Toetoe]], as well as fortifications on the *Oosterface* of the *Koeta* west of [[Indra-Perwa]]. These works controlled the coastal approaches and would have to be reduced if the marine landing was to be effective. The Dutch plan called for the *marinelandingsdivisie* to land, take stelling, dismantle the *versterkingen*, and — by threatening the Achehnese from the north — to drive any *bende* toward the colonne of Van Heutsz advancing from the east.

## The Landing

The *marinelandingsdivisie* — a division of marines trained for amphibious assault — was embarked in the warships of the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Dutch Navy]] operating off the northern coast. At 8:30 a.m. on [[5 June]] [[1896]] the marines disembarked via a boat-flotilla — a fleet of ships' boats, each capable of carrying a section of marines, rowed or sailed from the warships to the beach. The disembarkation was conducted in good order; the boats approached the beach in line; the marines waded ashore and formed up on the beach.

The Achehnese, recognising that the marine landing had turned their position, did not contest the disembarkation. No shot was fired. The marines advanced on [[Lam-Baroe]], talso stelling, and proceeded to dismantle the *versterkingen*. The *borstwering* west of [[Lam-Toetoe]] was cleared; the *Oosterface* of the *Koeta* west of [[Indra-Perwa]] was cleared. The works were dismantled — a thorough demolition that prevented their re-occupation by the Achehnese. The marines withdrew at 11:15 a.m., having accomplished their mission in less than three hours and without loss.

## The Coordination with the Land Colonne

The marine landing at [[Koeala Pantjoer]] / [[Lam-Baroe]] was coordinated with the advance of the 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], which had marched at 5 a.m. on [[5 June]] [[1896]] from [[Lamtih]] to [[Lam-Pagar]], [[Lam-Badak]], and [[Lam-Baroe]]. The plan called for the marines, by threatening the Achehnese from the north, to drive any *bende* toward the colonne of Van Heutsz, which would engage and destroy them. The Achehnese, however, declined to be driven: they withdrew to the interior, preferring to preserve their forces rather than to risk engagement with two convergent Dutch forces. The marine landing, in this sense, achieved its political and operational purpose — the demonstration of Dutch reach and the dismantling of the coastal *versterkingen* — even though it did not produce the hoped-for engagement.

## Significance

The Marine Landing at Koeala Pantjoer / Lam-Baroe on [[5 June]] [[1896]] was, in operational terms, a small affair — a three-hour disembarkation with no engagement and no casualties. In strategic and doctrinal terms, however, it was the prototype of the larger amphibious operations that would follow in the second half of June. The [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing of 22–23 June 1896]] — a two-pronged attack on the Glé Broeë stelling east of [[Kroeng-Kali]] — would repeat the pattern of [[5 June]] [[1896]] on a larger scale, with a sea-landing colonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel A. G. Hansen|Lt.-Col. Hansen]] and a land colonne under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] converging on the same objective. The marine landing at Koeala Pantjoer also demonstrated, for the first time in the June campaign, the operational integration of the [[Marine (Dutch Navy)|Dutch Navy]] and the Dutch Army in a single coordinated operation — an integration that would become the hallmark of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in its later phases. The absence of enemy resistance was, in its way, the most significant outcome of the landing: the Achehnese had recognised that the Dutch could now strike from any direction, land or sea, and that the coastal mukims were no longer a sanctuary.

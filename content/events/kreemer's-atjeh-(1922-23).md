**Kreemer's *Atjeh* (1922-23)** — full title *Atjèh: algemeen samenvattend overzicht van land en volk van Atjèh en Onderhoorigheden* ("Atjeh: a general summary overview of the land and people of Atjeh and Dependencies") — is the two-volume general survey of Acheh|Atjeh and its dependencies published by J. Kreemer|J. Kreemer in 1922-1923, which stands, alongside Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje's Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (1893-94), as one of the two principal Dutch scholarly syntheses on Atjeh as a country and people. The work is cited throughout Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956)|Kreemer's *De Karbouw* (1956)]] — and throughout the wider Atjeh scholarly literature — as "Kreemer, *Atjeh*, I/II" with specific page references, and is the source of record for a substantial proportion of the Atjeh-specific ethnographic, linguistic, religious and historical material documented in the comparative literature.

## Scope
Kreemer's *Atjèh* treats Atjeh as a territorial-administrative unit — the colonial Atjeh en Onderhorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhorigheden — and gathers together ethnographic, linguistic, religious and historical material on the Atjehnese and on the neighbouring Gajoland|Gayo and Alasland|Alas peoples of the interior. The work is, as its subtitle indicates, a "general summary overview" rather than a focused monograph: it covers land and people, language and custom, religious practice and adat, historical tradition and contemporary administration, in a synthesis that drew on Kreemer's own field experience in northern Sumatra (as a former *Kontroleur B.B.* on Sumatra's Westkust, with personal fieldwork in Atjeh proper and in the Gayo country) as well as on the published scholarly literature (principally Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje's Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893-94)|*De Atjehers*]] and Snouck Hurgronje's Het Gajoland en zijne bewoners ([[1903)|*Het Gajoland*]], G. A. J. Hazeu|Hazeu's *Gajosch-Nederlandsch Woordenboek*, and Hoesein Djajadiningrat|Djajadiningrat's *Atjehsch-Nederlandsch Woordenboek*).

- The goeroe boeja|*goeroe boeja* Atjehse crocodile-charmers (I, 188, 201).
- The Atjeh bloeitijdperk of 1607-1636 (I, 192).
- The koeta djaq|*koeta djaq* "wandelende fort" of buffalo-hide (I, 307).

- The magic circle baris-Laksamana|*baris-Laksamana* (I, 312; II, 586-587, 661 nt. 2).
- The white pennants before the stall (I, 365).
- The Poephon blang|*poephon blang* field-consecration rite (I, 471; II, 578 vg.).
- The magic stone in the rice-store (I, 475; II, 521).

- The Atjeh pantang legend of the albino-buffalo and the *andjalai* storage (I, 507).
- The Kreemer register entry on "woordverwisseling" (word-substitution).

## Significance
Kreemer's *Atjeh* (1922-23) is significant for the encyclopedia of Acheh history as, with Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje's Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (1893-94), one of the two principal Dutch scholarly syntheses on Atjeh as a country and people, and as the source of record for a substantial proportion of the Atjeh-specific ethnographic, linguistic, religious and historical material documented in the comparative literature. The work's status as the principal authority for Kreemer's own late synthesis Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956)|*De Karbouw* (1956)]] is attested by the dozens of *Atjeh*-citations throughout that book, and the work is accordingly cited throughout the Atjeh-specific entries in this wiki.

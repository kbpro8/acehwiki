**The Dismission of Teuku Oemar (26 April 1896)** — Besluit No. 159 of the acting [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] — was the formal act by which the [[Dutch]] colonial government dismissed [[Teuku Umar]] — *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*, [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government — from his twin posts as [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government and as territorial chief of [[Lepong]], with the stipulation that he would henceforth be officially designated by his original name *Toekoe Oemar* — a symbolic stripping of the honours conferred by the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]]. Authorised by Government Telegram of [[26 April]] [[1896]], No. 409, the dismissal was the official response to the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of approximately [[26 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] and the legal foundation for the [[Proclamation of Vetter (27 April 1896)|proclamation of 27 April 1896]] announcing military operations against the former ally.

## Background

The [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[Teuku Umar]] on approximately [[26 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] had been answered, on [[30 March]] [[1896]], by Umar's [[First Letter of Teuku Umar|first letter]] to the [[Governor of Atjeh]] — a letter setting out his grievances (chiefly, alleged insults by the *Controleur te Oleh-leh* and the *hoofddjaksa* [[Mohamad Arief]]) and his demands (a Royal decoration — "kruis" — and a monthly subsidy of ƒ150,000). The [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar|second letter]] of [[12 April]] [[1896]] had repeated and amplified the demands. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], had arrived on [[7 April]] [[1896]], had ordered the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]], and had presided over the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of the two posts]] on [[12 April]] [[1896]]. The strategic situation had been stabilised; the political response was now required.

The legal instrument chosen was a Government Resolution of the acting [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] — at this point the civil-military government was being exercised provisionally, with [[Resident K. F. H. van Langen|Resident van Langen]] in charge of civil affairs and [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] in charge of military command, under the supreme authority of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]]. The resolution was numbered 159 and was issued under the authority of the [[Government Resolution of 5 September 1893]] — the same resolution that had conferred the titles on Umar in [[September]] [[1893]] and that the new resolution now formally revoked. The dismissal was authorised by Government Telegram of [[26 April]] [[1896]], No. 409 — a telegram from [[Batavia]] conveying the [[Gouverneur-Generaal Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General]]'s authorisation for the act.

## The Resolution

The Besluit No. 159 of [[26 April]] [[1896]] — preserved in the Bijlagen of *ATJEH '96* — declared, in substance: first, that [[Teuku Umar]] was dismissed from the post of [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the Government of [[Nederlandsch-Indië]] — a post he had held since the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|confirmation act]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]]; second, that he was dismissed from the post of *hoeloebalang* of [[Lepong]] — a post that, while hereditary in Achehnese customary law, had been formally recognised by the Dutch government in [[1893]] and was now formally withdrawn; third, that he would henceforth be officially designated by his original name *Toekoe Oemar* — a symbolic stripping of the honour name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* conferred by the [[Government Resolution of 5 September 1893]].

The act was, in legal terms, the formal unwinding of the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]]. In political terms it was the public declaration that the [[Dutch]] government no longer regarded Umar as an ally, a *hoeloebalang*, or a *Panglima Prang Besar* — that he was, henceforth, simply an enemy chief, to be dealt with by military means.

## The Proclamation of 27 April 1896

The dismission of [[26 April]] [[1896]] was followed, on [[27 April]] [[1896]], by the [[Proclamation of Vetter (27 April 1896)|proclamation of the Regeeringscommissaris]] announcing military operations against the former ally. The proclamation — addressed to the population of Atjeh and to the *hoeloebalang* of the mukims — declared that the Government, having dismissed Umar from his posts, would now undertake military operations against him; that those who had cooperated with Umar could expect protection; and that those who continued to follow him would be treated as enemies. The proclamation is preserved, in Dutch, in the Bijlagen of *ATJEH '96*.

## The Aftermath

The dismission of [[26 April]] [[1896]] and the proclamation of [[27 April]] [[1896]] inaugurated the offensive phase of the spring campaign. On [[27 April]] [[1896]] the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] began. On [[28 April]]–[[29 April]] [[1896]] the [[Night Surprise of Benting Batoe (28–29 April 1896)|night surprise of Benting Batoe]] was executed. On [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]] the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai]] was conducted. On [[2 May]] [[1896]] the [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik]] was mounted. On [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] was launched, culminating in the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang and the destruction of Umar's house]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]]. The dismission of [[26 April]] [[1896]] was the legal foundation on which all of these operations rested.

## Significance

The Dismission of Teuku Oemar on [[26 April]] [[1896]] was the formal political response of the [[Dutch]] colonial government to the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. It was, in legal terms, the unwinding of the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]] — the dissolution of the native-allied policy of [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] and the formal acknowledgment that the [[1893]]–[[1896]] experiment in war-by-proxy had failed. In political terms it was the symbolic stripping of the honour name *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan* — a public humiliation that, while it could not undo the defection, marked the Government's definitive repudiation of its former ally. In military terms it was the legal prerequisite for the [[Proclamation of Vetter (27 April 1896)|proclamation of 27 April 1896]] and the offensive that followed. The dismission of [[26 April]] [[1896]] was, in this sense, the political pivot of the first semester of [[1896]]: the moment at which the Dutch government ceased to regard Umar as a wayward ally and began, instead, to regard him as an enemy to be destroyed.

**Capture of the Aceh Kraton** was the historical military capture and occupation of [[Kraton of Aceh]]—the fortified royal palace city of the [[Aceh Sultanate]]—by troops of the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] under General [[J. van Swieten]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]].

## Narrative of the Final Approach and Assault

Throughout January [[1874]], Dutch columns advancing from [[Pekan Badak]] and [[Sinangri]] slowly tightened their encirclement around the royal palace. Following the [[Capture of Kotta Alam]], engineering detachments constructed covered sap approaches toward the western face of the palace walls.

On morning of [[24 January]] [[1874]], Dutch artillery commenced a final bombardment of the kraton's ramparts. When infantry assault waves of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] breached the gates, they discovered that Sultan [[Sultan Mahmud Syah of Aceh]] and his royal court had silently evacuated the palace during the night, withdrawing into the interior mukims.

## Aftermath and Historical Controversy

Standing in the deserted palace, General [[J. van Swieten]] formally proclaimed the dissolution of the [[Aceh Sultanate]] and transmitted the [[General van Swieten War Telegrams]] to [[Batavia]], reporting that the war was ended.

However, as documented in *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* ([[1876]]), resistance leaders [[Panglima Polem]], [[Teuku Imam Lueng Batoh]], and [[Teuku Nanta Setia]] refused to submit. Furthermore, overcrowding inside the captured palace triggered the [[Field Hospital and Cholera Epidemics in Aceh]], inflicting severe casualties on the garrison before General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] dispersed troops to riverbank barracks at [[Pantei Perak]].

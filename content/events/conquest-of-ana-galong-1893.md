**The Conquest of Ana' Galong (30 November 1893)** was the storming and capture of the Achehnese fortification of [[Ana' Galong]] by the *legioen* of [[Teuku Umar]] — then operating as *Toekoe Djohan Pahalawan*, [[Panglima Prang Besar]] of the [[Dutch]] Government — and the most spectacular early success of the allied-native policy inaugurated by Umar's [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]]. Mounted scarcely two months after Umar received his commission, the conquest demonstrated the operational value of the [[Legioen van T. Djohan]] and helped persuade the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]], [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]], to entrust Umar with ever-wider responsibilities — including, on [[21 October]] [[1893]], the leadership of the affairs of the [[XXVI Moekims]] *sagi*. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the conquest also marked the limit of Umar's westward advance: he refused, on grounds of superstition, to push further toward [[Lamkrak]].

## Background

In the autumn of [[1893]] the colonial government was eager to demonstrate that its new policy of arming native auxiliaries was producing results. Umar's *legioen*, only weeks old, was thrown against the nearest still-defiant Achehnese strongpoint: the *benteng* of [[Ana' Galong]], which stood in the western approaches to the [[XXVI Moekims]] and constituted one of the principal Achehnese fortifications beyond the Dutch [[buitenlinie]]. Umar had been given leadership of the affairs of the [[XXVI Moekims]] on [[21 October]] [[1893]], and the conquest of Ana' Galong was to be his first major test as *Panglima Prang Besar*.

The *benteng* itself was a substantial earthwork with bamboo-thorn (*bamboedoeri*) obstructions and the typical Achehnese system of double palisades, designed to slow an assaulting column while the defenders poured fire through loopholes at near-point-blank range. Its capture by native auxiliaries — without Dutch infantry in the assault line — would be a powerful advertisement for the [[Legioen van T. Djohan]].

## The Assault

On [[30 November]] [[1893]] Umar led his *legioen* of approximately 250 warriors, supported by his forty *panglima's* and the three blood-relative commanders authorised by the [[Government Resolution of 5 September 1893]], against the *benteng*. The assault followed the pattern of Achehnese siege-craft: a short preparatory fire from the legion's Dutch-supplied rifles, followed by a rush at the defences under cover of the *bamboedoeri*, and finally hand-to-hand fighting inside the enclosure. The *benteng* was carried. Kruisheer does not enumerate the casualties on either side but stresses that the victory was clean, decisive, and immediately hailed by the colonial press as a vindication of Deykerhoff's policy.

In the immediate aftermath Umar did not, however, advance further. The road to [[Lamkrak]] lay open; but Umar held back. Kruisheer records that superstition stayed his hand: a prophecy current among the [[Achehnese]] foretold that any chief who entered [[Lamkrak]] would die there. Whatever the deeper reasons — and Umar's reluctance to commit his newly raised force to the most dangerous theatre of the war may have weighed equally — the consequence was that [[Lamkrak]] remained unsubdued for the next two and a half years. The [[Lamkrak]] mukims would only be reduced by the major [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|Dutch punitive expedition of 9–10 June 1896]].

## Significance

The conquest of Ana' Galong had three significant consequences. First, it cemented Umar's reputation as the Government's most effective native auxiliary and emboldened Deykerhoff to extend his authority across the [[XXVI Moekims]] and, later, into the [[XXII Moekims]]. Second, by leaving [[Lamkrak]] untouched, it allowed the [[Lamkrak]] mukims to consolidate as the principal Achehnese redoubt in the western *sagi* — a position from which they would besiege [[Senelop]] and [[blokhuis Aloer Oe]] in the spring of [[1896]]. Third, it handed Umar a credential — the storming of a major *benteng* by his own *legioen* — that he would later deploy, in his [[First Letter of Teuku Umar|first letter]] of [[30 March]] [[1896]], as proof that he had served the Government loyally and at considerable personal cost before his [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]]. The post at [[Ana' Galong]] would become, by the end of [[1895]], one of the most exposed Dutch positions on the western *buitenlinie*, the site of the celebrated [[Patrol of Captain Van Blokland (7 March 1896)|patrol of Captain Van Blokland]] on [[7 March]] [[1896]].

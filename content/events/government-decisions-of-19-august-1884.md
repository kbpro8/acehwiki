**The government decisions of 19 August 1884** were the decisions taken by the Gouverneur-Generaal van Nederlandsch-Indië on [[19 August]] [[1884]], by which the experiment of the civil government of [[Atjeh]] was ended and the military authority restored and concentrated. The decision followed the discharge requested by the civil Gouverneur [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]] and is recorded, with its criticism, by [[W. A. Coblijn|Coblijn]] in *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]*.

## The contents of the decisions

By the decisions of 19 August 1884:

- the *civiele bestuur* (civil administration) of Atjeh was abolished;
- kolonel [[Demmenie|Demmeni]] was named military governor (*militair gouverneur*) of Atjeh;
- the *controleurs* (district officers) in [[Groot-Atjeh]] were abolished;
- the functions of assistent-resident of the [[Meulaboh|Westkust (Meulaboh)]] and of controleur of [[Sigli|Segli]] were transferred to the military commanders;
- the posts of [[Lho' Seumawe|Telok Semawé]] and [[Samalanga]] were withdrawn, and the troops *geconcentreerd* (concentrated).

Under the energetic and able officer Demmeni a more concentrated position was thus taken up — the system that became the *[[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentratie-stelsel]]* of 1884–1896, with its *[[Geconcentreerde linie|geconcentreerde linie]]* and *[[Ceintuur-trambaan|ceintuurbaan]]*.

## Coblijn's criticism

Coblijn judged that the concentration in itself was not at all indefensible: even he, in his earlier *geschrift* *De Indische Infanterie*, had most strongly disapproved of the withdrawal into a more concentrated position in another case, but in Atjeh, given the means, it might serve. His reproach was addressed to the *manner*: that the position had been given too defensive a character, that the troops in Atjeh had been cut down again, while tons of gold were spent to make the concentrated position as complete as possible — a ceintuurbaan was laid, the necessary railway material and luxurious barracks acquired, the telegraph carried everywhere — that the withdrawn posts had sacrificed the moral hold on the population, and that the thousand-metre cleared strip along the line had achieved precisely the *versinning* (estrangement) of the population it was designed to prevent.

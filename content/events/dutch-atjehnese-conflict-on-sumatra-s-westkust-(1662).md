**The Dutch-Atjehnese conflict on Sumatra's Westkust of 1662** was the military operation by which the [[Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie|V.O.C.]], taking the part of the Sultan of Menangkabau against the Sultan of Atjeh, dispatched **Pieter de Bitter** with a fleet to Sumatra's Westkust, conquered [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] and the kingdom of [[Padang|Padang]] from the Atjehnese, and established Padang as the centre of Dutch authority on Sumatra's Westkust. The episode is recorded in [[Dr. W. A. Terwogt|Terwogt]]'s *[[Het Land van Jan Pieterszoon Coen (Terwogt)|Het Land van Jan Pieterszoon Coen]]* as the operation by which the Dutch, "van handelslichaam zonder het te willen, maar door de omstandigheden gedwongen steeds breeder tot staatsmacht zich ontwikkelende" — as a trading body, without willing it, but forced by circumstances to develop ever more broadly into a state power — extended their authority on Sumatra's Westkust at the expense of the Atjehnese.

## Background

On Sumatra's South-East coast, the Dutch already had a firm foothold at [[Palembang|Palembang]], and on the Westkust a factory at [[Padang|Padang]]. Their power there — and thereby their ability to make the trade they drove into a *alleenhandel* (monopoly) and to extend it ever further — was hampered by the Sultan of Atjeh. Gradually, Sumatra's Westkust had come, as far as [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]], under the influence of Atjeh, "zeer ten nadeele van onzen handel" (very much to the detriment of our trade). Hence a hostile disposition toward Atjeh, which sought the first opportunity to break the influence and power of that kingdom.

## The alliance with Menangkabau

The Dutch sought, therefore, to persuade the heads of various places, by the prospect of profits to be gained, to enter into an alliance with them — which succeeded in [[1662]]. They placed themselves under the protection of the Dutch Compagnie. The Sultan of Menangkabau, his power curtailed by the Sultan of Atjeh, called in the help of the Compagnie. The Compagnie — "van handelslichaam zonder het te willen, maar door de omstandigheden gedwongen steeds breeder tot staatsmacht zich ontwikkelende" — seized the opportunity with both hands. It chose the side of the Sultan of Menangkabau: he was the *opperheer* (overlord), and the Sultan of Atjeh was *in zijn onrecht* (in the wrong). It sent Pieter de Bitter with some ships to Sumatra's Westkust, but at the same time demanded, as its reward, that all the *zeeplaatsen* (coastal places) that would be conquered from the Atjehnese should belong to the Compagnie.

## The conquest

[[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]] and the kingdom of [[Padang|Padang]] came thereby into Dutch power, and Padang itself soon became the *middelpunt* (centre) for the Dutch authority on Sumatra's Westkust. This did not, however, go *voetstoots* (without a struggle). "De Atjehers verweerden zich, evenals hunne naneven te dezer tijd, met groote dapperheid en brachten hunnen vijanden zoo groote verliezen toe, dat de onzen meermalen genoodzaakt waren versterking aan te vragen te Batavia" — the Atjehnese defended themselves, like their kinsmen of this time, with great bravery, and inflicted such great losses on their enemies that the Dutch were repeatedly compelled to request reinforcement from Batavia.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the Dutch-Atjehnese conflict on Sumatra's Westkust of [[1662]] is significant as the 17th-century episode by which the V.O.C., taking the part of the Sultan of Menangkabau against the Sultan of Atjeh, conquered Indrapoera and Padang from the Atjehnese and established Padang as the centre of Dutch authority on Sumatra's Westkust. The episode is a key moment in the long Dutch-Atjehnese rivalry on Sumatra's Westkust — a rivalry that would continue, in various forms, through the 18th and 19th centuries and that would culminate, after the [[Padri War|Padri-oorlog]] and the [[Michiels's campaign against the Atjehnese on Sumatra's Westkust (1839-1840)|Michiels campaign of 1839–1840]], in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]–[[1891]]. The episode likewise illustrates the V.O.C.'s transformation from a *handelslichaam* (trading body) into a *staatsmacht* (state power) — a transformation driven, as [[Dr. W. A. Terwogt|Terwogt]] notes, by circumstances rather than by design.

**The Pidië Expedition** of [[1897]]–[[1898]] was one of the great successes of the glorious period of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] — a military expedition under Van Heutsz that ended the existence of the two Pidiësche federations, **Pidië-Ndjöng** and **Keumangan**, which had divided the Pidiësche landschappen. It is the first-named of the expeditions and excursions that [[Musket]] lists in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]), and represents the offensive phase of Dutch consolidation over [[Aceh|Atjèh]] during the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Atjèh-oorlog]].

## The Pidië federations before the expedition

Before the expedition, the [[Pidië|Pidië]] region was divided between two federations of landschappen:

1. **Pidië-Ndjöng** — a federation headed by the Pakèh-geslacht of Pidië and the Ndjöng landschap
2. **Keumangan** — a federation headed by the Béntara of Keumangan, which led a six-Oelèëbalang federation against the Pidië-Ndjöng federation

The Pidië region had been briefly a sultanate before c. [[1521]], then governed by the Sultan of Atjèh's *stedehouders* (lieutenants). The federation of XII Moekim's was headed by the Pakèh-geslacht.

## The great successes of the glorious period

In Merac's framing, the Pidië Expedition opens the catalogue of Van Heutsz's "great successes": "the time also of our great successes! The Pidië expedition, the expeditions in Idi and Peusangan, the Pasé expedition, the Samalanga expedition, the expeditions to the Gayo and Alas lands, the action in the Tangsé and Geumpang area and on the North Coast of Atjeh, the patrol actions on the always restless West Coast, and finally the Battle of Batèë-Ilië, by which the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good." The Pidië Expedition is the first-named of these — the first of the expeditions and excursions of the glorious period of Van Heutsz.

## The expedition

The expedition under Van Heutsz in [[1897]]–[[1898]] broke the power of both federations. This was part of Van Heutsz's broader strategy, developed in consultation with [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]], of using military force to consolidate Dutch control while replacing the earlier 6- and 18-article declarations with the new three-article *[[Korte Verklaring|korte Atjèhverklaring]]*. The expedition followed the defection of Teukoe Oemar on [[29 March]] [[1896]], which had opened the way for an all-out Dutch offensive in Pidië.

## Consequences

The end of the Pidiësche federations paved the way for the extension of Dutch direct rule over the Pidië region. The Pakèh of Pidië, **T. Radja Pakèh Moehamat Ali**, came under the voogdij (guardianship) of his uncle in [[1896]] and became self-governing after [[1914]]. The expedition also cleared the way for the construction of the [[Construction of the Atjeh-tram (1875–1914)|Atjèh-tram]] mountain line from Seulimeum to Padang Tidji, which crossed the watershed into the Pidië plain.

## Significance

The Pidië Expedition is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the first-named of the great successes of the glorious period of Van Heutsz. The book's argument — that the great successes of the glorious period were the expeditions and excursions by which the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good — is the frame for the offensive phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. In Kreemer's account, the expedition is also the moment at which the indigenous federations of Pidië — which had structured the region's politics since before the Sultanate's absorption of Pidië in the early sixteenth century — were finally dissolved and replaced by Dutch direct rule. The Pidië Expedition is therefore the book's — and the colonial memory's — first great success of the glorious period.

## Background: the flight to Pidië

In his column area, the *[[sagi]]* of the [[XXII Moekims]] — the two other columns operating from [[Lho' Nga]] and [[Tjòt Mantjang]], van Heutsz himself from [[Indrapoeri]] — van Heutsz attacked the positions of the great resistance leader [[Panglima Pòlém]], the *sagi*-head, and drove him to [[Pidië]]. The column at Lho' Nga attacked [[Teukoe Oemar]] and drove him from [[Leupoeëng]], but his bands returned repeatedly, so that it lasted until [[1898]] before T. Oemar likewise withdrew to Pidië and joined Panglima Pòlém and the pretender-sultan [[Toeankoe Mochamad Dawot]]. From that moment Pidië was the hearth of the resistance.

## The new course

From the first day van Heutsz set the new course with a firm hand. On [[1 June]] [[1898]] he entered the Pidië district at the head of a force of four battalions with auxiliary arms. On that same day, after a bold storm — *"The whole line forward!"* — the resistance centre [[Garot]] fell, where the pretender-sultan, Teukoe Oemar, Panglima Pòlém and also [[Teungkoe di Tjòt Plieng]], head of the *[[oelama]]* party, had gathered. From that moment their rest and their plans were finished: they were attacked, hunted and surprised everywhere.

The attackers now fought in a wholly different manner. "Seek cover and above all take no wounded" had had its day; the offensive and the *[[klewang]]* came to the fore, the bulletproof little forts were demolished, the parapets of posts and bivouacs were replaced by barbed wire, patrols and ambushes prevented the nocturnal shootings; *"thuis brengen"* became far too dangerous against so mobile and aggressive a force, and base and line of operations became obsolete concepts.

[[J. C. Lamster|Lamster]] described the consequence: independent columns could now operate at full strength against the enemy for days at a time, up to a full week, without having to fritter away part of their force on a normally idle line of operations. Later, the days a column acted uninterruptedly became weeks and the weeks months. Provisions were taken for only five or six days and the rest bought on the way or, in wholly hostile territory, foraged. When [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]], the unsurpassed *marechaussée* leader of those days, finally undertook his famous march into the unknown interior of the [[Gajòlanden|Gajò]] and [[Alaslanden|Alas]] country, he marched with a column of 200 *marechaussées* provided with only six days' provisions over the passes of the [[Boekit Barisan|Barisan]] ranges, and although he met extremely vigorous and fanatical resistance he operated with his numerically small column for five and a half months almost entirely independently.

## Through Pidië

Van Heutsz crossed and re-crossed Pidië. The stubbornly defended *[[benteng]]s* [[Koeta Poetoih]] of [[Teungkoe di Tjòt Plieng]] were taken; the bands of the other resistance leaders were relentlessly hunted; T. Oemar had to withdraw to the mountains and chose as his residence the unreachable [[Tangsé]].

But for van Heutsz and his men nothing was unreachable. First they settled accounts with [[Teungkoe Tapa']], a religious zealot from the [[Gajòland]] who had thrown the [[Oostkust van Atjeh|east coast]] into commotion — accomplished, to the Teungkoe's detriment, in a few days, and so conclusively that this religious leader could reach the safer [[Westkust van Atjeh|west coast]] only with great difficulty and with a mere handful of followers.

Then came Tangsé's turn, protected by the strongly fortified and apparently impregnable gorge in the [[Kroeëng Beuniët]]. That produced the famous turning movement to the left through the impassable terrain around the gorge, through downpours, primeval forest and ravines, "towards the unreachable unknown." It was a gamble, a specimen of daring and unshakeable self-confidence, and it succeeded: Tangsé was reached and occupied, and Teukoe Oemar too took his threatened body and his followers to the west coast.

It cost van Heutsz his shoes: he returned barefoot to [[Sigli]] on [[28 August]] [[1898]], held a reception at [[Koeta Radja]] on [[31 August]], returned to Pidië and had one of the most dangerous of his opponents, [[Teukoe Béntara Gloempang Pajoeng]], lifted by the *marechaussée*. "In the space of four months Pidië was subjected."

## Significance (du Croo, 1943)

The Pidië campaign was the demonstration in the field of the doctrine van Heutsz had argued in *[[The Subjection of Atjeh (1892)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* and Snouck Hurgronje in his report: that a freedom-loving, warlike people submits only under compulsion, and that only he who shows he has the power to make his will respected will bring Atjeh to submission. It established the operational method — self-contained, fast-moving, independent columns living off the country — that the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* would use throughout the pacification years under [[H. N. A. Swart]].

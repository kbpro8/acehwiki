**Peusangan and Pasai Campaign of 1899** was a military sweep executed in [[1899]] by Dutch colonial forces under Major-General [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] along the northern coast of Aceh.

## Narrative of the Operation

Building on the victories of the [[Pidie Expedition of 1898]], Major-General Van Heutsz extended military pacification eastward into the coastal districts of [[Peusangan]] and [[Pasai]]. These regions were vital trade hubs where Acehnese leaders exported pepper to Penang and imported illicit munitions.

The campaign relied heavily on the newly expanded [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]], which had been reorganized under [[Governementsbesluit of 3 March 1899]] into 5 mobile divisions. Marechaussee patrols under veteran officers such as Captain [[Karel van der Maaten]] and Captain [[M. W. E. van Bloemen Waanders]] conducted night raids and forest ambushes, cutting off insurgent supply routes.

Faced with relentless pursuit, the local rulers of Peusangan and Pasai capitulated, bringing the northern coastal trade under Dutch customs control and naval blockade.

## Historical Significance

The subjugation of Peusangan and Pasai severely weakened the financial foundation of the Acehnese resistance, demonstrating the strategic value of combining naval coastal blockades with deep inland Marechaussee patrols.

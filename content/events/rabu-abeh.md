**Rabu Abeh** ("the final Wednesday") is the Achehnese name for the last Wednesday of the Mohammedan month of **Sapha** (Arabic *Ṣafar*), observed throughout [[Acheh]] as a day of bathing, charmed water, and social gatherings. The observance belongs to a widespread Mohammedan belief that the month of Ṣafar is pregnant with evil, and that one may feel very thankful upon reaching its final Wednesday without mishap. In Acheh, as in [[Java]] and the [[Malay Peninsula|Malay peninsula]] (where the parallel festival is called *Mandi Safar*), the day passes nowhere wholly without notice.

## Origin and Traditional Meaning

The reason for the ill-omened character of Ṣafar has been stated to be that in this month the fatal sickness of [[Prophet Mohammad|Mohammad]], to which he succumbed in the third month of the year, first began to show itself. However that may be, the belief in the malignity of Ṣafar is universal in the Mohammedan world. The Achehnese call the month *bulèën apuy* ("fire-month") in reference to these adat-rules, a name peculiar to Acheh; Hurgronje suggests that there may lurk here a further allusion to the dances of the [[Hasan and Husain|Hasan-Husain]] feasters round fires, as practised to this day in [[Trunion|Trunion]] and in the [[Deccan|Deccan]].

## The Bath and Charmed Water

On Rabu Abeh many Achehnese take a ritual bath. The dwellers on the coast bathe in the sea, others in the river or at the well. It is considered desirable to use for this bath water consecrated by contact with certain verses of the Quran. To this end a [[teungku]] in the [[gampong]] gives to all who ask slips of paper on which he has written the seven verses of the Quran in which Allah addresses certain men with the word *salām* ("blessing" or "peace") — namely Chapter 36:58; 37:77, 109, 120, 130; 39:73 and 97:5. These papers are thrown into sea, river, or well, and the water is thereby believed to acquire salutary powers. Others drink water from a platter on which these verses are inscribed, the writing being partially dissolved in the water. The bath taken on this occasion is a ritual one (*ghusl*) and is preceded by the utterance of the *niyyat* or intention to perform a task prescribed by the law of Allah.

## Picnics and Devotional Observance

With the bathing are connected other regulations in regard to the toilet — shaving, cutting of nails, and the like — but the Achehnese do not pay much attention to these. Those who live near the sea-shore are especially fond of the Rabu Abeh picnics. Each participant brings his contribution (*ripèu*) for the feast, which exhibits not the smallest trace of its religious origin. These social gatherings are called *meuramiën*. In Java also these picnics generally take place in seaside localities. The common people know no more than that this "Final Wednesday" is appointed for bathing, drinking charmed water, and holding social gatherings, and do not concern themselves with the traditional origin of the custom; such is also the case in [[Arabia|Arabia]]. Some pious persons perform on the afternoon of the Rabu Abeh a special voluntary *seumayang* consisting of two or more divisions, on the ground of a tradition characterized as "weak" by the expounders of the law.

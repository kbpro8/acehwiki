**Sultan Ali Moegajat Sjah's conquests / founding of Groot-Atjeh** (early 16th century) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. The second underking (stadhouder) of Atjeh under Pidië, known by the royal name Sultan Ali Moegajat Sjah, set aside his father the stadhouder, then secured his rear by conquering the nearby Westkust kingdom of Daja, then in almost one breath conquered virtually the whole Noord- and Oostkust. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to early [[16th century]].

## Account

The second underking (stadhouder) of Atjeh under [[Pidië]], known by the royal name [[Sultan Ali Moegajat Sjah]], set aside his father the stadhouder, then secured his rear by conquering the nearby [[Westkust]] kingdom of [[Daja]], then in almost one breath conquered virtually the whole Noord- and [[Atjeh's Oostkust (East Coast of Aceh)|Oostkust]]. His successors followed in his footsteps but could not extend further. The unified grip on Atjeh was achieved and would persist.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

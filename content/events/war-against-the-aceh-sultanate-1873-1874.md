**War against the Aceh Sultanate (1873–1874)** was a Dutch colonial military campaign launched to subjugate the independent sultanate of [[Atjeh]]; it marked the opening phase of the protracted **Aceh War** and generated a series of royal decorations for members of the [[Korps Mariniers (Netherlands)]]. The campaign is recorded in the award list of the Dutch marines, notably the honour bestowed on [[H. B. Stenfert]] for his role in the 1873‑1874 operations.

## Campaign Overview

The Dutch government, seeking to secure the strategic Strait of Malacca, dispatched expeditionary forces in [[1873]] and [[1874]]. The operations involved amphibious landings, inland marches, and engagements with Acehnese regulars and tribal levies. The source (page 185) lists the following participants:

* **H. B. Stenfert** – awarded the [[Ridder Militaire Willemsorde]] for distinguished conduct during the 1873‑1874 actions.  
* Several other officers received honourable mentions for their service in the same period, though the primary focus of the source is on Stenfert’s award.

## Outcomes

Although the Dutch achieved limited territorial gains, the campaign set the stage for a long‑lasting conflict that would continue until [[1904]]. The awarding of the Military Order of William underscored the perceived importance of the early victories.

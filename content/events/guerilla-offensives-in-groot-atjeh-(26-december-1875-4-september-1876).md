**The guerilla offensives in Groot-Atjeh between [[26 December]] [[1875]] and [[4 September]] [[1876]]** were the continuous series of attacks, ambushes, assaults and raids which the [[Atjeh]]nese enemy carried out against the Dutch posts, patrols and convoys in [[Groot-Atjeh]] during and after the seventy-day campaign of [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]]. They were described in detail, on the basis of the official field reports, by [[Kolonel Meijer|H. F. Meijer]] in his book *[[Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876]]* ([[1883]]).

## Background

When General Pel began his offensive on 26 December [[1875]], the Dutch troops conquered and occupied the whole [[Sagi]] of the [[XXV Moekims]] and then advanced into the [[XXVI Moekims]]. Hardly had the operating force left one district, however, than the enemy — perfectly informed of the weakness of the Dutch position — returned, settled between and behind the Dutch posts and began to attack patrols, transports and fortifications. The [[Guerilla warfare in Groot-Atjeh|guerilla war]] that followed cost the Netherlands Indies Army (*Indische leger*) a long series of bloody incidents, most of which Meijer attributed to carelessness, poor vigilance and neglect of the elementary rules of field security by troops who, in [[1876]], consisted in large part of young, badly trained soldiers.

## Chronology of the principal incidents

### During Pel's campaign (December 1875 - March 1876)

- [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1876]] — the [[Attack on Lemboeq N.O. (23-24 January 1876)|night attack on Lemboeq N.O.]]: about a hundred Atjehers surprised the post; four of the garrison were killed, among them the young officer [[Regensburge]], and twenty-two were wounded.
- [[8 February]] [[1876]] — the [[Grass-fire attack on Lamprit (8 February 1876)|grass-fire attack on Lamprit]]: the enemy set the dry *alang-alang* grass alight around the post.
- [[13 February]] [[1876]] — the [[Atoeh catastrophe (13 February 1876)|Atoeh catastrophe]]: the detachment of Captain [[Jan van Swieten (Kapitein)|J. M. E. Van Swieten]], 47 armed and 13 unarmed men, was destroyed on the sawah near [[Lamsajoen]].
- [[17 February]] [[1876]] — the [[Lamkoenjit ambush (17 February 1876)|ambush near Lamkoenjit]]: a detachment of 13 men between Biloel and [[Boekit Daroe]] was attacked by an overwhelming force.
- [[4 March]] [[1876]] — the [[Ambush of the Buijs detachment (4 March 1876)|ambush of Buijs' detachment]] in the narrows between Lamsajoeng and Tjot Bēhēl: 1 killed and 19 wounded.
- [[6 March|6]]–[[7 March]] [[1876]] — the [[Bugis couriers from Kajoe-Lē (6 March 1876)|escape of the two Bugis couriers]] from besieged Kajoe-Lē, followed by the [[Storming of Lambaroe (7 March 1876)|storming of Lambaroe]] by the column of [[Kolonel Meijer|Lieutenant-Colonel H. F. Meijer]].

### The consolidation period (March - September 1876)

- [[21 March]] [[1876]] — the [[Convoy ambush near Atoë (21 March 1876)|ambush of Van Heeckeren's convoy]] near Atoë; the following night the new bivouac at [[Night attack on Atoë Noord (22-23 March 1876)|Atoë Noord was attacked]].
- [[7 April]] [[1876]] — the [[Koewala Pantjoer patrol ambush (7 April 1876)|ambush at kampong Lambaroe]] of the patrol returning from Koewala Pantjoer: five of six wounded men died within hours.
- [[16 April]] [[1876]] — the [[Oleh Karang convoy ambush (16 April 1876)|convoy ambush between Kota Alam and Oleh Karang]].
- [[21 April|21]]–[[22 April]] [[1876]] — the [[Night attack on Lamprit (21-22 April 1876)|night attack on Lamprit]], in which the enemy slaughtered the forced-labourers sleeping in the kitchen outside the work.
- [[2 May|2]]–[[3 May]] [[1876]] — the [[Night attack on Lampagar (2-3 May 1876)|night attack on Lampagar]], one of the saddest events of the war: the post commander Captain [[Hoynck van Papendrecht (Kapitein)|Hoynck van Papendrecht]] and Lieutenant [[Van de Roemer]] were killed inside their own undefended camp.
- [[8 May]] [[1876]] — the [[Arrest of Mat Saïd (8 May 1876)|arrest of Mat Saïd]], the kampong head of Ketapan Doewa, on suspicion of collusion with the enemy.
- [[13 May|13]]–[[15 May]] [[1876]] — the [[Biloel Zuid operation (13-15 May 1876)|march of the three columns to Biloel Zuid]] and the founding of that post beneath the mountains; on [[24 May]] [[1876]] Captain [[Kapitein Lucas (1876)|Lucas]] [[Capture of the gun position near Biloel Zuid (24 May 1876)|captured the enemy gun position]] above it.
- [[28 May|28]]–[[29 May]] [[1876]] — [[Night attacks on Lamara N.O. and Lamara Z.O. (28-29 May 1876)|night attacks on Lamara N.O. and Lamara Z.O.]].
- [[1 June|1]]–[[3 June]] [[1876]] — the [[Voigt expedition to the IV Moekims (1-3 June 1876)|expedition of Major Voigt]] against the band of [[Toekoe Tjihiq Lamngga]], and the enemy's answer: the [[Surprise of the west hill-post at Biloel Zuid (2-3 June 1876)|surprise of the west hill-post at Biloel Zuid]] in the night of [[2 June|2]]–[[3 June]].
- [[18 June]] [[1876]] — the [[Ambush between Oleh Karang and Kota Alam (18 June 1876)|ambush of the fouraging detachment of Sergeant-major Deingens]].
- [[8 July|8]]–[[15 July]] [[1876]] — the [[Sweep of the IV and VI Moekims (8-15 July 1876)|sweep of the IV and VI Moekims]] and the founding of the post at [[Kroeëng Raba]].
- [[14 July]] [[1876]] — the [[Ambush between Pango and Kota Alam (14 July 1876)|destruction of Ribbink's fouraging detachment]] between Pango and Kota Alam.
- [[17 July]] [[1876]] — the [[Ambush between Boekit Daroe and Ketapan Doewa (17 July 1876)|ambush of the escort between Boekit Daroe and Ketapan Doewa]].
- [[25 July]] [[1876]] — the [[Reconnaissance to Lepong (25 July 1876)|reconnaissance to Lepong]] by Major [[Majoor Diepenheim|Diepenheim]].
- [[28 July]] [[1876]] — the [[Ambush between Kota Alam and Oleh Karang (28 July 1876)|ambush of the escort of the medical officer Dr. Döring]], who rallied the scattered soldiers and counter-attacked.
- [[8 August|8]]–[[9 August]] [[1876]] — the [[Gli Taroem night ambush (8-9 August 1876)|night ambush in the Gli Taroem pass]], in which the young Lieutenant-adjutant [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. Van Heutsz]] took part.
- [[28 August]] [[1876]] — the [[Lo-oe ambush (28 August 1876)|ambush of Corporal De Jong's detachment]] near kampong Lo-oe.

### Pidir and Gigiëng

- [[31 May]] – [[4 June]] [[1876]] — the [[Occupation of Pidir (31 May - 4 June 1876)|landing and occupation of Pidir]] by the detachment of Captain [[J. R. Engelhard]].
- [[19 August]] [[1876]] — the [[Cancellation of friendship with Gigiëng (19 August 1876)|cancellation of the friendship with Gigiëng]] after the defection of its ruler [[Toengkoe Bintara Kamangan]] to the war party.

## The role of the Habib

The greater boldness of the guerilla from March [[1876]] onward was, in Meijer's judgement, to be ascribed above all to the arrival of [[Habib Abdoerrachman el Zahir|Habib Abdoer Rachman ben Zahir]], who had escaped from [[Penang]] disguised as a sailor, landed near [[Edi]] and travelled along the north coast to the centre of resistance, binding princes, chiefs and people to himself. He obtained written declarations, confirmed by oaths, from the leading chiefs of the three [[Sagi]]s, organised the payment and feeding of the fighters, raised a war tax of 30,000 dollars, and repeatedly announced a general attack on the Dutch troops — for the night of 17–18 August and again for 30 August [[1876]] — which, however, never materialised.

## Outcome

Between 9 March and 4 September [[1876]] the Dutch troops did not act offensively. [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|General Wiggers van Kerchem]] was instructed to consolidate the occupied position: roads were built, the terrain was cleared around the posts, reserves of provisions were stored, and the new posts of [[Night attack on Atoë Noord (22-23 March 1876)|Atoë Noord]], [[Biloel Zuid]] and [[Sweep of the IV and VI Moekims (8-15 July 1876)|Kroeëng Raba]] were established. On 4 September [[1876]] the period described by Meijer closed with the march of part of the troops, under Wiggers van Kerchem, into the [[XXVI Moekims]], to complete the closing of the XXII and XXVI Moekims from the sea as Pel had planned.

## Significance

The events of this period showed the Dutch command that the [[Benting|benting]] system alone, spread over territory the enemy knew intimately, could not end the war. Meijer's book became the standard Dutch analysis of the guerilla war, and many of its lessons — on march discipline, the security of convoys and the fortification of posts — were quoted throughout the rest of the [[Aceh War]].

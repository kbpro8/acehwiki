**The action of the sloops of the Benkoelen at Lehong** was the naval engagement in the Riouw bay (Riouwbaai) at [[Leuhong|Lehong]] on the West Coast of [[Atjeh]], reported in *Het Vaderland* in [[1893]], in which the joint sloop flotilla of the *Benkoelen* came to blows with the enemy. The affair was quoted by [[W. A. Coblijn|Coblijn]] in the naschrift of *[[Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen (Coblijn, 1893)|Hoe Atjeh te onderwerpen]]* in the context of the discussion that majoor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s brochure *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]* had raised over the [[Issuance of firearms to Acehnese|issue of firearms to Acehnese]].

## The engagement

Under command of luitenant ter zee 2e klasse [[Wichers]] the Dutch had chased a *prauw* (native vessel) belonging to [[Teukoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] and endeavoured to bring her up from the strand. The landing division sent for the purpose was fired upon from the shrub; the fire was silenced with the support of the guns of two sloops, and the prauw was shot to pieces.

## The reward of the radja of Pasangan

The same newspaper report stated that the radja of Pasangan, on the East Coast of Sumatra — who had returned to the princes expelled by Toekoe Oemar the small states (*staatjes*) of their fathers and had hoisted the Dutch flag upon his own state — had presumably been rewarded for all this with permission (*verlof*) to import fifty Martini-Henry rifles with the necessary ammunition, and tin for fishing-nets. The irony was not lost on the pamphleteers of 1893: at the very same time that the rifles issued earlier to the Atjehers were denounced by Van Heutsz as a *dwaze illusie* (foolish illusion) that might have to be taken back bloodily, the government itself continued to license the import of breech-loading arms into the neighbourhood of the enemy.

**Second Anglo‑Dutch War** was a naval conflict between the [[Dutch Republic]] and the [[Kingdom of England]] that lasted from [[1665]] to [[1667]]. The war featured major sea battles, including the Dutch victory at the [[Battle of Solebay]] [[1667]] and the daring Dutch raid on the River Thames in [[1667]], culminating in the capture of the English flagship [[Royal Charles]].

## Causes and course
Tensions over trade rivalry and colonial competition sparked the war. The Dutch navy, under admirals such as [[Michiel de Ruyter]] and [[Banekert]], sought to protect Dutch merchant shipping, while the English aimed to challenge Dutch dominance in the Atlantic and Indian Oceans.

## Thames raid (1667)
In August [[1667]], a Dutch fleet sailed up the River Thames, seized the fort at [[Sheerness]], and proceeded to the shipyards at [[Chatham]] and [[Rochester]]. The operation, coordinated by [[Cornelis de Witt]] and executed by marine officers including [[François Palm]] and [[Colonel Dolman]], resulted in the capture of the [[Royal Charles]] and the destruction of numerous English warships.

## Link to Aceh
The war’s outcome reinforced Dutch naval supremacy, allowing the [[Dutch East India Company]] to continue its aggressive expansion in the East Indies, where it was engaged in a protracted struggle with the Sultanate of [[Aceh]] (the 1659‑1660 Aceh campaign). The resources freed by the victory at Solebay enabled the VOC to maintain its fleet in the Indian Ocean.

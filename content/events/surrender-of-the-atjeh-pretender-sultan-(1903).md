**The surrender of the Atjeh pretender-sultan and the other Atjehnese commanders in [[1903]]** was the formal act of submission by which the pretender-sultan (*pretendent-sultan*) of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] and the remaining Atjehnese military leaders (*Atjehsche aanvoerders*) offered their submission to the Dutch colonial government — the act that, in the canonical Dutch schoolroom memory fixed by the [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook of F. Roorda, brought the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] to its military conclusion. The surrender followed the definitive subjugation of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in the years [[1898]]–[[1902]], and it cleared the way for Van Heutsz's appointment as [[Governor-General of the Netherlands East Indies|Governor-General]] in [[1904]] and the subsequent transition from military to civil administration of the [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]].

## The 1903 surrender

The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] school-history textbook of F. Roorda records the surrender in a single canonical sentence:

> *"1903. De pretendent-sultan en de andere Atjehsche aanvoerders bieden hun onderwerping aan. De oorlog kan hiermee als geëindigd worden beschouwd."*

The textbook's wording — "*bieden hun onderwerping aan*" ("offer their submission") and "*De oorlog kan hiermee als geëindigd worden beschouwd*" ("the war can with this be considered ended") — fixes the surrender as the formal close of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the Dutch schoolroom memory. The use of the phrase "*pretendent-sultan*" (pretender-sultan) rather than simply "*sultan*" is itself a documentary fact: it marks the Dutch refusal, throughout the war, to recognise the Atjehnese claimant as the legitimate sovereign, and the consequent framing of the war as the suppression of a pretender rather than the conquest of a sovereign state.

## The end of the war

The textbook's evaluation — "*De oorlog kan hiermee als geëindigd worden beschouwd*" ("the war can with this be considered ended") — is the rhetorical close of the schoolroom narrative of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] that had opened, thirty years earlier, with the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first expedition]] and the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]]. The phrasing — "*als geëindigd worden beschouwd*" ("considered ended") — is carefully hedged: the war "can be considered ended," rather than simply "ended." The hedge is itself a document of the Dutch recognition that the formal surrender of the pretender-sultan and the principal commanders did not necessarily entail the cessation of all Atjehnese resistance — a recognition borne out by the subsequent persistence of intermittent resistance in the interior, and by the eventual Dutch reversion to a more nuanced *pacification* policy under [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] and his successors.

## Aftermath

The [[1903]] surrender cleared the way for [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s appointment as [[Governor-General of the Netherlands East Indies|Governor-General]] in [[1904]] — a promotion that fixed, in the colonial memory, the success of his Atjeh command — and for the subsequent extension of the programme of conquest to the [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]] south of Atjeh. The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook records the transition in its chronological table: "*1904-'09. Van Heutz gouverneur-general ... De Bataklanden onderworpen.*" The post-surrender Atjeh, however, retained a residual military presence: the formal transition from military to civil administration did not occur until [[1918|1918]] — a date the [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook records as the moment when "*het militaire door een burgerlijk bestuur vervangen [werd], wat de goede verstandhouding tusschen de regeering en de inlanders heeft bevorderd*" ("the military was replaced by a civil administration, which has promoted the good understanding between the government and the natives").

## Significance

The surrender of [[1903]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history on three counts. First, it is the formal close of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the act by which the pretender-sultan and the principal Atjehnese commanders acknowledged Dutch authority, and by which the war, in the canonical Dutch framing, "can be considered ended." Second, it cleared the way for the colonial transition from military to civil administration — a transition that, though formally begun in [[1903|1903]]–[[1904|1904]], was not completed until the [[1918|1918]] replacement of the military by a civil administration. Third, it is the proximate cause of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s promotion to the [[Governor-General of the Netherlands East Indies|Governor-Generalship]] and of the subsequent extension of the conquest to the [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]] — the document, in the colonial schoolroom memory, of the successful close of the longest Dutch colonial war.

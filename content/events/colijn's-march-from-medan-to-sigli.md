**Colijn's march from Medan to Sigli** was the celebrated march of a hundred and sixty days, made by [[Hendrikus Colijn]] — the later minister and, in [[1913]], ex-Minister of War — from [[Medan]] on the East Coast of Sumatra over the high mountains and the primeval forest to [[Sigli]] on the north coast of [[Atjeh]], *midden door het toen nog vijandelijk land* (right through the then still hostile country). The account given by [[W. Moens|Moens]] in the *[[Reisindrukken van Atjeh (Moens, 1914)|Reisindrukken van Atjeh]]* — where it was brought up at the table of majoor [[Th. J. Veltman|Veldman]] at Sigli in November [[1913]] — does not date the march; it belongs to the years of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] preceding the definitive *[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]]*.

## The hardships of the march

The tradition preserved at Sigli pictured the march as one of the utmost exertion. The *koelies* (coolies) who carried the provisions fled, so that the sick and severely wounded of the column had themselves to be carried by the troops; the soldiers subsisted at one point with the greatest difficulty and passed, it was said, forty-four hours between one meal and the next; trees were at night the only roof. When the story was called to mind at Veldman's dinner, the company — which counted three bearers of the higher classes of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] and an *[[Eresabel|eresabel]]* (sword of honour) — regarded it as one of the great memories of the war.

## Significance

Colijn's march from Medan to Sigli is significant in the colonial memory of the Atjeh war as the type of the long-distance penetration march by which the Dutch columns demonstrated that no part of the country was closed to them: a hundred and sixty days through mountain and virgin forest in enemy country. It is not to be confused with the famine march known at court circles as the *[[Beruchtste hongertocht van Atjeh (1913)|beruchtste hongertocht van Atjeh]]* of 1913; the march remembered at Sigli was recounted to Moens and his companions as belonging to the war-time career of the ex-Minister whom the chiefs of [[Samalanga]] now greeted as *toewan Colijn*, the *eerste raadsman van de Koningin, speciaal voor oorlogszaken* — the first counsellor of the Queen, specially for matters of war.

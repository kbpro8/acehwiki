**The Battle of Kotta Mogat** (also written Kotta Mogat) on [[8 April]] [[1873]] was the failed Dutch storming of the enemy fortification of Kotta Mogat on the first day of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]], undertaken by the twelfth bataljon against the eastern of two fortifications near the landing beach west of the mouth of the Atjeh river. The failure — for want of the necessary ammunition and storm-ladders — caused the first day of the expedition to end in an échec, and was an early omen of the disaster that culminated in the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]] on [[14 April]] [[1873]].

## Background

The landing of the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] on [[8 April]] [[1873]] west of the mouth of the Atjeh river immediately presented more difficulties than expected. From the fortification Kotta Pantej Termin, which had been taken on [[6 April]] without a blow but had been evacuated, shots were now fired that cost the adelborst Zimmer his life. Hardly were a couple of companies on the beach when some 400 Atjehers emerged from the undergrowth, armed with flint-locks, blunderbusses, klewangs and lances, and stormed, after firing their small-arms, in blind rage, despite the fire of the skirmishers, with the white arms upon the troops. After a furious fight they had to leave the field with a loss of 82 dead.

## The Storming and its Failure

When the infantry had landed, the twelfth bataljon was ordered to take the two eastern fortifications, [[Kotta Pantej Termin|Kotta Pantej Termin]] and [[Kotta Mogat|Kotta Mogat]]. The first was found abandoned, but the storming of the second, even a second time after receiving reinforcements, failed, among other reasons for want of the necessary ammunition and storm-ladders. "Thus the first day already ended with an échec." The next morning Kotta Mogat, after a breach had been shot in it by the guns of the warships, was found abandoned by the enemy and occupied without trouble by the Dutch.

## Significance

The échec at Kotta Mogat was an early indication that the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]] had been prepared on false assumptions. The Indies government had no general staff in the Indies, and was "almost wholly unacquainted with land and population of Atjeh." Although already in [[July 1871]] the commander of the Indies army, Lieutenant-general [[W. E. Kroesen|W. E. Kroesen]], had instructed the then colonel Köhler, military commandant of Sumatra's Westkust, to collect all possible military data about Atjeh, the Indies government had refused a proposal to send a captain of the Indies army aboard the *Djambi* to inform himself locally of conditions. The failure at Kotta Mogat was followed by the failure at the [[Battle of the Missigit (10 April 1873)|missigit]] on [[10 April]] and the death of Köhler on [[14 April]].

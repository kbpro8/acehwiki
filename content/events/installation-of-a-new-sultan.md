The **installation of a new Sultan** of [[Acheh]] was the principal ceremonial of the Achehnese court, in which the great officers of state, the chiefs of the three sagis, and the heads of the traditional tribes (*sukeës*) took part. C. Snouck Hurgronje, in *The Achehnese*, describes the ceremonial in connection with his account of the sukeë of **Ja Sandang** — the tribe from which the [[Kali Malikon Ade|Kali Malikon Ade]] was drawn, and whose hereditary head played the part of master of ceremonies at the installation.

## The Ja Sandang Legend

The ceremonial role of the Ja Sandang at the installation is explained by a legend current among the Achehnese. According to this story — unreliable as history but much more generally current than the traditions preserved by [[Van Langen|Van Langen]] — one of the Sultans of Acheh once found himself constrained to go and enforce in person his authority over the district of [[Pidie|Pidie]]. His route thither lay through [[Lam Panaih|Lam Panaih]] in the [[XXII Mukims|XXII Mukims]]. Here he suffered from thirst, but none was found to bring him anything to drink, till at last a man of humble rank brought him milk in a bamboo vessel (*peuchò'*), which he carried in the way expressed by the word *sandang* (suspended by a cord passing over the shoulder).

The Sultan was extremely grateful and invited him to come to him when the war was over and His Majesty had returned to the [[Dalam]], as he wished to give him an earnest of the honour and favour in which he held him. "But how," objected the old man, "shall an insignificant person like myself be recognized as he who helped to assuage your thirst, and admission to the Dalam be accorded me?" "You must," replied the Sultan, "twist a white cocoanut spathe round your head by way of sign, and hang your bamboo vessel over your shoulder as it now is." Ja Sandang did so, and both he and his descendants enjoyed the utmost consideration at the Court.

## The Master of Ceremonies

Later on, the ruler of Acheh who reformed or endeavoured to reform the administration of justice chose his court judge from the tribe of Ja Sandang, which had meantime greatly increased in dignity. This official received the title of [[Kali Malikul Adil|Kali Malikul-Adil]] (Malikon Ade), and the judicial office — or at least the title appertaining to it — remained hereditary in his family. The later bearers of this title gradually acquired the position of masters of ceremonies at the Court. Those who have seen them in the exercise of their functions at the installation of a new Sultan can testify that on such occasions the Teuku Kali wore a white cocoanut spathe under his head-cloth, and a blunderbuss attached to a leathern belt passing over his shoulder. The reason given for adhering to this adat is that the titular "judge" appeared also in the character of the representative of his tribe, the Ja Sandang. As, however, customs change with lapse of time, the later Sultans had permitted the holders of this office to exchange the bamboo vessel for a blunderbuss and to hide the cocoanut spathe to some extent beneath their head-cloth.

The members of the Ja Sandang tribe are not allowed to eat the flesh of white buffaloes or the salt-water fish *alu-alu*, both of which are tabooed (*pantang*) for them — prohibitions which date from pre-Mohammedan times and which perhaps argue a much greater antiquity for the sukeë than the Achehnese themselves are aware of.

## Election and Recognition

The installation followed upon an election which was usually decided by war between the different parties. At the election of a new Sultan, the scale was turned not so much by the support of the *panglimas* as by that of the real wielders of power in each sagi: the chief uleebalangs of the [[XXVI Mukims|XXVI Mukims]] ([[Teuku Ateue'|Teuku Ateue']] and [[Teuku Imeum Tungkob|Teuku Imeum Tungkob]]), of the [[XXII Mukims|XXII Mukims]] ([[Teuku Ba'et|Teuku Ba'et]] and [[Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh|Teuku Waki Chi' Gampong Baroh]]), and of the [[XXV Mukims|XXV Mukims]] (the two Ne's and [[Teuku Nanta Seutia|Teuku Nanta Seutia]]). The ceremony of installation itself was carried out at the Dalam, with the Teuku Kali — wearing the insignia of his tribe — officiating as master of ceremonies, and with the [[kali|kali]] pronouncing the formal nomination in the presence of the assembled chiefs and people.

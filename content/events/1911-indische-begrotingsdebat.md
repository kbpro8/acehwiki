**The 1911 Indische begrotingsdebat** — the 1911 Indies budget debate in the [[Tweede Kamer]] — is the parliamentary debate that "concentreerde zich vooral om de door deze adviseur van het ministerie van Koloniën ontvouwde denkbeelden" — concentrated especially on the ideas unfolded by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], the advisor to the [[Ministerie van Koloniën|Ministry of Colonies]], in his recently published *[[Nederland en de Islam ([[1911]])|Nederland en de Islam]]*. The debate is the canonical parliamentary exchange on the question of government support for Christian *zending* (missions) in the Indies — including, by direct implication, the recently-pacified territories of Atjeh.

## The Kuyper-Malefijt exchange

In the [[1911 Indische begrotingsdebat|1911 begrotingsdebat]] [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] (a.r.) mounted a principled argument against [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]]'s position. The neutral (*neutraal*) education that [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] advocated was, in [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s reading, equivalent to "modernistic" education: "Het zou de inlanders tot vrijdenkerij brengen of tot het hindoeisme terug doen zinken" — it would bring the natives to freethinking or sink them back into Hinduism. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s rule for the Indies was: "de bizondere school regel, de gouvernementschool aanvulling" — the confessional school the rule, the government school a supplement. To the objection that, where the private initiative could not provide, the government must step in, [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] replied: "Ik heb gezegd: de bizondere school regel, de gouvernementschool aanvulling. Moeten er 100 scholen komen en richt het particulier initiatief er slechts 11 op, dan moeten er eenvoudig 89 door het gouvernement worden opgericht. Ik laat geen enkele plaats onbezet".

## Malefijt's response

[[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] — minister of Colonies — replied that he adhered to the position of his predecessors: no direct support for the *zending* (missions), but strong support where the missions operated on social terrain. [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s rule for confessional schools was, in his view, possible only in a few regions of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Buitenbezettingen]] where the missions had gained a firm foothold; on [[Java]] — where the Muslim majority lacked the organisation to act on its own initiative — the rule was inapplicable.

## The Kuyper-Heemskerk incident

[[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s feeling that [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] had failed to "warm genoeg voor de a.r. beginselen uitkomen" — to come out warmly enough for the anti-revolutionary principles — and his suspicion of "de explosie van gejubel bij de linkse heren" — the explosion of jubilation among the gentlemen of the left — led to an incident in which [[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] "zich op zijn beurt door [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] gekrenkt [voelde]" — felt himself in turn injured by Kuyper — and the first minister [[Heemskerk (first minister)|Heemskerk]] had to come to his defence. The incident, "die in die tijd veel opzien baarde" — which in its day caused much stir — was, in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]]'s judgement, perhaps most of all a symptom of the "geprikkelde stemming" between [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] and the [[Heemskerk (first minister)|Heemskerk]]-[[J. H. de Waal Malefijt|De Waal Malefijt]] government.

## The Snouck position

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] had argued that the Dutch state — religiously neutral — could not, and should not, attempt to convert its Muslim subjects to Christianity. The state should confine itself to "een actie, die bedoelt de inlanders op veel steviger wijze dan tot dusver het geval is, bij ons staatsverband en bij onze nationaliteit in te lijven" — an action intended to incorporate the natives far more firmly than hitherto into our state-association and our nationality. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] hoped that, with a proper *associatiepolitiek*, the Indonesians would of their own free will remain as equals within the realm.

## Significance for Atjeh

The [[1911 Indische begrotingsdebat|1911 begrotingsdebat]] bears directly on Atjeh because the post-[[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] administration of the sultanate was, in part, a question of education and zending. The Snouck-Kuyper-Malefijt exchange set the framework within which the question — whether the Atjehnese population should be educated in government-neutral or in Christian-confessional schools — would be decided.

**Van Langen's *Atjeh's Westkust* ([[1888]])** — full title "Atjeh's Westkust," published in volume II of the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* in [[1888]] — is the article by [[K. F. H. van Langen|K. F. H. van Langen]] that supplied one of the earliest systematic Western geographical accounts of the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] (West Coast of Atjeh) and that remained a standard reference for the region through the early decades of the twentieth century. The article is cited in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] (p. 104) — alongside [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s [[Snouck Hurgronje's De Atjehers ([[1893]]-94)|*De Atjehers*]] (vol. I, p. 120) and [[G. A. J. Wilken|Wilken]]'s *Verspreide Geschriften* (vol. II, p. 488) — as one of the three principal authorities for the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Atjeh *adat* schimpstraf]] that paraded a wife-beater seated on a female buffalo.

## The article
Van Langen's article — published in [[1888]] in volume II of the *Tijdschrift van het Koninklijk Nederlandsch Aardrijkskundig Genootschap* (the journal of the Royal Dutch Geographical Society, founded [[1873]]) — treated the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a geographical and administrative unit, and appears to have included ethnographic material on local Malay adat practices of the kind that [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] cites. The [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|buffalo-riding schimpstraf]] recorded at p. 481 of the article is a particularly vivid example: a man convicted of wife-beating was paraded through the village seated on a female buffalo, dressed in a banana-leaf head-cloth and hung with wooden weapons and a shell necklace; the alternative form of the punishment required the delinquent to crawl on all fours for several days in public, wearing a nose-ring and eating grass "as if he were a buffalo." The detail that the punishment centred on the buffalo — symbolically converting the malefactor into the very animal he was thought to have abused his authority over — is preserved in Van Langen's account, and was passed on through [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] into the comparative ethnographic literature.


## Significance
Van Langen's *Atjeh's Westkust* ([[1888]]) is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as the earliest named Western source for the [[Atjeh's Westkust|Westkust van Atjeh]] as a describable territorial unit, and as one of the three authorities on which the standard account of the [[Atjeh adat schimpstraf (buffalo-riding wife-beater)|Atjeh buffalo-riding schimpstraf]] rests. The article is also significant as a document of the early Dutch-colonial geographical literature on Atjeh, predating the great ethnographic syntheses of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] ([[1893]]-94) and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] ([[1922]]-23) by a generation.

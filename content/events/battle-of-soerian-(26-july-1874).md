**The Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)** was the first major Dutch offensive west of [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]], undertaken by [[generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] in person with the whole available force against the kampongs of [[setee|Setee]], [[poe-oe|Poe Oe]], [[soerian|Soerian]] and *Poengej Blang Tjoet*, in order to gain a forward line covering the [[marassa|Marassa]] approach and the K. Radja–[[oleh-leh|Oeleh Leh]] road, and to threaten the flank and rear of [[toekoe-nanta|Toekoe Nanta]] of the [[vi-moekims|VI Moekims]]. Borel insists the whole bloody fight could have been avoided if Soerian — in Dutch hands in February [[1874]] — had never been given back.

## Pel's object

Pel's object, Borel writes, was fourfold: to conquer the kampongs Setee, Poengej Blang Tjoet, Poe Oe and Soerian; to bring [[bital|Bital]] and Lamtermin under observation; and to threaten the rear of Toekoe Nanta, "onzen ijverigen tegenstander," whose territory formed the single weak point of the future [[stelling-pel|stelling]] between Oeleh Leh and Soerian. On the morning of [[26-july|26 July]] [[1874]] the troops assembled at [[blang-oe|Blang Oe]] and advanced.

## The fight

The enemy was prepared. The Dutch met immediate stiff resistance and failed to carry Soerian outright. They did, however, manage to surround a band of defenders inside a *benteng* near Soerian who refused quarter, and, because they would not surrender, killed them "tot den laatsten man" — about seventy enemy dead. Dutch losses were six dead and more than forty wounded, including three officers. A post was at once erected on the conquered ground. In the days following, the Atjehneezen abandoned all their remaining positions; the magnificent *sawahs* (irrigated rice fields) stretching to the edge of Bital and [[ketapang-doewa|Ketapang Doewa]] fell into Dutch hands without further fighting.

## Borel's commentary

The relinquishing of captured ground was, in Borel's view, the cardinal error of the [[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]] inherited from [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]. The fight at Soerian was also the first appearance of the *Poengej Blang Tjoet* position, which would become the western link of the defensive ring completed in [[Completion of the defensive ring around Kota Radja (May 1875)|May 1875]]. [[poe-oe|Poe Oe]] itself was occupied without a shot on [[16-august|16 August]] [[1874]]. The magnificence of the abandoned *sawahs* — stretching to the edge of Bital and Ketapang Doewa — gave the Dutch, for the first time since the landing, a real field of fire and a clear field of view west of K. Radja.

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft treats the battle of Soerian on [[26-july|26 July]] [[1874]] as the next step in the extension of the Dutch fortified line after Garouw. He credits [[Majoor Romswinckel|Romswinckel]]'s column with carrying the Acehnese position at Soerian after stubborn fighting and notes that the action, together with subsequent operations, allowed [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] to begin pushing the fortified perimeter further out from [[koeta-radja|Koeta-Radja]].

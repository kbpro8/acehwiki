**Marine operations on Sumatra (1874–1876)** were a series of Dutch naval and marine actions conducted along the northern coast of [[Sumatra]], principally in the Sultanate of [[Aceh]], between [[July]] [[1874]] and [[February]] [[1876]].

## Objectives and Activities

The operations, documented in the *Rapport van den luitenant der mariniers J. B. Verhey betreffende de verrichtingen der mariniers op Sumatra van Juli [[1874]] tot Febr. [[1876]]*, aimed to:

* Secure coastal forts and harbours against Acehnese attacks.
* Protect Dutch merchant shipping in the Strait of Malacca.
* Provide fire‑support for the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] during its inland campaigns.

## Outcomes

Verhey’s report notes that the mariniers “successfully repelled several Acehnese raids” and established a network of coastal batteries that remained in Dutch hands until the final pacification of Aceh in the early 20th century.

## Connection to Larger Campaigns

These marine actions were integral to the broader [[Aceh War]] and directly preceded the larger expedition recorded by [[M. P. Meyer]] covering [[26 December]] [[1875]] to [[4 September]] [[1876]].

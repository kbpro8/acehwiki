**The declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)** was the act by which the [[Netherlands]] opened the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The declaration was the immediate fruit of the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] and was justified to the [[Tweede Kamer]] on three grounds: piracy, internal warfare, and fear of foreign interference.

## The three grounds

The official *motivering* (justification) advanced by the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] government in [[1873]] comprised three causes. The first was alleged piracy emanating from Atjehese waters; the second was the alleged prevalence of internal warfare within the sultanate; the third — and, in the government's view, pre-eminent — was "vrees voor buitenlandse inmenging" — fear of foreign interference — sharpened by the [[Soezkanaal|opening of the Suez Canal]] in [[1869]]. The [[Sultan of Acheh|sultan]]'s contacts with the Italian and American consuls in [[Singapore]] during the abortive Dutch-Atjehnese negotiations were cited as evidence of the danger.

## Parliamentary reception

The chamber's reception of the *motivering* was mixed. [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) later asked whether Atjehese piracy really was of such alarming proportions, since the colonial reports of the years immediately preceding the declaration did not single out [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in this regard. The colonial report for [[1897]] itself admitted that on the [[Mentawai-eilanden]] prahus were still being robbed and their crews murdered, and that even [[Straat Madoera]] was unsafe after sunset. The second reason — internal warfare — was equally unconvincing to critics, since the [[Netherlands]] routinely declined to intervene in inter-tribal wars in regions already formally under Dutch sovereignty.

## Kuyper's "straf des hemels"

[[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] (a.r.) was so little convinced by the official *motivering* that, when cholera broke out among the Dutch troops the following year, he called it a "straf des hemels" — a punishment from heaven. The remark is, in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] narrative, the measure of [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s conviction that the official justification was inadequate.

## The Sumatra-tractaat connexion

Many chambermembers — [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] among them — "vermochten niet de kracht van dit motief in te zien" — could not see the force of the third motive — since the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] of [[1871]] had just removed the principal external threat. The government, however, "zag dit anders" — saw this differently — because it had "meer dan de kamerleden oog voor de veranderde politieke constellatie, die het gevolg was van de opening van het [[Soezkanaal|Suezkanaal]]" — more than the chambermembers had an eye for the changed political constellation that had resulted from the opening of the Suez Canal. The [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] was, on this view, the diplomatic instrument by which the [[Netherlands]] sought to *secure* its control of the [[Straat Malakka|strait]] against the increased traffic that the canal had brought.

## Significance

The [[Declaration of war against the Sultan of Atjeh (1873)|declaration of war of 1873]] is the foundational act of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Every subsequent parliamentary debate on Atjeh — the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie]] of [[1884]]-[[1896]], the [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|defection of Toekoe Oemar]] in [[1896]], the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]] in [[1904]], the [[Avondpost "Wekeer" articles (October 1907)|"Wekeer" affair]] of [[1907]] — refers back to the decision taken in [[1873]].

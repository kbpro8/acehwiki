**Atjeh as opperheer in Langkat, Deli and Serdang (1854)** — the reimposition, in [[1854]], of [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] overlordship over the Malay sultanates of [[Langkat|Langkat]], [[Deli|Deli]] and [[Serdang|Serdang]] — was, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]], the most consequential nineteenth-century revival of [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] power on [[Sumatra's East Coast|Sumatra's East Coast]]. Coming after a century of formal [[Siak|Siak]] suzerainty and a quarter-century of practical independence, the reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship transformed the East Coast statelets from autonomous Malay polities into tributaries of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — and set in motion the chain of appeals, interventions, and annexations by which the Dutch colonial state finally absorbed the East Coast between [[1862]] and [[1908]].

## Background: the decay of Siak (1821–1854)

The reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship in [[1854]] was the result of the long decay of [[Siak|Siak]]'s authority on the East Coast. The Bureau's account begins with the death of [[Sultan Said Ali|Sultan Said Ali of Siak]] in [[1821]] and the accession of [[Tongkoe Ismail|Sultan Tongkoe Ismail]] in [[1827]], under whom "nam Siaks macht af en waren de Noordelijke Onderhoorigheden weldra inderdaad geheel onafhankelijk" — "Siak's power declined and the Northern Onderhoorigheden were soon in fact entirely independent." Even the old ceremonial exchange of *galar*s — the titles of recognition that the East Coast sultans had once received from the hands of the [[Sultan of Siak|Sultan of Siak]] — had been replaced by the chiefs' mutual recognition of each other's titles. By the early 1850s the East Coast statelets were, in effect, sovereign.

## The 1854 reimposition

It was this vacuum that [[Acheh|Atjeh]] filled in [[1854]]. The Bureau records the reimposition with brutal directness: "Serdang, Deli en Langkat, welke zich los hadden weten te maken van Siak's invloed, waren al sinds [[1854]] gedwongen geworden in plaats daarvan dien van Atjeh toe te laten, welk rijk daar sedert dat jaar inderdaad opperheer was" — "[[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Deli|Deli]] and [[Langkat|Langkat]], which had managed to free themselves from [[Siak|Siak]]'s influence, had been forced since [[1854]] to admit, in its place, that of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], which realm was, since that year, in fact overlord there." The Atjehnese overlordship, in the Bureau's account, was not a ceremonial revival of the old [[Sultan Iskander Muda|Iskander Moeda]] arrangement but a *de facto* exercise of Atjehnese power, sustained by coercion.

## The Atjehnese pressure and the Langkat appeal

The Atjehnese overlordship was not a light burden. The Bureau records, in its account of the [[langkat-appeal-to-siak-against-atjeh-(1862)|1862 appeal of the Radja of Langkat to Siak]], that the Radja was "de onderdrukking door Atjeh moede" — "weary of the oppression by Atjeh" — and that he turned to his former overlord [[Siak|Siak]] for an act of recognition that would unlock Dutch protection. The appeal was the first formal breach in the Atjehnese overlordship, and it set in motion the Dutch intervention that would, within four years, extinguish Atjehnese power on the East Coast entirely.

## The Dutch intervention (1862–1866)

The Dutch response to the [[langkat-appeal-to-siak-against-atjeh-(1862)|Langkat appeal]] was rapid. At the insistence of [[Tongkoe Ismail|Sultan Tongkoe Ismail of Siak]], the Government dispatched [[Netscher|Resident Netscher of Riouw]] on a tour of the East Coast statelets in [[1862]]; the statelets of [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Langkat|Langkat]] and several smaller polities signed acts of recognition of Siak's overlordship, transferring in effect their allegiance from [[Acheh|Atjeh]] to [[Siak|Siak]] — and from Siak to the Netherlands. [[Asahan|Asahan]] refused, and was reduced by armed force in [[1865]]. In [[1866]] a Dutch naval intervention ended the "vexaties der Atjehers in het Langkatsche" — "the vexations of the Atjehers in the Langkat territory" — and drove the Atjehers back into their own territory. The Atjehnese overlordship of the East Coast was at an end.

## Significance

The [[1854]]–[[1866]] reimposition and extinguishment of Atjehnese overlordship over the East Coast is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the immediate prelude to the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]. The Dutch intervention of [[1862]]–[[1866]] had transformed the East Coast statelets from Atjehnese tributaries into Dutch protectorates, and the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] sultanate's loss of its East Coast revenues was one of the principal grievances that would, in the early 1870s, contribute to the breakdown of Dutch-Atjehnese relations. The reimposition is thus a hinge between two eras: the long centuries of [[atjehsche-overheersing-der-noordelijke-kuststaatjes|pre-colonial Atjehnese overlordship]] and the colonial era of Dutch effective authority.

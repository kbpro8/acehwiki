**The Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)** was the large-scale Dutch military expedition against [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] mounted in November [[1873]] under [[Jan van Swieten|opperbevelhebber Jan van Swieten]] — who combined the military command with a civil appointment as regeerings-commissaris possessing "bijna onbeperkte volmacht" — which landed at [[Pedro-punt]] on [[6 December]] [[1873]], fought the [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|bloody reconnaissances at Lemboe]] on [[25 December|25]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]], stormed the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]], bombarded the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] for nineteen days, and finally carried the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]] by an [[Omtrekking|omtrekkende beweging]] led by [[Generaal Verspijck]]. Borel's judgement is famously severe: he calls the operation, after some months of reflection, "slechts ten deele gelukt," and insists the critic [[Generaal Knoop|Knoop]] might just as well have said "mislukt."

## Planning and Command

Three brigades were fully equipped for the expedition. [[Generaal Verspijck]] was appointed tweede bevelhebber, his diverging inzichten, Borel notes, "niet gekend" by the government and therefore destined to play only an ondergeschikte rol. [[Generaal Pel|Kolonel Pel]] commanded the 2de brigade, which would bear the brunt of the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|Mesigit storming]] and inherit the occupation. [[Generaal Booms|Booms]] had already published a "zeer juist geschreven critiek" of the plan; [[Luitenant-kolonel de Rochemont|de Rochemont]] would later add his own brochure. The [[Tactische aanbevelingen en wenken (1873)|Departement van Oorlog's tactical booklet]] warned explicitly that "in onze Indische veldtochten somtijds niet genoeg partij getrokken wordt van behaalde voordeelen," and that "slechts eene krachtige vervolging kan de overwinning vruchtbaar maken" — a counsel the campaign would disregard.

## The Padang Decision and the Landings

The first great strategic error, in Borel's account, came before a shot was fired: van Swieten, believing two brigades would suffice, left the entire [[Padangsche brigade|derde (Padangsche) brigade]] behind at Padang. Borel calls this "eene groote strategische fout." Verspijck had proposed landing it at the [[Kroeng Raba-baai]] to take the Kraton between two fires; van Swieten rejected the proposal out of "vrees," though, Borel would later insist, "drie jaren later bewees de generaal Pel het tegendeel" — the march was feasible, the distance being less than three uren in straight line. The rejection left the enemy's retreat route from the Kraton permanently open. After the [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing at Pedro-punt]] on [[6 December]] [[1873]] — "uitstekend bedacht en zeer goed uitgevoerd" — and the [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|costly reconnaissances at Lemboe]] that van Swieten inexplicably ordered abandoned, the campaign bogged down before the Mesigit.

## From the Mesigit to the Kraton

The [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|frontal storm of the Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] cost 220 dood en gekwetsten, the worst single butcher's bill of the expedition, and was launched, Borel charges, "tegen alle Indische traditiën in, zonder noemenswaardige voorbereiding." Pel's flanking movement finally carried the work. The [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan (12 January 1874)|easy capture of Kota Goenoegan]] on [[12 January]] proved the enemy's hoofdmacht had already melted away; Verspijck's [[Omtrekking|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24 January]] [[1874]] completed the work, and the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|Kraton was found deserted]] "op een grijsaard en een zieken Atjehnees na." A botched [[Reconnaissance of the Atjeh-rivier (29 January 1874)|reconnaissance above the Kraton]] on [[29 January]] cost two officers and about twenty men.

## Aftermath and Judgement

After the [[Death of the Sultan of Atjeh and annexation proclamation (1874)|death of the sultan and annexation]] in early February [[1874]], van Swieten conducted two short-lived expeditions to [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|Bital]] and [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|Ketapang Doewa]], then declared the offensive over and ordered the troops to "werken met schop en spade." The Javaansche hulptroepen of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]] were sent home; the [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] and the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen of 12 c.M.]] — the best geschut the Dutch possessed — were shipped back to Java. After the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|échec at Lampoe Oek]] of [[16 April]] [[1874]] and the [[Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)|afscheidsrede]] of [[23 April|23]] claiming the war was "geëindigd," van Swieten boarded the *Prins Alexander* and departed on [[26 April]] [[1874]] — leaving [[Generaal Pel|Pel]] to inherit what Borel calls a "zeer gebrekkige stelling" and the [[Desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe (1874)|first bitter fruit]] of the passive system.

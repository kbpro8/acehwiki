**The Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, and Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)** — the completion of the lifting of the buitenlinie — was the final phase of the wholesale abandonment of the Dutch western defensive line in the spring of [[1896]], executed by three colonnes — the Noordercolonne, the Middencolonne, and the Zuidercolonne — under the overall command of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] and culminating in the handover of the post of [[Toengkoeb]] to the Achehnese *bendehoofd* [[Toekoe Nja Banta]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the evacuation of [[20 April]] [[1896]] brought to an end the three-week unraveling of the *buitenlinie* that had begun with the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]] and marked the strategic reconstitution of the Dutch position on the consolidated line of [[1893]].

## Background

By [[20 April]] [[1896]] the western *buitenlinie* had already been largely abandoned. The [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] on [[12 April]], the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]] on [[14 April]], and the [[Evacuation of Anak-Galoeng, Lam-Barih, Lam-Soet, Senelop (17 April 1896)|evacuation of Anak-Galoeng, Lam-Barih, Lam-Soet, and Senelop]] on [[17 April]] had undone the western line. The three remaining posts — [[Kroeng-Gloempang]], [[Tjot-Rang]], and [[Toengkoeb]] — formed the central and southern segments of the *buitenlinie* and were the last to be lifted. [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] resolved to complete the evacuation in a single coordinated operation, both to minimise the window of Achehnese opportunity and to demonstrate the orderly character of the withdrawal.

Three colonnes were formed: the **Noordercolonne**, advancing from the north; the **Middencolonne**, advancing from the consolidated line at [[Koeta Radja]] directly westward; and the **Zuidercolonne**, advancing from the south. The overall command was vested in [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]], who had already commanded the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] and the [[Evacuation of Anak-Galoeng, Lam-Barih, Lam-Soet, Senelop (17 April 1896)|evacuation of 17 April]]. Each colonne comprised infantry, a detachment of *maréchaussée*, a section of mountain artillery, and the necessary engineers and ambulances for demolition and casualty evacuation.

## The Operation of 20 April 1896

The three colonnes advanced on [[20 April]] [[1896]] against the three remaining posts. The Noordercolonne moved on [[Kroeng-Gloempang]] in the north; the Middencolonne moved on [[Tjot-Rang]] in the centre; the Zuidercolonne moved on [[Toengkoeb]] in the south. The Achehnese, recognising — as they had at [[Anak-Galoeng]] and [[Lam-Barih]] three days earlier — that the Dutch intent was to withdraw rather than to hold, contented themselves with harassment rather than the determined defence that had characterised the [[Siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe (28–29 March 1896)|siege of Aloer Oe]] and the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]]. The garrisons of the three posts were withdrawn in good order; the post installations were burned; the stores were loaded onto the baggage train.

The most politically significant moment of the day came at [[Toengkoeb]]. The post — the southernmost of the *buitenlinie* — was formally handed over to the Achehnese *bendehoofd* [[Toekoe Nja Banta|Toekoe Nja Banta]], in whose territory it stood. The handover was a deliberate act of political management: by transferring the post to a known chief rather than simply abandoning it, [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] hoped to retain some political influence in the southern mukims and to draw a distinction between the *bendehoofd* who accepted the handover and the resistance that had forced the evacuation. The handover was, however, a transparent fig leaf: the Dutch were withdrawing under pressure, and [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] — however well-disposed — could not long resist the tide of resistance that had carried [[Teuku Umar]] back to the Achehnese side.

## The Completion of the Buitenlinie Lifting

With the evacuation of [[Kroeng-Gloempang]], [[Tjot-Rang]], and [[Toengkoeb]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]], the *buitenlinie* — the entire defensive perimeter that the Dutch had constructed beyond the consolidated line of [[Koeta Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh]] since [[1893]] — ceased to exist. The *buitenlinie* had been the physical expression of the native-allied policy of [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] and of the *legioen* of [[Teuku Umar]]: a chain of posts, blockhouses, and allied *hoeloebalang* reaching into the western and southern mukims, anchored on the posts of [[Ana' Galong]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]], [[Lamkoenjit]], [[Anak-Galoeng]], [[Lam-Barih]], [[Lam-Soet]], [[Kroeng-Gloempang]], [[Tjot-Rang]], and [[Toengkoeb]]. In three weeks of fighting and burning, all of it had been undone.

The Dutch were back on the consolidated line of [[1893]]. The Achehnese, for the first time since the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection of Umar]] in [[September]] [[1893]], held the initiative across the entire theatre.

## Aftermath

The completion of the *buitenlinie* lifting on [[20 April]] [[1896]] inaugurated a brief operational pause, during which [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] reorganised his forces, received reinforcements, and prepared the counter-offensive. On [[21 April]] [[1896]] the [[Three-Column Operation to Free Tjot Rang and Kroeng Gloempang (21 April 1896)|three-column operation to free Tjot Rang and Kroeng Gloempang]] was mounted — a final attempt to clear the road for provisioning before the line was finally abandoned. On [[27 April]] [[1896]] Vetter issued his [[Proclamation of Vetter (27 April 1896)|proclamation]] announcing military operations against [[Teuku Umar]], and on the same day the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] began. The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] of [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]] would, five weeks later, restore the strategic initiative.

## Significance

The evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, and Toengkoeb on [[20 April]] [[1896]] was the formal completion of the abandonment of the *buitenlinie* — the strategic nadir of the Dutch campaign in the first semester of [[1896]]. The handover of [[Toengkoeb]] to [[Toekoe Nja Banta]] was the last political gesture of the pre-counter-offensive phase. Yet the evacuation was also, paradoxically, the precondition of recovery: by abandoning the indefensible line, Vetter freed the troops, the supplies, and the strategic attention that the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] of [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]] and the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] would require. The evacuation of [[20 April]] [[1896]] was the close of the retreat and the prelude to the advance.

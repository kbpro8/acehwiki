**The Nisero affair of November 1883 – September 1884** — *de Nisero-kwestie* — was the diplomatic and military incident that began with the wrecking of the English steamship *[[Nisero|Nisero]]* on the [[Westkust|West Coast]] of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] near [[Tenom|Tenom]] in [[November 1883|November]] [[1883]], the taking captive of her crew by the *radja* of Tenom, the failure of all Dutch attempts to release or ransom them, the consequent English interference in Dutch Atjeh affairs, and the release of the crew in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] on payment of a ransom of ƒ100,000 and a Dutch undertaking no longer to close the harbour of Tenom. The affair, occurring under the civil governorship of [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], exposed Dutch impotence to the outside world, contributed to the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]]'s decision of [[16 June|16]]–[[17 June]] [[1884]] to adopt the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]], and was one of the principal causes of Laging Tobias's resignation in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]].

## The Wreck and the Captivity (November 1883)

On the [[Westkust|West Coast]] of Atjeh, near [[Tenom|Tenom]], the English steamship *Nisero* was wrecked in [[November 1883|November]] [[1883]]. The crew was held captive by the *radja* of Tenom. All attempts to release or ransom them failed, by which Dutch impotence was fully exposed, even to the outside world. The Dutch had no force at their disposal capable of compelling the *radja* of Tenom to release the crew; the *radja* of Tenom, for his part, knew that the Dutch were in no position to compel him, and could therefore set his own price.

## English Interference

English interference ensued. The British government of the [[Straits Settlements|Straits Settlements]] — whose subjects and shipping the *radja* of Tenom had effectively seized — intervened with the Dutch on behalf of the captive crew. The intervention was the more unwelcome to the Dutch in that it came at a moment when their position in Atjeh was visibly collapsing under the civil government of [[Pruijs van der Hoeven|Pruijs van der Hoeven]] and [[Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]], and when the *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[s Jacob|'s Jacob]] had visited Atjeh in [[August 1883|August]] [[1883]] and found the situation highly unfavourable. The Dutch government, unable to release the crew by force, was compelled to negotiate a ransom.

## The Ransom and the Undertaking (September 1884)

Only in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]] was the release of the unfortunate crew obtained, on payment of a ransom of ƒ100,000, while the Dutch bound themselves no longer to close the harbour of Tenom. The ransom of ƒ100,000 was a substantial sum — the equivalent of nearly half a year's pension for [[Habib Abdul Rachman El-Zahir|Habib Abdul Rachman]] (30,000 guilders a year), or of the gift of ƒ25,000 plus five years of the monthly allowance of ƒ400 granted to [[Toeankoe Abdul Medjid|Toeankoe Abdul Medjid]] in [[June 1887|June]] [[1887]]. The undertaking no longer to close the harbour of Tenom was a further concession, compromising the Dutch freedom to blockade hostile statelets.

## Kruijt's Judgement

Kruijt's account of the Nisero affair is brief but pointed. The affair is cited as one of the principal instances of the Dutch impotence under civil government — "*waarbij onze onmacht ten volle, ook naar buiten, aan het licht kwam*" (by which our impotence was fully revealed, even to the outside). The affair is part of the broader pattern of Dutch collapse in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and on the coasts during the [[Civil Government Period (1881-1884)|civil government period]] of [[1881]]–[[1884]], which culminated in the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]] of [[1884]] and the resignation of Laging Tobias in [[September 1884|September]] [[1884]].

## Significance

The Nisero affair is the principal diplomatic incident of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. It exposed Dutch impotence to the outside world, drew British interference into Dutch Atjeh affairs, contributed to the [[Staten-Generaal|Staten-Generaal]]'s decision of [[June 1884|June]] [[1884]] to adopt the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentration system]], and was one of the principal causes of Laging Tobias's resignation. The ransom of ƒ100,000 and the undertaking no longer to close the harbour of Tenom were substantial concessions that compromised the Dutch freedom of action on the coasts. The affair is, with the [[Hok Canton affair|Hok Canton affair]] of [[June 1886|June]] [[1886]], one of the two principal incidents in which Atjehese acts against foreign shipping drew foreign interference into the Dutch war effort.

**The Defection of Teuku Umar of 29 March [[1896]]** — *Toekoe Oemar's verraad* (the betrayal of Toekoe Oemar) — was the event in which [[Teuku Umar|Teuku Umar]] (also written *Toekoe Oemar*), the Atjehese chieftain whom the Dutch had armed, paid, and styled *[[T. Djohan Pahlawan|T. Djohan Pahlawan]]* with the rank of *[[Panglima Prang Besar|Panglima Prang Besar]]* (Great War Commander) since his pardon of [[30 September]] [[30 September]] [[1893]], threw off the mask and withdrew into the mountains with his bands, taking with him the Dutch rifles, cartridges, and money he had been supplied with for a combined operation against the [[Lamkrah|Lamkrah]] region fixed for [[29 March]] [[29 March]] [[1896]]. The defection — the immediate occasion of [[J. A. Kruijt|Kruijt]]'s brochure — exposed the failure of the Dutch policy of arming Atjehese auxiliaries against Atjehese enemies, precipitated the honourable discharge of [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]], and inaugurated the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] under [[Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as *Regeeringscommissaris*.

## The Plan for the Lamkrah Operation

Under [[Deijkerhoff|Deijkerhoff]]'s governorship, the Dutch had in [[1895]] proposed to make permanent and equip the hitherto temporary posts outside the line in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. And indeed Deijkerhoff managed to get this through at [[Batavia|Batavia]]. Expansion again. But first, with the help of [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]], the [[Lamkrah|Lamkrah]] region — a hotbed of fanaticism, where the Dutch had never been master, where Dutch troops had recently suffered very serious losses — had to be cleared of enemies.

There Toekoe Oemar seemed to feel little inclination, whether he did not dare, or he could no longer get enough money out and considered the suitable moment now come to throw off the mask for the umpteenth time. Yet he finally promised to march. He received at the end of [[March 1896|March]] the necessary funds, a number of breech-loading rifles, cartridges, rations, and so on. The combined operation of Dutch troops and the Atjehese auxiliaries was to take place on [[29 March]] [[29 March]] [[1896]].

## The Discovery of the Plan

Toekoe Oemar's plan was, when the Dutch troops had marched out in mass outside the line, to throw himself on the exposed [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]]. "*Gelukkig kwam zijn plan om, wanneer onze troepen in massa buiten de linie zouden zijn gerukt, zich op het ontbloote Kota Radja en Oleh-leh te werpen, nog tijdig, hetzij ter elfder ure, aan het licht.*" (Fortunately his plan — when our troops had marched out in mass outside the line, to throw himself on the exposed Kota Radja and Oleh-leh — came to light in time, as it were at the eleventh hour.)

It cost effort to convince Deijkerhoff. The marching out of the troops was countermanded, and so a catastrophe was prevented, in which the surprise attack on [[Lombok|Lombok]] in [[August 1894|August]] [[1894]] would have been child's play.

## The Withdrawal into the Mountains

Finding himself discovered, Toekoe Oemar withdrew with his bands into the mountains and fortified himself there mightily; most of the paid friends (?) outside the line joined him. The Dutch posts lying outside the line were surrounded and cut off. "*Er moest dadelijk om versterking naar Java worden getelegrafeerd. Deze kwam onmiddellijk.*" (Reinforcements had to be telegraphed for immediately to Java. These came at once.)

## The Appointment of Vetter and the Discharge of Deijkerhoff

The military commander [[Vetter|Vetter]] accompanied the expedition as *Regeeringscommissaris*, and Deijkerhoff was honourably relieved of his office. The reason for discharge, according to the *Java-bode*, was: the disguising of the truth in the reports. "*Ook bij dit stelsel — het gebruiken van Atjehers tegen Atjehers — waren de adviezen onzer beste deskundigen slechts weer ten halve gevolgd. Halfheid is de vloek geweest van ons beleid in Atjeh.*" (Also in this system — the use of Atjehers against Atjehers — the advice of our best experts had been only half followed. Half-measures have been the curse of our policy in Atjeh.)

## The Dutch Had Been Warned

The defection of Toekoe Oemar was not unforeseen. From all sides warning voices had gone up. For it was universally believed, also among the Atjehers and beyond, that Toekoe Oemar was deceiving the Dutch, that the Dutch were playing a high game, putting everything on one card. But of course, one could not prove it. The *Gouverneur-Generaal* [[Van Rees|Van Rees]] and many after him had been seriously warned, convinced that from every rifle supplied to Atjehers the Dutch would later themselves be fired upon. "*Welnu, helaas! maar weer al te zeer bewaarheid.*" (Well, alas! but again only too truly fulfilled.) Even Atjehers had repeatedly pointed to the danger of these licences or supplies.

## The Counter-Offensive (March – April 1896)

As soon as the necessary troops, despatched with great promptness from Java, had arrived, vigorous action was taken against the enemy — who stood against the Dutch in thousands, including the Sultan and numerous bands from the coasts. First, the surrounded posts were relieved, gradually dismantled, and the troops concentrated within the line. Then Toekoe Oemar was driven from his positions. And finally, everywhere outside the line, the enemy bands were defeated and scattered and the possessions of the Dutch's opponents destroyed.

## Kruijt's Judgement

Kruijt's judgement of the defection is severe on the Dutch and admiring of Toekoe Oemar:

> "*Welnu, wij kunnen, van zijn standpunt beschouwd, zijn slim overleg, zijn diplomatisch talent, zijn groote krijgslist slechts bewonderen, om ons des te meer te ergeren over de verregaande lichtgeloovigheid en blindheid van het bestuur in Atjeh.*" (Well, from his standpoint, we can only admire his shrewd deliberation, his diplomatic talent, his great military cunning, the more to be vexed at the extreme credulity and blindness of the administration in Atjeh.)

The Dutch had had Toekoe Oemar against the Atjehese resistance, but Toekoe Oemar had used the Dutch. Above all, the supply of masses of rifles and cartridges had been irresponsible and reckless. "*Wij hadden T. Oemar moeten gebruiken op de wijze door van Heutz aangegeven, maar wij hebben ons door hem laten gebruiken.*" (The Dutch should have used Toekoe Oemar in the manner indicated by Van Heutsz, but the Dutch had let themselves be used by him.)

## Significance

The Defection of Teuku Umar of [[29 March]] [[29 March]] [[1896]] is the immediate occasion of Kruijt's brochure. It exposed the failure of the Dutch policy of using Atjehese auxiliaries against Atjehese enemies — the policy of *Verdeel en heersch* (divide and rule) — and inaugurated the final phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in which the Dutch reverted to full military operations under [[Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] as *Regeeringscommissaris*, and ultimately to the systematic pacification under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] after [[1898]]. The counter-offensive of the spring of [[1896]] — driving Toekoe Oemar from his positions, relieving the surrounded posts, and dispersing the enemy bands — restored the prestige of Dutch arms that the defection had shattered, and prepared the way for the systematic pacification that would carry the war to its conclusion.

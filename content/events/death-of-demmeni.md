**General H. Demmeni**, military and civil Governor of Atjeh and Dependencies, died on **[[13-december|13 December]] [[1886]]** at [[Pajakombo|Padang Pandjang? Pajakombo]] in the [[padang|Bovenlanden van Padang|Padang Highlands]] of Sumatra's West Coast, where he had gone on sick leave barely a month earlier. His death, which came just as [[P. Brooshooft|Brooshooft]] was finishing his [[Geschiedenis van den Atjeh-oorlog 1873–1886 (Brooshooft, 1886)|popular history]] of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]], robbed the Dutch of the officer whom Brooshooft called the indispensable man of the concentration period, just as [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] had been the indispensable man of [[1876]].

## Context

After the failure of the forward policy of the civil-governor period ([[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|Pruys van der Hoeven]], [[P. F. Laging Tobias|Laging Tobias]]) and the adoption of the [[Concentratie-stelsel (1884-1896)|concentrated-stance policy]] in August [[1884]], Demmeni had been appointed military commander and then Governor of Atjeh. Brooshooft credits him with designing the existing fortified line around [[koeta-radja|Kota Radja]] and [[oleh-leh|Oleh-leh]], the telephone network, the steam tram, the ring-dijk polder system, and the strict internal security regime of the final years. By late [[1886]] he was fifty-five years old and worn out by incessant work and the climate.

## Illness, Leave, and Death

Brooshooft writes that Demmeni, like [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] before him, appeared physically strong — able to work almost without rest on little sleep and very moderate food. In reality, like Pel, he sustained that capacity with large doses of **quinine (chinine)** to suppress the fevers from which he constantly suffered, and in the long run this unnatural regime undermined his constitution. At the end of [[october|October]] [[1886]] he applied for sick leave to recuperate in the healthier mountain climate of the [[padang|Bovenlanden van Padang]], and he departed for [[padang|Padang]] in [[november|November]]. The leave did him no good. Hardly had he reached the mountain resort when he died at the age of 55 at Pajakombo, the chief town of a division of that residency.

The sad news reached the Netherlands by telegraph on **13 December 1886**. On 14 December the Minister of the Colonies announced it in the [[Tweede Kamer|Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal]], and the Speaker of the House expressed the deep regret of the members on behalf of the chamber.

## Career Recalled by Brooshooft

Brooshooft's chapter on Demmeni's death, written while the news was still fresh, gives the following biographical summary:

- Demmeni was of **Alsatian** (*Elzasser*) origin, according to Brooshooft, and probably entered Dutch Indies service through family connections.
- He rose through the various ranks to reach lieutenant-colonel.
- In [[1879]] he appeared at Atjeh as commander of the south-eastern line, and subsequently took part as a column commander in [[General van der Heijden|General van der Heyden]]'s campaign in the XXII Moekims, distinguishing himself personally at the storming of [[Benteng Lamgani|benting Lamgani]].
- His further distinguished service at Atjeh earned him appointment as Military Commander of [[ambon|Amboina]] and Ternate.
- Governor-General [[O. van Rees|'s Jacob]] came to value him highly in that capacity and placed him at Atjeh as Military Commander on the departure of Colonel [[Kolonel Schäfer|Schäfer]], warmly recommending him to his successor [[O. van Rees|van Rees]] for the governorship if it were again conferred upon a military officer.
- In September [[1884]] he assumed the governorship and, according to Brooshooft, fulfilled the trust placed in him with energy.

Demmeni had been decorated with the **Military William Order** (*Militaire Willemsorde*) and with the Knight's Cross of the [[Militaire Willemsorde|Order of the Netherlands Lion]]. Brooshooft concludes that the "excellent arrangement of our present position at Atjeh remains for him a more lasting monument" than any formal honour, and closes his book with the hope that Demmeni's successor would be "an energetic military governor" lest his work be broken off as [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heyden]]'s had been.

**Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)** was the military campaign led by lieutenant-colonel [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] — later governor and military commander of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — through the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]], [[Alasland|Alas-]] and [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]] of interior Atjeh in [[1904]], in the course of which, according to the official figures, 2,922 inlanders — among them 1,149 women and children — were killed "in weinige dagen" — in a few days. The expedition is the bloodiest single episode of the post-[[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|Oemar]] phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the political-military climax of the war's concluding phase.

## Origins

After the recovery of [[Groot-Atjeh]] following [[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|the defection of Toekoe Oemar]] in [[1896]], the principal resistance leaders did not submit but withdrew to [[Pedirse|Pedir]]. They were pursued — first to [[Pedirse|Pedir]], then to the coasts — and finally, in [[1904]], to the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]] and [[Alasland|Alaslanden]]. The expedition of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]], [[Alasland|Alas-]] and [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]] was, formally, intended to begin "bespreking van wederzijdse belangen" — discussion of mutual interests.

## The expedition

In practice the expedition became a campaign of annihilation. According to the official figures cited by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]], 2,922 inlanders — among them 1,149 women and children — were killed "in weinige dagen" — in a few days. The expedition "verstoorde echter reeds zeer spoedig de illusie van de naderende vrede" — shattered very soon the illusion of approaching peace — that [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]]'s [[Van Kol's 1902 traverse of Atjeh|1902 traverse]] of Atjeh had encouraged.

## Parliamentary reactions

[[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) declared the expedition "erger dan de dagen van Alva's schrikbewind" — worse than the days of Alva's reign of terror. The Catholic [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] went further back into history and compared the expedition to the campaigns of [[Timoer Lenk]] and [[Djengis Khan]]: "Het is of men op de inlanders een bende bloedhonden heeft losgelaten, het is beulenwerk" — it is as if a pack of bloodhounds had been set loose on the natives, it is the work of executioners. [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]]' conclusion echoed that of [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]]: "Laten wij beschaving brengen, maar niet door middel van bloedbaden! Wat is daarvoor dan nodig? Eén ding. Geduld. Dat nu ontbreekt".

## The Lohman-Rutgers defence

[[Lohman (De Savornin Lohman)|Lohman]] (c.h.) framed the question as one between "een voortdurende toestand van anarchie" — a perpetual state of anarchy — and "één bloedige oorlog, die ten slotte leidt tot orde, rust en recht" — one bloody war that finally leads to order, rest and right. His verdict: "Wanneer wij door middel van geweld de mensen willen onderwerpen, niet aan onze heerschappij, maar aan het gezag van recht en vrede, dan is dat een geoorloofd doel en dan meen ik, dat dit doel alle middelen heiligt" — when we wish, by means of violence, to subject men, not to our rule, but to the authority of right and peace, then that is a permissible end and then, I hold, this end sanctifies the means.

[[Rutgers van Rozenburg|Rutgers van Rozenburg]] (lib.) went further, justifying even the cutting-in-two of slain Atjehnese as a means to disarm the *prang sabil* preaching of the Atjehnese priests. There were, [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] drily notes, "weinig kamerleden die Rutgers van Rozenburg of Lohman in dergelijke theorieën wilden volgen, hoezeer zij ook het betrekkelijke gelijk aan hun zijde hadden" — few chambermembers who wished to follow them in such theories, however much they might have a relative right on their side.

## The ministerial defence

[[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] (a.r.) defended the expedition in the [[Tweede Kamer]]. Patience had been tried in Atjeh during the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie]] period with the result that the resistance had only grown stronger; patience had been tried in [[Djambi]] until "absolute anarchie" had set in; patience had been tried for years on [[Lombok]], and the [[Netherlands]] had had to pay for it with hundreds of lives. Following the argument of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[A. W. F. Idenburg|Idenburg]] maintained that Islam forbade the faithful to submit to any authority that had not proven its unshakeable superiority. The women and children who sacrificed themselves had done so under the influence of religious fanaticism: [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], it was argued, had made the futility of the struggle clear in advance.

## The De Stuers-Kolkman incident

[[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]]' comparison of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to [[Timoer Lenk]] was so far beyond the bounds of criticism customary for a member of a governing party that he was openly and solemnly *gedesavoueerd* — disavowed — by his fractieleider Kolkman. At the next begrotingsdebat — after the elections — [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] called Kolkman's statement "het product ener electorale nachtmerrie" — the product of an electoral nightmare. The members of the expedition had, meanwhile, received 148 decorations — including 44 military Willemsorden — for "180 bajonetten" (it was actually about 230), a generosity that [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]] found "wat overdadig".

## Significance

[[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|The 1904 expedition]] is the military and political hinge of the Atjehnese war. It broke the back of organised Atjehnese resistance; the colonial authority could thereafter begin, in earnest, the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] that would extend over the next four years and culminate in the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] report of [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908]]. The parliamentary debate it provoked is the moral climax of the Atjehnese-war record.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

**Van Daalen's expedition through the Gajo, Alas and Batak lands** — the march for which Lieutenant-Colonel [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]], then commanding in the Dependencies of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], was charged in February [[1904]] by Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Johannes van Heutsz]] — was named by author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]] "one of the principal military operations" ("een der voornaamste krijgstochten") of the Van Heutsz period, and prepared the definitive creation of the department of [[Gayo- en Alaslanden|Gajo- en Alaslanden]] in [[1908]].

### The commission

"The expedition to the Gajo- and Alas-lands, wherewith Colonel Van Daalen in February 1904 was charged" — thus Langhout announces it, one of the foremost military expeditions that took place under Van Heutsz's administration. For the military particulars of that march he refers to the book of [[J. C. J. Kempees]]: *"De tocht van Overste Van Daalen door de Gajo-, Alas- en Bataklanden"* ("The march of Colonel Van Daalen through the Gajo-, Alas- and Batak lands"), since his own theme permits only the economic reading of the period (and since the march together with the [[Pidië]] expedition framed Van Heutsz's war-years from [[1898]] onward).

### Consequences for the highlands

The expedition opened the central highlands to Dutch authority: the Gajo and Alas lands, already sketched into the first general map of Atjeh ([[1901]]) from data collected by Dr. C. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], received their administration from Van Daalen himself until, in May [[1904]] — one of Van Heutsz's last pacification measures — the department of the Dependencies of Atjeh passed from military hands to a civilian assistant-resident. When in [[1908]] the last piece of the province, the department of the Gajo- and Alaslanden, was definitively created, it was Governor Van Daalen who tried to realize the political unity of the whole province also in the economic sense: the [[Roads and bridges in Atjeh|Gajo road]] from [[Bireuen]] to the [[Laoet Tawar]], with the iron bridge at [[Teupen Blang Manè]] ([[1906]]), the introduction of the potato in the Gajo lands, and the traffic and credit measures of his administration (see [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]]).

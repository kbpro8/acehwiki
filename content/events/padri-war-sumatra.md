**Padri War (Sumatra)** was a religious‑military conflict that unfolded on the island of *Sumatra* in the early nineteenth century, pitting the reformist *Padri* movement against the Dutch colonial authorities, including the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]], and whose later phases intersected with the political interests of the neighboring [[Aceh]] Sultanate.

## Background  
The *Padri* movement, inspired by a strict interpretation of Islam and the teachings of the *Wahhabi* reformers, sought to purge local customs deemed un‑Islamic. Their uprising spread across western *Sumatra* and eventually drew the attention of the Dutch colonial administration, which aimed to protect its trade and territorial interests.

## Dutch Involvement  
Dutch military response included regular army units and specialised marine detachments. As recorded in the *De Geschiedenis van het Nederlandsche Korps Mariniers* (pp. 102‑104), a detachment of the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] took part in an **expedition against the Padri’s (1838)**, demonstrating the Crown’s willingness to project force beyond the immediate theatre of the war.

## Connection to Aceh  
Although the main theatre lay in West *Sumatra*, the *Padri* conflict affected the broader political landscape of the northern tip of the island. The [[Aceh]] Sultanate, wary of Dutch encroachment, monitored the war closely and at times offered covert support to Padri factions to counterbalance Dutch influence.

## Outcome  
The Dutch ultimately subdued the Padri resistance, consolidating their control over the interior of *Sumatra*. The war’s conclusion paved the way for further Dutch expansion toward the Aceh coast, setting the stage for the later **Aceh War (1873‑1904)**.

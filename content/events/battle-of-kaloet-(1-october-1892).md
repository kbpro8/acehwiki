**The Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)** was the principal Dutch military assault on the Atjehnese fortification of [[Kaloet]] in the [[XXV Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — an assault by a strong column from [[Lambaroe]] under a captain and three lieutenants, supported by 75 men from [[Lampermej]] and 50 men of the 14th Battalion, that was repulsed with 7 killed and more than 30 wounded, including Lieutenant [[Hoolboom]] (mortally wounded) and Lieutenant [[Drijber]]. The engagement was the principal Dutch military failure of the late *concentratie* period, and the [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] characterises the failure as evidence that the *benting* of Kaloet could not be taken by assault — even with a force five times stronger than the one that was actually employed.

## The previous reconnaissance

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)]] was preceded by two earlier operations against the fortification. The first was the night-patrol of Lieutenant [[Brant]] of the *maréchaussée* on the night of [[22 August|22-23 August]] [[1892]], which lay in ambush in the dry bed of the [[Kroeng Kali]] opposite Kaloet but was surprised by the Atjehnese garrison's *versliepen zich* (oversleeping) and had to return to [[Lamreng]] at 7 a.m. without having caught anyone. The second was the [[Reconnaissance of Kaloet (23 August 1892)|reconnaissance of 23 August 1892]] — a reconnaissance column under Captain [[Jhr. Graafland]] with Lieutenant [[Brant]] as guide, which set out from Lamreng up the [[Kroeng Tandjong]] and destroyed the Atjehnese fortifications in the river before approaching to within 50 metres of Kaloet's *vóórwal* (front wall) and withdrawing. A further attempt was made on [[27 September]] [[1892]] — when a company from Lambaroe attempted to take the *benting* by surprise and was repulsed.

## The assault of 1 October 1892

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the [[Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)|battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892)]] was a *krachtige poging* (forceful attempt) at the *verovering* (capture) of the *benting*. The assault was undertaken by a strong column from [[Lambaroe]] under a captain and three lieutenants — supported by 75 men from [[Lampermej]], 50 men of the 14th Battalion, and a detachment of *maréchaussée*s — which approached Kaloet from the north-east at 5 a.m. and was *in stelling* (in position) by 5 a.m. A second detachment, from [[Lamreng]], attempted to approach the west side of the *benting*.

## The Dutch repulse

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the assault was repulsed. The album's narrative describes the Atjehnese defensive works — *vol met allerlei verdedigingswerkjes en hindernissen* — *full of all kinds of defensive works and obstacles*: open earthworks 1 to 2 metres broad and man-high, with parapets, on the flanks bent backwards; some with *geschutstand* (gun-positions) for *lila*s (swivel-guns); deep pits *waarin punjoes, glasscherven enz.* (in which bamboo stakes, glass shards etc.) were placed, with the opening *zooveel mogelijk bedekt* (as much as possible covered); *Weerbarstige sterke plant, met doorns* — *Stubborn strong plant, with thorns*; *Scherp aangepunte stokjes, die vooral voor de ongehoede inlanders hoogst gevaarlijk zijn* — *Sharply pointed stakes, that are especially dangerous for the incautious natives*.

The column was repulsed at all points. Lieutenant [[Drijber]] *veroverde hier een drietal poorten* (captured here three gates), but *in den nauwen gang werden zijne mannen van uit ter zijde aangebrachte bamboekokers als muizen neergeschoten* (in the narrow lane his men were shot down like mice from bamboo-tubes placed at the side). Drijber himself was *zwaar gekwetst* (severely bruised). With much effort he managed to withdraw with all the wounded, dead and weapons. Lieutenant [[Hoolboom]] *kreeg een doodelijk schot in het hoofd* (received a mortal shot in the head) and died the next day. The total casualties were 7 killed and more than 30 wounded — mostly severely.

## The Dutch response

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that, after the failed assault of [[1 October]] [[1892]], General [[Deykerhoff]] *wilde niet op nieuw kostbare menschenlevens wagen* (did not want to risk precious human lives again) — even though *van verschillende zijden stemmen opgingen, die eischten dat het échec gewroken zou worden* (from various sides voices were raised demanding that the failure be avenged). The album characterises Deykerhoff's refusal as an act of moral courage: *Voor het weigeren van dit verlangen, daar waar de eer het niet strikt noodzakelijk maakte, is misschien meer moed noodig geweest, dan voor het toestaan* — *For the refusal of this demand, there where honour did not strictly require it, perhaps more courage was necessary than for the granting*.

The album records that Kaloet was *voorloopig ongemoeid* (provisionally left alone) until [[Toekoe Oemar]] *het in onze handen speelde, zonder dat het een druppel bloeds meer kostte* (played it into our hands without costing another drop of blood) — a reference to the [[Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)|bloodless capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar in August 1893]].

## Significance

The Battle of Kaloet (1 October 1892) is significant in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as the principal Dutch military failure of the late *concentratie* period — the engagement that demonstrated the impossibility of taking the *benting* of Kaloet by assault, even with a strong column. The engagement cost the life of Lieutenant [[Hoolboom]] and severely wounded Lieutenant [[Drijber]], and it produced General [[Deykerhoff]]'s refusal to risk further lives — the principal documented Dutch act of military restraint of the late *concentratie* period. The engagement was followed, eleven months later, by the [[Capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar (August 1893)|bloodless capture of Kaloet by Toekoe Oemar]] — the principal Dutch-Atjehnese auxiliary success of the same period.

**Samalanga Campaign and Assault on Batèë Ilië** was an intensive military operation conducted in [[1901]] in [[Samalanga]], culminating in the storming and capture of the famous mountain fortress of [[Batèë Ilië]].

## Narrative of the Assault

For decades, the mountain fortress of [[Batèë Ilië]] in Samalanga had been celebrated across Aceh as an unassailable bastion of resistance. Situated atop sheer, rocky cliffs surrounded by deep ravines, the fortress was defended by religious scholars (*ulama*) and armed bands who had successfully repulsed previous Dutch expeditions dating back to the governorship of General [[Karel van der Heyden]].

In [[1901]], Governor [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] ordered a multi-pronged assault on Samalanga. While heavy artillery bombarded the lower outer fortifications, elite strike columns of the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]], armed with [[Klewang|klewangs]] and carbines, scaled the precipitous rock faces under cover of darkness and mountain mist.

The Marechaussee troops achieved tactical surprise, cresting the inner walls and engaging the defenders in fierce hand-to-hand combat. After intense fighting, the stronghold was completely taken by storm, and its defensive works were demolished.

## Consequences

The fall of Batèë Ilië eliminated the last major static mountain fortress in eastern Aceh, breaking organized regional resistance and compelling numerous local lords to submit to colonial governance.

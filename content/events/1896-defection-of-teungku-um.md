**The 1896 defection of Teungku Um** was the act by which [[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]] — the Atjehnese warlord whom the governor of Atjeh [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]] and the governor-general [[Pijnacker Hordijk|Pijnacker Hordijk]] had armed and subsidised against the explicit warning of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] — "wierp het masker af" (threw off the mask) and turned his modern-Dutch arms against his former patrons. The [[1896]] defection is, in the source's narrative, the immediate political-military hinge of the [[Atjehoorlog|Atjehoorlog]].

## The arming and subsidising

The source records that [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]] and [[Pijnacker Hordijk|Pijnacker Hordijk]] "schenkten zij hun vertrouwen aan Teungku Um, die officieel begenadigd werd, een legioen van 250 man op onze kosten mocht onderhouden en later nog 1000 geweren kreeg, veel ammunitie en geld" (placed their trust in Teungku Um, who was officially granted clemency, permitted to maintain a legion of 250 men at our expense, and later received a further 1,000 rifles, much ammunition and money). The arming and subsidising was undertaken "tegen diens waarschuwing in" (against his warning) — i.e. against the explicit [[1892]] warning of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]].

## The 1896 defection

The source records: "Korte tijd daarna (1896) wierp Teungku Um het masker af en hadden onze soldaten te vechten tegen met modern geweren bewapende Atjehers" (A short time later (1896) Teungku Um threw off the mask, and our soldiers had to fight against Atjehnese armed with modern rifles). The "masker" metaphor is the source's principal characterisation of [[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]]'s pre-[[1896]] posture: a co-operative warlord whose "masker" of co-operation concealed an Atjehnese resistance that the Dutch had armed.

## The Van der Wijck reversal

The [[1896]] defection produced the [[Van der Wijck (governor-general)|Van der Wijck]] reversal: "De G.-G. Van der Wijck (1893-99) begreep, dat Snouck Hurgronje's adviezen gevolgd moesten worden. Ook de overste Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz had dit begrepen. In 1896 werd hij belast met de leiding der militaire operaties in Groot-Atjeh". The [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] appointment of [[1896]] is the operational consequence of the [[1896]] defection: the [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] advice was now to be implemented, and [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] was the officer charged with its implementation.

## Significance

The [[1896]] defection of [[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]] is, in the source's narrative, the immediate political-military hinge of the [[Atjehoorlog|Atjehoorlog]]. The defection — together with the contemporaneous [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] appointment — marks the [[1896]] turning-point of the war.

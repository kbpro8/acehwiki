**Langkat's appeal to Siak against Atjeh (1862)** was the formal request, made in [[1862]] by the [[Radja of Langkat|Radja of Langkat]] to [[Tongkoe Ismail|Sultan Tongkoe Ismail of Siak]], for an act of Siak recognition that would unlock the protection of the [[Netherlands-Indies Government|Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement]] against [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] oppression. Recorded by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] as the immediate occasion for the Dutch intervention that extinguished [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|Atjehnese overlordship over the East Coast]], the appeal is the moment at which the East Coast statelets — formally Siak dependencies, in fact Atjehnese tributaries since [[1854]] — turned back to [[Siak|Siak]] and the Dutch as their protectors against [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

## Background: the Atjehnese overlordship since 1854

The appeal of [[1862]] was made against the background of the [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship]]. The Bureau records that [[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Deli|Deli]] and [[Langkat|Langkat]] — having freed themselves from [[Siak|Siak]]'s influence in the decay of the Siak sultanate after [[Sultan Said Ali|Sultan Said Ali]]'s death in [[1821]] — had been forced, since [[1854]], to admit the overlordship of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The Atjehnese overlordship was not a light burden; the [[Radja of Langkat|Radja of Langkat]] was, the Bureau records, "de onderdrukking door Atjeh moede" — "weary of the oppression by Atjeh." His appeal to [[Siak|Siak]] was a deliberate strategic move: by securing Siak's formal recognition of Langkat's status as a Siak dependency, he would unlock the protection of the Netherlands-Indies Government, which had formally guaranteed Siak's overlordship over the East Coast statelets in the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|Siak tractate of 1 February [[1858]]]].

## The appeal

The Bureau records the appeal in a single, dense sentence: "In dat jaar wendde de Radja van Langkat, de onderdrukking door Atjeh moede, zich tot zijn vroegeren opperheer Siak, om door diens erkenning de hulp van het N. I. Gouvern. tegen Atjeh te verkrijgen" — "In that year [[1862]] the Radja of Langkat, weary of the oppression by Atjeh, turned to his former overlord Siak, in order, through Siak's recognition, to obtain the help of the Netherlands-Indies Government against Atjeh."

The appeal was thus a triple-stranded instrument: an act of submission to [[Siak|Siak]] (formally re-establishing the Siak overlordship that had lapsed in practice); an act of deference to [[Tongkoe Ismail|Sultan Tongkoe Ismail of Siak]] personally (whose intercession with the Netherlands-Indies Government was the immediate channel of Dutch protection); and an act of appeal to the [[Netherlands-Indies Government|Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement]] (whose guarantee of Siak's overlordship in the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|Siak tractate of 1858]] was the legal basis on which Dutch protection could now be claimed).

## The Dutch response: the Netscher mission

The response of the Netherlands-Indies Government was rapid. "Hierna werd, op aandrang van Tongkoe Ismail van Siak, door het Gouvernement ten slotte aan den resident [[Netscher|Netscher]] van [[Riouw en Onderhoorigheden|Riouw]], waaronder Siak toen nog ressorteerde, in [[1862]] opgedragen, meer aanrakingen te zoeken met de verschillende hoofden der in het Siak-tractaat als onderhoorigheden van Siak genoemde staatjes, teneinde deze te bewegen tot eene erkenning van die onderhoorigheid" — "Hereafter, at the insistence of [[Tongkoe Ismail|Tongkoe Ismail of Siak]], the Government finally instructed [[Netscher|Resident Netscher of Riouw]] — under whose jurisdiction Siak then still fell — in [[1862]] to seek more contacts with the various chiefs of the statelets named in the Siak tractate as dependencies of Siak, in order to persuade them to an acknowledgement of that dependency."

The [[Netscher|Netscher]] mission of [[1862]] was thus the direct sequel to the Langkat appeal. The statelets of [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Langkat|Langkat]] and several smaller polities signed acts of recognition of Siak's overlordship; [[Asahan|Asahan]] refused, and was reduced by armed force in [[1865]]. In [[1866]] a Dutch naval intervention ended the "vexaties der Atjehers in het Langkatsche" — "the vexations of the Atjehers in the Langkat territory" — and drove the Atjehers back into their own territory.

## Significance

The [[1862]] appeal is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the diplomatic turning-point of the nineteenth-century East Coast. It is the moment at which the East Coast statelets, formally Siak dependencies, in fact Atjehnese tributaries, were transferred — by their own act of appeal — from the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] to the Dutch sphere. The appeal thus closes the [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|1854 reimposition of Atjehnese overlordship]] and opens the sequence of Dutch interventions — the [[Netscher|Netscher]] mission of [[1862]], the [[dutch-marine-expulsion-of-atjehers-from-langkat-(1866)|naval expulsion of the Atjehers in 1866]], the [[1878 Langkat–Tamiang transfer|1878 transfer of Langkat's Tamiang rights]] — by which the East Coast was finally absorbed into the colonial state. The appeal is also, in the longer perspective, the immediate prelude to the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]: the loss of its East Coast tributaries was one of the principal grievances of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] in the breakdown of Dutch-Atjehnese relations.

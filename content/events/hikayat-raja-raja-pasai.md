**Hikayat Raja-raja Pasai** is a Malay chronicle detailing the genealogy and reigns of the sultans of [[Pasai]], an early Islamic sultanate on the north Sumatra coast within the [[Aceh]] region. The work is a key source for the political and cultural history of the western archipelago from the thirteenth to sixteenth centuries.

## Textual History
The Hikayat exists in numerous manuscript copies and was first brought to scholarly attention through the edition of [[E. Dulaurier|Dulaurier]], who printed selected passages with French translation. Subsequent editions include the [[Bijdragen tot de Taal-, Land- en Volkenkunde van Nederlandsch-Indië|B.K.I.]] volume 65 (1911), pages 232–245, and the version edited by [[J. P. Mead]] published in the [[Journal of the Straits Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society|J.S.B.R.ASS]] volume 66 (1914). The text also appears in linguistic discussions concerning Malay lexical items, as noted by scholars such as [[R. J. Wilkinson|Wilkinson]] and [[Klinkert]].

## Content
The chronicle records the lineage of Pasai’s rulers, their conversion to Islam, diplomatic relations with neighbouring polities, and the eventual decline of the sultanate. It contains passages that have been cited for the study of Malay proverbs, legal formulae, and poetic expression, including references to the *Burda* of [[Al-Busiri]] in later Malay literary contexts.

## Significance
As one of the oldest surviving Malay historical texts, the Hikayat Raja-raja Pasai provides essential insight into the early Islamisation of Sumatra, the formation of Malay court culture, and the intercultural exchanges that preceded the rise of the [[Aceh Sultanate]]. Its linguistic richness makes it a frequent reference point in dictionaries and grammars of classical Malay.

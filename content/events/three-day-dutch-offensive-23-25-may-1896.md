**The Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896** — the *operatiën tegen de VI Moekims* and the *bestorming van Oemar's stelling* — was the major three-day combined-arms offensive ordered by the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], against [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s fortified *stelling* around [[Lam-Asan]], [[Lam-Pisang]], [[Tjot Tjako]], [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Lam-Tjroeng]], and [[Lam-Poelau]], the central military event of the first semester of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the strategic reversal of the disaster provoked by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. The offensive was executed by a complex of colonnes — the **Bergcolonne** under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]], the **Colonne Lamtih** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet|Lt.-Col. van Vliet]], the **Colonne Djeroe Loeng** under [[Majoor J. F. T. Veeren|Maj. Veeren]], the **Colonne tegen de XXII Moekims** under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Maj. Okhuizen]], and the **Reserve Colonne** under [[Majoor J. R. Jacobs|Maj. Jacobs]], with overall command vested in [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] — and culminated in the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|storming of Tjot Tjako]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]], the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's house]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]], and the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|bestorming of the Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]]. By the evening of [[25 May]] [[1896]] Oemar's warriors had retreated behind the [[Kroeng-Raba]] and his house at [[Lam-Pisang]] had been [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|destroyed with dynamite, tar, and petroleum]].

## Background

By the third week of [[May]] [[1896]] the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had completed the [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|evacuation of the buitenlinie]], the [[Dismission of Teuku Oemar (26 April 1896)|formal dismission of Teuku Oemar]] of [[26 April]] [[1896]], the [[Proclamation of Vetter (27 April 1896)|proclamation of 27 April]] announcing military operations, and four weeks of [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|sustained bombardment of Umar's stellingen]] from the battery at [[Lam-Djamoe]]. The Dutch forces were concentrated and ready. The Achehnese — pinned between the land bombardment at [[Lam-Pisang]] and the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai]] — were tired, their communications disrupted, and their works degraded.

The Dutch intelligence picture of Oemar's *stelling* was exhaustive. A detailed reconnaissance — preserved in the source as an intelligence summary organised by left wing, centre, and right wing — listed every *versterking*, *loopgraaf*, and *benteng* with location and estimated occupation. Lead was hidden in wells at [[Bineh Kroeng]]; men from [[Pidië|Pedir]] were stationed at [[Boekit Kesoemba|Bt. Kesoemba]]; Oemar himself was hiding south of the *heuvelrug*; the people of [[Lam-Pagar]] had a warlike reputation; [[Panglima Gimba]] held the left wing; [[Pa Karim Lampagar]] was at [[Lam-Pagar]]; [[T. Mat Amin]] — an Islamic scholar — held a sector; [[Teungkoe Pantai Glima]] and [[Imam Djempit]] were also named. The stage was set for the assault.

The Dutch order of battle was divided into five colonnes. The **Bergcolonne** under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] — Chef van den Staf [[Kapitein G. C. E. van Daalen|Kapt. van Daalen]] — comprised 10 brigades *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] (4 officers, 189 men), the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor G. A. Hansen|Maj. Hansen]] (15 officers, 403 men), engineers under [[1e Luit. A. S. Ruzette|1e Luit. Ruzette]], train, ambulance, reserve munition, and a section of mountain artillery under [[Kapitein Kempers|Kapt. Kempers]]. Its mission was the storming of [[Tjot Tjako]]. The **Colonne Lamtih** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet|Lt.-Col. van Vliet]] comprised the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters|Lt.-Col. Soeters]] (18 officers, 240 European, 205 native), the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. van der Noordaa]] (16 officers, 142 European, 312 native), a cavalry platoon under Lisnet (32 European), two sections of mountain artillery under [[Kapitein G. J. E. Nauta|Kapt. Nauta]], and engineers under [[Kapitein Zelle|Kapt. Zelle]]. The **Colonne Djeroe Loeng** under [[Majoor J. F. T. Veeren|Maj. Veeren]] comprised the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] (14 officers, 500 men), engineers under [[1e Luit. E. J. de Rochemont|1e Luit. de Rochemont]], a section of mountain artillery under Deibert, a cavalry platoon under [[1e Luit. C. W. F. Happé|1e Luit. Happé]] (32 *sabels*), and an ambulance under Diephuis. The **Colonne tegen de XXII Moekims** under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Maj. Okhuizen]] (who also commanded the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]]) comprised the 6e Bataljon (14 officers, 467 men including 243 European), a section of mountain artillery under Steenkamp, and an ambulance under [[Off. van Gesondheid J. L. W. F. van Leent|van Leent]]. The **Reserve Colonne** under [[Majoor J. R. Jacobs|Maj. Jacobs]] comprised 12 officers, 253 European and 176 native, plus two sections of mountain artillery under [[Kapitein Kempers|Kapt. Kempers]] and a cavalry platoon. Overall command vested in [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]].

## First Day — 23 May 1896: The Storming of Tjot Tjako

On [[23 May]] [[1896]] the **Bergcolonne** under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] marched from [[Ketapan Doea]] at 3:30 a.m. to surprise the enemy. The voorhoede under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] — 10 brigades *maréchaussée* and a peloton of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] — advanced via the footpath north-west of Ketapan Doea. At 5:20 a.m. the climb of [[Tjot Tjako]] began in two groups: lieutenants [[H. M. Vis|Vis]] and Wagener on the eastern slope, lieutenants Stoop and Dijkstra on the north-eastern side. The enemy raised alarm cries and rolled stones down on the climbing Dutch. At 5:43 a.m. lieutenants Wagener and Vis, with the *maréchaussée* Wongsodikromo (No. 33780) and Todikromo (No. 28097), plus lieutenant Rijnen and European fusiliers, reached the summit. Lieutenant Vis then drove approximately 30 armed Achehnese from their *schietstellingen* on the north-eastern slope. By 6:40 a.m. the entire colonne was on top; the enemy was driven from [[Tjot Tjako]]. From the summit, repeater-rifle fire was directed on [[Lam-Pisang]] and Oemar's house, causing some 50 persons to flee south.

Sergeant Grebner, on his own initiative, attacked the western ridgeline, drove off the enemy (leaving one dead), and occupied the southernmost top — an action that caused [[Lam-Pisang]] and [[Bineh Kroeng]] to be evacuated. Van Heutsz sent a series of four messages to the commander: the first at 6:40 a.m. reporting that [[Tjot Tjako]] was not climbable for artillery; the second at 8:17 a.m. requesting reserve munition, drinking water, and ice; the third at 9:15 a.m. — the crucial fourth message (received by the *bevelhebber* only at 11:15 a.m.) — reporting that [[Lam-Pisang]] was abandoned and that the troops could advance. This fourth message reversed the *bevelhebber*'s initial decision, taken on the advice of the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] by telephone, to halt operations for the day.

Concurrently, the **Colonne Lamtih** under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet|van Vliet]] engaged the enemy at [[Lam-Manjang]] and [[Lam-Isi]]. The [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters|Soeters]] talso position at [[Lam-Manjang]] but was held down by enemy fire; the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] supported. The **Colonne Djeroe Loeng** under [[Majoor J. F. T. Veeren|Veeren]] advanced on [[Lam-Asan]]. The [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor J. R. Jacobs|Maj. Jacobs]] advanced along a narrow footpath and came under heavy fire from the south-eastern *rand* of [[Lam-Asan]]: 2e lieutenant Ramaer and several men were wounded, and the service horse of Majoor Jacobs was killed. Without waiting for the "voorwaarts" signal, Jacobs ordered the 1e compagnie under [[Kapitein van Gemmingen|Kapt. van Gemmingen]] to attack. The compagnie crossed 200 m of muddy *sawah* with élan, led by 1e luit. Verstege; kapitein van Gemmingen, korporaal Egeter, and hoornblazer Bollegraaf were first into the *versterking*. The 3e compagnie under [[Kapitein Scheuer|Kapt. Scheuer]] covered the flank. The reserve compagnie under luit. Roijen followed. The north-eastern *rand* of Lam-Asan fell similarly to the 9e Bataljon. The *bevelhebber* [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] came in person with his staff to inspect. The first day ended with the Dutch in possession of [[Tjot Tjako]] and [[Lam-Asan]], and with Oemar's forces retreating south. The bivak of the 7e Bataljon was heavily fired on from [[Lam-Doerian]] from 4:30 to 5:30 p.m.; a shrapnel shell hitting the enemy *versterking* face ended the fire.

## Second Day — 24 May 1896: The Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House

On [[24 May]] [[1896]] the **Colonne Laceulle** — newly formed under [[Kapitein C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. C. J. Laceulle]] of the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e Bataljon]] (12 officers, 364 men), with two sections of mountain artillery (Vrijdag, van der Waarden), two cavalry platoons under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|Ritmeester de Lannoy]] (3 officers, 64 riders), and engineers under [[Kapitein Zelle|Kapt. Zelle]] — marched from [[Lam-Djamoe]] at 6 a.m. Pre-dawn, the Bergcolonne (*maréchaussée*) occupied the southern top from which so much fire had come the day before, plus a stelling in the saddle with the lone tree; from these positions the *maréchaussée* could observe the entire valley below. The bridge in the road to [[Pakan-Badak]] (broken by the enemy) had to be repaired by the engineers (1½ hours). Three companies of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] marched toward [[Lam-Isi]] and from there to [[Lam-Gemoek]] and [[Lam-Roekam]].

The cavalry under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|de Lannoy]] reconnoitred toward [[Lam-Pisang]]. At 8:30 a.m. the first shots came from the south-western *kampong*s. Laceulle advanced to the northern *rand* of [[Lam-Goe-Oe]]. Heavy fire came from a *versterking* in the south-western *rand*. At 9:46 a.m. the cavalry reported the gebergte west of Gemi-Roi strongly occupied; at 9:56 a.m. many enemy were descending toward Lampoih-Eumpei; at 10:06 a.m. [[Pasar Oleh-Gli]], Rimah's, and Gemi-Roi were *ontruimd*; at 10:13 a.m. **[[Lam-Pisang]] and Oemar's house were reported *onbezet* but under heavy fire from the west**. At 10:20 a.m. the order came to advance to the south corner of Gemi-Roi — already reached. The 1e compagnie of the 5e Bataljon under [[Kapitein Boer|Kapt. Boer]] advanced with the 1e sectie under [[1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh|1e Luit. de Jongh]]. Heavy fire from west and south-west opened as the sectie left [[Pasar Oleh-Gli]]. The colonne-commandant ordered the 4e and 2e compagnie to cross the dyke at fastest pace, with artillery in battery on the dyke answering enemy fire.

**At 10:35 a.m., 1e luit. M. D. J. de Jongh — accompanied by only three the rest in the *rawah* or wounded — was the first to reach Oemar's *verblijf* at Lam-Pisang**, immediately followed by sectie 4e compagnie under [[Kapitein J. P. Linck|Kapt. Linck]], then a small cavalry detachment. A hoornblazer of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] on the eastern hills blew the "Wilhelmus" to announce the capture. A patrol under [[1e Luit. C. W. F. Happé|luit. Happé]] pulled down an Achehnese flag. The text explicitly corrects a popular legend that credited the cavalry (wachtmeester Jonker) with being first; the official text credits de Jongh, noting the cavalry legend was "minder juist" (less accurate). Some small cannons, furniture, and the [[Correspondentie van Oemar|correspondence of Umar]] were captured. unhusked rice stocks in various houses were burned. Oemar's house was subsequently destroyed with dynamite, tar, petroleum.

The **Colonne v.d. Noordaa** — three companies of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] (11 officers, 324 men), with [[Kapitein van den generalen staf H. C. Kronouer|Kapt. Kronouer]] of the General Staff attached — occupied [[Lam-Roekam]] and [[Lam-Oedjoeng]]. A failed night surprise attempt on [[Lam-Tiroeng]] / [[Boekit Seboen]] closed the day.

## Third Day — 25 May 1896: The Bestorming of Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau

On [[25 May]] [[1896]] [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] planned to drive the enemy from the hill-position [[Boekit Seboen]]–[[Lam-Poelau]]–[[Lam-Tjroeng]]–[[Daman]]. Three battalions with auxiliary weapons participated. The enemy *stelling* was threatened by artillery fire in front and on the left flank, plus feint attacks; the main attack was to be a flanking movement on the right flank. Reinforcements were brought up: the [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]], 1½ companies of the [[7e Bataljon Infanterie|7e Bataljon]], two companies of *maréchaussée*, two cavalry platoons, one engineer peloton, two sections of mountain artillery, and three ambulances.

At 6:30 a.m. the cavalry under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|de Lannoy]] marched from [[Lam-Djamoe]] to reconnoitre toward [[Boekit Seboen]]. The [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]] talso stelling along the foot of the gebergte near [[Lam-Pisang]]. At 7:55 a.m. the enemy fired from a top approximately 70 m high; the 9e Bataljon occupied it. At 9:30 a.m. 2½ companies of the 7e Bataljon under [[Majoor J. R. Jacobs|Jacobs]] were placed at [[Majoor J. F. T. Veeren|Veeren]]'s disposal. At 10:45 a.m. optical signal communication with [[Ketapan Doea]] was established. The cavalry found [[Lam-Goe-Oe-Zuid]] (Lam Oekoe) *onbezet*; the northern *rand* of [[Beradin]] was weakly held; about 6 enemies left the southern *rand versterking* and fled to [[Lam-Poelau]]; a band of about 25–30 men on [[Boekit Seboen]] fired on [[1e Luit. C. W. F. Happé|luit. Happé]]'s patrol. Beradin was assigned to the 5de compagnie of the 12e Bataljon under [[Kapitein S. A. Drijber|Kapt. Drijber]]. The cavalry's initial report was wrong — Drijber's compagnie was met by maintained crossfire from *kampong*, *versterking*, and western hills. Thomson led a *stormhandeling* on the *versterking* with about 10 men; the hoornblazer Karel, Beekman, and van den Wollenberg distinguished themselves. Drijber was wounded, with 12 *minderen*. Five artillery sections opened fire on enemy positions.

The [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor D. A. Okhuizen|Okhuizen]] was ordered to outflank and attack the enemy position at [[Daman]] and [[Lam-Tjroeng]]. The 3e compagnie (kapitein Janssen) was to approach Lam-Tjroeng over the hills covered, the 1e compagnie along the foot of the gebergte. Janssen marched through scorching cogon grass to a small *versterking* at Daman. After a few salvoes, the 1e peloton under [[1e Luit. Keetell|1e Luit. Keetell]] stormed. The 12-man garrison fled toward [[Lam-Tjroeng]]. The 2e luit. Lamster was first into the Lam-Tjroeng *versterking* when it was attacked next. **By 11:45 a.m. the entire stelling Daman–Lam-Tjroeng–Lam-Poelau was *ontruimd* by the enemy.**

The afternoon was devoted to the destruction of the enemy's *kampong*s. The cavalry was ordered to continue reconnaissance to Lama-Paja, Lam-Gaboes, and Anak-Paja, and, if *onbezet*, to burn them. The [[12e Bataljon Infanterie|12e Bataljon]] under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. F. Soeters|Soeters]] marched to Djirat with orders to burn all *kampong*s on the return. Soeters also had the 5e compagnie burn [[Lam-Caboes]] and the 2e compagnie burn [[Lam-Paja]]. The *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Graafland]] reached [[Anak-Paja]] and burned the houses. At Djirat, [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|de Lannoy]] decided to also investigate [[Lam-Kroet]] and [[Lho-Nga]]. About 10 enemies posted on the right bank of the [[Kroeng-Raja]] fired on the cavalry, preventing closer reconnaissance of the bridge. Soeters sent the order: "Keer terug, en steek so molike Lho-Nga on fire" (Return, and, if possible, set Lho-Nga on fire). After burning about 10 houses, de Lannoy halted the burning — considering battle-readiness — and returned to [[Boekit Seboen]]. To make the punishment as complete as possible, the *bevelhebber* at 1 p.m. sent a compagnie of the 5e Bataljon from Lam-Poelau to [[Mantjang]], [[Lam-Baroe]], and [[Lamlon]]; at 1:50 p.m. a peloton of the 6e Bataljon to [[Noesa]] — all to destroy those *kampong*s.

By the evening of [[25 May]] [[1896]] the task was accomplished: Oemar's warriors had retreated behind the [[Kroeng-Raba]]; Umar's house at [[Lam-Pisang]] had been destroyed; the *stellingen* of [[Tjot Tjako]], [[Boekit Seboen]], [[Lam-Tjroeng]], [[Daman]], and [[Lam-Poelau]] were in Dutch hands; the *kampong*s of [[Anak-Paja]], [[Djirat]], [[Lho-Nga]], [[Lam-Caboes]], [[Lam-Paja]], [[Mantjang]], [[Lam-Baroe]], [[Lamlon]], and [[Noesa]] had been burned. Stemfoort decided to hold the captured positions overnight and to retire under cover of darkness to [[Lam-Pisang]], where they would stay until the next morning.

## Significance

The Three-day Dutch offensive of [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]] was the strategic reversal of the disaster provoked by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. In three days of hard fighting the Dutch had broken Umar's *stelling*, captured and destroyed his house at [[Lam-Pisang]], driven his warriors behind the [[Kroeng-Raba]], and burned the principal *kampong*s of the [[VI Moekims]]. The offensive also marked the emergence of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as the pre-eminent field commander of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — the architect of the Bergcolonne's night march on [[Tjot Tjako]] and the recipient of the crucial fourth message that reversed the *bevelhebber*'s initial decision to halt. The Dutch casualties of the three days — though not enumerated here in full — were heavy, and the Achehnese had not been destroyed: Umar himself had escaped south, and his warriors would continue the war for years to come. But the strategic initiative had been recovered, the prestige of the Dutch arms restored, and the conditions set for the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lam-Krak mukims]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]]. The three-day offensive was, in this sense, the high point of the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] interregnum and the principal military achievement of the first semester of [[1896]].

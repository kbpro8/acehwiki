**The Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)** was a Dutch military engagement fought at the Dutch post of [[Tjot Rang]] in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] on [[14 April]] [[1896]] — five days after the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]]. The engagement was the second of the three relief operations of April [[1896]], and it was fought to relieve the besieged post at Tjot Rang — reached by a *bruikbaar pad* (serviceable path) from [[Tjot Iri]] of the *geconcentreerde linie*. The engagement cost the life of Lieutenant [[H. L. Maarschalk|Maarschalk]] and three subordinates, with 47 subordinates wounded, and it also saw the burning of [[Lamgoet]] — the former seat of [[Teungku di Tiro]].

## The strategic context

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that, after [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]], the post at [[Tjot Rang]] — together with the posts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Senelop]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] — was besieged by the forces of the defected warlord. The album's narrative of the spring [[1896]] crisis places the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]] as the second of the three April relief operations — between the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]] and the relief of [[Anagaloeng]] of [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1896]].

## The engagement

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the engagement at Tjot Rang was *niet minder hevig dan die bij Biloel had plaats gehad* — *not less fierce than that which had taken place at Biloel*. The album's terse narrative records the casualties in a single sentence: *Luitenant Maarschalk en drie minderen daarbij sneuvelden en dat er 47 minderen gewond werden* — *Lieutenant Maarschalk and three subordinates fell there, and 47 subordinates were wounded*. The engagement was, in the album's narrative, fought to relieve the besieged post at Tjot Rang, and it was the second of the three relief operations of April [[1896]].

## The burning of Lamgoet

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]] also saw the burning of [[Lamgoet]] — the former seat of [[Teungku di Tiro]] that had *den generaals Demmenie en Van Teijn zoo lastig* (so troubled the generals Demmenie and Van Teijn). The album notes that Lamgoet had been briefly occupied by [[Toekoe Bantah]] in [[1893]], that Toekoe Bantah had been unable to maintain himself there, and that it had been [[Toekoe Oemar]] who had brought Lamgoet a second time under Dutch control. The simultaneous relief of Tjot Rang and burning of Lamgoet on [[14 April]] [[1896]] — both costly engagements — was, in the album's narrative, the moment at which the Dutch began to recover the initiative from [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s defected forces.

## The sound of the engagement at Anagaloeng

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the besieged garrison at [[Anagaloeng]] — some four days' march from Tjot Rang — heard the sound of the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]], but could not distinguish it from the sound of the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]], because the sound of Tjot Rang was *niet weerkaatst … tegen den hoogen en steilen bergwand* (not echoed against the high and steep mountain wall) to the south of Anagaloeng. The besieged garrison could only judge from the sound *of de strijd nog veel noordelijker, bij Toengkoop gevoerd werd* — *whether the battle was still being conducted much more to the north, near Toengkoop*.

## Significance

The Battle of Tjot Rang is significant in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as the second of the three relief operations of April [[1896]] — the engagement that produced the fourth officer fatality of the spring [[1896]] crisis, Lieutenant [[H. L. Maarschalk|Maarschalk]]. The engagement was also the occasion of the burning of [[Lamgoet]] — the former seat of [[Teungku di Tiro]] — and it was, in the album's narrative, one of the moments at which the Dutch began to recover the initiative from [[Toekoe Oemar]]'s defected forces. The engagement's high casualty count — three subordinates killed and 47 wounded, in addition to Maarschalk — places it among the costliest single engagements of the spring [[1896]] crisis.

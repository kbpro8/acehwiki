**The Three-Column Operation to Free Tjot Rang and Kroeng Gloempang (21 April 1896)** was the last major Dutch operation of the retreat phase of the spring [[1896]] crisis — a three-colonne descent, comprising the Noordercolonne under [[Majoor G. F. Soeters|Maj. Soeters]], the Middencolonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]], and the Zuidercolonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet|Lt.-Col. van Vliet]] / [[Luitenant-Kolonel H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]], mounted one day after the [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|formal completion of the buitenlinie lifting]] to clear the road to [[Kroeng-Gloempang]] and to free [[Tjot-Rang]]. The operation was marked by heavy combat at [[Lam-Hasan]] — where the cavalry came under fire — and by sharp engagements at [[Lam-Tangah]] (where [[Kapitein Drijber|Kapt. Drijber]] counter-attacked the Achehnese and inflicted 30 dead) and at [[Lam-Tjeu]] (where the *versterking* was taken by [[Kapitein M. W. Thijssen|Kapt. Thijssen]]). [[Kapitein Kramer|Kapt. Kramer]] was killed. The retreat was contested and the casualties heavy. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the operation was the last attempt to operate on the abandoned *buitenlinie* before the strategic reconstitution of the Dutch position on the consolidated line.

## Background

The [[Evacuation of Kroeng-Gloempang, Tjot-Rang, Toengkoeb (20 April 1896)|formal completion of the buitenlinie lifting]] on [[20 April]] [[1896]] had left a residue of unfinished business. The road to [[Kroeng-Gloempang]] — the principal axis of advance into the central mukims — was still blocked by Achehnese *benteng*s, and the post of [[Tjot-Rang]] was still invested. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], resolved to mount one final operation to clear the road and to free Tjot-Rang before definitively abandoning the line. Three colonnes were assembled.

The **Noordercolonne** was placed under [[Majoor G. F. Soeters|Maj. Soeters]] — the same officer who would, six weeks later, command the reserve colonne at the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]]. The **Middencolonne** was placed under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] — recently blooded in the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]] of [[17 April]] [[1896]] and destined to command the [[Bergcolonne]] at the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] of [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]]. The **Zuidercolonne** was placed under [[Luitenant-Kolonel C. P. J. van Vliet|Lt.-Col. van Vliet]], with [[Luitenant-Kolonel H. Bendien|Lt.-Col. Bendien]] — both of whom had commanded at the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] and the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]]. [[Majoor K. W. Steinmetz|Maj. Steinmetz]] — commander of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] — supported the Zuidercolonne. The overall design was a convergent advance: the three colonnes, moving from north, centre, and south, were to clear the road to Kroeng-Gloempang and to free Tjot-Rang.

## The Combat at Lam Hasan

The Zuidercolonne, advancing from the south, ran into the first serious opposition at [[Lam-Hasan]]. The cavalry attached to the colonne came under fire from Achehnese positions in the *kampong* edges and was forced to retire; the infantry was deployed and a firefight developed. The Achehnese — recognising the moment of greatest Dutch vulnerability, the moment when the cavalry was unhorsed and the infantry was deploying — pressed their advantage. [[Kapitein Kramer|Kapt. Kramer]] was killed in the course of the engagement. The cavalry reformed and the infantry advanced, but the firefight continued for some hours.

## Drijber's Counter-Attack at Lam Tangah

The hardest fighting of the day came at [[Lam-Tangah]], where [[Kapitein Drijber|Kapt. S. A. Drijber]] — commanding a company of the Zuidercolonne — found his position overlapped by Achehnese bands working around his flank. Recognising that passive defence would be fatal, Drijber launched a counter-attack at the head of his company. The counter-attack was successful: the Achehnese were driven back with 30 killed — a substantial loss that broke the immediate pressure on the colonne. Drijber's counter-attack at [[Lam-Tangah]] was one of the few genuinely aggressive Dutch actions of the retreat phase, and it demonstrated, in miniature, the tactical pattern that would characterise the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] a month later: aggressive infantry, supported by fire, breaking the Achehnese defensive positions at close range.

## Thijssen at Lam Tjeu

Concurrently, the fortified work at [[Lam-Tjeu]] was taken by [[Kapitein M. W. Thijssen|Kapt. M. W. Thijssen]]. The *versterking* — an earthwork with the usual Achehnese *bamboedoeri* abatis — was stormed by Thijssen's company, the defenders driven out, and the work occupied. The capture of [[Lam-Tjeu]] opened the road to [[Kroeng-Gloempang]] for the central colonne. [[Kapitein Kramer|Kapt. Kramer]]'s death at [[Lam-Hasan]], however, was a serious loss, and the casualties of the day — though not enumerated in detail by Kruisheer — were sufficient to colour the operation as a costly success.

## The Retreat and the Casualties

The three colonnes, having cleared the road to [[Kroeng-Gloempang]] and freed [[Tjot-Rang]], withdrew to the consolidated line. The retreat was contested throughout, the Achehnese following up sharply and inflicting casualties at every step. The Dutch losses of the day — including [[Kapitein Kramer|Kapt. Kramer]] killed, an unknown number of NCOs and men killed and wounded, and a substantial expenditure of ammunition — were heavy for an operation whose strategic purpose was already exhausted. The *buitenlinie* had been formally abandoned on [[20 April]] [[1896]]; the operation of [[21 April]] [[1896]] was, in essence, a mopping-up exercise that had revealed, in its cost, the futility of further operations on the abandoned line.

## Significance

The three-column operation to free Tjot Rang and Kroeng Gloempang on [[21 April]] [[1896]] was the last Dutch operation of the retreat phase of the spring [[1896]] crisis. It demonstrated, in the heavy combat at [[Lam-Hasan]], [[Lam-Tangah]], and [[Lam-Tjeu]], that the Achehnese remained capable of contesting every Dutch movement on the abandoned *buitenlinie*; but it also demonstrated, in [[Kapitein Drijber|Drijber]]'s counter-attack at Lam-Tangah and [[Kapitein M. W. Thijssen|Thijssen]]'s storming of Lam-Tjeu, that the Dutch retained the tactical capacity to break Achehnese positions when they chose to fight aggressively. The operation was, in this sense, a bridge between the retreat of April and the counter-offensive of May. Within six weeks the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] of [[23 May]]–[[25 May]] [[1896]] would translate the tactical lesson of [[Lam-Tangah]] into a strategic recovery.

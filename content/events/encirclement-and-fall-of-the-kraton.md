The **Encirclement and Fall of the Kraton** took place between [[24 January]] and [[26 January]] [[1874]], marking the climax of the [[Second Aceh Expedition]] and the capture of the royal palace of the [[Sultanate of Aceh]].

## Detailed Narrative

Recognizing that a direct frontal breach was impossible, General [[Jan van Swieten]] designed an encirclement maneuver to envelop the southern and eastern faces of the [[Kraton of Aceh]].

At 4:30 AM on [[24 January]] [[1874]], Major-General [[G.M. Verspijck]] led a main column of 10 infantry companies across the Kroeng Daroe river, while Lieutenant-Colonel [[Karel van der Heijden]] commanded a secondary column 1,000 meters further south. Van der Heijden cleared Acehnese outposts at Gitjiel Olejlo and occupied the white stone mosque (*Mandarsah poetih*). The two columns reunited at Kampong Nesoeh at 2:00 PM.

On [[25 January]] [[1874]], Van der Heijden led three half-battalions through swampy terrain near Lohong to cut off the retreat line toward [[Longbatta]].

On [[26 January]] [[1874]] at 9:00 AM, a reconnaissance company from the 14th Battalion crossed the Kroeng Daroe near [[Kota Goenoengan]] and discovered that the royal palace wall was unguarded. Infantrymen scaled the main wall and entered the [[Kraton of Aceh]] to find it completely deserted; [[Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX]] and his garrison had evacuated under cover of darkness.

At 11:30 AM, buglers inside the palace grounds played the Dutch national anthem (*Wilhelmus*). Dutch forces seized 56 cannons inside the palace walls and permanently occupied the royal grounds, renaming the complex *Kota Radja*.

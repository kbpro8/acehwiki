**The Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)** — the *inname van Lam Pisang en Oemar's Huis* — was the storming and capture of [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s residence at [[Lam-Pisang]] by the **Colonne Laceulle** under [[Kapitein C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. C. J. Laceulle]] of the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e Bataljon]] on the second day of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]], the symbolic climax of the Dutch counter-offensive against the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. At 10:35 a.m. on [[24 May]] [[1896]], [[1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh|1e luit. M. D. J. de Jongh]] — accompanied by only three lower ranks, the rest being in the marsh or wounded — was the first to reach Oemar's *verblijf* at [[Lam-Pisang]], immediately followed by the sectie 4e compagnie under [[Kapitein J. P. Linck|Kapt. J. P. Linck]] and then a small cavalry detachment. A hoornblazer of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] on the eastern hills blew the "Wilhelmus" to announce the capture. [[1e Luit. C. W. F. Happé|Luit. Happé]] pulled down an Achehnese flag. Some small cannons, furniture, and the [[Correspondentie van Oemar|correspondence of Umar]] were captured. *Padi* stocks in various houses were burned. Umar's house was subsequently destroyed with dynamite, tar, petroleum.

## Background

The [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|storming of Tjot Tjako]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]] had opened the way to [[Lam-Pisang]]. Sergeant Grebner's initiative on the western *hoogtekam* had caused the Achehnese to evacuate both [[Lam-Pisang]] and [[Bineh Kroeng]] on the afternoon of the first day. Van Heutsz's fourth message — received by the *bevelhebber* at 11:15 a.m. on [[23 May]] [[1896]] — had reversed the initial decision to halt operations and had confirmed that the troops could advance. The stage was set for the assault on [[Lam-Pisang]] and on Oemar's house — the symbolic heart of the Achehnese resistance and the principal objective of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]].

The **Colonne Laceulle** was formed on [[24 May]] [[1896]] under [[Kapitein C. J. Laceulle|Lt.-Col. C. J. Laceulle]] of the [[5e Bataljon Infanterie|5e Bataljon]] (12 officers, 364 men). Its composition was: three *compagnieën* of the 5e Bataljon; two sections of mountain artillery (Vrijdag, van der Waarden); two cavalry platoons under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|Ritmeester Jhr. de Lannoy]] (3 officers, 64 riders); a detachment of engineers under [[Kapitein Zelle|Kapt. Zelle]] (43 *minderen*); ambulance; train. The Colonne was later reinforced with *compagnieën* of the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]]. Concurrently, the **Colonne v.d. Noordaa** — three *compagnieën* of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] (11 officers, 324 men) under [[Majoor G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. van der Noordaa]], with [[Kapitein van den generalen staf H. C. Kronouer|Kapt. Kronouer]] of the General Staff attached — was tasked with occupying [[Lam-Roekam]] and [[Lam-Oedjoeng]] in support.

## The Advance of the Colonne Laceulle

Pre-dawn on [[24 May]] [[1896]] the Bergcolonne (*maréchaussée*) occupied the southern top from which so much fire had come the day before, plus a stelling in the saddle with the lone tree. The *maréchaussée* discovered huts, a water put, and shot Achehnese at these positions. From these positions the *maréchaussée* could observe the entire valley below.

The Colonne Laceulle marched from [[Lam-Djamoe]] at 6 a.m. The bridge in the road to [[Pakan-Badak]] (broken by the enemy) had to be repaired by the engineers — an operation that talso 1½ hours. Three *compagnieën* of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] marched toward [[Lam-Isi]] and from there to [[Lam-Gemoek]] and [[Lam-Roekam]]. The cavalry under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|de Lannoy]] reconnoitred toward [[Lam-Pisang]].

At 8:30 a.m. the first shots came from the south-western *kampong*s. Laceulle advanced to the northern *rand* of [[Lam-Goe-Oe]]. Heavy fire came from a *versterking* in the south-western *rand*. The morning was a sequence of cavalry reports and colonne adjustments: at 9:46 a.m. the cavalry reported the gebergte west of Gemi-Roi strongly occupied; at 9:56 a.m. many enemy were descending toward Lampoih-Eumpei; at 10:06 a.m. [[Pasar Oleh-Gli]], Rimah's, and Gemi-Roi were *ontruimd*; at 10:13 a.m. **[[Lam-Pisang]] and Oemar's house were reported *onbezet* but under heavy fire from the west**. At 10:20 a.m. the order came to advance to the south corner of Gemi-Roi — already reached.

## The Storming of Lam-Pisang

The 1e compagnie of the 5e Bataljon under [[Kapitein Boer|Kapt. Boer]] advanced with the 1e sectie under [[1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh|1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh]]. Heavy fire from west and south-west opened as the sectie left [[Pasar Oleh-Gli]]. The colonne-commandant ordered the 4e and 2e compagnie to cross the dyke at fastest pace, with artillery in battery on the dyke answering enemy fire. The sectie of de Jongh — moving fast across the open ground — outpaced its supports.

**At 10:35 a.m., 1e luit. M. D. J. de Jongh — accompanied by only three the rest being in the *rawah* or wounded — was the first to reach Oemar's *verblijf* at Lam-Pisang.** He was immediately followed by the sectie 4e compagnie under [[Kapitein J. P. Linck|Kapt. J. P. Linck]], then by a small cavalry detachment. The capture was announced by a hoornblazer of the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] on the eastern hills, who blew the "Wilhelmus" — the Dutch national anthem — to signal to the garrison of Lampisang that the residence had been taken. The episode is one of the most-celebrated moments of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]: a single lieutenant, with three men, reaching the residence of the enemy commander ahead of his own supports.

The text explicitly corrects a popular legend that credited the cavalry — specifically wachtmeester Jonker — with being first into Oemar's house. The official text credits [[1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh|de Jongh]], noting that the cavalry legend was "minder juist" (less accurate). The correction is preserved by Kruisheer as a matter of historical record, against the romanticised version that had gained currency in the colonial press.

## The Spoils and the Destruction

A patrol under [[1e Luit. C. W. F. Happé|1e Luit. C. W. F. Happé]] pulled down an Achehnese flag — the *Atjehsche vlag* — that flew over the residence. The spoils of the capture included some small cannons, furniture, and the [[Correspondentie van Oemar|correspondence of Umar]] — a cache of letters and documents that would subsequently be studied by the Dutch intelligence services for insight into Umar's network of allies and correspondents. unhusked rice stocks in various houses were burned — a deliberate destruction of the enemy's food supply.

Oemar's house itself was subsequently destroyed. The engineers prepared the demolition on the night of [[25 May]]–[[26 May]] [[1896]] (after the third day's operations) and the house was blown up and burned with dynamite, tar, petroleum. The destruction of the residence was a deliberate act of symbolic warfare: the house of the *Panglima Prang Besar* — the title conferred by the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]] and revoked by the [[Dismission of Teuku Oemar (26 April 1896)|dismission]] of [[26 April]] [[1896]] — was erased from the face of Atjeh.

## The Concurrent Operations of the Colonne v.d. Noordaa

Concurrently, the **Colonne v.d. Noordaa** — three *compagnieën* of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]] under [[Majoor G. P. M. van der Noordaa|Maj. van der Noordaa]] — occupied [[Lam-Roekam]] and [[Lam-Oedjoeng]]. The Colonne was attached to [[Kapitein van den generalen staf H. C. Kronouer|Kapt. Kronouer]] of the General Staff. The occupation of Lam-Roekam and Lam-Oedjoeng secured the southern flank of the Colonne Laceulle and prevented Achehnese reinforcements from interfering with the capture of [[Lam-Pisang]]. The day's operations closed with a failed night surprise attempt on [[Lam-Tiroeng]] / [[Boekit Seboen]] — an ambitious attempt to carry the third day's objective by surprise that did not succeed.

## Significance

The capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's house on [[24 May]] [[1896]] was the symbolic climax of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]] and of the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Vetter]] interregnum as a whole. The fall of the residence — barely two months after the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]] — was a stunning reversal of fortune. The "Wilhelmus" played on the eastern hills; the Achehnese flag pulled down; the correspondence captured; the house destroyed with dynamite, tar, and petroleum — each element was invested with symbolic weight in the colonial press and in the official record. The distinction of [[1e Luit. M. D. J. de Jongh|1e Luit. de Jongh]] as the first to reach the residence — and the explicit correction of the cavalry legend — were preserved by Kruisheer as a matter of historical integrity. The capture of [[Lam-Pisang]] did not, however, end the war: Oemar himself had escaped south, and his warriors would continue the resistance for years. But the strategic initiative had been recovered, and the political damage of the defection had been, in large part, repaired.

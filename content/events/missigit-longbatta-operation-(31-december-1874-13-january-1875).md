The **Missigit Longbatta operation** ([[31 December]] [[31 December]] [[1874]] – [[13 January]] [[13 January]] [[1875]]) was a Dutch engineer operation of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in which [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Kolonel PEL]], civil and military governor of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], ordered the capture of the mosque (*missigit*) of [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] — stronghold of the [[Imam of Longbatta|Iman Longbatta]], "een van de voornaamste verzetslieden" — and its conversion into a fortified redoubt enclosing the mosque. The redoubt was constructed under continuous Atjehese rifle and gun fire by a detachment of the [[Bataillon Mineurs en Sappeurs|Compagnie Mineurs en Sappeurs]] commanded by [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Tweede-Luitenant C. J. Snijders]], with a working party of about 100 vrije koelies, at a cost of three dead and twelve wounded among the workers; the work was largely completed in two weeks and was visited and approved by [[W. J. C. Leers|Majoor der Genie W. J. C. Leers]] on [[13 January]] [[13 January]] [[1875]].

## Context

By the end of [[1874]] the Dutch perimeter at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] was being progressively widened by [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Kolonel PEL]]. After the [[Benting Kota Alam operation (13 November 1874)|Benting Kota Alam operation of 13 November]] had secured the eastern flank, PEL turned to the south-east, where the kampong [[Longbatta|Longbatta]] — headed by the [[Imam of Longbatta|Iman Longbatta]] — was one of the most bitterly contested strongholds in the immediate neighbourhood of the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]]. The [[31 December]] [[31 December]] [[1874]] action was intended "de Atjehers tot op grooteren afstand van onze hoofdstelling terug te drijven en de Atjeh-Rivier tot oostelijke beveiliging daarvan te maken."

## The Action of 31 December 1874

In the morning of [[31 December]] [[31 December]] [[1874]] [[J. L. J. H. Pel|PEL]] launched an action against [[Longbatta|Longbatta]]. [[Cornelis Jacobus Snijders|Snijders]] was held ready at [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] with 200 vrije koelies and a large quantity of materials and tools to follow one of the columns and, on success, to put the captured ground in a state of defence. Toward midday he received orders and marched his workers, under a covering infantry detachment, up the left bank of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh River]]. At about 2 p.m. he joined [[J. L. J. H. Pel|PEL]] and the reserve at some distance from the [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit]], which the troops had by then taken. While PEL was beginning to give Snijders his instructions, the action took an unfavourable turn, and the proposed engineering work had to be abandoned for the day. Snijders was ordered back to [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] without loss.

## The Fortification, 1–13 January 1875

On New Year's Day [[1875]] Snijders marched again, with a detachment of mineurs and sappeurs and about 100 vrije koelies, accompanying an infantry column escorting the guns to arm the [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit]] and a convoy of food, ammunition, and building materials. The column was repeatedly fired on, especially crossing the open sawah north of the kampong. Around noon the Missigit was reached and work began.

The redoubt was traced as a langwerpige redoute about 50 × 60 metres enclosing the mosque. The east front was given a wet ditch fed by an existing water conduit. The first work was a sandbag breastwork along the perimeter, with the Missigit itself fitted up as officers' quarters; the rest of the garrison and the coolies bivouacked in the open. Once the parapet was closed and raised, the construction of bamboo barracks (covered with asphalt-felt), kitchens, latrines, and so forth outside the work was begun.

The technical detail is preserved in the eyewitness lecture of [[T. W. G. Merz|Eerste-Luitenant T. W. G. Merz]]:

> "De Luitenant der Mineurs SNIJDERS [...] maakte middelerwijl een begin met het tracé der borstwering. Longbatta-Mesigii had bij de inname een lage, aarden omwalling, die nagenoeg de gedaante had van een regthoek, waarvan de langste zijde ongeveer 30, de kortste 25 strekkende meter vuurlijn telde: daarom werd besloten nagenoeg in de richting van de bestaande wal een langwerpige redoute te traceeren, terwijl de Zuid-Oostelijke en Noord-Westelijke saillant tot een cirkel-bastion zouden worden uitgebouwd. [...] de toegang tot de versterking of benteng kwam aan de N.O.-zijde [...] en werd met een travers, waarvoor Friesche ruiters werden geplaatst, gesloten. De keukens en latrines zouden geplaatst worden aan de Noordzijde in een vorig Atjineesch voorwerk van de versterking Longbatta-Mesigit [...] Verder zou de versterking omgeven worden door een horizontale draadversperring waarom een twee meter breede ranjoebeplanting, welke laatste natuurlijk gaandeweg moest worden aangemaakt, door voor te schrijven, dat iedere man per dag in zijn vrijen tijd 30 ranjoe's moest leveren."

### The Atjehese Counter-attacks

The [[Achehnese|Atjehers]] refused to accept the loss of their sanctuary. From three sides, day after day, they kept the Missigit and the working parties under small-arms fire of varying intensity, with occasional artillery fire that daily produced wounded. The occupation of the post Longbatta Zuid by Dutch troops brought little relief. The opening of the foreground and the felling of the many palms Snijders needed for his work drew heavy fire, the Atjehers building new works on the right bank of the [[Acheh River|Atjeh River]] at distances as short as 600 metres. On [[9 January]] [[9 January]] [[1875]] the post was so heavily bombarded that one officer and three men were severely wounded. In the night of [[10 January|10]]/[[11 January]] [[11 January]] the enemy pressed to within 50 metres of the parapet before being driven off by a case-shot and infantry volleys; at dawn the infantry cover of the corvée in the foreground had to drive the Atjehers back at bayonet-point after several volleys.

### Completion and Hand-over

Despite losses of three dead and twelve wounded among his workers, Snijders in two weeks largely completed the redoubt. When [[W. J. C. Leers|Majoor Leers]] visited on [[13 January]] [[13 January]] [[1875]] — the same day on which a point in the kampong Blang Tjoet was occupied and fortified — he judged the work could be finished under an NCO of the mineurs, and Snijders was sent back to [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]]. He remained in general charge, visiting the Missigit every two or three days with the daily convoy.

## Eyewitness Appraisal

[[T. W. G. Merz|Merz]] concluded his account of the work:

> "De Luitenant SNIJDERS der Genie, een braaf officier, kweet zich uitmuntend van zijn plicht; het vijandelijk vuur in het minst niet schuwende, altijd opgewekt en vroolijk, ging hij de zijnen voorbeeldig voor, zoodat binnen drie weken de wallen uit zandzakken bestaande, voltooid en de gebouwen gereed waren, waardoor ten minste iedereen behoorlijk onderkomen had. Waarna hij met zijne Mineurs en werklieden naar Kota Radja terugkeerde; ieder onzer speet het, dat deze brave, trouwe makker van ons ging; iedereen waardeerde hem in hooge mate om zijn moed en warmen kameraadschappelijken geest."

## Significance

The Missigit Longbatta operation is one of the best-documented engineer actions of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in its middle phase, thanks to the survival of [[T. W. G. Merz|Merz]]'s eyewitness lecture and its preservation by [[C. A. Bannink|Bannink]] and [[D. van den Berg|Van den Berg]]. It illustrates the characteristic Dutch method of the period — the rapid construction of a [[benting|benting]] enclosing a captured strongpoint, the use of vrije koelies and dwangarbeiders under fire, the integration of [[ranjoe|ranjoe]] plantings and wire entanglements, and the daily struggle against an enemy who could put fire on the work from less than 600 metres. The conversion of a mosque into a fortified redoubt also illustrates the religious-cum-military character of the resistance: the [[Achehnese|Atjehers]] "wilden niet lijdelijk in het verlies van hun heiligdom berusten" — a wording that captures the dual nature of the [[Missigit Longbatta|Missigit]] as both a strategic landmark and a sacred site.

**Pacificatie van Atjeh** ("the Pacification of Atjeh") was the post-[[1896]] Dutch campaign of conquest and administrative consolidation in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] that, according to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau, followed from the "new direction" in Dutch Atjeh policy inaugurated by the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad (betrayal) of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]]. The Bureau frames the Pacificatie as the originating event of the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch "effective authority" across the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]]: a project whose "complete" realisation in Atjeh would, in the Bureau's periodisation, become the template for the colonial-wide turn to "active policy."

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Pacificatie van Atjeh

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account places the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] at the centre of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" of the Buitenbezittingen. The Pacificatie is the principal context for the Bureau's narrative of administrative consolidation (the [[1912]] creation of fifteen new *onderafdeelingskassen* in [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]]), of infrastructural extension (the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network's expansion from [[1897]] onward), and of strategic reorientation (the [[1902]] extension of the Atjeh model to [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]]).

The Bureau's account of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] explicitly identifies the [[1904]]–[[1908]] *berglijntje* from [[Keude Breue]] to [[Seulimeum]] as the moment at which the military administration acquired "a powerful instrument for achieving the complete [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] of the intersected regions" of [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] and the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]]. The [[1908]] "change for the better" in the economic value of the Atjeh-tram is likewise attributed by the Bureau to "the advancing [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] of the land since [[1908]]."

The Bureau's narrative of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] also frames the [[1910]] centralisation of the territorial treasuries, and the [[1912]] creation of fifteen new onderafdeelingskassen in Atjeh, as administrative consolidations of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie]]. The Bureau's account of the [[Gewapende Politie|armed police]] likewise notes that [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] was the only Buitenbezittingen gewest in which the armed police division was not under the command of a retired or active army officer — a continuing military exception that reflects the still-recent character of the Pacificatie.

## The three milestone periodisation

The Bureau's periodisation of the wider Buitenbezittingen turn places the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] as the second of three milestone moments:

1. [[1894]]: the subjection of [[Lombok]] under geregeld bestuur (regular administration) — the first major extension of Dutch direct administration in the Buitenbezittingen in the late nineteenth century.
2. [[1896]]: the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar]] — the originating event of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] itself.
3. [[1902]]: the extension of the Atjeh model of "active policy" to [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]] — the moment at which the Pacificatie of Atjeh was deliberately translated into a colonial-wide project of "effective authority."

## Significance

The [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the originating event of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" of the Buitenbezittingen — the campaign whose completion in Atjeh became the template for the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch "effective authority" across the outer possessions. The Pacificatie is the strategic and administrative frame within which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was extended, the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] were consolidated, and the [[1914]] [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] was promulgated.


## Account from *Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis* (1955)

The following account is drawn from the parliamentary history of the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] [[Tweede Kamer der Staten-Generaal (colonial business)|Second Chamber]] as narrated by [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] in the colonial section of the [[Schets eener parlementaire geschiedenis van Nederland van 1849 tot 1901 (Rengers-Vermeulen, 1955)|1955 fourth revised edition of Rengers's *Schets*]].


**The Pacificatie van Atjeh** — the "Pacification of Atjeh" — is the name given in the [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] retrospective to the post-[[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|1904 expedition]] phase of the Dutch administration of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], in which the conquest of the sultanate was consolidated by the establishment of fortified posts, the pursuit of remaining resistance groups, and the co-optation of indigenous *hoofden* (chiefs) into the colonial administration. The *pacificatie* is the strategic doctrine under which the [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908 report]] of governor-general [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] installed the new course: administration through the *hoofden*, bound to Dutch authority by "tactvolle omgang" — tactful intercourse.

### The Van Heutsz principle

The *beginsel der pacificatie* — principle of pacification — laid down by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] in his [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908 report]] was the "vanouds beproefd gebleken stelsel van bestuur door middel van de hoofden, die men door tactvolle omgang aan het Nederlandse gezag moest binden" — the long-tried system of administration through the chiefs, who were to be bound to Dutch authority by tactful intercourse. The principle reversed the [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] maxim — "Gij kunt die kerels niet vertrouwen, gij moet ze ook niet vertrouwen, gij moet ze slaan en drukken en dan zullen ze u gehoorzamen" — you cannot trust those fellows, you must not trust them, you must beat them and press them and then they will obey you — under which the post-[[1905]] administration had been conducted.

### The pre-1908 system

The post-[[1896]] system associated with the names of [[Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] and [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had been "het opjagen en achtervolgen van hen die zich niet bij onze heerschappij wilden neerleggen tot in de verst verwijderde schuilhoeken, waarbij het aantal versterkte posten gaandeweg over een groter gebied uitgebreid kon worden" — the rousing and pursuit of those who would not submit to our rule into the remotest hiding-places, with the number of fortified posts being gradually extended over a wider area. The system required, as [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] himself noted, the support of the repeating rifle (*repeteergeweer*), the suppression of beri-beri and venereal disease among the Dutch troops, and the rigorous political subordination of the conquered territory to the costs of its own subjugation.

### The 1902 traverse of Van Kol

In [[1902]] [[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) traversed [[Acheh|Atjeh]] "van Noord tot Zuid" — from north to south — and certified that the *pacificatie* had progressed further than he had thought, and that the "gruwelen" — the horrors — of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] were already past. The illusion of approaching peace was, however, almost immediately dispelled by the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition of Van Daalen]] in [[1904]].

### The post-1908 course

The *pacificatie* of the post-[[1908]] period was implemented through the restoration of the *hoofden*, the construction of roads and telegraph lines, the imposition of taxes and the demand of *herendiensten* (corvée) — including *spionnen-* and *gidsendiensten* (spy- and guide-services) — to defray the cost of the occupation. [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.) noted in [[1908]] that "Men legde de bevolking zware lasten en boeten op" — heavy burdens and fines were imposed on the population — and suspected that [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had permitted [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] to act in accordance with the [[1893]] view set out in his *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*: "Atjeh moet onderworpen worden, dat is de zaak, en om dit resultaat na al ons morsen en knoeien nog te erlangen moet Atjeh zelf betalen".

### The continued toll

The *pacificatie* continued to take a smaller annual toll than the war years. "Het aantal doden onder de Atjehers werd van nu af ieder jaar kleiner, maar bedroeg in [[1910]] toch nog 678" — the number of dead among the Atjehnese became smaller every year from now on, but in [[1910]] still amounted to 678. [[J. K. J. de Stuers|De Stuers]]' comment — "De voorraad wild begint te dunne" — the stock of game is beginning to thin — is the bitterest single line in the parliamentary record of the *pacificatie*.

### Significance

The *pacificatie van Atjeh* is the strategic doctrine under which the post-[[1908]] administration of Atjeh was conducted. Its principal text is the [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|June 1908 report]] of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]; its principal critic, in the [[Tweede Kamer]], is [[C. Th. van Deventer|Van Deventer]] (v.d.), who traced the *pacificatie*'s exaction of *herendiensten* back to [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] brochure.

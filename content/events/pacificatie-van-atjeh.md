**Pacificatie van Atjeh** ("the Pacification of Atjeh") was the post-[[1896]] Dutch campaign of conquest and administrative consolidation in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] that, according to the Encyclopaedisch Bureau, followed from the "new direction" in Dutch Atjeh policy inaugurated by the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad (betrayal) of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]]. The Bureau frames the Pacificatie as the originating event of the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch "effective authority" across the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]]: a project whose "complete" realisation in Atjeh would, in the Bureau's periodisation, become the template for the colonial-wide turn to "active policy."

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Pacificatie van Atjeh

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account places the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] at the centre of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" of the Buitenbezittingen. The Pacificatie is the principal context for the Bureau's narrative of administrative consolidation (the [[1912]] creation of fifteen new *onderafdeelingskassen* in [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]]), of infrastructural extension (the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] network's expansion from [[1897]] onward), and of strategic reorientation (the [[1902]] extension of the Atjeh model to [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]]).

The Bureau's account of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] explicitly identifies the [[1904]]–[[1908]] *berglijntje* from [[Keude Breue]] to [[Seulimeum]] as the moment at which the military administration acquired "a powerful instrument for achieving the complete [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] of the intersected regions" of [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] and the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]]. The [[1908]] "change for the better" in the economic value of the Atjeh-tram is likewise attributed by the Bureau to "the advancing [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]] of the land since [[1908]]."

The Bureau's narrative of the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] also frames the [[1910]] centralisation of the territorial treasuries, and the [[1912]] creation of fifteen new onderafdeelingskassen in Atjeh, as administrative consolidations of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie]]. The Bureau's account of the [[Gewapende Politie|armed police]] likewise notes that [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] was the only Buitenbezittingen gewest in which the armed police division was not under the command of a retired or active army officer — a continuing military exception that reflects the still-recent character of the Pacificatie.

## The three milestone periodisation

The Bureau's periodisation of the wider Buitenbezittingen turn places the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] as the second of three milestone moments:

1. [[1894]]: the subjection of [[Lombok]] under geregeld bestuur (regular administration) — the first major extension of Dutch direct administration in the Buitenbezittingen in the late nineteenth century.
2. [[1896]]: the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar]] — the originating event of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] itself.
3. [[1902]]: the extension of the Atjeh model of "active policy" to [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]] — the moment at which the Pacificatie of Atjeh was deliberately translated into a colonial-wide project of "effective authority."

## Significance

The [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|Pacificatie van Atjeh]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the originating event of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" of the Buitenbezittingen — the campaign whose completion in Atjeh became the template for the wider post-[[1902]] extension of Dutch "effective authority" across the outer possessions. The Pacificatie is the strategic and administrative frame within which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was extended, the [[Landschapskassen|landschapskassen]] were consolidated, and the [[1914]] [[Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie|Zelfbestuurs-ordonnantie]] was promulgated.

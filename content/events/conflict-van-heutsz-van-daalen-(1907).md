**The Conflict Van Heutsz-Van Daalen** of [[1907]] was the breakdown of the relationship between Governor-General [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[Gouvernement Atjeh|Governor of Atjeh]] [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], culminating in Van Daalen's request for relief from the Atjeh governorship on [[28 December]] [[1907]]. The conflict's correspondence was published posthumously by Van Daalen's children in a brochure titled *Het Conflict Van Heutsz-Van Daalen* (1907), containing letters from [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], [[Van der Maaten|Van der Maaten]], [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], [[H. M. Neeb|H. M. Neeb]], and [[Kamerling|Kamerling]], along with citations from [[Joh. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s *Vijftig jaren Economische Staatkunde in Atjeh*, a memorial word from the *Nieuwe Rotterdamsche Courant* of [[25 February]] [[1930]], and a passage from [[Van Heekeren|Van Heekeren]]'s article in *De Indische Gids* of [[1914]].

## The brochure and its purpose

The brochure, a 65-page publication available for 60 cents, had a stated dual purpose: first, to assist historians; second, to do justice to Van Daalen. [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] noted in his review that the postscript suggested other intentions as well. The publication of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s correspondence with Van Daalen could be justified, but the inclusion of [[Van der Maaten|Van der Maaten]]'s letters — containing damning accounts of a fellow officer who was still alive — had been published without Van der Maaten's prior knowledge.

## The relationship before the conflict

[[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] noted that the brochure's assertion of a good intimate understanding between [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] "in the days of before the conflict" did not actually exist. Before September [[1907]], [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had never written unofficially to [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]], although such informal correspondence between [[Buitenzorg|Buitenzorg]] and [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] had previously been the rule. [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s annotations on [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s official letters and reports of [[1905]]/[[1906]]/[[1907]], preserved at the general secretariat in [[Buitenzorg|Buitenzorg]], were not friendly. Van Daalen only became aware of these annotations many years later, when he was army commander.

## Van Daalen's press problem

The brochure claimed that the [[Atjeh-rapport (1908)|1908 Atjeh report]] had compelled Van Daalen to request relief. [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] corrected this: Van Daalen's bad press had already led him to request relief on [[28 December]] [[1907]] — before the report appeared. The explanation for that bad press, found on page 16 of the brochure from [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s own pen, was that Van Heutsz kept the war correspondents under control while Van Daalen ignored them, and consequently the correspondents "sprang out of band." Special indignation that they were not as well served by Van Daalen as by Van Heutsz contributed to the hostile coverage.

## Van Daalen's own assessment

[[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] himself, at [[Sigli]] in [[1901]] when his star was still rising, confided to [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] how "widig" (unseemly) he found a duel between two generals before the public, such as had previously occurred between Van Swieten and Verspijck. On the [[Atjeh-rapport (1908)|1908 Atjeh report]] published in June [[1908]], Van Daalen had been able to remain silent.

## Damsté's judgment

[[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] assessed the brochure as follows: viewed as a historical source, it was gain, though not "pure" gain; viewed as a monument to honour Van Daalen, the general deserved better, but the attempt was praiseworthy.

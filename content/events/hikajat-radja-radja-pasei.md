The **Hikajat Radja-radja Pasei** (modern spelling *Hikayat Raja-Raja Pasai*) is the Malay chronicle of the kings of [[Pasei|Samudra Pasai]], the historical Muslim kingdom on the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], and one of the foundational Malay historical texts. The chronicle was made accessible to European scholarship by [[E. Dulaurier]] in his [[1849]] edition, and was translated into French by [[A. Marre]] in [[1874]] as *Sumatra, Histoire des rois de Pasey*. In [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw* ([[1956]])]] (p. 209, footnote) the chronicle is cited among the textual witnesses to the famous Minangkabau buffalo-fight founding legend, alongside [[A. Marre|Marre]]'s [[1874]] translation, [[J. L. A. Brandes|Brandes]]'s *Pararaton* (2nd ed., p. 149 vg.), and [[L. C. Westenenk|Westenenk]]'s Minangkabau writings.

## The text
The *Hikajat Radja-radja Pasei* is a Malay prose chronicle treating the early Muslim kings of [[Pasei|Samudra Pasai]], the North-Acehnese polity that was, before the rise of [[Banda Acheh|Koetaradja]] under [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]], the principal Muslim state of the northern Sumatran coast and one of the earliest Muslim kingdoms in the Indonesian archipelago. According to the chronicle's own chronology, the kingdom was founded in the late thirteenth century (traditionally 1267 CE) by Merah Silau, who is said to have converted to Islam and assumed the title Malik as-Saleh; the chronicle then traces the succession of the Pasai kings down to the absorption of the kingdom by the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate in the early sixteenth century.

The chronicle is one of the principal Malay historical texts and is significant both as a document of the early Muslim history of northern Sumatra and as a literary monument of the Malay chronicle tradition. It circulated in Malay textual tradition in close proximity to the Javanese-Minangkabau historical-literary complex — a proximity attested by its inclusion, alongside the *Pararaton* and the Minangkabau writings, in the list of authorities for the Minangkabau buffalo-fight founding legend in *De Karbouw*.

## The Minangkabau buffalo-fight legend
The Minangkabau buffalo-fight founding legend — in which the political fate of the Minangkabau country was decided by a single combat between a Javanese buffalo (a large, fierce beast) and a Minangkabau buffalo (a starved calf with iron spikes attached to its head, which the Javanese buffalo mistook for its mother and was killed by when it pressed its head into the calf's chest seeking milk) — is attested in the *Hikajat Radja-radja Pasei* and in a number of Sumatran and Javanese historical-literary texts. [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] in *De Karbouw* (p. 209, footnote) cites the chronicle, alongside [[A. Marre|Marre]]'s [[1874]] translation (pp. 100 vg., 125 vg.), [[J. L. A. Brandes|Brandes]]'s *Pararaton* (2nd ed., p. 149 vg.), [[L. C. Westenenk|Westenenk]]'s Minangkabau writings (*Land en volk van Minangkabau*, p. 644; *Opstellen over Minangkabau*, pp. 256 vg.), and [[J. E. Jasper|Jasper]]'s *Padangsche Bovenlanden* (p. 854), as the textual authorities for the legend.


## The European editions
The chronicle was made accessible to European scholarship by two nineteenth-century French orientalists:

- [[E. Dulaurier]]'s [[1849]] edition — *Hikajat Radja-radja Pasei* (Paris, [[1849]]) — the original edition of the Malay text, and the first European publication of the chronicle.
- [[A. Marre]]'s [[1874]] French translation — *Sumatra, Histoire des rois de Pasey* (Paris, [[1874]]) — a free French rendering that brought the chronicle to a French-reading public.
Both editions are listed in the *Lijst van geschriften* (bibliography) of [[Kreemer's De Karbouw ([[1956]])|*De Karbouw*]] (pp. 261, 264), and remained the principal European-language access-points to the Pasai chronicle through the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## Significance
The *Hikajat Radja-radja Pasei* is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh]] history as the foundational Malay chronicle of the kings of [[Pasei|Samudra Pasai]] — the principal Muslim predecessor-state of the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] sultanate on the northern Sumatran coast, and one of the earliest Muslim kingdoms in the Indonesian archipelago. The chronicle is a document of the early Muslim history of northern Sumatra, a literary monument of the Malay chronicle tradition, and a textual witness to the Minangkabau buffalo-fight founding legend. Its European editions by [[E. Dulaurier]] ([[1849]]) and [[A. Marre]] ([[1874]]) anchored the Pasai material in European scholarship a generation before the great Dutch ethnographic syntheses of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] ([[1893]]-94) and [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]] ([[1922]]-23), and remain citable reference-points in modern scholarly literature on early Muslim Atjeh.

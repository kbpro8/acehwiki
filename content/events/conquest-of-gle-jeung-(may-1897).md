**The conquest of Glé Jeuëng** on [[16 May]] [[1897]] was the Dutch military operation that captured the strong position (*stelling*) of [[Panglima Polém|Panglima Polém]] on the Glé Jeuëng, one of the sagi chief's residences on the route between [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] and [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] in [[Groot-Atjeh|Greater Atjeh]]. The battle — described by Governor-General [[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] as a "verwoed gevecht" (furious fight) — cost heavy casualties and marked a significant step in the consolidation of Dutch control over the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekim]].

## The road to Seulimeum and the stelling at Glé Jeuëng

Between [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] and [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]], a road was constructed: 17 km long and 5 m wide. As this roadwork, begun from [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]], approached [[Glé Jeuëng|Glé Jeuëng]], the troops protecting the road-workers had increasing contact with the enemy, who had occupied a strong *stelling* on the Glé Jeuëng. [[Glé Jeuëng|Glé Jeuëng]] was one of the residences of [[Panglima Polém|the sagihoofd]] and lay directly on the route.

By the wet of [[26 May]] [[1897]], funds were authorised for tram construction. The section [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]]-[[Lambaré|Lambare]] of the old *ceintuurbaan* (circumference line) was extended and by October had already reached [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]]; a year later the line was complete to [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]]. Troops that had for so long had to cover transport convoys became available for more direct action against the enemy: conditions improved.

## The battle of 16 May 1897

The conquest of [[Glé Jeuëng|Glé Jeuëng]] on [[16 May]] [[1897]] was a hard-fought engagement. [[C. A. van der Wijck|Van der Wijck]] described it as a "verwoed gevecht" — a furious battle — that cost "much too many dead and wounded." The victory, though purchased at heavy price, was a milestone in the advance toward [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] and the subjugation of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekim]].

## Aftermath and the post system

After the conquest of [[Glé Jeuëng|Glé Jeuëng]], the consolidation of Dutch positions in [[Groot-Atjeh|Greater Atjeh]] continued. In September [[1897]], the post [[Lam Peureuneu'|Lam Peureuneu']] was abolished; followed by [[Lam Reuèng|Lam Reuèng]], [[Sirón|Sirón]], [[Boekét Kareuëng|Boekét Kareuëng]], [[Peukan Kroeeng Tjoet|Peukan Kroeeng Tjoet]], [[Blang|Blang]], and in March [[1898]] [[Lam Tih|Lam Tih]]. However, [[Kroeëng Raja|Kroeëng Raja]], [[Leupoeëng|Leupoeëng]], [[Glé Jeuëng|Glé Jeuëng]], [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]], and finally [[Reuëngreuëng|Reuëngreuëng]] were occupied. With these, complete mastery of [[Groot-Atjeh|Greater Atjeh]] was achieved: disarmed and registered, thanks to active patrols and healthy surveillance, the interior was no longer peacefully inhabitable for enemies and became increasingly difficult to access.

[[Panglima Polém|Polém]] and other heads still persevering in their resistance no longer ventured on the Atjeh side of the watershed. The work already done was considerable, but the remaining task was still immense.

**The Bombardment of Pedir** on [[29 December|29]]-[[31 December]] [[1873]] was the punitive operation carried out by a Dutch squadron — consisting of the warships *Zeeland*, *Metalen Kruis*, *Citadel van Antwerpen*, *Borneo* and *Banda* — against the radja of [[Pedir|Pedir]] on the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], in punishment for his march with his men to the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] to help defend it against the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] of Lieutenant-general [[J. van Swieten|Van Swieten]]. The squadron was ordered to bombard the chief kampong and, if possible, to destroy it by a landing.

## Background

During the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]], which had landed east of the Atjeh river on [[9 December]] [[1873]], the Dutch received intelligence that the radja of [[Pedir|Pedir]] on the north coast had marched with his men to the Kraton to help defend it against the Dutch. This was a clear act of hostility against the expedition, and the supreme commander ordered a squadron — consisting of the *Zeeland*, the *Metalen Kruis*, the *Citadel van Antwerpen*, the *Borneo* and the *Banda* — to punish Pedir, to bombard the chief kampong and, if possible, to destroy it by a landing. The order was fulfilled in the days of [[29 December|29]] to [[31 December]] [[1873]].

## Significance

The bombardment of Pedir was part of the wider naval operations of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]], in which the navy rendered great services on several occasions. It also marked the beginning of a long series of Dutch operations against Pedir, which remained a persistent centre of resistance throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. In [[1876]] the Dutch decided to set up a fortification at Pedir and to place an official there under the protection of a sufficient military force; in the establishment of this they met all kinds of opposition and difficulties from the population, among whom a self-reliant chief in Pedir, [[Toekoe di Tiroe|Toekoe di Tiroe]], proved particularly hostile. In [[February 1877|February 1877]] it was decided to move the establishment from Pedir to [[Segli|Segli]], and in [[May 1878]] the governor [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] himself led an expedition to Gighen and the upper lands of Pedir against Toekoe di Tiroe.

**Van Rijn's visit to Aroe-baai in [[1888]]** was the official colonial tour of the [[Aroe-baai|bay of Aroe]] made by Controleur S.B. [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn van Alkemade]] from [[Bengkalis|Bengkalis]], in connection, [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] notes, with [[Tiele|Tiele]]'s essay on the history of those days. The visit produced the principal Dutch colonial description of the bay and its islets in the late nineteenth century — the report on which De Roo de la Faille draws for the [[1888|1888 visit]] that confirmed the *bedekte taal* identifications of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]], and that recorded the folk tradition of the [[Queen of Aroe|Queen of Aroe]]'s flight.

## The visit

Entering [[Aroe-baai|the bay of Aroe]] between the islands of [[Pulo Sembilan|Sembilan]] and [[Poelo Kampi|Koempej]], [[Van Rijn van Alkemade|Van Rijn]] recorded the half-moon-shaped islet of Kampi close under the shore, set with 50-to-150-metre hills, whose clear white strand slowly gave way to higher ground with light-green grass, behind which the fortified houses of the *Datoe Bintara* and the Inlandsch Opziener came into view. "The whole makes the impression as if one has a fortress with breastwork before one" — a remark that, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, was the *djoeragan* of Van Rijn's barkas pointing out the same image that the Tanjore inscription of [[1030]] had recorded as "the ancient Malayur situated on a high hill", and that in [[1862]] was still marked by the *benteng* of an [[Atjehsche avonturier at Aroe-baai (1862)|Atjehschen avonturier]] who flew the Atjehsche flag.

## Pangkalan Soesoe and the folk tradition

Crossing the bay, Van Rijn came to [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] — "the name 'milk' recalls the milky-white colour of the earth-oil here" — where ten dwellings of Malays who had fled from [[Besitang|Besitang]] stood on the high bank, on the site of "a former large kampong, destroyed by sea-rovers". A little inland he found the two old *bentengs* of the *soengej Si Kondoer* and *Sérang djaja*, said to have been the fortifications of the earlier rajas of Aroe. The *soengej Si Djanda*, which empties into the [[Besitang|Besitang]], still recalled, the locals told him, "the widow *Djanda Gidau* named, of the last raja of Aroe, who fled to that river at the destruction of the realm by Atjeh" — a folk echo of the [[Queen of Aroe|Queen of Aroe]]'s flight of [[1539]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Van Rijn's [[1888]] visit is the principal Dutch colonial confirmation of the *bedekte taal* identifications of [[Poelo Kampi|Kampi]] and [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]], and the principal recorded instance of the survival of the [[Queen of Aroe|Queen of Aroe]]'s memory in east-coast folk tradition three centuries after the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese conquest of Aroe]].

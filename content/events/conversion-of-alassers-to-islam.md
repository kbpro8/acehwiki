**Conversion of Alassers to Islam** (pre-Dutch, after first Batak heathen settlement) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. The first heathen inhabitants of the Alaslanden came from the Bataklanden (around the Toba-meer) and settled at Batoe Mboelan. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to pre-Dutch, after first Batak heathen settlement.

## Account

The first heathen inhabitants of the [[De Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] came from the [[Bataklanden]] (around the [[Toba-meer]]) and settled at [[Batoe Mboelan]]. Later, people came from Rau ([[Menangkabau]]) under their leader [[Radja Dewa]], who also brought [[Islam]]. [[Radja Dewa]] became the stamvader of the influential geslacht of the [[penghoeloe Tjiq]]. Still later, the [[Atjehers]] came and intermarried with the population, putting the [[De Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] in contact with Aceh proper. [[Radja Kemala]], a descendant of [[Radja Dewa]], visited [[Sultan of Aceh|the Sultan]] in [[Dalam|the Dalam]] and received a '[[Bawar (Gayo regalia)|bawar]]' (curved stabbing weapon in a long sheath) as a sign of good relations; possession of a [[Bawar (Gayo regalia)|bawar]] was highly prized and elevated the possessor's status.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

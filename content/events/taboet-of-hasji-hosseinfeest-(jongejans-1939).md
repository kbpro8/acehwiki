**Taboet- of Hasji-Hosseinfeest** — *"Taboet or Hasji-Hossein festival"* — is an illustrated article by **[[J. J. Jongejans|J. J. Jongejans]]**, published in [[1939]] in *Onze Aarde* (Our Earth), p. 326 *e.v.*, recording the **Taboet** — also called **Hasji-Hosseinfeest** — the Shi'ite-derived commemoration of the martyrdom of Hussein that is observed in parts of Sumatra under the name *Taboet*. The article is recorded as No. 571a in [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]]'s *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939]]* (The Hague, [[1940]]), in the Sumatra chapter, as a companion piece to No. 571, Jongejans's book *[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu]]*.

## Subject

The article's subject, as recorded by Van Meurs, is the **Taboet** — also called **Hasji-Hosseinfeest** — the festival of the Taboet. The **Taboet** (from Arabic *tabūt*, "ark" or "bier") is the Sumatran name for the commemoration of the martyrdom of **Hussein** — the grandson of the Prophet [[Prophet Muhammad|Muhammad]] — at the Battle of Karbala in AH 61 / CE 680, the central observance of the Shi'ite calendar. The festival, known in the wider Muslim world as *ʿĀshūrāʾ* and in Persia and South Asia as *Muharram*, is observed in parts of Sumatra — notably in the west-coast regions of [[Pariaman|Pariaman]] and [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]] — under the name *Taboet*, with the construction and procession of tall decorated tabernacles (*taboet*) that are eventually cast into the sea. The alternative name **Hasji-Hosseinfeest** — "Hasji [Haji] Hossein festival" — records the identification of the festival with Hussein, and the Acehnese/Malay honorific *Hasji* (Haji) prefixed to the name.

The observation of the Taboet festival on [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]] — within the broader sphere of Atjehnese influence on the west coast — is the point of the article's inclusion in the Sumatra chapter of Van Meurs's survey, and the festival's presence on the west coast of Sumatra is itself a trace of the Shi'ite-derived devotional practice that entered the archipelago through the west-coast ports and that persisted in the region alongside the Sunni mainstream.

## Author and venue

[[J. J. Jongejans|Jongejans]] — already known in the AcehWiki corpus as [[resident-jongejans|Resident Jongejans]] and as the author of the [[1939]] survey *[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu]]* — published the article in *Onze Aarde* (Our Earth), a popular geographical and ethnographic monthly that carried illustrated articles on the lands and peoples of the world for a general Dutch readership. The article's appearance in *Onze Aarde* — alongside the companion piece *Het heiligdom te Oelakan* (No. 572) — indicates that Jongejans was, in [[1939]], presenting the religious and ethnographic material of Atjeh to a general readership in parallel with the more substantial survey published as the book.

## Place in the 1939 Literatuur-Overzicht

The article is No. 571a in the Sumatra chapter (Hoofdstuk III, Nos. 548–658) of Van Meurs's survey — the lettered sub-entry *a* to No. 571, Jongejans's book. The use of a lettered sub-entry indicates that Van Meurs regarded the article as a companion piece to the book, to be read alongside it. No. 572 — *Het heiligdom te Oelakan*, also by Jongejans in *Onze Aarde* — is a further companion piece, and the three together — the book and the two *Onze Aarde* articles — mark Jongejans as one of the principal Atjeh-focused authors of [[1939]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the article is significant as a [[1939]] record of the Taboet or Hasji-Hossein festival on [[Sumatra's Westkust|Sumatra's Westkust]], and as a companion piece to Jongejans's survey *[[Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu (Jongejans, 1939)|Land en volk van Atjeh vroeger en nu]]*. The article furnishes an independent source for the west-coast observance of the Taboet festival, and it complements the corpus's existing entries on [[westkust-van-sumatra|the Westkust of Sumatra]], on [[Pariaman|Pariaman]], and on the religious life of the Atjehnese-west-coast sphere. The festival's Shi'ite derivation — in a region otherwise overwhelmingly Sunni — is of comparative interest for the history of Islam in the archipelago, and the article's record of its observance in [[1939]] is a point of reference for the study of the festival's persistence and transformation in the modern period.

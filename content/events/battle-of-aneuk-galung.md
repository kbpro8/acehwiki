**Battle of Aneuk Galung** was a fierce military engagement fought on [[29 June]] [[1896]] at the fortified village of [[Aneuk Galung]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], marking the first major full-force deployment of the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]].

## Narrative of the Engagement

In the summer of [[1896]], following the shocking defecting of [[Teuku Umar]] in the [[Treason of Teuku Umar]], Acehnese resistance forces built heavy earthwork fortifications and wooden palisades at [[Aneuk Galung]] to block Dutch expansion across the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The fort was defended by heavily armed Acehnese warriors equipped with modern rifles and traditional [[Klewang]] sabers.

On the morning of [[29 June]] [[1896]], Captain [[Jonkheer G. J. W. C. Graafland]] assembled the entire operational strength of the newly reorganized Marechaussee corps—comprising 3 European officers and 221 non-European NCOs, corporals, and privates. Rather than engaging in prolonged artillery bombardment or slow trenching as traditional line infantry did, Captain Graafland ordered a swift, surprise bayonet and klewang assault.

The Marechaussee troops sprinted across open ground under heavy defensive fire, scrambled over thorn entanglements, and penetrated the outer ramparts of Aneuk Galung. Engaging in fierce, hand-to-hand combat inside the fort, the Marechaussee overwhelmed the defenders in exactly fifteen minutes. The rapid capture of this formidable position shocked local resistance leaders and proved to the Dutch high command that mobile, lightly armed counter-insurgency units could rapidly neutralize fortified Acehnese positions.

## Historical Impact

The decisive victory at Aneuk Galung validated Captain Graafland's aggressive tactical vision. It led directly to the abandonment of the defensive [[Concentratie-Stelsel|concentratie-stelsel]] and established the Marechaussee as the primary offensive strike force of the [[Koninklijk Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] (KNIL) in Aceh.

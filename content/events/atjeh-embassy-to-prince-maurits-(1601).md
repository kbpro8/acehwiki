**The Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits of 1601** — the first Atjehnese diplomatic mission to the Netherlands, sent by the sultan of [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] to [[Prince Maurits|Prince Maurits]] of Orange in the summer of [[1601]] bearing letters and gifts in response to Maurits's earlier letter and gifts transmitted by two Zeeland ships — is the opening Atjehnese-Dutch diplomatic encounter of the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative of Atjeh history. The embassy is the diplomatic counterpart to the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' visit of 1599]] and the *terminus a quo* of formal Atjehnese-Dutch relations.

## Background: the second Zeeland visit and the release of Frederik Houtman

The [[De Kleine Oosterling|Wijma school reader]] records that "ruim twee jaar later" — that is, in [[1601]], two years after the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' visit of 1599]] — "verschenen er twee andere Zeeuwsche vaartuigen op de kust. Er waren voor den sultan van Atjeh fraaie geschenken aan boord en ook een brief van prins Maurits." The new mission, bearing gifts and Maurits's letter, succeeded where the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|Houtman brothers' visit of 1599]] had failed: "Nu kwamen de Nederlanders in de gunst bij dien vorst. Hij stelde de gevangenen in vrijheid en gaf verlof, een factorij of handelskantoor in zijn land te bouwen." The sultan, "in beleefdheid voor prins Maurits niet [willende] onderdoen," sent his own embassy with letters and gifts in return.

## The embassy at the siege of Grave

The embassy arrived in the Netherlands during the siege of Grave — Maurits's campaign of [[1602]] in the Dutch Revolt against Spain. "Die prins belegerde de stad Grave, toen de Atjehneezen in Nederland aankwamen. Hij zond zijn fraaie koets, om hen af te halen, deed hun groote eerbewijzen aan. Ze overhandigden de brieven en geschenken aan Maurits, terwijl deze door een menigte officieren omringd was. De prins liet hen de geheele legerplaats rondleiden en te hunner eere een groot spiegelgevecht geven."

The Atjehnese embassy was thus received by Maurits in the field — at the siege of Grave, in the presence of "een menigte officieren" — and was honoured with a *spiegelgevecht* (a mock battle or *sham fight*) staged in its honour. The reception is the staff history's principal instance of the early-modern Dutch diplomatic practice of receiving non-European envoys in the field — a practice that linked the Dutch Revolt's military culture to the emerging Dutch-Asian commercial diplomacy.

## The return and the death of one envoy

"De gezanten bezochten daarna Amsterdam en keerden vandaar naar hun land terug. Een van hen was echter in ons land gestorven." The embassy's return to Atjeh was thus marked by the death of one of its members in the Netherlands — the first recorded Atjehnese death on Dutch soil, and the staff history's principal instance of the human cost of early-modern diplomatic exchange.

## Significance

The Atjeh embassy to Prince Maurits of [[1601]] is, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the *terminus a quo* of formal Atjehnese-Dutch relations, and the staff history's principal instance of the early-modern Dutch practice of receiving Asian envoys in the field. The embassy's reception at the siege of Grave — in the presence of Maurits's army — frames the Dutch-Atjehnese diplomatic relationship as an extension of the Dutch Revolt's military culture, and the Atjehnese sultan's release of the Houtman captives and permission for a Dutch factorij as the diplomatic achievement of Maurits's letter. The embassy is also the staff history's principal instance of the *beleefdheid* (courtesy) that the school reader frames as the sultan's response to Maurits's gifts — a framing that would be inverted in the lessons on the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|eerste Atjeh-tocht]] of [[1873]], when the sultan's *beleefdheid* had given way to *fanatiek* resistance.

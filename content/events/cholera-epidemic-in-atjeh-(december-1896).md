**The Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh** in December [[1896]] — *de heerschende cholera* — was the prevailing cholera which prevented the *colonne tegen Oemar* (column against Umar) from taking the field in the closing month of [[1896]]. The epidemic — which struck the Dutch and Acehnese populations of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in the early weeks of December [[1896]] — paralysed Dutch military action and forced the postponement of the planned operation against [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]], who had been located in [[Lohong]] by the [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|excursie to Lepong and Lohong]] of [[10 August|10]]–[[14 August]] [[1896]]. The cholera epidemic was the closing event of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* — the natural catastrophe which closed the second semester of [[1896]] and which prevented the Dutch from capitalising on the intelligence gathered over the preceding five months.

## The Strategic Context

By December [[1896]], the Dutch, under [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]]'s new governorship (which had begun with the [[Handover of Command from Stemfoort to Van Vliet (17 November 1896)|handover of 17 November 1896]]), had completed the [[Trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah (29 November 1896)|trip to Tibang-Poei and Birah]] of [[29 November]] [[1896]] — the operation to recover the artillery lost on [[17 April]] [[1896]] during the [[Battle of Senelop (17 April 1896)|battle of Senelop]]. The intelligence gathered on the [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|excursie to Lepong and Lohong]] of [[10 August|10]]–[[14 August]] [[1896]] — that Umar had moved to Lohong, that his band had been reduced to 100 men, and that he had fallen out with the *kedjoeroean* — formed the basis of the Dutch strategic planning for December [[1896]]. The Dutch were thus preparing the *uitrusten eener colonne tegen Oemar* (equipping of a colonne against Umar) — the operation which would, it was hoped, finally capture or destroy the Acehnese resistance leader.

## The Epidemic and its Effects

The cholera epidemic — *de heerschende cholera belet de actie* (the prevailing cholera prevents the action) — struck the Dutch and Acehnese populations of Groot-Atjeh in the early weeks of December [[1896]]. The epidemic was not the first to affect the Dutch forces in Atjeh — cholera had been a recurring feature of the Atjeh-oorlog, and the *heerschende cholera* (prevailing cholera) of December [[1896]] was the latest in a series of epidemic outbreaks which had periodically paralysed Dutch military operations. The epidemic of December [[1896]] was, however, particularly severe in its timing — it struck at the very moment when the Dutch had completed their preparations for the *colonne tegen Oemar*, and it forced the postponement of the operation until the epidemic should subside. The Dutch command was thus obliged to remain inactive in December [[1896]], while Umar remained at large in Lohong and the Acehnese resistance retained its capacity for harassment.

## The Postponement of the Colonne tegen Oemar

The postponement of the *colonne tegen Oemar* — the operation which the cholera epidemic of December [[1896]] prevented from taking the field — was a significant strategic setback for the Dutch. The intelligence gathered on the [[Excursie to Lepong and Lohong (10–14 August 1896)|excursie to Lepong and Lohong]] of August [[1896]] had suggested that Umar was vulnerable — his band had been reduced to 100 men, he had fallen out with the *kedjoeroean*, and he was forced to hide his *schatten en kostbaarheden* with various well-to-do people in Lohong. The Dutch had hoped to capitalise on this vulnerability with the *colonne tegen Oemar* (column against Umar) in December [[1896]], but the cholera epidemic forced the postponement of the operation. The postponement gave Umar a further respite — a respite which he would use to rebuild his forces and to extend his operations in the western highlands of Groot-Atjeh. The *colonne tegen Oemar* (column against Umar) would eventually be undertaken in the new year, but by then the strategic situation had shifted, and the opportunity which the August intelligence had presented was lost.

## Significance

The Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh of December [[1896]] is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as the closing event of the volume — the natural catastrophe which prevented the Dutch from capitalising on the intelligence gathered over the preceding five months. The epidemic — which struck at the very moment when the Dutch had completed their preparations for the *colonne tegen Oemar* — forced the postponement of the operation and gave Umar a further respite. The cholera epidemic thus closed the second semester of [[1896]] on a note of frustration and unfinished business — a note which Kruisheer's *Besluit* (Conclusion) on page 155 would seek to transform into a defence of the *pacificatie* (pacification) policy of the second semester, with the claim that *men in [[1896]] den rechten weg tot pacificatie insloeg* (the right path to pacification was entered in [[1896]]). The cholera epidemic — by preventing the *colonne tegen Oemar* — demonstrated the limits of the Dutch *pacificatie* (pacification) policy, and the dependence of the Dutch strategic position on factors — like epidemic disease — which lay beyond the control of the colonial military command.

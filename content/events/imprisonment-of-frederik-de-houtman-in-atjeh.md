The **Imprisonment of Frederik de Houtman in Atjeh** spans from his capture on [[11 September]] [[1599]] during the [[Houtman brothers' visit to Atjeh (1599)|murder of Cornelis de Houtman]] to his daring escape on [[30 December]] [[1600]]. Held captive by the Sultan of [[Atjeh]] and subjected to intense pressure to convert to Islam, Frederik's captivity became a focal point of early Dutch-Acehnese diplomatic friction and maritime intrigue.

## Capture and relocation to Pedir

Following the massacre of his brother [[Cornelis Houtman|Cornelis de Houtman]] and the Dutch delegation, [[Frederik Houtman|Frederik de Houtman]] and twenty-seven others were taken prisoner. They were initially held in [[Pedir]], a coastal dependency of Atjeh. When the surviving Dutch ships attempted to negotiate their release, the Sultan demanded the surrender of one of the Dutch vessels in exchange for the prisoners. Frederik, communicating from captivity, advised his countrymen to refuse, arguing that the crew had no authority to surrender company property and that doing so would only invite further Acehnese aggression.

The prisoners were subsequently transported back to the capital of Atjeh, where they were kept in heavy irons. During this period, the Sultan repeatedly pressured Frederik to abandon his Christian faith and embrace Islam, a test of wills that Frederik resisted with notable stoicism.

## The arrival of Paulus van Caerden

In [[September]] [[1600]], a new Dutch fleet under the command of [[Paulus van Caerden]] arrived at Atjeh. Van Caerden was desperate to secure a cargo of pepper but was deeply suspicious of the Sultan's intentions, having been warned by a Franciscan monk and by covert messages from Frederik de Houtman himself.

Van Caerden negotiated a new trade contract, but the Sultan demanded an advance payment before releasing the prisoners or delivering the pepper. Acting on intelligence from Frederik that the Acehnese were secretly preparing an attack on the Dutch ships and had no intention of honoring the agreement, Van Caerden preemptively seized several Arab and Acehnese merchant vessels in the harbor, burning some and taking hostages.

## Escape

Amidst the diplomatic chaos and the Sultan's subsequent blockade of the harbor, a group of five Dutch prisoners managed to steal a prau and escape to Van Caerden's ships. On [[30 December]] [[1600]], Frederik de Houtman and three companions executed a desperate escape plan. They fashioned a makeshift sail and oars, stole a small prau from the riverbank, and navigated through the breaking surf under the cover of darkness. Despite nearly capsizing, they successfully reached the open sea and were rescued by Van Caerden's fleet on New Year's Eve.

Upon returning to the ship, Frederik advised Van Caerden to inform the Sultan of his escape to maintain a facade of good faith, offering to return ashore to guarantee the peace. The Sultan, recognizing the futility of further conflict with the heavily armed Dutch ships, formally granted Frederik his freedom, bringing the fifteen-month ordeal to an end.

Frederik's release was formalised, together with a commercial agreement securing the Dutch a monopoly on Acehnese pepper purchases, in the [[Treaty of Friendship and Trade with Aceh (1600)|treaty signed by Van Caerden and the Sultan]] on [[28 December]] [[1600]].

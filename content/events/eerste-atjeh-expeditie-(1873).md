**The Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)** — the first Dutch military expedition against the Sultanate of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — was the opening action of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], conducted against the [[Missigit (Meusigit)|Missigit]] (the great mosque) of [[Koeta-Radja]] in [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]] and ending in failure. It included the historic storming of the mosque of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]] by Dutch troops, when "the building burned down and later was completely demolished." The expedition would be followed by the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]] of [[1873]]–[[1874]] — which took the Missigit on [[6 January]] [[1874]] — and by the forty-five-year war that would end with the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]].

## The expedition

The Eerste Atjeh-expeditie of [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]] was the first Dutch military expedition against the Sultanate of [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. The expedition, conducted against the [[Missigit (Meusigit)|Missigit]] of [[Koeta-Radja]], failed. The Missigit was not taken until [[6 January]] [[1874]].

## The storming of the mosque (April 1873)

"At the historic storming of this mosque by the Dutch troops in April 1873, the building burned down and later was completely demolished." The April [[1873]] storming is the marker of the opening of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]: the colonial army's storming of the mosque that the Acehnese population had built on the site of the first mosque of [[Sultan Iskandar Muda]].

## The aftermath: the Van Swieten rebuilding

The aftermath of the [[1873]] storming — [[generaal Van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s letter of [[8 February]] [[1874]] and the rebuilding of the [[Meusigit Raja]] — is the marker of the colonial state's religious politics in [[Atjeh|Aceh]]. General Van Swieten — the vanquisher of the Acehnese — managed to bring about that the colonial government, entirely at its own expense, built a new mosque, which was given as a gift to the Acehnese population "to make them forget the suffering of the war." The [[1873]] storming and the [[1874]] rebuilding are the colonial memory's markers of the opening of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and of the colonial state's religious politics.

## The Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873–1874)

The [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]] — which began in [[1873]] and concluded with the taking of the Missigit on [[6 January]] [[1874]] — opened the forty-five-year war. The book notes that "de hevige gevechten van de eerste maanden van [[1876]]" followed, and that the war would continue, in one form or another, until the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]].

## The forty-five-year war

The [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] that opened with the Eerste Atjeh-expeditie of [[April 1873|April]] [[1873]] would last forty-five years — from [[1873]] to the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]] — and would be the principal theatre of the [[K.N.I.L.]] for the next half-century. The war would be pronounced over several times before it actually was; it would be the subject of political controversy in the Netherlands; it would make the reputation of [[Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

## Significance

The Eerste Atjeh-expeditie is significant in both *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* and *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as the opening action of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]] and the storming of the mosque of [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]]. In *Oom Ambon*, it is the book's opening of the colonial arc — the forty-five years from [[1873]] to [[1918]] — that the rest of the book narrates. In *Oost-Indisch Journael*, the [[1873]] storming and the [[1874]] rebuilding are the book's markers of the opening of the Aceh War and of the colonial state's religious politics.

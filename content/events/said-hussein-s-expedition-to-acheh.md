**Said Hussein's expedition to Acheh of mid-[[1815]]** was the maritime undertaking by which [[Said Hussein|Said Hussein]], a wealthy Penang-based merchant of remote Achehnese royal descent, and his son [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] sailed from [[Penang]] to [[Acheh]] to seize the sultanate on the latter's behalf. The expedition was the decisive act in the [[Acheh succession dispute (1802-1819)|Acheh civil war]], installing [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] as the rival sultan and inaugurating the dual rule that would persist — [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] at [[Pedir]], [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] at [[Telok Semawé]] — until the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)|treaty of 22 April 1819]].

## Background

Since [[1802]] [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] had reigned at [[Acheh]] "in so far as circumstances permitted." After a ten-year reign the opposition party judged itself strong enough to seek a replacement, and fixed its eye upon [[Said Hussein|Said Hussein]]. Hussein was said to be a remote kinsman of the sultans' house, but his chief qualification was presumably his wealth: he was a very rich merchant established at [[Penang|Penang]]. The party offered him the throne, with the proviso that, should he deem himself too old, his second son [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] would be acceptable as a counter-king. The son — Van der Kemp's characterisation is unsparing — was "a scoundrel like the rest" and had hitherto served as boatswain, or something of the sort, on his father's proas.

Hussein declined the crown for himself but accepted it on behalf of his son. The throne, however, still had to be won, and to that end a fleet was fitted out at [[Penang]].

## The Expedition

The British administration at [[Penang]] had no particular reason to intervene: as recently as [[1814]] the [[Captain Canning's mission to Acheh (1814)|Canning mission]] had received a highly insulting reception from [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]] and his court, and the Pinang government was content to let the Achehnese sort out their own affairs. Hussein therefore sailed in the middle of 1815, accompanied by his son [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]], and on arrival the latter was proclaimed sultan as *Sil-ul-illum Shak* by the assembled chiefs.

Among the supporters of the revolution was the relatively powerful coastal chief of [[Pedir]], [[Tunku Pakih|Tuku Paekie]], who had contributed much to its success. Before long, however, [[Tunku Pakih|Paekie]] quarrelled with [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] and reverted to the party of the dispossessed [[Sultan Jauhar Alum]], whose seat he established at [[Pedir]]. For [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] Acheh itself soon became too hot: following [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]]'s example, he withdrew to the east coast and fixed his residence at [[Telok Semawé]], between [[Pedir]] and [[Penang]]. At [[Banda Acheh]] itself the federation of the [[Three Sagis]] held effective power.

## Consequences

The expedition created the dual sultanate — [[Sultan Jauhar Alum|Jauhar Alum]] at [[Pedir]], [[Sultan Saif ul-Alum|Syf]] at [[Telok Semawé]] — that would form the immediate background to the [[Raffles-Coombs Acheh Commission (1819)|Raffles-Coombs commission]] of 1819. The civil war continued for several years with such varying fortune that the government of [[Penang]] eventually did not know which of the two pretenders should be recognised. The dispute would, as Van der Kemp observes, generate the conflict between [[Sir Thomas Stamford Raffles|Raffles]] and [[Colonel John Alexander Bannerman|Bannerman]] that gave rise to the commission, and ultimately to the [[Treaty of Pedir (1819)]].

The expedition also implicated the [[Tunku Pakih|Tunku Pakih of Pedir]] in the larger story: his [[Capture of British brig by Tunku Pakih|capture of a British brig]] shortly before [[Captain Coombs' first Acheh mission (1817-1818)|Captain Coombs's first mission]] to Acheh, and his subsequent [[Death of Tunku Pakih|death]] at the hands of the [[Shahbandar of Acheh|Shahbandar of Acheh]] during the Raffles-Coombs commission, were both episodes in the web of intrigue that began with Hussein's expedition.

**Declaration of War on Aceh (26 March 1873)** was the formal proclamation by the [[Dutch East Indies Government]] on [[26 March]] [[1873]] that initiated hostilities against the [[Aceh Sultanate|Sultan of Aceh]]. The declaration followed the signing of the [[Sumatra Treaty (1871)]] and represented the Dutch response to what they described as “unregulated conditions” and “piratical activities” of the Aceh people.

The proclamation authorized the deployment of a combined naval and land force, including the [[Dutch Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]], under the overall command of [[General-Major Köhler]]. The ensuing campaign marked the beginning of the most intensive phase of the Aceh War, lasting until [[1876]] when the marines were withdrawn.

## Causes
- **Treaty obligations**: The Dutch interpreted the Sumatra Treaty as removing British objections to a military solution.
- **Piracy and resistance**: Dutch reports described Acehnese “zeerovers” (sea‑raiders) as a threat to trade and security.
- **Colonial ambition**: The desire to extend Dutch sovereignty over the northern tip of Sumatra.

## Immediate Consequences
- Mobilisation of a fleet comprising the warships **Antwerpen**, **Marnix**, **Soerabaja**, and **Sumatra**.
- Landing of a marine expeditionary force on the Aceh coast on [[5 April]] [[1873]].

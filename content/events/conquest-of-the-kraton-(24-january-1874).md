**The Conquest of the Kraton of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] on 24 January [[1874]]** — *de verovering van den kraton* — was the principal military success of the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|Tweede Atjeh-expeditie]] under [[Jan van Swieten|General van Swieten]] and the foundational event of the Dutch colonial administration of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. The fall of the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] — the walled palace complex of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan of Atjeh]] at [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] — to the Dutch troops on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]], two days before the death of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]], marked the formal end of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] as a sovereign state and the beginning of more than three decades of Dutch military and administrative engagement with Atjeh.

## The Approach (December 1873 – January 1874)

The [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|second Dutch expedition]] under [[Jan van Swieten|General van Swieten]] was assembled at [[Batavia|Batavia]] in the second half of [[1873]], with all available information about Atjeh gathered and prepared. An outbreak of cholera on Java briefly delayed the expedition's departure; even on the ships the disease raged among the troops, and heavy rains further delayed the landing until [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]]. The expeditionary force of approximately 9,000 combatants was divided into three brigades, one of which remained in reserve at [[Padang|Padang]] and only appeared on the scene at the end of [[January 1874|January]] [[1874]]. A strong squadron of the [[Koninklijke Marine|Marine]] supported the operations.

The troops advanced under heavy fighting. On [[7 January]] [[7 January]] [[1874]] the [[Meusigit Raja|groote Missigit]] (great mosque) was taken. "*Dit feit maakte overal diepen indruk.*" (This fact made a deep impression everywhere), Kruijt writes; several chiefs came forward to make their submission.

## The Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)

On [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] itself fell into Dutch hands. Kruijt's account of the conquest is brief — "*het hoofddoel, de verovering van den kraton, bereikt werd*" (the principal object, the conquest of the kraton, was achieved) — but its strategic and symbolic significance receives sustained attention throughout the brochure. The kraton was not merely a building but the walled palace complex of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan of Atjeh]], the political and ceremonial heart of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]], and the symbol of Atjehese sovereignty. Its fall marked the formal end of the sultanate as a sovereign state.

## The Death of the Sultan (26 January 1874)

Two days after the fall of the kraton, on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]], the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] of Atjeh died of cholera. The question of what to do next — Kruijt writes — "*heeft den toestand in Atjeh tot op den huidigen dag beheerscht*" (has governed the situation in Atjeh down to the present day). The original plan had been to conclude a treaty with the Sultan and to leave the integrity of the country intact. Now, with the Sultan dead, Van Swieten proposed instead that the Dutch should step into the Sultan's place, and drafted a proclamation to the chiefs accordingly. To those who submitted and recognised Dutch supremacy, self-government would be left; where this was not the case, the Dutch would attempt direct rule. To give effect to this policy, a strong defensive position would be taken up in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] as a permanent establishment, and from it the work of subjugation would be prosecuted.

## The Consequences

The position chosen was the kraton itself, with certain fixed points on the [[Atjeh River|Atjeh-rivier]] securing the undisturbed possession of that waterway, and other points around the kraton. All available energies were devoted to preparing the position for a permanent garrison. The proclamation was circulated around the coasts by the [[Koninklijke Marine|Marine]]; the chiefs were required to sign an *[[onderwerpingsacte|onderwerpingsacte]]* (act of submission), in which they acknowledged Dutch supremacy, undertook to fly the Dutch flag, and promised to enter into no political relations with other powers.

The fall of the kraton inaugurated more than three decades of Dutch military and administrative engagement with Atjeh. The kraton would become the nucleus of the Dutch *stelling* in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]; the seat of the *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur*; the headquarters of the Dutch military command; the inner fortress of the *geconcentreerde linie*. Its symbolic weight — as the seat of the vanished sultanate — would be invoked by every Dutch governor, and would be invoked again by [[Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] in his [[Proclamation of the Regeeringscommissaris (12 April 1896)|Proclamation to the heads and population of Atjeh]] of [[April]] [[1896]].

## Significance

The conquest of the kraton on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] is the foundational event of the Dutch colonial administration of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. It marked the formal end of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] as a sovereign state, the death of the Sultan two days later, and the Dutch decision to step into the Sultan's place as the sovereign of Atjeh. The kraton — once the seat of the Sultan's authority — became the seat of the Dutch *Civiel en Militair Gouvernur* and the headquarters of the Dutch military command. The Dutch *stelling* in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]], which would consume Dutch military power for three decades, was built around the kraton. The symbolic weight of the kraton — as the seat of the vanished sultanate — would be invoked by every Dutch governor to legitimise the Dutch succession to the Sultan's authority.

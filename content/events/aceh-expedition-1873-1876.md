The **Aceh Expedition (1873–1876)** — also referred to as the **Atjeh Expedition (1875–1876)** or the more focused **Atjeh campaign of 26 December 1875 – 4 September 1876** — was a series of Dutch military operations conducted by the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] and the [[indische-leger|Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] against the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh (Aceh)]]. The expedition marked the opening phase of the protracted [[aceh-war|Aceh War]] and involved amphibious landings, river patrols, and the establishment of temporary camps in [[Great Aceh|Groot Atjeh]]. The 1875–1876 sub-campaign, documented in detail by [[R. J. J. P. Cambier]] in his monograph *De mariniers te Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876* (The Marines in Aceh from 26 December 1875 to 4 September 1876), is the most fully recorded component of the operation.

## Chronology

- **[[1873]]** — Initial naval engagements and reconnaissance missions along the Aceh coast. The early phase culminated in the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the kraton]] on [[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]], after which the war was formally suspended.
- **[[1875]]** — Intensified ground operations resumed under [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]], who returned to Atjeh on [[8 November]] [[1875]] with instructions of [[10-november|10 November]] [[1875]] authorising him to break with [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s purely defensive policy. Pel's campaigns of [[December 1875]]–[[February 1876]] carried the Dutch through the [[XXII Mukims|VI Moekims]], [[XXVI Mukims|IV Moekims]] and [[XXVI Mukims|IX Moekims]] of the [[XXV Mukims|Sagi der XXV Moekims]] before his sudden death from an arterial rupture at the kampong Tonga on [[24-february|24 February]] [[1876]].
- **[[1876]]** — Continued fighting in the interior; the focused offensive of [[26-december|26 December]] [[1875]]–[[4 September]] [[1876]] concluded with a temporary cease-fire on [[4 September]] [[1876]].

## The 26 December 1875 – 4 September 1876 campaign

The focused Dutch offensive was conducted by the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Korps Mariniers]] against fortified positions in the interior of [[Atjeh|Atjeh (Aceh)]]. Its campaign phases were:

- **Landing ([[26-december|26 December]] [[1875]])** — Marines disembarked at the coastal base of [[Banda Aceh|Kota Radja]] and moved inland.
- **Bivouacs (January–March [[1876]])** — Temporary camps were established in [[Great Aceh|Groot Atjeh]], described in the source as "*Bivaks op Groot Atjeh*" (Bivouacs on Great Aceh) (page 471).
- **Final Assault (April–June [[1876]])** — Coordinated attacks on rebel strongholds, culminating in the cease-fire of [[4 September]] [[1876]].

The operation is documented in [[R. J. J. P. Cambier|Cambier]]'s monograph *De mariniers te Atjeh van 26 December 1875 tot 4 September 1876* and referenced in the *Atjeh Marineblad* ([[1890]]–[[1891]]). Cambier's detailed narrative (page 473) provides day-by-day accounts, while the *Atjeh Marineblad* publishes official reports and casualty lists.

## Operational highlights

The expedition combined naval bombardments with infantry advances, constructing forts at strategic points such as [[Koeta-Radja|Banda Aceh]] and the riverine outpost of [[Krueng Seumantok]]. The marines operated under the command of **Colonel Heuter**, whose leadership is noted in the corps' records. Uniforms of the marines from 1873–1876 are depicted in the illustrated plates (page 471). The *Map of Groot Atjeh* (c. [[1873]]) was employed for planning bivouac sites (see [[bivouacs-on-groot-atjeh|Bivouacs on Groot Atjeh]]).

## Significance

The 1875–1876 campaign marked a turning point in the Dutch approach to Aceh, shifting from short punitive raids to a strategy of permanent occupation and infrastructure development. The campaign also laid the groundwork for the further sub-campaigns of [[1879]] (the [[Campaign against the XXII Moekims (1879)|submission of the XXII Moekims]] and the [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|reduction of the XXVI Moekims]]) that brought the first phase of the Aceh War to its initial conclusion.

## Brooshooft's Account

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft treats the years [[1873]]–[[1876]] as the opening phase of the war. This period covers the [[April 1873 Atjeh Expedition|first expedition]], the interlude of blockade and naval action, the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]] under [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] culminating in the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]], the consolidation under [[Verspijck|Verspyck]] and [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]], the building of the fortified ring around [[koeta-radja|Kota Radja]] through the actions at [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|Garouw]], [[Battle of Soerian (26 July 1874)|Soerian]], [[Battle of Longbatta (31 December 1874)|Longbatta]] and [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|Lemboe]], and Pel's death from cholera on [[24-february|24 February]] [[1876]]. It was, he argues, the period in which the Dutch learned the true nature of the war they had undertaken.

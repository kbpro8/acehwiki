The **Relief of Tjot Rang** on [[16 April]] [[1896]] was the operation by which two Dutch columns, together numbering 2,700 men, broke the investment of the temporary post of [[Tjot Rang]] in the [[Sagi der XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and replaced its garrison, at the cost of forty-five killed and wounded incurred chiefly during their withdrawal.

## The investment

After [[Luitenant Neelmeyer]] delivered his warning of the defection of [[Teukoe Oemar]], the Achehnese-held watch-posts nearby were suddenly abandoned, and both the great road from Tjot Rang to [[Tjot Iri]] — along which the lieutenant had escaped — and the road to [[Toengkoeb]] were found to be occupied by the enemy. At seventy-five metres from the *benteng* the Achehnese, so skilful with the *[[Patjol|patjol]]*, dug a trench fifty metres long from which the post was fired on with rifle and *[[Lilla|lilla]]*. To send any message thence to [[Kota Radja]] proved impossible.

Day after day the garrison looked out for relief, while the *[[Kettingganger|kettingganger]]* who served as lamplighter tried under enemy fire to catch fish from the surrounding pools as a delicacy for the officers.

## The relief

*Totdat eindelijk den 16 April 1896 kanonvuur in de verte gehoord werd, en twee colonnes, te zamen 2700 hoofden tellende, op kwamen dagen om den vijand te verjagen en de bezetting door nieuwe troepen te doen vervangen* — until at last on 16 April 1896 cannon-fire was heard in the distance and two columns, together numbering 2,700 men, appeared to drive off the enemy and replace the garrison with fresh troops.

The enemy's resistance had not been great, but when the columns withdrew he followed them and raised their losses to forty-five dead and wounded. A few days later the post was abandoned and slighted without serious opposition.

**The Bataq doeloe poeloh toedjéh** — the "Twenty-Seven Bataks" (Malay *dua puluh tujuh*) — is the legendary immigration of a party of [[Bataks|Batak]] settlers into the [[Gajoland|Gajolanden]] that established the colony of [[Wibasan|Wibasan]] to the west of [[Laoet Tawar|Laoet Tawar]] and later swarmed out from [[Bobasan|Bobasan]] to populate a wide territory, displacing the earlier Saëda-descended [[Boekét|Boekét]] chiefs and founding the [[Redjë Tjèq|Redjë Tjèq]] polity. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau records that the immigration "nog door tal van legenden in de herinnering van het volk voortleeft" — still lives on in the memory of the people through numerous legends.

## The colony at Wibasan

The original settlement at Wibasan was, the handbook notes, a "oorspronkelijk kleine kolonie" — originally a small colony — that has since "zich sterk uitgebreid" — strongly expanded. From Bobasan the settlers "uitgezwermd over een wijd gebied." Strikingly for a Gayo context, in which the endemic pattern was the splintering of small *snipperrepubliekjes*, the colony "niets van haren onderlingen samenhang heeft ingeboet" — lost nothing of its internal cohesion. [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje|Prof. Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]] is quoted in the handbook (p. 151) as observing that "in heel het Gajöland met zijne tallooze snipperrepubliekjes" there is no commonwealth of such extent "welks deelen zoo innig verbonden zijn" — whose parts are so intimately connected.

## The five original stammen

The genealogical units present in the first colony persisted after the expansion, and five *oorspronkelijke stammen* are still recognisable as the genealogical backbone of the Redjë Tjèq territory: (1) the **Trélè-rë**, headed by *Rëdjë Tjèq*, whose *pëtoeo* and *imëm* bear the superior titles *Këtoeo Dalën* and *Imëm Tjèq*; (2) the **Tjélala**, split by jealousy among the *rédjë*'s *Gémbéra* and *Péngoeloe Sagi*; (3) the **Pëngi**, divided into the *Garang*, *Angën* and *Balohén* lines; (4) the **Linggö**, partly under *Péngoeloe Linggö* and partly under *rédjë Kemala* and *rédjë Kaja*; and (5) the **Moenté**, distinguished into Moenté Padang, Moenté Gajö and Moenté Kala. With the exception of the Moenté Gajö — who actually descend from the wide-spread **Löt** stam of the Dërot area and were later incorporated — all trace their descent to the original 27 Bataks.

## Displacement of the Boekét chiefs

The Saëda foundation-legend, "welke als de minst onbetrouwbare gevolgd wordt," tells of a certain Saëda who emerged at [[Seroelë|Seroelë]] in the [[Dërotstreek|Dërotstreek]] from a piece of clay that had fallen from heaven, and who obtained from the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] the dignity of *Rëdjë Boekét* and a *bawar* of five *ornamenten*. Saëda settled in the [[Meerstreek|Meerstreek]] and, with the title *Mérhoem Meukoeta Alam*, "vorst over allen werd" — became prince over all — until the "invasie der *Bataq doeloe poeloh toedjéh*." In the resulting war the Boekét family "werd gedwongen te vluchten"; Saëda returned to Seroelë and disappears "voorgoed van het tooneel" — forever from the scene. Mérah Lama withdrew to [[Kébajakan|Kébajakan]], and [[Panglima Dagang|Panglima Dagang]] to [[Meureudoe|Meureudoe]], leaving the Redjë Tjèq territory in the hands of the new Batak-descended settlers.

## Significance

The immigration of the *Bataq doeloe poeloh toedjéh* is the foundational legend of the Redjë Tjèq polity and the touchstone of Gayo genealogical identity. Unlike the Boekét chiefs, whose genealogies dissolve into myth and ad hoc splits, the Redjë Tjèq stammen preserved a cohesion rare in Gayo political life — a cohesion that survived colonisation and that the Dutch administration recognised when it confirmed the *Rëdjë Tjèq* of Bobasan as the senior chief of the western Gayo lands.

**Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)** — the [[1896]] act of betrayal (*verraad*) by the [[Acheh|Atjehnese]] military leader [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] (Teuku Umar) against the Dutch — is identified by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau as the originating event of the "new direction" in Dutch policy in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] that would lead to the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|complete pacification of the territory]]. The Bureau's account frames the betrayal as a defining caesura in the history of the Dutch East Indies' outer possessions: together with the [[1894]] subjection of [[Lombok]] and the [[1902]] turn toward active policy in [[Djambi]]/[[Koerintji]], it is one of the three milestone moments by which the Bureau periodises the Dutch "ommekeer" (about-turn) in policy toward the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar]] appears in two highly charged passages. The first passage, in the chapter on administrative organisation, frames the verraad as the originating event of the post-[[1896]] Dutch strategic reorientation:

> "Nadat — ongereken eenige gevallen van minder belang, waarbij vestiging van onzen daadwerkelijken bestuursinvloed had plaats gehad in tot dusverre slechts nominatief onder ons gezag staande binnenlanden — reeds in [[1894]] het eiland [[Lombok|Lombok]] onder geregeld bestuur gebracht was, werd in [[1896]], na het verraad van [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]], eene nieuwe richting ingeluid in zake ons optreden in [[Acheh|Atjeh]], welke tot de volledige [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] van dat gewest zou leiden."

The second passage, in the chapter on the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]], links the verraad directly to the dismantling of the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] — the famous line of forts around [[Kota Radja]] that had defined the geography of the Dutch position in Atjeh since the [[1870s]]:

> "Met de opheffing der [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] na het verraad van [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] in [[1896]] verviel het nut van de [[Ceintuur-trambaan|ceintuurbaan]], welke sedert geleidelijk opgeruimd werd. In de plaats daarvan kwam de lange tramlijn, welke sedert [[1897]] onze gezagsuitbreiding in [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden]] op den voet volgde, en post na post der zich steeds meer uitbreidende militaire bezetting vastknoopte aan het gezagscentrum te [[Kota Radja|Kota-Radja]]."

The two passages together establish the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad]] as the originating event of two parallel transformations: the strategic reorientation of Dutch Atjeh policy (which would lead to the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|complete pacification]]) and the physical reorganisation of the Dutch military infrastructure (from the defensive [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] strategy of [[1885]] to the offensive long-line strategy of [[1897]] onward).

## Significance

The [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar in 1896]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's periodisation, the originating event of the post-[[1896]] Dutch strategic reorientation in Atjeh — and, by extension, of the wider Buitenbezittingen project of "effective authority" that the Bureau dates from [[1902]]. The Bureau's choice of the word *verraad* (betrayal) places the Dutch interpretation firmly on the surface of the account; the reader is invited to understand the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]] as the Dutch response to [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]]'s act. The same act, viewed from the Atjehnese side, was of course an act of patriotic resistance — a fact the Bureau's account does not foreground but which the very prominence of the *verraad* in the Dutch narrative indirectly confirms.

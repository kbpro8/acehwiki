**The first Dutch expedition to [[Atjeh]] in [[1873]]** was the initial military attempt by the [[Dutch East Indies]] to subjugate the [[Sultanate of Aceh]]. The expedition ended in failure, but it established the pattern of resistance—particularly from the [[XXII Moekims]]—that would characterise the entire thirty-year conflict.

## The expedition and its failure

The first expedition, launched in [[1873]], was unsuccessful. Although Dutch troops did not enter the [[XXII Moekims]], the inhabitants of that sagi played a major role in resisting the "kaffirs"—the Christian Dutch, described as enemies of the [[Koran]]. This demonstrated from the very beginning that resistance was not limited to direct confrontation but extended to all areas where Dutch forces sought to establish authority.

## The second expedition and the kraton (late 1873-1874)

In the latter part of the same year, a second expedition—under [[Generaal Van Swieten]]—achieved greater success. The Dutch forces eventually captured the [[kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]]. The impression of this success was considerable: significant chiefs from the [[XXV]] and [[XXVI Moekims]] came to pay their respects to [[Van Swieten]], and the population of those sagi's gave signs of being tired of further struggle.

However, among the chiefs who refused any approach—who were not deterred by Dutch success and who prevented the population that desired peace from expressing it—[[Panglima Polim]] (then [[Sri Moeda Perkasa]]) stood at the forefront. He made his territory a gathering place for all who wished to continue the war, and his influence radiated over all the landscapes of [[Groot-Atjeh]].

## The beginning of prolonged resistance

Although the capture of the [[kraton]] and the death of the young sultan two days later ([[26 January]] [[1874]]) seemed to improve the prospects for rapid pacification, the resistance led by [[Panglima Polim]] demonstrated that the Dutch could not rely on the disappearance of the sultanate to end the conflict. The resistance would continue—organised from the [[XXII Moekims]], sustained by spiritual leaders in [[Keumala]], and guided by the persistent family line of the [[Polims]]—for the next three decades.

The **Hasan-Husain feast** is the commemoration observed, in [[Acheh]] as in other parts of the East Indian Archipelago, in honour of the deaths of Hasan and Husain — the grandsons of [[Prophet Mohammad|the Prophet Mohammad]] — and marked especially by the procession of the *tabut* or *talbit*. C. Snouck Hurgronje treats it in *The Achehnese* chiefly in connection with the Achehnese *jeunadah* — the ark-like bier in which the bodies of unmarried persons are carried to the grave — which takes its name, by a transfer of meaning, from the similar cofrer used in the Hasan-Husain procession.

## The Procession

The Hasan-Husain feast, as Snouck Hurgronje describes it, is observed by the Klings (the South Indian Muslims of the Straits Settlements) and the people of [[Padang]] (on the west coast of [[Sumatra]]) by carrying round in procession a coffer called the *talbit* or *tabut*. The same name is given, in Acheh, to a similar coffer which is used to send yellow glutinous rice, with a certain amount of pomp, as a present on the occasion of the [[peusunat|circumcision]] of the son of a relative or friend. The custom is one of the many traces of South Indian and Hadrami influence on the religious life of the East Indian Archipelago, and reflects the strong emotional appeal that the story of the martyrdom of Husain at Karbala has had upon Mohammedans of every school.

## The Achehnese Jeunadah

Although the Hasan-Husain feast is not a national Achehnese festival, its name and ceremonial have left a mark on Achehnese funeral practice. The Arabic word for the interment — *janāzah* — is corrupted by the Achehnese into **jeunadah**, but has with them taken on an entirely different signification from its Arabic original. If a child dies unmarried, and also sometimes in case of the death of young married people or women in childbed, the custom is to bear the body to the grave in a sort of ark, the frame of which is made of wood or bamboo, entirely surrounded by a cloth covering, over which are scattered flowers such as betelnut blossom, chempaka, melati, and so forth. This ark, which is usually prepared by the women, is called the *jeunadah*; after the burial it is laid on the top of the grave.

The same name is given to the *talbit* which is carried round in procession by the Klings and the people of Padang during the Hasan-Husain feast. The transfer of the name is an instructive example of the way in which an external Mohammedan usage has been adapted, in Acheh, to a quite different local purpose — the decoration of the graves of the young and unmarried.

## Position in the Achehnese Calendar

Snouck Hurgronje does not include the Hasan-Husain feast among the festivals of the Achehnese calendar proper. The Mohammedan year of Acheh is opened by the [[Ashura|kanduri Ashura]] of [[Muharram]] — a feast observed in some Mohammedan countries in connection with the Hasan-Husain commemoration — but the Achehnese observance of Ashura is detached from the Hasan-Husain mourning and transformed into a kanduri of its own. The Hasan-Husain feast proper is confined to the foreign Muslim communities — the Klings of [[Kuta Raja]] and the people of Padang origin — and the Achehnese who take part in it do so only as guests.

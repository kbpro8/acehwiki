**The Death of Generaal Pel (24 February 1876)** was the sudden death, in the night of [[24 February]] [[1876]], of [[Generaal Pel|generaal Pel]] at the bivouac at [[Lamjoeng]] — between [[Tongah]] and Silang, on the far side of the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] — of a *slagaderbreuk* (ruptured artery), in the very days when his *noorderwaartse* march was sealing off the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] mouth and completing the closure of Atjeh from the sea, an event Borel calls "eene ware nationale ramp."

## The march

On [[22 February]] [[1876]] Pel marched from [[Pango]] northward via Lamkapang, Loeëng Iek, Poeklat and Oeleh Karang to the kampong of Pinang, which was found abandoned — "in den rug genomen" — as were all the enemy's earlier *versterkingen* there. The troops bivouacked that night at the Mesigit te Kajoe Adang. On [[23 February]] [[1876]] Langgoegoeb was *doorgetrokken* and [[Tongah]] on the left bank of the [[Kroeng Tjoet]] was reached. At the bivouac at [[Lamjoeng]], on the far side of the river, between Tongah and Silang, Pel in the night of [[24 February]] [[1876]] suddenly *overleed* of the consequences of a *slagaderbreuk*.

## Borel's elegy

Borel's elegy is the most quoted passage of the book. "Het vaderland verloor in hem een zijner trouwste, ijverigste en meest bekwame dienaren; dat verlies was in de gegeven omstandigheden bijna onherstelbaar en kon eene ware nationale ramp genoemd worden." Pel's plan — the great [[Pel's Buitenzorg planning session (1875)|Kroeng Raba–Kroeng Raya line]] to isolate Atjeh from the sea — remained *geheim*: he had not communicated it even to his chief of staff, and nothing about it was found in his archive. The plan, Borel concludes, was lost with him.

## Engel continues the march

[[Luitenant-kolonel Engel|Lt.-kol. Engel]], who had arrived at Lamjoeng with the 9de battalion to *versterk* the *ageerende troepen*, assumed *tijdelijk oppergezag* as the oldest *hoofdofficier* in Atjeh. He immediately took the required measures and continued the march via Tjade to [[Kwala Gigieng]], reached on [[26 February]] [[1876]], where a bivouac was *opgeslagen*. Posts were *opgericht* at Kwala Gigieng and on the island of [[Kota Pohama]] in the lagune, to completely seal the *gemeenschap* between sea and the mouth of the Kroeng Tjoet. The closure was Pel's last completed work.

## Aftermath

On [[10 March]] [[1876]] [[Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem|generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] assumed military and civil command. From then on, Borel writes, the war entered another phase: Wiggers was "geen voorstander van het aanvallende stelsel"; he wished not to extend the *linie* further and to adopt a "strijkt afwachtende houding." The line Kroeng Raba–Kwala Gigieng has remained "nagenoeg onveranderd" ever since. Pel's death, in Borel's view, turned a war that was on the point of being won into a war that would drag on for years.

**Moens' journey through Atjeh (November 1913)** was the fourteen-day tour of the pacified [[Atjeh]] made in November [[1913]] by the retired naval lieutenant [[W. Moens]] at the invitation of luitenant-generaal [[H. N. A. Swart]], Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden — a journey which, because Moens had served about twenty years earlier in the naval blockade of the Acehnese coast, became a documented comparison between the Atjeh of the war and the Atjeh of the peace, and was published as the feuilleton series *[[Reisindrukken van Atjeh (Moens, 1914)|Reisindrukken van Atjeh]]* in the *[[Algemeen Handelsblad]]* and as a booklet by [[Jacob van Campen (publisher)|Jacob van Campen]] in [[1914]].

## The invitation at Sabang

Moens had left Holland in July 1913 for a business journey through other parts of the Indies; the fortnight in Atjeh *stond niet op het programma* (was not on the programme). On the outward voyage, arriving at [[Sabang]], he met there Gouverneur Swart, an old acquaintance, who pressed him to reserve fourteen days at the end of his business trip in order to convince himself with his own eyes of the state of the country. What decided Moens was Swart's remark that it pleased him when Hollanders themselves came to see what Atjeh now was, so that more confidence in the land's conditions might arise in the mother country.

## The route

The journey began with the crossing from [[Pangkalan Brandan]] by steam launch over the [[Aru Bay|Aroe-baai]], past the petroleum-tank village of [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoeh]] and up the [[Salahadji]] river to the little place of that name, and then over a hardened art-road to [[Koeala Simpang|Kwalla Simpang]] on the [[Tamiang]] river, the first post of a government official on Acehnese soil — where the civiel-gezaghebber [[W. Wessel|Wessels]] greeted him: *Nu is u werkelijk in het gevaarlijke (!) Atjeh* (Now you really are in dangerous (!) Atjeh). At Kwalla Simpang Moens witnessed the placing of the last span of the [[Completion of the Tamiang bridge (November 1913)|Tamiang bridge]], visited the first native school on Acehnese soil, saw the market, the cinema of an Arab, and the tram terminus, and was shown the rubber estate [[Soengei Lipoet]]. By the [[Atjeh-tram]] he travelled over [[Langsa|Langsar]] and [[Peureula]] — where the oil of the [[Perlak-Petroleum-Mij|Perlak]] fields was pumped in a heavy pipeline to Pangkalan Brandan — to [[Idi]], with a noon-day halt, and so along the foot of the hills past [[Lho Soekon|Loh Soekoen]] to [[Lho' Seumawe|Loh Semaweu]], the once fortified post and headquarter of the assistent-resident of the North Coast, [[M. C. Roos van Raadshoven]].

From Loh Semaweu Moens went by dogcart some twenty kilometres to the maréchaussée-bivak of [[Boeloh Blang Ara]], where four brigades and two officers still patrolled after the remaining [[Kwaadwilligen|kwaadwilligen]] of the mountains; with the archaeologist [[De Vink]] he visited the grave of the queen of [[Pasé]] at [[Koeta Kareuëng]]; and he called upon the oelèëbalang, the [[Mahraja of Lho' Seumawe|Maharadja of L. Semaweu]], who offered him a glass of Heidsieck monopole. From [[Bireuën]] he drove up the [[Gajo-weg|Gajoe-weg]] as far as [[Kroeng Seumpoe]], passing the iron bridge over the [[Peusanganrivier (Peusangan River)|Peusangan]] and the region where kapitein [[Heinzenknecht]] had worked and been murdered. With Gouverneur Swart and the ex-Minister of War [[Hendrikus Colijn|Colijn]] — the whole in an extra train consisting of locomotive, tender, goods van and salon wagon with after platform — he went on over [[Samalanga]] to [[Sigli]], where majoor [[Th. J. Veltman|Veldman]] received them and where Moens visited the courtroom of the [[Scheepens murder (1913)|Scheepens murder]], slept in the *Fransche barak*, and sat at dinner with three bearers of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]]. Over the waterscheiding (watershed) at [[Selimoen]] he reached the valley of [[Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Koeta-Radja|Koeta Radja]], where he lodged at the Governor's house; one day after Colijn had departed — his presence having made the last part of the journey *vruchtbaarder en interessanter* (more fruitful and more interesting) — Moens left by way of [[Sabang]] for Europe.

## The events of the peace

The journey coincided with the last weeks before the *onafhankelijkheidsfeesten* (the [[Onafhankelijkheidsfeesten at Koeta Radja (November 1913)|independence festivities]] of November 1913), when the officials left their posts for the capital without concern; it passed the ground of the [[Expedition against Tamiang (1893)|Tamiang expeditions of 1893]], the empty military hospital of [[Pante Perak|Panteh Perak]], and the ruins of the old *[[Ceintuur-trambaan|ceintuurbaan]]* embankment near Koeta Radja. On the way Swart gave Moens his reasoning on the pursuit of the kwaadwilligen, and the chiefs of Samalanga greeted *toewan Colijn* — of whose 160 days' [[Colijn's march from Medan to Sigli|march from Medan to Sigli]] Moens heard the story at Veldman's table — while in Koeta Radja Moens heard the case of [[Panglima Polim]]'s sister, which Swart had decided, with the council of the oldest oelama's of Groot-Atjeh, in the [[Panglima Polim divorce case (c. 1913)|divorce case against the dictates of adat]].

## Significance

Moens's journey is significant as one of the earliest published Dutch inspections of pacified Atjeh by an independent eyewitness. It gave the Netherlands a route-by-route description of the country as it stood after forty years of war: the trams and roads in use, the markets busy, the schools full, the Europeans travelling alone, the tax-collectors carrying cash unescorted. The editorial note of the *Algemeen Handelsblad* underlined that the value of the impressions lay, besides the picture of a considerable part of Sumatra, in the establishment of a historical fact of the greatest significance for the Netherlands and its colonies: *Er is vrede op Atjeh, de Atjeh-oorlog is voorbij* (There is peace in Atjeh, the Atjeh war is over).

The **Second Aceh Expedition** was a massive colonial military invasion launched by the Kingdom of the Netherlands between [[November]] [[1873]] and [[April]] [[1874]] to conquer the [[Sultanate of Aceh]] and avenge the repulse of the [[First Aceh Expedition]].

## Preparations and Command

Following the [[1873]] retreat, the Dutch government appointed retired Lieutenant-General [[Jan van Swieten]] as Civil Government Commissioner and Supreme Military Commander on [[11 June]] [[1873]]. Van Swieten assembled an expeditionary force comprising 13,000 personnel, including 8,200 soldiers, 4,800 laborers, 74 heavy siege guns, and 3,100 naval personnel aboard 25 warships and 22 transports. Major-General [[G.M. Verspijck]] served as second-in-command.

## Operational Narrative

The fleet arrived off Aceh in [[November]] [[1873]], delayed by a severe cholera epidemic. On [[9 December]] [[1873]], Van Swieten executed the [[Landing at Pedro Point]], disembarking the 2nd and 3rd Brigades 11 kilometers east of the Aceh River mouth.

Advancing along the coast, Dutch forces captured [[Kota Pohama]] and [[Kota Musapi]] before assaulting the heavy fortifications of the [[Battle of the Tiban Line]] on [[14 December]] [[1873]], expelling Acehnese defenders under [[Tuanku Hashim]].

To secure a river supply line, Colonel [[G. Wiggers van Kerchem]] and Lieutenant-Colonel [[Jacobus Pel]] led columns through dense coastal swamps, achieving the [[Capture of Peunayong]] on [[18 December]] [[1873]]. Dutch forces established a massive fortified bivouac at [[Peunayong]].

On [[25 December]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]], Dutch troops fought the [[Battles of Lembu]], capturing key redoutes. Sappers constructed siege trenches and front mortar batteries facing the [[Mesjid Raya Aceh]]. On [[6 January]] [[1874]], the 2nd Brigade executed the [[Third Capture of Mesjid Raya]] under heavy artillery support.

On [[12 January]] [[1874]], Colonel [[K. Schultze]] completed the [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan]], securing the western approach to the palace. [[Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX]] evacuated the [[Kraton of Aceh]] on [[15 January]] [[1874]] for [[Longbatta]].

From [[24 January]] to [[26 January]] [[1874]], General Verspijck and Lieutenant-Colonel [[Karel van der Heijden]] led columns in the [[Encirclement and Fall of the Kraton]]. Finding the palace evacuated, Dutch forces occupied the Kraton on [[26 January]] [[1874]].

## Annexation and Aftermath

Sultan Mahmud Syah IX died of cholera at Longbatta on [[28 January]] [[1874]]. General Van Swieten issued the [[Proclamation of Annexation of Aceh]] on [[31 January]] [[1874]]. Leaving Colonel [[Jacobus Pel]] with a garrison of 3,200 men, Van Swieten dissolved the expedition force in [[April]] [[1874]].

## Account from *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu* (dated end 1873)

End of [[1873]], a second expedition, twice as strong (~[[7200]] men), landed under General [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]]. The occupied area was not relinquished because the hoped-for submission of [[Sultan of Aceh|the Sultan]] with '[[aanbreng]]' (tribute) of his whole kingdom failed to materialize.

*Source: J. Jongejans, Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu (Baarn: Hollandia Drukkerij N.V., c. 1938), pp. 38.*

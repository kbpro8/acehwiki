**Second Aceh Expedition** was the massive follow-up military campaign launched by the Dutch in late [[1873]] and [[1874]] after the failure of the [[First Aceh Expedition]].

## The Campaign
Landing on 9 December [[1873]], the heavily reinforced Dutch army established strong beachheads and bivouacs, including a major camp at [[Village Penajoeng]]. Unlike the first expedition, this campaign utilized methodical siege tactics, slowly advancing across the [[Pantej Perak]] plains. This culminated in the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] and the subsequent capture of the [[Kraton of Atjeh]], although Acehnese resistance continued for decades.

## Nineteenth-Century Historiography & Campaign Analysis

## Preparation and Armada Mobilization

Following the defeat of the [[First Aceh Expedition]] in April [[1873]], the government in [[Batavia]] established the [[Bureau for War Equipments on Sumatra]] in June [[1873]]. More than 13,000 soldiers of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]], supported by 2,770 convict laborers ([[Forced Labor in Aceh Military Campaigns]]) and 2,500 free coolies ([[Transport and Coolie System in Aceh]]), were assembled under the veteran General [[J. van Swieten]].

## Narrative of the Coastal Landings and Advance

To avoid coastal fortifications around [[Meuraxa]] and [[Oleh-leh]], General [[J. van Swieten]] landed his invasion force at [[Koerong Raba Bay]] in December [[1873]]. Fighting through torrential rain and flooded rice fields ([[Sawah Cultivation and Supply in Aceh]]), Dutch brigades executed the [[Attack on Koerong Raba]] and captured coastal settlements.

On [[30 December]] [[1873]], as recorded in the [[Commander's Journal of the Second Aceh Expedition]], General [[J. van Swieten]] received the [[Raja of Gighen Letter of Submission]]. Simultaneously, indigenous leader [[Teuku Nek of Meuraxa]] defected to the Dutch side, providing crucial topographical intelligence that enabled Dutch columns to seize [[Pekan Badak]] and [[Sinangri]].

## Storming of Kotta Alam and Capture of the Kraton

In early January [[1874]], Dutch artillery pulverized the fortified redoubt of [[Kotta Alam]] during the [[Capture of Kotta Alam]]. Advancing steadily along the Aceh River, General [[J. van Swieten]] launched the final assault on the royal capital on [[24 January]] [[1874]]. 

Troops entered [[Kraton of Aceh]] only to find that Sultan [[Sultan Mahmud Syah of Aceh]] and his court had evacuated into the interior, accompanied by leaders such as [[Panglima Polem]], [[Teuku Imam Lueng Batoh]], and [[Teuku Nanta Setia]].

## Telegrams and the Myth of Victory

Convinced that the fall of the palace meant total victory, General [[J. van Swieten]] transmitted the [[General van Swieten War Telegrams]] on [[28 January]] and [[2 February]] [[1874]], predicting general peace. However, resistance in the [[XXII Moekims]] and [[XXV Moekims]] intensified. In April [[1874]], [[J. van Swieten]] returned to Java, leaving General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] to manage an overextended occupation force ravaged by cholera ([[Field Hospital and Cholera Epidemics in Aceh]]).

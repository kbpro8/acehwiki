**The Siege of Naarden (1674)** was a military operation undertaken by the **[[Prince of Orange (William III)]]** and his Dutch forces against French‑supported garrisons in the town of **[[Naarden]]** on **[[3 September]] [[1674]]**.  The source describes the opening of artillery fire on the town’s walls and gates, the storming of the **Huizerpoort**, and the eventual capitulation of the French commander **Dupas** on **[[12 September]] [[1674]]** (see [[276]]‑[[277]]).

## Connection to Aceh
The siege formed part of the broader **[[Dutch–French War]]**, whose outcome affected the security of the **[[Indische retourvloot]]**.  By repelling French forces, the Dutch ensured that their navy could continue to protect merchant convoys bound for the **[[East Indies]]**, including the spice‑rich Sultanate of **[[Aceh]]**.  The victory also freed Dutch resources to be redeployed to the **[[Dutch East India Company]]**’s Asian operations.

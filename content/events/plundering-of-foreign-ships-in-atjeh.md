**Plundering of foreign ships in Atjeh** (1844, 1851, 1855, 1868) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. Series of plunders of foreign ships on Atjeh territory: in 1844 two English merchant ships; in 1851 one from Naples; in 1855 another English ship; in 1868 yet again. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to [[1844]], [[1851]], [[1855]], [[1868]].

## Account

Series of plunders of foreign ships on Atjeh territory: in [[1844]] two English merchant ships; in [[1851]] one from Naples; in [[1855]] another English ship; in [[1868]] yet again. These incidents illustrated the untenable nature of the [[1824]] treaty arrangement and gradually pushed toward Dutch intervention.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

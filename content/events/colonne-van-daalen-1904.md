**The Colonne van Daalen naar de Gajolanden en Alaslanden (1904)** was the military *zegetocht* — victorious campaign — led by the then-Colonel [[G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]] in June [[1904]] through the interior highlands of northern Sumatra, by which the [[Gajoland|Gajolanden]] and the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] were "ineens geheel onder onze macht" — at once brought entirely under Dutch control — and incorporated into the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. Together with the sequel expedition of [[1905]], it ended the long isolation in which the "onverzoenlijken" — the irreconcilables — of the interior had preserved their autonomy throughout the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]].

## Background: an unknown interior

Before [[1904]], *De Buitenbezittingen* records, the Dutch "wisten goed als niets" — knew effectively nothing — of the inhabitants of the Alas valley; "van land en volk slechts van weinig beteekenis." The Gayo and Alas lands had served throughout the [[Aceh War]] as refuges for Acehnese fighters driven out of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and the coastal *afdeelingen*. Their incorporation was the logical extension of the "systeem Graafland, van Daalen en van Heutsz," under which "kleine, vliegende colonnes" had been striking at irreconcilable bands everywhere beyond the *postenlinie* since [[1896]].

## The Gayo Loeës episode: the minor Kédjoerön Bédén

In [[Gajoland|Gajo Loeës]] the column's intervention turned on the person of the minor [[Kédjoerön Bédén]]. In [[1902]] a *kédjoerön* had once again been driven out, whereupon a previously deposed *kédjoerön*, then living in [[Tamiang|Tamiang]] and in contact with the Dutch administration, contrived to have his young son Bédén chosen as successor. He then spirited the minor out of the Gayo lands without the *verplichte overleg* — the required consultation — with the other *rédjë's* of Gajo Loeës, took him to [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]], and had him sign the [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]]. In [[1904]] the *colonne van Daalen* escorted this nominal *kédjoerön* back to his country and had him recognised there as such. The result was a *volhardend streven onzerzijds* — a persistent effort on the Dutch side — to give the *kédjoerön* the real power of a central territorial prince, against the *hevig verzet* of the other *tjèqs* of Gajo Loeës, who "algemeen meende" that Bédén and his father had sold the country to the "*Kompeni*."

## The Alaslanden: Radja Tjèq's submission

In the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] the column brought the [[Radja Tjèq of Alas|Radja Tjèq]] — the *roi fainéant* theoretically overlord of the entire Alas valley — and the two *Kédjoerön*s of [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]] within the Dutch sphere. Their submission extinguished the last claim of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] to sovereignty in the interior.

## Significance

The campaign opened the previously isolated highlands to Dutch administration and, eventually, to the great *verkeersweg* via the [[Karo-hoogvlakte|Karo-hoogvlakte]] to the [[Oostkust van Atjeh|Oostkust]] that would bind the Alas valley economically to the rest of Sumatra. Yet the peace that followed was contested: "niet zonder verzet te ondervinden, vooral in de beginjaren," the handbook notes, the Dutch had to forge a "hardnekkig toestand van orde en rust" — a stubborn condition of order and quiet. The over-harsh policies of van Daalen's subsequent governorship ([[1905]]–[[1908]]) would provoke the [[1907–1908 Aceh resistance|1907–1908 flare-up of resistance]] that his successor [[H. N. A. Swart|Swart]] had to quell by conciliation.

## Account from *Dispereert Niet Vol. XI* (1946) — Van Daalen's Gajo Expedition

**Van Daalen's Gajo Expedition** was a major military campaign in [[1904]] that brought the Gajo (Gayo) and Alas highlands of central **[[Sumatra]]** under Dutch control. The expedition, commanded by **Van Daalen**, is considered one of the most impressive military operations of the **[[Aceh War]]**, involving a small force crossing extremely difficult terrain and defeating a series of fortified positions.

### Context

By [[1904]], the lowlands and coastal regions of **[[Aceh]]** were largely under Dutch control, but the highland regions of Gajo and Alas—a territory larger than the Netherlands—remained beyond effective Dutch authority. The region was virtually unknown to Europeans, protected by high mountains and nearly impenetrable forests, with only a few passes guarded by fortified *bentengs*.

### The Expedition

Van Daalen departed in **[[February]] [[1904]]** with a force of approximately 200 *marechaussees*. His objective was to traverse the unknown territory and demonstrate Dutch power in the highlands.

The expedition faced extreme conditions:
- **Difficult terrain**: The force crossed mountain passes comparable to the Alps
- **Fortified positions**: Each pass was defended by *bentengs* with stone walls, bamboo *doerihagen* (spiked barriers), and thousands of defenders
- **Hostile populations**: The Gajo people, previously untouched by Dutch authority, fought fiercely against the incursion
- **Supply shortages**: The expedition was often close to starvation, at one point surviving on a captured python

### The Campaign

Van Daalen's tactics were the same as those used in the lowlands: relentless pursuit of the enemy using small, mobile units. The *marechaussees* attacked *benteng* after *benteng*, suffering heavy losses but continuing to advance.

### Duration and Outcome

The expedition lasted **163 days**. Van Daalen reached **[[Tarotoeng]]** in the **[[Tapanoeli]]** residency on **[[18 June]] [[1904]]**. The campaign established Dutch control over the Gajo and Alas highlands, completing the pacification of Aceh.

### Significance

The Gajo expedition demonstrated the effectiveness of Dutch *marechaussee* tactics in the most demanding conditions. It also brought an end to the Aceh War's highland phase and left the Acehnese resistance with no remaining major strongholds.

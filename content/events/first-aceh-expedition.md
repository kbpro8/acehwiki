**First Aceh Expedition** was the initial Dutch military invasion of the Acehnese Sultanate in [[1873]], marking the outbreak of the long and bloody Aceh War.

## The Campaign
Commanded initially by General [[J.H.R. Köhler]], the expedition began with the [[Landing on Atjeh's Coasts]] on [[8 April]] [[1873]]. The forces immediately pushed towards the [[Kraton of Atjeh]], resulting in the [[First Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[10 April]] [[1873]] and the [[Second Conquest of the Great Missigit]] on [[14 April]] [[1873]]. The expedition was plagued by underestimation of the Acehnese defenses, heavy casualties, and the death of General Köhler. The Dutch eventually withdrew later in April, concluding the expedition in failure and setting the stage for a much larger second invasion later that year.

## Nineteenth-Century Historiography & Campaign Analysis

## Background and the Declaration of War

Following the Anglo-Dutch Sumatra Treaty of [[1871]] and parliamentary discussions in The Hague ([[Parliamentary Note on Relations with Aceh 1872-1873]]), tensions escalated between [[Batavia]] and the royal court at [[Kraton of Aceh]]. In early [[1873]], reports that Acehnese diplomats were purchasing 15,000 rifles in [[Penang]] prompted Minister of Colonies [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte]] and Governor-General to demand immediate capitulation. 

On [[22 March]] [[1873]], Commander-in-Chief General [[J. H. R. Köhler]] issued the [[Proclamation of 22 March 1873]], declaring war against the [[Aceh Sultanate]].

## Narrative of the Invasion and Landings

The expeditionary fleet arrived off the northern coast of Sumatra on [[5 April]] [[1873]]. On morning of [[8 April]] [[1873]], Dutch naval guns bombarded Acehnese redoubts, and infantry battalions debarked under heavy surf at [[Meuraxa]]. Acehnese defenders, led by commanders from the coastal mukims, resisted fiercely at Coastal Fortification No. 2 ([[Coastal Fortifications in Aceh]]), inflicting heavy casualties before retreating toward the capital.

As General [[G. G. Booms]] later documented in his [[1875]] treatise *Over de eerste expeditie tegen Atjeh*, the Dutch army advanced into an unknown landscape. Lacking a permanent [[General Staff of the KNIL]] and having rejected General [[T. H. Kroesen]]'s [[1871]] reconnaissance proposal, officers possessed no maps of [[Kraton of Aceh]] or the surrounding river networks.

## Battle of the Mosque and Köhler's Death

On [[10 April]] [[1873]], Dutch columns assaulted the fortified enclosure of the [[Baiturrahman Grand Mosque]], believing it to be a key outwork of the sultan's palace. After intense hand-to-hand combat, troops captured the mosque, but Acehnese counterattacks soon forced an evacuation. 

On morning of [[14 April]] [[1873]], General [[J. H. R. Köhler]] directed a second storming of the mosque. As he stood inspecting the captured courtyard, an Acehnese marksman shot him through the heart, killing him instantly.

## The Withdrawal After 16 April

Command devolved upon Colonel [[E. C. van Daalen]], who was promoted to general. On [[16 April]] [[1873]], [[E. C. van Daalen]] surveyed his exhausted, demoralized army. With hospital tents overflowing, ammunition running low, and the dreaded [[Southwest Monsoon in Aceh Operations]] approaching, van Daalen recognized that remaining ashore risked total annihilation.

Following consultations with his officers, [[E. C. van Daalen]] ordered the evacuation of all troops back to the warships. The withdrawal of the [[First Aceh Expedition]] stunned Europe and [[Batavia]], sparking the [[Jonckbloet Note of 10 May 1873]] and leading directly to the appointment of the [[Commission of Inquiry into the First Aceh Expedition]].

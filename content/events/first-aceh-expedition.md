The **First Aceh Expedition** was a major colonial military campaign launched by the Kingdom of the Netherlands in [[April]] [[1873]] to invade and subjugate the [[Sultanate of Aceh]], marking the official opening of the thirty-year Aceh War.

## Background and Diplomatic Breakdown

In early [[1873]], tension escalated between the Netherlands East Indies government and [[Sultan Alauddin Mahmud Syah IX]]. Following the [[Treaty of Sumatra]] in [[1871]], the Netherlands felt empowered to assert control over northern Sumatra. However, confidential reports from Dutch Consul-General W. Read in Singapore revealed that Acehnese harbourmaster [[Panglima Tiban Mohammad]] had held covert discussions with the American and Italian consuls to request military protection.

Reacting swiftly to prevent foreign intervention, Governor-General James Loudon dispatched Government Commissioner [[Frederik Nieuwenhuijzen]] to Aceh aboard a warship. When the Acehnese court offered evasive responses to Dutch demands for formal recognition of sovereignty, Nieuwenhuijzen issued an official Declaration of War on [[26 March]] [[1873]].

## Operational Narrative

The expeditionary force was placed under the command of Major-General [[Johan Harmen Rudolf Köhler]], with Colonel [[Eduard Cornelis van Daalen]] serving as second-in-command and infantry chief. The invasion army comprised 116 officers, 3,200 infantrymen, cavalry units, mountain artillery, sappers, and a naval landing division commanded by Captain-Lieutenant [[J.W. Binkes]], supported by 6 steam warships and 11 transport vessels.

On [[8 April]] [[1873]], Dutch troops executed the [[Landing at Pante Cermin]]. Acehnese fighters mounted fierce resistance at [[Kota Mogat]], repulsing an initial infantry assault before naval shelling forced their retreat on [[9 April]].

On [[10 April]] [[1873]], Major-General Köhler ordered an advance toward the capital, leading to the [[First Capture of Mesjid Raya]]. Acehnese musket fire and heavy resistance at the [[Mesjid Raya Aceh]] stalled the column. Mortar fire set the wooden mosque roof on fire, creating intense heat that forced both sides to evacuate the ruins.

On [[14 April]] [[1873]], Dutch columns executed the [[Second Capture of Mesjid Raya]], successfully climbing the masonry walls with storming ladders. Shortly after 9:00 AM, while Major-General Köhler was standing near the eastern wall inspecting defensive positions, an Acehnese sniper shot him through the chest. Köhler died within minutes, causing panic and disorganization.

Colonel Van Daalen assumed command and launched a direct [[Attack on the Kraton]] on [[16 April]] [[1873]]. Acehnese warriors defended the royal palace gates with devastating volleys. Lacking scaling ladders and heavy siege guns, the Dutch assault failed, suffering 18 killed and 112 wounded within thirty minutes.

## Retreat and Aftermath

Faced with mounting casualties, exhaustion, and impending monsoonal storms, Colonel Van Daalen convened a council of war. On [[17 April]] [[1873]], the colonial army withdrew to the beachhead bivouac. Re-embarkation commenced on [[25 April]], and the fleet departed Aceh on [[29 April]] [[1873]], returning to Batavia on [[10 May]] [[1873]].

The defeat of the First Aceh Expedition shattered the myth of European military invincibility in Southeast Asia, inspiring Acehnese patriotic pride and compelling the Netherlands to organize the far larger [[Second Aceh Expedition]].

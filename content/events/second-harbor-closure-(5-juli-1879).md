The **second harbor closure of [[5 July]] [[1879]]** — the *tweede sluiting der havens* (second closure of the harbours) ordered by [[Van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] on his *eigen persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* (own personal responsibility) — his own personal responsibility — and announced by telegram to the [[Van Lansberge|Gouverneur-Generaal]] on [[5 July]] [[1879]] — is, in [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s [[1893]] tract *[[De onderwerping van Atjeh (Van Heutsz, 1893)|De onderwerping van Atjeh]]*, the action the author calls "*karakter*": character. The closure was the second of Van der Heijden's two unilateral *haven-sluitingen* (harbour-closures) — the sequel to the [[First harbor closure (1878)|first harbor closure of 1878]], which the Gouverneur-Generaal had revoked by letter of [[7 February]] [[1879]] — and was, in Van Heutsz's reading, "*geen daad van militaire willekeur*" — not an act of military arbitrariness — but "*een krachtige door het gezond verstand geboden dwangmaatregel*" — a forceful coercive measure enjoined by common sense. The closure was reluctantly tolerated by the Gouverneur-Generaal, who wrote — in the qualification Van Heutsz preserves verbatim — that he "*slechts in den maatregel berustte onder uitdrukkelijk voorbehoud, dat daaraan het karakter van een zeer tijdelijken oorlogsmaatregel niet mocht worden ontnomen*".

## The 5 July 1879 telegram

The closure was announced to the [[Van Lansberge|Gouverneur-Generaal]] (Governor-General) by telegram of [[5 July]] [[1879]] — the formal notification by which the General informed Batavia that the havens had been closed again, this time on his own personal responsibility. Van Heutsz reproduces the telegram's substance:

> "hij sloot ze ten tweedemale en gaf bij telegram d.d. 5 Juli 1879 aan den Gouv. Generaal te kennen: «Dat de havens opnieuw door hem waren gesloten, ditmaal op zijne persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid»".

The *eigen persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* formula (own personal responsibility) — the General's own personal responsibility — was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the operational inversion of the Gouverneur-Generaal's objection to the [[First harbor closure (1878)|first harbor closure of 1878]]. The Gouverneur-Generaal had, in the [[7 February]] [[1879]] revocation letter, cited the [[Van Raders|Van Raders of Suriname]] precedent — the deposition of a Dutch colonial governor for an *eigenmachtig* (on-one's-own-authority) act against a foreign ship — as the warning the General should heed. Van der Heijden's response, in the [[5 July]] [[1879]] telegram, was to accept the responsibility the Gouverneur-Generaal had warned him against: the closure would be on his *eigen persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid*, and any consequences — diplomatic, constitutional, or political — would fall on him personally, not on the Regeering (Government).

## The "eigen persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid" formula

The *eigen persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* formula was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the General's constitutional answer to the Gouverneur-Generaal's *eigenmachtig* objection. The closure of [[1878]] had been disallowed because it had been ordered *eigenmachtig* — on the General's own authority, without prior authorisation from Batavia. The closure of [[5 July]] [[1879]] was ordered *op zijne persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* — on his personal responsibility — which was a different category of act: not an unauthorised assumption of the Regeering's authority, but a personal acceptance of the consequences of an act the General judged operationally necessary. The formula thus shifted the constitutional question from the General's authority to the General's responsibility — from *whether he could* to *whether he would answer for it* — and, in Van Heutsz's reading, established the precedent that the *krachtige individualiteit* (forceful individuality) of the *bevelhebber* (commander) was, in the colony's Atjeh circumstances, the only effective instrument of policy.

## Van Heutsz's praise: "Dat was karakter"

Van Heutsz's verdict on the closure is, in the [[1893]] tract, the highest praise he accords any single act of Van der Heijden's governorship:

> "Dat was karakter; terecht was gezegd: de krachtige individualiteit van den bevelhebber gaf den doorslag. Het was geen daad van militaire willekeur, het was een krachtige door het gezond verstand geboden dwangmaatregel, het was een maatregel in de richting, die ons in staat zou gesteld hebben de kroon op het werk te zetten".

The *karakter* verdict (character) — character — is the General's personal quality that, in Van Heutsz's reading, made the closure possible. The phrase "*de krachtige individualiteit van den bevelhebber gaf den doorslag*" — the powerful individuality of the commander made the decision — is the same phrase Van Heutsz would later use, in his [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order of 5 September 1879]] commentary, to describe the General's *krachtige individualiteit* as the operational cause of the [[XXII Moekims campaign (1878)|XXII]] and [[XXVI Moekims campaign (1879)|XXVI Moekims]] victories. The closure is, in Van Heutsz's reading, the political equivalent of those military victories: an act of *karakter* that the Gouverneur-Generaal's *verzoeningspolitiek* (politics of conciliation) could not have produced.

The denial — "*Het was geen daad van militaire willekeur*" — is Van Heutsz's direct response to the Gouverneur-Generaal's *eigenmachtig genomen willekeurig maatregel* objection of [[7 February]] [[1879]]. The closure of [[5 July]] [[1879]] was not, in Van Heutsz's reading, *willekeur* (arbitrariness) — arbitrariness — but "*een krachtige door het gezond verstand geboden dwangmaatregel*" — a forceful coercive measure enjoined by common sense. The closure was "*een maatregel in de richting, die ons in staat zou gesteld hebben de kroon op het werk te zetten*" — a measure in the direction that would have enabled us to crown the work — that is, to complete the conquest of [[Groot-Atjeh]] that the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order of 5 September 1879]] would, two months later, declare accomplished.

## The Governor-General's reluctant tolerance

The closure was reluctantly tolerated by the Gouverneur-Generaal, who wrote — in the qualification Van Heutsz preserves verbatim — that he "*slechts in den maatregel berustte onder uitdrukkelijk voorbehoud, dat daaraan het karakter van een zeer tijdelijken oorlogsmaatregel niet mocht worden ontnomen*":

> "Ware de Generaal v. d. Heijden in deze richting niet nader gedwarsboomd geworden door de onverzettelijkheid van den G. G., die schreef: «Dat hij slechts in den maatregel berustte onder uitdrukkelijk voorbehoud, dat daaraan het karakter van een zeer tijdelijken oorlogsmaatregel niet mocht worden ontnomen»".

The Gouverneur-Generaal's *voorbehoud* (reservation) — reservation — was that the closure must remain a *zeer tijdelijken oorlogsmaatregel* (very temporary war-measure) — a very temporary war-measure — and must not acquire the character of a permanent colonial policy. The reservation was, in Van Heutsz's reading, the Gouverneur-Generaal's attempt to confine the closure to the operational sphere and prevent it from becoming the *stelsel* (system) that the *scheepvaartregeling* (shipping regulation) of [[Scherer]] and [[Laging Tobias]] would later generalise. Van Heutsz's counter-factual — "*Ware de Generaal v. d. Heijden in deze richting niet nader gedwarsboomd geworden*" — is the rhetorical frame within which the entire post-[[1881]] decade is judged: had the General not been thwarted in this direction by the Gouverneur-Generaal's *onverzettelijkheid* (obstinacy), the closure would have been the *kroon op het werk* (crown on the work) — the crown on the work — that the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order of 5 September 1879]] would declare accomplished, and the *scheepvaartregeling* that the General would have prescribed in his *Nota* of [[19 April]] [[1880]] would have been the durable form of the temporary *dwangmaatregel* (coercive measure) the Gouverneur-Generaal had reluctantly tolerated.

## Significance

The second harbor closure of [[5 July]] [[1879]] is, in Van Heutsz's narrative, the act the author calls "*karakter*" — character — and the precedent that established the *eigen persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* formula as the General's constitutional answer to the Gouverneur-Generaal's *eigenmachtig* objection. Its significance in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record lies in four things. First, it was the operational complement of the [[XXVI Moekims campaign (1879)|XXVI Moekims campaign]] — the General's attempt to seal the coast against external supply at the moment of his decisive campaign against the second *Sagi* of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — and the *dwangmaatregel* that would, in Van Heutsz's reading, have enabled the colony to "*de kroon op het werk te zetten*" — to crown the work — that the [[Day-order of 5 September 1879|day-order of 5 September 1879]] would, two months later, declare accomplished. Second, it was the constitutional inversion of the [[First harbor closure (1878)|first harbor closure of 1878]] — the General's *eigen persoonlijke verantwoordelijkheid* answer to the Gouverneur-Generaal's *eigenmachtig* objection, and the precedent that established the *krachtige individualiteit* of the *bevelhebber* as the colony's only effective instrument of policy. Third, it was the act Van Heutsz names "*karakter*" — character — and the act he describes as "*geen daad van militaire willekeur*" but "*een krachtige door het gezond verstand geboden dwangmaatregel*": the direct response to the Gouverneur-Generaal's *eigenmachtig genomen willekeurig maatregel* objection of [[7 February]] [[1879]]. Fourth, it was the act the Gouverneur-Generaal reluctantly tolerated under the *voorbehoud* that it "*slechts in den maatregel berustte onder uitdrukkelijk voorbehoud, dat daaraan het karakter van een zeer tijdelijken oorlogsmaatregel niet mocht worden ontnomen*" — the qualification that confined the closure to the operational sphere and prevented it from becoming the *stelsel* that the [[Scheepvaartregeling|scheepvaartregeling]] of [[Scherer]] and [[Laging Tobias]] would later generalise.

**The Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)** was the series of administrative meetings and consultations conducted across Sumatra by Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff]] and Hoofdambtenaar [[J. de Groot]] to discuss the further preparation of a reform of the governance system (*hervorming van het bestuurswezen*) in the Netherlands East Indies, with specific focus on Sumatra's Buitenbezittingen (Outer Possessions). The formal title of the published proceedings was *Notulen der bestuursvergaderingen ter bespreking van de verdere voorbereiding eener hervorming van het bestuurswezen in Nederlandsch-Indië* (Minutes of the Administrative Meetings for the Discussion of the Further Preparation of a Reform of the Governance System in the Netherlands East Indies), printed by the Landsdrukkerij (State Printing Office) in Batavia in 1914. The document is catalogued as DLP 57 in the Dutch colonial records.

## Background

The reform of the colonial governance system had been under discussion in the Netherlands East Indies for several years prior to 1913. The central question was how to implement *bestuursdecentralisatie* (administrative decentralization) in the Buitenbezittingen—the territories outside Java and Madura—by granting greater autonomy and self-governance to local and regional bodies. The existing system, in which the central government in Batavia exercised tight control over all administrative matters, was widely regarded as inefficient and unsuited to the diverse conditions across the archipelago. Regeeringscommissaris De Graaff was appointed to lead a comprehensive consultation process with colonial officials across the Buitenbezittingen to gather input and build consensus for the proposed reforms.

## Meetings in Sumatra

The consultation process in Sumatra consisted of two rounds of meetings, separated by a hiatus of several months. The first round was held in August 1913 at three locations in southern and central Sumatra: Palembang (12-13 August, four sessions), Benkoelen (15-16 August, three sessions), and Padang (19-20 August, three sessions). The second round was held in January 1914 at two locations in northern Sumatra: Medan (13-15 January, four sessions) and [[Koeta-Radja]] (19-20 January, two sessions). The progression from south to north, and from general to specific, was deliberate: the earlier meetings established principles and identified concerns, while the later meetings applied those principles to the concrete administrative divisions of the proposed new gewesten (regions), including the critical question of how Atjeh would be incorporated.

In addition to the oral consultations, written submissions (*nota* and *missive*) were received from various Residents and former officials, including Oud-Resident Heyting (nota dated Tjiandjoer, 18 October 1913), the Assistent-Resident van Billiton (missive dated 25 January 1914), and the Residents of the Lampongsche Districten, Djambi, Tapanoeli, Riouw en Onderhoorigheden, and Banka en Onderhoorigheden.

## Key Conclusions for Atjeh

For the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]], the reform process produced several critical conclusions that distinguished Atjeh from all other Buitenbezittingen. At the [[Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen (19-20 January 1914)]], Gouverneur [[H. N. N. A. Swart]] declared that the standard form of local *zelfbestuur* (self-governance through autonomous bodies) could NOT be applied to Atjeh because only [[Groot-Atjeh]] and part of [[Singkel]] were *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory), while the remainder consisted of Inlandsche Zelfbesturen (Native Self-Governments). De Graaff concluded that only [[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]] (Regional Self-Governance) was feasible for Atjeh. The question of training Inlandsche ambtenaren (Native officials) for Atjeh was explicitly excluded from discussion due to existing *bestuursverhoudingen* (governance relationships). Atjeh was to be absorbed into the new Gouvernement [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]] with its capital at Medan, and its territory divided into several *afdeelingen* (administrative divisions).

## Proposed Administrative Division

The proposed division of the new Noord-Sumatra gewest, as discussed at the Medan and Koeta-Radja meetings, included seven *afdeelingen*, three of which were specifically Atjeh-based: (1) Deli benevens de Karo- en de Alaslanden (pop. 370,000), including the [[Alaslanden]] transferred from Atjeh; (2) Asahan met Simeloengoen (pop. 180,000); (3) Langkat met de Oostkust van Atjeh (pop. 230,000), incorporating [[Tamiang]] and [[Langsa]]; (4) Groot-Atjeh benevens de Westkust van Atjeh (pop. 210,000); (5) Noord- en Oostkust van Atjeh behalve Tamiang (pop. 480,000); (6) Bataklanden (pop. 350,000); and (7) Sibolga (pop. 260,000). The addition of Atjeh areas to the Deli and Langkat afdeelingen was left to the judgment of the Gouverneur van Atjeh.

## Significance

The Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra represents a pivotal moment in the colonial governance of Atjeh and the broader Netherlands East Indies. The recognition that Atjeh's unique dual structure—combining directly ruled territory with Inlandsche Zelfbesturen—made standard decentralisation inapplicable was a candid acknowledgment of the limits of uniform colonial policy. The decision to dissolve the Gouvernement Atjeh and absorb it into a larger Noord-Sumatra region marked the beginning of the end of Atjeh's exceptional status as a separately governed military territory, a process that would take decades to fully implement. The detailed discussions of the *sagi*-*peleebalangschap*-*moekimschap* hierarchy and the question of hereditary versus merit-based head appointments provide invaluable documentation of the indigenous administrative structures in Groot-Atjeh at a critical transitional moment in the territory's history.

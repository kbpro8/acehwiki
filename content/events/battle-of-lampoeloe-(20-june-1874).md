**The Battle of Lampoeloe (20 June 1874)** was the first Dutch offensive operation since the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]] four months earlier, launched by [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]] against the Atjehnese positions at [[Lampoeloe]] on the northern edge of the old bivouac, just one day after a costly reconnaissance in which [[Luitenant Sloet van Zwanenburg|luitenant Sloet van Zwanenburg]] was mortally wounded. Borel hails the action as "den eersten stap op een nieuwen weg" — the first step on a new road.

## Background: the failed reconnaissance of 19 June

On [[19 June]] [[1874]] a column of 150 bayonets with an artillery section had marched from [[Kota Radja|K. Radja]] to clear the left bank north of [[Kampong Djawa]], while Sloet van Zwanenburg led a patrol from [[Penajoeng]] on the right bank. The day was unfavourable. The Atjehneezen held Lampoeloe in force. Sloet was discovered in his ravine, shot up badly, and forced to retire *in aller ijl* — he died of his wounds days later. The left-bank column could find no position to enfilade and returned empty-handed. The same day the Atjehneezen attacked all along the line at K. Radja and [[Pakan Atjeh]].

## The attack

At dawn on [[20 June]] [[1874]] the enemy shelled K. Radja from the east with unusual violence. The whole garrison stood to arms. Salvoes of [[Beaumont-geweer|Beaumont]] rifles were needed at [[Kota Podjoet|K. Podjoct]] and Pakan Atjeh to drive back attackers pressing the parapet. [[Majoor Scharp|Major Scharp]] then marched out with a half-battalion against Lampoeloe. The enemy was driven from his positions, and a half-built *benteng* (fortification) by the river was captured. The cost was 34 Dutch dead and wounded.

## Borel's verdict

For four months after Ketapang Doewa the bezettingskorps had been nailed to its works by sickness, the broken surf, and the [[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]] inherited from van Swieten. Lampoeloe showed that the line could be carried forward again, and that the Atjehneezen, when met by an organised bayonet attack, would give way. It was followed within eight days by the [[Battle of Garouw (28 June 1874)|battle of Garouw]] on [[28 June]] [[1874]], which cleared the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] below Penajoeng and restored Dutch command of the river — the first permanently held Dutch advance beyond the Kraton since its [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall]].

The **unrest at [[Linggö]]** in [[1916]] was a threatened outbreak of resistance in the [[Dösön-vallei]] of the [[Meergebied]] — formerly a very turbulent district — set in motion by a woman *[[teungkoe]]* whom the population regarded as very holy, and suppressed within months with the co-operation of the population itself.

## Course of events

There a female Teungkoe, considered by the population extremely holy, succeeded in bringing some twelve *gampong* people to withdraw into the mountains, where they joined the still-hostile [[Penghoeloe Möngkör]].

Naturally they then committed an attack on a marching bivouac, which however — though at the cost of three wounded soldiers — did not give way and inflicted on the attackers three dead and one severely wounded.

Then it was soon "*mèl sadja*" again: two more encounters with patrols and two more dead, then eight came in submission, and in September the population itself laid low Penghoeloe Möngkör.

The female saint withdrew for safety's sake to the [[Gajò Loeös]], but found that formerly so safe refuge for *[[djahat]]s* likewise changed: two of her followers were killed by the population and she herself was taken prisoner with the remainder of her convinced adherents.

## Significance

The Linggö affair is one of du Croo's examples of the pattern of the outbreaks of [[1913]]–[[1918]]: brief, local, quickly suppressed, and above all suppressed with the active help of a population that no longer had any interest in the *bendewezen*. That even the [[Gajò Loeös]] — once the standing refuge of the irreconcilables of the coast — had become closed to a fugitive holy woman measured how far the pacification had gone by [[1916]].

A similar movement was set going in December [[1917]] at [[Serbödjadi]] by [[Penghoeloe Koeala]], and ran a comparable course.

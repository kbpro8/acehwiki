**Londensch tractaat van 1824** (1824) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. Dutch-English relations over the East, due to difficulties implementing the 1814 treaty, were re-regulated by the London Treaty of 1824. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to [[1824]].

## Account

Dutch-English relations over the East, due to difficulties implementing the [[1814]] treaty, were re-regulated by the [[London]] Treaty of [[1824]]. [[Leyden (Leiden)|Holland]] regained supremacy over the whole island of Sumatra, except Atjeh. Dutch negotiators had to declare they would: (1) follow a line so the foreign world would not suffer from the independent native state on Sumatra's northern tip (e.g., by piracy, molest); (2) not exercise political power over Atjeh. If an Englishman was robbed in Atjeh and got no satisfaction from [[Sultan of Aceh|the Sultan]], [[Leyden (Leiden)|Holland]] had to ensure satisfaction - even organizing a police service was forbidden because it would undermine Atjeh's political independence. The relationship became untenable: border trespassing, mutual trade hindrance, plunder of foreign ships in Atjehse territory.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

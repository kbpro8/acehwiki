**Mendez Pinto's mission to Sumatra's west coast in [[1539]]** was the first of the two Sumatran missions that [[Pero de Faria|Pero de Faria]], the new Portuguese governor of [[Malacca|Malacca]], entrusted to the young [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] on his arrival in [[1539]] — a mission to the "king of [[Batu|Batu]]", the islands off [[Pasaman|Ophir]] long identified with the gold of Solomon's temple, who had appealed to Faria for aid against [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The mission produced the European record of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s pressure on the Sumatran west coast, and was the immediate prelude to Pinto's [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|second Sumatran mission to Aroe]] of October [[1539]].

## The appeal of the king of Batu

On [[Pero de Faria|Faria]]'s arrival at [[Malacca|Malacca]] in [[1539]], [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] records, an embassy came from the "king of [[Batu|Batu]]" — the islands off [[Pasaman|Ophir]] — offering to recognise the King of Portugal as suzerain in exchange for protection against Atjeh and free trade for his people. The gift was accompanied by [[kapoer baroer|kamfer]] and [[kajoe garoe|kajoe garoe]] (eaglewood). "For the sultan of Atjeh", Pinto notes, "his transition to Islam was a welcome motive to throw himself on his neighbours right and left, in order to bring them by fire and sword to submission and the new faith". Faria accepted the request, but had insufficient armed force to send; he supplied munitions and the young Pinto, and added a *cargasoen* for trade. The ship was put under a Malay Muslim of Malacca.

## The voyage to the west coast

[[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] sailed across the [[Straits of Malacca|Strait of Malacca]] to a roadstead he calls *Surotilau* (which [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] decodes as *Soera-sakti-lawoe-kir*, a *bedekte taal* form for the roadstead of [[Malajoer|Malajoer]] in [[Aroe]]). Northward up the Sumatran coast, past [[Pedir|Pedir]], he came to a place he calls *Minhatoley* — decoded as *mina-ng-k-a da-toe Olèh-leh*, the territory of the *dotoe* (clan-chief) of [[Olèh-leh|Olèh-leh]], an Atjeh-chief marking the start of the Suratte-passage around the corner of Sumatra. The coast was heavily forested, with apes, pythons, and crocodiles that "dragged men from the prauwen and devoured them", and the pilot's joke *eaquesseitan* — *kaki sètan*, "devilish ancestors" — was a riddle on the *kalang* (flying-foxes) of the coast.

## The *bandahara* and the march against Atjeh

Brought before the *bandahara* — the "governor of the realm" — [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] delivered the governor of Malacca's letter and gifts, on the pretext of scouting how the city of Atjeh might best be damaged. The *bandahara* assembled 15,000 men with 40 elephants and twelve pieces of light artillery — three of them, Pinto says, bearing the arms of France taken from a French ship captured in [[1526]] — at *Turban* (decoded as *Batoe-poer-wa-boewana*), for a march against the Atjehers. The expedition had some early success, but on the arrival of Atjehnese reinforcements the *Panaju* forces withdrew. De Roo de la Faille notes, in this connection, that three French ships had left Dieppe in [[1527]] for the Sumatran pepper trade and reached [[Tikoe|Tikoe]], where they were wrecked and plundered — the *ketawan karang* — which may explain how captured French arms came to be in Sumatran hands by the time of Pinto's mission.

## The king's understanding

"The king", Pinto records, "had in the meantime understood that from military aid against Atjeh nothing was to be expected; and yet the danger of Atjeh for Malaka was as much to be feared as he had experienced: three of his sons were already dead, his land destroyed". With a wide detour on the return — at [[Perlis|Perlis]] on [[Malacca|Malacca]] the *nakoda* who had accompanied Pinto was cruelly put to death for his free speech about the radja's cruelties — the mission to the gold-land ended.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Pinto's first Sumatran mission is the immediate prelude to the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)]] — the mission that fixed the [[Acheh|Atjehnese sultanate]]'s pressure on the Sumatran west coast in European record, and that brought Pinto into the theatre from which he would sail, in October [[1539]], to [[Aroe|Aru]]. Its record of the *bandahara* and of the abortive march against the Atjehers is the only European account of the Sumatran west-coast response to Atjehnese expansion in this period.

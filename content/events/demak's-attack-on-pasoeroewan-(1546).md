**Demak's attack on Pasoeroewan in [[1546]]** was the [[Demak|Sultanate of Demak]]'s final military blow against the Hindu-Javanese kingdom of [[Pasuruan|Pasoeroewan]] in East Java — the campaign that, in [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]]'s reading, is the historical kernel of the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]]'s account of the death of the king of Demak and the building of the [[Demak|Demak mosque]], and that fixed the year of the mosque's completion (traditionally [[1468]] Saka, but which De Roo de la Faille reads as [[1546]] A.D.). The campaign is a key Javanese event in the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]–[[Acheh|Atjeh]]–Java triangle of the early sixteenth century, and the one by which the [[Faletahan|Faletahan/Sunan Gunung Jati]] line and the [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes/Raden Sahid]] line converged on the final Islamic conquest of Hindu Java.

## The campaign

In [[1545]] [[Mendez Pinto|Pinto]] arrived at [[Banten|Bantam]] on a junk from [[Goa|Goa]], bound for trade with [[China|China]]. There an old woman, **Njahi Pombaja**, came aboard as envoy of the king of [[Demak|Demak]] to summon the king of [[Sunda Kelapa|Soenda Tagaril]] (i.e. [[Faletahan|Sunan Gunung Jati]]) to provide troops for the campaign against Hindu [[Pasuruan|Pasoeroewan]]. Pinto and a number of other Portuguese were persuaded to join. The expedition left [[Banten|Bantam]] on [[5 January]] [[1546|1540]] (Pinto's date), assembled at [[Japara|Japara]] under the *admiralschap* of the "regent of [[Panarukan|Panaroekan]]", and on [[11 February]] [[1546]] arrived before [[Pasuruan|Pasoeroewan]] — which Pinto calls *Hikan dureh*, a *bedekte taal* form for *hin-doe rèh*, "the land under Hindu rule".

The city was invested but defended itself stoutly, and many fell on both sides. The king of Demak, in a war-council, asked his *panakawan* (squire) for a *sirih*-prize; the squire — the son of the regent of [[Surabaya|Soerabaja]], deep in deliberation — did not hear; one of the chiefs pulled him by his state-*dodot* and reminded him; the squire, kneeling, offered the king his golden *sirih*-box; the king took a *sirih*-leaf and, mildly, tapped the squire on the head, asking: "are you deaf, that you do not hear me?". That tap — for a Balinese of high house, an untheiling of the godhead whose seat is the head — drove the squire to madness; he sprang up and stabbed the king to death with a *beladau* (dagger). The king's last words, Pinto records, were "I am dead".

## The empaling and the aftermath

The squire's family — the substantial house of [[Surabaya|Soerabaja]] — was held collectively responsible: sixty-two persons, old and young, of either sex, were empaled (*soela*). "This was the cause, later, of certain troubles, which took place in the land of Java and in the isles of [[Bali|Bali]], [[Timor|Timor]], [[Madura|Madura]]". The king's corpse, on the advice of the Portuguese, was placed in a chest filled with lime and camphor, and sent by a junk laden with earth to [[Demak|Demak]]. The army withdrew in full retreat. An heir was lacking; an eight-man council of electors was appointed; disturbances broke out; **Djaka Tingkir** (i.e. [[Raden Sahid|the later Sultan of Padjang]]) — "Le Roy de Panaruca, Prince de Balambuan et Amiral de la flotte" — had some eighty rioters hanged, and the city-governor **Kéjan-sedahan** ("*Kéjan-sedahan, doek dja-di tjara ke-prabon*") had forty mutinous sailors killed, but died himself in the tumult. An agreement was finally reached to fetch the successor from [[Sidayu|Sidajoe]].

## Decoding the *bedekte taal*

[[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] reads the entire campaign as a *bedekte taal* record of the final Islamic conquest of Hindu Java. The squire's *tap on the head* is the untheiling of the divine head — the godhead that the Balinese high house carries in the head; the empaling of the sixty-two is the extirpation of the *wong agung* (great men) of the old order; and the king's burial in lime and camphor — the same materials as the [[kapoer|kapoer]] of the [[Banten|Bantam]] mosques — is the allegorical sign of the Islamic order's closing of the Hindu age. The successor from [[Sidayu|Sidajoe]] De Roo de la Faille identifies with [[Raden Sahid|the later Sultan of Padjang]], i.e. with [[Soenan Kalidjaga|the Soenan Kalidjaga line]] that the [[Babad Tanah Djawi|Babad Tanah Djawi]] records as the builder of the [[Demak|Demak mosque]].

## Significance

For AcehWiki, Demak's attack on [[Pasuruan|Pasoeroewan]] in [[1546]] is the Javanese counterpart of the [[Atjeh-Aroe War (1539)|Atjehnese war on Aroe]] of [[1539]] — the closing event of the Hindu-Javanese age in Java, as the Atjeh-Aru war was the closing event of the Hindu-Javanese age on the Sumatran east coast. The convergence of the [[Faletahan|Faletahan/Sunan Gunung Jati]] and [[Pati Oenoes|Pati Oenoes/Raden Sahid]] lines on the campaign — both figures identified by [[P. de Roo de la Faille|De Roo de la Faille]] with the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]]-[[Acheh|Atjeh]] religious tradition — also marks the campaign as the moment at which the [[Pasej-Samodra|Pasej]] Islamic tradition completed its migration into the Javanese *wali* world.

**The revolt in Great Atjèh** was the principal armed rising of the pre-invasion insurgent movement, fought in the subdistrict of [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] — the mountain country of the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]] *sagi* under [[Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali|Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali]] — in two waves of sabotage beginning on the night of [[19 February|19]]–[[20 February]] [[1942]] and culminating, after a brief lull, in a second wave from the night of [[7 March|7]]–[[8 March]] [[1942]] until the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion]] of [[12 March]] [[1942]]. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* (Chapter III § 1) treats it as the central episode of the pre-invasion fifth-column movement: a "large-scale resistance action" centred on [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]] and aimed at severing the main road, railway, and telephone connections between [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]] and [[Medan|Medan]].

## The first wave (19–24 February 1942)

"The first case of sabotage occurred on the night of 19 to 20 February 1942, when in this area the telephone line was cut at only a single place, so that one could still think of mere mischief." On the night of [[23 February|23]]–[[24 February]] [[1942]] "the sabotage reached its peak. The railway line was broken in two places, worst at [[Indrapoeri|Indrapoeri]], where moreover switches were put out of order, a steam roller was placed across the road, the workshop of the [[Atjèh-tram|Atjèh-tram]] was plundered, and a small warehouse was burned." The *controleur* of Seulimeum, [[J. C. Tiggelman|Mr. J. C. Tiggelman]], was "lured outside at night by the sounding of the alarm and murdered in a beastly manner," while the Atjèhnese manager of the auxiliary post office, [[Tjoet Amat|Tjoet Amat]], "made off with the cash register (containing an amount of around 5,000 guilders)." The following morning [[Count U. Bernstorff Von Sperling|Count Von Sperling]], head of operations of the Atjèh state railways, was killed in an encounter at [[Keumiróë|Keumiróë]] in the *moekim* Lam Leu'Ot.

## Leadership and instigation

Piekaar identifies the *sagi*-head [[Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali|Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Ali]] as the principal leader. "After the death of the old Teukoe Panglima Pólém Moehamad Daoed," his son "openly encouraged" the politicised young religious elements "and even took a direct part in their activities." The religious leader [[Teungkoe Sjech Ibrahim|Teungkoe Sjech Ibrahim]] of [[Meintasië'|Meintasië']], "a peculiar figure among Seulimeum's oelama's," returned from Singapore in early [[1942]] "as an honored and respected man, behind whom only a small group of initiates knew the 5th colonne." Second lieutenant [[L. C. G. F. Onvlee|Onvlee]] of the marechaussee was sent to Seulimeum, and the commander of the marechaussee corps, [[W. F. Palmer van den Broek|Major W. F. Palmer van den Broek]], was placed in charge of both the subdistrict and the military action.

## The coup d'état of 11 March 1942

On [[11 March]] [[1942]], with the second sabotage wave in full spate, [[Colonel Gosenson|Colonel Gosenson]] carried out his celebrated "coup d'état" in the [[Heutsz-room|Heutsz-room]] of the Residency: eight Great-Atjèhnese chiefs — including [[Teukoe Oemar Djohan Pahlawan|Teukoe Oemar Djohan Pahlawan]], [[Teukoe Radja Djoema'at|Teukoe Radja Djoema'at]] of [[Lhóng|Lhóng]], and [[Teukoe Ali Basjah|Teukoe Ali Basjah]] of [[Peukan Bada|Peukan Bada]] — were arrested and shipped aboard the *Sabangbaai* to [[Sigli|Sigli]]. "The following night the Japanese landings came as a welcome relief."

## Significance

The revolt in Great Atjèh was the only one of the 1942 risings to assume "the appearance of a popular movement, whereby the parallel with the [[Atjèh War|Atjèh War]] was complete." Its suppression by Colonel Gosenson, on the very eve of the invasion, deprived Koetaradja of its Indonesian administrative organisation and "removed the strongest brake on a further expansion of the resistance movement."

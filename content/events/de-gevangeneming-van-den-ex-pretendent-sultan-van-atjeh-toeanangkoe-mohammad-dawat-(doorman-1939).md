**De gevangeneming van den ex-pretendent Sultan van Atjeh, Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat, na 32 jaar ballingschap den 7en Febr. 1939 overleden te Mr.-Cornelis** — *"The capture of the ex-pretender Sultan of Atjeh, Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat, who after 32 years of exile died at Meester-Cornelis on 7 February 1939"* — is an article by **[[R. G. Doorman|R. G. Doorman]]**, published in [[1939]] in *De Indische Gids* (The Indies Guide), vol. 61, p. 308 *e.v.*, recording the capture and subsequent exile of the Atjehnese pretender Sultan [[Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat|Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]] — also spelled *Tocangkoe Mohammad Dawat* — and noting his death at [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester-Cornelis]] (now [[Jatinegara|Jatinegara]], a suburb of [[Batavia|Batavia]]) on [[7 February]] [[1939]] after thirty-two years of banishment. The article is recorded as No. 563 in [[H. van Meurs|Van Meurs]]'s *[[Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939 (Van Meurs, 1940)|Literatuur-Overzicht over het jaar 1939]]* (The Hague, [[1940]]), in the Sumatra chapter.

## Subject

The article's subject, as recorded by Van Meurs, is the capture (*gevangeneming*) of the ex-pretender Sultan of Atjeh — *ex-pretendent Sultan van Atjeh* in Van Meurs's spelling, *ex-pretendent* in the OCR — bearing the title **[[Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat|Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]]** (also *Tocangkoe Mohammad Dawat*), and the circumstance of his death in exile. The title *Toeanangkoe* (or *Tocangkoe*) is a rendering of the Acehnese/Malay *Tuanaku* — "our lord" — a royal title borne by Atjehnese princes; *Mohammad Dawat* is the personal name, known elsewhere in the corpus in the forms [[muhammad-dawot-shah|Muhammad Dawot Shah]], [[teuku-muda-dawot|Teuku Muda Dawot]], and [[tuanku-muhamat-dawot|Tuanku Muhamat Dawot]]. The designation *ex-pretendent Sultan* indicates that Mohammad Dawat had been, at some point before his capture, put forward as a claimant to the Atjehnese sultanate — a pretender — and that his capture removed him from the political arena of Atjeh.

The article notes that Mohammad Dawat died at [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester-Cornelis]] — the suburb of [[Batavia|Batavia]] now known as [[Jatinegara|Jatinegara]] — on [[7 February]] [[1939]], after thirty-two years of *ballingschap* (banishment, exile). The figure of thirty-two years places the beginning of his exile in [[1907]] — the year of the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] and of the [[1907-1908-aceh-resistance|1907–1908 flare-up of resistance]] under [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|van Daalen]]'s governorship — suggesting that the pretender's capture and banishment were part of the Dutch response to the late phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and to the residual royalist politics of the sultanate. His death in exile, in [[1939]], marks the close of the line of Atjehnese pretender-sultans in the colonial period.

## Author and venue

[[R. G. Doorman|Doorman]] published the article in *De Indische Gids*, vol. 61, p. 308 *e.v.* — one of the principal monthly review journals of the Netherlands Indies, which carried articles on colonial administration, politics, and current affairs. The journal's audience — colonial officials, planters, merchants, and the interested Dutch public — was precisely the readership to which a notice of the death of the last Atjehnese pretender-sultan would be of interest. The year [[1939]] — the year of Mohammad Dawat's death — was the year of publication, indicating that the article was a prompt obituary notice.

## Place in the 1939 Literatuur-Overzicht

The article is No. 563 in the Sumatra chapter (Hoofdstuk III, Nos. 548–658) of Van Meurs's survey, where it stands among the D-letter entries of the Sumatra chapter, between the [[H. T. Damsté|Damsté]] edition of the [[De legende van de heilige zeven slapers in het Atjehsch (Damsté, 1939)|Seven Sleepers legend]] (No. 560) and the [[J. Alting van Geusau|Geusau]] account of the [[De tocht van Overste van Daalen door de Gajo-, Alas- en Bataklanden (Geusau, 1939)|van Daalen campaign of 1904]] (No. 566). Its placement there marks it as one of the retrospective political-historical accounts of Atjeh published in [[1939]], and the only one to record the death of the last Atjehnese pretender-sultan.

## Significance

For AcehWiki, the article is significant as the [[1939]] record of the death — in exile, at [[Mr.-Cornelis|Meester-Cornelis]], on [[7 February]] [[1939]] — of the last Atjehnese pretender Sultan, [[Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat|Toeanangkoe Mohammad Dawat]], after thirty-two years of banishment dating from [[1907]]. The article furnishes an independent source for the end of the line of Atjehnese pretender-sultans in the colonial period, and it complements the corpus's existing entries on the Atjehnese sultanate and its pretenders — including [[muhammad-dawot-shah|Muhammad Dawot Shah]], [[teuku-muda-dawot|Teuku Muda Dawot]], and [[tuanku-muhamat-dawot|Tuanku Muhamat Dawot]] — and on the late phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and the [[Atjéh-enquête|Atjéh-enquête]] of [[1907]]. The date [[7 February]] [[1939]] is the terminus ad quem of the colonial-period Atjehnese sultanate, and the article is the source for that date in the corpus.

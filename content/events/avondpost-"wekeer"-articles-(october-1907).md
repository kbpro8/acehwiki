**The Avondpost "Wekeer" articles (October 1907)** were a series of articles published in the Dutch newspaper *[[De Avondpost]]* in October [[1907]] under the pseudonym "Wekeer" — a *maréchaussée*-officer from Atjeh — exposing "nodeloze wreedheden" — needless cruelties — committed against the Atjehnese population, "toegelaten zo niet aangemoedigd door [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]" — tolerated, if not encouraged, by [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] in his capacity as governor and military commander of Atjeh. The articles are the proximate cause of the [[Van Daalen's resignation (December 1907)|December 1907 resignation of Van Daalen]] and the [[Van Heutsz's inspection tour of Atjeh (December 1907)|inspection tour of Van Heutsz]] of the same month.

## The "grote klok" of Van Heutsz's warning

In [[1905]] [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — then governor-general — had appointed [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] as *militair commandant en gouverneur van Atjeh*, against the recommendation of the [[Raad van Indië]] (which had favoured Colonel Swart). [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] had said to [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] on the occasion: "Ga je gang, ik vertrouw je. Ge hebt goede en kwade eigenschappen; ik geef u die post en als gij nu maar zorgt, dat er geen gedonder komt dan kunt gij zolang uw gang gaan, maar als het aan de grote klok komt dan voor jou een ander". The "grote klok" — the great bell — of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s warning was, in the event, "Wekeer": the public exposure of atrocities in the press.

## The articles

Under the pseudonym "Wekeer" — a *maréchaussée*-officer from Atjeh — a series of articles appeared in *[[De Avondpost]]* in October [[1907]] detailing needless cruelties allegedly committed against the Atjehnese population and tolerated, if not encouraged, by [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] in his capacity as governor and military commander.

## Ministerial reaction

Minister [[D. Fock|Fock]] — then minister of Colonies — "gaf de kamerleden de raad hun tijd niet te verspillen aan deze artikelen" — advised the chambermembers not to waste their time on these articles. The governor-general, he said, had full confidence in [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]; both he and [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] considered reinforcement of the troop strength in Atjeh unnecessary. The inspection tour that [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] undertook in [[Van Heutsz's inspection tour of Atjeh (December 1907)|December 1907]] — at [[D. Fock|Fock]]'s request — was, however, to reverse both judgements.

## The consequences

The "Wekeer" articles set in train the sequence that ended [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s tenure as governor of Atjeh. In [[December 1907|December]] [[1907]] [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] submitted his resignation. In [[June 1908|June]] [[1908]] the [[Van Heutsz's report on Atjeh (June 1908)|report of the governor-general]] confirmed the fall of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] and installed the new course of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]]: administration through the *hoofden* (chiefs), bound to Dutch authority by "tactvolle omgang" — tactful intercourse.

## The Thomson context

The "Wekeer" exposures came three years after the [[Tweede Kamer]] testimony of [[H. C. Thomson|Thomson]] — the liberal MP and former captain in Atjeh — who had declared in the [[1904]] debate that he had himself, "zonder enige wroeging" — without any scruple — taken part in the burning of entire kampongs, the felling of fruit-trees and the killing of abandoned cattle, and that such practices were the routine consequence of the under-manning of the Dutch forces. [[H. C. Thomson|Thomson]] had warned that "seventy men to patrol the whole of [[Gajoland]]" was "verregaand onvoldoende" — grossly insufficient. The [[Avondpost "Wekeer" articles (October 1907)|"Wekeer" articles]] are, in this sense, the vindication of [[H. C. Thomson|Thomson]]'s warning.

## Significance

The [[Avondpost "Wekeer" articles (October 1907)|"Wekeer" affair]] is the political climax of the post-conquest period of Atjehnese history. The fall of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] — the most consequential single event of the post-[[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|1904 expedition]] period — cleared the way for the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] report of [[1908]] that would set the course of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] for the next decade.

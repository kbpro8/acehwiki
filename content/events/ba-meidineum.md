The **ba meidineum** is the Achehnese ceremony of the second formal visit paid by the mother-in-law to her pregnant daughter, about a month after the earlier visit known as the **vie bu**. As [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] describes it in his account of Achehnese pregnancy customs, "a second visit of the same description and of similar duration is paid by the mother-in-law about a month after the *vie bu*, and is known as *ba meidineum*. On this occasion she brings with her sundry kinds of sweetmeats (*peunajoh*) and fruits." Together with the *vie bu*, the *ba meidineum* marks out the progress of an Achehnese pregnancy by the formal attentions which the family of the husband's mother is bound to pay to the household of her daughter-in-law.

## The *vie bu* and the *ba meidineum*

The two visits are part of the same cycle of duties. The first visit — the *vie bu* — is the more substantial of the two. On that occasion the mother-in-law brings to her daughter-in-law's house "a large pot (*kanèt*) of boiled rice, another of goats'-flesh and a great tray (*dalong*) of fruit, to the total value of about 25 dollars, or else they save themselves the trouble by giving 'the *dangdang* uncooked' (*dangdang meuntah*), i.e. the actual sum of money named above." Those of humbler rank confine their gift to a *kathig* of cooked rice and a bowl of meat, or to *bu kulah* and some additional dishes. The mother-in-law remains for two or three nights under her daughter-in-law's roof on the occasion of the *vie bu*. The *ba meidineum*, paid about a month later, is of similar duration but lighter in its gifts: it consists principally of *peunajoh* (sweetmeats) and fruits.

## The place of the visits in Achehnese pregnancy

The two visits belong to that complex of *pantang* (mysterious rules) by which the pregnant woman in Acheh must regulate her conduct. "In Acheh, no less than in Java, the pregnant woman must pay particular regard to certain mysterious rules [*pantang*], carelessness in the observance of which is supposed to be injurious to her or to her child." The visits of the mother-in-law are, on one side, the formal outward expression of the concern of the husband's family for the wellbeing of mother and child; on the other, they are the means by which that concern is made practically effective, by the provision of meat, fruit, and sweetmeats at the moments when the pregnant woman is most in need of nourishment.

## The pattern of Achehnese life-cycle ceremonies

The *ba meidineum* is one of a long series of ceremonial attentions by which the Achehnese mark the great epochs of life — pregnancy, birth, marriage, and death. As with the other life-cycle ceremonies described by Hurgronje, it is governed by *adat* rather than by Mohammedan law; "it is the *adat* which has so far almost exclusively controlled" such customs. The *ba meidineum*, like the *vie bu*, is not a religious ceremony in the strict sense; no *teungku* is called, no prayer recited; the visit is simply the formal performance of a domestic duty. It is characteristic of the Achehnese treatment of life-cycle events that even the periods of pregnancy should be punctuated by formal visits of this kind — visits which, in their fixed sequence and their prescribed gifts, take on something of the character of a ritual.

**Achehnese Navigation and Fisheries** formed a major component of the Acehnese economy and culture, treated extensively by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] in Chapter II (§§ 5–6, pp. 275–294) of *The Achehnese*.

## The Pawang

The central figure of Acehnese sea-fishing was the **pawang** — the fishing master who possessed special unwritten knowledge of the sea, the fish, and the rules (*pantang*) governing fishing behaviour. The *awa'* (crew members) obeyed the pawang not only because he was their hired commander but also because he alone possessed this special knowledge. The pawang and his crew were too busy with the management of their boat and nets to spare time to bring the fish to market themselves.

## Pantang (Taboos) at Sea

Such unwritten pantangs had a very widespread range. In [[Great Acheh|Acheh]], those at sea must not call a mountain by its proper name (*gunöng*), lest waves as high as mountains should overwhelm their vessel; the euphemism employed was *tanöh manyang* ("high ground"). The elephant (*gajah*) was called by its nickname *pö meurah*. If a fisherman wished to say that something was "ready," he must not use the ordinary word *'lheuëh*" because it also had the meaning of "free" or "loose," and its use might give the imprisoned fish a chance of escaping; accordingly the less dangerous synonym *leungka* was employed.

These taboo rules resembled those found among fishermen on the North Coast of Java, in the Deccan, in Hadramaut, and throughout the Eastern Archipelago.

## Connection to Ancient Galuh

In the neighbourhood of the ancient [[Galuh]] there were places where the Mohammedan confession of faith must not be uttered while fishing, for fear of disturbing the spirits of the ancient heathen kingdom — evidence of the layering of pre-Islamic and Islamic traditions in Acehnese maritime practice.

**The Excursie to Lepong and Lohong** — *excursie naar Lepong en Lohong van 10 tot en met 14 Augustus* [[1896]] — was a five-day Dutch reconnaissance into the [[Lepong]] and [[Lohong]] regions of [[Groot-Atjeh]], undertaken between [[10 August]] [[1896|10]] and [[14 August]] [[1896]]. The excursion was the principal Dutch probe into the Lepong region in the second semester of [[1896]] — a region which had been the centre of [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]]'s operations throughout the first semester — and was undertaken to gather intelligence on the movements and disposition of Umar. The excursion established that *Oemar zich nog wel degelijk in dat landschap ophield* (Oemar was still indeed in that territory), that *hij in onmin met den kedjoeroean was geraakt* (he had fallen out with the *kedjoeroean* (council of headmen) of Lohong), and that *hij zich alleen wat meer binnenwaarts verplaatst* (he had moved only somewhat further inland). The intelligence gathered on the excursion formed the basis of Dutch strategic planning for the next four months.

## The Strategic Context

By August [[1896]], the Dutch had lost track of [[Teuku Umar|Toekoe Oemar]]'s precise location. Umar, who had defected from the Dutch alliance on [[29 March]] [[1896]], had been the object of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] and the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] of [[9 June|9]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] — operations which had destroyed his *stelling* (fortification (stelling)) at Lam-Pisang but had not resulted in his capture. The Dutch knew that Umar was still at large, but his precise location was uncertain. The excursion to Lepong and Lohong of [[10 August|10]]–[[14 August]] [[1896]] was thus both a reconnaissance and a hunt — designed to ascertain Umar's location and to assess the possibility of his capture.

## The Intelligence on Umar

The excursion established that *Toekoe Oemar verlaat Lepong en vestigt zich in Lohong* — Toekoe Oemar leaves Lepong and settles in Lohong. *Uit Lohong terugkeerende lieden, die brieven voor den controleur van Oleh-leh van de kedjoeroean medebrachten, deelden mede, dat Oemar zich nog wel degelijk in dat landschap ophield. Wijl hij in onmin met den kedjoeroean was geraakt, had hij zich alleen wat meer binnenwaarts verplaatst. Schatten en kostbaarheden waren door hem bij verschillende welgestelde lieden in dat landschap verborgen, bevreesd als hij was, ze, bij een nieuw bezoek onzer troepen, niet in veiligheid te kunnen brengen. Zijn eigen bende telde 100 man.* — People returning from Lohong, who brought letters for the controleur of Oleh-leh from the *kedjoeroean*, reported that Oemar was still indeed in that territory. Since he had fallen out with the *kedjoeroean*, he had moved only somewhat further inland. Treasures and valuables had been hidden by him with various well-to-do people in that territory, afraid as he was that, on a new visit by our troops, he would not be able to bring them to safety. His own band numbered 100 men.

The intelligence was significant in three respects. First, it established that Umar's band had been reduced to 100 men — a significant reduction from the larger forces he had commanded in the first semester. Second, it established that Umar had fallen out with the *kedjoeroean* (the headmen) of Lohong — a political deterioration which suggested that Umar's position in the interior was becoming increasingly precarious. Third, it established that Umar was forced to hide his *schatten en kostbaarheden* (treasures and valuables) with various well-to-do people in Lohong — a sign that his freedom of movement was constrained by the Dutch threat. The intelligence thus suggested that Umar was vulnerable, but that his capture would require a more extended operation than the Dutch had yet undertaken.

## The Five-Day Excursion

The excursion was undertaken over five days — from [[10 August]] to [[14 August]] [[1896]] — by a Dutch colonne, which traversed the Lepong and Lohong regions and made contact with the local population. The detailed narrative of the excursion is not separately preserved in the *Besluit* (Decree (Besluit)) of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel*, but the operation is recorded in the *Inhoud* (Table of Contents) of the volume as one of the principal operations of August [[1896]]. The excursion formed part of the broader Dutch strategy of the second semester — the slow extension of effective Dutch knowledge of, and influence over, the interior of Groot-Atjeh, in preference to the permanent occupation of points beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line).

## Significance

The Excursie to Lepong and Lohong of [[10 August|10]]–[[14 August]] [[1896]] is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as the principal Dutch reconnaissance into the Lepong region in the second semester of [[1896]]. The intelligence gathered on the excursion — that Umar had moved to Lohong, that his band had been reduced to 100 men, that he had fallen out with the *kedjoeroean*, and that he was forced to hide his *schatten en kostbaarheden* with various well-to-do people — formed the basis of Dutch strategic planning for the next four months. The intelligence suggested that Umar was vulnerable, but that his capture would require a more extended operation than the Dutch had yet undertaken — a conclusion which would lead, in December [[1896]], to the *uitrusten eener colonne tegen Oemar* (equipping of a colonne against Umar), the operation which the *heerschende cholera* (prevailing cholera) of December [[1896]] would prevent from taking the field.

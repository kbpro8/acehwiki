**The *Laṭāʾif al-asrār*** ("The Subtleties of the Secrets") is a lost Malay Sufi treatise by [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nuruddin ar-Raniri]], cited by Raniri himself in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] as the source to which the reader is referred for a fuller refutation of the heretical [[Wujudiyyah]] than the *Tibjān* itself provides. No manuscript of the *Laṭāʾif al-asrār* is known to survive; its existence is attested solely by Raniri's own references to it. [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] notes that the work "has been lost" (*Dit geschrift van Rānīrī is verloren gegaan*).

## Citations by Raniri

The *Laṭāʾif al-asrār* is cited by Raniri in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] at the close of his survey of the heretical Ṣūfī sects (p. 95 of the Voorhoeve facsimile). After treating the Wujūdiyyah and refuting in detail the *[[Kitab al-Muntahi|Kitāb al-Muntahī]]* of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] and the *[[Mir'at al-muhakkikin|Mirʾāt al-muḥaqqiqīn]]* and *[[Chirkah|Chirkah]]* of [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]], Raniri refers the reader to the *Laṭāʾif al-asrār* for a fuller refutation. The reference is significant: it shows that the *Tibjān*, although a work of encyclopaedic scope, was not Raniri's last word on the Wujūdiyyah, and that the fuller polemic was reserved for a separate treatise of which the *Laṭāʾif al-asrār* was the title.

## Character and probable content

The very title — "The Subtleties of the Secrets" — places the work in the genre of Sufi *laṭāʾif* literature, the literature of the "subtleties" (*laṭīfa*, plural *laṭāʾif*) by which the inner meanings of the mystical path are disclosed to the adept. The word *asrār* ("secrets") reinforces the esoteric character of the work. It may be conjectured that the *Laṭāʾif al-asrār* was a sustained Sufi treatise, of the same character as the [[Hall al-zill|*Ḥall aẓ-ẓill*]] and the [[Hudjdjat as-siddiq li-daf az-zindiq|*Ḥujjat aṣ-ṣiddīq*]], in which the orthodox Sufi position on the divine being and its relation to the world was set out in greater detail than the *Tibjān* permitted, and in which the doctrines of [[Hamzah Pansuri]] and [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]] were refuted at length.

## Significance

The loss of the *Laṭāʾif al-asrār* is one of the major lacunae in the literary remains of Raniri's Achehnese period. Its existence is nonetheless of consequence for the interpretation of the *Tibjān*: it shows that the *Tibjān*, although comprehensive, was conceived by its author as a work of polemical introduction rather than as a definitive refutation, and that the fuller argument was reserved for a separate treatise. The *Laṭāʾif al-asrār* thus belongs, with the [[Hall al-zill|*Ḥall aẓ-ẓill*]] and the [[Djawahir al-'ulum|*Jawāhir al-ʿulūm*]], to that body of Raniri's writings in which the orthodox Sufi position was developed at length.

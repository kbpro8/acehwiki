**The First Selimoen Expedition** — *eerste tocht naar Selimoen van 6 tot en met 11 September [[1896]]* — was the first major Dutch expedition into the [[Selimoen]] highlands of southern [[Groot-Atjeh]] in the second semester of [[1896]], undertaken by a colonne under [[J. B. van Heutsz|Luitenant-Kolonel J. B. van Heutsz]] between [[6 September]] [[1896|6]] and [[11 September]] [[1896]]. The expedition — ordered one day before the [[Construction of Blang Bintang Camp (7 September 1896)|construction of the Blang Bintang camp]] began on [[7 September]] [[1896]] — represented the most ambitious Dutch operation of September [[1896]] and was the principal Dutch probe into the Selimoen region in the second semester of the year. The Selimoen highlands — the centre of Acehnese resistance in the south of Groot-Atjeh — had been the object of repeated Dutch reconnaissances and *tuchtigingen* throughout the war, but had never been permanently occupied.

## Strategic Background

By September [[1896]] the Dutch, under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s second tenure, had extended their effective control from the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) into the [[XXVI Moekims]] and [[XXII Moekims]] through the *bezetting* of [[Kroeng Raba]] (Lok-Nga) on [[15 July]] [[1896]] and a series of reconnaissances in July and August. The Selimoen highlands — lying south of the XXII Moekims and forming the southernmost extension of Groot-Atjeh — remained, however, beyond the limit of effective Dutch control. The *eerste tocht naar Selimoen* of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]] was thus both a reconnaissance and a *machtsvertoon* (show of force) — designed to ascertain the disposition of the Acehnese forces in the Selimoen and to demonstrate the Dutch capacity for force projection into the highlands.

## The Colonne under Van Heutsz

The colonne for the first Selimoen expedition was commanded by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Luitenant-Kolonel Van Heutsz]] — the officer who, two years later in [[1898]], would be appointed [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] and would launch the decisive *korte oorlog* phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The composition of the colonne is not separately narrated by Kruisheer in the detail given for the [[Surprise of Ana' Galoeng (29 June 1896)|surprise of Ana' Galoeng]] of [[29 June]] [[1896]], but it is recorded that the colonne was *uitgerust* (equipped) for a five-day expedition and that it operated from the [[Blang Bintang]] position — the construction of which began on [[7 September]] [[1896]], one day after the expedition's departure. The use of Blang Bintang as the base for the first Selimoen expedition — even before the camp there had been completed — reflects the strategic importance of the Blang Bintang position for operations in the Selimoen.

## The Five-Day Expedition

The expedition departed on [[6 September]] [[1896]] and returned on [[11 September]] [[1896]] — a five-day *tocht* (expedition) into the Selimoen highlands. The detailed narrative of the expedition is not preserved in the *Besluit* (Decree (Besluit)) of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel*, but the operation is recorded in the *Inhoud* (Table of Contents) of the volume as one of the principal operations of the second semester of [[1896]]. The expedition traversed the Selimoen region, engaged the Acehnese forces where they were encountered, and returned to the Blang Bintang position on [[11 September]] [[1896]] — by which time the construction of the camp had been sufficiently advanced to serve as its base. The first Selimoen expedition was thus the opening operation of the southern arm of the Dutch forward movement of the second semester of [[1896]], and was followed — after an interval of nearly two months — by the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]] of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]].

## The Significance of the Expedition

The First Selimoen Expedition is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as the first major Dutch expedition into the Selimoen highlands in the second semester of [[1896]]. The expedition — undertaken by Van Heutsz, the future *Civiel en Militair Gouverneur* — represented the most ambitious Dutch operation of September [[1896]] and was the principal Dutch probe into the Selimoen region in the second semester of the year. The expedition's reliance on the Blang Bintang position — the construction of which began on [[7 September]] [[1896]], one day after the expedition's departure — established the strategic importance of Blang Bintang for operations in the Selimoen, and the camp would serve as the base for the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]] of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]] and the [[Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri (28 October 1896)|temporary occupation of Indrapoeri]] of [[28 October]] [[1896]]. The first Selimoen expedition was thus the opening operation of the southern arm of the Dutch forward movement of the second semester of [[1896]], and was the precursor to the more extended operations in the Selimoen which would be undertaken under [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]] in November and December [[1896]].

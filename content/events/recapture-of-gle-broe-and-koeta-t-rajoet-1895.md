**The Recapture of bentengs Glé Broë and Koeta T. Rajoet (4 August 1895)** was the [[Dutch]] recovery of two Achehnese fortifications at [[Glé Broeë]] and [[Koeta T. Rajoet]] east of [[Kroeng-Kali]], which the colonial forces had previously been compelled to abandon and which the [[Achehnese]] had re-fortified as part of the defensive stellingen covering the eastern approaches to [[Pidië]] and the [[Glé Broeë|Gle-Broeë]] massif. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the operation of [[4 August]] [[1895]] was a typical late-1895 column expedition: a short, sharp descent on a known objective, the storming of the works, the destruction of the *benteng*, and a return march before the enemy could concentrate superior forces. It prefigured, in miniature, the much larger [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack]] of [[22 June]] – [[23 June]] [[1896]], which would revisit the same theatre.

## Background

By the summer of [[1895]] the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] had entered its long second stalemate, in which the Dutch held the [[buitenlinie]] around [[Koeta Radja]] and [[Oleh-leh]] but could not break the Achehnese interior. The colonial command — under [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] — had reverted to the policy of small *excursieën*, mounted to keep the enemy off-balance, to demonstrate the Government's reach, and to test the political temperature of the eastern mukims. Two Achehnese *benteng* — at [[Glé Broeë]] on the slopes of the eponymous hill, and at [[Koeta T. Rajoet]] east of [[Kroeng-Kali]] — had become symbols of Achehnese defiance after their earlier abandonment by the Dutch. Their recapture became a matter of prestige.

The *benteng* at Glé Broeë in particular occupied a position of considerable tactical importance. From its parapet the defenders could observe the plain of the [[XXVI Moekims]] and the lower reaches of the [[Kroeng-Kali]] river; its loss to the Achehnese had been felt as a humiliation. The *benteng* at Koeta T. Rajoet formed a complementary work on the eastern bank, its flanks covered by the river and its approaches obstructed with bamboo-thorn abatis in the customary Achehnese manner. Together the two works constituted the outer crust of the stelling that would, in the spring of [[1896]], be the target of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day Dutch offensive]].

## The Operation of 4 August 1895

On [[4 August]] [[1895]] a Dutch column — Kruisheer does not specify the composition in detail but places it among the typical late-1895 *excursie* columns combining infantry, mountain artillery, and a detachment of *maréchaussée* — advanced from the [[buitenlinie]] against the two *benteng*. The approach march was contested in the usual Achehnese fashion: scattered fire from the *kampong* edges, sniping from the low hills, and a gradual withdrawal of the defenders into the main works as the column closed. The artillery was brought into action against the parapets, and after a short bombardment the infantry stormed the breaches. Both *benteng* were carried; the works were then systematically demolished by the engineers to prevent their re-occupation by the Achehnese.

The casualties on the Dutch side appear to have been light, and Kruisheer records no Achehnese casualty figures. The political effect, however, was disproportionate: the recapture demonstrated that the Government could still reach into the eastern mukims at will, and it briefly rattled the confidence of the local *hoeloebalang*. The success of [[4 August]] [[1895]] encouraged Deykerhoff's optimism about the trajectory of the war — an optimism that the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[Teuku Umar]] less than eight months later would cruelly expose.

## Significance

The recapture of [[Glé Broeë]] and [[Koeta T. Rajoet]] was, in its way, the last success of the pre-1896 phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. It confirmed the operational pattern — short *excursie*, demolition of works, return march — that the Dutch would continue to employ through the spring of [[1896]], and it established Glé Broeë as a theatre to which the colonial command would return, with much greater force, in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing of 22–23 June 1896]]. The operation also exemplified the liprovided that of Deykerhoff's strategy: the *benteng* could be stormed and razed, but the Achehnese would simply rebuild them once the column withdrew. The cycle of conquest, abandonment, and reconquest would only be broken after [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]]'s arrival as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] and the sustained campaigns of May–June [[1896]].

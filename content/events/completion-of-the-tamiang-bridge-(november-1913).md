**The completion of the Tamiang bridge** was the finishing, in November [[1913]], of the fairly large bridge over the [[Tamiang]] river at [[Koeala Simpang|Kwalla Simpang]] on the line of the [[Atjeh-tram]], which had long held up the further extension of the tramway toward the south-east of [[Atjeh]]. The traveller [[W. Moens]], who was then at Kwalla Simpang in the course of his *[[Moens' journey through Atjeh (November 1913)|journey through Atjeh]]*, recorded that the further construction of the line had been temporarily checked by the necessity of throwing over the Tamiang river a rather large bridge — *een werkje, dat veel oponthoud heeft ondervonden* (a work that had suffered much delay) — and that he was precisely permitted to witness how *de laatste spanning dezer brug op haar plaats geschoven werd, waardoor de overbrugging een feit was* — the last span of the bridge was slid into its place, whereby the bridging was an accomplished fact.

## Significance

The completion of the Tamiang bridge is significant in the account of the pacified Atjeh as a marker of the state of the engineering works: the same tramway that during the war had crept forward as a military line under fortresses, and whose extension the Atjehnese chiefs themselves named as the proof that *the government means it now*, was in November 1913 carried bodily across the great river that had been the frontier of the war expeditions of [[1893]] — the [[Expedition against Tamiang (1893)|naval expeditions against Tamiang]] of twenty years before. With the bridging accomplished, the terminus at Kwalla Simpang ceased to be provisional, and the way stood open for the projected continuation of the line and the [[Atjeh-Deli tram connection|junction with the Deli railways]] by which the traffic of Atjeh was to compete for the rice and export trade of the East Coast of Sumatra.

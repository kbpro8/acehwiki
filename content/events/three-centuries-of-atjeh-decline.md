**Three centuries of Atjeh decline** (c. 1600s-1800s) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. After Iskandar Moeda, the Atjeh kingdom crumbled piece by piece; its trade withered and its profits went into Dutch pockets. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to c. 1600s-1800s.

## Account

After [[Iskandar Moeda]], the Atjeh kingdom crumbled piece by piece; its trade withered and its profits went into Dutch pockets. The pepper, tin, and gold trade initially came largely to the Dutch. Dutch intervention was first negative and disruptive, putting nothing new in place of the old. Atjeh entered a long period - three centuries - of stagnation. By the end, an independent Atjeh no longer existed.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

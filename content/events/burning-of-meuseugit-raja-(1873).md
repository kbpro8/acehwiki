**Burning of Meuseugit Raja (1873)** — the destruction by fire of the great mosque (*Meuseugit Raja*, also rendered *Meuseugit Raya*) of [[Kutaradja|Koeta Radja]], the capital of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], during the [[Eerste Atjeh Expeditie (1873)|First Aceh Expedition]] of April [[1873]] — is the event that prompted the Dutch colonial government, some three decades later, to construct a replacement mosque in Byzantine style at a cost of more than *f* 200,000 (over 200,000 guilders). The replacement mosque, and the Acehnese refusal to use it, are recorded in Prof. Dr. [[W. J. A. Kernkamp|Kernkamp]]'s chapter *De invloed van den Islam* in *[[Wat Indië Ontving en Schonk]]* ([[1946]]).

## Kernkamp's account

Kernkamp's text introduces the burning and the rebuilding in a single sentence: "The only more or less pompous mosque, in Koeta Radja, built in Byzantine style, was built by us and offered to the Atjehsche population to replace the Meuseugit Raja, which was shot to fire in 1873. The gesture was grand enough, for it then already cost more than 2 tons. The success was not proportionate. The Atjehers considered the religious exercises in this God's house built by *kåfirs* (unbelievers) as worthless. [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] spoke of 'an empty monument of our folly' (*een ledig monument onzer dwaasheid*)."

## Context: the First Aceh Expedition

The burning of the Meuseugit Raja took place during the [[Eerste Atjeh Expeditie (1873)|First Aceh Expedition]] of [[5 April]]–[[29 April]] [[1873]], in which a Dutch expeditionary force under Major-General [[J. H. R. Kohler|Köhler]] bombarded and assaulted the defences of Koeta Radja. Köhler himself was killed in the assault on the Benteng (fort) of the Sultan, and the expedition was repulsed with heavy losses. The mosque — the principal religious building of the Atjeh capital — was destroyed in the course of the bombardment and assault, whether intentionally or as collateral damage. The [[Rebuilding of the Meuseugit Raya|replacement mosque]], in Byzantine style, was built by the Dutch colonial government at Koeta Radja in the early twentieth century, after the final Dutch subjugation of Atjeh under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]].

## Significance

For the [[Acheh|Atjeh]]-focused reader, the burning of the Meuseugit Raja is significant as both a military and a religious-symbolic event. As a military event, it marks the destruction of the principal religious building of the Atjeh capital in the first major Dutch assault on the sultanate. As a religious-symbolic event, it sets up the long Acehnese refusal — recorded by Kernkamp in [[1946]] — to use the Dutch-built replacement mosque, on the grounds that "religious exercises in this God's house built by *kåfirs* (unbelievers)" were "worthless." Snouck Hurgronje's verdict — *\"een ledig monument onzer dwaasheid\"* ("an empty monument of our folly") — captures the assessment of the colonial government's own chief Islam adviser that the building of a replacement mosque by the infidel Dutch could not, in Acehnese eyes, undo the religious damage done by the original burning.

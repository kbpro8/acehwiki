The **Dutch expedition to the North-East Coast of Sumatra** of [[1865]] was the Netherlands Indian answer to the Atjehnese utterances of hostility that followed the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|contract with the Sultan of Siak of 1858]] and the renewal of the Atjehnese pretensions upon that realm — an expedition which, in author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s sober judgement, "wel orde, doch geen verbetering van de gezindheid der bewoners van de Atjehsche randstaten met zich bracht" ("brought order indeed, but no improvement of the disposition of the inhabitants of the Atjehnese border states").

## Context and effect

The Sultan of Atjeh's sudden assertion of lapsed claims upon Siak and Dependencies — rejected by the Dutch side — had as consequence several utterances of a less good understanding, among them the Atjehnese firing upon [[Batoe Baroe]]; "een expeditie in 1865 naar de N. O. kust onzerzijds" followed. Its meagre diplomatic harvest left the troubles of the Atjehnese border states smouldering: in [[1868]] the Atjehnese chiefs [[Offer of Atjeh's sovereignty to the Sultan of Turkey (1868)|offered the sovereignty of their country to the Sultan of Turkey]], while the chiefs of several coast states, weary of the anarchy in the realm, asked to be placed under Dutch authority — requests which the Government, bound still by the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|London treaty]], declined.

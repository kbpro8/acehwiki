The **bombardment of the Atjehse coast by the British** of [[1844]] was a punitive naval action by the [[Britain|British government]] against the [[Achehnese|Atjehse]] coastal population, undertaken in retaliation for Atjehese piracy against British merchant shipping. The action followed the precedent of the [[American bombardment of the Atjehse coast (1831)|American bombardment of 1831]] and is recorded by [[J. Frijling|Ir. J. Frijling]] in his [[1946]] sketch *Een stukje geschiedenis der Indische Buitengewesten* as one of the two nineteenth-century precedents for the use of foreign naval force against the Atjehse coast in the absence of effective Dutch authority. Together with the American action, the British bombardment formed the international-pressure background to the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824|Sumatra-contract]] of [[1871]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]–[[1904]].

## Context

The same conditions that had produced the [[American bombardment of the Atjehse coast (1831)|American bombardment of 1831]] continued to obtain through the 1830s and 1840s. The Dutch policy of *niet-inmenging* — non-intervention — left the Atjehse coast effectively unpoliced, and Atjehese piracy continued to claim foreign merchant ships among its victims. In [[1844]] the British government followed the American precedent and "volgde dit voorbeeld" — bombarded the Atjehse coast in chastisement.

## The Action

Frijling records the action in the same terms as the American precedent. He gives no detail of the warship, the commanding officer, or the precise target of the bombardment. "Of schuldigen dan wel onschuldigen door de granaten werden getroffen, vertelt de geschiedenis niet" — the comment applies equally to both actions. The British, like the Americans, acted on the practical assumption that the Atjehse coast was not effectively under Dutch sovereignty and that direct retribution was therefore permissible.

## Significance

The [[1844]] British bombardment, together with the [[American bombardment of the Atjehse coast (1831)|American bombardment of 1831]], is one of the two precedents for foreign naval action against the Atjehse coast in the period before the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Both actions illustrate the international pressure on the Dutch to suppress Atjehese piracy — pressure that ultimately led, in [[1872]], to the Dutch decision to take military measures against the Atjehse coast and, in [[1873]], to the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first Aceh expedition]]. The same year [[1844]] also saw the British adventurer [[James Brooke|James Brooke]] conquer North-West Borneo with his crew and have himself proclaimed Rajah, an event Frijling cites as a parallel instance of British willingness to act unilaterally in the archipelago when the Dutch failed to do so.

## Account from *Vijftig jaren economische staatkunde in Atjeh* ([[1923]])

The **British chastisement of Kuala Batee and Kuala Meureudoe** in [[1844]] was the action of English warships against those two Atjehnese coast places "in connection with occurred plundering of merchant ships" — a repetition of the American punishment of [[1831]], which, in author [[J. Langhout|Joh. Langhout]]'s words, "availed little".

### The action and its sequel

"Verdrijving ... Baros en Singkel ... In 1844 tuchtigen Engelsche oorlogschepen opnieuw Koeala Batië en Koeala Meureudoe in verband met voorgekomen plundering van handelsschepen. Het baatte weinig" — the plundering of merchant ships on the coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] continued, and the years that followed brought new incidents: in [[1851]] a Neapolitan schooner was robbed on the Atjehnese coasts, and in [[1852]] an English merchant ship. It was this chain of incidents that moved the Netherlands Indian Government to seek "once more, along the road of reconciliation, to call another, better state of affairs into being", instructing General [[Jan van Swieten|J. van Swieten]], Governor of [[Sumatra]]'s West Coast, to conclude with Sultan [[Sultan Ibrahim Mansur Shah|Ibrahim Mansur]] the [[Treaty of 1857 between Netherlands and Aceh|treaty of peace, friendship and commerce of 1857]], directed in particular against sea-, strand- and man-robbery.

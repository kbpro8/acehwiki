**The [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] in the [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] school-history textbook of F. Roorda** is the canonical Dutch schoolroom account of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — a compressed chronological narrative, intended for the higher-burgerschool and the candidate-notary examination, that fixed the war in the Dutch national memory as a sequence of named commanders (Köhler, Van Swieten, Van der Heyden, Vetter, Van Heutsz), named places (Kraton, Kota Radja, Groot-Atjeh, Melaboeh, Pedir), and a single named Atjehnese leader (Toekoe Oemar). The account is the principal document of the Dutch schoolroom memory of the war in the early twentieth century, and it is the source of the canonical clauses — "*Köhler sneuvelde*," "*De Kraton werd ingenomen*," "*Toekoe Oemar sneuvelde bij Melaboeh*" — by which the war was transmitted to generations of Dutch schoolchildren.

## The casus belli

The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook records the *casus belli* in a single parenthetical clause, in the entry for [[1873]]:

> *"1873. Oorlogsverklaring aan Atjeh (wegens hun zéerooverijen en omdat de sultan vreemde hulp tegen de Hollanders wilde inroepen)."*

The two causes adduced — Atjehnese piracy (*zéerooverijen*) and the [[Sultan of Acheh|sultan]]'s negotiations to invoke foreign (i.e., Italian, Turkish, or American) assistance against the Dutch — are the standard Dutch framing of the *casus belli*. The juxtaposition of the two — a moral accusation (piracy) and a strategic accusation (solicitation of foreign help) — is the rhetorical structure of the Dutch justification: the war was both a punitive action against piracy and a preventive action against the intrusion of a foreign power into the Dutch sphere.

## The 1873 expeditions

The textbook records the [[1873]] expeditions in a single sentence of the canonical form:

> *"De 1e expeditie mislukte, Köhler sneuvelde, maar de 2e expeditie onder Van Swieten bracht succes. De Kraton (paleis) van den sultan werd ingenomen, Kota Radja werd de zetel van ons gezag."*

The two expeditions — the [[First Aceh Expedition (1873)|first]] (March–April [[1873]]) and the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second]] (November [[1873]] – January [[1874]]) — are fixed in the schoolroom memory by the names of their commanders and the outcome: [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]]'s death in the first, [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]]'s success in the second, the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|capture of the Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]], and the establishment of [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] as the seat of Dutch authority. The name *Köhler* — paired with the verb *sneuvelen* (to fall in battle) — is the rhetorical fix of the failure of the first expedition.

## The 1879 conquest of Groot-Atjeh

The textbook records the [[1879|1879]] conquest of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] under General [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] and the subsequent Dutch error:

> *"1879. Generaal Van der Heyden bracht Groot Atjeh onder ons gezag: de opstand scheen bedwongen. Evenwel beging de regeering nu de fout het militaire door een burgerlijk bestuur te vervangen. Het verzet begon opnieuw, met het gevolg, dat de Indische regeering overging tot het stelsel van concentratie, d. w. z. de veroverde streken werden prijsgegeven en men beperkte zich tot de verdediging van een versterkte stelling om Kota-Radja. Langen tijd scheen het, dat de Atjehneezen zouden winnen."*

The textbook's evaluation of the [[1879|1879]] conquest — paired with the immediate record of the Dutch error of replacing the military by a civil administration — fixes the [[Concentration system in Atjeh|concentration system]] as the consequence of that error: the abandonment of the conquered interior, the retreat to a fortified perimeter around [[Koeta Radja|Kota-Radja]], and the apparent imminent victory of the Atjehnese. The clause "*Langen tijd scheen het, dat de Atjehneezen zouden winnen*" ("For a long time it seemed that the Atjehnese would win") is the schoolroom memory of the low point of the Dutch war effort.

## The Toekoe Oemar episode (1893–1896)

The textbook records the [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] episode in a single paragraph:

> *"1893-'96. Toekoe-Oemar werd onze bondgenoot. Hij bleek echter onbetrouwbaar, want in 1896 sloot hij zich weer bij zijn landgenooten aan. Generaal Vetter werd daarna met onbeperkte volmacht naar Atjeh gezonden. Hij behaalde groote voordeelen; doch pas aan van Heutz gelukte het, Groot Atjeh te onderwerpen. Toekoe Oemar sneuvelde bij Melaboeh."*

The episode — the alliance of [[1893]]–[[1896]], the [[1896]] defection, the appointment of [[General Vetter|Vetter]] with unlimited authority, the partial successes of Vetter, the definitive subjugation by [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], and the death of Toekoe Oemar at [[Melaboeh|Melaboeh]] — is the central narrative block of the late phase of the war. The clause "*Hij bleek echter onbetrouwbaar*" ("He proved, however, untrustworthy") is the schoolroom moral judgement on Toekoe Oemar's defection.

## The 1903 surrender and aftermath

The textbook records the [[Surrender of the Atjeh pretender-sultan (1903)|1903 surrender]] in a single sentence:

> *"1903. De pretendent-sultan en de andere Atjehsche aanvoerders bieden hun onderwerping aan. De oorlog kan hiermee als geëindigd worden beschouwd."*

The phrasing — "*De oorlog kan hiermee als geëindigd worden beschouwd*" ("the war can with this be considered ended") — is carefully hedged: the war "can be considered ended," rather than simply "ended." The hedge is itself a document of the Dutch recognition that the formal surrender did not necessarily entail the cessation of all resistance. The subsequent entry — "*1904-'09. Van Heutz gouverneur-general ... De Bataklanden onderworpen.*" — fixes Van Heutsz's promotion and the extension of the conquest to the [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]]. The final entry — "*In 1918 werd het militaire door een burgerlijk bestuur vervangen, wat de goede verstandhouding tusschen de regeering en de inlanders heeft bevorderd.*" — records the formal transition from military to civil administration, and the textbook's positive evaluation of that transition.

## Significance

The [[Na 1795 (1924)|*Na 1795*]] textbook's account of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history as the principal document of the Dutch schoolroom memory of the war in the early twentieth century. The account's compression — the reduction of a thirty-year war to a sequence of named commanders, named places, and a single named Atjehnese leader — is itself a document of the way the war was transmitted to generations of Dutch schoolchildren, and of the canonical clauses ("*Köhler sneuvelde*," "*De Kraton werd ingenomen*," "*Toekoe Oemar sneuvelde bij Melaboeh*") by which the war was fixed in the national memory. The account's moral judgements — the framing of the *casus belli* as piracy and the solicitation of foreign help, the judgement of Toekoe Oemar as "untrustworthy," the hedge on the end of the war — are the documents of the Dutch colonial framing of the conflict, and they are the principal source of the schoolroom memory against which the more nuanced scholarly accounts of the war (e.g., [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]'s, [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s) must be read.

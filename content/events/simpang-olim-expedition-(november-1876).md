The **Simpang Olim expedition** of [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]] — *de militaire expeditie* (the military expedition) sent by [[Diemont|Generaal Diemont]] "onmiddellijk na zijn optreden in November 1876" (immediately on taking up the command in November 1876) against the east-coast statelets of [[Simpang Olim]] and [[Tandjong Semantoh]] — was the first military operation of Diemont's governorship and the immediate instrument by which the two statelets were "tot erkenning van onze souvereiniteit gedwongen" (forced to the recognition of our sovereignty). The expedition marked the operational replacement of [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]], whom [[Van Lansberge|G.-G. van Lansberge]] had relieved of the Atjeh command precisely because Wiggers van Kerchem "kon zich met deze inzichten niet vereenigen" — could not bring himself to agree with Van Lansberge's wish to coerce the [[Onderhoorigheden]] (Dependencies) by force. The campaign was the first armed Dutch intervention in the *Onderhoorigheden* since the [[Pel's Plan (30 April 1875)|Pel plan]] of [[30 April]] [[1875]] had committed the colony to the coastal occupation of [[Groot-Atjeh]] proper, and it produced the model — a *controleur* (civil-administrative official) placed under the cover of a *militaire bezetting* (military garrison) — that would be repeated in every subsequent submission of an *Onderhoorigheden* statelet.

## Background: the Wiggers van Kerchem dispute

The expedition's immediate background was the dispute between [[Wiggers van Kerchem|Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem]] and [[Van Lansberge|G.-G. van Lansberge]] over the use of force in the *Onderhoorigheden*. Van Lansberge, in [[1876]], "zag op dit tijdstip voornamelijk heil in een gewapend optreden in de Onderhoorigheden" — saw his chief hope in an armed intervention in the *Onderhoorigheden* — "om de staatjes die onze souvereiniteit nog niet erkend hadden, door de wapens daartoe te dwingen" (in order to compel by arms the statelets that had not yet recognised our sovereignty to do so). Wiggers van Kerchem, who had succeeded [[Pel (governor)|Pel]] on Pel's death in early [[1876]], disagreed:

> "De Generaal-Majoor Wiggers van Kerchem, opvolger van Generaal Pel, kon zich met deze inzichten niet vereenigen, hij zag geen heil in een agressief optreden met de wapenen in de Onderhoorigheden, waarvan de gevolgen z. i. niet te berekenen waren en gaf er verre de voorkeur aan, om vooralsnog alle beschikbare troepen in Groot-Atjeh te houden, noodig ter voltooiing van het programma van den Generaal Pel".

The immediate occasion of the dispute was the war that had broken out on the East Coast "tusschen Simpang Olim en Kerti" (between Simpang Olim and Kerti), in which "het ons, evenals Simpang Olim, vijandige Tandjong Semantoh aan de zijde van Simpang Olim stond" (Tandjong Semantoh, hostile like Simpang Olim to us, stood on the side of Simpang Olim). Van Lansberge wished to send troops to reinforce the friendly statelet of [[Kerti]]. Wiggers van Kerchem refused, preferring to arm a proxy — "aan het vroeger verdreven hoofd van Merbou, Tekoe Moeda Augkasa, die Kerti met 500 man wilde helpen, 150 achterlaadgeweren met munitie en 3000 dollars te zenden, teneinde Simpang Olim en Tandjong Semantoh afbreuk te doen" (to send to the formerly expelled chief of Merbou, Tekoe Moeda Augkasa, who wished to help Kerti with 500 men, 150 breech-loading rifles with ammunition and 3000 dollars, in order to do damage to Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh) — rather than commit Dutch troops directly. Van Lansberge, "die, door het met de wapenen dwingen van erkenning onzer souvereiniteit, hoopte de voor die weerspannige staatjes nog steeds voortdurende dure blokkade te kunnen opheffen" (who, by compelling recognition of our sovereignty with arms, hoped to be able to lift the still-continuous costly blockade of those refractory statelets), relieved him of the command.

## Diemont's succession (November 1876)

[[Diemont|Generaal Diemont]] was appointed Governor of Atjeh in November [[1876]] specifically to carry out the armed coercion Wiggers van Kerchem had refused. Van Heutsz's account makes the succession and its purpose inseparable:

> "De Gouverneur-Generaal echter, die, door het met de wapenen dwingen van erkenning onzer souvereiniteit, hoopte de voor die weerspannige staatjes nog steeds voortdurende dure blokkade te kunnen opheffen, liet toen den Generaal Wiggers van Kerchem vervangen door den Generaal Diemont, die, onmiddelljkk na zijn op- treden in November 1876, door het zenden van eene militaire expeditie, Simpang Olim en Tandjong Semantoh, tot erkenning van onze souve- reiniteit dwong".

The phrase "onmiddellijk na zijn optreden" — immediately on taking up the command — is Van Heutsz's note that the expedition was the *first act* of the new governor, not a deliberated operation of his third or fourth month. Diemont had been chosen by Van Lansberge for precisely this purpose; the expedition was its execution.

## The expedition and the recognition of sovereignty

The expedition itself is described in Van Heutsz's text only in its political result: Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh were "tot erkenning van onze souvereiniteit gedwongen" — forced to the recognition of Dutch sovereignty. The military detail is omitted; the political outcome is the entire content. The submission was, however, the first formal Dutch assertion of sovereignty over an *Onderhoorigheden* statelet by direct military coercion, and it set the template for every subsequent submission: a *controleur* (controller) was placed in the submitted statelet, "gedekt en gesteund door een militaire bezetting" — covered and supported by a military garrison — to regulate its *bestuursaangelegenheden* (governmental affairs) (the OCR reads *destursaangllgenneeenn*). Van Heutsz's phrase is preserved here in its OCR-damaged form:

> "door het zenden van eene militaire expeditie, Simpang Olim en Tandjong Semantoh, tot erkenning van onze souve- reiniteit dwong en tot regeling van de destursaangllgenneeenn daar een controleur plaatste, gedekt en gesteund door een militaire bezetting".

The placement of a *controleur* with a *militaire bezetting* (military garrison) — a civilian official backed by a military garrison — became the standard instrument of Dutch rule in the submitted *Onderhoorigheden* statelets: the same formula would be applied at [[Pedir]] (June [[1876]]), at [[Edi]] (long-standing Dutch post), at [[Simpang Olim]] again in [[1877]] (the *expeditie naar Simpang Olim* of [[1877]]), and at the statelets of the [[Noordkust]] and [[Westkust]] in the years that followed.

## The 47-post benchmark (end of 1876)

The submission of Simpang Olim and Tandjong Semantoh brought the number of Dutch-occupied points in Atjeh and the *Onderhoorigheden* to 47 by the end of [[1876]] — the benchmark Van Heutsz uses to mark the operational scale of the colony's commitment by the end of Pel's *programma* (programme) year. Van Heutsz's accounting:

> "Hadden wij, zooals wij zagen, in Mei 1875 op Groot-Atjeh 38 verschillende posten bezet, in 't einde van 1876, dus vóórdat nog het programma van Generaal Pel geheel voltooid was, was het aantal bezette punten in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden geklomen tot 47, waar- van Pedir en Simpang Olim-in de Onderhoorigheden (Edi werd in beide gevallen niet medegerekend)".

The benchmark is double-edged in Van Heutsz's reading: the colony had grown from 38 posts in May [[1875]] to 47 by the end of [[1876]], but the growth had not advanced the political objective — "zonder dat deze uitbreiding ons ten opzichte van de onderwerping -dus op staatkundig gebied- één schrede had vooruitgebracht" (without this extension having advanced us one step towards the submission — thus in the political domain) was Van Heutsz's verdict on the parallel expansion in the valley of Groot-Atjeh. The 47-post figure also marks the moment at which the colony's military commitment exceeded Van Swieten's 2500-man garrison by more than three times — "Sedert het vertrek van den Generaal van Swieten was voorts de krijgsmacht te Atjeh al meer dan verdriedubbeld" (Since the departure of General Van Swieten, moreover, the military force at Atjeh had more than tripled).

## Significance

The Simpang Olim expedition of [[November 1876|November]] [[1876]] is significant in the [[Aceh Wiki History]] record for four reasons. First, it was the first formal Dutch assertion of sovereignty over an *Onderhoorigheden* statelet by direct military coercion — the operational replacement of the *overreding* (persuasion) and *blokkade* (blockade) instruments Van Swieten had prescribed in [[Van Swieten's Instructie (22 April 1874)|article 5 of his Instructie]]. Second, it set the template — a *controleur* placed "gedekt en gesteund door een militaire bezetting" — that became the standard instrument of Dutch rule in every subsequently submitted *Onderhoorigheden* statelet. Third, it was the *casus belli* of the Wiggers van Kerchem–Van Lansberge dispute — the moment at which the colony's Governor-General overrode the professional military judgement of his Governor of Atjeh in favour of an armed intervention the Governor had refused to execute — and thus the prototype of every subsequent colonial-military dispute in the Atjeh record. Fourth, it produced the 47-post benchmark of end-[[1876]] — the operational scale against which every subsequent governor's commitment was measured. The expedition is also the proximate cause of [[Diemont]]'s own further governorship, in which the *programma Pel* was finally completed in February [[1877]] — and of his evacuation "wegens ziekte" (on account of illness) on [[30 June]] [[1877]], which brought [[Van der Heijden|Generaal van der Heijden]] to the Atjeh command and opened the most decisive phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

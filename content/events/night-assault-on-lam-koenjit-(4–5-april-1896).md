The **Night assault on Lam Koenjit** in the night of [[4 April]] to [[5 April]] [[1896]] was an Achehnese attempt to storm one of the bastions of the invested Dutch post of [[Lam Koenjit]] in [[Groot-Atjeh]], repelled only because the gun in that bastion happened to be loaded with case-shot.

## The investment

The post, nicknamed *Lam Loempoer* — mud village — for the state of its ground, had drawn its water from [[Tjot Goeë]] until the patrols, always accompanied by a tame black sheep, came on the *ongeluksdag* of [[29 March]] [[1896]] to the conviction that their post was surrounded and that they would henceforth have to make do with muddy water.

## The attack

The commandant, [[Luitenant Creutz Lechleitner]], hoped to prevent the enemy from penetrating the post: above the outwork surrounded by *[[IJzerdraadversperring|ijzerdraadversperring]]*, within which the kitchen stood, rose the watch-house named *[[Eikhoren watch-house|Eikhoren]]*, from which the enemy's approach could be barred. Within the bastions of the *benteng* proper stood two 7½ cm. field-pieces and a *[[Cochoornmortier|cochoornmortier]]*.

Hooijer recorded that *in den nacht van 4 op 5 April 1896 werd een der bastions door den vijand bestormd, en ware het toen niet dat het stuk met een kartets geladen was, dan had die aanval ons noodlottig kunnen zijn* — in the night of 4 to 5 April 1896 one of the bastions was stormed by the enemy, and had the gun not been loaded with case-shot the attack might have proved fatal.

**The Arrival of Vetter and Dismissal of Deykerhoff (7 April 1896)** was the landing at [[Koeta Radja]] of [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. J. A. Vetter]] — [[Commandant van het Leger|Commandant of the Dutch Indies Army]] — aboard the steamer or *Bantam* with the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9th Battalion Infantry]] and the immediate honorable relief of [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. C. Deykerhoff]] from his functions as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]] of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. Vetter came vested with full authority as [[Regeeringscommissaris]] — Government Commissioner — to take civil and military command of the residency in the crisis provoked by the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection of Teuku Umar]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the arrival of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] and [[Majoor J. F. Breijer|Maj. Breijer]] aboard the same vessel — together with the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9th Battalion]] — also marked the formal transfer of strategic direction of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] from the colonial civil-military administration to the metropolitan high command.

## Background

By the first days of [[April]] [[1896]] the strategic situation in Atjeh had collapsed with a speed that talso the colonial administration in [[Batavia]] by surprise. The [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[Teuku Umar]] on approximately [[26 March]]–[[29 March]] [[1896]] had been followed immediately by the [[Siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe (28–29 March 1896)|siege of blokhuis Aloer Oe]], the investment of the posts of [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]], and the open repudiation by Umar of his oath of [[30 September]] [[1893]]. [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] — who had been warned by [[Controleur 2e kl. bij het binnenlandsch bestuur Gisolf|Controleur Gisolf]] of Umar's "more than suspicious attitude" but had refused, "in any case little inclined to give credence to the various reports" (in any case little inclined to give credence to the various reports), to countenance the arrest of his ally — was held politically responsible. The *Koloniaal Verslag*, quoted by Kruisheer, would subsequently observe that "Waar de afval van Tockoe Djohan, aanstonds tot so ver strekkende gevolgen aanleiding gaf, dat al het sedert 1893 verkregene, was, as it were, set on loose screws, moest wel ernstige twijfel rijzen, of de Civiele en Militaire Gouverneur den toestand te voren wel inderdaad juist beoordeeld had" — given that the defection of T. Djohan gave rise at once to consequences so far-reaching that all that had been gained since 1893 was, as it were, set on loose screws, serious doubt must arise whether the Civil and Military Governor had in fact correctly judged the situation beforehand.

The metropolitan government in [[The Hague]], acting through the [[Gouverneur-Generaal Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General]] in [[Batavia]], resolved to send the [[Commandant van het Leger|Army Commander]] in person. [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]] was appointed [[Regeeringscommissaris]] for Atjeh — a position of extraordinary authority that, in the colonial constitution, superseded the ordinary chain of command. With him were dispatched [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] — destined to take over provisional military command and ultimately the civil-military government itself — and [[Majoor J. F. Breijer|Maj. Breijer]] of the General Staff, who would serve as Vetter's adjutant. The [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9th Battalion Infantry]] was embarked as immediate reinforcement.

## The Arrival of 7 April 1896

On [[7 April]] [[1896]] the steamer Kruisheer records an alternative tradition that names the vessel *Bantam* entered the roadstead of [[Oleh-leh]] and disembarked [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]], [[Majoor J. F. Breijer|Maj. Breijer]], and the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9th Battalion]] at [[Koeta Radja]]. Vetter came ashore vested with the full authority of the [[Regeeringscommissaris]] — authority that, in the colonial constitution, gave him not only supreme military command in Atjeh but also superseded the ordinary civil jurisdiction of the [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]]. The first act of his mission was the honorable relief of [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]]. Kruisheer records the formula used: Deykerhoff was "eervol ontheven van zijne functien als Civiel en Militair Gouverneur" — honorably relieved of his functions as Civil and Military Governor. The choice of the word honorable was deliberate: it permitted Deykerhoff to retire without formal censure even as it held him responsible, in political fact, for the catastrophe of the defection.

[[Resident K. F. H. van Langen|Resident van Langen]] — *Resident voor Atjehsche zaken en de scheepvaartregeling* — was temporarily charged with civil affairs pending the formal transfer. [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] was temporarily charged with military command. The arrival of the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9th Battalion]] immediately stiffened the garrison at a moment when every company was needed for the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] that Vetter would order on the very next day.

## Aftermath

The relief of Deykerhoff and the arrival of Vetter inaugurated a wholly new phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Vetter immediately issued the secret operational instructions for the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (8 April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[8 April]] [[1896]] — an operation he placed under the command of [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]]. He issued, on [[12 April]] [[1896]], the [[Proclamation of 12 April 1896|proclamation]] ordering the [[Evacuation of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (12 April 1896)|evacuation]] of the two posts. He received [[Teuku Umar]]'s [[Second Letter of Teuku Umar|second]] and [[Third Letter of Teuku Umar|third]] letters, and countersigned the [[Government Resolution of 26 April 1896 No. 159/11]] dismissing Umar from his posts. He ordered the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] on [[27 April]] [[1896]] and the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai]] of [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]]. He launched the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] and the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the Lam-Krak mukims]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]]. He would depart Atjeh on [[28 June]] [[1896]] (after his [[Vetter's Farewell Parade (27 June 1896)|farewell parade]] of [[27 June]]), having restored the strategic situation, destroyed Umar's stelling, and razed the principal Achehnese bases in the western mukims.

## Significance

The arrival of Vetter and the dismissal of Deykerhoff on [[7 April]] [[1896]] were the political and military pivot of the first semester of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. They signified that the metropolitan government had lost confidence in the colonial administration's handling of the native-allied policy and had decided to commit the full weight of the Indies Army to a conventional campaign against the unified Achehnese resistance. The decision also marked the formal end of the [[1893]]–[[1896]] experiment of war-by-proxy through [[Teuku Umar]]'s *legioen*: from [[7 April]] [[1896]] onward the war in Atjeh would be fought, in the first instance, by Dutch and Ambonese troops. The installation of a [[Regeeringscommissaris]] — an extraordinary office, used sparingly and only in moments of supreme crisis — underlined the gravity of the situation. Vetter's three-month mission would result in the [[Departure of Vetter and Restoration of Civil and Military Governor (28 June 1896)|restoration]] of the ordinary civil-military government under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] on [[28 June]] [[1896]], and the lessons drawn from his tenure would shape Dutch strategy in Atjeh for the remainder of the war.

**The First Aceh Expedition of April [[1873]]** — *Eerste Atjeh-expeditie* — was the opening military campaign of the [[Aceh War]], in which Dutch forces launched a major assault against the [[Aceh Sultanate]]. The expedition resulted in initial Dutch setbacks, including the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]], and ultimately led to the fall of the Acehnese kraton (royal palace) in January [[1874]].

The force consisted of a detachment of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]] integrated into the [[Indische legerverband]]. It landed at the coastal town of [[Kuta (Atjeh)|Kuta]] and proceeded inland, establishing a temporary fortification before withdrawing to await reinforcements.

## Background

Following the [[Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1871]], which removed British objections to Dutch expansion in southeastern Sumatra, the Dutch colonial government decided to resolve the "Aceh question." Aceh had long maintained independence despite Dutch presence in surrounding areas. Reports of Acehnese negotiations with foreign powers (Italian, Turkish, or American) provided the *casus belli* (justification for war).

## The Campaign

### April 1873 Assault

Dutch forces launched their main assault in April [[1873]]. The attack encountered fierce resistance from Acehnese defenders. Dutch casualties were significant, including the death of [[J. H. R. Köhler|General Köhler]], who fell during the fighting.

### Outcome

The expedition achieved limited tactical success, capturing several villages but failing to impose lasting control, which prompted a second expedition in September [[1873]]. Following continued operations, the kraton of the Sultan fell in January [[1874]]. The Sultan fled and died during the flight, marking the effective end of the [[Aceh Sultanate]] as an independent political entity.

### Establishment of Colonial Rule

Following the conquest, the Dutch established their administrative center at [[Koeta-Radja|Kota-Radja]], near the ruins of the old Acehnese capital. The [[Aceh Sultanate]] was formally dissolved and the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] established.

## Aftermath

The fall of the kraton did not end Acehnese resistance. The conflict evolved into a prolonged guerrilla war that continued for decades. Religious leaders proclaimed the *heilige oorlog* (holy war), and the population increasingly joined the resistance. The legitimate succession to the sultanate — ultimately recognized in the person of [[Toeankoe Mochamad Dawot|Toeankoe Mohammed Daoed]] — remained a symbolic focus for the Acehnese cause long after the military conquest.

## Significance

The First Aceh Expedition marked the beginning of the colonial subjugation of Aceh. Though the war continued for decades, the expedition established Dutch sovereignty over the region and set the stage for the establishment of the [[Geconcentreerde Stelling|geconcentreerde stelling]] (concentrated position) of [[1884]]–[[1885]].

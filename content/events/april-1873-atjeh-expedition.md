**April 1873 Atjeh Expedition** was the first Dutch marine operation against the sultanate of [[atjeh|Atjeh]] in the nineteenth‑century colonial wars. Launched in April [[1873]], the expedition aimed to subdue local resistance along the western coast and to secure a foothold for subsequent operations.

The force consisted of a detachment of the [[Royal Netherlands Marine Corps|Dutch Marine Corps]] integrated into the [[indische-leger|Indische legerverband]]. It landed at the coastal town of [[Kuta (Atjeh)|Kuta]] and proceeded inland, establishing a temporary fortification before withdrawing to await reinforcements.

## Outcome
The expedition achieved limited tactical success, capturing several villages but failing to impose lasting control, which prompted a second expedition in September [[1873]].

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft's fourth chapter is dedicated to the first expedition under [[J. H. R. Köhler|Köhler]] and [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|van Daalen]]. He describes the hasty assembly of the force, the landing at [[Battle of Pantei Tjermin and Kota Mogat (8 April 1873)|Pantei-Tjermin]] on [[8-april|8 April]] [[1873]], the costly assaults on the [[mesigit|Great Missigit]] on [[10-april|10 April]] and [[14-april|14 April]], the death of Köhler, the council of war of [[18-april|18 April]] that decided against further assault on the Kraton, and the final withdrawal on [[29-april|29 April]]. He treats the expedition as a well-intentioned but under-estimated operation whose lessons — above all the need for [[beaumont-geweer|breech-loading rifles]], heavier artillery, and much larger numbers — were taken to heart in preparing the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second expedition]].

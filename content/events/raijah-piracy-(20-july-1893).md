**The Raijah piracy** was the seizure and massacre aboard the steamer *[[Raijah (steamer)|Raijah]]* of the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart-Maatschappij|Indische Paketvaart]] on the night of [[20 July]] [[1893]] off the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], in which seven [[Pedir|Pedirese]] pirates who had been smuggled aboard killed the helmsman, the second mate and the captain, ran the ship ashore near [[Keureutoe|Kerti]], and left twenty-four of the passengers and crew dead, twelve wounded and eighteen drowned. [[P. H. R. Beuming]] related the episode at length in *[[Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh (1911)|Schetsen uit den strijd op Groot-Atjeh]]* as *een enkel staaltje van de driestheid der Atjehsche zeeroovers* — a single specimen of the audacity of the Acehnese sea-rovers.

## The voyage

The *Raijah*, a steamer of the [[Koninklijke Paketvaart-Maatschappij|Indische Paketvaart]], left [[Oleh-leh]] on [[20 July]] [[1893]] bound for [[Penang]] by way of [[Segli]], [[Telok Semawé]] and [[Edi]]. Besides her ordinary cargo the ship was rumoured to have a large sum of money on board, intended for the *[[Controleur|controleur]]* at [[Edi]].

That rumour was sufficient for seven Pedirese to lay a plan to make themselves master of ship and cargo. At the moment of departure from [[Telok Semawé]] the seven well-armed robbers were smuggled aboard in an adroit manner by an Acehnese belonging to the crew, and stowed in the hold among the cargo.

## The night attack

When in the evening all on the ship lay in deep rest, and only the monotonous stamping of the engine broke the silence, one of them came on deck and struck up a conversation with the helmsman, asking him whether the ship was in the neighbourhood of [[Keureutoe|Kerti]]. Kerti, Beuming explains, was a small statelet on the north coast of Atjeh lying eastward of Telok Semawé; this part of the coast appeared most suitable for their plan.

Scarcely had the helmsman given an affirmative answer when he was suddenly overpowered and despatched with a *[[Rentjong|rentjong]]* (the Acehnese dagger or *kris*). The bandits then hurried to the bridge and fell upon the second mate, Mr. [[Alexander (second mate)|Alexander]], who had the watch; he too was despatched after a short hand-to-hand struggle.

There followed *een algemeen bloedbad* — a general bloodbath. All who came in their way were stabbed down. When the captain of the boat heard the tumult he went above, but scarcely had he appeared on deck when he received two thrusts in the belly and fled back to his cabin. He was still able to close the door, but died shortly afterwards.

## The wreck and the losses

The panic on board was indescribable and the ship was now wholly delivered to the robbers. The European engineers saved themselves by hiding in the engine-room. A part of the crew was pressed to help the robbers further. The ship was run onto the beach, whereupon the bandits went ashore with their booty and five women.

The booty must have been a severe disappointment, for the money-consignment mentioned had not gone with the *Raijah* but with the *[[Hok Canton (ship)|Hok Canton]]*. A part of the passengers also wished to go ashore, and in the attempt thirteen people perished, for the robbers had stove in the boats left aboard before quitting the ship. In all, of the passengers and crew, twenty-four were killed, twelve wounded and eighteen drowned.

After the sea-rovers had departed, the engineers came out and gave distress signals with rockets to the warship *[[Madura (warship)|Madura]]*, which was in the neighbourhood and immediately rendered assistance.

## Beuming's use of the episode

Beuming placed the narrative at the close of his introductory chapter on the origin of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], as the final illustration of his argument that the Acehnese had for centuries been *de schrik van geheel Sumatra en omliggende eilanden* — the terror of all Sumatra and the surrounding islands. With this specimen, he wrote, the audacity of the Acehnese was sufficiently placed in the light, *en kan men zich eens een voorstelling maken, tot welke daden dit volkje in staat is* — and one may form a conception of what deeds this little people is capable of.

The judgement is the author's own and belongs to the polemical frame of his introduction; see [[Beuming's account of the origin of the Atjeh-oorlog]].

**The Disarmament of Lam-Djamoe Batteries (12 June 1896)** — the disarmament and demolition of the siege batteries at [[Lam-Djamoe]] — was the formal decommissioning of the [[Vestingartillerie|fortress artillery]] batteries that had, since [[27 April]] [[1896]], maintained the sustained [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Umar's stellingen]] in the [[Lampoeng]] / [[Lampisang]] region. The batteries — equipped with mortars of 20 c.M., 12 c.M. A., and a 7 c.M. A. *veldbatterij*, and reinforced on [[27 April]] [[1896]] with 5 officers and 105 *minderen* of the *vestingartillerie* — were disarmed and demolished on [[12 June]] [[1896]], marking the end of the sustained bombardment of Umar's *stellingen* and the operational close of the siege phase of the spring [[1896]] campaign.

## Background

The [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] had begun on [[27 April]] [[1896]] — the same day as the [[Proclamation of Vetter (27 April 1896)|proclamation of the Regeeringscommissaris]] announcing military operations against [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]. The principal battery at [[Lam-Djamoe]] — the closest Dutch post to Umar's *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] — had been equipped with the heaviest siege artillery available in Atjeh: mortars of 20 c.M. (heavy bronze siege mortars, throwing a shell of substantial explosive content), 12 c.M. A. (mountain howitzers), and a 7 c.M. A. field battery. On [[27 April]] [[1896]] the battery had been reinforced with 5 officers and 105 lower ranks of the fortress artillery — a deliberate augmentation that brought the firepower of a siege train to what had been, until then, a forward post.

The bombardment had lasted, with intervals, for nearly four weeks. The 20 c.M. mortars had been directed against the interior of the works at [[Lam-Pisang]], [[Tjot Tjako]], and [[Boekit Seboen]], where their plunging fire could reach the defenders behind the parapets. The 12 c.M. A. howitzers had been used against the *bamboedoeri* abatis and the earthen walls. The 7 c.M. A. *veldbatterij* had been used for counter-battery fire and for harassing fire against the *kampong* edges. The bombardment had culminated in the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May]], in which the [[Slag bij Tjot Tjako (23 May 1896)|storming of Tjot Tjako]] on [[23 May]] [[1896]], the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's house]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]], and the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|bestorming of the Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]] on [[25 May]] [[1896]] had broken the Achehnese *stelling* and had driven Oemar's warriors behind the [[Kroeng-Raba]].

## The Disarmament of 12 June 1896

By the second week of [[June]] [[1896]] the strategic situation had been transformed. The *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] had been broken; the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] was complete; the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] had reduced the western redoubt. The sustained bombardment from [[Lam-Djamoe]] — which had served its purpose of softening up Umar's *stellingen* for the infantry assault — was no longer required. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] resolved to disarm and demolish the batteries.

On [[12 June]] [[1896]] the *vestingartillerie* batteries at [[Lam-Djamoe]] were disarmed and demolished. The 20 c.M. mortars, the 12 c.M. A. howitzers, and the 7 c.M. A. *veldbatterij* were dismounted, their emplacements were filled in, and their magazines were cleared. The 5 officers and 105 *minderen* of the *vestingartillerie* who had been dispatched to [[Lam-Djamoe]] on [[27 April]] [[1896]] were, presumably, either returned to their parent units or reassigned to other duties. The demolition of the batteries was a deliberate act: the Dutch were not merely suspending the bombardment but were dismantling the infrastructure that had sustained it, signalling that the siege phase of the spring campaign was at an end.

## Significance

The Disarmament of Lam-Djamoe Batteries on [[12 June]] [[1896]] was the operational close of the siege phase of the spring [[1896]] campaign and the formal end of the [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]]. The disarmament was, in tactical terms, a small matter — the dismounting of a few siege pieces and the filling-in of their emplacements. In strategic terms, however, it was a significant declaration: the Dutch had concluded that the sustained bombardment of Umar's *stellingen* was no longer required, and that the resources committed to the siege could be released for other operations. The decision was vindicated by the subsequent course of the campaign: the [[Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba Bridge (13 June 1896)|reconnaissance to the Kroeng Raba bridge]] of [[13 June]] [[1896]], the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] of [[16 June]]–[[17 June]] [[1896]], the [[Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|submission of Panglima Bintang]] of [[20 June]] [[1896]], and the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]] would all be conducted without the support of the siege batteries. The disarmament of [[12 June]] [[1896]] was, in this sense, both the close of one phase of the spring campaign and the precondition of the next — the release of the siege resources for the operations that would carry the spring campaign to its close on [[28 June]] [[1896]].

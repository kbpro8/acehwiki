**The Siak Contract of 23 June 1884** was the political contract by which the [[Siak|Siak sultanate]] formally transferred to the [[Netherlands-Indies Government|Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement]] all its residual claims over the [[Northern Onderhoorigheden|Noordelijke Onderhoorigheden]] of [[Siak|Siak]] from [[Panei|Panei]] northward as far as [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. Recorded by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] as the legal instrument by which the Dutch state succeeded to the last remaining Siak claims over the East Coast statelets — and hence to the formal responsibility for the boundary with [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — the [[1884]] contract was, in the Bureau's framing, the closing act of the long Siak-Dutch settlement that had begun with the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|tractate of 1 February 1858]].

## Background: the decay of Siak's "schijn" overlordship

The [[1884]] contract was the inevitable consequence of the long decay of effective Siak authority on the East Coast. The Bureau records that the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|1858 tractate]]'s confirmation of Siak's overlordship over the East Coast Dependencies had been "meer schijn dan wezen" — "more appearance than reality" — because "de landsvorsten zich steeds bleven verzetten tegen iedere daadwerkeljjke inmenging van dien kant in hunne aangelegenheden" — "the territorial princes continued to resist every actual interference from that side in their affairs." Repeated boundary disputes between the statelets — especially between [[Deli|Deli]] and [[Serdang|Serdang]], often settled by arms, without [[Siak|Siak]] being able to intervene — had demonstrated the hollowness of the Siak overlordship. The transfer of the Dutch administrative seat from [[Riouw en Onderhoorigheden|Riouw]] to [[Bengkalis|Bengkalis]] in [[1873]] (which Siak had ceded to the Government against an annual compensation of f 8000) had, the Bureau records, "onze directe invloed steeds meer gestegen" — "our direct influence steadily increased" — until the formal transfer of the residual Siak claims became the natural next step.

## The contract

The Bureau records the contract in a single, dense sentence: "bij het nieuwe contract met Siak, dd. [[23 June]] [[1884]], het Gouvernement alle aanspraken van dit rijk op de Noordelijke Onderhoorigheden van [[Panei|Panei]] af tot [[Acheh|Atjeh]] toe, overnam tegen betaling eener jaarlijksche schadeloosstelling van ƒ 40.000 en kwijtschelding van een schuld van ƒ 50.000" — "by the new contract with Siak, dated 23 June 1884, the Government took over all the claims of this realm over the Northern Onderhoorigheden from Panei onward as far as Atjeh, against payment of an annual compensation of ƒ 40,000 and the remission of a debt of ƒ 50,000."

The contract thus has four operative features. First, the Dutch Government took over — *overnam* — all Siak's residual claims over the Northern Onderhoorigheden. Second, the geographical extent of those claims was defined, at the southern limit, by [[Panei|Panei]] (so that the southern statelets were excluded from the transfer) and, at the northern limit, by [[Acheh|Atjeh]] (so that the transfer extended as far as the Atjeh boundary). Third, the compensation took the form of an annual payment of f 40,000. Fourth, an outstanding Siak debt of f 50,000 was remitted as part of the consideration.

## The "tot Atjeh toe" boundary

The Bureau's specification of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] as the northern limit of the transfer — "tot Atjeh toe" — is, in the long perspective of the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the most consequential feature of the [[1884]] contract. The "tot Atjeh toe" boundary fixed, for the first time, the entire northern stretch of the East Coast — from [[Panei|Panei]] to the [[Acheh|Atjeh]] boundary — as a Dutch sphere, with [[Siak|Siak]]'s residual claims extinguished. The boundary would, two decades later, be refined and demarcated by the [[1878 Langkat–Tamiang transfer|1908 attachment of Tamiang to Atjeh]] and the [[Boundary between Oostkust van Sumatra and Atjeh|1908 Oostkust-Atjeh boundary settlement]].

## The transfer of the administrative seat to Medan (1887)

The [[1884]] contract was immediately followed by the transfer of the administrative seat of the East Coast from [[Bengkalis|Bengkalis]] to [[Medan|Medan]] in [[1887]]. The Bureau records: "Sedert dat jaar werd ons gezag daar steeds krachtig gehandhaafd, vooral sedert de bestuurszetel in [[1887]] verlegd werd naar [[Medan|Medan]], het centrum dier, door de tabakscultuur zoo buitengewoon in belangrijkheid toegenomen, Noordelijke kuststaatjes" — "Since that year [[1884]] our authority there was always vigorously maintained, especially after the administrative seat was transferred, in 1887, to Medan, the centre of those Northern coastal statelets whose importance had so extraordinarily increased through the tobacco cultivation." The [[1884]] contract and the [[1887]] transfer together mark, in the Bureau's framing, the consolidation of Dutch effective authority over the entire East Coast from [[Panei|Panei]] to [[Acheh|Atjeh]].

## Significance

The [[1884]] contract is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the closing act of the long Siak-Dutch settlement of the East Coast. It transfers the last residual Siak claims over the Northern Onderhoorigheden to the Dutch state; it fixes, for the first time, the "tot Atjeh toe" boundary of the East Coast; and it provides the legal foundation for the [[1887]] transfer of the administrative seat to [[Medan|Medan]] and for the subsequent consolidation of Dutch effective authority over the entire East Coast. The contract is thus the diplomatic complement of the [[dutch-marine-expulsion-of-atjehers-from-langkat-(1866)|1866 expulsion of the Atjehers from Langkat]]: where the latter extinguished [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|Atjehnese overlordship over the East Coast]] by force, the former extinguished [[Siak|Siak]]'s residual claims by treaty.

**The troonrede of 1901** — the speech from the throne at the opening of the [[1901]] session of the [[Staten-Generaal]] — was the official proclamation of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical policy]]. Drafted under the [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] ministry, the troonrede gave an *ereplaats* — place of honour — to the "staatkunde van zedelijke verplichting" — policy of moral obligation — that [[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]]'s anti-revolutionary *[[Ons Program ([[1878]])|Ons Program]]* of [[1878]] had been the first to formulate in political form.

## The proclamation

The [[Troonrede of 1901|troonrede of 1901]] proclaimed that the entire government policy in the [[Nederlandsch-Indië|Indies]] was to be "doordrongen van het besef, dat Nederland tegenover de bevolking dezer gewesten een zedelijke roeping heeft te vervullen" — penetrated by the consciousness that the [[Netherlands]] owed a moral vocation to the population of these regions. The doctrine of the moral vocation of the [[Netherlands]] towards its colonial populations was soon adopted by the other political parties; "het zou trouwens niet denkbaar zijn, dat ze was afgewezen" — it would not have been conceivable, after all, that it should have been rejected.

## The Kuyperian source

For the Christian parties, especially the anti-revolutionaries, the *zedelijke roeping* was clear: the first Dutch duty was to bring to the Indies "die zegeningen van veiligheid en orde, die Nederland, sinds hier het licht der evangeliën doordrong, door Gods goede gunst ontving en, God zij lof, nog steeds bezitten mag" — those blessings of security and order that the [[Netherlands]], since the light of the Gospels penetrated here, has received by God's good grace and, praise God, still possesses. The second duty — preparing the Indies for greater autonomy — was more acceptable to the non-confessional parties than the first, with its Christian-zending subtext. The two duties together were the political seed of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical-policy]] state.

## The Atjeh context

The [[Troonrede of 1901|troonrede of 1901]] was proclaimed at the very moment the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] was being brought to its crisis. In [[1901]] the [[Netherlands|Dutch]] also undertook expeditions to [[Djambi]] and [[Korintji]]; in [[1904]] [[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]]'s [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|expedition to the Gajoe- and Alaslanden]] would complete the conquest of the sultanate. The conquest of Atjeh and the proclamation of the ethical policy were thus two faces of a single program: subjugation in the name of civilisation.

## The material consequences

The year [[1901]] also marked the beginning of concrete reforms. Misoogst and hongersnood in parts of [[Java]] had made it impossible to wait for the report of the [[Commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart ([[1902]])|commissie tot onderzoek van de mindere welvaart]] that would be installed in [[1902]]. The principal causes of the Indies poverty — population growth and the pressure that the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]] was exerting on the colonial budget — were already well known, even if not always openly acknowledged.

## Significance

The [[Troonrede of 1901|troonrede of 1901]] is the official proclamation of the [[Ethische Politiek|ethical policy]]. After [[1901]] the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]], the [[Van Daalen's Gajo-Alas-Batak expedition (1904)|Van Daalen expedition]], and the annexation of [[Djambi]]-[[Korintji]] ([[1901]]), [[Borneo]] ([[1904]]), [[Celebes]] ([[1905]]) and [[Bali]] ([[1906]]) all proceeded in the name of the moral vocation the troonrede had proclaimed.

**The First Aceh Expedition** of [[1873]] — the Dutch *Eerste Atjeh-expeditie* — was the opening military operation of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]. Commanded by the *generaal-majoor* [[J.H.R. Köhler|Köhler]], it landed at [[Koetaradja]] in [[April]] [[1873]] with the aim of punishing the Sultan of [[Aceh]] for the negotiations he had opened with foreign powers; it ended in failure within weeks. *Bij den aanvang van dien krijg hadden wij eene koloniale ervaring welke eene periode van bijna twee en een halve eeuw omvatte*, Zentgraff writes — Holland, with two-and-a-half centuries of colonial experience, was nevertheless *bad starters* in Atjeh, and the first expedition did not bring success.

## Köhler's death, 8 April 1873

On [[8 April]] [[1873]] *gen.-maj.* Köhler, advancing on the Sultan's *kraton* at Koetaradja, was killed beneath a tree at the edge of the *mesdjid*. The tree, long known as the *Köhler-boom*, marked the spot until [[1936]], when it was felled; a photograph of [[1936]] shows *de in 1936 gevelden Kohler-boom*. From the lower part of the trunk a table was made for the Atjeh-museum, on which stood — *op het papier* — *twee kogels, ongeveer ter manshoogte in de schors van den boom gevonden*: two bullets, found at about man's height in the bark, souvenirs of the firefight in which Köhler fell. The *mesdjid* itself was built at government expense; on a photograph of the *mesdjid te Koetaradja* one sees *geheel links: de Köhler-boom*.

## The failure and the Second Expedition

The failure of the first expedition was, Zentgraff writes, a Holland *op koloniaal terrein nog in de periode van het experimenteeren der beginnelingen*. The column withdrew; the war did not end. A second expedition followed late in [[1873]] under a new commander, and his *overwinningsberichten* — the victory bulletins of [[28 January]] [[1874]] (*"Alles doet gelooven dat de strijd geëindigd is. Panglima Polim en de Sultan schijnen den strijd te willen voortzetten, maar zullen niet kunnen"*), of [[6 February]] [[1874]] (*"Beneden met het volkskarakter zeggen, dat onderwerping van allen kan worden verwacht"*), and of [[13 February]] [[1874]] (*"Blijken, dat het volk den oorlog moede is en zich overwonnen acht, nemen toe"*) — were, Zentgraff writes, *het kunstmatig en tot het uiterste geforceerd optimisme van den bevelhebber, die behoefte heeft aan het snel afsluiten eener periode — zijn eigen periode — welke hij nog juist als eene succesvolle kan exposeeren, vóór de mislukking zich begint af te teekenen*.

The reality was otherwise: in [[1888]] — *nog tien jaren later, en het aspect is totaal veranderd* — [[Tengkoe Sjech Saman di Tiro|Tgk. Sjech Saman di Tiro]] could still write to the resident demanding that the governor embrace Islam as the price of avoiding *eene smadelijke verjaging uit Atjeh*. *Het was de realiteit van 1888 nà de snorkende overwinningsberichten van December 1873, Januari en Februari 1874*.

## Significance

The death of Köhler and the failure of the First Expedition set the pattern of the first twenty-five years of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]]: an oscillation between *geforceerd optimisme* in the bulletins of commanders, and the actuality of an undefeated enemy writing *beledigende brieven* from the *rimboe*. The *Köhler-boom* — table and bullets in the Atjeh-museum — was for decades the visible monument of that opening defeat, and only the offensive of [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] and [[G.C.E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] at the end of the century finally drew the line under it.

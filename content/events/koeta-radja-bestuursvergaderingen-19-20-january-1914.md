**The Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen** were the two final meetings of the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]], held at [[Koeta-Radja]] on the evening of [[19 January]] [[1914]] (5:30 PM) and the morning of [[20 January]] [[1914]] (9:30 AM). They were the only meetings held within the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] and were attended by the full administrative and military leadership of the territory, making them the most directly Atjeh-focused sessions of the entire reform consultation process. The minutes of these meetings (Notulen #15 and #16) constitute the primary documentary record of how Atjeh's own officials understood and responded to the proposed dissolution of their Gouvernement and its absorption into the new [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]].

## Attendees

The meetings were attended by the following officials, representing every level of the Atjeh administrative hierarchy:

- **[[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. N. A. Swart]]** — Luitenant-Generaal der Infanterie, Civiel en Militair Gouverneur van Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden (presiding host)
- **[[S. de Graaff]]** — Regeeringscommissaris voor de reorganisatie van het Bestuurswezen (chairing the consultations)
- **[[J. de Groot]]** — Hoofdambtenaar voor de reorganisatie van het Bestuurswezen
- **[[C. Lulofs]]** — Waarnemend Adviseur voor de bestuurszaken der Buitenbezittingen
- **[[W. F. J. Kroon]]** — Assistent-Resident ter beschikking van den Gouverneur van Atjeh
- **[[I. L. O'Brien]]** — Assistent-Resident der afdeeling Westkust van Atjeh
- **[[Th. J. Veltman]]** — Majoor der Infanterie, Civiel Gezaghebber van de onderafdeeling Pidië
- **[[H. J. Krugers]]** — Assistent-Resident der afdeeling Oostkust van Atjeh
- **[[W. Doornik]]** — Assistent-Resident van de afdeeling Groot-Atjeh
- **[[M. C. Roos van Raadshoven]]** — Assistent-Resident van de afdeeling Noordkust van Atjeh
- **[[F. W. A. L. de Nijs]]** — Controleur van de onderafdeeling Oeléë-Lhenë en de XXVI Moekims
- **[[L. Berkhout]]** — Controleur van de onderafdeeling Sinabang
- **[[V. A. Doeve]]** — Secretaris van het Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden
- **[[G. F. B. Watrin]]** — Kapitein der Infanterie, Civiel Gezaghebber van de onderafdeeling XXII Moekims

- **[[W. Wessel]]** — Civiel Gezaghebber van de onderafdeeling Tamiang

The presence of all four Assistent-Residenten (covering the four *afdeelingen* of Atjeh), all the Controleurs and Civiel Gezaghebbers of the *onderafdeelingen*, the Gouvernement Secretaris, and the military-civil Governor himself demonstrates the comprehensive nature of the consultation. Every official with direct administrative responsibility in Atjeh was present and given the opportunity to participate.

## First Meeting (19 January 1914, Evening)

The first meeting opened with Regeeringscommissaris [[S. de Graaff]] explaining the principles of *decentralisatie* and *zelfbestuur* that underlay the proposed reform. The critical moment came when Gouverneur [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. N. A. Swart]] declared that for the Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, the usual form of local *bestuur* (autonome lichamen / autonomous bodies) could NOT apply, because only [[Groot-Atjeh]] and part of the onderafdeeling [[Singkel]] were *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory), while the rest consisted of Inlandsche Zelfbesturen (Native Self-Governments / *landschappen*). This declaration was the decisive intervention of the entire Koeta-Radja consultation, as it established the fundamental constraint that would shape all subsequent decisions about Atjeh's governance future. De Graaff concluded that for Atjeh, only [[Gewestelijk Zelfbestuur]] (Regional Self-Governance) was feasible—the transfer of certain branches of *algemeen burgerlijk bestuur* (general civil administration) to lower organs at the gewest level, rather than the creation of autonomous local bodies.

When De Graaff asked what indigenous institutions existed in Atjeh comparable to Palembang's *marga's*, Gouverneur Swart provided a detailed explanation of the [[sagi]] system in Groot-Atjeh. He described three *sagi*'s as more or less comparable to Javanese *regentschappen*, the [[peleebalangschap]]pen as somewhat comparable to *districten*, and the [[moekimschap]]pen as comparable to *onderdistricten*. This systematic analogy with the well-known Javanese hierarchy allowed the reform commission to understand Atjeh's indigenous governance structure in terms they could relate to their broader reform framework.

## Discussion of Heredity and Head Appointments

Waarnemend Adviseur [[C. Lulofs]] asked whether the Atjeh population proposed candidates for Inlandsche hoofd (Native head) appointments or whether they were nominated by the European Bestuur. Gouverneur Swart explained that *erfelijkheid* (heredity) existed in the positions of Inlandsche hoofden, but that it was increasingly being deviated from—"meer en meer daarvan wordt afgeweken"—and that *bekwaamheden* (competence) for the office was gradually becoming the decisive factor in appointments. The population was heard in the appointment process, but their recommendations could be overridden if the *rechthebbende* (claimant / rightful heir) lacked the required qualifications. This explanation demonstrated that Atjeh's indigenous governance was not static but was undergoing a gradual transition from hereditary to merit-based principles, a transition that the colonial administration was actively encouraging.

## Second Meeting (20 January 1914, Morning)

The second meeting addressed several remaining topics. The discussion of the Gewestelijke Bestuursadviseur (Regional Administrative Advisor) noted that the Assistent-Resident ter beschikking (Assistant-Resident at disposal) already existed in Atjeh's Gouvernement as a precursor to this proposed position. The fusion of *bestuurskorpsen* (administrative corps) was discussed, with Gouverneur Swart asking whether Assistent-Residenten could serve alternately on Java and in the Buitenbezittingen. On the question of training Inlandsche ambtenaren, De Graaff stated that this should be "buiten bespreking" (left outside discussion) for Atjeh, given the existing *bestuursverhoudingen* (governance relationships)—a significant qualification that acknowledged the exceptional nature of Atjeh's administrative situation.

[[G. J. van Dongen]] asked whether Inlandsche ambtenaren (Native officials) could take seats in the proposed [[Gewestelijke Raad Noord-Sumatra]] (Regional Council), touching on the broader question of indigenous participation in colonial governance. Gouverneur Swart asked whether the *ambtelijk element* (official element) in the Gewestelijke Raad would be overly dominant, and whether the rank of [[Civiel Gezaghebber]] would be open to military Officers with ambition for the position. These questions reflected practical concerns about how the new governance structures would operate in the unique Atjeh context, where military officers routinely served in civil administrative positions and the indigenous governance system was still in transition.

## Proposed Administrative Division and Closing

The detailed administrative division of the new Noord-Sumatra gewest was presented and discussed, with Atjeh split into specific *afdeelingen*. All present agreed unanimously with the proposed division. The *representatiekosten* (representation expenses) for the Resident at [[Koeta-Radja]] were noted as necessary, reflecting the continued importance of the city as an administrative center even after its downgrade from Gouvernement capital to afdeeling seat. The closing statement by Gouverneur Swart was recorded in the minutes: he thanked De Graaff for the "heldere, zakelijke uiteenzetting" (clear, business-like explanation) of the pending reform plan and expressed the hope that this "grootsche plan" (grand plan) might soon be realized. This endorsement from Atjeh's highest official was the strongest possible confirmation that the territory's leadership accepted the proposed reforms.

## Significance

The Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen are among the most important documentary sources for understanding the colonial governance of Atjeh at a critical transitional moment. The minutes provide a detailed record of the indigenous administrative structures (the *sagi*-*peleebalangschap*-*moekimschap* hierarchy), the evolving relationship between hereditary and merit-based head appointments, the exceptional status of the Gouvernement Atjeh as a territory combining direct rule with Inlandsche Zelfbesturen, and the process by which Atjeh's own leadership consented to the dissolution of their Gouvernement and its absorption into a larger administrative unit. The unanimity of the agreement is particularly notable, suggesting either genuine confidence in the reform or a pragmatic recognition that resistance would be futile.

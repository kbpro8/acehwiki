**Van Heurn's Atjeh bird collecting expeditions of [[1920]]** were two successive ornithological collecting expeditions undertaken by the Dutch chemical engineer and naturalist [[F. C. van Heurn|Ir. Jhr. F. C. van Heurn]] in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the year [[1920]], the results of which were worked up and published by the ornithologist [[R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg|Mr. Dr. Baron R. C. E. G. J. Snouckaert van Schauburg]] in the *Jaarberichten* (Yearbook) of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] in [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]]. The first collection was made in the highland region of [[Takengon|Lake Takengon]] (1200 metres above sea level) in [[Gajo-land|Gajo-land]], and the second at the lowland estates [[Alas Peurba|Alas Peurba]] (200 metres) and [[Aloer Djamboe|Aloer Djamboe]] (50 metres) on the northeast coast of Atjeh in [[September]]–[[November]] [[1920]]. Together, the two collections gave a sample of the avifauna of Atjeh from the interior highlands to the coastal plain.

## The first collection: Lake Takengon and the Gajo-land

The first of Van Heurn's two Atjeh collections was made in the region of [[Takengon|Lake Takengon]] — the lake known to the Gayo as *Laut Tawar* — at an elevation of 1200 metres above sea level, in the highlands of [[Gajo-land|Gajo-land]] (Atjeh). The choice of the Takengon region reflected the scientific interest of the montane avifauna of the interior of Atjeh, which had been little collected by European ornithologists. The highland, montane character of the collecting locality distinguished this first collection from the second, and gave the two collections a complementary character: the first sampled the montane avifauna of the interior, the second the lowland avifauna of the coastal plain.

The first collection was described by Snouckaert in the *[[Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen|Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen]]* — *Avifaunal Studies in the Gayo Lands* — published in issue I (*afl. I*) of the previous *Jaarbericht* of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]]. The paper provided the map to which the second-collection paper later referred its reader.

## The second collection: Alas Peurba and Aloer Djamboe

Van Heurn returned to Atjeh later in the same year [[1920]] and collected for a second time, in the months [[September]]–[[November]] [[1920]]. The choice of localities for the second collection was deliberately different from that of the first: *Ditmaal viel de keuze op veel lager gelegen localiteiten meer nabij de N.O.-kust, te weten de ondernemingen Alas Peurba (200 M.) en Aloer Djamboe (50 M.)* — "This time the choice fell on much lower-lying localities nearer the northeast coast, namely the estates Alas Peurba (200 M.) and Aloer Djamboe (50 M.)."

The two estates stood under the management of the agency of the [[Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam|Handelsvereeniging Amsterdam]], which granted Van Heurn permission and all requested facilities. The collecting was done *op zwaar heuvelland, aan de grens van plantage en oorspronkelijk bosch* — "on heavy hill country at the boundary of plantation and original forest" — the transitional situation that the later, joint paper by Van Heurn and Snouckaert held to be the most productive for tropical collecting, since it gave access both to the typical primeval-forest species and to those of the cultivated zone.

### The chronology of collecting at Alas Peurba

At [[Alas Peurba|Alas Peurba]], Van Heurn collected from [[11 September]] [[1920]] — the date of the earliest specimens recorded in the second-collection paper, including specimens of the crested wood-partridge (*Rollulus roulroul*), the streak-breasted woodpecker (*Picus vittatus*), the forest wagtail (*Dendronanthus indicus*), and the black-headed bulbul (*Microtarsus melanocephalus*) — through [[September]] and into early [[October]] [[1920]]. The paper records specimens from a wide range of bird families collected at Alas Peurba, including the crested wood-partridge, the jambu fruit-dove (*Ptilinopus jambu*), the spotted dove (*Streptopelia chinensis tigrina*), the crested serpent-eagle (*Spilornis cheela bacha*), several kingfishers (including *Ceyx tridactyla* and *Halcyon concreta*), trogons, broadbills, and the garnet pitta.

### The chronology of collecting at Aloer Djamboe

At [[Aloer Djamboe|Aloer Djamboe]], Van Heurn collected through [[November]] [[1920]], with specimens dated from early through late November. The latest date recorded in the second-collection paper is [[25 November]] [[1920]] — the date of specimens of the crested serpent-eagle (*Spilornis cheela bacha*), the broad-billed roller (*Eurystomus orientalis*), the red-billed malcoha (*Zanclostomus javanicus*), the blue-fronted leafbird (*Chloropsis cyanopogon*), and the long-billed spiderhunter (*Arachnothera affinis*) at Aloer Djamboe. The paper records specimens from a wide range of bird families collected at Aloer Djamboe, including the crested serpent-eagle, the broad-billed roller, several cuckoos, woodpeckers, broadbills, bulbuls, and spiderhunters.

## Snouckaert's working up of the collection

After Van Heurn's return to the Netherlands — *onlangs in het vaderland teruggekeerd* — he asked Snouckaert to work up the second collection: *De Heer van Heurn, onlangs in het vaderland teruggekeerd, heeft mij verzocht ook deze verzameling te bewerken, van welke opdracht ik mij met genoegen kwijt* — "Mr. Van Heurn, recently returned to the fatherland, has asked me to work up this collection too, a commission which I have discharged with pleasure." Snouckaert followed the systematic order used by [[Herbert C. Robinson|Robinson]] and [[C. Boden Kloss|Kloss]] for their species list of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]], so that the Atjeh material could be compared readily with the wider Sumatran record. The paper is arranged as a taxonomic catalogue, family by family, with each entry giving the original binomial citation, the sex and number of specimens, the locality, and the date of collection.

The collection was deposited with the *Boekerij van het Koloniaal Instituut* — the Library of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] — in [[Amsterdam|Amsterdam]], as recorded on the final page of the paper.

## The *Pitta granatina vanheurni* specimen

The second collection is notable for the record of *Pitta granatina vanheurni* — a subspecies of the garnet pitta named after Van Heurn by [[C. Boden Kloss|Kloss]] — of which Snouckaert records a juvenile male collected at [[Alas Peurba|Alas Peurba]] on [[13 September]] [[1920]]. Snouckaert notes that the Sumatran form of the garnet pitta had only recently become known to science, and that Van Heurn's two (or three) specimens were among the very few then in collections. Van Heurn himself, however, on the basis of the material he had examined, was of the opinion that the distinction of the Sumatran form from the Malayan *coccinea* was not well founded — a difference of judgement between the collector and the ornithologist who named the subspecies.

## The later, joint paper

The two collections of [[1920]] furnished the material not only for the two papers by Snouckaert alone — the *[[Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen|Avifaunistische Studien in de Gajo-landen]]* and the *[[Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn|Over de tweede Atjeh-collectie van Jhr. F. C. van Heurn]]* — but also for the later, more comprehensive paper *[[Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh|Over eene collectie vogelhuiden van Oost-Atjeh]]*, co-authored by Van Heurn and Snouckaert, which described material collected on the same estates (Aloer Djamboe and Alas Peurba) and opened with the authors' argument that the richest field for tropical avifaunal collecting is the virgin primeval forest.

## Significance

Van Heurn's Atjeh bird collecting expeditions of [[1920]] are significant within the AcehWiki corpus as a document of the natural-historical research that was carried on in Atjeh in the early civilian period — after the [[Aceh War|Atjeh War]] and the [[pacification of Atjeh|pacification]] — by Dutch scientific collectors working through the estates and the research institutions of the colony. The two collections, made in complementary highland and lowland localities, gave a sample of the avifauna of Atjeh from the interior highlands to the coastal plain, and their publication in the *Jaarberichten* of the [[Koninklijke Vereeniging Koloniaal Instituut|Koloniaal Instituut]] is a document of the role of the Institute as the metropolitan venue for the publication of that research. The naming of *Pitta granatina vanheurni* after the collector is a document of the contribution of Van Heurn's collecting to the formal taxonomy of the Sumatran avifauna.

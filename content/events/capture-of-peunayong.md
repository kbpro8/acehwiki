The **Capture of Peunayong** took place on [[18 December]] [[1873]] when Dutch flank columns cleared a passage through coastal nipa swamps to occupy the strategic settlement of [[Peunayong]].

## Detailed Narrative

On [[18 December]] [[1873]], Colonel [[G. Wiggers van Kerchem]] and Lieutenant-Colonel [[Jacobus Pel]] led two infantry columns through dense, waterlogged nipa palm forests south of the Tiban Line. Simultaneously, Captain-Lieutenant Bunnik led 19 armed naval sloops carrying 600 marines up the Aceh River.

Overcoming heavy resistance and river obstacles, the infantry columns emerged from the swamps at 3:30 PM, occupying the deserted village of [[Peunayong]] on the eastern bank of the river. During the occupation, the grand residence of [[Teuku Kadli]], supreme judge of Aceh, caught fire, destroying the royal state archives.

Naval marines stormed an Acehnese earthwork along the river, establishing the permanent *Marinebenteng* post. The capture of Peunayong secured an inland river supply line for the [[Second Aceh Expedition]].

**The Michiels Monument** (Dutch: *Het Michielsmonument*) stood at the Waterlooplein in [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], commemorating [[Andreas Victor Michiels|General Andreas Victor Michiels]] ([[1797]]–[[1849]]), who was killed in action on [[Bali]] in [[1849]].

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — The Monument

The atlas depicts the Michiels Monument alongside the [[Atjeh Monument]] on a single plate, noting: "Het Michielsmonument aan het Waterlooplein te Weltevreden, Batavia." The accompanying text describes Michiels's career: he entered military service at seventeen, distinguished himself in every possible way, rose to General's rank, and fell on Bali in [[1849]].

## Significance for Achehnese History

While Michiels himself had no direct connection to Atjeh — he died twenty-four years before the first Dutch expedition in [[1873]] — the pairing of his monument with the [[Atjeh Monument]] in the atlas is illustrative. Together, the two monuments represented, in the physical landscape of the colonial capital, the long arc of Dutch military expansion across the archipelago: from the mid-nineteenth-century campaigns in Bali and elsewhere, to the eventual subjugation of Atjeh, the last great independent state in the Indonesian Archipelago to fall under Dutch control. Both stood in the administrative heart of Weltevreden as assertions of colonial authority built upon military sacrifice.

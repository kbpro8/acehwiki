**Slavernij abolition in Gajö- and Alaslanden** was the Dutch abolition of slavery in the [[Gajöland|Gajöland]] and [[Alasland|Alasland]] in [[1904]], announced at two grote hoofdvergaderingen (great head-assemblies) by overste [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]]. Slavery was effectively ended in the gewest [[Aceh|Atjèh en Onderhoorigheden]] by [[1908]] (K.V. [[1908]] kol. 117).

## The two hoofdvergaderingen

At two grote hoofdvergaderingen in [[1904]]:
- **[[2 June]] [[1904]]** — for [[Gajö Loeüs|Gajö Loeüs]], where the new stand of affairs was explained to the heads, including that all matters must go through the Këdjoeroen
- **[[29 June]] [[1904]]** — for [[Alaslanden|Alasland]], where overste [[Gotfried Coenraad Ernst van Daalen|Van Daalen]] announced the submission of Alasland to Dutch authority and recognition of the two Këdjoeroen's as supreme landshoofden

At both assemblies, Dutch authority announced that slavery would no longer be tolerated and all slaves freed.

## Transitional measure: pandelingschap

Conversion to *pandelingschap* (debt-bondage) with registration was used as a transitional measure (K.V. [[1906]]). This allowed a gradual transition from full slavery to a system where bonded labourers could eventually buy their freedom, rather than an abrupt emancipation that might disrupt the social order.

## Sources of slaves

Most slaves in Atjèh came from [[Nias|Nias]] (eigenlijk Atjèh) and the [[Bataklanden|Bataklanden]] (Gajö/Alas). Some Chinese slave-girls came from the Straits, and African slaves (**Abeusi**, from Arab. *habsji*) were brought from Mekka as a status symbol. Slaves could be bought, captured, or born to slave mothers.

## Atjèhnese slave terminology

Atjèhnese (teumön, namiët, hamba), Gayo (temoeloe, hömbö), and Alas (hambö) words for "slave" were distinguished from *pandeling* (*oereuëng doeë oetang*) who could buy freedom. Children of slave-mothers and free fathers were called *aneu' meuïh* ("children of gold"), treated as free by adat though their slave-origin was remembered. A formal *peusaban* (or *peusabé*, "gelijk-gemaakt") ceremony made a freed slave fully equal to a free man.

## Earlier abolition measures

The Stbl. [[1872]] N°. 114 made *pandelingschap* contrabande. **Raffles** had concluded a Nias contract in [[1820]] regarding slave trade. Slavery in the gewest was effectively ended by [[1908]].

**Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies** was the period from [[1942]] to [[1945]] during which the Imperial Japanese Army and Navy controlled the Dutch colonial territories in Southeast Asia, including the province of [[Aceh]]. The occupation ended the Dutch colonial administration and introduced a new regime that exploited local resources and labor for the Japanese war effort.

## Beginning of the occupation
After a series of swift amphibious assaults in early [[1942]], Japanese forces seized key ports and airfields throughout the archipelago. The surrender of the Dutch colonial government on 8 March [[1942]] marked the formal start of Japanese rule.

## Administration in Aceh
In Aceh, the Japanese established a military administration that imposed forced labor (romusha), requisitioned rice, and suppressed dissent. Local elites were co‑opted to assist the occupiers, while resistance movements began to form.

## Economic and social effects
The occupation disrupted the plantation economy that had been central to Dutch colonial exploitation of Aceh. Food shortages, forced conscription, and harsh disciplinary measures caused widespread hardship.

## Liberation
Allied forces, primarily the United States and Britain, began liberating the Dutch East Indies in late [[1944]] and early [[1945]]. Japanese surrender on 15 August [[1945]] ended the occupation, leading to the re‑establishment of Dutch authority briefly before the Indonesian National Revolution.

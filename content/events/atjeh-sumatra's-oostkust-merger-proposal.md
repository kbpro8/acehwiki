**The Atjeh-Sumatra's Oostkust merger proposal** was the plan discussed during the [[Bestuursreorganisatie Sumatra (1913-1914)]] to combine the [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] with the Residency of Sumatra's Oostkust (East Coast of Sumatra) into a single administrative gewest (region) to be called [[Noord-Sumatra (proposed gewest)]], with its capital at Medan. This was one of the most significant and controversial proposals affecting Atjeh in the entire reorganization process, as it would have ended Atjeh's long-standing status as a separately governed territory under a Civiel en Militair Gouverneur and placed it under the authority of a civilian Resident sharing power with the formerly distinct East Coast Residency.

## Initial Concerns

The merger was first raised as a potential difficulty at the Palembang meeting on [[12 August]] [[1913]], when Controleur [[J. Tideman]] explicitly warned about the moeilijkheden (difficulties) that might arise from combining territories of fundamentally different character. Tideman drew a parallel with another problematic proposed merger—Menado with its overwegend Christelijk element (predominant Christian element) being combined with the Gouvernement Celebes en Onderhoorigheden. His concern reflected a widely held view in the colonial service that Atjeh's unique governance structure—combining *rechtstreeks bestuurd gebied* (directly ruled territory) with Inlandsche Zelfbesturen (Native Self-Governments)—made its merger with the commercially oriented Oostkust, which had an entirely different administrative tradition based on plantation agriculture and private enterprise, deeply problematic. The fear was that the institutional incompatibility between Atjeh's military-civil hybrid governance and the Oostkust's civilian commercial administration would create friction and administrative confusion.

## De Groot's Reassurance

Hoofdambtenaar [[J. de Groot]] responded to Tideman's concerns with a revealing personal account. He admitted that he too had previously considered the *samenvoeging* (combination) of Atjeh with Sumatra's Oostkust as a difficulty—"ook spreker heeft eene samenvoeging van Atjeh met Sumatra's Oostkust te voren steeds als eene moeilijkheid beschouwd." However, after discussing the matter with an unnamed Assistent-Resident who was familiar with the Atjeh territory, De Groot reported that this official had declared that in his opinion no objections would exist against the merger. Furthermore, the Assistent-Resident believed that the Gouverneur van Atjeh would not raise any overwegende bedenkingen (overriding objections) either. De Graaff added that if one kept in mind that not every regulation of a general nature needed to be applied to every part of the gewest, the difficulties could be managed. This pragmatic argument—that uniform rules were not necessary across an administrative unit—became the operating principle for the merger proposal as it moved forward through the consultation process.

## Confirmation at Koeta-Radja

At the [[Koeta-Radja bestuursvergaderingen (19-20 January 1914)]], the merger proposal was effectively confirmed through the unanimous agreement of all Atjeh officials present to the proposed administrative division that incorporated Atjeh into Noord-Sumatra. Gouverneur [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. N. A. Swart]] himself closed the meetings by thanking De Graaff for the "heldere, zakelijke uiteenzetting" (clear, business-like explanation) and expressing hope that the "grootsche plan" (grand plan) would soon be realized. This endorsement from the Gouverneur of Atjeh himself was significant, as it represented the most authoritative confirmation that the merger was acceptable to the leadership of the territory that would be most affected by it.

## Border Adjustments

The specific border adjustments between Atjeh and the Oostkust were worked out in detail at the Medan and Koeta-Radja meetings. Resident [[Van der Plas]] proposed that [[Tamiang]] and [[Langsa]], both border areas on the eastern side of Atjeh, be transferred from Atjeh's jurisdiction to the Langkat afdeeling. The [[Alaslanden]] (Alas Lands) in the interior were proposed for transfer to the Deli afdeeling. Van der Plas stated that the precise addition of Atjeh areas to the Deli and Langkat afdeelingen should be left to the judgment of the Gouverneur van Atjeh, recognizing that local knowledge was essential for final border determinations. At the Koeta-Radja meeting, the Atjeh territory was divided into three *afdeelingen*: (1) Groot-Atjeh benevens de Westkust van Atjeh (pop. 210,000); (2) Noord- en Oostkust van Atjeh behalve Tamiang (pop. 480,000); and (3) the areas transferred to Langkat and Deli.

## Significance

The Atjeh-Sumatra's Oostkust merger proposal represented a fundamental reimagining of Atjeh's place within the colonial administrative structure. By proposing to dissolve the Gouvernement Atjeh and absorb it into a larger civilian-administered region, the reform implicitly acknowledged that the era of Atjeh as a special military-governed territory was drawing to a close—or at least that the colonial government aspired to bring it to a close. The fact that the merger was endorsed by the Gouverneur of Atjeh and all his senior officials, despite earlier concerns about institutional incompatibility, suggests that by 1914 there was genuine confidence within the colonial service that the pacification of Atjeh had progressed sufficiently to permit its integration into the regular civilian administrative framework. The transfer of border territories like Tamiang and the Alaslanden out of Atjeh's jurisdiction also reflected the practical recognition that these areas had stronger economic and social ties to the East Coast plantation belt than to the Atjeh highlands.

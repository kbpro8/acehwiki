**General Pel's Campaign in Aceh** was the series of aggressive interior military offensives directed by General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] of the [[Royal Netherlands East Indies Army]] between December [[1875]] and spring [[1876]], which broke out of the defensive perimeter around [[Kraton of Aceh]] to invade the agricultural heartlands of the [[IV Moekims]], [[VI Moekims]], and [[XXII Moekims]].

## Narrative of the Opening Offensive (December 1875)

On [[26 December]] [[1875]], General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] mobilized a strike force from an available garrison of 7,012 men. Resolving to punish Acehnese forces for harassing Dutch supply convoys near [[Kotta Alam]] and [[Oleh Karang]], Pel ordered two battalions of auxiliary troops ([[Barissans of Bangkallan]]), the [[6th Infantry Battalion]], and artillery to execute the [[Attack on Mibouw]].

Following the capture of Mibouw, column commander [[Lieutenant Colonel Meijer]] led the [[3rd Infantry Battalion]], the [[8th Infantry Battalion]], and the [[Marine Corps in Aceh]] in a dramatic mountain advance during the [[Crossing of Blankala Pass]], driving Acehnese defenders out of the [[IV Moekims]].

## The March through the XXII Moekims (February 1876)

In February [[1876]], General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]] expanded the offensive into the territory of [[Panglima Polem]]. On [[13 February]] [[1876]], a combined force comprising the [[3rd Infantry Battalion]], [[12th Infantry Battalion]], [[13th Infantry Battalion]], and Madurese auxiliaries launched the [[March through the XXII Moekims]]. 

As documented in *Atjeh gedurende de tweede expeditie en later* ([[1876]]), Dutch columns systematically destroyed bentengs and granaries across the sawahs ([[Sawah Cultivation and Supply in Aceh]]).

## Historiographical Significance

In contemporary debates, anonymous pamphleteers like "Brutus" ([[Open Letter by Brutus to General Knoop]]) used General [[J. L. J. H. Pel]]'s offensive successes in [[1875]] and [[1876]] to disparage General [[J. van Swieten]]'s earlier cautious strategy. 

However, veteran analysts demonstrated that [[J. L. J. H. Pel]]'s advance was possible only because [[J. van Swieten]] had already established secure coastal bases at [[Pekan Badak]] and [[Sinangri]], and that Pel's speed was still restricted by the [[Transport and Coolie System in Aceh]].

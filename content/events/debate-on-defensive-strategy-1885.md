**The debate on defensive strategy in 1885** centered on the question of whether the Dutch geconcentreerde stelling (concentrated position) in [[Groot-Atjeh]] should employ purely passive defense or allow limited offensive operations. Contemporary observers debated whether absolute passivity would undermine Dutch morale while emboldening the enemy, or whether offensive actions would violate the spirit of the concentration strategy.

## The Issue

The concentration decision of [[1884]] was fundamentally defensive. The posts were designed to hold their ground against attack; the troops were to remain within the line. However, the practical application raised questions about what "defensive" meant.

### Arguments for Pure Passivity

1. **Strategic Consistency:** The concentration was intended to reduce commitments, not create new ones. Going outside the line would undermine the strategic retreat.

2. **Force Protection:** Every soldier lost defending the position was a loss; every soldier lost on offensive operations was worse.

3. **Resource Constraints:** The reduced garrison could not spare forces for offensive action.

### Arguments for Limited Offense

1. **Morale:** Passive defense undermined the spirit of the troops, who had to endure attacks without being allowed to pursue the attackers.

2. **Enemy Initiative:** Absolute passivity ceded all initiative to the enemy, who could shell positions with impunity.

3. **Deterrence:** Occasional offensive action would demonstrate that the Dutch were not weak and would punish attacks.

## The Assessment

The author of the 1885 analysis supported limited offensive action as an exception ("hooge uitzondering"). When enemy bands continuously attacked from a particular area, or when robbers and murderers repeatedly slipped between posts to attack and return to their kampongs, it became a duty to attack the enemy on their own terrain and punish them.

Such operations, launched suddenly and withdrawing immediately after completion, could be supported by the nearby posts and would likely succeed with minimal losses. The damage to enemy morale would more than compensate for any losses suffered.

## Implementation

The policy that emerged combined:
- Strict defense of the perimeter
- Tolerance for limited offensive action in specific circumstances
- Aggressive patrolling and ambush operations
- Vigorous pursuit of infiltrators within the line

## Significance

The debate illustrated the tension between strategic clarity and tactical flexibility. The concentration was a strategic decision, but rigid adherence to its defensive logic could undermine its own purpose. The resolution—to allow limited offense while maintaining strategic defense—represented a pragmatic middle course.

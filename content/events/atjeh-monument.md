**The Atjeh Monument** (Dutch: *Het Atjeh-monument*) was a memorial erected in the Wilhelminapark at [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], in memory of the [[Pacification of Atjeh|pacification of Atjeh]]. It stood as one of the principal colonial monuments in the capital of the [[Netherlands India|Dutch East Indies]].

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — The Monument

The atlas depicts the Atjeh Monument on the same plate as the [[Michiels Monument]] and provides the following description: "Het Atjeh-monument in het Wilhelminapark te Weltevreden, Batavia. [...] Dit monument werd opgericht ter gedachtenis aan de pacificatie van Atjeh." (The Atjeh Monument in the Wilhelminapark at Weltevreden, Batavia. [...] This monument was erected in memory of the pacification of Atjeh.)

The Wilhelminapark, named after [[Queen Wilhelmina]], was one of the principal public spaces in the upper city (*bovenstad*) of Weltevreden, the administrative capital of the Dutch East Indies. The placement of the Atjeh Monument in this prominent location reflected the significance that the colonial government attached to the eventual subjugation of Acheh after decades of warfare.

## Context and Significance

The erection of a monument specifically commemorating the pacification of Atjeh, rather than a military victory, is significant in itself. By [[1926]], when the atlas was published, the narrative had shifted from conquest to pacification and orderly administration. This framing was consistent with the broader policy of the period, which emphasised the civilising mission and the benefits of Dutch governance. The monument stood alongside the [[Peutjot Cemetery]] near [[Kota Radja]] as the two principal physical memorials to the [[Aceh War]]: one in the imperial capital, the other on the battlefield itself.

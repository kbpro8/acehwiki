The Aceh War was the long armed conflict between the Sultanate of Acheh and the Dutch colonial government, beginning with the Dutch declaration of war and first attack in [[1873]] and continuing for decades as a guerrilla resistance led by ulamas and uleebalangs across the country. The war is documented extensively in the Hikayat Prang Gompeuni and produced many of Acheh's most celebrated religious-military leaders, including Teungku Tiro, Teungku Kuta Karang, Habib Abdurrahman Zahir, Habib Samalanga, and Teuku Uma. It effectively ended with the submission of the young pretender-Sultan Muhammad Dawot Shah to the Dutch in January [[1903]], though scattered resistance continued for years afterward.

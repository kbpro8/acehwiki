**The Second Selimoen Expedition** — *tocht naar Selimoen van 3 tot en met 6 November* [[1896]] — was the second major Dutch expedition into the [[Selimoen]] highlands of southern [[Groot-Atjeh]], undertaken between [[3 November]] [[1896|3]] and [[6 November]] [[1896]]. The expedition was undertaken in the transitional period between the [[Arrival of Van Vliet as Civiel en Militair Gouverneur (8 November 1896)|arrival of Van Vliet]] on [[8 November]] [[1896]] (which postdated the expedition) and the [[Handover of Command from Stemfoort to Van Vliet (17 November 1896)|formal handover of command]] of [[17 November]] [[1896]]. The expedition was a four-day reconnaissance-in-force, building on the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]] and the [[Capture of Kota Ba Teuē (28 October 1896)|capture of Kota Ba Teuē]] of [[28 October]] [[1896]], and was the last major operation of the second semester of [[1896]] before the [[Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh (December 1896)|cholera epidemic of December 1896]] paralysed Dutch military action.

## The Strategic Context

By early November [[1896]], the Dutch, under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s second tenure, had completed the [[Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri (28 October 1896)|temporary occupation of Indrapoeri]] and the [[Capture of Kota Ba Teuē (28 October 1896)|capture of Kota Ba Teuē]] on [[28 October]] [[1896]], and had undertaken the [[Trip to Ladoeng and Kroeng Raja (30–31 October 1896)|trip to Ladoeng and Kroeng Raja]] of [[30 October|30]]–[[31 October]] [[1896]]. The Selimoen highlands — the centre of Acehnese resistance in the south of Groot-Atjeh — had been the object of the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]], but had not been permanently occupied. The second Selimoen expedition of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]] was thus a reconnaissance-in-force — designed to consolidate the Dutch position in the Selimoen and to demonstrate the Dutch capacity for sustained force projection into the highlands.

## The Bridge over the Kroeng Pengapit (8 November [[1896]])

The second Selimoen expedition was followed, on [[8 November]] [[1896]], by the *slaan van een brug over de Kroeng Pengapit* — the construction of a bridge over the [[Kroeng Pengapit]] — which improved the Dutch lines of communication between the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) and the Blang Bintang / Selimoen region. The bridge — built by the *Genietroepen* (engineers) — was a significant logistical achievement, and reflected the Dutch commitment to the forward movement of the second semester of [[1896]]. The construction of the bridge on the same day as the [[Arrival of Van Vliet as Civiel en Militair Gouverneur (8 November 1896)|arrival of Van Vliet]] was a coincidence, but it symbolised the convergence of the military and administrative extensions of Dutch control which characterised the second semester of [[1896]].

## Significance

The Second Selimoen Expedition of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]] is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as the last major Dutch military operation of the second semester of [[1896]]. The expedition — undertaken in the transitional period between the Stemfoort and Van Vliet governorships — consolidated the Dutch position in the Selimoen and demonstrated the Dutch capacity for sustained force projection into the highlands. The expedition was followed, on [[8 November]] [[1896]], by the construction of a bridge over the Kroeng Pengapit — a logistical achievement which reflected the Dutch commitment to the forward movement of the second semester. The second Selimoen expedition thus closed the military operations of the second semester of [[1896]], and the [[Cholera Epidemic in Atjeh (December 1896)|cholera epidemic of December 1896]] would prevent the Dutch from undertaking any further operations until the new year.

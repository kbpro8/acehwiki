**Acehnese-Portuguese wars** (16th-17th centuries) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. Repeated Acehnese expeditions against Portuguese Malacca failed to dislodge the Portuguese from their overseas fortress, however great the effort. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to 16th-17th centuries.

## Account

Repeated Acehnese expeditions against Portuguese [[Malacca]] failed to dislodge [[Portuguese Traders|the Portuguese]] from their overseas fortress, however great the effort. Acehnese aggression peaked under [[Sultan Iskandar Moeda]], who drew on well-stocked state treasuries and framed the wars as against 'ongeloovigen' (unbelievers) - the simplest interpretation of Islamic religious propaganda. Atjeh as the longest-Islamized East Indian people fought against 'ungeloovige indringers' (unbelieving invaders) with massive religiously-based [[levées en masse]].

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

**The Pasé Expedition** was one of the great successes of the glorious period of Van Heutsz in the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]], the third-named of the expeditions and excursions that [[Musket]] lists in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* by Merac ([[1947]]). The Pasé Expedition was the expedition to the Pasé region of [[Atjeh|Aceh]] — the region of the [[Pasé Samoedrase rijk|Samudra Pasai kingdom]] — conducted under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] as part of the all-out offensive.

## The great successes of the glorious period

"the time also of our great successes! The Pidië expedition, the expeditions in Idi and Peusangan, the Pasé expedition, the Samalanga expedition, the expeditions to the Gayo and Alas lands, the action in the Tangsé and Geumpang area and on the North Coast of Atjeh, the patrol actions on the always restless West Coast, and finally the Battle of Batèë-Ilië, by which the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good." The Pasé Expedition is the third-named of the great successes, after the [[Pidië-expeditie]] and the expeditions in Idi and Peusangan.

## Significance

The Pasé Expedition is significant in *[[Oost-Indisch Journael]]* as one of the great successes of the glorious period of Van Heutsz. The book's argument — that the great successes of the glorious period were the expeditions and excursions by which the organised, general resistance in Atjeh was broken for good — is the book's frame for the offensive phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog|Aceh War]]. The Pasé Expedition is therefore one of the book's — and the colonial memory's — great successes of the glorious period.

## The crossing at Geudong

Fierce resistance was offered at the river. The far bank was strongly held, its key point being a fortified *missigit* (mosque), and to make matters worse the river was in *bandjir* (flood).

On [[21 June]] [[1899]], during the operations at [[Geudong]] in support of the *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]*, Swart's company of the 12th Battalion Infantry was sent to the front line. Halted by the greatly swollen Pasè river, and despite heavy enemy fire delivered at short range, he at once took energetic measures for a crossing, went over the river, gathered some seventeen soldiers about him on the right bank, and with great skill and boldness drove the superior enemy from his position to the south by a flank attack with heavy loss, thereafter maintaining himself in the captured position pending the arrival of further troops.

Swart's own narrative added the details omitted from the official citation. Working himself onto dry land from an improvised raft, he found beside him — to his surprise — lieutenant [[Mac Gillavry]] of Van Heutsz's staff, who had come along without his knowledge. Their little rafts happened to reach a stretch of the enemy position that was unoccupied. They worked their way cautiously and slowly along the bank in the direction of the *missigit* and reached the edge of the fortification, where Swart carefully posted his men. When all had recovered their breath he gave the signal *"voorwaarts"*: they sprang forward, over the wall of the *missigit* and into the midst of a startled garrison certainly a couple of hundred strong. The surprise had robbed the defenders of their courage; after a short but violent fight the garrison fled and left the *missigit* to Swart and his eighteen men.

At that moment Swart saw Van Heutsz standing with his staff on the far bank and heard him shout: "who is that commander there and with how many men is he posted?" Swart shouted back: "Captain Swart with eighteen men, but he requests reinforcement!" The reinforcement was got across.

## The action on the river fortification

In a further engagement of the same campaign, Swart's battalion advanced against a fortification lying on the river. "We were of course heavily fired upon," he recalled, "and I remember how at a certain moment, when we were at the fortification, I pushed someone who was standing in front of me through the fence and at once gave the order to try to get onto the entrenchment. That succeeded, and we even pressed on over the entrenchment, and then the enemy, who could go nowhere else, was free to swim for it."

During that fight Swart suddenly felt something against his belly and found he had received a *contourschot* which had not penetrated. The doctor treated it in the evening, after his wounded had been dressed: "you got off lightly there," he said.

## Related actions of 1899

Two further wounds sustained by Swart in the operations of [[1899]] are recorded in his service record: at the storming of the *kampong* [[Bambi]] in [[Pidië]], a grazing shot above the right temple on [[8 May]] [[1899]]; and at the taking of the enemy positions on the [[Glé Risé]], a blood-blister on the belly from a thrown stone on [[21 November]] [[1899]]. At the Glé Risé he led his company "with judgement and with great intrepidity" in the attack on a very strong and stubbornly defended position.

For his distinction in the operations in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] between [[1 June]] [[1899]] and [[31 December]] of that year, Swart was promoted Knight 3rd Class of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]] by royal decree of [[13 July]] [[1900]] no. 29.

## Significance (du Croo, 1943)

The Pasè expedition belongs to the phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog]] in which [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]], as Governor, drove the campaign forward at a tempo his predecessors had not attempted. For [[H. N. A. Swart]] personally it converted a dispirited *Jannen*-company of the 12th Battalion into an élite unit and confirmed the reputation as a fighting officer on which his subsequent administrative career was built.

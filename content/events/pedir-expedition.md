The **Pedir Expedition** was a military campaign carried out by Dutch forces under kolonel (later generaal) [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] against the Pedir region on **[[Aceh]]**'s north-east coast, beginning in June [[1898]], as part of the broader **[[Aceh War]]**. The defected Atjehnese chief [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] marched with his strijders on Pedir in the campaign's course. The Jesuit missionary **[[H. C. Verbraak S.J.]]** reported on the expedition — under the year [[1899]] — in his article "Atjeh. Pedir-expeditie" in the magazine **[[St. Claverbond]]**, while the Dutch primary-school reader *[[De Kleine Oosterling]]* (1901) records it as the operational turning point that ended Pedir's 25-year role as a *wijkplaats* (refuge) for Dutch enemies.

## Context

After the initial Dutch conquest of **[[Kutaradja]]** in [[1874]], the war devolved into a prolonged guerrilla conflict. The Pedir area, with its rugged terrain and loyalist population, served as a refuge for resistance fighters for some 25 years. The expedition aimed to clear the region of insurgents and secure Dutch control over the coast.

## The defection of Toekoe Oemar

The [[De Kleine Oosterling|Wijma school reader]] records the defection of [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] as the operational context of the Pedir expedition: "Ook na het vertrek van den held van Samalangan werd in Atjeh nog veel gestreden. Vooral Toekoe Oemar gaf ons veel te doen. Dit Atjehsch hoofd had ons eerst als bondgenoot goede diensten bewezen, maar sloot zich later weer bij den vijand aan." The school reader thus compresses the [[Defection of Teuku Uma|verraad of Toekoe Oemar of 1896]] into a single sentence — without date and without explicit reference to the [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad van 1896]] — and frames Toekoe Oemar as the *Atjehsch hoofd* whose defection was the principal operational difficulty of the late [[1890s]].

## The June 1898 expedition

The school reader records the June [[1898]] Pedir expedition as the operational turning point: "Hij begaf zich met het grootste deel van zijn strijders naar Pedir. Pedir was al 25 jaar lang een veilige wijkplaats voor onze vijanden geweest. De Atjehers noemden Groot-Atjeh het hoofd, maar Pedir het hart van Atjeh. Nu begon in Juni 1898 onder kolonel Van Heutsz een veldtocht, die een goeden uitslag had." The expedition was thus conducted under kolonel Van Heutsz's command, with Toekoe Oemar's *strijders* marching on Pedir. Its "goeden uitslag" (good outcome) ended Pedir's 25-year role as a *wijkplaats* for Dutch enemies, and established the Van Heutsz–Toekoe Oemar partnership as the operational instrument of the late Atjeh-oorlog.

The school reader's recording of the Atjehnese proverb — "De Atjehers noemden Groot-Atjeh het hoofd, maar Pedir het hart van Atjeh" — is its principal instance of the Atjehnese onomastic tradition of personified geography: Groot-Atjeh as the *hoofd* (head) and Pedir as the *hart* (heart) of Atjeh. The capture of the *hart* by the Van Heutsz–Toekoe Oemar expedition is thus framed as the capture of the operational centre of the Atjehnese resistance.

## Verbraak's account

The operation also involved a combined force of infantry and artillery, supported by naval units, as recorded by the Jesuit missionary [[H. C. Verbraak S.J.|Verbraak]] under the year [[1899]] in *[[St. Claverbond]]*. Verbraak's account described the march, skirmishes, and the challenges of campaigning in the tropical environment, bringing these events to the attention of Catholic readers in the Netherlands. The expedition was part of the ongoing "pacification" efforts that would continue until [[1904]].

## Significance

The Pedir Expedition of June [[1898]] is, in the [[De Kleine Oosterling|school-textbook]] narrative, the principal instance of the Van Heutsz–Toekoe Oemar partnership that would close the Atjeh-oorlog, and the campaign that ended Pedir's 25-year role as a *wijkplaats* for Dutch enemies. It was also one of many small-scale operations that characterised the later phase of the Aceh War, demonstrating the Dutch commitment to controlling the entire coastline and illustrating the difficulties of suppressing a determined guerrilla insurgency. The school reader's emphasis on the expedition's *goeden uitslag* — and on the Atjehnese proverb of the *hoofd* and the *hart* — frames the Pedir Expedition as the operational turning point of the late Atjeh-oorlog, and as an instance of the Dutch operational consolidation that would lead, within a few years, to the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacification]]'s completion.


## Account from *Vierhonderd jaren missie* (1934) — the chaplaincy extension to Pedir

**The Pedir Expedition of [[1898]]** was the military campaign conducted by Colonel [[J. B. van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] — newly appointed [[Governor of Atjeh|Governor of Atjeh]] — against the [[Pedir|Pedir]] (Pidie) region of the north coast of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] in the course of the renewed Dutch offensive that, under Van Heutsz's command, brought the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] to its military conclusion. The expedition is documented in the [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume]] through the episode of the chaplain [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] — whose extension of ministry from [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] to [[Pedir|Pedir]] was a direct consequence of the expedition — and it marks the operational opening of Van Heutsz's command and the formal resumption of the offensive after the [[Concentration system in Atjeh|concentration system]] of the late [[1880s]] and early [[1890s]].

### The 1898 command and the planned expedition

The [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume]] records the expedition in the chapter on [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor Verbraak]]'s chaplaincy, in the context of the [[1898]] governorship of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]:

> *"Toen in 1898 de toenmalige kolonel van Heutsz als gouverneur van Atjeh optrad, deelde deze mede dat er bij de regeering te Batavia het plan had bestaan om aan de ophanden zijnde Pedir-expeditie afzonderlijke geestelijken toe te voegen. En verder dat van dien maatregel was afgezien omdat hij, door jarenlange ondervinding, overtuigd was dat de predikant, en vooral de algemeen beminde pastoor Verbraak hun geestelijke werkzaamheden wel zouden willen verdeelen tusschen Groot-Atjeh en Pedir."*

The episode fixes three facts: (1) that a Pedir-expedition was planned for [[1898]]; (2) that the original plan had been to attach separate chaplains to it; and (3) that this measure was set aside in favour of distributing the existing predikant and the universally beloved pastoor Verbraak between [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and [[Pedir|Pedir]]. The decision is itself a document of the structural dependence of the colonial military chaplaincy on a small circle of trusted clerics — and, more specifically, of the institutional weight of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Verbraak]]'s reputation in the operational planning of the campaign.

### Operational context

The Pedir-expedition of [[1898]] was conducted in the immediate aftermath of the death of [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] at [[Melaboeh|Melaboeh]] and the partial successes of [[General Vetter|General Vetter]]'s command with *onbeperkte volmacht* (unlimited authority). Van Heutsz's expedition against [[Pedir|Pedir]] — the region of the north coast of Atjeh that the [[Atlas van Nederland en de Indiën (1930)|*Atlas van Nederland en de Indiën*]] identifies as the homeland of the *Pederse* (Pidie) variant of the *poephon blang* field-consecration rite — extended the renewed offensive to the north coast and prepared the way for the definitive subjugation of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] in the years [[1899]]–[[1903]].

### The chaplaincy extension

Verbraak did not refuse the extension of his ministry to [[Pedir|Pedir]]. The mission jubilee volume records that "*Pastoor Verbraak nam Pedir er bij*" ("Pastoor Verbraak took Pedir on as well"). The addition of Pedir to Verbraak's existing Groot-Atjeh responsibilities is the documentary fact that fixes the chaplaincy dimension of the expedition: the same priest who had served [[Koeta Radja|Kotta-Radja]] since [[1874]] and the [[Olehleh|Olehleh]] chapel since [[1896]] now extended his ministry to the troops of the [[Pedir|Pedir]] campaign. The episode is the last major documented extension of Verbraak's chaplaincy before his [[1908]] departure from Atjeh.

### Significance of the chaplaincy-account

The Pedir Expedition of [[1898]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history, in the mission-jubilee account, on three counts. First, it was the operational opening of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]'s command — the campaign that inaugurated the renewed Dutch offensive which, over the following five years, brought the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] to its military conclusion. Second, it extended the renewed offensive to the [[Pedir|Pedir]] (Pidie) region of the north coast — a region of distinctive Atjehnese ritual practice (the *Pederse* variant of the *poephon blang* field-consecration rite) and of strategic importance as the northern approach to [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]]. Third, it is the documented occasion of the extension of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Verbraak]]'s chaplaincy from [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] to [[Pedir|Pedir]] — a documentary fact that fixes the institutional dependence of the colonial military chaplaincy on a single trusted figure, and that marks the limits of the chaplaincy's capacity to scale to the renewed offensive.

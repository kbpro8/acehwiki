**The Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba Bridge (13 June 1896)** was the armed reconnaissance conducted by a colonne under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. G. A. Hansen]] — comprising the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e]] and [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljons]], one cavalry peloton, two sections of *bergartillerie*, one detachment of *genietroepen*, two ambulances, and the train — to the bridge over the [[Kroeng Raba]] on [[13 June]] [[1896]], in the immediate aftermath of the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] and the [[Disarmament of Lam-Djamoe Batteries (12 June 1896)|disarmament of the Lam-Djamoe batteries]] of [[12 June]] [[1896]]. Various points along the way were occupied; no resistance was encountered. The *genietroepen* blew up old cannons at [[Lam-Poelau]]. The colonne returned to [[Lam-Djamoe]]. In [[Lho-Nga]] women were again found in the *kampong*.

## Background

By the second week of [[June]] [[1896]] the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] had accomplished the principal military objectives of the spring campaign. The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]]; the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]] had reduced the western redoubt; and the [[Disarmament of Lam-Djamoe Batteries (12 June 1896)|disarmament of the Lam-Djamoe batteries]] of [[12 June]] [[1896]] had marked the end of the sustained bombardment of Umar's *stellingen*. The strategic situation, however, remained fluid: Oemar himself had retreated behind the [[Kroeng-Raba]] — the river that formed the western boundary of the operational area — and the disposition of his warriors in the country beyond the river was imperfectly known.

The [[Kroeng Raba]] bridge — the principal crossing of the river — was the key to any future Dutch operation against Oemar's coastal base at [[Lepong]]. The bridge had been the objective of the [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai]] of [[1 May]]–[[4 May]] [[1896]] and of the subsequent naval bombardments of [[Lepong]] on [[25 May]], [[4 June]]–[[5 June]], and [[8 June]]–[[9 June]] [[1896]]. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] now resolved to send an armed reconnaissance to the bridge — both to gather intelligence on the disposition of the Achehnese on the western bank and to test the Achehnese reaction to a Dutch advance toward the river.

## The Composition of the Colonne

The reconnaissance colonne was placed under [[Luitenant-Kolonel G. A. Hansen|Lt.-Col. G. A. Hansen]] — the same officer who had commanded the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] at the [[Machtsvertoon in IX Moekims (29 May 1896)|machtsvertoon in the IX Moekims]] of [[29 May]] [[1896]] and who would command the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] at the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]]. The composition was: the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]] and the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]] — two of the principal infantry battalions of the spring campaign; one cavalry peloton; two sections of mountain artillery; one detachment of engineers; two ambulances; and the train. The total force was substantial — a reinforced brigade — for what was, in formal terms, a reconnaissance. The strength of the colonne reflected both the importance of the objective and the residual risk of Achehnese counter-attack in the country between [[Lam-Djamoe]] and the [[Kroeng Raba]].

## The Course of the Reconnaissance

The colonne advanced from [[Lam-Djamoe]] toward the [[Kroeng Raba]] bridge on [[13 June]] [[1896]]. Various points along the way were occupied in succession — the Dutch moving methodically through the *kampong* belt and the rice-fields that lay between [[Lam-Djamoe]] and the river. No resistance was encountered. The Achehnese, recognising the strength of the Dutch colonne and the futility of opposing it in the open, declined to contest the advance. The cavalry peloton reconnoitred ahead; the infantry occupied the key points; the *bergartillerie* was held in reserve.

The principal engineering task of the day fell to the *genietroepen*, who blew up old cannons at [[Lam-Poelau]] — presumably Achehnese pieces captured in the [[Bestorming van de Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau (25 May 1896)|bestorming of the Stelling Daman–Lam Tjroeng–Lam Poelau]] of [[25 May]] [[1896]] or abandoned in the subsequent Dutch occupation of the position. The demolition of the old cannons was a small but symbolic act: the Achehnese artillery — never a major factor in the war, but a visible symbol of the resistance — was being systematically destroyed by the Dutch engineers.

The colonne reached the [[Kroeng Raba]] bridge, conducted its reconnaissance, and returned to [[Lam-Djamoe]]. The intelligence gathered was, in tactical terms, unremarkable: the Achehnese had withdrawn behind the river; the bridge was intact but the western bank was held in sufficient strength to make a crossing costly. In [[Lho-Nga]] — the coastal *kampong* through which the colonne passed on its return — women were again found in the *kampong*. The detail is preserved by Kruisheer as a sign of the partial return of the civilian population to the *kampong*s that had been evacuated during the spring campaign — a small but significant indicator of the slow normalisation of life in the wake of the Dutch operations.

## Significance

The Reconnaissance to Kroeng Raba Bridge on [[13 June]] [[1896]] was, in operational terms, a small affair — an armed reconnaissance to the river with no engagement and no casualties. In strategic terms, however, it was an essential step in the consolidation of the Dutch position after the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]]. The reconnaissance confirmed that the Achehnese had withdrawn behind the [[Kroeng-Raba]] — a strategic decision that, while it shortened the Achehnese lines, also confirmed that the Dutch held the initiative. The demolition of the old cannons at [[Lam-Poelau]] by the *genietroepen* was a small but symbolic act of destruction — the systematic dismantling of the Achehnese military infrastructure in the operational area. The finding of women in the *kampong* of [[Lho-Nga]] was, in its way, the most politically significant outcome of the reconnaissance: it suggested that the civilian population was beginning to return to the *kampong*s that had been evacuated during the spring campaign, and that the political realignment — confirmed three days later by the [[Disarmament of Buitenlinie Chiefs (mid-June 1896)|disarmament of the buitenlinie chiefs]] and a week later by the [[Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|submission of Panglima Bintang]] — was under way. The reconnaissance of [[13 June]] [[1896]] was, in this sense, both a military and a political operation: a test of the Achehnese disposition on the [[Kroeng-Raba]] and a confirmation of the slow return of the civilian population to the operational area.

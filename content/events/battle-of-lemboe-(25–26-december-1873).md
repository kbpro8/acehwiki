**The Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)** was the first major engagement of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Aceh expedition]] on land, fought on [[25 December|25]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]] when strong Dutch columns under [[Jan van Swieten|opperbevelhebber van Swieten]] sallied south from the bivouak of [[Penajoeng]] in search of a grasvlakte on which to deploy, ran into heavily entrenched Atjehnees positions at the kampong of [[Lemboe]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]], and after a "bloedigen strijd" carried the position at the cost of nearly a hundred and fifty dood en gekwetsten — only for van Swieten, "onbegrijpelijk genoeg," to order the captured ground immediately abandoned.

## The Situation at Penajoeng

The [[Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)|landing at Pedro-punt]] of [[6 December]] [[1873]] had placed the expeditionary force on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]] at [[Penajoeng]], some 1200 passen from the great fortified [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] that guarded the northern approach to the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]]. For eight days after the landing the army sat idle in camp. The Atjehneezen, Borel insists, used every hour of that respite: when the Dutch finally stirred, they found that the enemy "had zich overal ingegraven." Trenches, [[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboe-doeri]] hedges, schietkokers and versterkingen had sprung up across the entire face of the country between Penajoeng and the Kraton. The high reeds and the palm groves gave the defenders every advantage of an ambush country.

## The Action of 25–26 December

On [[25 December]] [[1873]] strong Dutch columns rukten zuidwaarts to find a grasvlakte on which the troops might be exercised and the veldgeschut deployed. They hit the enemy stellingen at [[Lemboe]] — a kampong on the right bank of the river — and were "van alle zijden hevig beschoten." For hours the Dutch infantry were exposed on the open [[Sawah|sawah]] to what Borel calls "moordend vuur." The columns pressed on, and after a "bloedigen strijd" the kampong was carried on [[26 December]]. Dutch losses were severe: "nagenoeg 150 gesneuvelden en gekwetsten." The enemy's losses are recorded as "vermoedelijk slechts onbeduidend," since the Atjehneezen fired from cover and retired swiftly when their positions became untenable.

## The Abandonment

What happened next is, in Borel's account, one of the most inexplicable decisions of the campaign. The captured ground at Lemboe — paid for in Dutch blood — was ordered abandoned the very day it was taken. The explanation, so far as one can be reconstructed, lay in van Swieten's horror of further casualties and his "gruwel van bloedvergieten": a forward post at Lemboe would have to be garrisoned, supplied, and defended against nightly attacks, and van Swieten, Borel writes, "stak het zwaard voor goed in de scheede" rather than accept the burden. The consequence was decisive. By abandoning Lemboe the Dutch surrendered the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]] to the enemy, and the position would have to be [[Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)|re-fought in November]] [[1874]] at fresh cost by [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]]. The same pattern — capture and immediate abandonment — would recur at the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|expedition to Bital]] and the [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|expedition to Ketapang Doewa]] two months later. Borel treats the abandonment of Lemboe as the first clear signal of the [[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]] that would dominate the rest of van Swieten's command.

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft describes the sharp fighting around the fortified villages of Lemboe and Langgoep on Christmas Day and Boxing Day [[1873]] as the first serious blooding of the second expedition after the landing. On [[25 December|25 December]] the columns pushing south-east from the beach-head were repeatedly attacked from the *kampongs* and *bentings* that screened the approach to Penajoeng; 1st Lieutenant Schoenmarckers was killed at the head of his company in the assault on Lemboe. On [[26 December|26 December]] Captain La Fors was killed during a reconnaissance out from Penajoeng. The actions convinced Van Swieten that Acehnese resistance was stiffening rather than collapsing and that the fortified line between the beach and the missigit would have to be reduced methodically.

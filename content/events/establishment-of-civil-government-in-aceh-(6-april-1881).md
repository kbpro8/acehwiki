**The Establishment of Civil Government in Aceh** on [[6 April]] [[6 April]] [[1881]] was the separation of the civil and military commands in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] by the Governor-General [[J. W. van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] and the appointment of [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|A. Pruys van der Hoeven]] as civil governor of the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, with Colonel [[J. F. Haus|J. F. Haus]] taking the military command. The establishment of civil government was preceded by the administrative reorganisation drafted by the commission of [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] and Pruys van der Hoeven in [[1880]]-[[1881]], and by the mission of [[T. H. der Kinderen|T. H. der Kinderen]] to Atjeh in [[November 1880|November 1880]]-[[February 1881|February 1881]] for the reorganisation of justice in the buitenbezittingen. It was one of the last administrative acts of Van Lansberge, who had himself requested discharge for health reasons.

## Background

After the close of large-scale operations in Great Acheh in [[August 1879|August 1879]] the Dutch government initiated the definitive administrative reorganisation. [[K. van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]] was placed in commission with [[A. Pruys van der Hoeven|A. Pruys van der Hoeven]], last resident of [[Palembang|Palembang]], as government commissioners. They were charged, in joint consultation, "to arrange and propose everything necessary to arrive at an adequate organisation of the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden, in connection with the political situation and the relation of the various Onderhoorigheden to our administration." The assistant-resident of Groot-Atjeh was attached to them as secretary for that assignment.

Pruys van der Hoeven arrived in Atjeh on [[21 February]] [[21 February]] [[1880]] and visited in succession the territory occupied by the Dutch, both in Great Acheh and in the vazal staten. On [[10 July]] [[10 July]] the commissioners declared their task ended. After in an extraordinary meeting of the [[Council of Netherlands India|Raad of Nederlandsch-Indië]] with both commissioners a further discussion had taken place, and the Governor-General had taken several decisions in preparation, the governor was yet charged with the submission of new proposals regarding the organisation of the inland administration in Great Acheh, based on an investigation into the existing situation and the institutions under the sultans. He was to consult with the member of the Raad of Nederlandsch-Indië, [[T. H. der Kinderen|Mr. T. H. der Kinderen]], who would also come to Atjeh as government commissioner for the reorganisation of justice in the possessions outside Java and Madura, to draft proposals concerning the regulation of justice there. Der Kinderen arrived in Atjeh in [[November 1880]] and remained there until [[February 1881]].

## The Administrative Organisation of March 1881

In accordance with the proposals submitted, the definitive administrative organisation of Atjeh came into force in [[March 1881|March 1881]]. According to this organisation, the administration of the gewest Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden would rest with a governor, whose residence would be at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. Under him would stand three assistant-residents and ten controleurs. The assistant-residencies were:

1. that of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] with [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]] as chief place, in which direct Dutch administration was introduced;
2. that of the [[Noord- en Oostkust|Noord- en Oostkust]] with [[Telok Semawe|Telok Semawé]] as chief place, with controleurs at [[Edi|Edi]], [[Samalanga|Samalanga]] and [[Segli|Segli]];
3. that of the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust van Atjeh]] with [[Malaboeh|Malaboeh]] as chief place.

To the landscapes belonging to the two last afdeelingen, self-government was left. In Groot-Atjeh the inland administration would be conducted by district-heads or [[Uleebalang|hoeloebalangs]], to be appointed by the Governor-General; their replacements and helpers, as well as the moekim-heads, imams, were appointed by the governor; the replacements and helpers of the imams, as well as the kampong-heads, ketjihiks, by the assistant-resident; the heads of the wards, mandarsé's, by the controleur.

## The Separation of Commands

By the Governor-General's decision, by virtue of authority already granted by the King in [[1879]], it was decided to separate the civil and military commands and to entrust the civil administration in Atjeh to a civil governor. The Lieutenant-general Van der Heyden was honourably discharged from his functions, and Pruys van der Hoeven was appointed civil governor; the military command was entrusted to Colonel [[J. F. Haus|J. F. Haus]] of the infantry. Both assumed their functions on [[6 April]] [[6 April]] [[1881]].

## Significance

The discharge to Van der Heyden — "which set many pens in motion, and also gave rise to discussions in the [[Staten-Generaal|States-General]]" — was one of the last administrative acts of Governor-General Van Lansberge. For health reasons he had requested discharge in the spring of [[1881]], which was granted by Royal Decree of [[24 November]] [[24 November]] [[1880]]; on [[12 April]] [[12 April]] [[1881]] he handed over his dignity to his successor. The establishment of civil government marked the formal transition of Atjeh from a military command to a regular administrative gewest of Netherlands India — though the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] was by no means over, and was to continue for more than two decades in the form of guerrilla resistance, especially in the upper lands of [[Pedir|Pedir]] and the [[XXII Moekims|XXII Moekims]].

**The Campaign against the XXII Moekims** of [[23 March]] [[1879]] – [[9 July]] [[1879]] was the Dutch military campaign in the [[XXII Moekims|Sagi der XXII Moekims]] of [[Acheh|Atjeh]], led by the governor [[K. van der Heyden|Major-general K. van der Heyden]] personally, which began with the [[Capture of Indrapuri (24 March 1879)|capture of Indrapuri]] on [[24 March]] [[1879]] and culminated in the [[Capture of Gleiêng (9 July 1879)|fall of Gleiêng]] on [[9 July]] [[1879]], the fortified chief place of [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]]. With the parallel [[Campaign against the XXVI Moekims (July 1879)|campaign against the XXVI Moekims]] of early July and the [[Selimoen Expedition (August 1879)|Selimoen expedition]] of August, it completed the first conquest of Great Acheh.

## The Governor-General's Cabinet Letter of 21 March 1879

In his cabinet letter of [[21 March]] [[1879]] the Governor-General [[Johan Willem van Lansberge|Van Lansberge]] wrote to Van der Heyden: "One thing must be held by you in the first place before your eyes. The war must now come to an end, whether the expected submission is obtained, or such punishment is inflicted on the enemy that no doubt exists as to our overlordship and his defeat, and we can withdraw our fighting force within the limits to be designated for our definitive establishment." If, contrary to expectation, the submission of the XXII Moekims did not follow upon the conquest of Indrapuri, further advance was to be made and the enemy sought out. If the population should seek to escape pursuit by flight, the punishment inflicted on their possessions should leave no doubt as to their defeat.

## The Capture of Indrapuri, 24 March 1879

The troops, which had marched on [[23 March]] [1879], reached on [[24 March]] [1879] along the sultan's road, now and then coming into fight with the enemy, Gle Kambing, opposite the missigit of Indrapuri. As the river proved not to be fordable, the guide was allowed, with five unarmed native soldiers, to swim across the river to set fire to some houses. "This bold deed indeed had the predicted effect; the enemy, already plagued from three sides by our fire, evidently got into the belief that part of the troops had crossed the river, ceased his fire and abandoned the missigit. But as this was not soon occupied by our men, he saw his mistake and occupied his fortification anew. When, however, some fifty soldiers, mostly Buginese, had swum across the river and charged the mosque at the double, the enemy abandoned it anew and it fell without a blow into our hands."

## The Continuation of the Campaign

The road from Anak Galoeëng to Gle Kambing had first to be put in good order and the enemy's withdrawal cleared. For the conquest of Indrapuri did not lead to the desired submission. "On the contrary, it was as if the struggle, under the leadership of our embittered enemies — [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]], [[Imam of Longbatta|Imam Longbatta]] and others — flared up with new strength." Heavy fighting followed at [[Djerreek|Djerreek]], [[Redep|Redep]], [[Panteh Karang|Panteh Karang]]. Djerreek was destroyed two, three times over, but each time fortified anew. On [[6 May]] and [[7 May]] [1879] great losses were inflicted on the Dutch here, but in the end the enemy was driven out and the population sorely punished. In the storming of [[6 May]] [1879] at Panteh Karang, all the officers engaged were put out of action one after another. Repeatedly the storming, however often repeated, was repulsed, so that the enemy that day remained in fact master of the terrain. Only on [[7 May]] [1879] did the fortress fall with the help of new reinforcement, and in the following days the enemy was driven from the neighbourhood.

## The Capture of Gleiêng, 9 July 1879

As the operations for the submission of the XXII Moekims had to be continued, the troops on [[31 May]] [1879] again advanced on [[Gleiêng|Gléiëng]], the fortified and well-armed chief place of [[Panglima Polem|Panglima Polem]]. "After a tiring march and repeated fights, this was at last reached and taken on [[9 July]] [1879]. With this the campaign in the XXII Moekims was over and the objective fully attained. The enemy's resistance was broken, and those who wished to continue the war had fled to the [[XXVI Moekims|XXVI Moekims]]."

**The Death of Sultan Aladin Mahmood Shah** (also written Aladin Mahmood Shah, Alaoe'd-din Mahmoed Shah) on [[26-january|26 January]] [[1874]] at Pagger Ajer was the death of the fourteen-year-old sultan of [[acheh|Atjeh]] from cholera, two days after the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of his Kraton]] to the Dutch under [[Jan van Swieten|Lieutenant-general Van Swieten]] in the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]]. His death, the cholera that had first raged among the Dutch troops and now made its ravages among the Atjehers, extinguished the regular line of sultans recognised by the Dutch and led directly to the [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation of annexation]] of [[groot-atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] by right of conquest.

## Accession

In [[1870]] the previous sultan had died and had been succeeded by the fourteen-year-old Aladin Mahmood Shah under the regency of the first rijksbestuurder, the Arab sayyid [[Habib Abdoerrahman|Habib Abdoe'r Rachman]] (properly Sayyid Abdurrahman Zair). The young sultan had been recognised by the Dutch — though in the somewhat ambiguous form familiar from earlier recognitions. The Habib assumed a high tone, demanded the return of former Acehnese territory on the east and west coasts of Sumatra, and threatened the intervention of England, France and Turkey. He was later more polite, but it appeared that at the same time he had asked the Governor of the [[straits-settlements|Straits Settlements]] for advice and help against the Dutch.

## The Fall of the Kraton

When the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] landed east of the Atjeh river on [[9-december|9 December]] [[1873]], the sultan was still in the Kraton. The Dutch advance carried the [[Missigit|missigit]] of the Kraton on [[6-january|6 January]] [[1874]] and the [[Taman (Kota Goenoengan)|Taman]] or Kota Goenoengan on [[12-january|12 January]] [[1874]]. From the west side too the Kraton could now be fired upon, and the Dutch fire "made the stay there so unsafe that, according to information received, the sultan had left it and had betaken himself to kampong [[longbatta|Longbatta]]." On [[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]] the Kraton was found evacuated and occupied by the Dutch; on [[26-january|26 January]] [[1874]] the sultan died of cholera at Pagger Ajer.

## The Question of the Succession

"What was to be done in these circumstances? Of making a tractaat there was now, also after all that had happened, no more question. The knowledge that one had obtained of the conditions and the form of government in Atjeh gave little hope of it." On [[31-january|31 January]] [[1874]] Van Swieten issued his [[Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874|proclamation]] declaring the land, by right of conquest, to belong to the Netherlands-Indian government.

The death of the sultan did not, however, extinguish the institution. The [[toeankoe-hasjim|Toewankoe Haschim]] and the war-party maintained the sultanate in shadowy form at [[keumala|Keumala]] (or Kemala), in the interior south of [[pidie|Pedir]]. [[J. L. J. H. Pel|General Pel]] in his note of [[30-april|30 April]] [[1875]] named the Toewankoe's Haschim, Hoesin, and Abdoel Madjid among the relatives of the former sultans who had attached themselves to the war-party: "they rightly see that they have nothing more to expect from a foreign overrule." A young pretender, [[tuanku-muhamat-dawot|Toewankoe Daoed]], was destined for the sultan's rank in [[1874]] and was still being watched over by Toewankoe Haschim in [[1879]]. After the close of large-scale operations in Great Acheh in August 1879, "the principal among the hostile chiefs — Toewankoe Haschim, who still kept watch over the young pretender to the sultanship, Toewankoe Daoed, the Imam Longbatta, Panglima Polim, his son Radja Koelala and others — persisted in their hostile attitude, but their means were scattered and they had placed themselves beyond the reach of our troops, namely at Kemala, in the interior south of Pedir."

## Significance

The death of Aladin Mahmood Shah removed the last sultan recognised by the Dutch in the regular line. It opened the long second phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], in which the formal annexation of Groot-Atjeh coexisted with a shadow sultanate at Keumala and a protracted guerrilla resistance. The sultan's place as the focus of Acehnese loyalty was gradually taken by the religious leaders — the ulamas and teungkus — who, after the period of the [[Dutch Concentration Scheme|concentration scheme]], organised the [[Holy War (Jihad) against the Dutch|jihad against the Dutch]].

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft records the death of Sultan [[Alaedin Mahmut Shah|Aladin Mahmoed Shah]] from cholera at Pagger Ajer on [[26-january|26 January]] [[1874]], two days after the fall of the Kraton. He treats the Sultan's death as a decisive turning-point: it removed the single recognised authority with whom [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] might have concluded a peace, and it allowed the war party under [[toeankoe-machmoed|Toeankoe Machmoed]] and the *ulama* to continue resistance from inland, first at Longbatta and later at Keumala.

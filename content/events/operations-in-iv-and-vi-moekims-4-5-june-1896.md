**The Operations in IV and VI Moekims (4–5 June 1896)** — a two-colonne Dutch descent on the northern coastal stretch of the [[VI Moekims]] — was a combined-arms operation mounted in the first week of [[June]] [[1896]] to clear the *moekim*s of [[Lam-Tengah]] and [[Lam-Pagar]] of Achehnese bands and to close the [[Blang Kala]]-pas, executed by a 1e colonne under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] (12 brigades *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]], the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e]], [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e]], and [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljons]], two companies of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]], one company of the [[15de Bataljon Infanterie|15de Bataljon]], two cavalry platoons under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|Ritmeester Jhr. de Lannoy]], two *bergbatterijen*, two engineer detachments, and four ambulances) marching at 4 a.m. on [[4 June]] [[1896]] from [[Lam-Djamoe]] to the IV Moekims, and a 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] marching at 5 a.m. on [[5 June]] [[1896]] from [[Lamtih]] to [[Lam-Pagar]], [[Lam-Badak]], and [[Lam-Baroe]]. Simultaneously, a marine landing division was to land at [[Koeala Pantjoer]] on [[5 June]] [[1896]] to drive any Achehnese band toward Van Heutsz's colonne. The operation's first engagement, on [[4 June]] [[1896]], saw the spits under inlandsch korporaal Brodjo encounter a ~30-man *bende* near [[Beradin]] at 50 m distance; the *bende* fled with 2 dead, one of whom was subsequently identified as [[Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]].

## Background

By the first week of [[June]] [[1896]] the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had broken [[Teuku Umar|Teungkoe Oemar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]] and the [[Razing of Lam-Asan (30 May – 2 June 1896)|razing of Lam-Asan]] was complete. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], now turned his attention to the northern coastal stretch of the [[VI Moekims]] — the *moekim*s of [[Lam-Tengah]] and [[Lam-Pagar]] — which remained a centre of Achehnese resistance. The geography of the operation was exacting: the [[Raja gebergte]] (the Raja mountain range) ran along the southern edge of the operational area, and the [[Blang Kala]]-pas — the principal pass through the gebergte — was the conduit through which Achehnese reinforcements from the south could reach the coastal mukims. The Dutch plan called for a convergent advance: the 1e colonne under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]] would advance from [[Lam-Djamoe]] into the IV Moekims, search the *kampong*s on the southern slopes of the Raja gebergte, take the *versterkingen*, close the Blang Kala-pas, and block any Achehnese *bende* coming from the south; the 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] would advance from [[Lamtih]] to [[Lam-Pagar]], [[Lam-Badak]], and [[Lam-Baroe]], taking and clearing *versterkingen* on the way; and a *marinelandingsdivisie* would land at [[Koeala Pantjoer]] on [[5 June]] [[1896]] to drive any *bende* toward Van Heutsz's colonne.

## The 1e Colonne under Stemfoort

The 1e colonne under [[Kolonel der Infanterie J. W. Stemfoort|Kol. Stemfoort]] was a substantial force. Its composition was: 12 brigades *maréchaussée* under [[Kapitein Jhr. Graafland|Kapt. Jhr. Graafland]] (4 officers, 12 European under-officers, 207 *minderen*); the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie|3e Bataljon]]; the [[6e Bataljon Infanterie|6e Bataljon]]; the [[9e Bataljon Infanterie|9e Bataljon]]; two companies of the [[14e Bataljon Infanterie|14e Bataljon]]; one company of the [[15de Bataljon Infanterie|15de Bataljon]]; two cavalry platoons under [[Ritmeester Jhr. L. D. C. de Lannoy|Ritmeester Jhr. de Lannoy]]; two *bergbatterijen* of two sections each; two engineer detachments; four ambulances; and *dwangarbeiders*. The colonne marched at 4 a.m. on [[4 June]] [[1896]] from [[Lam-Djamoe]] to the IV Moekims. The assignment was to search the *kampong*s on the southern slopes of the [[Raja gebergte]], to take the *versterkingen*, to close the [[Blang Kala]]-pas, and to block any Achehnese *bende* coming from the south.

## The Engagement at Beradin — 4 June 1896

The first engagement of the operation came on [[4 June]] [[1896]]. The spits (advance-guard) under inlandsch korporaal Brodjo — a native NCO of the *maréchaussée* — encountered a *bende* of approximately 30 men near [[Beradin]] at a distance of 50 m. Brodjo, demonstrating the initiative for which the native NCOs of the *maréchaussée* were celebrated, ordered his men to kneel, delivered volleys, and attacked. Both front brigades supported the attack. The *bende* fled with 2 dead, leaving behind breech-loading rifles. It was subsequently learned that one of the dead was [[Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]] — also known as [[Toekoe Hoesin Lehong Bata]] — the *bendehoofd* of [[Lam-Goet]] whose residence had been the target of the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]] of [[14 April]] [[1896]]. The death of [[Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]] was a substantial blow to the Achehnese resistance in the central mukims and a small but satisfying reversal for the Dutch, who had failed to kill him at Lam Goet itself.

## The 2e Colonne under Van Heutsz — 5 June 1896

The 2e colonne under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Lt.-Col. Van Heutsz]] marched at 5 a.m. on [[5 June]] [[1896]] from [[Lamtih]] to [[Lam-Pagar]], [[Lam-Badak]], and [[Lam-Baroe]]. The assignment was to take and clear the *versterkingen* on the way. The people of [[Lam-Pagar]] had, in the intelligence summary of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]], been noted for their "warlike reputation" — a reputation that the 2e colonne would now have the opportunity to test. Simultaneously, a marine landing division was to land at [[Koeala Pantjoer]] on [[5 June]] [[1896]] — the [[Marine Landing at Koeala Pantjoer / Lam-Baroe (5 June 1896)|marine landing at Koeala Pantjoer / Lam-Baroe]] — to drive any Achehnese *bende* toward Van Heutsz's colonne. The combined effect of the convergent advance — the 1e colonne from the south, the 2e colonne from the east, and the *marinelandingsdivisie* from the north — was intended to compress the Achehnese bands in the [[VI Moekims]] and to deny them any avenue of escape.

## The Marine Landing and the Coordination

The [[Marine Landing at Koeala Pantjoer / Lam-Baroe (5 June 1896)|marine landing at Koeala Pantjoer]] on [[5 June]] [[1896]] was, in its way, the most novel element of the operation. A *marinelandingsdivisie* — a division of marines trained for amphibious assault — was disembarked at the kampong of [[Lam-Baroe]] via a boat-flotilla at 8:30 a.m. No shot was fired; the *versterkingen* were dismantled; the breastwork west of [[Lam-Toetoe]] and the *Oosterface* of the *Koeta* west of [[Indra-Perwa]] were cleared. The marines withdrew at 11:15 a.m. The landing demonstrated the Government's capacity to project force from the sea and to coordinate land and naval operations in a single theatre — a capacity that would be expanded, three weeks later, in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] of [[22 June]]–[[23 June]] [[1896]].

## Significance

The Operations in IV and VI Moekims of [[4 June]]–[[5 June]] [[1896]] were, in tactical terms, a success: the *bende* of [[Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]] was broken and its leader killed; the *versterkingen* of the coastal stretch were dismantled; and the *marinelandingsdivisie* demonstrated the Government's capacity for amphibious operations. In strategic terms, the operations were part of the consolidation phase that followed the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] — a sustained effort to clear the residual Achehnese resistance from the *moekim*s adjacent to the area of operations. The death of [[Toekoe Hoesin Long Bata|Toekoe Hoesin Lhongbatta]] — the *bendehoofd* of [[Lam-Goet]] — was a particular satisfaction, given his survival of the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]] two months earlier. The operations also marked the operational debut of the coordinated land-sea pattern that would characterise the rest of June — a pattern that would culminate, three weeks later, in the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|Ladoeng landing]] and the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]].

**Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)** was the act by which the Atjehnese war-leader [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar (Teuku Umar)]] — formerly the principal Atjehnese ally of the Dutch in [[Groot-Atjeh]] — turned against his former patrons, unhinged the [[Concentratie-stelsel ([[1884]]-[[1896]])|concentratie-stelsel]] of [[1884]]-[[1896]] and ushered in the final, decisive phase of the [[Atjeh-oorlog ([[1873]]-[[1904]])|Atjeh-oorlog]]. The defection is the immediate trigger of the [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-[[G. C. E. van Daalen|Van Daalen]] conquest of Atjeh.

## The pre-1896 alliance

Before [[1896]] [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] was the principal Atjehnese ally of the Dutch in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The *concentratie-stelling* around [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] — the "Noordoostpolder"-sized perimeter held since [[1884]] — was sustained in part by alliances of this kind; the Dutch forces, confined to the perimeter, depended on indigenous auxiliaries to extend their effective reach. The earlier period of co-operation is reflected in the [[1893]] *Submission of Teuku Uma to the Dutch Government* and the so-called *Uma period* of Dutch-Atjehnese relations.

## The defection

In [[1896]] [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]] defected, turning his weapons and his influence against his former patrons. The *concentratie-stelling* around [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] was thereby unhinged; even the small "Noordoostpolder"-sized area the Dutch had held since [[1884]] was "niet meer geheel zeker" — no longer wholly secure.

## The 1896 parliamentary debate

The crisis of [[1896]] brought the long-simmering criticism of Atjeh policy to a head in the [[Tweede Kamer]]. [[I. D. Fransen van de Putte|Fransen van de Putte]] (u.l.) declared that "naar mijn oordeel zijn wij er niet op aangekleed zulk een land te veroveren" — in my judgement we are not equipped to conquer such a land — and recalled that the [[Netherlands]] had sat in Makassar for 150 years without being able to move beyond the city limits. He compared the Atjeh war unfavourably with the government's patient toleration of de facto self-rule on [[Celebes]].

[[H. B. van Kol|Van Kol]] (s.d.) "stelde voor, dat Nederland zich geheel uit Atjeh zou terugtrekken" — proposed that the Netherlands withdraw entirely from Atjeh. With the [[Sumatra-tractaat ([[1871]])|Sumatra-tractaat]] in hand the [[Netherlands]] had nothing to fear from [[Great Britain|England]]; other powers would readily recognise Dutch claims on Atjeh. The only danger was that, by continuing "onze wrede wijze van oorlogvoeren" — our cruel way of waging war — the [[Netherlands]] would give a great power a pretext for intervention.

[[Abraham Kuyper|Kuyper]] (a.r.) acknowledged that the [[Netherlands]] bore guilt for unleashing the war in [[1873]] but held that this injustice had now prescribed. He summed up the Atjeh policy as a "volledige staalkaart van alle denkbare methoden om Atjeh te bestrijden" — a complete sample-card of every conceivable method of combating Atjeh — and observed sardonically that, by [[1896]], the government had returned to "het reeds eenmaal beproefde stelsel van [[Van der Heyden|Van der Heyden]]" and that "een negende nieuw stelsel heeft men blijkbaar niet kunnen vinden".

[[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] (lib.) and [[Rutgers van Rozenburg|Rutgers van Rozenburg]] (lib.) called for "een drastisch optreden in Atjeh om, 'deze kanker' te verwijderen, ook in het belang van onze financiën" — drastic action in Atjeh to remove "this cancer", also in the interest of our finances.

## The post-1896 recovery

The post-[[1896]] campaigns of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — the [[Van der Heyden]]-[[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] system of pursuit into the remotest hiding-places — restored [[Groot-Atjeh]] outside the perimeter. The resistance chiefs withdrew, first to [[Pedirse|Pedir]], and were pursued: first to [[Pedirse|Pedir]], then to the coasts, and finally — in [[1904]] — to the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]] and [[Alasland|Alaslanden]]. The [[J. T. Cremer|Cremer]] footnote in [[W. H. Vermeulen|Vermeulen]] observes that what complicated the recovery was the fear of the population that the Dutch troops would again withdraw, and that it would then be at the mercy of a figure like [[Toekoe Oemar|Toekoe Oemar]]. Once the population was given confidence that the Dutch would not leave again, "hadden wij het spel half gewonnen".

## Significance

[[Toekoe Oemar's defection ([[1896]])|The 1896 defection]] is the political-military hinge of the Atjehnese war. The post-[[1896]] policy of [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] — the recovery of [[Groot-Atjeh]], the pursuit of the resistance chiefs to [[Pedirse|Pedir]] and thence to the [[Gajoland|Gajoe-]] and [[Alasland|Alaslanden]] — is the direct consequence of the collapse of the pre-[[1896]] alliance system that [[Toekoe Oemar|Oemar]]'s defection revealed.

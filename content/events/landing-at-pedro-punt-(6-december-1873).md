**The Landing at Pedro-punt (6 December 1873)** was the amphibious landing of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second Aceh expedition]] under [[Jan van Swieten|opperbevelhebber van Swieten]] on the coast east of [[Kwala Gigieng|Gigieng]] on [[6 December]] [[1873]], which Borel describes as "uitstekend bedacht en zeer goed uitgevoerd" — admirably conceived and superbly executed — and which culminated in the occupation of the kampong of [[Penajoeng]] on the right bank of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]], the base from which all subsequent operatiën against the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] and the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] would be conducted. It is one of the very few operations of the campaign that Borel praises unreservedly.

## Strategic Choice

The decision to land at [[Pedro-punt]] rather than at the obvious mouth of the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]] was, in Borel's account, a genuine piece of operational imagination. The spot lay east of Gigieng, "waar de vijand ons stellig niet verwacht had." The enemy had presumably concentrated his defenses around the river-mouth and the approaches to the Kraton, and the displacement of the landing-place took the expeditionary force completely by surprise. The choice also avoided the muddy and exposed river-mouth, where the kwade moesson would soon begin to make debarkation "hoe langer hoe moeilijker" — a consideration that would, only months later, prove how shortsighted was van Swieten's failure to build a proper [[Zeehoofd|zeehoofd]] at [[Oeleh Leh]].

## The Operation

The troops were rowed ashore in good order. Once a beachhead had been secured, the columns began their victorious march along the strand toward the [[Atjeh-rivier|Atjeh-rivier]]. The Atjehneezen, taken in flank and unable to bring their prepared defences to bear, suffered what Borel calls "zware verliezen." The Dutch columns pressed forward, and after a short advance the kampong of [[Penajoeng]] on the right bank of the river was occupied without serious resistance. The choice of Penajoeng as the new bivouak was dictated by the river, by the open ground of its sawahs, and by its proximity to the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] — only some 1200 passen of open veld separated the new bivouak from the great fortified mosque that guarded the northern approach to the Kraton.

## Significance

The landing marked the true opening of the campaign. From Penajoeng the Dutch would, within three weeks, conduct the [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|bloody reconnaissances of Lemboe]] on [[25 December|25]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]], launch the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|disastrous frontal storm of the Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]], endure the [[Acehnese attack on Penajoeng bivouak (13 January 1874)|daylight raid on the bivouak]] of [[13 January]], and finally prepare the [[Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)|nineteen-day bombardment]] of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] and the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] that would carry it on [[24 January]] [[1874]]. Yet the same Penajoeng, Borel would later note, became a "pesthol" of cholera during the kwade moesson, and the entire bivouak was so poorly sited against the boschrand that the Atjehneezen were able to [[Acehnese attack on Penajoeng bivouak (13 January 1874)|burst into it at two o'clock in the afternoon]] of [[13 January]] [[1874]] — reaching the very tent of the opperbevelhebber. The brilliancy of the landing thus stands in telling contrast to the operational errors that, in Borel's account, would follow.

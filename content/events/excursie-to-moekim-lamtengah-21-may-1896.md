**The Excursie to Moekim Lamtengah (21 May 1896)** — the column expedition against the district of [[Moekim Lamtengah]] — was a punitive expedition commanded by [[Luitenant-Kolonel Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] against the territory of the *bendehoofd* [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]], whose promises (after [[15 May]] [[1896]]) to return borrowed rifles had proved false and whose territory had offered heavy fire against Dutch patrols. The Dutch *excursie* destroyed his possessions. Kapitein [[J. P. Linck|J. P. Linck]] commanded the 4e compagnie that stormed the fortified work of [[Belang]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the *excursie* of [[21 May]] [[1896]] was a shaping operation in the final days before the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive of 23–25 May 1896]] — a demonstration that the Dutch could reach into the [[Moekim Lamtengah]] at will and a reminder to the *bendehoofd* of Lamtengah that his promises of loyalty would be tested.

## Background

By the third week of [[May]] [[1896]] the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], was in the final stages of preparation for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] against [[Teuku Umar]]'s *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]]. The [[Bombardment of Lampisang (27 April – 23 May 1896)|bombardment of Lampisang]] was in its fourth week. The [[Naval Demonstration in Kroeng-Rababaai (1–4 May 1896)|naval demonstration in the Kroeng-Rababaai]] had been completed. The [[Diversie to Montassik (2 May 1896)|diversie to Montassik]] had been mounted. The Dutch forces were concentrating for the offensive.

In the midst of these preparations, the political situation in the *moekim* of [[Moekim Lamtengah]] required attention. The *bendehoofd* [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]] — who had borrowed rifles from the Government under the native-allied policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]] — had, after [[15 May]] [[1896]], promised to return them. His promises had proved false. Worse, his *gebied* had offered heavy fire against Dutch patrols — a clear indication that he had thrown in his lot with the resistance. A *maréchaussée* probe to [[Moekim Lamtengah]] on [[15 May]] [[1896]] had failed; patrols to the [[Djempit]] area on [[18 May]]–[[20 May]] [[1896]] had confirmed the hostile disposition. Vetter resolved to send a punitive expedition — an *excursie* — to chastise Lamtengah and to destroy the possessions of [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]].

## The Excursie

The *excursie* of [[21 May]] [[1896]] was placed under the command of [[Luitenant-Kolonel Bisschoff van Heemskerck|Lt.-Col. Bisschoff van Heemskerck]] — the same officer who had commanded the 1e Colonne at the [[Tuchtiging van Lam Goet (14 April 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam Goet]] of [[14 April]] [[1896]] and who would command the 1e Colonne at the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] of [[9 June]]–[[10 June]] [[1896]]. The column comprised the usual mix of infantry, *maréchaussée*, mountain artillery, and engineers. The objective was the *gebied* of [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]] — his *kampong*s, his *versterkingen*, and his possessions.

The advance was contested. [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]]'s warriors, drawn from the *moekim* of [[Moekim Lamtengah]] and supplemented by allied bands from the neighbouring mukims, offered heavy fire from the *kampong* edges and from the low hills that characterised the broken ground of the district. The Dutch column pushed forward methodically, the *maréchaussée* brigades clearing the *kampong* edges, the artillery shelling the *versterkingen*, and the infantry advancing in support. The principal obstacle was the fortified work of [[Belang]] — a substantial earthwork with the usual Achehnese *bamboedoeri* abatis.

## The Storming of Belang

The storming of the *versterking* of [[Belang]] fell to Kapitein [[J. P. Linck|J. P. Linck]], commanding the 4e compagnie. Linck — who would later command the 4e compagnie at the [[Capture of Lam-Pisang and Oemar's House (24 May 1896)|capture of Lam-Pisang]] on [[24 May]] [[1896]] and at the [[Ladoeng Landing and Glé Broeë Attack (22–23 June 1896)|storming of the Kroeng-Kali benteng]] on [[23 June]] [[1896]] — led his company in a direct assault on the work. The *versterking* was carried; the defenders were driven out; the work was occupied and destroyed. The capture of [[Belang]] opened the way to the possessions of [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]], which were systematically destroyed — houses burned, stores confiscated or ruined, *sawah* dykes broken. The *bendehoofd* himself escaped, but his *moekim* had been chastised and his capacity to make trouble on the flank of the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had been degraded.

## Significance

The Excursie to Moekim Lamtengah on [[21 May]] [[1896]] was a shaping operation in the final days before the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]]. Its principal effects were three. First, it chastised [[Toekoe Tjoet Lamtengah]] and degraded his capacity to interfere with the offensive — a small but real contribution to the security of the Dutch flank. Second, it tested, in combat, the 4e compagnie of Kapitein [[J. P. Linck|Linck]] — a test that the compagnie passed and that would be repeated, with greater consequence, three days later at [[Lam-Pisang]]. Third, it confirmed to the *hoeloebalang* of the mukims that the Dutch retained the capacity to reach into any district that harboured resistance — a political message of considerable importance in the fluid environment of mid-May [[1896]]. The *excursie* was, in this sense, a small operation with a large strategic purpose: the securing of the conditions for the [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] that would, two days later, begin the recovery of the strategic initiative.

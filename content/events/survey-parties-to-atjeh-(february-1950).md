**The survey parties to Atjeh (February 1950)** were groups of plantation officials and government advisors dispatched in early February [[1950]] to assess the condition of the approximately fifty plantation enterprises in [[Atjeh|Atjeh]] following the Dutch-Indonesian hostilities and to evaluate the prospects for their return to operation.

## Background

The survey parties were dispatched following discussions between representatives of the Entrepreneurs' Association for Indonesia (*Ondernemersbond voor Indonesië*) on 9 and 10 December [[1949]] at [[Kotaradja]], at which it was resolved to send survey parties (*survey-parties*) to assess the situation of the enterprises in Atjeh. These discussions took place in the context of labour disputes that had been considered by a local arbitral commission (P4D) and a central commission (P4P).

## First group (2 February 1950)

The first group of seven planters flew from Medan to Kotaradja on 2 February [[1950]]. They were drawn from:
- The Société Financière des Caoutchoucs;
- The Netherlands Trading Company (*Ned. Handel Mij.*);
- The State Agricultural Enterprises (*Landslandbouwbedrijven*).

They were accompanied by [[Rasjid Siregar]], commissioner of the [[Sarbupri]] (*Sarekat Buruh Perkebunan Republik Indonesia*). The planters of the Société Financière proceeded to examine the oil-palm plantations at Meulaboh, while the others travelled via Bireuen.

## Second group (3 February 1950)

The second group of some twenty planters travelled by jeep via P. Brandan to [[Langsar Estate]], where the party would split. This group was accompanied by [[Sutan Barus]], inspector-general of enterprises in Atjeh, his secretary [[G. Kars Gultom]], and [[N. W. L. Stofkoper]], advisor for agricultural enterprises.

## B.P.M. survey party

Simultaneously, the B.P.M. (*Bataafsche Petroleum Maatschappij*, the Batavian Petroleum Company) sent its own survey party of eight technicians to Atjeh, including its Medan representative [[W. J. J. A. van den Bosch]]. They found the factory grounds completely ruined (they had already been partially destroyed before the Japanese invasion) and the oil fields in a somewhat patched-up condition.

## Follow-up: V.V.R.O.S. delegation (August 1950)

On 29 August [[1950]], a [[V.V.R.O.S.]] delegation, together with six representatives of the companies with interests in Atjeh, proceeded to Kotaradja for further discussions with a commission formed by the governor of Atjeh. Shortly thereafter, approximately 40 of the roughly 50 enterprises in Atjeh could be released, of which 20 were immediately taken into management. [[Said Abubakar]] was appointed manager (*beheerder*), with technical advice provided by [[C. A. Kleingeld]] of the Sipef.

**The formation of the Province of North Sumatra** was the administrative reorganisation, decreed by the central Indonesian government in early [[1951]], by which [[Atjèh|Atjeh]] was merged with [[Tapanoeli]] and the former federal state of [[East Sumatra]] into a single province — reducing Atjeh, in Henri J. H. Alers's phrase, to "an anything-but-autonomous residency" and precipitating the chain of events that led, two years later, to [[Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh|Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh]]'s rebellion and [[Atjèh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia (1953)|Atjeh's accession to the Negara Islam Indonesia]].

## Background: Atjeh's contested autonomy

Atjeh had, since the [[Ulama–Uleëbalang social revolution in Atjeh (1945–1946)|1945–1946 social revolution]], been governed largely as a self-contained polity under Teungkoe Moehamad Daoed Beureu-éh as republican military governor. During a visit in [[August]] [[1949]], the head of the Republic's Emergency Government, [[Mr. Sjafrudin Prawiranegara|Sjafrudin Prawiranegara]], had conceded on every point of Daoed Beureuh's demands and recognised Atjeh as a *de jure* autonomous province — a status the central government at [[Djokja|Djokja]] then partly disavowed by cable, before allowing the arrangement to continue on a merely "*de facto*" basis, judging its own legal authority to disavow the concession doubtful in the first place.

## The merger and its politics

In early 1951, buoyed by recent successes in liquidating the former federal states and ending the [[Ambon affair|Ambon affair]], the central government proceeded regardless of Pusa objections — voiced most sharply at a [[Poesa|Poesa]] congress held [[23 December|23]]–[[27 December]] [[1950]] to campaign for the retention of Atjeh's autonomous status — and created the merged province of [[North Sumatra|North Sumatra]], comprising Atjeh, Tapanoeli, and the former federal state of East Sumatra. Alers records that the government began addressing correspondence to a "resident-coordinator" of Atjeh even before Daoed Beureuh's dismissal as governor had been formally completed; Daoed Beureuh simply returned such letters unopened. He was subsequently ordered transferred to a ministry post in [[Djakarta]] and, when he requested resignation from government service instead, had it granted "with suspicious speed." He was replaced by the Javanese official [[Danubroto|Danubroto]] as resident-coordinator.

## Consequences

Danubroto's attempt to restore *[[Uleebalang|oelèëbalang]]*-class and non-Atjèhnese officials to posts held since the revolution by [[Poesa|Poesa]] members emboldened the surviving Uleëbalang class, which organised as the [[Badan Keinsjafan Rakjat|Badan Keinsjafan Rakjat]] under [[Teuku Mohammed Ali Samalanga|Teuku Mohammed Ali Samalanga]]. Alers presents the North Sumatra merger as the underlying grievance behind Daoed Beureuh's escalating denunciations of central-government officials as "*kafirs*" from [[1951]] onward, and treats it as the structural precondition for the rebellion he would proclaim in [[September]] [[1953]].

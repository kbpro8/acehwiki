**The kerapatan** — the *groote raadsvergadering*, or great council meeting — was the supreme adat assembly of the [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]], convened under native custom to decide the most important public affairs of the two *landschappen* of [[Batoe Mboelan|Batoe Mboelan]] and [[Bambél|Bambél]]. *De Buitenbezittingen* records that, "volgens de adat," important matters were treated in the *kerapatan*, "waaraan deelnemen de Kedjoeron, met den Radja moeda, de Si-empats met hunne moeda's en wakéls, en Pengoeloes soekoe voor hun eigen persoon." Its best-documented convocation was the [[1914|1914]] session that ratified the territorial reorganisation of the Alas valley under Dutch supervision.

## Composition and procedure

The *kerapatan* embodied the pyramid of Alas adat authority. At its head sat the **Kédjoerön** of each *landschap*, accompanied by his deputy the [[Radja moeda|Radja moeda]]. Next came the four **Si-empat** — the members of the *Rijksraad* — each accompanied by his own *moeda* and *wakél*. Finally the **Péngoeloes soekoe**, the genealogical heads of the constituent lineages, attended in person. The assembly thus represented, in a single body, every layer of the constitution: territorial overlordship, the council of four, and the genealogical heads of the *soekoe*. Decisions taken in this forum carried the legitimacy that no individual chief could confer alone.

## The 1914 territorial reorganisation

When the Dutch administration sought to bring the *verwarde, tweeslachtige bestuurs-* — confused and ambiguous administrative — conditions of the Alas valley into a rational territorial shape, it did so through the *kerapatan*. The handbook records that, "in overeenstemming met de zelfbestuurders en andere hoofden der Alaslanden," a solution "kort daarna in de kerapatan (groote raadsvergadering) der beide landschappen" was found for the desired territorial reorganisation, "welke sedert geheel werd ingevoerd."

The settlement had three main elements. First, the border between the two *landschappen* was fixed precisely, ending the anomalous position of [[Radja Tjèq of Alas|Radja Tjèq]] of Batoe Mboelan, whose territory had straddled both; he was transferred to Bambél, where he was enrolled as a *Péngoeloe si-émpat* under the title *Radja Tjèq Batoe Mboelan*, while his namesake *landschap* was merged into Bambél. Second, the *Kédjoerön* of Batoe Mboelan moved his residence to [[Poeloe Nas|Poeloe Nas]] and signed a new [[Korte Verklaring|Korte Verklaring]] for the *landschap* of that name. Third, each *landschap* was divided into four *mérga*'s under salaried *Péngoeloe si-émpat* — eight territorial units in all, replacing the older genealogical patchwork.

## Significance

The *kerapatan* of [[1914]] demonstrates how the Dutch chose, in the Alaslanden, to work *through* the native constitution rather than around it. The handbook notes that the territorial organisation, "welke in de Gajö-landschappen nog als een betrekkelijk ver verwijderd desideratum moet worden beschouwd," had in the Alaslanden "reeds haar beslag gekregen" — had already been settled — precisely because the *kerapatan* could be convened to legitimate it. The same instrument continued, in attenuated form, to serve as the venue for the disposition of the many minor *hoofden, wakéls en andere autoriteitjes* whose genealogical titles were allowed to lapse on death but not be transferred — a slow, adat-sanctioned extinction that completed the consolidation.

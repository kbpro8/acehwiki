The **Feast of the Birthday of Mohammad**, known in Achehnese as **Maulud** or **Mo'lot** (from the Arabic *Mawlūd*), is the annual commemoration of the birth of [[Prophet Mohammad|the Prophet Mohammad]], celebrated on the 12th day of Rabī' al-awwal, the third month of the Mohammedan year. C. Snouck Hurgronje treats the feast in *The Achehnese* both as a calendar event and as a model for the formal *idang* (meal-tray) arrangement used at all great Achehnese feasts.

## Theological and Calendrical Position

According to the now generally accepted tradition, the 12th of Rabī' al-awwal was the date both of the birth and of the death of the Prophet, and on this day many other important occurrences are said to have taken place during the sixty-three years which separate these two events. The festival is regarded as specially binding on the Achehnese by reason of a legend concerning an [[Embassy from Acheh to the Sultan of Turkey|embassy to the Sultan of Turkey]]: when Acheh first sought to bring itself to the notice of the lord of all believers at [[Stambul]], the Sultan is said to have replied that the observance of the feast of Mohammad's birth was among the most meritorious of works of devotion, and that there should be no village in Acheh where the inhabitants did not publicly hold a Mo'lot feast.

In the Achehnese calendar, the Mo'lot must be held in all [[gampong]]s either in the month of Mo'lot itself (on or after the 12th day) or in one of the two following months — **Adbe Mo'lot** ("Younger Brother of Mo'lot", Jumādā al-awwal) and **Mo'lot Seumeulheueh** ("Final Mo'lot", Jumādā al-akhir) — both of which derive their popular names from the commemoration.

## The Kanduri Mo'lot

The feast itself is the [[Kanduri Mo'lot|kanduri Mo'lot]]. The place where it is celebrated is the [[meunasah]]. The day is fixed year by year by the [[keuchi']] so as not to clash with the pursuits of the villagers, and care is taken that no two gampongs of the same mukim shall celebrate the Mo'lot on the same day or after too short an interval. As meat is indispensable for the *idang Mo'lot*, the feast is preceded by a general slaughter of animals — a slaughter that takes place on no other occasions except the last days of the eighth and ninth months, and on a smaller scale at the "great" feast of the sacrifices. The recitation proper to the Mo'lot is called **meuliku** in Achehnese (from *dikr*); some of the maulids most in use have been handsomely lithographed at [[Cairo]].

## The Feast-Idangs

Snouck Hurgronje notes that the same fixed rules which the [[adat]] lays down for the arrangement of *idangs* at wedding-feasts hold good for the *idangs* of the Feast of the Birthday of Mohammad, and for what are called "guest-meals" — feasts offered to specially honoured guests. These, however, are not in any sense of religious character, nor are they, like [[Kanduri|kanduri]]s, marked by the recitation of selections from the Quran or litanies with final prayer. A feast-*idang* is formed of two *dalongs* (presentation trays), one containing rice and the other bowls of meat, fish, and side-dishes piled in as many as nine layers separated by plantain-leaves; the whole stands beneath a handsomely worked cover (*sange*) and a costly cloth (*seulunong*).

## Great Mo'lot at Teungku Anjong's Tomb

A specially great *kanduri Mo'lot* is held on the actual day at the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] in [[Gampong Jawa]], where one or more buffaloes are slaughtered and pieces of meat are sent to sundry ulamas by the guardian of the tomb. This is one of three principal annual kanduris celebrated at that sacred tomb — the others being the [[Kanduri Aja Bseutiri|kanduri Aja Eseutiri]] on the 18th of Rajab and a third on the 14th of [[Puasa]].

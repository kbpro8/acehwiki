**The Construction of Blang Bintang Camp** on [[7 September]] [[1896]] — *begin van den bouw van een kampement op de Blang Bintang (Tjot Mantjang)* — was the construction of a forward Dutch *kampement* (camp) at [[Blang Bintang]] — known also by the name of its location, [[Tjot Mantjang]] — which served as the advanced base for the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]] and the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]] of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]]. The construction — authorised under the modified *postenstelsel* (system of posts) policy of the [[18 July|18 July 1896]] telegram of [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General Van der Wijck]] — represented the second permanent Dutch forward position beyond the *geconcenteerd linie* in the second semester of [[1896]], after the [[Occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga) (15 July 1896)|occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga)]] of [[15 July]] [[1896]].

## The Strategic Location

The choice of Blang Bintang (Tjot Mantjang) as the site of the second permanent Dutch forward position was determined by its strategic location. Blang Bintang — situated on the high ground between the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) and the [[XXII Moekims]] — commanded the principal route from Koeta-Radja to the Selimoen highlands, and its occupation gave the Dutch a strategic foothold for the projection of force into the Selimoen region. The Selimoen highlands — the centre of Acehnese resistance in the south of [[Groot-Atjeh]] — had been the object of repeated Dutch reconnaissances and *tuchtigingen* throughout the war, but had never been permanently occupied. The construction of the Blang Bintang camp — placed within striking distance of the Selimoen — was the preparatory step for the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]], which had been ordered before the construction of the camp began.

## The Construction and the First Selimoen Expedition

The construction of the camp began on [[7 September]] [[1896]] — one day after the departure of the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] on [[6 September]] [[1896]]. The construction was undertaken by a detachment of *Genietroepen* (engineers), with the protection of an infantry garrison, and the camp was sufficiently advanced by the time the first Selimoen expedition returned on [[11 September]] [[1896]] to serve as its base. The camp — which would be expanded over the following weeks — became the principal Dutch forward position for the next two months, and served as the base for the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]] of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]] and the [[Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri (28 October 1896)|temporary occupation of Indrapoeri]] of [[28 October]] [[1896]].

## The Authorisation under the Modified Policy

The construction of the Blang Bintang camp was authorised under the modified *postenstelsel* (system of posts) policy of the [[18 July|18 July 1896]] telegram of Governor-General Van der Wijck. The telegram had modified the *geen postenstelsel* policy of the [[Notulen der conferentie van 18 Juni 1896|conference of 18 June 1896]] by authorising the *bezetting dezer moekims* (occupation of these mukims) on condition that *voortdurende aanvulling van troepen en dwangarbeiders steeds mogelijk zullen zijn* (continuous replenishment of troops and forced labourers would always be possible). The construction of Blang Bintang — like the occupation of Kroeng-Raba (Lok-Nga) before it — was thus a *voorloopige* (provisional) occupation, justified by the explicit authorisation of the Governor-General for the temporary occupation of points beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line).

## Significance

The Construction of Blang Bintang Camp on [[7 September]] [[1896]] is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as the second permanent Dutch forward position beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) in the second semester of [[1896]]. The camp — placed within striking distance of the Selimoen highlands — was the preparatory step for the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] of [[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]] and the [[Second Selimoen Expedition (3–6 November 1896)|second Selimoen expedition]] of [[3 November|3]]–[[6 November]] [[1896]], and represented the slow, methodical extension of Dutch forward positions which characterised the *pacificatie* (pacification) of the second semester of [[1896]] under [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s second tenure.

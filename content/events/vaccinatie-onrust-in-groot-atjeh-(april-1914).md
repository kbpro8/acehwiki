The **vaccination unrest in Groot Atjeh** of April [[1914]] was a brief flare-up of tension in the quiet, directly administered territory of [[Groot Atjeh]], caused by the conduct of an offensive vaccinator under a district head to whom the population dared not complain, and resolved without violence after the intervention of the *controleur* and a full discussion with the population.

## Causes

Vaccination remained always a sensitive point in Atjeh. On this occasion it was moreover carried out by an "onhebbelijken vaccinateur" and under a district head to whom the population did not dare complain. "All the elements for a resistance were thus present again — and then unfriendly chance brought still further confusion."

A follower of the district head pointed out that if people did not let themselves be vaccinated, the *[[Kompeuni]]* would be in the *gampong* within a few hours and *then* one would be vaccinated by means of the bayonet.

Chance willed that a couple of hours later a patrol under an officer came to bivouac in that *[[moekim]]*, whereupon the population brought the women and children into safety outside the *gampong*, while the younger men armed themselves with *[[klewang]]s* in order to offer resistance if possible. The *[[keutjhi']]* and the elders were able to restrain them.

A few days later another patrol happened to spend the night there, and again the younger men armed themselves.

## Resolution

After the *controleur*, accompanied by the *[[oelèëbalang]]* and the *keutjhi's*, had discussed the matter fully with the population, all concerned asked *ampon* (forgiveness). They requested, however, that the vaccinator should not be sent back to [[Lam Seuninja]], and that the *Toean Besar* should point out to the district head that he should be somewhat more accessible to his population.

"Where the complaints proved well founded, the district head was seriously spoken to by the reporting officer about his conduct," [[H. N. A. Swart]] wrote in the monthly report — "and such an interview with Swart was never pleasant."

## Significance

The affair passed off well, but it belongs to a recurring pattern: the same cause produced the more serious [[Coconut and Sugar-Cane Excursion (April 1918)|Teungkoe Moet affair]] in the [[Boven Wòjla]] in April [[1918]], and the *[[Koloniaal Verslag]]* records vaccination and re-vaccination on a large scale during the smallpox epidemic on the [[Noordkust van Atjeh|north coast]] in [[1915]], brought to a standstill in [[1916]]. It illustrates du Croo's argument that the outbreaks of the later pacification years arose from local grievances and offended dignity rather than from political resistance — and that patient investigation and redress, rather than force, disposed of them.

"This case still ended well, but in 1914 more serious things happened on the West Coast": the [[Resistance in Daja and Lhong (1914)|murder of civil authority W. van Kregten at Lam No]] followed on [[20 May]] [[1914]].

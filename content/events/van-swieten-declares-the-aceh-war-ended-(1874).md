**Van Swieten declares the Aceh War ended (1874)** was the public pronouncement delivered by [[Jan van Swieten|opperbevelhebber Jan van Swieten]] in his [[Van Swieten's afscheidsrede (1874)|afscheidsrede]] (farewell address) of [[23 April]] [[1874]] in the conquered [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] of [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] — five days before his [[Departure of Jan van Swieten from Atjeh (April 1874)|departure]] — opening with the famous words: "Mijne heeren, ik beschouw den oorlog met Atjeh als geëindigd" (Gentlemen, I consider the war with Atjeh to be at an end). Borel records that the speech "maakte op zijne toehoorders bepaald een pijnlijken indruk" — made a distinctly painful impression on its hearers — and offers the lapidary verdict: "qui veut trop prouver, ne prouve rien."

## The Address

The [[Van Swieten's afscheidsrede (1874)|afscheidsrede]] was delivered on [[23 April]] [[1874]] in the great hall of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]], which since the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall]] of [[24 January]] [[1874]] had been converted into the Dutch hoofdplaats of [[Kota Radja]]. The assembled officers expected a valedictory review of the campaign, a tribute to the troops, and a handing-over of command to [[Generaal Pel|kolonel Pel]]. Van Swieten did indeed praise the "dapperheid, ijver en volharding" of the troops. Then came the famous opening: "Mijne heeren, ik beschouw den oorlog met Atjeh als geëindigd."

## The Argument

To support the declaration, van Swieten offered a series of optimistic reasons. The enemy was, he insisted, "geheel verslagen en ontmoedigd" — completely defeated and discouraged. The bevolking lacked wapens, munitie, kleederen and "zelfs aan de voornaamste levensbehoeften zooals rijst, zout." The Atjehnees trade was vernietigd. The coastal vazal-staten — among them the [[Radja van Gigieng|radja van Gigieng]] — had accepted the Dutch flag. On these grounds van Swieten concluded that the war was over. He promised the troops that they would have nothing more to do but rust; [[Kota Radja]] would become "eene aangename gezellige garnizoensplaats" — a pleasant and convivial garrison town.

## The Reaction

The speech, Borel records, "maakte op zijne toehoorders bepaald een pijnlijken indruk." The officers who had fought the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|Mesigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]] at a cost of 220 dood en gekwetsten, who had endured the [[Acehnese attack on Penajoeng bivouak (13 January 1874)|daylight raid on Penajoeng]] of [[13 January]], who had seen the [[Reconnaissance of the Atjeh-rivier (29 January 1874)|échec above the Kraton]] of [[29 January]] and the [[Expedition to Bital (12 February 1874)|futile expeditions]] to [[Expedition to Ketapang Doewa (15 February 1874)|Bital and Ketapang Doewa]] of February, knew that the enemy was far from "geheel verslagen." Three days later, on [[16 April]] [[1874]], came the [[Battle of Lampoe Oek (16 April 1874)|noodlottige échec at Lampoe Oek]], with nearly a hundred Dutch dead and wounded. The speech, Borel remarks, was made in defiance of the most recent intelligence from the field. The lapidary verdict — "qui veut trop prouver, ne prouve rien" — captures the reaction of the audience: nobody believed him.

## The Programme

What followed the speech was the practical application of the doctrine it proclaimed. Van Swieten "stak het zwaard voor goed in de scheede" — for good sheathed the sword — and applied the [[Afwachtende houding|afwachtende houding]]. "Geen vechten meer met den vijand, maar werken met schop en spade" — no more fighting with the enemy, but work with spade and shovel. Excess troops were removed: the Javaansche hulptroepen of [[Mangkoe Negoro]] and [[Pakoe Alam]] were sent back to Java, along with a veld-batterij. The [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] and the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen of 12 c.M.]] — the best geschut the Dutch had — were shipped back with them. Work began on a vierkante gehastionneerde redoute at [[Penajoeng]], on the improvement of the [[Marine-benteng|marine-benteng]], and on the conversion of the Kraton into a permanent hoofdplaats. Within three days van Swieten had boarded the *Prins Alexander der Nederlanden* and departed. The [[Desecration of Dutch graves at Lampoeloe (1874)|first bitter fruit]] of the new system would be plucked the very morning of his departure.

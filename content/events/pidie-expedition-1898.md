**Pidie Expedition of 1898** was a major Dutch military campaign conducted in the summer of [[1898]] to subjugate the powerful coastal and inland region of [[Pidie]] in northern Aceh.

## Narrative of the Campaign

Following the collapse of the [[Concentratie-Stelsel]] and his appointment as Governor of Aceh, Colonel [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz]] determined that Dutch rule could only be established by destroying the main strongholds of resistance outside [[Groot-Atjeh]]. [[Pidie]], ruled by independent [[Uleebelang]] lords and religious scholars (*ulama*), served as the primary center of resistance and arms smuggling.

In [[1898]], Colonel Van Heutsz personally took supreme command of the Pidie Expedition. Reorganizing his forces into mobile columns, he placed the [[Korps Maréchaussée te voet in Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Korps Maréchaussée]] at the vanguard. The Marechaussee units, armed with Mannlicher carbines and [[Klewang|klewangs]], marched rapidly through mountain passes, bypassing traditional road blockades and striking Acehnese encampments from the flanks and rear.

Despite fierce resistance from Pidie fighters who fortified villages and rice paddies, the Marechaussee columns systematically captured insurgent headquarters, seized hidden stores of pepper and weapons, and forced local chieftains to sign submission agreements (*Korte Verklaring*).

## Operational Results

The success of the Pidie Expedition established Van Heutsz's military reputation and led to his promotion to Major-General. It demonstrated that the Marechaussee could operate effectively far beyond supply bases in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. Following the campaign, a permanent Marechaussee garrison post was established at [[Lam Meulo]].

**The capture of [[Keumala]] on [[14 June]] [[1898]]** was the decisive military action of the [[Pidië Expedition]] under [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]. The fall of [[Keumala]]—until then the spiritual and organisational centre of resistance in [[Groot-Atjeh]]—eliminated the hub of the religious resistance party and shattered the remaining illusions about the restoration of the sultanate.

## The operation

The [[Pidië Expedition]] had begun on [[1 June]] [[1898]] from [[Segli]] in [[Pedir]]. On [[14 June]]—only thirteen days after the campaign's start—Dutch forces under [[Van Heutsz]] took [[Keumala]]. The centre, which Dutch authorities had long protected with care out of respect for the sultanate party, was finally eliminated as a strategic factor.

The capture was accompanied by the phrase "savve qui peut" (save himself who can) from the sultan's party, marking the definitive end of any realistic hope for restoring the sultanate in [[Groot-Atjeh]]. The event demonstrated that [[Van Heutsz]] had shown his qualities as a commander, and that the further military events on the operational level had sufficiently illustrated that the [[Pidië Expedition]] was a worthy introduction to the further completion of the Dutch-Indies state structure.

## Strategic significance

The fall of [[Keumala]] eliminated the centre from which resistance had been organised. From this centre the organiser of the resistance—[[Panglima Polim]]—had received the favours and benefits of the government, while at the same time using it as the base for spiritual and political coordination of the war effort. Once [[Keumala]] was in Dutch hands, the organisational structure of the resistance was irreparably damaged.

The event also confirmed the collaboration between [[Van Heutsz]] and [[Dr. Snouck Hurgronje]]. The scholar's analysis—that the spiritual leaders in [[Keumala]] were the core of resistance—had guided the military targeting. The officer's execution—rapid, decisive, and relentless—translated that analysis into territorial control.

The capture of [[Keumala]] was followed by the systematic campaign against [[Panglima Polim]] through the [[XXII Moekims]] and into the highlands, a campaign that would continue until [[Polim]]'s unconditional submission in [[1903]].

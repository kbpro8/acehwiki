The **attack on lieutenant [[G. F. P. Gosenson]] at [[Djeuram]]** on [[29 December]] [[1917]] was, in [[Maurice Henri du Croo|du Croo]]'s judgement, "a serious attack, because it had a political background" — the one incident of the later pacification years that was genuinely political rather than personal in origin.

## The attack

The attack was committed by some young Acehnese of [[Seunagan]] on the bivouac commander of Djeuram in the [[Meulaboh]] district, *1ste luitenant* G. F. P. Gosenson, who was returning with a few *[[Corps of Military Constabulary of Atjeh and Dependencies|marechaussée]]* from an elephant hunt undertaken at the request of the inhabitants of [[Goenòng Balè]].

Gosenson was severely wounded by a *[[klewang]]* blow straight across the face, by which he remained disfigured for life, and by a cut across the thigh. He and his *marechaussée*, though all wounded, laid the three attackers low.

## The investigation

The investigation, conducted by [[H. J. Schmidt]], brought to light that the notorious [[Tgk. Poeti']], through the intervention of a certain *[[krani]]* (clerk) [[Hamit]], had organised the attack with the intention of causing resistance in Seunagan to break out again.

Tgk. Poeti' was arrested and banished; "order was not disturbed."

## Significance

The attack on Gosenson was one of the "zware tegenslagen" that [[H. N. A. Swart]] had to absorb alone at his high and lonely post — du Croo names it with the [[Murder of Lieutenant-Colonel Scheepens (10 October 1913)|murder of Scheepens]] and the flare-up of resistance in the [[Daja]] districts as one of the three heaviest blows of the governorship — "but none of his people ever observed a wavering in his self-confidence, a disturbance of his composure, or a stiffening of his cheerfulness."

Gosenson survived, was made Knight of the [[Militaire Willemsorde]], and later became, as colonel, Provincial Military Commander of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. Tgk. Poeti' — who had submitted with all his *teungkoes*, *panglimas* and sixty followers in December [[1910]] — thereby ended his career for the second and last time.

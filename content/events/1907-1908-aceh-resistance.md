**The 1907–1908 Aceh resistance** was the "vrij hevige opflikkering van het verzet" — fairly fierce flare-up of resistance — that erupted in [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]] in [[1907]] and [[1908]] under the governorship of [[G. C. E. van Daalen|G. C. E. van Daalen]], the last organised spasm of armed defiance in the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-krijg]] before the pacificatory policy of his successor [[H. N. A. Swart|H. N. A. Swart]] drew the Acehnese elites into the colonial order. The Encyclopaedisch Bureau attributes the flare-up to two specific causes: van Daalen's "al te hard doorgevoerde represaille-maatregelen" — overly harshly executed reprisal measures — and the "scherpe besnoeiing der adat-inkomsten" — sharp curtailment of the customary revenues — to which the contemporary administration subjected the native chiefs.

## Causes

The first cause was military. Van Daalen, who had won his reputation as the conqueror of the [[Gajoland|Gajoland]] and [[Alaslanden|Alaslanden]] in [[1904]], continued in the governorship a doctrine of disproportionate retaliation against any act of defiance; the result, the handbook implies, was to alienate populations that had been moving toward accommodation. The second cause was administrative and fiscal. Throughout the *Gouvernement* the customary revenues of the [[Oelèëbalangs|oelèëbalangs]] and other *volkshoofden* — the *wasé oelèëbalang*, the *wasé pangkaj*, the *wasé peutoeha* — had been tolerated, even formalised, as the price of cooperation. The "scherpe besnoeiing" of these *adat-inkomsten* cut directly at the economic basis of chiefly authority, and many chiefs who had submitted under van Heutsz now had reason to reconsider.

## Course of the resistance

*De Buitenbezittingen* does not narrate the events of [[1907]]–[[1908]] in detail, but the [[Bijlage V|Bijlage V]] tables of losses recorded for those years indicate the scale of the fighting. In [[1907]] the [[Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger|Nederlandsch-Indisch Leger]] suffered 18 killed and 214 wounded; on the Acehnese side 1,917 were killed, 649 wounded, 488 captured and 136 who submitted. In [[1908]] the army's losses rose to 19 killed and 290 wounded, with Acehnese losses of 855 killed, 1,143 wounded, 41 captured and 333 submissions — figures that mark [[1908]] as the heaviest year of combat since [[1905]]. The casualty curves of [[Bijlage V]] then descend steeply through [[1909]], [[1910]] and [[1911]] as Swart's policy took hold.

## Swart's response

The resolution of the crisis came with the succession of [[H. N. A. Swart|Lieutenant-General Swart]] as Civil and Military Governor in [[1908]]. Swart's response was double: first to demonstrate, "in de eerste jaren van zijn bewind," the "bewijzen onzer overmacht" — proofs of Dutch overwhelming power — and then, having done so, to pivot to a "verzoenende en vertrouwenwekkende politiek." The chiefs who had been coerced into submission under van Daalen were thereby "steeds meer ook uit overtuiging aan onze zijde gebracht" — increasingly brought to the Dutch side by conviction rather than by force. The *volksleiders* "die toen door dwang onderworpen waren" — who had then been subdued by force — were drawn into cooperation.

## Significance

The 1907–1908 resistance was the last major convulsion of the Atjeh-krijg. Its suppression did not produce peace in the older military sense so much as transform the conflict into a slower, administrative one — the "voorzichtige en geleidelijke vervanging van militaire bestuurders door civiele ambtenaren" and the accompanying "inkrimping der troepenmacht" that the handbook cites as "een krachtig bewijs" of the consolidation that came, after years of stubborn struggle, "betrekkelijk snel en onverwacht." The episode also fixed the negative reputation of van Daalen's governorship against the positive legend of Swart's, a contrast that framed Dutch memory of the Atjeh pacification for the remainder of the colonial period.

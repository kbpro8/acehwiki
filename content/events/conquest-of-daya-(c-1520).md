**The conquest of Daya** — in [[J. Kreemer|Kreemer]]'s history, the annexation by [[Sultan Ali Mughayat Shah|Sultan Ali Mughayat Shah (Raja Ibrahim)]] of the West Coast kingdom of [[Daya|Daya]] in approximately [[1520]] — was the first major territorial expansion of the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Atjèh sultanate]] beyond its harbour at [[Banda Acheh|Koeta Radja]], and the opening move of Atjèh's westward expansion along the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust of Sumatra]].

## The place of Daya in Atjèh's rise

Kreemer records the conquest in a single compressed clause within his account of the reign of [[Sultan Ali Mughayat Shah|Ali Mughayat Shah]]: "in ± [[1520]] lijfde hij het op de Westkust gelegen rijk [[Daya|Daja]] in" — "in approximately 1520 he annexed the kingdom of Daya situated on the West Coast." The annexation is the first of three conquests Kreemer attributes to Ali Mughayat Shah — followed by the conquest of [[Pidië|Pidië]] and [[Pasei|Pasè]] around [[1524]] and the war against [[Aru|Aroe]] — and it is the only one directed westward.

## Significance

The conquest of Daya established the precedent — renewed under [[Iskander Muda|Iskandar Muda]] in the early seventeenth century and contested with the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|VOC]] throughout the seventeenth — that the [[Sultanate of Acheh|Atjèh sultanate]] claimed sovereignty over the [[Westkust van Atjeh|Westkust of Sumatra]]. Daya, together with [[Indrapoera|Indrapoera]], [[Salida|Salida]], [[Padang|Padang]], [[Tikoe|Tikoe]] and [[Priaman|Priaman]], would form the chain of pepper-ports through which Atjehnese authority on the West Coast was articulated, and through which the [[Vereenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie|Compagnie]] would progressively displace Atjèh after [[1659]].

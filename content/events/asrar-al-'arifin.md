**The *Asrār al-ʿārifīn*** ("The Secrets of the Gnostics") is a Malay Sufi treatise by [[Hamzah Pansuri]], the celebrated pantheistic mystic of [[Acheh]] who flourished in the late sixteenth century and whose school dominated the religious life of the Achehnese court under [[Sultan Iskandar Muda|Iskandar Muda]] ([[1607]]–[[1636]]). The work is one of the principal sources for the [[Wujudiyyah]] mysticism that [[Nur al-Din al-Raniri|Nuruddin ar-Raniri]] attacked after his arrival in Acheh in [[1637]]; it is cited in the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān fī maʿrifat al-adyān*]] and in the apparatus of [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]]'s [[1955]] edition of Raniri's two Malay treatises. An edition of the *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* was published by [[J. Doorenbos|Doorenbos]].

## Content and character

The *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* is a sustained exposition of the Wujūdiyyah mysticism that Hamzah Pansuri had brought to its highest development at the Achehnese court. The work develops the doctrine of the unity of being (*waḥdat al-wujūd*) in its Indonesian form, drawing on the Sufi tradition of [[Ibn Arabi|Ibn ʿArabī]] and on Persian Sufi poetry, and setting out the stages of the mystical path by which the gnostic (ʿārif) advances from the outward forms of religion to the inner vision of the divine reality. In Voorhoeve's analysis of the *Tibjān*, the *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* is cited in connection with Hamzah's account of the fall of Iblīs from the light of God — an account that Raniri inserts, in the *Tibjān*, into his translation from the [[Tamhid fi bayan at-tawhid|*Tamhīd*]] of [[Abu Syakur as-Salimi|Abū Shakūr as-Sālimī]] in order to refute it.

## Raniri's refutation

The *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* is one of the works whose refutation occupies the central section of the [[Tibjan fi ma'rifat al-adyan|*Tibjān*]] — the section on the Wujūdiyyah — together with Hamzah's *[[Kitab al-Muntahi|Kitāb al-Muntahī]]* and [[Shaikh Shamsuddin of Sumatra|Shamsuddin of Pasai]]'s *[[Mir'at al-muhakkikin|Mirʾāt al-muḥaqqiqīn]]* and *[[Chirkah|Chirkah]]*. Raniri there charges Hamzah with teaching doctrines akin to those of the Tanāsuḥiyya (metempsychosists), the Falāsifa (philosophers), the Barāhima-Wathaniyya, the Sāmaniyya of Tibet, and the Ḥulūliyya (those who teach the indwelling of God in creation). Voorhoeve cites the *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* from the Doorenbos edition (p. 142) for the parallel passage; he notes that Raniri has inserted the quotation from Hamzah into his translation from the *Tamhīd*.

## Survival and editions

The *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* survived the burning of Hamzah Pansuri's books ordered by [[Iskandar Thani|Sultan Iskandar Thani]] — the suppression of the Wujūdiyyah that followed Raniri's [[Raniri's public debate against the Wujudiyyah in Acheh|public disputation]] at the Achehnese court — and is preserved in several Malay manuscripts. The edition of [[J. Doorenbos|Doorenbos]] made the text available to European scholarship and is the form in which [[P. Voorhoeve|Voorhoeve]] cites it in his apparatus to the *Tibjān*.

## Significance

The *Asrār al-ʿārifīn* is one of the principal monuments of the Wujūdiyyah mysticism of [[Acheh]] in its golden age, and one of the principal sources — together with the *[[Kitab al-Muntahi|Kitāb al-Muntahī]]* — for the doctrines against which Raniri's orthodox reaction was directed. Its survival, in manuscripts and in Doorenbos's edition, makes it possible to compare Raniri's polemical citations with Hamzah's own text, and so to follow the controversy over the unity of being in Acheh in the 1630s with a precision rare in the religious history of the early modern Malay world.

**Completion of Gajoweg** (1913) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. The 103 km Gajoweg from Bireuën to Takengon was completed in 1913. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to [[1913]].

## Account

The 103 km [[Gajoweg (Gayo Road)|Gajoweg]] from [[Bireuën]] to [[Takengon]] was completed in [[1913]]. Cost: ~f 20,000 per km, totaling ~2 million guilders. [[Van Kol]] called 2/10 of this 'doelloos verspild' (senselessly wasted); [[Resident Jongejans|Jongejans]] disputes this as undervaluing the peaceful development of the central region. The road is a conditio sine qua non for the coffee and tea enterprises, the [[Gouvernements Hars- en Terpentijnbedrijf]], and smaller agricultural enterprises. Replaced the old '[[vrouwenwegen]]' (women's paths) described by [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]]. Has had 'clearly confirmed' value in reducing [[Bantah (Acehnese term)|resistance]] by enabling European cultures, contact between hinterland population and the busy coast, increase of sales and needs.

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

**The unveiling of the bronze statue of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Pastoor H. C. Verbraak]] at [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]] on [[27 January]] [[1922]]** was the principal public commemoration of the *soldatenpastoor* of [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — a bronze statue (*bronzen standbeeld*) raised by subscription after Verbraak's [[1918|1918]] death at [[Magelang|Magelang]] and unveiled in the colonial city of [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]] with a commemorative address (*gedenkrede*) by [[Generaal Dijkstra|Generaal Dijkstra]] and a vote of thanks by the Very Reverend [[Pater van Hoof|Pater van Hoof]] on behalf of the Bishop and the clergy. The ceremony, fixed in the documentary record by the [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume of 1934]], is the most visible single monument to the Catholic military chaplaincy of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]].

## The earlier Kotta-Radja bust (1908)

The [[1922]] bronze statue at [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]] was not the first public monument raised to Verbraak. The [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume]] records that in [[1908]] — the year of Verbraak's departure from [[Acheh|Atjeh]] — a *borstbeeld* (bust) was raised to him at [[Koeta Radja|Kotta-Radja]] by the garrison he had served for thirty-four years. The Kotta-Radja bust was the first public monument to the chaplain, raised at the site of his ministry by the soldiers and officers who had known him personally. The Bandoeng bronze statue of [[1922]] was thus the second, more monumental, commemoration — raised after his death, in the colonial city rather than at the site of his ministry.

## The 27 January 1922 ceremony

The [[Vierhonderd jaren missie in Nederlandsch Indië (1934)|mission jubilee volume]] records the [[1922]] ceremony in a single compressed sentence:

> *"Later, op 27 Jan. 1922, werd te Bandoeng ter zijner eere een bronzen standbeeld onthuld, waarbij Generaal Dijkstra een gedenkrede uitsprak en den HoogEerw. Pater van Hoof namens den Bisschop en de geestelijkheid zijn dank betuigde."*

The record fixes three facts of the ceremony:

1. **The date and place**: [[27 January]] [[1922]] at [[Bandoeng|Bandoeng]] — the colonial city of the Preanger Highlands, by [[1922]] the rising administrative centre of the [[Netherlands East Indies|Netherlands East Indies]].
2. **The orator**: [[Generaal Dijkstra|Generaal Dijkstra]] — a senior Dutch colonial general officer, presumably with personal knowledge of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], who delivered the commemorative address (*gedenkrede*).
3. **The ecclesiastical response**: the Very Reverend [[Pater van Hoof|Pater van Hoof]] — on behalf of the Bishop and the clergy — expressed the thanks of the church.

The presence of a general as the orator at a clerical-statuary ceremony is itself a document of the institutional bond between the colonial army and the memory of its wartime chaplain — a bond that the foundation of the [[Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting|Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting]] in the same period was also intended to formalise. The Bandoeng statue, raised in bronze — the monumental medium — and unveiled with a military oration, was thus the most visible single monument to the Catholic military chaplaincy of the Atjeh war-footing.

## Significance

The unveiling of the Verbraak bronze statue at Bandoeng on [[27 January]] [[1922]] is significant for the encyclopedia of [[Acheh|Achehnese]] history on three counts. First, it is the most visible single public monument to the Catholic military chaplaincy of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — a bronze statue raised in the colonial city, with a military oration and an ecclesiastical vote of thanks. Second, it is the principal formally recorded military speech in honour of [[Pastoor H. C. Verbraak|Verbraak]] — the [[Generaal Dijkstra|Dijkstra]] *gedenkrede* — and therefore the principal official military testimony to the chaplain's place in the institutional memory of the colonial army. Third, it is the document, in the colonial memory, of the institutional bond between the colonial army and the Catholic church — a bond formalised in the same period by the foundation of the [[Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting|Pastoor Verbraak-Stichting]], to which the existing military homes were transferred. The Bandoeng statue, together with the earlier Kotta-Radja bust of [[1908]], constitutes the documentary monument-fleet of the Catholic chaplaincy of Atjeh.

The **Submission of Aceh** (in Dutch: *Onderwerping van Atjeh*) refers to the military campaign and subsequent colonial victory led by [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|J. B. van Heutsz]] in [[1896]] that successfully brought the [[Aceh]] region under definitive [[Dutch]] colonial control — see [[Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz|Van Heutsz's governorship]] for the fuller Snouck Hurgronje account of the campaign itself. This event was later cited by [[Keuchenius|W. H. J. Keuchenius]] as a model of effective and decisive governance.

## Keuchenius's perspective

In April [[1896]], as Keuchenius was publishing his [[Atjeh-opstellen]] (Aceh essays), he interpreted the transfer of Lieutenant-Colonel J. B. van Heutsz to Aceh as a critical turning point. He argued that the situation in Aceh, after many failed experiments, could only be resolved through "[[Kracht van wapenen|kracht van wapenen]]" (force of arms). He identified Van Heutsz as the only man capable of this, based on his understanding of the situation. The success of this campaign was seen as a "brilliant outcome" that served as a guarantee for what the colony could expect from Van Heutsz in his subsequent role as Governor-General.

Keuchenius later, in his [[1905]] letter, used the successful submission of Aceh as an analogy for the boldness he believed was needed to solve colonial economic crises. He adapted a phrase from Van Heutsz's own earlier brochure on the subject, stating: "The three mentioned subjects gnaw at all economic progress in these regions; they must be provided for as soon as possible. Let us finally show the civilized world through decisive actions that we want to and are able to do so."

## Significance

The submission of Aceh represents a pivotal moment in Dutch colonial history. It ended a long and costly war, established a more stable colonial administration in northern Sumatra, and became a symbol of effective, forceful colonial policy. Its legacy was used by contemporaries like Keuchenius to advocate for a similar, uncompromising approach to other challenges facing the colony, such as economic reform and the management of the Chinese community.

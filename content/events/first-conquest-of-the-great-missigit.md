**First Conquest of the Great Missigit** was a significant military engagement that occurred on [[10 April]] [[1873]] during the [[First Aceh Expedition]]. The Dutch forces, led by General [[J.H.R. Köhler]] and field commanders like Major [[F.P. Cavaljé]], attacked and temporarily captured [[Atjeh's Groote Missigit]] from Acehnese defenders.

## Background
Following the [[Landing on Atjeh's Coasts]] on [[8 April]] [[1873]] and the [[Reconnaissance of the Kraton]] on [[9 April]] [[1873]], the Dutch high command determined that a major assault was required to seize the strongly fortified [[Atjeh's Groote Missigit]], which served as a crucial defensive position protecting the [[Kraton of Atjeh]]. On the morning of [[10 April]] [[1873]], the troops marched from their coastal bivouac towards the interior.

## The Battle
The Dutch forces were organized into a vanguard led by Major [[F.P. Cavaljé]] and a main body under Captain-Lieutenant [[J.W. Binkes]]. The vanguard included units from the [[3e Bataljon Infanterie]] and was spearheaded by Captain [[J.P. van Lier]], alongside Lieutenants [[H.L.J. Ligtenberg]], [[A.S.H. Booms]], and [[C.J. Zwager]]. The assault was marked by fierce hand-to-hand combat, as Acehnese defenders resisted vigorously from the walls and inner sanctuaries of the mosque.

Despite heavy fire, the Dutch managed to breach the western walls using [[Stormladders]]. Captain [[J.P. van Lier]]'s column was the first to enter the enclosure. However, the position proved untenable due to continuous attacks and heavy casualties. By late afternoon, General [[J.H.R. Köhler]] ordered the [[Retreat from the Missigit]], and the forces withdrew to a bivouac near [[Village Merdoehati]]. During this engagement, several notable acts of bravery occurred, though the mosque was swiftly reoccupied by the Acehnese after the Dutch departed.

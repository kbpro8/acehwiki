**Van Swieten's Proclamation of 31 January 1874** was the official proclamation issued by Lieutenant-general [[jan-van-swieten|Jan van Swieten]] in his capacity as civil and military commissioner in [[acheh|Atjeh]], by which the Dutch government annexed [[groot-atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] by right of conquest following the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]] and the death of the sultan on [[26-january|26 January]] [[1874]]. The proclamation — which was approved by telegram of [[1 February]] [[1874]] from the Opperbestuur — invited the chiefs of the three [[sagi|sagis]] and of the [[onderhoorigheden|Onderhoorigheden]] to make written submission, guaranteed the free exercise of religion and the preservation of popular institutions, and prohibited the carrying of weapons.

## Background

After the [[Capture of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the Kraton]] on [[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]] and the death of the sultan of Atjeh on [[26-january|26 January]] [[1874]] of cholera at Pagger Ajer, the question arose: "What was to be done in these circumstances?" As the source work observes, "Of making a tractaat there was now, also after all that had happened, no more question. The knowledge that one had obtained of the conditions and the form of government in Atjeh gave little hope of it." On [[31-january|31 January]] [[1874]] Van Swieten accordingly issued his proclamation.

## Text of the Proclamation

The proclamation announced:

- "that the people of Groot-Atjeh, after a brave defence and a stubborn struggle, had had to yield to the Dutch arms;
- that the people was conquered, the Kraton taken, the sultan dead, and many chiefs fallen in the struggle;
- that the land therefore, by right of conquest, belonged to the Netherlands-Indian government;
- that he, in the interest of trade, industry and agriculture, which are the foundations of popular prosperity, wished to make an end of the consequences of the war, and therefore invited the chiefs of the three Sagis and of the Onderhoorigheden to make written submission;
- that the blockade would be raised for the states that had given written proofs of submission, and that trade, except in weapons of war, could be resumed; on condition, however, that they flew the Dutch flag and provided their ships with proper war-passes;
- that henceforth no other flag than the Dutch and that of friendly powers would be recognised, and neither the Acehnese nor own flag might be flown;
- that all states that should have submitted to the new order of things could count on the protection of the Netherlands-Indian government, and that free exercise of religion and the preservation of popular institutions were guaranteed, but that the carrying of weapons would be excluded;
- that those, however, who should not have offered submission within a reasonable time, nor have resumed their ordinary occupations and trades, would be considered hostile, and would expose themselves and their dependants to the destruction of their kampongs and other hostile acts."

## Approval and Reception

The Opperbestuur approved this procedure by telegram of [[1 February]] [1874], and considered it also necessary to set up a fortification in Atjeh proper and to establish the Dutch there permanently. At the same time the Onderhoorigheden were to be visited and invited or compelled to accept the Dutch flag and to sign acts of recognition. Where no acts of recognition were signed, direct Dutch administration would have to be introduced.

## Significance

The proclamation marked the formal annexation of Groot-Atjeh and the formal end of the sultanate — though the sultanate continued in shadowy form at [[keumala|Keumala]] under [[toeankoe-hasjim|Toewankoe Haschim]] and the war-party, with the young pretender [[tuanku-muhamat-dawot|Toewankoe Daoed]] destined for the sultan's rank. As the source work observes, in the opinion of the supreme commander the Kraton and some other points were to be made into permanent fortifications; the necessary bridges and roads were to be made and everything put in order for a permanent establishment. A mobile column was to be charged with the necessary offensive operations, but for the time being all fighting was to be avoided as much as possible, for the pacification of chiefs and population was now the sole object.

In fact the population did not submit; and the proclamation inaugurated the long second phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]], in which the Dutch held the hoofd-stelling of [[Koeta-Radja|Kota Radja]] and a growing ring of posts while the Acehnese carried on a stubborn guerrilla resistance for more than three decades.

## Brooshooft's Account

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft presents the proclamation of [[31-january|31 January]] [[1874]] as the solemn constitutional act by which Dutch sovereignty over [[aceh|Atjeh]] was proclaimed by right of conquest, two days after the unopposed entry into the [[Kraton (institution)|Kraton]] and five days after the death of Sultan [[Alaedin Mahmut Shah|Aladin Mahmoed Shah]] from cholera. Read out by [[Jan van Swieten|Van Swieten]] in the presence of the troops and the few Acehnese chiefs who had come in, the proclamation declared Groot-Atjeh annexed to the [[netherlands|Netherlands]] crown and promised respect for religion, custom and property. Brooshooft notes that the proclamation was issued in spite of the absence of a recognised sultan with whom to treat, and that its practical effect was to transform the war from a punitive expedition into a permanent colonial occupation.

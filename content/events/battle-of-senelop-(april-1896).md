**The Battle of Senelop (April 1896)** was the Dutch military engagement fought at the besieged Dutch post of [[Senelop]] in the [[XXII Moekims]] of [[Groot-Atjeh]] in the course of the spring [[1896]] crisis that followed [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|Toekoe Oemar's defection]] from the Dutch alliance. The engagement produced two of the seven officer fatalities of the spring [[1896]] crisis — Lieutenant [[J. W. Vuijk|Vuijk]] on [[30 March]] [[1896]] and Lieutenant [[J. B. Zon|Zon]] on [[19 April]] [[1896]] — and was one of the costliest single engagements of the spring crisis. Major [[Okhuysen]] and fifty subordinates were wounded in the *verwoede gevecht dat om Senelop geleverd was* — the fierce battle that was fought around Senelop.

## The siege of Senelop

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album of 1896]] records that the post at [[Senelop]] — together with the posts at [[Anagaloeng]], [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] — was besieged by the forces of [[Toekoe Oemar]] after his [[Toekoe Oemar's defection (1896)|defection]] in [[March]] [[1896]]. The album's narrative of the siege records the desperate situation of the garrison, which was *slechts 350 passen afstand van Anagaloeng* (only 350 paces from Anagaloeng) but could not be relieved from that post because of the smallness of the Anagaloeng garrison. The siege was, in the album's chronology, the longest of the four sieges of the spring [[1896]] crisis — from [[March]] to [[April]] [[1896]].

## The death of Vuijk on 30 March 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Lieutenant [[J. W. Vuijk|Vuijk]] was killed at Senelop on [[30 March]] [[1896]] — three weeks after the defection, and ten days before the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] of [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]]. The album's narrative of Vuijk's death records that his body, *gevolgd door elf gewonden, en eenige soldaten*, was *de poort van onze benting binnengedragen, als eerste offer van Toekoe Oemars afval* — *followed by eleven wounded, and some soldiers, was carried through the gate of our benting, as the first offering of Toekoe Oemar's defection*. The sacrificial framing of Vuijk's death is the album's principal characterisation of the [[1896]] crisis — the moment at which the long *concentratie* period was redeemed by Dutch blood.

## The death of Zon on 19 April 1896

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that Lieutenant [[J. B. Zon|Zon]] was killed at Senelop on [[19 April]] [[1896]] — ten days after the [[Relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit (April 1896)|relief of Biloel and Lamkoenjit]] in which Captain [[E. P. A. Geluk|Geluk]] had been killed, and five days after the [[Battle of Tjot Rang (14 April 1896)|battle of Tjot Rang]] in which Lieutenant [[H. L. Maarschalk|Maarschalk]] had been killed. The album's narrative of Zon's death places it within the chronology of the seven officer fatalities of the spring [[1896]] crisis:

- [[K. H. Westendorp|Lieutenant K. H. Westendorp]] — died of wounds [[11 March]] [[1896]]
- [[J. W. Vuijk|Lieutenant J. W. Vuijk]] — killed at Senelop [[30 March]] [[1896]]
- [[E. P. A. Geluk|Captain E. P. A. Geluk]] — killed at Biloel-Lamkoenjit [[9 April]] [[1896]]
- [[H. L. Maarschalk|Lieutenant H. L. Maarschalk]] — killed at Tjot Rang [[14 April]] [[1896]]
- [[J. B. Zon|Lieutenant J. B. Zon]] — killed at Senelop [[19 April]] [[1896]]
- [[C. G. A. C. Krohne|Lieutenant C. G. A. C. Krohne]] — died of wounds [[4 May]] [[1896]]
- [[H. O. W. L. Kramer|Captain H. O. W. L. Kramer]] — killed at Lampisang [[23 May]] [[1896]]

## The casualties

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records the casualties of the *verwoede gevecht dat om Senelop geleverd was* — the fierce battle that was fought around Senelop — as: Major [[Okhuysen]] and fifty subordinates wounded, and thirteen killed. The engagement was, in the album's narrative, one of the costliest single engagements of the spring [[1896]] crisis — and Senelop was the costliest single post, with two officer fatalities (Vuijk and Zon) and thirteen other ranks killed.

## The relief

The [[Atjeh-Album (Eigen Haard, 1896)|Atjeh-Album]] records that the post at Senelop was relieved by the column that had previously relieved [[Biloel]] and [[Lamkoenjit]] on [[9 April]] [[9 April]] [[1896]] and [[Anagaloeng]] on [[18 April]] [[18 April]] [[1896]]. The relief of Senelop was the last of the four relief operations of April [[1896]], and it was followed by the dismantling of the post and the withdrawal of the garrison to the *geconcentreerde linie*.

## Significance

The Battle of Senelop is significant in the documentary record of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] as the costliest single engagement of the spring [[1896]] crisis — the engagement that produced two of the seven officer fatalities of the spring, and that cost Major [[Okhuysen]] and fifty subordinates wounded, and thirteen killed. The engagement was the longest of the four sieges of the spring [[1896]] crisis, and the album's sacrificial framing of the death of [[J. W. Vuijk|Vuijk]] on [[30 March]] [[1896]] — as the *first offering* of the defection — is the album's principal characterisation of the [[1896]] crisis.

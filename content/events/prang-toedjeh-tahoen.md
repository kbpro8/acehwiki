**The Prang toedjéh tahoen** — the "War of Ten Years" — was the conflict between [[Ménèt]] of the [[Boekét Ewéh|Boekét Ewéh]] lineage and the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultan of Atjeh]] that broke out when the former refused to pay the *haol* — the customary tribute — owed to the Acehnese overlord, and that ended with the capture of many [[Redjë's|*rédjë*'s]] of the [[Meerstreek|Meerstreek]] by an Acehnese expeditionary force and the surrender, as ransom, of the *bawar* known as the *keris kepala soeasa*. The war occupies a central place in the foundation legends of the [[Boekét|Boekét]] polity of the [[Gajoland|Gajolanden]] and explains the long-running rivalry between the Boekét-Lah and Boekét-Ewéh lines over the dignity of *kédjoerön Boekét*.

## Background: the stolen bawar

The immediate prelude to the war was an act of deception within the Boekét family itself. [[Mérah Lama|Mérah Lama]], eldest son of [[Saëda|Saëda]] by the latter's second Acehnese wife, had inherited the *bawar* — the set of five *ornamenten* conferred by the Sultan of Atjeh on Saëda as tokens of his dignity as *Rëdjë Boekét* — and ruled as *kédjoerön* from [[Kébajakan|Kébajakan]]. During Mérah Lama's reign, however, his stepbrother [[Ménèt|Ménèt]] "door een list" secured the principal *bawar*, the "*keris kepala soeasa*," and refused to return it. As the adat held that "wie de bawar heeft, ook kédjoerön is," Ménèt thereby arrogated the *kédjoerönship* to himself — a usurpation that his half-brother [[Panglima Dagang|Panglima Dagang]], the progenitor of the [[Boekét Lah|Boekét Lah]] lineage, sought to remedy by returning to the Meerstreek and assuming the title of *kédjoerön Boekét* from [[Seroelë|Seroelë]].

## Refusal of tribute and Acehnese reprisal

The two parallel *kédjoeröns* now coexisted uneasily — Ménèt in the Meerstreek, Panglima Dagang at Seroelë. The crisis came when Ménèt refused to pay the *haol* due to the Sultan of Atjeh. The "weigering om haol voor den Sultan van Atjeh op te brengen" precipitated the *prang toedjéh tahoen*. The war "eindigde met de gevangenneming van tal van *rédjë*'s uit de Meerstreek door de Atjehers" — the Acehnese captured many *rédjë*'s of the Meerstreek. To ransom them, Ménèt was compelled to hand over the *bawar* — the *keris* — to the Acehnese commander. He thereby "verloor feitelijk zijne aanspraken op het *kédjoerönship*" — in fact lost his claims to the *kédjoerönship* — the more so because Panglima Dagang, "de andere *kédjoerön* Boekét," managed to buy back the *kris* and so to reunite "alle bawars in één hand."

## Significance

The *prang toedjéh tahoen* resolved the contest for the Boekét *kédjoerönship* in favour of the Boekét-Lah line: Panglima Dagang's grandson, as *Rëdjë Boekét* of Seroelë, was after a three-month war acknowledged by all the chiefs as *kédjoerön Boekét*, and the dignity "ook steeds in het geslacht Boekét-Lah is gebleven." Yet the Boekét-Ewéh descendants of Ménèt, "ondanks het gemis der officieele waardigheidsteekenen," continued to style themselves *kédjoerön* and to act as the equals of the Lah's, generating "voortdurenden naijver en onderlingen strijd" that persisted into the Dutch period. The episode is also the clearest demonstration in the Gayo foundation-legends of the reach of the [[Sultans of Atjeh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] into the interior — a suzerainty exercised not through permanent garrisons but through occasional punitive expeditions backed by the symbolic capital of the *bawar*.

**The Battle of Aneu Galong (1896)** was the defence of the Acehnese position at [[Aneu Galong|Aneu'Galong]] in [[1896]] in which the eldest son and successor of *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]]* was killed as commandant, and in which the *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]* — father of the future resistance leader *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]* — also fell as a *geloofsgetuige* (a witness of the faith). The battle is the chronological anchor of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]] narrative in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*: it is the moment at which the patriarchal generation of the Tiriō family was killed, leaving the inheritance of the resistance to be carried forward by the surviving sons and, eventually, by the grandson who was an infant at the time.

## The eldest son as commandant

"Van de vier zoons van *[[Teungkoe Sjech Saman di Tiri]]* was de oudste, tevens de opvolger, in [[1896]] gesneuveld als commandant bij de verdediging [[Aneu Galong|Aneu Galong]]." The eldest son was his father's successor as head of the family; his death at Aneu Galong in [[1896]] therefore transferred the leadership of the Tiriō resistance to the surviving brothers — *[[Tgk. Lambada]]*, *[[Tgk. di Boekét]]* and *[[Tgk. Tjhi Majét]]* — and, eventually, to the next generation. The boy *[[Tgk. Tjhi Ma'at]]*, son of the *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]* who also fell in the same defence, was a *zuigeling* — an infant at the breast — when his father died; by [[1911]] he would be fifteen, and the last leader of the Tiriō bende.

## Tgk. Mat Amin as geloofsgetuige

"De bij [[Aneu Galong|Aneu'Galong]] gevallen *[[Tgk. Mat Amin]]*." The phrase *geloofsgetuige* — witness of the faith — is the book's term for those who fell in the resistance as *[[sjahid]]*, martyrs of the *[[prang sabi]]*. Mat Amin's death at Aneu Galong placed his infant son in the inheritance of the holy war: the boy would grow up to refuse submission on the grounds that "Zijn Vader was als geloofsgetuige gevallen, hij zou in diens voetstappen treden."

## 1896 as the turning point of the resistance

The year [[1896]] is, in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]*, the moment at which "het lijdelijk afwachten was afgelopen en de troepen zelf tot actie overgingen" — the moment at which the passive waiting was over and the troops themselves went over to the offensive. [[Dominee Thenu]] — who had come to [[Atjeh|Aceh]] in [[1894]] — spoke the troops before battle for the first time in [[1896]]. The Battle of Aneu Galong therefore belongs to the same turn as the broader Dutch offensive of the late 1890s, the offensive that would culminate in the [[Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]]-era colonnes of the early 1900s.

## Significance

The Battle of Aneu Galong is significant in *[[Oom Ambon van het K.N.I.L.]]* as the chronological anchor of the Tiriō narrative. It is the moment at which the patriarchal generation of the family was killed — the successor-son and the *geloofsgetuige* Mat Amin — and the inheritance of the resistance was transferred to the surviving sons and the infant grandson. The boy who was a *zuigeling* at Aneu Galong would, fifteen years later, be the last leader of the Tiriō bende, and would be killed by the korporaal [[Nussy]] on [[3 December]] [[1911]]. The battle is therefore the beginning of the end of the Acehnese religious resistance, and the precondition of the [[colonne Schmidt campaign against the Tiriō Teungkoes (1910-1911)|colonne Schmidt]].

**The Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)** was the operation by which [[kolonel Diepenbrock|overste Diepenbrock]], at the head of two half-battalions and a field-artillery battery, finally carried the kampong of [[lemboe|Lemboe]] — the "key to the dike" at the southern end of the [[pedir-dijk|Pedir-dijk]] — six weeks after the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] and almost a year after the position had been briefly held and then abandoned in the [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|battle of Lemboe]] of Christmas [[1873]].

## The object

Lemboe was, in Borel's phrase, "the key to the dike." It had been briefly in Dutch hands on [[25 December|25]]–[[26-december|26 December]] [[1873]] and then abandoned by van Swieten; the Atjehneezen had since heavily fortified it. The bend of the [[acheh-river|Atjeh-rivier]] at [[kuta-alam|Kota Alam]] just downstream, and the [[iii-mukims|III Moekims]] beyond, were both commanded from its works.

## The march

At four in the morning of [[7-november|7 November]] [[1874]] Diepenbrock marched. The attack came from two directions at once: from [[penajoeng|Penajoeng]] and from [[langkroek|Langkroek]], across the open *sawah* (irrigated rice field). Contrary to expectation, resistance was light. The western part of Lemboe — known as [[berauw|Berauw]] — with the main dike fortifications, fell first. Three posts were at once built, including a strong southern *rivier-benteng* armed with an 8 c.M. rifled gun, and a north-eastern post.

## Casualties and Borel's verdict

The day's cost was slight — Diepenbrock himself and some twenty men and coolies wounded, one officer's servant killed — but Diepenbrock was among the wounded. The lightness of the resistance surprised the Dutch; Borel attributes it partly to the demoralisation that had followed the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|fall of Langkroek]] and the [[Destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification (18 September 1874)|destruction of Lampoe Oek]]. He contrasts the easy conquest of [[7-november|7 November]] [[1874]] with the costly failure of [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|December 1873]], and uses the contrast to hammer home his old thesis: that van Swieten's abandonment of captured ground had multiplied Dutch losses. The conquest of Lemboe opened the way for the [[Conquest of Kota Alam (13 November 1874)|conquest of Kota Alam]] six days later, on [[13-november|13 November]] [[1874]].

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft records that after the withdrawal of Van Swieten and the departure of the bulk of the second-expedition troops in April [[1874]], Pel systematically reduced the remaining enemy strongpoints around Kota Radja. Lemboe, which had repulsed the Dutch in December [[1873]], was finally taken and permanently occupied in the late [[september|September]]–[[november|November]] [[1874]] operations, becoming the site of one of the chain of posts (Langkroek West and Oost, Berawang, Lemboe, Rivierbenting, Kota Alam) that made up the new fortified ring. The heavy guns that Van Swieten had sent back to Java had to be recalled to reduce these positions, and the operations cost a steady drain of casualties.

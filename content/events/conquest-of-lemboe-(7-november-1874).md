**The Conquest of Lemboe (7 November 1874)** was the operation by which [[Kolonel Diepenbrock|overste Diepenbrock]], at the head of two half-battalions and a field-artillery battery, finally carried the kampong of [[Lemboe]] — the "key to the dike" at the southern end of the [[Pedir-dijk]] — six weeks after the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|conquest of Langkroek]] and almost a year after the position had been briefly held and then abandoned in the [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|battle of Lemboe]] of Christmas [[1873]].

## The object

Lemboe was, in Borel's phrase, "the key to the dike." It had been briefly in Dutch hands on [[25 December|25]]–[[26 December]] [[1873]] and then abandoned by van Swieten; the Atjehneezen had since heavily fortified it. The bend of the [[Atjeh-rivier]] at [[Kota Alam]] just downstream, and the [[III Moekims]] beyond, were both commanded from its works.

## The march

At four in the morning of [[7 November]] [[1874]] Diepenbrock marched. The attack came from two directions at once: from [[Penajoeng]] and from [[Langkroek]], across the open *sawah* (irrigated rice field). Contrary to expectation, resistance was light. The western part of Lemboe — known as [[Berauw]] — with the main dike fortifications, fell first. Three posts were at once built, including a strong southern *rivier-benteng* armed with an 8 c.M. rifled gun, and a north-eastern post.

## Casualties and Borel's verdict

The day's cost was slight — Diepenbrock himself and some twenty men and coolies wounded, one officer's servant killed — but Diepenbrock was among the wounded. The lightness of the resistance surprised the Dutch; Borel attributes it partly to the demoralisation that had followed the [[Conquest of Langkroek (28 September 1874)|fall of Langkroek]] and the [[Destruction of the Lampoe Oek fortification (18 September 1874)|destruction of Lampoe Oek]]. He contrasts the easy conquest of [[7 November]] [[1874]] with the costly failure of [[Battle of Lemboe (25–26 December 1873)|December 1873]], and uses the contrast to hammer home his old thesis: that van Swieten's abandonment of captured ground had multiplied Dutch losses. The conquest of Lemboe opened the way for the [[Conquest of Kota Alam (13 November 1874)|conquest of Kota Alam]] six days later, on [[13 November]] [[1874]].

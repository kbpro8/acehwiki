**The Tweede Atjeh-expeditie of December [[1873]] – April [[1874]]** — the second Dutch military expedition against the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] — was the expedition, under the command of [[Jan van Swieten|General van Swieten]], that began with the landing at [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] on [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]] and concluded with the [[Conquest of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the kraton]] of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]], the death of the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]], and Van Swieten's proclamation annexing Atjeh to the Dutch dominions. The expedition opened the forty-five-year war that would end with the [[Handover of Aceh to a civil Gouverneur (1918)|handover of 1918]], and its principal legacy — the Dutch *stelling* (defensive position) around the conquered kraton — would consume Dutch military power for three decades.

## The Assembly at Batavia

While the [[Tweede Atjeh-expeditie (1873-1874)|second expedition]] was being assembled at [[Batavia|Batavia]], all available information about Atjeh — "*die bij de eerste expeditie geheel ontbraken*" (which had been entirely lacking in the first expedition) — was gathered and prepared. The Atjehers themselves had in this period made vain attempts to obtain help from foreign powers. [[Habib Abdul Rachman El-Zahir|Habib Abdul Rachman el-Zahir]], already before the first expedition despatched to [[Constantinople|Konstantinopel]] for this purpose, returned empty-handed to [[Penang|Penang]]. There the so-called *[[Atjehsche Raad van Achten|Atjehsche Raad van Achten]]* (Atjeh Council of Eight) was formed, to manage the external affairs of Atjeh. Peace proposals made by it to the Dutch were rejected.

## The Landing (9 December 1873)

The expedition was excellently prepared and equipped, but an outbreak of cholera on Java briefly delayed its departure. The ships arrived at the roadstead of [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] towards the end of [[November 1873|November]] [[1873]]. Even on the ships the cholera raged among the troops, while heavy rains further delayed the landing until [[9 December]] [[9 December]] [[1873]].

## The Force

The expeditionary force of approximately 9,000 combatants was divided into three brigades, one of which remained in reserve at [[Padang|Padang]] and only appeared on the scene at the end of [[January 1874|January]] [[1874]]. A strong squadron of the [[Koninklijke Marine|Marine]] supported the operations.

## The Advance and the Taking of the Missigit (7 January 1874)

The troops advanced under heavy fighting. On [[7 January]] [[7 January]] [[1874]] the [[Meusigit Raja|groote Missigit]] (great mosque) was taken. "*Dit feit maakte overal diepen indruk.*" (This fact made a deep impression everywhere), Kruijt writes; several chiefs came forward to make their submission.

## The Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)

On [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] the [[Kraton of Aceh|kraton]] itself fell into Dutch hands. "*Het hoofddoel, de verovering van den kraton, bereikt werd*" (the principal object, the conquest of the kraton, was achieved). The fall of the kraton marked the formal end of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] as a sovereign state.

## The Death of the Sultan (26 January 1874)

Two days after the fall of the kraton, on [[26 January]] [[26 January]] [[1874]], the [[Sultan of Aceh|Sultan]] of Atjeh died of cholera. The question of what to do next — Kruijt writes — "*heeft den toestand in Atjeh tot op den huidigen dag beheerscht*" (has governed the situation in Atjeh down to the present day). The original plan had been to conclude a treaty with the Sultan and to leave the integrity of the country intact. Now, with the Sultan dead, Van Swieten proposed instead that the Dutch should step into the Sultan's place, and drafted a proclamation to the chiefs accordingly.

## The Proclamation and the Stelling

To those chiefs who submitted and recognised Dutch supremacy, self-government would be left; where this was not the case, the Dutch would attempt direct rule. To give effect to this policy and to show the Atjehers that the Dutch would remain in the country, a strong defensive position would be taken up in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] as a permanent establishment, and from it the work of subjugation would be prosecuted. The position chosen was the kraton itself, with certain fixed points on the [[Atjeh River|Atjeh-rivier]] securing the undisturbed possession of that waterway, and other points around the kraton. All available energies were devoted to preparing the position for a permanent garrison.

The proclamation was circulated around the coasts by the [[Koninklijke Marine|Marine]]; the chiefs were required to sign an *[[onderwerpingsacte|onderwerpingsacte]]* (act of submission), in which they acknowledged Dutch supremacy, undertook to fly the Dutch flag, and promised to enter into no political relations with other powers.

## The Coastal Submission

The result of Van Swieten's *proclamatietocht* (proclamation tour) was that several chiefs submitted and signed the act of sovereignty. Many of these had already, before the war, wished to follow the example of [[Edi|Edi]] in submitting and had asked for the Dutch flag, but had been put off. Others from the [[Westkust|West Coast]] submitted at [[Kota Radja|Kota Radja]]. On the [[Oostkust|East Coast]] the new policy made little difference, thanks to the trade control already in force there. But on the [[Noordkust|North]] and [[Westkust|West Coasts]] the flag and the act of recognition were "*veelal eenvoudig door een boodschapper aan land gezonden, zonder meer*" (usually simply sent ashore by a messenger, and nothing more), and trade was opened without supervision — "*zoodat de deuren er wijd werden opengezet om den vijand van alles te voorzien en het verzet te stijven*" (so that the doors were thrown wide open to supply the enemy with everything and to stiffen the resistance).

## The Departure (26 April 1874)

On [[16 April]] [[16 April]] [[1874]] a numerous Dutch column, after a bloody engagement, was repulsed before an enemy fortification just south of the kraton — an *échec* that went unavenged. Nevertheless, Van Swieten judged his task accomplished. On [[26 April]] [[26 April]] [[1874]] — one year to the day after the re-embarkation of the [[Eerste Atjeh-expeditie (1873)|first expedition]] — he returned with the main force to Java, leaving [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Colonel Pel]] with 3,000 men to carry out his programme.

## Kruijt's Judgement

Kruijt's judgement of the second expedition is double-edged. The expedition had achieved its principal object — the conquest of the kraton — and had thereby marked the formal end of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] as a sovereign state. But the *Nota* that Van Swieten left to Colonel Pel was, in Kruijt's judgement, "*in bewoordingen, die voor verschillende uitleggingen vatbaar waren*" (in terms that were susceptible to various interpretations), and without regard to the actual situation. It was the first instance of the recurrent Dutch pattern — "*dat ophemelen van kleine of onbeteekenende gebeurtenissen in ons voordeel, dat blindblijven voor verschijnselen in ons nadeel*" (that extolling of small or insignificant events in our favour, that blindness to phenomena to our disadvantage) — from which an untrue representation of the actual state of affairs was born.

## Significance

The Tweede Atjeh-expeditie of [[December 1873|December]] [[1873]]–[[April 1874|April]] [[1874]] is the foundational military event of the Dutch colonial administration of [[Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden]]. The [[Conquest of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|fall of the kraton]] of [[Kota Radja|Koeta Radja]] on [[24 January]] [[24 January]] [[1874]] marked the formal end of the [[Sultanate of Aceh|Sultanate of Atjeh]] as a sovereign state and the beginning of more than three decades of Dutch military and administrative engagement with Atjeh. Van Swieten's decision to step into the Sultan's place, rather than to seek a treaty with his successor, bound the Dutch to a permanent military presence in [[Groot-Atjeh|Groot-Atjeh]] and committed them to the subjugation of the *onderhoorigheden* along the coasts. The *stelling* (defensive position) he established around the kraton would be expanded by [[J. L. J. H. Pel|Pel]] and [[General van der Heijden|Van der Heijden]] into the elaborate *postenlinie* that consumed Dutch military power for the next two decades.

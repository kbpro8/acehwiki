**The Bombardment of the Kraton (January 1874)** was the sustained artillery fire maintained by the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)|second Aceh expedition]] against the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] of [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]] for nineteen days — from the fall of the [[Mesigit of Atjeh|Mesigit]] on [[6-january|6 January]] [[1874]] to the [[Fall of the Kraton (24 January 1874)|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[24-january|24 January]] — during which a kanonbatterij built into the eastern face of the Mesigit, supported by mortieren of 20 c.M., battered the earthen walls of the Kraton "met meer of minder kracht," and which Borel credits, alone among the arms of the expedition, with actually driving the Atjehneezen out of the works. Yet the same bombardment, he insists, was mismanaged in its planning, miscalculated in its munitie, and — most damning of all — its authors, the [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]], were "met geen wapen op meer stiefmoederlijke wijze bedeeld" when the decorations were distributed.

## The Batteries

Once the [[Storming of the Mesigit (6 January 1874)|Mesigit]] had been carried on [[6-january|6 January]] [[1874]], the engineers and the artillerie set to work. A kanonbatterij was built into the eastern face of the captured voorwerk, with embrasures opened through the borstwering toward the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]]. Heavy mortieren of 20 c.M. — large enough to throw their schrapnel and granaten over the walls into the interior — were brought up by hand from the beach at [[penajoeng|Penajoeng]] and emplaced behind the Mesigit. From these batteries a continuous fire was maintained against the Kraton for the next nineteen days, varying in intensity with the munitie supply, the weather, and the perceived effect on the enemy. Half of the 12 c.M. puntgranaten fired by the [[Voorlaadkanonnen in Atjeh|voorlaad-kanonnen]] failed to explode — a defect Borel, an artilleryman himself, treats as scandalous — and the better [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen of 12 c.M.]] were used sparingly, for fear of expending their harder-to-replace munitie.

## The Misjudgement

The Dutch had over-estimated the construction of the Kraton. They had believed the walls to be of thick stone, against which only sustained, deliberate fire could prevail. In fact, as Borel would discover on entering the works on [[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]], the borstwering was an aarden wall — a mere earthen rampart — hidden under a thick crust of [[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboe-doeri]]. Against such a target the lichte veldgeschut, had it been properly served, could have made short work; the elaborate mortar bombardment was, in retrospect, largely wasted. The munitie calculations, Borel adds, had not foreseen a sustained fire of nineteen days, and the batteries were repeatedly forced to slacken their rate for want of shells.

## Outcome

Despite these mismanagements, Borel's verdict is clear: it was the artillerie — and the artillerie alone — that drove the Atjehneezen out of the Kraton. The infantry assaults of [[6-january|6 January]] had failed at a cost of 220 dood en gekwetsten; the [[Capture of Kota Goenoengan (12 January 1874)|easy capture of Kota Goenoengan]] on [[12-january|12 January]] had been ignored; the [[omtrekking|omtrekkende beweging]] of [[23 January|23]]–[[24-january|24 January]] [[1874]] would find the works already deserted, "op een grijsaard en een zieken Atjehnees na." It was the nineteen days of mortar fire, landing schrapnel and granaten among the dwellings and the mosques of the interior, that broke the enemy's grip. Yet when the belooningen were distributed, the [[Vesting-artillerie in Atjeh|vesting-artillerie]] — the very arm that had carried the Kraton — was treated, Borel writes, "met geen wapen op meer stiefmoederlijke wijze" — with no arm more step-motherly. Within weeks [[Jan van Swieten|van Swieten]] would ship the vesting-artillerie and the [[Achterlaadkanonnen van 12 c.M.|achterlaadkanonnen]] back to Java, depriving the bezettingskorps of the very geschut that had just proved its worth.

## Brooshooft's Account

Brooshooft describes the naval and land bombardment of the Kraton and surrounding positions in the first weeks of [[january|January]] [[1874]], between the fall of the Great Missigit on [[6-january|6 January]] and the entry on [[24-january|24 January]]. Mortars and field-guns, now more favourably sited after the capture of the missigit, kept up a steady fire on the Kraton enclosure and on the fortified villages to the south; but the bombardment was not decisive in itself. It was [[Verspijck|Verspyck]]'s encircling movement on [[23-january|23 January]], which threatened to cut off the defenders' retreat, that persuaded the Acehnese to slip away during the night of 23/24 January, leaving the Kraton to be entered unopposed.

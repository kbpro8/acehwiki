**The Pacification of Atjeh** (Dutch: *De pacificatie van Atjeh*) refers to the eventual subjugation of the Achehnese sultanate by Dutch military and political means. The process, which began with the first Dutch expedition to Atjeh in [[1873]], was carried through to completion under [[Governor Van Heutsz]], who was appointed Governor of [[Gouvernement Atjeh en Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh and Dependencies]] in [[1897]] and who "bewerkte hij de onderwerping van dat gewest" (accomplished the subjugation of that territory).

## Account from *Nederlandsch Indië Platen Atlas* (1926) — The Pacification

The atlas refers to the pacification of Atjeh in three distinct contexts:

1. **Governor Van Heutsz's achievement**: As Governor of Atjeh from [[1897]], Van Heutsz "bewerkte de onderwerping van dat gewest" (accomplished the subjugation of that territory).

2. **The Atjeh Monument**: The monument in the Wilhelminapark at [[Weltevreden]], [[Batavia]], "werd opgericht ter gedachtenis aan de pacificatie van Atjeh" (was erected in memory of the pacification of Atjeh).

3. **The Peutjot Cemetery**: Near [[Kota Radja]], the cemetery "werd voornamelijk bestemd voor de op de verschillende expedities in Atjeh gesneuvelden" (was primarily intended for those who fell on the various expeditions in Atjeh).

## The Policy Shift

The atlas also provides crucial context for understanding how the pacification was achieved. It notes that the Buitengewesten had previously suffered from neglect under the policy of abstention (*onthoudingspolitiek*), but that at the end of the previous century this was replaced "onder den Gouverneur-Generaal Van Heutsz door een krachtige en doelbewuste politiek van het onder ons gezag brengen er van" (under the Governor-General Van Heutsz by an energetic and purposeful policy of bringing [the territories] under our authority). The pacification was not merely a military event but was accompanied by — and made possible by — the construction of roads, railways, and other infrastructure that integrated the formerly independent territories into the colonial state.

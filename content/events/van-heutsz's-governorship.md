The **governorship of Van Heutsz** — the administration of [[General Van Heutsz|General J. B. van Heutsz]] as Governor of [[Acheh]] from [[1898]] onwards — was the period during which, in the words of [[C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]], "everything has been methodically done to make the necessary end, once and for all, of this insurrection" that had constituted the [[Achehnese War|war in Acheh]]. In less than six years General Van Heutsz "made such progress that now even the most sceptical must admit that we have come to the beginning of the end." His governorship thus closed the long parenthesis of Dutch impotence that had begun with the [[Civil government period|civil government period]] of [[1881]]–[[1884]] and continued under the disastrous [[Concentration Scheme|concentration scheme]] and the [[Uma period|"Uma" period]].

## The resumption of the war

The resumption of offensive operations in [[1896]], at the close of the [[Defection of Teuku Uma|defection of Teuku Uma]] episode, had opened all eyes to the need of vigorous action "if one did not wish to abandon altogether the subjugation of Acheh." The enemy, by nature more warlike than any race in the neighbouring islands, was by this time far better equipped than at the outset of the Dutch invasion: "He had a superfluity of arms and munitions; he possessed a better knowledge of the tactics of European troops and the difficulties that they had to overcome; and he had, during the concentration-time, formed no high estimate of his foe's intelligence. Thus then his self-confidence stood higher than ever." What was needed was not more troops, but a different way of using them.

## The new method

The method that broke the back of the insurrection was the maréchaussée system: small, lightly equipped native troops under European officers of the first rank, operating in small detachments against the Achehnese guerrillas on their own terms. Only thus, Hurgronje writes, "could any real results be arrived at." In less than six years General Van Heutsz made such progress that the end of the war was in sight. "The traitor [[Teuku Uma|Uma]] and numbers of adventurers, fanatical leaders and guerilla chiefs perished; the chiefs of dependent states, the members of the Sultan's family, and finally their head himself — [[Tuanku Muhamat Dawot|the pretender to the sultanate]], who in [[1878]] as a child had been proclaimed Sultan — submitted almost without exception; and thousands of refugees came back to their gampongs under the authority of Government."

## The religious factor confirmed

The continuance of the revolt after Van Heutsz's accession was, Hurgronje notes, "censured by all chiefs of mark except a limited number of universally respected religious leaders. This exception — and in fact the whole attitude of the teungkus (men learned in the scriptures) during the closing years of the war — confirms anew the accuracy of what was said in this book in [[1892]] regarding the significance of the religious factor in the war; at that time no one believed it, but for the last two years every one has accepted it as a truth that he acknowledged from the first." The eventual close of the war under Van Heutsz thus vindicated the analysis Hurgronje had offered a decade earlier.

## The rule of restraint

From [[1898]] the rule was enforced that "all houses and settlements should be spared in the case of enemies as well as in the case of those who submitted; that one should always behave with the greatest indulgence towards hereditary chiefs, and that even repeated evidence of treachery formed no sufficient reason for not" — continuing to seek submission rather than destruction. This combination of military method and political clemency was the practical expression of the new policy. Several military expeditions, lasting weeks or even months, were necessary towards the central highlands of the [[Gayos|Gayos]] in order to track down the enemy; the country was vast and desolate, and operations had to be continually carried on by day and still more by night. It was this patient combination, prosecuted throughout Van Heutsz's governorship, that brought the [[Achehnese War|thirty years' conflict]] to its close.

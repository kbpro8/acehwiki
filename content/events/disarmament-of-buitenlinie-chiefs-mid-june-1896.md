**The Disarmament of Buitenlinie Chiefs (mid-June 1896)** was the order issued by the [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]], [[Lt.-Gen. Vetter]], that the rifles lent in loan to the chiefs outside the concentrated line be returned to the Government, and the subsequent return — partial or complete — of those weapons by the principal *hoeloebalang* of the *buitenlinie*. [[Panglima Bintang|T. Panglima Bintang]] of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] returned 12 of 16 muzzle-loaders. [[T. Nja Mohamad]] returned 20 *trompladers* and 1 Beaumont. [[T. Nja Geh van Oleh-Soesoeh]] returned 4 Beaumonts. [[T. Nja Imeum]] — son of the actual *hoeloebalang* of the [[IX Moekims]] — brought all his *imam*s (except [[Lam-Koenjit]]'s, who was "sick") to the administration on [[15 June]] [[1896]]. As described in [[A. Kruisheer|Kruisheer]]'s *ATJEH '96*, the disarmament was the political complement to the military operations of May–June [[1896]] — the physical unwinding of the native-allied policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]] that had culminated in the [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]].

## Background

The native-allied policy of [[Generaal-Majoor C. Deykerhoff|Gen.-Maj. Deykerhoff]] — inaugurated by the [[Subjection of Teuku Umar (30 September 1893)|subjection of Teuku Umar]] of [[30 September]] [[1893]] — had involved the distribution of Dutch government weapons to cooperative *hoeloebalang* on the *buitenlinie*. The weapons were lent in loan: the Government retained ownership, and the *hoeloebalang* were expected to use them in the Government's service against the recalcitrant interior. The [[Defection of Teuku Umar (late March 1896)|defection]] of [[29 March]] [[1896]] — when [[Teuku Umar]] turned his Dutch-supplied arms against his patrons — had revealed the catastrophic risk of this policy. The [[Besluit Forbidding Weapon Supply to Acehnese (9 May 1896)|Besluit of 9 May 1896]] had formally prohibited the further supply of weapons to Achehnese. The [[Luitenant-Generaal J. A. Vetter|Regeeringscommissaris]] now resolved to recover the weapons that had already been distributed.

The political context was favourable. The [[Three-day Dutch offensive of 23–25 May 1896|three-day offensive]] had broken Oemar's *stelling* at [[Lam-Pisang]]; the [[Tuchtiging of Lam-Krak Moekims (9–10 June 1896)|tuchtiging of Lam-Krak]] had reduced the western redoubt; the [[Tuchtiging der V Moekims Montassik (16–17 June 1896)|tuchtiging of the V Moekims Montassik]] was about to break the south-western *benteng*s. The *hoeloebalang* of the *buitenlinie* — whatever their private disposition — could no longer doubt the Government's capacity to enforce its will. The recovery of the lent weapons was, in this sense, a test of the political realignment of the spring of [[1896]]: those who returned their weapons were acknowledging the Government's supremacy; those who refused or prevaricated were marking themselves out as disaffected.

## The Returns

The returns of weapons by the principal *hoeloebalang* of the *buitenlinie* were as follows:

- **[[Panglima Bintang|T. Panglima Bintang]]** of the [[III Moekims Daroe]] returned 12 of 16 muzzle-loading rifles. The partial return — 12 of 16 — was a political gesture: [[Panglima Bintang]] was, at this point, on the point of his [[Submission of Panglima Bintang (20 June 1896)|formal submission]] of [[20 June]] [[1896]], and the return of 12 weapons was a tangible demonstration of his goodwill. The withholding of 4 weapons was, however, a hedge: [[Panglima Bintang]] was not yet prepared to place himself entirely at the Government's mercy, and the retention of 4 weapons preserved a residual capacity for self-defence should the political wind shift again.

- **[[T. Nja Mohamad]]** returned 20 *trompladers* and 1 Beaumont — the latter a modern Dutch breech-loading rifle of the type carried by the Dutch infantry. The return of a Beaumont was particularly significant: the Beaumont was a government weapon, and its return was a clear acknowledgment that the *bruikleen* was at an end. The return of 20 *trompladers* — a substantial arsenal — was, in political terms, a major submission.

- **[[T. Nja Geh van Oleh-Soesoeh]]** returned 4 Beaumonts — four modern Dutch breech-loading rifles. The return was, given the small number of weapons, presumably the complete return of the *bruikleen* to this chief.

- **[[T. Nja Imeum]]** — son of the actual *hoeloebalang* of the [[IX Moekims]] — brought all his *imam*s (except [[Lam-Koenjit]]'s, who was "sick") to the administration on [[15 June]] [[1896]]. The *imam*s were told again that troop movements only aimed to prevent the establishment of foreign and evil elements. The population of the [[III Moekims Daroe|III]] and [[IX Moekims]] soon returned fully to their *kampong*s, including [[Lam-Pagar]] and [[Lam-Tengah]] (VI Moekims).

## The Political Effect

The political effect of the disarmament was substantial. The return of the weapons — particularly the Beaumonts — was a public acknowledgment by the *hoeloebalang* of the *buitenlinie* that the Government's supremacy had been restored and that the native-allied policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]] was at an end. The *hoeloebalang* who returned their weapons placed themselves, politically, on the Government's side; those who withheld them — like [[Panglima Bintang]]'s retention of 4 *trompladers* — hedged their bets but acknowledged, in the act of partial return, the direction of the political wind. The concurrent submission of [[T. Nja Imeum]] and his *imam*s, and the subsequent full return of the population of the [[III Moekims Daroe|III]] and [[IX Moekims]] to their *kampong*s, were further indications of the political realignment.

## Significance

The Disarmament of Buitenlinie Chiefs in mid-June [[1896]] was the political complement to the military operations of May–June [[1896]] and the physical unwinding of the native-allied policy of [[1893]]–[[1896]]. The recovery of the lent weapons was, in itself, a small matter — a few dozen rifles, mostly obsolete *trompladers*. The political significance of the disarmament was, however, out of all proportion to the material recovery: each weapon returned was a public act of submission, a tangible acknowledgment that the Government's supremacy had been restored. The partial return by [[Panglima Bintang]] — 12 of 16 — was, in its way, the most politically revealing: it showed that the *hoeloebalang* of the *buitenlinie* were willing to acknowledge the Government but were not yet willing to place themselves entirely at its mercy. The full submission of [[Panglima Bintang]] would follow on [[20 June]] [[1896]]; the broader political realignment of the *buitenlinie* would continue through the summer and autumn of [[1896]]. The disarmament was, in this sense, both the close of the native-allied policy and the opening of the next phase of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] — a phase in which the Dutch would rely, once again, on their own troops rather than on native auxiliaries.

**The Japanese invasion of Atjèh** was the amphibious assault launched by the Imperial Japanese Army against the northern tip of [[Sumatra|Sumatra]] in the night of [[11 March|11]] to [[12 March]] [[1942]], comprising four simultaneous landings — at [[Sabang|Sabang]], at [[Ujong Batëë|Ujong Batëë]] north-east of [[Koetaradja|Koetaradja]], at [[Kuala Bughah|Kuala Bughah]] (Koeala Boega') north of [[Langsa|Langsa]], and at [[Tandjong Tiram|Tandjong Tiram]] south of [[Medan|Medan]] — which, meeting only nominal resistance, brought an end to Dutch authority in Atjèh within a fortnight. Piekaar's *Atjèh and the War with Japan* treats the invasion as the denouement of three months of [[fall of Penang|strategic deterioration]] and of the [[revolt in Great Atjèh|pre-invasion insurgent movement]] which had paralysed the colonial administration from within.

## The four landings

"On Sabang the Japanese encountered only two infantry brigades." These had been relieved of their defensive task by telephone consultation with Koetaradja that same night, and were instead "assigned to field police services." The Aceh aircraft watch on [[Balóhan|Balóhan]] reported "the appearance of the Japanese fleet in the Balóhanbaai on the southern side of the island Wè." "No resistance was therefore offered. The airfield, harbor, oil and gasoline supplies fell undamaged into the hands of the enemy." On the mainland, [[Colonel Bekkers|Colonel G. J. Bekkers]], the Koetaradja troop commander, decided upon receiving news of the Sabang landing "to evacuate Koetaradja that same night." The main force retreated along the North Coast towards [[Sigli|Sigli]]; the garrison of the [[Lhó' Nga|Lhó' Nga]] airfield, sixteen brigades under [[Captain Steinhorst|Captain Steinhorst]], withdrew along the West Coast towards [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]].

## Collapse of the defence

"The next day, March 13th, the Japanese vanguard reached Sigli." [[Lam Meuló|Lam Meuló]] was occupied on [[16 March|16 March]], [[Bireuën|Bireuën]] and [[Lhó' Seumawè|Lhó' Seumawè]] on [[14 March|14 March]]. The defence line at the [[Peusangan|Peusangan]] bridge at Teupin Blang Mané was outflanked, and the retreat to [[Takéngón|Takéngón]] became a rout. By [[18 March|18 March]] Japanese assault troops had broken through the resin factory at [[Lampahan|Lampahan]] at km 78 of the [[Gajóweg|Gajóweg]], forcing the capitulation of the demoralised North-Coast force at [[Tangsé|Tangsé]] that same day. On the West Coast the Lhó' Nga and West Coast troops under [[Captain Kloprogge|Captain Kloprogge]] retreated 600 km southwards to the [[Lae Boetar|Lae Boetar]] enterprise, where they surrendered on [[28 March]] [[1942]].

## Significance

The invasion ended 39 years of Dutch rule in Atjèh and installed the [[Masubuchi|Masubuchi]] military administration. The pre-invasion [[Fujiwara-kikan|Fujiwara-kikan]] network now stepped forward openly: [[Said Aboe Bakar|Said Aboe Bakar]] and [[Said Doellah|Said Doellah]] entered with the first troops at [[Meulabóh|Meulabóh]] to displace the loyal *oelèëbalang*s. The capitulation of the highland forces on [[28 March]] [[1942]] opened the three-and-a-half-year occupation that would last until the [[Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945|Japanese capitulation of 15 August 1945]].

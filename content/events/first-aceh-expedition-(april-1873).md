**The First Aceh Expedition (April 1873)** was the initial Dutch military attempt to subdue the sultanate of [[Atjeh (Borel account)|Atjeh]], conducted under [[Generaal Köhler]] in [[1873]] and ending in catastrophic failure when the storming of the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] on [[1 April]] [[1873]] was repulsed and Köhler himself was killed outside the walls. Borel treats the disaster as "slechts een voorspel" — a mere prelude — to the far larger second expedition that would follow under [[Jan van Swieten]] in November [[1873]].

## Background and Force

The colonial government in Batavia and the legerbestuur there had, Borel concedes, every reason to believe that the expeditionary force dispatched against Atjeh would be sufficient. The hooge regeering in Indië knew that it faced a "krijgshaftig, woest en energiek volk" and could expect "geduchten tegenstand" as well as difficult terrain, but otherwise almost nothing reliable was known of the country. The troops were well trained; the infanterie was partly armed with the newly introduced [[Beaumont-geweer]]; and Köhler himself "stond bekend als een kundig, dapper en beproefd aanvoerder," enjoying the algemeene achting and full vertrouwen of his subordinates.

## The Storm and Its Failure

The decisive moment came on [[1 April]] [[1873]] with a frontal stormaanval on the [[Kraton of Atjeh|Kraton]] under the wakkeren [[Majoor Cavaljé]]. Borel's verdict is sharp: the lichte veldgeschut available to the Dutch could achieve nothing against the Kraton's omwalling — whether of earth or stone — and still less against the "levenden muur van [[Bamboe-doeri versperringen|bamboe-doeri]]" that crowned the borstwering. For an instant success seemed within reach: one Dutch compagnie actually reached the hoofdpoort (main gate) of the Kraton and stood on the point of bursting in. It was not sufficiently supported, however, and Borel adds that even had it broken through, it was "zeer de vraag" whether the men could have held the binnenruimte. At that critical moment Köhler was struck down and killed outside the walls; the "woedende tegenstand" of the Atjehneezen surging in from all sides, the failure of the storm, and the mounting tally of dooden en gekwetsten shattered the moreel of the troops and brought the aanvoerders' confidence to wankelen.

## Borel's Judgement

The expedition was, in Borel's account, "eene groote nationale ramp" — less for its materieele verliezen than for its morale and political consequences. Dutch prestige in Indië was "gevoelig geknakt," while the Atjehneezen learned their own strength and held themselves thereafter for onoverwinnelijk. He acknowledges operational mistakes — the poor use of artillerie, the failure to prepare the storm, the "geheel verlaten der kust" without holding any versterkt punt — yet insists the real question is whether the doel could have been achieved at all with the voorhanden middelen, even without these errors and even had Köhler not fallen. The enemy's "woede en verbittering, die aan het ongeloofelijke grensden," and the bedekt terrain that nullified the Beaumont's superiority, made success doubtful from the first. The news caused an "enorme indruk" in both Indië and Nederland; the whole Dutch nation engaged with the war and demanded vengeance. The government realised that "door een tweede échec zou ons prestige in Indië geheel vernietigd zijn" — and so the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873–1874)|second, far larger expedition]] was set in motion, preceded by the controversial [[Atjeh-enquête|inquiry]] into the failure.

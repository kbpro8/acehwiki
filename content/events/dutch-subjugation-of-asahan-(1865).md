**The Dutch subjugation of Asahan (1865)** was the armed operation by which the [[Netherlands-Indies Government|Nederlandsch-Indisch Gouvernement]] broke the resistance of the [[Sultan of Asahan|Sultan of Asahan]] to the recognition of [[Siak|Siak]]'s (and hence the Dutch) overlordship, completing the political consolidation of the East Coast statelets begun by the [[Netscher|Netscher]] mission of [[1862]]. Recorded by the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey of the [[Buitenbezittingen|Outer Possessions]] as the only military operation required to enforce the recognition of the Siak overlordship, the [[1865]] subjugation of Asahan prepared the ground for the [[dutch-marine-expulsion-of-atjehers-from-langkat-(1866)|1866 expulsion of the Atjehers from Langkat]] and the end of [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|Atjehnese overlordship over the East Coast]].

## Background: the Netscher mission and Asahan's refusal

The [[1865]] subjugation was the direct sequel to the [[Netscher|Netscher]] mission of [[1862]]. The Bureau records that, after the [[langkat-appeal-to-siak-against-atjeh-(1862)|1862 Langkat appeal to Siak]], the Government dispatched [[Netscher|Resident Netscher of Riouw]] on a tour of the East Coast statelets named in the [[Siak tractate of 1 February 1858|Siak tractate of 1858]] as dependencies of [[Siak|Siak]]. The statelets of [[Deli|Deli]], [[Serdang|Serdang]], [[Langkat|Langkat]] and several smaller coastal polities signed acts of recognition of the Siak overlordship. [[Asahan|Asahan]] — the most southerly of the East Coast sultanates, and the one whose power had been built up over the first half of the nineteenth century through marriage, subjection, and successful war — refused. The Bureau records: "Asahan en zijne vasallen weigerden dit evenwel hardnekkig, en namen zelfs tegenover de Commissie onder resident [[Netscher|Netscher]], welke een reis langs die kuststaatjes maakte, eene dreigende houding aan" — "Asahan and its vassals, however, refused this obstinately, and even adopted a threatening attitude toward the Commission under Resident Netscher, which made a journey along those coastal statelets."

## The 1865 subjugation

The Bureau records the subjugation in a single, dense sentence: "Het duurde echter nog tot [[1865]], voordat het Gouvernement ertoe overging dit verzet door gewapend optreden te breken, wat zeer spoedig gelukte" — "It lasted, however, until 1865, before the Government proceeded to break this resistance by armed action, which succeeded very speedily." The Bureau does not enumerate the units involved, but its characterisation of the operation as "zeer spoedig" — "very speedily" — succeeded — signals the overwhelming disparity of forces. The subjugation of [[Asahan|Asahan]] was the only military operation required to enforce the recognition of the Siak overlordship; the other statelets had acquiesced voluntarily in [[1862]].

## Significance

The [[1865]] subjugation of [[Asahan|Asahan]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's survey, the military complement of the [[Netscher|Netscher]] mission of [[1862]]. It completes the political consolidation of the East Coast statelets under the [[Siak|Siak]]-Dutch overlordship, and it prepares the ground for the [[dutch-marine-expulsion-of-atjehers-from-langkat-(1866)|1866 expulsion of the Atjehers from Langkat]] that would extinguish [[atjeh-as-opperheer-in-langkat-deli-serdang-(1854)|Atjehnese overlordship over the East Coast]]. The subjugation is also, in the longer perspective, a prelude to the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] of [[1873]]: the loss of its East Coast tributaries — sealed by the [[1865]] subjugation — was one of the principal grievances of the [[Sultan of Acheh|Sultan of Atjeh]] in the breakdown of Dutch-Atjehnese relations.

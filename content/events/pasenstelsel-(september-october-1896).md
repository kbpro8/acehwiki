**The Pasenstelsel** — *het passenstelsel* — was the pass system introduced in [[Groot-Atjeh]] in the period *van medio September tot 28 October* [[1896]] — from mid-September to [[28 October]] [[1896]] — for the regulation of movement between the Dutch-held *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) and the Acehnese-held territories beyond. The system required all inhabitants of the Dutch-held area to obtain a *pas* (pass) in order to travel beyond the *geconcentreerde linie*, and all inhabitants of the Acehnese-held area to obtain a *pas* in order to enter the Dutch-held area. The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) was a critical innovation of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s second tenure as [[Civiel en Militair Gouverneur]], and represented a significant extension of Dutch administrative control beyond the military sphere — a step in the slow transformation of the Dutch presence in Atjeh from a military occupation into a civil administration.

## The Policy Framework

The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) was introduced under the modified *postenstelsel* (system of posts) policy of the [[18 July|18 July 1896]] telegram of [[Jhr. C. H. A. van der Wijck|Governor-General Van der Wijck]], which had authorised the *bezetting* (occupation) of points beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) on condition that *voortdurende aanvulling van troepen en dwangarbeiders steeds mogelijk zullen zijn* (continuous replenishment of troops and forced labourers would always be possible). The telegram had further laid down that *aan die hoofden mogen geen wapens worden verstrekt, en vuurwapens zelfs slechts tot zeer gering aantal in hun bezit gelaten worden* (no weapons may be furnished to those chiefs, and firearms even may be left in their possession only in very small numbers), and that *het beperken van de gemeenschap tusschen de buiten de linie wonenden, met het gebied daarbinnen, ongewenscht [is]* (the restriction of communication between those living outside the linie and the area within is undesirable). The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) was thus the administrative instrument by which the Dutch sought to regulate — but not to prohibit — the movement between the two areas.

## The Operation of the Pasenstelsel

The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) operated as a system of registration and identification. Every inhabitant of the Dutch-held area who wished to travel beyond the *geconcentreerde linie* (concentrated line) was required to obtain a *pas* from the Dutch authorities, which specified the traveller's identity, destination, and the duration of the authorised absence. Every inhabitant of the Acehnese-held area who wished to enter the Dutch-held area was likewise required to obtain a *pas* — and, on entering, was required to deposit his weapons at the Dutch post, *waar de wapens tijdelijk worden afgelegd* (where the weapons are temporarily laid down). The system was designed to facilitate the identification of *woelgeesten* (troublemakers) and the regulation of trade, but it also represented a significant restriction on the freedom of movement of the Acehnese population — a restriction which was justified by the Governor-General's *beheersching* (domination programme) programme of [[18 July]] [[1896]].

## The Period of Introduction (mid-September to 28 October [[1896]])

The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) was introduced in the period *van medio September tot 28 October* [[1896]] — a period which coincided with the [[Construction of Blang Bintang Camp (7 September 1896)|construction of the Blang Bintang camp]] (begun [[7 September]] [[1896]]), the [[First Selimoen Expedition (6–11 September 1896)|first Selimoen expedition]] ([[6 September|6]]–[[11 September]] [[1896]]), and the [[Temporary Occupation of Indrapoeri (28 October 1896)|temporary occupation of Indrapoeri]] ([[28 October]] [[1896]]). The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) was thus introduced in the same period as the Dutch forward movement into the [[XXII Moekims]] and the Selimoen — the geographical and administrative extensions of Dutch control which the *pasenstelsel* (pass system) was designed to regulate. The system was fully in place by [[28 October]] [[1896]], when the temporary occupation of Indrapoeri opened a new forward position which would also be subject to the *pasenstelsel* (pass system).

## Significance

The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) is significant in the documentary record of *ATJEH '96 — Tweede Deel* as the principal administrative innovation of [[J. W. Stemfoort|Stemfoort]]'s second tenure. The system represented a significant extension of Dutch administrative control beyond the military sphere — a step in the slow transformation of the Dutch presence in Atjeh from a military occupation into a civil administration. The *pasenstelsel* (pass system) would remain in force, with modifications, throughout the remainder of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-oorlog]] and into the civil government period, and would be one of the principal instruments of Dutch administrative control in the early twentieth century. The system's introduction in the second semester of [[1896]] — under the modified *postenstelsel* (system of posts) policy of the [[18 July|18 July 1896]] telegram — placed it within the *beheersching* (domination programme) programme which characterised the second semester of [[1896]] under Stemfoort's second tenure, and prepared the ground for the more systematic administrative reforms which would be undertaken under [[C. P. J. van Vliet|Van Vliet]] and his successors.

**1667 Thames Raid** was a Dutch naval and marine operation carried out between [[19 June]] and [[23 June]] [[1667]] that targeted English fortifications and shipyards along the River Thames, notably at [[Sheerness]], [[Chatham]] and [[Rochester]]. The expedition was authorised by [[Cornelius de Witt]] (deputy) and executed under the overall command of Admiral [[Michiel de Ruyter]] with marine leadership from [[François Palm]] and [[Colonel Dolman]].

## Planning and objectives
The Dutch plan, devised by the States of Holland, aimed to force the British king into peace by threatening the capital’s riverine approaches. The fleet was to sail up the Thames, capture English warships, and destroy naval stores.

## Execution
The Dutch forces entered the Thames on [[19 June]] [[1667]], seized the fort at [[Sheerness]] after minimal resistance, and proceeded upriver. Marine detachments of 450 men landed on the island of [[Chappey]] (modern [[Chatham]]) and captured the English magazines. The flagship [[Royal Charles]] was seized on [[23 June]] [[1667]].

## Significance for Aceh
The raid demonstrated Dutch amphibious capability, a skill later employed by the VOC in its campaigns against the Sultanate of [[Aceh]], where coordinated naval‑land assaults were essential for capturing coastal forts.

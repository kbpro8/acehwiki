**The construction of the Atjeh-tram (1875–1914)** — in Kreemer's spelling the **Atjèh-tram** — was the four-decade process by which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjèh steam tramway]] was built in successive stages from [[Oelèe Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] to [[Pangkalan Soesoe|Pangkalan Soesoe]] (511 km total), beginning with the Gouv. Bt. of [[26 June]] [[1874]] N°. 4 and continuing through the [[1914]] close of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress" (with the final link to Pangkalan Soesoe opened [[2 April]] [[1917]]). In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the construction of the Atjeh-tram is the principal material embodiment of the Dutch presence in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from the [[1873]] outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] to the [[1914]] close of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade.

## Phase I: The first military line (1874–1885)

Construction began with the Gouv. Bt. of [[26 June]] [[1874]] N°. 4, which authorised building a sea-head (*zeehoofd*) at [[Oelèe Lheuë|Oelèë Lheuë]] with a 5 km railway (1.067 m gauge) to [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]]. The iron-screw-pile zeehoofd was built by the **Genie** (Corps of Engineers), and the railway was opened on [[12 November]] [[1876]]. On [[1 January]] [[1882]] the management of the line was transferred to the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|Department of Civil Public Works (B.O.W.)]] and the line was "narrowed" (*versmald*) into a tramway of 0.75 m gauge, operated as a *tramweg* from [[8 June]] [[1884]]. The line was extended to Lam Barö (~7 km), and a *ceintuurbaan* (belt-tramway) of about 16 km was built in [[1885]] along the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Koeta Radja|Kota Radja]] — the *concentratie* (concentrated stelling) posts — connected to Koeta Radja by three *straalbanen* (radial lines) to Keutapang Doea, Lam Barö, and Lam Njöng.

## Phase II: Reversion to War Department (1891–1896)

In [[1891]] (formally [[1 March]] [[1891]]) the management of the Atjeh lines reverted from the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]] back to the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]] — a transfer that closed the first chapter of the Atjeh-tram's history and prepared the ground for the post-[[1896]] long-line construction. The [[1896]] [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar]] rendered the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] obsolete; the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway of 1885]] was "gradually cleared" in the years after [[1896]].

## Phase III: The long line of conquest (1897–1908)

In place of the dismantled belt-tramway, the long tramway of [[1897]] onward followed the Dutch extension of authority into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]], tying post after post of the ever-expanding military occupation to the command centre at [[Koeta Radja]]. The line was extended to Glé Kambéng (on the left bank of the Atjèh-rivier) on [[15 November]] [[1897]] and to [[Seulimeum|Seulimeum]] on [[11 January]] [[1898]] — the *vlaktelijn* (plain line) from Oelèë Lheuë to Seulimeum totalled 45 km (cost ƒ846,700). From [[1899]] onward, the construction of a second tramway in the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] was also begun: between [[1899]] and [[1906]] the line was successively extended from [[Sigli]] to the harbour of [[Langsa]], with a western branch from [[Sigli]] over [[Padang Tiji|Padang Tidji]] to [[Keude Breue]] already built in [[1899]]. The [[1904]]–[[1908]] *berglijntje* (mountain line) from [[Keude Breue]] over the hills to [[Seulimeum]] — 33 km, cost ƒ3,100,900, opened on [[1 April]] [[1908]] — crossed the watershed between Groot Atjèh and the North coast, and connected the second tramway of the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] with the old tramway of [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] — the engineering keystone of the integrated network.

## Phase IV: Extension and economic reorientation (1910–1913)

The *vlaktelijn* from Padang Tidji to Koeala Langsa (343 km, cost ƒ10,000,000) was built in stages:

- Keudè-Breuëh to Padang Tidji (from [[1899]])
- Sigli–Lhö' Seumawè ([[1901]]–[[1904]])
- Geudöng–Lhö' Soekön ([[1903]])
- Lhö' Soekön–Idi ([[1 July]] [[1904]])
- Idi–Langsa ([[1906]])
- Langsa–Koeala Simpang ([[1912]])

The [[1910]] reduction of the third-class tariff from 2 cents to 1 cent per kilometre marked the beginning of the line's economic reorientation from military to civilian traffic. The [[1913]] Gouvernementsbesluiten (G. Bn. of [[17 June]] [[1913]] No. 59, [[16 October]] [[1913]] No. 25, [[8 November]] [[1913]] No. 40) approved the doortrekking of the Atjeh-tram from [[Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban]] on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]].

## Phase V: The Aroe-baai-plannen, the Deli junction, and the final link (1913–1917)

The [[1913]] Aroe-baai-plannen to develop [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as a deep-water harbour — and the parallel proposal for a direct [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat]] junction with the [[Deli Spoorweg Maatschappij|Deli railway]] network — were deferred in [[1913]] in favour of a connection from [[Pangkalan Brandan]] to the Atjeh-tram. The [[1914]] outbreak of the European war then suspended further extension work.

Construction resumed after the war. The extensions Langsa–Sëmadam ([[1916]], 45 km, cost ƒ1,583,100), Sëmadam–Bësitang ([[1916]]), and Bësitang–Pangkalan Soesoe ([[2 April]] [[1917]], completing the main line at 511 km from Koeta Radja) brought the Atjèh-tram to its full extent. Side-branches included: Langsa–Koeala Langsa (9 km, [[1906]]), Beureunoen–Lam Meulö (6 km, [[1906]]), and Koeala Simpang–Soengai Lipoet ([[1914]]). On [[29 December]] [[1919]], the link Bësitang–Pangkalan Brandan was completed, connecting the Atjèh-tram network with that of the Deli-Spoorwegmaatschappij. Since then, [[Medan|Medan]] could be reached from [[Koeta Radja|Koeta Radja]] by land in two days.

## Management transfers

The Atjèh-tram was managed successively by:

- The Genie (Corps of Engineers, [[1874]]–[[1882]])
- The BOW (Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken, [[1882]]–[[1891]])
- The Departement van Oorlog (War Department, [[1891]]–[[1916]])
- The Departement van Gouvernements-Bedrijven (Department of Government Enterprises) from [[1 January]] [[1916]] under the Algemeen Tramweg Reglement (Stbl. [[1905]] N°. 516, applied by Ordonnantie of [[18 March]] [[1916]], Stbl. N°. 272).

The tariff revision of [[1 September]] [[1919]] raised all freight and passenger tariffs to align with Stoomvaart Maatschappij (S.S.) tariffs on Java.

## Statistical summary

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's statistical tables show the Atjeh-tram's growth over the [[1904]]–[[1913]] decade: from 322 K.M. of line and f 9,000,000 of construction capital in [[1904]], to 465 K.M. of line and f 18,544,000 of construction capital in [[1913]]. The line's operating account showed a deficit every year until [[1912]]; the [[1913]] figures show the line approaching break-even, with the share of Gouvernements-vervoer in total freight falling from 60% in [[1904]] to 11% in [[1913]].

## Significance

The construction of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] over the four decades [[1874]]–[[1917]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's and Kreemer's accounts, the principal material embodiment of the Dutch presence in Atjeh — from the [[1873]] outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] to the [[1914]] close of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade and the post-war completion of the line to Pangkalan Soesoe in [[1917]]. The line's successive phases mirror the successive phases of the Dutch strategic reorientation: from the defensive *postenlinie* of [[1885]] to the offensive long-line of [[1897]] onward; from the military administration of [[1891]] to the civilian tariff reform of [[1910]]; from the purely military instrument of [[1904]] (with 60% of freight on Gouvernements-rekening) to the predominantly economic institution of [[1913]] (with only 11% of freight on Gouvernements-rekening). Kreemer's account, in turn, supplies the precise dates and costs of every stage of construction — from the [[1874]] sea-head authorisation through the [[1917]] completion of the main line at 511 km — that allow the engineering history of the line to be reconstructed in detail.

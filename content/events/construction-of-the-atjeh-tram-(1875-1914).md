**The construction of the Atjeh-tram (1875–1914)** was the four-decade process by which the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] was built in successive phases — from the small military spoorbaantje of [[1875]] between [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and [[Kota Radja]] to the projected [[1913]] extensions toward [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] and the [[Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij|Deli railway]] network. In the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the construction of the Atjeh-tram is the principal material embodiment of the Dutch presence in [[Acheh|Atjeh]] from the [[1873]] outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] to the [[1914]] close of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] "decade of progress."

## Account from *De Buitenbezittingen* (1917) — Construction of the Atjeh-tram (1875–1914)

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] identifies five principal phases of construction:

### Phase I: The first military line (1875–1885)

The first Atjeh railway was built in [[1875]] by the [[Departement van Oorlog|Department of War]] shortly after the outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] in [[1873]] — a small spoorbaantje between the roadstead of [[Oelèe Lheuë]] and the principal Dutch military station at [[Kota Radja]]. In [[1882]] the management of the line was transferred to the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|Department of Civil Public Works (B.O.W.)]] and the line was "narrowed" (*versmald*) into a tramway. In [[1885]] military necessity drove the construction of a *ceintuur-trambaan* — a belt-tramway — along the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie (line of forts)]] surrounding [[Kota Radja]], connected by three radial lines to the original tramway to the roadstead.

### Phase II: Reversion to War Department (1891–1896)

In [[1891]] the management of the Atjeh lines reverted from the [[Departement der Burgerlijke Openbare Werken|B.O.W.]] back to the [[Departement van Oorlog|War Department]] — a transfer that closed the first chapter of the Atjeh-tram's history and prepared the ground for the post-[[1896]] long-line construction. The [[1896]] [[Toekoe Oemar's verraad (1896)|verraad of Toekoe Oemar]] rendered the [[Postenlinie|postenlinie]] obsolete; the [[Ceintuur-trambaan|belt-tramway of 1885]] was "gradually cleared" in the years after [[1896]].

### Phase III: The long line of conquest (1897–1908)

In place of the dismantled belt-tramway, the long tramway of [[1897]] onward followed the Dutch extension of authority into the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Atjeh Dependencies]], tying post after post of the ever-expanding military occupation to the command centre at [[Kota Radja]]. From [[1899]] onward, the construction of a second tramway in the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] was also begun: between [[1899]] and [[1906]] the line was successively extended from [[Sigli]] to the harbour of [[Langsa]], with a western branch from [[Sigli]] over [[Padang Tidji]] to [[Keude Breue]] already built in [[1899]]. The [[1904]]–[[1908]] *berglijntje* from [[Keude Breue]] over the hills to [[Seulimeum]] then connected the second tramway of the [[Atjeh's Onderhoorigheden|Dependencies]] with the old tramway of [[Groot-Atjeh|Great Atjeh]] — the engineering keystone of the integrated network.

### Phase IV: Extension and economic reorientation (1910–1913)

The [[1910]]–[[1912]] extension of the second tramway from [[Langsa]] to [[Koeala Simpang]] completed the original axis of the second line. The [[1913]] Gouvernementsbesluiten (G. Bn. of [[17 June]] [[1913]] No. 59, [[16 October]] [[1913]] No. 25, [[8 November]] [[1913]] No. 40) approved the doortrekking of the Atjeh-tram from [[Koeala Simpang]] through [[Tamiang]] to [[Besitang]], and from [[Pangkalan Soesoe]] to [[Telok Taboeban]] on [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]]. The [[1910]] reduction of the third-class tariff from 2 cents to 1 cent per kilometre marked the beginning of the line's economic reorientation from military to civilian traffic.

### Phase V: The Aroe-baai-plannen and the deferral of the Deli junction (1913–1914)

The [[1913]] Aroe-baai-plannen to develop [[Aroebai|Arube Bay]] as a deep-water harbour — and the parallel proposal for a direct [[Besitang]]–[[Tandjoeng Slamat]] junction with the [[Deli-Spoorweg-Maatschappij|Deli railway]] network — were deferred in [[1913]] in favour of a connection from [[Pangkalan Brandan]] to the Atjeh-tram. The [[1914]] outbreak of the European war then suspended further extension work.

## Statistical summary

The Encyclopaedisch Bureau's statistical tables show the Atjeh-tram's growth over the [[1904]]–[[1913]] decade: from 322 K.M. of line and f 9,000,000 of construction capital in [[1904]], to 465 K.M. of line and f 18,544,000 of construction capital in [[1913]]. The line's operating account showed a deficit every year until [[1912]]; the [[1913]] figures show the line approaching break-even, with the share of Gouvernements-vervoer in total freight falling from 60% in [[1904]] to 11% in [[1913]].

## Significance

The construction of the [[Atjeh-tram|Atjeh-tramway]] over the four decades [[1875]]–[[1914]] is, in the Encyclopaedisch Bureau's account, the principal material embodiment of the Dutch presence in Atjeh — from the [[1873]] outbreak of the [[Aceh War|Atjeh-war]] to the [[1914]] close of the [[1904]]–[[1914]] decade. The line's successive phases mirror the successive phases of the Dutch strategic reorientation: from the defensive *postenlinie* of [[1885]] to the offensive long-line of [[1897]] onward; from the military administration of [[1891]] to the civilian tariff reform of [[1910]]; from the purely military instrument of [[1904]] (with 60% of freight on Gouvernements-rekening) to the predominantly economic institution of [[1913]] (with only 11% of freight on Gouvernements-rekening).

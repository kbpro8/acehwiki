**The capitulation at Lae Boetar** on [[29 March]] [[29 March 1942|29 March 1942]] — the surrender of Captain [[L. H. J. Kloprogge|Kloprogge]]'s reduced Westkust force of about 140 men at the *cultuuronderneming* [[Lae Boetar|Lae Boetar]] 60 km north-east of [[Singkel|Singkel]] — was the final act of the Westkust theatre of the [[Japanese invasion of Atjèh|Japanese invasion of March 1942]], and the largest single Dutch surrender of the Westkust campaign. In the [[Nederlands-Indië contra Japan|Dutch staff history's]] account (Deel VI, Hoofdstuk V), the Lae Boetar capitulation is presented as the *moral* close of the Westkust campaign — a surrender forced not by enemy pressure but by the presence of the Westkust women and children in the convoy.

## The approach to Lae Boetar (18–29 March 1942)

By [[18 March]] [[18 March 1942|18 March 1942]] the combined Meulaboh–Blang Pidië–Tapatoean column had reached the Bakongan bivak. The staff history records that "het gros moest tenslotte in Tapatoean en Bakongan worden achtergelaten" — the bulk of the 3,000 women and children could not be moved further south than Bakongan, owing to the approach of the Japanese battalion-strength force from the north and to the *verraad* of the convoy's positions by the Atjehnese population. The European women and children were evacuated in private passenger cars over "smalle en slechte wegen" to the Lae Boetar onderneming, but "begrijpelijker wijze maakte dit geen goede indruk op de inheemse militairen, die hun gezinnen onder bescherming van enkele vrijwilligers hadden moeten achterlaten." The Dutch *geest* of the indigenous soldaten collapsed; "gevallen van desertie waren schering en inslag."

## The 29 March 1942 capitulation

By [[29 March]] [[29 March 1942|29 March 1942]] Kloprogge's reduced force — about 140 men — had reached Lae Boetar. The staff history records the decision in a single sentence: "Op 29 Maart liet kapitein Kloprogge, die de ongelijke strijd toch wilde doorzetten, met het lot van de vrouwen en kinderen voor ogen, overreden met het restant van de troepen (ongeveer 140 man) te capituleren."

The phrasing is significant. Kloprogge is presented as *willing* to continue the fight ("die de ongelijke strijd toch wilde doorzetten"); the surrender is presented as a *overreding* — a persuasion — exercised by the *lot van de vrouwen en kinderen*. The decision is thus framed as a moral rather than a military one: the Dutch force could have fought on, but the cost would have been the women and children left behind at Tapatoean and Bakongan.

## Refusal of four officers

The capitulation at Lae Boetar produced the staff history's principal instance of Dutch officers' refusal to recognise a surrender. Captain [[F. Steinhorst|Steinhorst]], Lt [[E. F. K. R. Mathus|Mathus]], Lt [[M. C. Thomson|Thomson]] and the *officier van gezondheid* V. A. C. L. Dingemans — "deze militairen waren echter niet op de hoogte van de slechte aan gang van zaken in het Blangkedjerense en de algehele capitulatie" — refused to surrender and attempted to break through to the still-fighting Dutch forces around [[Blangkedjeren|Blangkedjeren]]. Their fates are the staff history's principal post-Lae Boetar narrative:

- **Steinhorst**: captured by *opstandelingen* and killed.
- **Mathus** and **Dingemans**: captured by the Japanese and "enige tijd daarna terechtgesteld."
- **Thomson**: the only escapee, eventually reaching the [[Medan|Medanse]] before his capture on [[26 April]] [[26 April 1942|26 April]].

## Significance

The capitulation at Lae Boetar is, in the staff history's Westkust narrative, the *moral* close of the campaign — the moment at which the Dutch command's responsibility for the women and children of the convoy took precedence over the operational decision to continue resistance. The staff history's emphasis on the *overreding* by which Kloprogge was brought to surrender — set against the officers' refusal and the killings that followed — is the staff history's principal illustration of the moral weight of the Westkust evacuation on the Dutch command decision. The capitulation closed the Westkust theatre ten days before the [[Sumatra capitulation (28 March 1942)|general capitulation at Blangkedjeren]] — but in moral terms it closed the campaign.

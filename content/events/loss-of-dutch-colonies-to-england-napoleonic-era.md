**Loss of Dutch colonies to England (Napoleonic era)** (late 18th century - 1811) was an event in the history of [[Aceh]] recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] in *Land en Volk van Atjeh Vroeger en Nu*. At the end of the 18th century, the Dutch colony-rich Holland came under French control. 

## Date and Context

The event is dated to late 18th century - [[1811]].

## Account

At the end of the 18th century, the Dutch colony-rich [[Leyden (Leiden)|Holland]] came under French control. In about 15 years it lost to [[Engeland (England)|England]] all it possessed: West-Indië, possessions in Africa, Voor-Indië ([[Hindostan|British India]]), and Oost-Indië. [[Madiun and Surabaya|Java]] finally fell to the English at end of [[1811]]. Sumatra was largely returned later, except parts of the [[Westkust]] and Atjeh (which remained an independent native state).

## Significance

The event is recorded by [[J. Jongejans|Jongejan]] as part of the broader historical narrative of [[Aceh]] and the [[Aceh War|Atjeh conflict]] period. It illustrates the complex interplay of local [[Acehnese|Atjehers]] politics, Dutch colonial administration, and the religious and military leadership that shaped the region's history.

**Maulud** (Achehnese **Mo'lot**, from the Arabic *Mawlūd*), the feast of the birth of [[Prophet Mohammad|the Prophet Mohammad]], is celebrated on the 12th day of Rabi' al-awwal, the third month of the Mohammedan year, which takes its Achehnese name from the feast. The festival is regarded as specially binding on the Achehnese by reason of a legend concerning an embassy to the [[Sultan of Turkey|Sultan of Turkey]]: when Acheh first sought to bring itself to the notice of the lord of all believers, the Raja Rom at [[Stambul|Stambul]], the Sultan — having at last received the strangers and heard of the existence of their country — is said to have replied, in the words recorded by Achehnese tradition, that the observance of the feast of Mohammad's birth was among the most meritorious of works of devotion, and that there should be no village in Acheh where the inhabitants did not publicly hold a Mo'lot feast as their tribute to the lord of the Faithful.

Such is the explanation given for the fact that the festival has been regarded in Acheh as obligatory. The Mo'lot, in the Achehnese understanding, must be held in all gampongs either in the month of Mo'lot itself (on or after the 12th day) or in one of the two following months, **Adbe Mo'lot** ("Younger Brother of Mo'lot", Rabi' al-akhir) and **Mo'lot Seumeulheueh** ("Final Mo'lot", Jumada al-awwal). It is from this commemoration that the latter two derive their popular names; the birth of the Prophet is held to be commemorated in them also. The feast itself is the [[Kanduri Mo'lot|kanduri Mo'lot]].

The place where the Mo'lot is celebrated is the meunasah. The day is fixed year by year by the headman so as not to clash with the pursuits of the villagers; care is taken at the same time so to fix the dates that no two gampongs of the same name shall celebrate the Mo'lot on the same day or after too short an interval, because all the people of the same mukim are invited to each feast. Those who live in other gampongs in the same mukim are the guests of the whole gampong and receive a formal invitation through a messenger of the keuchi'. As meat is indispensable for the *idang Mo'lot*, the feast is preceded by the slaughter of animals — a general slaughter that takes place on no other occasions except the last days of the eighth and ninth months and on a smaller scale at the "great" feast of the sacrifices.

The recitation proper to the Mo'lot is called **meuliku** in Achehnese (from *dikr*), just as the Javanese speak of the *dikir maulud*. Some of the maulids most in use have been handsomely lithographed at [[Cairo]] by [[Hasan at-Tochi Ahmad|Hasan at-Tochi Ahmad]]. After the recitation, the teungku and leubes sing a hymn of praise while a vessel of incense stands among them, and the kanduri closes with a long prayer from the end of the *Charapah anam*. A specially great kanduri Mo'lot is held on the actual day at the tomb of [[Teungku Anjong]] in [[Gampong Jawa]], where one or more buffaloes are slaughtered and pieces of meat are sent to sundry ulamas by the guardian of the tomb. The Mo'lot is also employed as a festal accompaniment on other solemn occasions: funeral feasts, for example, are often enlivened by a Mo'lot recitation.

**The Acehnese People** (Acehnese: *ureuëng Acheh*; Dutch: *Atjehers*) were the inhabitants of [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] — a diverse population united by common language, customs, and Islamic faith, though divided between highlanders (*ureuëng tunöng*) and lowlanders (*ureuëng baröh*), and containing various ethnic and racial elements drawn from across the archipelago.

## Composition

[[C. Snouck Hurgronje]] observed that in historic times various races contributed to ennoble or degrade the people of Acheh. Apart from this, the people — as an established unity — differed from one another in numerous local peculiarities of language, manners, superstition, and dress. Most of these local distinctions, when compared with the agreement in essential features, were too insignificant to be noticed; however, the differences between the highlanders and the lowlanders were pronounced.

## Highlanders and Lowlanders

The **highlanders** (*ureuëng tunöng*) were particularly understood to be the people of the [[Sagi System|Sagi]] of the XXII Mukims, and the **lowlanders** (*ureuëng baröh*) who inhabited the greater part of the two remaining sagis, including the capital. Some portions of the lowland sagis — for instance the *ureuëng Buëng* inhabiting the VII Mukims Buëng in the Sagi of the XXVI Mukims — had almost the same language and customs as the *ureuëng tunöng*.

## Banda and Dusön

As regards language and manners, the lowlanders followed the people of the capital. The inhabitants of the most important [[Gampong|gampongs]] near [[Banda Acheh]] were distinguished by the epithet *banda* ("town-bred" or civilized); those who spoke their own local dialects and were unacquainted with the manners of the town were called *dusön* (countrified, uncivilized — like the Sundanese *dusun* and the Javanese).

## Islam

The Acehnese were universally Muslim, following the Shafi'ite school of Islamic jurisprudence. Islam had taken deep root in [[Great Acheh|Acheh]] since at least the sixteenth century, and religious identity was central to Acehnese resistance during the [[Aceh War]].

## Language

The Acehnese language had its own distinct phonology and script. Snouck Hurgronje's transcription system for Acehnese sounds is explained in the introduction to *The Achehnese*, noting that certain vowels and consonants had sounds distinct from their Dutch or European equivalents.

**The 1892 Snouck Hurgronje advice on Atjeh** was the advisory of [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje]] that, in the source's narrative, "de methode aangaf tot onderwerping en pacificatie" (indicated the method for subjugation and pacification) of [[Acheh|Atjeh]]. The [[1892]] advice is the intellectual basis of the post-[[1896]] Dutch conquest of Atjeh and the principal didactic episode of the source's Atjehoorlog narrative.

## The three components

The [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] advice of [[1892]] had three components:

1. **Ignore the sultan's party**: "het hof van de 'sultanspartij' te negeren, als van geen betekenis" (to ignore the court of the 'sultan's party', as of no significance).
2. **Strike, do not negotiate**: "met de vijand niet te onderhandelen maar hem gevoelig te slaan, daar de Atjeher zijn overwinnaarswijsheid niet zou afleggen zonder gevoelig geslagen te zijn" (not to negotiate with the enemy but to strike him sensibly, since the Atjehnese would not lay down his pride of victory without being sensibly struck).
3. **Win the population**: "daarna te trachten, het vertrouwen van de bevolking te winnen door een goede welvaartspolitiek" (thereafter to seek to win the confidence of the population by a good welfare policy).

## The basis in Atjehnese social structure

The [[1892]] advice was based on [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]]'s "in zeer korte tijd volbrachte studie van land en volk van Atjeh" (study of the land and people of Atjeh completed in a very short time). The source preserves the [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck]] analysis of Atjehnese social structure: the *kampong*/*mukim*/*uléebalang*/*sagi*/*panglima sagi* hierarchy, and the verdict that the sultan's authority over the *panglima's sagi* and the *uléebalangs* existed "alleen in naam" (in name only). The analysis supplied the empirical basis for the first component of the advice: the sultan's party was to be ignored because the sultan's authority was nominal.

## The Dutch misreading

The source's verdict on Dutch policy before [[1892]] is direct: "Onze politiek werd veel te veel beheerst door de geheel verkeerde voorstelling, die wij ons van de betekenis en de macht van de Sultan maakten" (Our policy was far too much dominated by the wholly wrong conception we had of the significance and power of the Sultan). The [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] advice of [[1892]] supplied the corrective.

## The rejection and the reversal

The [[1892]] advice was rejected by the governor of Atjeh [[Deykerhoff|Deykerhoff]] and the governor-general [[Pijnacker Hordijk|Pijnacker Hordijk]], who instead armed and subsidised [[Teungku Um|Teungku Um]] — the warlord who would defect in [[1896]] and turn his modern-Dutch arms against his former patrons. The [[1896]] defection produced the [[Van der Wijck (governor-general)|Van der Wijck]] reversal: "De G.-G. Van der Wijck (1893-99) begreep, dat Snouck Hurgronje's adviezen gevolgd moesten worden. Ook de overste Joannes Benedictus van Heutsz had dit begrepen. In 1896 werd hij belast met de leiding der militaire operaties in Groot-Atjeh".

## Significance

The [[1892]] [[Dr. C. Snouck Hurgronje|Snouck Hurgronje]] advice is, in the source's narrative, the intellectual basis of the post-[[1896]] Dutch conquest of Atjeh. The advice's three components — ignore the sultan's party, strike the enemy sensibly, win the population by welfare policy — are the method of the [[Pacificatie van Atjeh|pacificatie]] that [[J. B. van Heutsz|Van Heutsz]] would implement after [[1896]].

**The Advance from Penajoeng to the Kraton (14 December 1873 – 24 January 1874)** was the main inland campaign phase of the [[Second Aceh Expedition (1873-1874)]]. It began after the Dutch success at [[Battle of Tiban (14 December 1873)|Tiban]] and ended with the Dutch occupation of the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] on [[24 January]] [[1874]]. In later debate, especially in [[Open Letter by Brutus to General Knoop]], this whole phase became the main example used to argue that the Dutch moved too slowly after each success.

## From Tiban to Penajoeng

After the victory at [[Battle of Tiban (14 December 1873)|Tiban]] on [[14 December]] [[1873]], the Dutch had broken the first strong line that blocked the road inland. According to [[Brutus]], the army should then have used the same afternoon and the next day for hard reconnaissance and immediate preparation for a quick move forward. Instead, he argued, valuable time was lost before the Dutch pressed on toward [[Penajoeng]].

The issue was not only speed for its own sake. In Brutus's view, every day of delay in [[Aceh|Atjeh]] gave the defenders more time to strengthen the next line of earthworks, bamboo obstacles, and firing positions.

## The concentration at Penajoeng

By [[18 December]] [[1873]], Dutch forces had reached [[Penajoeng]]. Over the next few days more brigades gathered there, and by [[23 December]] [[1873]] the force was large enough for new action. Brutus later argued that too many days passed with little more than camp organization. While the Dutch settled in, the Acehnese used the pause to prepare stronger defenses ahead.

## The move on Lemboe

Only on [[25 December]] [[1873]] did a stronger reconnaissance move toward [[Lemboe]]. Brutus believed this happened too late. In his reading, earlier movement might have prevented the fortification of [[Lemboe]] and the next line around the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Great Missigit]]. Instead, the Dutch had to attack positions that were already stronger than they would have been a few days earlier.

## The road to the Missigit

By [[3 January]] [[1874]], Dutch batteries were ready, but the ground between [[Gampong Jawa|Kampong Djawa]] and the [[Missigit]] had still not been fully cleared. Brutus used this to argue that the command was again losing time. The Dutch finally took the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Great Missigit]] on [[6 January]] [[1874]], which brought them much closer to the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]].

## After the Missigit

Even after the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Missigit]] fell, Brutus argued that the Dutch did not press forward quickly enough. Important ground west of the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] was not occupied until [[12 January]] [[1874]]. He counted these lost days carefully and treated them as proof that the command kept pausing when it should have kept moving.

## The Kraton falls

On [[24 January]] [[1874]], the Dutch finally enclosed the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]] and found that it had been abandoned. The royal center was now in Dutch hands, but Brutus argued that the slow advance had already shown a deeper weakness in the campaign. The expedition had reached its visible goal, yet it had not broken Acehnese resistance enough to end the war.

## Significance

**The Advance from Penajoeng to the Kraton** mattered because it linked several famous places and dates of the early war into one continuous story: [[Tiban]], [[Penajoeng]], [[Lemboe]], the [[Third Conquest of the Great Missigit|Great Missigit]], and finally the [[Kraton of Aceh|Kraton]]. In later criticism, this campaign phase became the main factual basis for the claim that the Dutch command moved too slowly and relied too much on the hope that taking the capital would bring automatic submission.

**Aceh** was the historic sultanate located on the northern tip of [[Sumatra]] that, according to a sixteenth‑century Malay manuscript note, may have received a Malay translation of al‑Ushi's *Bad' al-Amali* from [[India]].

## Possible reception of the Malay translation
The source observes that “het uit Indië naar Atjēh is gekomen” (the work may have come from India to Atjēh) {{p.98}}. This suggests that a Malay manuscript containing an interlinear translation of al‑Ushi's *Bad' al-Amali* could have reached the Acehnese sultanate during the [[1501]]–[[1600]].

## Historical context of Aceh in the sixteenth century
During the sixteenth century Aceh was a powerful Islamic polity that maintained extensive trade and scholarly links with [[India]] and other Indian Ocean ports. The possibility of receiving religious‑didactic texts such as the Malay *Bad' al-Amali* fits within this pattern of cultural exchange.

## Significance
The potential arrival of this manuscript highlights Aceh’s integration into a broader network of Malay‑language Islamic literature and underscores the sultanate’s role as a recipient of intellectual currents from the Indian subcontinent.
